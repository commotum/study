## Introduction

We can simplify the addition of two logarithms that share a base using the **product rule** for logarithms.

The **product rule** states that two logarithms which share a common base $\color{blue}n$ satisfy

$$
\log_{\color{blue}n}(a) + \log_{\color{blue}n}(b) = \log_{\color{blue}n}\left(ab\right)
$$

So, we can use the product rule to rewrite $\log_{\color{blue}5}(4) + \log_{\color{blue}5}(3)$ as

$$
\begin{aligned}
\log_{5} 4 + \log_{5} (3) &= \log_{5} (4 \cdot 3) \\
&= \log_{5} (12)
\end{aligned}
$$

The product rule works exactly the same for the natural and common logarithms.

## Combining Logarithms Using the Product Rule

**Example:** Express $\log_3(2)+\log_3(5)$ as a single logarithm.

**Explanation**

First, let's recall the product rule for logarithms:

$$
\log_n a + \log_n b = \log_n(ab)
$$

Using the product rule for logarithms, the given expression can be rewritten as

$$
\begin{aligned}
\log_{3} 2 + \log_{3} 5 &= \log_{3} (2 \cdot 5) \\
&= \log_{3} 10
\end{aligned}
$$

## Combining Logarithms With Coefficients Using the Product Rule

**Example:** Express $3\log_{5}{7}+3\log_{5}{3}$ as a single logarithm.

**Explanation**

First, let's recall the product rule for logarithms:

$$
\log_n a + \log_n b = \log_n(ab)
$$

If we factor the given expression and use the product rule for logarithms, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
3\log_{5} 7 + 3\log_{5} 3 &= 3(\log_{5} 7 + \log_{5} 3) \\
&= 3\log_{5} (7 \cdot 3) \\
&= 3\log_{5} 21
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
3\log_{5}{7}+3\log_{5}{3} = 3\log_{5} 21
$$

## Separating a Logarithm Into Two Logarithms Using the Product Rule

**Example:** Express $\ln\left(x^2 y\right)$ as a sum of two logarithms.

**Explanation**

First, let's recall the product rule for natural logarithms:

$$
\ln{a} + \ln{b} = \ln(ab)
$$

Using the product rule, the given expression can be rewritten as

$$
\begin{aligned}
\ln (x^{2}y) &= \ln (x^{2} \cdot y) \\
&= \ln (x^{2}) + \ln y
\end{aligned}
$$

## Evaluating an Expression Using the Product Rule

**Example:** If $\log_2(3)=k$ and $\log_2(x)=p$, what is the value of $\log_2(3x)$?

**Explanation**

First, let's recall the product rule for logarithms:

$$
\log_n a + \log_n b = \log_n(ab)
$$

Using the product rule, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
\log_{2} (3x) &= \log_{2} 3 + \log_{2} x \\
&= k + p
\end{aligned}
$$
