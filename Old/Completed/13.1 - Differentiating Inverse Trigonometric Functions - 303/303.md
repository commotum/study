## Introduction

Let's learn how to differentiate the inverse trigonometric functions: $\arcsin x$, $\arccos x$, and $\arctan x$.

We begin with arcsine. From the definition, we know that writing

$$
y=\arcsin{x}
$$

is equivalent to writing
$\sin y=x$.

Now, we differentiate both sides with respect to $x$ using implicit differentiation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{d}{dx}(\sin y) &= \frac{d}{dx}(x) \\
\frac{d}{dy}(\sin y) \cdot \frac{dy}{dx} &= \frac{d}{dx}(x) \\
\cos y \cdot \frac{dy}{dx} &= 1 \\
\frac{dy}{dx} &= \frac{1}{\cos y}
\end{aligned}
$$

So we've found the derivative of $\arcsin x$ in terms of $y$, but we want it to be expressed in terms of $x$ only.

To do that, we use the trigonometric identity

$$
\sin^2 y+\cos^2y= 1
$$

and the fact that $\sin y =x$. We write $\cos y$ as

$$
\cos y= \sqrt{1- \sin^2 y} = \sqrt{1 -x^2}
$$

Recall that the derivative of a function at a point equals the slope of the function's graph at that point. Since the graph of $\arcsin{x}$ has a positive slope everywhere in its domain, we take the positive square root.

![](Source/Images/8c81ea37649bc87cade5f34a4b646808.png)

Now, if we plug the last expression into our derivative of $y$, we get the derivative of $\arcsin x$ in terms of $x$:

$$
\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{1}{\cos y} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - x^{2}}}
$$

We can use the same technique to find the derivatives of $\arccos x$ and $\arctan x$. Try it for yourself! The results are given below.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{d}{dx}(\arcsin x) &= \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - x^{2}}} \\
\frac{d}{dx}(\arccos x) &= -\frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - x^{2}}} \\
\frac{d}{dx}(\arctan x) &= \frac{1}{1 + x^{2}}
\end{aligned}
$$

**Note:** To remember the derivative formulas above, it's helpful to notice the following:

- The derivative of $\arccos x$ is just like the derivative of $\arcsin x$, except that it's negative. The negative sign makes sense because $\arccos x$ is a "co"-function, and the derivative of a "co"-function is negative (for example, the derivative of $\cos x$ is negative).
- The derivative of $\arctan x$ is similar to the others, except that there is no square root and there are no negative signs.

## Differentiating an Inverse Sine Function

**Example:** Calculate $f'(x)$ for $f(x) = 5\arcsin(3x^2)$.

**Explanation**

The formula for the derivative of arcsine is

$$
\frac{\textrm{d}}{\textrm{d}x}(\arcsin x) = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}
$$

Using the chain rule and the formula above, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
f^{′}(x) &= \frac{d}{dx}(5\arcsin (3x^{2})) \\
&= 5 \cdot \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - (3x^{2})^{2}}} \cdot \frac{d}{dx}(3x^{2}) \\
&= \frac{5}{\sqrt{1 - 9x^{4}}} \cdot (6x) \\
&= \frac{30x}{\sqrt{1 - 9x^{4}}}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Differentiating an Inverse Cosine Function

**Example:** Calculate $\dfrac{\textrm{d}y}{\textrm{d}x}$, if $y = \arccos (5-8x)$.

**Explanation**

The formula for the derivative of arccosine is

$$
\frac{\textrm{d}}{\textrm{d}x}(\arccos x) = -\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}
$$

Using the chain rule and the formula above, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{dy}{dx} &= \frac{d}{dx}(\arccos (5 - 8x)) \\
&= - \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - (5 - 8x)^{2}}} \cdot \frac{d}{dx}(5 - 8x) \\
&= - \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - (25 - 80x + 64x^{2})}} \cdot (-8) \\
&= \frac{8}{\sqrt{80x - 24 - 64x^{2}}} \\
&= \frac{8}{\sqrt{4(20x - 6 - 16x^{2})}} \\
&= \frac{8}{2\sqrt{20x - 6 - 16x^{2}}} \\
&= \frac{4}{\sqrt{20x - 6 - 16x^{2}}}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Differentiating an Inverse Tangent Function

**Example:** Find the derivative of $y = \arctan (-7x)$.

**Explanation**

The formula for the derivative of arctangent is

$$
\frac{\textrm{d}}{\textrm{d}x}(\arctan x) = \dfrac{1}{1+x^2}
$$

Using the chain rule and the formula above, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{dy}{dx} &= \frac{d}{dx}(\arctan (-7x)) \\
&= \frac{1}{1 + (-7x)^{2}} \cdot \frac{d}{dx}(-7x) \\
&= \frac{1}{1 + 49x^{2}} \cdot (-7) \\
&=-\frac{7}{1 + 49x^{2}}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Differentiating Inverse Trigonometric Functions Using the Product or Quotient Rule

**Example:** If $y= x \arccos x$, calculate $\dfrac{\textrm{d}y}{\textrm{d}x}$.

**Explanation**

Applying the product rule and using the formula for the derivative of $\arccos x$, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{d}{dx}(x\arccos x) &= x \cdot \frac{d}{dx}(\arccos x) + \arccos x\frac{d}{dx}(x) \\
&= x \cdot (-\frac{1}{\sqrt{1 - x^{2}}}) + \arccos x \cdot 1 \\
&=-\frac{x}{\sqrt{1 - x^{2}}} + \arccos x
\end{aligned}
$$
