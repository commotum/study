## Introduction to Trapezoidal Outputs from Rectangular Pulses

For rectangular-pulse convolution, the key object to watch is the overlap between the fixed pulse $x(\tau)$ and the shifted pulse $h(t - \tau)$. At each value of t, the convolution value is the area of the product $x(\tau)$$h(t - \tau)$.

$$
y(t)=(x*h)(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x(\tau)h(t-\tau)\,d\tau
$$

Use the simplest unequal-width case first: let both pulses have height 1, with $x(t)$ nonzero on [0,5] and $h(t)$ nonzero on [0,2]. Then $h(t - \tau)$ is a width-2 rectangle sliding across the width-5 rectangle $x(\tau)$. Because the heights are both 1, the area is just the overlap length.

$$
y(t)=\text{overlap length of }[0,5]\text{ and }[t-2,t]
$$

![](<../Source/7.2.4. Trapezoidal Outputs from Rectangular Pulses/Images/l004-s001-te-section-001.png>)

Unequal widths create one extra middle behavior. With equal widths, the overlap grows and then immediately shrinks. Here, the shorter width-2 pulse can fit completely inside the longer width-5 pulse, so the overlap grows, stays constant for a while, and then shrinks.

That gives the trapezoid pattern. Before first contact, the output is zero. During entry, the overlap length increases, so the output is an increasing ramp. During full overlap, the overlap length is constant, so the output has a flat plateau. During exit, the overlap length decreases, so the output is a decreasing ramp. After last contact, the output returns to zero.

$$
0,\quad \text{growing ramp},\quad \text{plateau},\quad \text{shrinking ramp},\quad 0
$$

The rest of the lesson uses this region pattern as the control structure: order the breakpoints, identify the no-overlap regions, write the growing overlap length, write the full-overlap length, write the shrinking overlap length, and then assemble the complete trapezoidal output.

## Ordering the Breakpoints for Unequal-Width Rectangular Pulses

**Example:** For unit-height rectangular pulses $x(t) = 1$ on $0 \le t \le 5$ and $h(t) = 1$ on $0 \le t \le 2$, list the convolution breakpoints that separate the no-overlap, growing-overlap, full-overlap, shrinking-overlap, and no-overlap regions.

**Explanation**

The cue is that the two rectangular pulses have unequal widths. Before computing any overlap length, first order the contact times where the overlap behavior changes.

In the convolution picture, keep $x(\tau)$ fixed and slide $h(t - \tau)$ across it. The fixed support has length 5, and the moving support has length 2.

$$
\operatorname{supp}x(\tau)=[0,5],\qquad \operatorname{supp}h(t-\tau)=[t-2,t]
$$

![](<../Source/7.2.4. Trapezoidal Outputs from Rectangular Pulses/Images/l004-s002-te-section-002.png>)

First contact happens when the right edge of the moving support just reaches the left edge of the fixed support. Since the right edge of $h(t - \tau)$ is $\tau = t$, first contact occurs at $t = 0$.

The growing-overlap region ends when the shorter pulse has fully entered the longer pulse. The shorter width is 2, so the first internal breakpoint is $t = 2$.

The full-overlap region ends when the right edge of the moving support reaches the right edge of the fixed support. The longer width is 5, so the next breakpoint is $t = 5$.

Last contact happens after the shorter pulse has slid all the way past the longer pulse. Add the two widths to get the final breakpoint.

$$
t=5+2=7
$$

So the ordered breakpoints are $t = 0$, $t = 2$, $t = 5$, and $t = 7$. These divide the output into the five regions that the rest of the lesson will compute.

$$
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{time interval} & \text{region}\\
\hline
t<0 & \text{no overlap}\\
0<t<2 & \text{growing overlap}\\
2<t<5 & \text{full overlap}\\
5<t<7 & \text{shrinking overlap}\\
t>7 & \text{no overlap}
\end{array}
$$

Endpoint choices do not change the area of a continuous-time convolution, so the important result here is the ordered region map: no overlap, growing overlap, full overlap, shrinking overlap, no overlap.

## Identifying No-Overlap Regions Before and After Contact

**Example:** For unit-height rectangular pulses $x(t) = 1$ on $0 \le t \le 5$ and $h(t) = 1$ on $0 \le t \le 2$, identify the time intervals where the convolution output is zero because the pulses do not overlap.

**Explanation**

The cue is the phrase zero because the pulses do not overlap. Use the breakpoint map from the previous section, but only keep the two outer regions before first contact and after last contact.

$$
\operatorname{supp}x(\tau)=[0,5],\qquad \operatorname{supp}h(t-\tau)=[t-2,t]
$$

$$
\text{ordered breakpoints: }0,\ 2,\ 5,\ 7
$$

![](<../Source/7.2.4. Trapezoidal Outputs from Rectangular Pulses/Images/l004-s003-te-section-003.png>)

Before first contact, $t < 0$. Then the right edge of the moving support is still to the left of the fixed support, so there is no $\tau$ interval where both pulses are nonzero.

$$
t<0:\qquad L(t)=0,\qquad y(t)=0
$$

After last contact, $t > 7$. The moving support has passed the fixed support because its left edge t - 2 is greater than 5, so the overlap length is again zero.

$$
t>7:\qquad L(t)=0,\qquad y(t)=0
$$

Therefore the no-overlap regions are $t < 0$ and $t > 7$, and the convolution output is zero on both. Endpoint choices do not change the continuous-time area, so the important structural result is the two outer zero regions.

$$
y(t)=0\quad \text{for }t<0\text{ or }t>7
$$

## Computing the Growing-Overlap Ramp

**Example:** For unit-height rectangular pulses $x(t) = 1$ on $0 \le t \le 5$ and $h(t) = 1$ on $0 \le t \le 2$, compute $y(t)$ on the growing-overlap region.

**Explanation**

The cue is the phrase growing-overlap region. From the breakpoint map, growing overlap is the first nonzero interval, after first contact at $t = 0$ and before the shorter pulse is fully inside the longer pulse at $t = 2$.

$$
\text{growing-overlap interval: }0<t<2
$$

Use the same support representation as before: keep $x(\tau)$ fixed and slide $h(t - \tau)$. On this interval, the moving support has entered from the left but has not fully entered the fixed support.

$$
\operatorname{supp}x(\tau)=[0,5],\qquad \operatorname{supp}h(t-\tau)=[t-2,t]
$$

![](<../Source/7.2.4. Trapezoidal Outputs from Rectangular Pulses/Images/l004-s004-te-section-004.png>)

For $0 < t < 2$, the left edge t - 2 of the moving support is still less than 0, while the right edge t lies between 0 and 2. So the common $\tau$ interval starts at 0 and ends at t.

$$
\text{overlap interval}=[0,t]
$$

Compute the overlap length as right endpoint minus left endpoint. This is the step that turns the picture into the ramp formula.

$$
L(t)=t-0=t
$$

Both pulses have height 1, so the convolution value is the overlap area: height product times overlap length. The height product is 1, which leaves the same expression as the length.

$$
y(t)=(1)(1)L(t)=t,\qquad 0<t<2
$$

So on the growing-overlap interval, the output is an increasing ramp: $y(t) = t$ for $0 < t < 2$. This only computes the first nonzero region; the plateau and shrinking ramp are handled separately.

## Computing the Full-Overlap Plateau

**Example:** For unit-height rectangular pulses $x(t) = 1$ on $0 \le t \le 5$ and $h(t) = 1$ on $0 \le t \le 2$, compute $y(t)$ on the full-overlap plateau.

**Explanation**

The cue is the phrase full-overlap plateau. From the breakpoint map, the plateau is the interval after the shorter pulse has fully entered the longer pulse and before it starts leaving.

$$
\text{full-overlap interval: }2<t<5
$$

Use the same support picture. The fixed pulse occupies [0,5], and the moving width-2 pulse occupies [t - 2,t]. On $2 < t < 5$, both edges of the moving support are inside the fixed support.

$$
\operatorname{supp}x(\tau)=[0,5],\qquad \operatorname{supp}h(t-\tau)=[t-2,t]
$$

![](<../Source/7.2.4. Trapezoidal Outputs from Rectangular Pulses/Images/l004-s005-te-section-005.png>)

Because the shorter pulse is fully covered, the overlap interval is the entire moving support. Its length is the shorter width, not the longer width.

$$
\text{overlap interval}=[t-2,t]
$$

Compute the overlap length as right endpoint minus left endpoint. The t terms cancel, which is why the output is constant on this interval.

$$
L(t)=t-(t-2)=2
$$

Both pulses have height 1, so the convolution value is the unit-height area of the overlap. That makes the plateau value equal to the overlap length.

$$
y(t)=(1)(1)L(t)=2,\qquad 2<t<5
$$

So on the full-overlap plateau, the output is constant: $y(t) = 2$ for $2 < t < 5$. The plateau height comes from the shorter pulse width.

## Computing the Shrinking-Overlap Ramp

**Example:** For unit-height rectangular pulses $x(t) = 1$ on $0 \le t \le 5$ and $h(t) = 1$ on $0 \le t \le 2$, compute $y(t)$ on the shrinking-overlap region.

**Explanation**

The cue is the phrase shrinking-overlap region. From the breakpoint map, shrinking overlap happens after the plateau ends at $t = 5$ and before last contact at $t = 7$.

$$
\text{shrinking-overlap interval: }5<t<7
$$

Use the same support representation as before. The fixed support is [0,5], and the moving support is [t - 2,t]. On this interval, the moving support is leaving the fixed support on the right.

$$
\operatorname{supp}x(\tau)=[0,5],\qquad \operatorname{supp}h(t-\tau)=[t-2,t]
$$

![](<../Source/7.2.4. Trapezoidal Outputs from Rectangular Pulses/Images/l004-s006-te-section-006.png>)

For $5 < t < 7$, the left edge t - 2 is still less than 5, but the right edge t is already past 5. So the common $\tau$ interval starts at t - 2 and ends at 5.

$$
\text{overlap interval}=[t-2,5]
$$

Compute the overlap length as right endpoint minus left endpoint. This gives the remaining contact length before the pulses separate completely at $t = 7$.

$$
L(t)=5-(t-2)=7-t
$$

Both pulses have height 1, so the convolution value is the overlap area: height product times overlap length. The height product is 1, so the output equals the remaining overlap length.

$$
y(t)=(1)(1)L(t)=7-t,\qquad 5<t<7
$$

So on the shrinking-overlap interval, the output is a decreasing ramp: $y(t) = 7 - t$ for $5 < t < 7$. The expression uses the total contact width 7, not the longer width 5 by itself.

## Assembling the Complete Trapezoidal Output

**Example:** For unit-height rectangular pulses $x(t) = 1$ on $0 \le t \le 5$ and $h(t) = 1$ on $0 \le t \le 2$, assemble the complete piecewise formula for the convolution output $y(t)$.

**Explanation**

The cue is assemble the complete piecewise formula. At this point, the breakpoints and each region formula have already been computed; the task is to place them in the correct left-to-right order.

$$
\text{ordered breakpoints: }0,\ 2,\ 5,\ 7
$$

![](<../Source/7.2.4. Trapezoidal Outputs from Rectangular Pulses/Images/l004-s007-te-section-007.png>)

Start with the two no-overlap regions. Before first contact and after last contact, the overlap length is zero, so the convolution output is zero.

$$
y(t)=0\quad \text{for }t<0\text{ or }t>7
$$

Insert the first nonzero region next. On the growing-overlap interval, the overlap length is t, so the output rises linearly.

$$
y(t)=t,\qquad 0<t<2
$$

Then insert the full-overlap plateau. The shorter pulse has width 2, so the overlap length stays fixed at 2 throughout the middle interval.

$$
y(t)=2,\qquad 2<t<5
$$

Finally, insert the shrinking-overlap region. The remaining overlap length is measured back from last contact at $t = 7$.

$$
y(t)=7-t,\qquad 5<t<7
$$

Putting the pieces together gives the complete trapezoidal output. Endpoint choices do not change the continuous-time convolution area, so the open intervals keep the region structure clear.

$$
y(t)=\begin{cases}
0, & t<0,\\
t, & 0<t<2,\\
2, & 2<t<5,\\
7-t, & 5<t<7,\\
0, & t>7.
\end{cases}
$$

A quick check confirms the trapezoid shape: the growing ramp reaches height 2 at $t = 2$, the plateau stays at height 2 through $t = 5$, and the shrinking ramp returns to zero at $t = 7$.
