- paragraph:
  text: The convolution setup already tells us where the two factors are nonzero at a fixed time t. The geometric area method starts with that overlap interval on the tau-axis and turns it directly into the value of the convolution.
- display-math:
  latex: y(t)=(x*h)(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x(\tau)h(t-\tau)\,d\tau
- paragraph:
  text: In the fixed-time picture, x(tau) stays fixed while h(t - tau) is the shifted pulse. Only the tau-values where both rectangles overlap contribute to the integral.
- image:
  image_slot_id: section-001
- paragraph:
  text: Use the simplest case first: suppose the two positive rectangular pulses overlap on one interval of length L(t). If x(tau) has height A and h(t - tau) has height B on that overlap, then the product x(tau)h(t - tau) is constant there with height AB.
- paragraph:
  text: So the integral over the nonzero overlap is just the area of a rectangle. Its width is the overlap length, and its height is the product of the pulse amplitudes.
- display-math:
  latex: y(t)=AB\,L(t)
- paragraph:
  text: As t changes, the same area rule is reused. The overlap length can grow, stay constant during full overlap, or shrink, and those three behaviors become the pieces of the triangular or trapezoidal convolution output.
