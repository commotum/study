## Introduction to Reconstructing a Time-Domain Signal from $X(s)$

Reconstruction uses a one-sided inverse-Laplace move that is only valid for causal terms. For this lesson, a term is treated as causal when it matches the form that already includes or implies $u(t)$.

For each $X(s)$ term, first identify the corresponding one-sided pair, then apply inverse mapping term-by-term. Use linearity: multiply, add, and subtract in $X(s)$ first, then invert each piece into the same operation pattern for $x(t)$.

The base-case anchor is $X(s)=1/(s+3)$. The move is to read this as the transform of an exponential decay with no shifts and no extra constants, so the reconstructed time signal is $x(t)=e^{-3t}u(t)$.

$$
X(s)=\frac{1}{s+a} \Rightarrow x(t)=e^{-at}u(t)
$$

Next, move from one known term to one-piece combinations. The same reconstruction rule works on sums and constants, so each part is inverted independently and then added in the time domain.

$$
X(s)=X_1(s)+X_2(s) \Rightarrow x(t)=x_1(t)+x_2(t)
$$

In the next sections you will do this as a pattern: first a single known pair, then a sum of known pairs, then delayed terms, and finally a full reconstructed signal from a full solved $X(s)$.

## Reconstructing from a Single Known One-Sided Pair

**Example:** Reconstruct the causal signal from $X(s)=\frac{3}{s+4}$.

**Explanation**

The cue is a single one-sided rational term with the denominator in the form $s+a$, so this is a direct pair-recognition case. Identify the base pair $\frac{1}{s+a} \leftrightarrow e^{-at}u(t)$ and read $a=4$.

$$
\mathcal{L}^{-1}_+\left\{\frac{1}{s+4}\right\}=e^{-4t}u(t)
$$

Now apply the numerator gain outside the inverse transform. Since $X(s)=3\cdot \frac{1}{s+4}$, multiply the time-domain map by $3$ without changing the exponential rate.

$$
\mathcal{L}^{-1}_+\left\{\frac{3}{s+4}\right\}=3\,\mathcal{L}^{-1}_+\left\{\frac{1}{s+4}\right\}=3e^{-4t}u(t)
$$

So the reconstructed causal signal is $x(t)=3e^{-4t}u(t)$, which matches the one-sided target of a scaled decaying exponential starting at $t=0$.

## Reconstructing from a Sum of Known One-Sided Pairs

**Example:** Find the causal signal for $X(s)=\frac{2}{s+1}+\frac{1}{s+4}$.

**Explanation**

The cue is a sum of two known one-sided pairs, so apply linearity first. Write $X(s)$ as separate terms instead of trying to invert the whole fraction stack at once.

Split the transform into pieces: $X(s)=\frac{2}{s+1}+\frac{1}{s+4}$.

$$
\mathcal{L}^{-1}_+\left\{\frac{1}{s+a}\right\}=e^{-at}u(t)
$$

For $a=1$, $\mathcal{L}^{-1}_+\{1/(s+1)\}=e^{-t}u(t)$, then scale by $2$. For $a=4$, $\mathcal{L}^{-1}_+\{1/(s+4)\}=e^{-4t}u(t)$. Keep each output causal with $u(t)$.

$$
x(t)=2e^{-t}u(t)+e^{-4t}u(t)
$$

The reconstructed signal is the sum of the inverted pieces, so the final result is $x(t)=2e^{-t}u(t)+e^{-4t}u(t)$.

## Reconstructing Delayed Terms from Time-Shifting Factors

**Example:** Find $x(t)$ for $X(s)=\frac{4}{s+1}e^{-2s}$.

**Explanation**

The cue is a single delayed one-sided pair: the factor $e^{-2s}$ indicates a right shift in time after inversion. Write $X(s)=e^{-2s}X_0(s)$ with $X_0(s)=\frac{4}{s+1}$ and invert $X_0(s)$ first.

$$
\mathcal{L}^{-1}_+\left\{\frac{4}{s+1}\right\}=4e^{-t}u(t)
$$

Apply the one-sided delay rule with $T=2$: $\mathcal{L}_+^{-1}\{e^{-sT}X_0(s)\}=x_0(t-T)u(t-T)$. Here that gives $x(t)=4e^{-(t-2)}u(t-2)$.

$$
\mathcal{L}_+^{-1}\left\{\frac{4}{s+1}e^{-2s}\right\}=4e^{-(t-2)}u(t-2)
$$

The sign is fixed by causality: delay is $t-2$ inside the exponential and $u(t-2)$ in the support, not $t+2$.

## Reconstructing a Full Causal Signal from a Solved $X(s)$

**Example:** Given $X(s)=\frac{2}{s+1}+\frac{3}{s+2}e^{-2s}+\frac{1}{s+4}$, find the causal time-domain signal.

**Explanation**

The target is a full decomposition: identify three terms, apply linearity, and invert each with the one-sided map. Write $X(s)=X_1(s)+X_2(s)+X_3(s)$, where $X_1(s)=\frac{2}{s+1}$, $X_2(s)=\frac{3}{s+2}e^{-2s}$, and $X_3(s)=\frac{1}{s+4}$.

For the first and third non-delayed terms, invert directly using $\mathcal{L}_+^{-1}\{1/(s+a)\}=e^{-at}u(t)$. Multiply by numerator gains before recombining.

$$
\mathcal{L}^{-1}_+\left\{\frac{2}{s+1}\right\}=2e^{-t}u(t),\quad\mathcal{L}^{-1}_+\left\{\frac{1}{s+4}\right\}=e^{-4t}u(t)
$$

The middle term has a delay factor $e^{-2s}$, so invert $\frac{3}{s+2}$ first, then apply the shift rule to produce $u(t-2)$ support.

$$
\mathcal{L}^{-1}_+\left\{\frac{3}{s+2}e^{-2s}\right\}=3e^{-(t-2)}u(t-2)
$$

Add the three reconstructed pieces: $x(t)=2e^{-t}u(t)+3e^{-(t-2)}u(t-2)+e^{-4t}u(t)$.
