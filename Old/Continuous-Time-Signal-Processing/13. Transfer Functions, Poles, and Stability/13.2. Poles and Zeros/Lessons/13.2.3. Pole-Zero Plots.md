## Introduction to Pole-Zero Plots

A pole-zero plot is a geometric map of a rational transfer function on the complex $s$-plane. The goal is to read key dynamics by locating where the transfer function numerator and denominator become zero.

Start from the transfer-function template $H(s)=\frac{N(s)}{D(s)}$. The **direct rule** is: roots of $N(s)=0$ become zero locations, and roots of $D(s)=0$ become pole locations. Keep numerator and denominator roles separate.

$$
H(s)=\frac{N(s)}{D(s)}
$$

$$
N(s)=\prod_i (s-z_i),\;D(s)=\prod_j (s-p_j)
$$

Use the $s$-plane convention with the horizontal axis as real part $\sigma$ and vertical axis as imaginary part $j\omega$. This convention is fixed for every section so coordinates are comparable across steps.

Markers are consistent: use an open circle for a zero and a cross for a pole. For the base case, use $H(s)=\frac{s+1}{s+2}$, so $N(s)=0\Rightarrow s=-1$ and $D(s)=0\Rightarrow s=-2$. The next step is to plot one zero and one pole from this equation.

$$
H(s)=\frac{s+1}{s+2},\;N(s)=s+1,\;D(s)=s+2
$$

From the base case, place a zero marker at $s=-1$ and a pole marker at $s=-2$ on the real axis. Interpretation is built next: each plotted point represents a structural feature of the transfer function, which downstream sections reuse as the same move grows into plotting and reading more complex real and complex locations.

## Plotting Real Zeros and Real Poles

**Example:** Plot poles and zeros for $H(s)=\frac{(s+1)(s+2)}{s(s-1)}$.

**Explanation**

The cue is a factored transfer function. For plotting, first read the zeros from the numerator and poles from the denominator: zeros are numerator roots, poles are denominator roots.

Start by identifying each factorized polynomial:

$$
H(s)=\frac{(s+1)(s+2)}{s(s-1)}
$$

Set the numerator equal to zero to get $s=-1$ and $s=-2$, so these are real-axis zeros. Set the denominator equal to zero to get $s=0$ and $s=1$, so these are real-axis poles.

$$
N(s)=(s+1)(s+2)=0\Rightarrow s=-1,-2
$$

$$
D(s)=s(s-1)=0\Rightarrow s=0,1
$$

Classify the same real points with stable markers: open circles at $s=-2$ and $s=-1$ for zeros, and crosses at $s=0$ and $s=1$ for poles. Read each on the real-axis baseline of the $s$-plane before moving to non-real examples.

$$
\{\text{zeros}\} = \{-1,-2\},\quad \{\text{poles}\} = \{0,1\}
$$

## Plotting Complex Zeros and Complex Poles

**Example:** Example: Plot poles and zeros for $H(s)=\frac{(s+2)(s^2+1)}{(s+1)(s^2+4)}$.

**Explanation**

The cue is a factorized transfer function, so apply the same rule as the previous section: roots of the numerator are zeros and roots of the denominator are poles. The new wrinkle is that each quadratic factor produces a conjugate pair in the complex plane.

Start by copying the transfer function into root-finding form so you can extract each factor one at a time:

$$
H(s)=\frac{(s+2)(s^2+1)}{(s+1)(s^2+4)}
$$

Find all roots and classify each set. From $N(s)=(s+2)(s^2+1)$, set each factor to zero to get one real zero at $s=-2$ and one conjugate pair at $s=\pm j$. From $D(s)=(s+1)(s^2+4)$, set each factor to zero to get one real pole at $s=-1$ and one conjugate pair at $s=\pm 2j$.

$$
\{z\} = \{-2,\, 0\pm j1\},\quad \{p\} = \{-1,\, 0\pm j2\}
$$

Write the nonreal members as coordinates: $s=\sigma+j\omega$, so $s=\pm j$ become $(0,\pm 1)$ and $s=\pm 2j$ become $(0,\pm 2)$. Mark open-circle zeros at $(0,1)$ and $(0,-1)$ and a zero at $(-2,0)$; mark crosses at $(0,2)$, $(0,-2)$ and $(-1,0)$.

$$
\text{zeros}: (-2,0),(0,1),(0,-1),\quad \text{poles}: (-1,0),(0,2),(0,-2)
$$

## Plotting Poles and Zeros from Factored Transfer Functions

**Example:** Example: Plot poles and zeros for $H(s)=\frac{(s+1)(s^2+1)}{(s-1)(s+2)(s^2+4)}$.

**Explanation**

The cue is a fully factored transfer function containing both real and complex factors, so use the same root-to-marker rule and do not switch conventions: numerator roots are zeros, denominator roots are poles.

Work through one pass over factors and list roots from each factor:

$$
H(s)=\frac{(s+1)(s^2+1)}{(s-1)(s+2)(s^2+4)}
$$

From the numerator $N(s)=(s+1)(s^2+1)$: set each factor to zero to get $s=-1$ and $s=\pm j$. From the denominator $D(s)=(s-1)(s+2)(s^2+4)$: set each factor to zero to get $s=1$, $s=-2$, and $s=\pm 2j$.

$$
\{z\}=\{-1,\,0\pm j\},\quad \{p\}=\{1,-2,\,0\pm2j\}
$$

Convert nonreal roots to coordinates with $s=\sigma+j\omega$: the pair $\pm j$ is at $(0,\pm 1)$ and the pair $\pm 2j$ is at $(0,\pm 2)$. Place the zero markers at $(-1,0)$, $(0,1)$, and $(0,-1)$; place the pole markers at $(1,0)$, $(-2,0)$, $(0,2)$, and $(0,-2)$. Verify that each complex pair is symmetric across the real axis.

$$
\text{zeros}: (-1,0),(0,1),(0,-1)\quad \text{poles}: (1,0),(-2,0),(0,2),(0,-2)
$$

## Plotting Poles and Zeros from Polynomial Transfer Functions

**Example:** Example: Plot poles and zeros for $H(s)=\frac{s^3+2s^2-s}{s^3+s^2-4s+4}$.

**Explanation**

The cue is an expanded transfer function, so the first wrinkle is to factor both numerator and denominator before plotting. After factoring, use the same root-to-marker rule: numerator roots are zeros; denominator roots are poles.

$$
H(s)=\frac{s^3+2s^2-s}{s^3+s^2-4s+4}
$$

Start by factoring the numerator from expanded form: $s^3+2s^2-s=s(s+1-\sqrt{2})(s+1+\sqrt{2})$. So zeros are at $s=0$, $s=-1+\sqrt{2}$, and $s=-1-\sqrt{2}$.

$$
N(s)=s(s+1-\sqrt{2})(s+1+\sqrt{2}),\quad \{z\}=\{0,-1+\sqrt{2},-1-\sqrt{2}\}
$$

Factor the denominator numerically, since it does not split into neat integer or simple radicals in this scope: $s^3+s^2-4s+4\approx (s+2.8751)(s^2-1.8751s+1.3912)$.

Use the quadratic formula on $s^2-1.8751s+1.3912=0$ to get $s=\frac{1.8751\pm\sqrt{1.8751^2-4(1.3912)}}{2}\approx0.9376\pm j\,0.7157$. So poles are at $s\approx-2.8751$, $s\approx0.9376\pm j0.7157$.

$$
\{p\}\approx\{-2.8751,\,0.9376\pm j0.7157\}
$$

Map all roots to coordinates using $s=\sigma+j\omega$. For the real-zero set: at $(0,0)$, $(\sigma,\omega)=(0.4142,0)$, and $(-2.4142,0)$. For poles: one at $(-2.8751,0)$ and one conjugate pair at $(0.9376,\pm0.7157)$. Place open circles for zeros and crosses for poles, then check that each conjugate pair is symmetric above and below the real axis.

$$
\text{zeros}: (0,0),\, (0.4142,0),\,(-2.4142,0)\quad \text{poles}: (-2.8751,0),\,(0.9376,0.7157),\,(0.9376,-0.7157)
$$
