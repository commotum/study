## Introduction to Interpreting Poles in Simple Transform Pairs

In this topic, all inverse Laplace rules are one-sided. After inverting each transform term, include the causal window $u(t)$ so the result is a causal time-domain component.

Use this matching move first: identify which denominator template a term uses, then map that denominator directly to its time-domain behavior. The three canonical templates are $\frac{1}{s}$, $\frac{1}{s+a}$, and $\frac{1}{s-a}$.

$$
\mathcal{L}\{u(t)\}=\frac{1}{s},\;\operatorname{Re}(s)>0
$$

A pole at $s=0$ corresponds to the constant template, a pole at $s=-a$ with $a>0$ corresponds to a decaying exponential, and a pole at $s=a$ with $a>0$ corresponds to a growing exponential. Keep this interpretation attached to the denominator sign.

$$
\mathcal{L}\{e^{-at}u(t)\}=\frac{1}{s+a},\;a>0
$$

Use scalar multiplication by inspection after classifying the pole: $\frac{K}{s+a}$ inverts to $K e^{-at}u(t)$ (decay), and $\frac{K}{s-a}$ inverts to $K e^{at}u(t)$ (growth). This is the same move for all subsequent steps, with only sign and coefficient changes.

$$
\mathcal{L}\{e^{at}u(t)\}=\frac{1}{s-a},\;\operatorname{Re}(s)>a
$$

Next examples will reuse the same mapping and only vary two things: the pole location and the numerator scale $K$. Your job is to classify the term first, then scale.

Common mistake to avoid: flipping the pole sign or dropping $u(t)$. Any positive $a$ in $s+a$ is decay, and every one-sided inverse in this topic keeps the unit-step window.

## Mapping the Pole at $s=0$ to a Constant Time-Domain Component

**Example:** Find $x(t)$ for $X(s)=\frac{5}{s}$.

**Explanation**

The cue is a single term with denominator $s$, so this is the constant-time template from the origin pole. Match $\frac{1}{s}$ to $u(t)$, then keep the one-sided causal window on the result.

$$
X(s)=\frac{5}{s}=5\cdot\frac{1}{s}
$$

Apply linearity to move the scalar outside the inverse transform: $\mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{X(s)\right\}=\mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{5\cdot\frac{1}{s}\right\}=5\,\mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\frac{1}{s}\right\}$.

$$
\mathcal{L}^{-1}\{\frac{1}{s}\}=u(t)
$$

Substitute the base inverse and report the final result as $x(t)=5u(t)$.

## Mapping a Negative Real Pole to a Decaying Exponential

**Example:** Find $x(t)$ for $X(s)=\frac{4}{s+3}$.

**Explanation**

This is the next controlled step: the denominator is no longer just $s$, so classify the pole using the sign in $\frac{K}{s+a}$ and keep the causal inverse form.

$$
X(s)=\frac{4}{s+3}=\frac{4}{s-(-3)}
$$

Read $s+3$ as $s-(-3)$, so the pole is at $s=-3$ in the left half-plane. That identifies the decaying template for the inverse.

$$
\frac{1}{s+a}\;\longleftrightarrow\;e^{-at}u(t),\quad a=3
$$

Apply linearity to the leading coefficient: $x(t)=4\,\mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\frac{1}{s+3}\right\}=4e^{-3t}u(t)$.

Final result: $x(t)=4e^{-3t}u(t)$.

## Mapping a Positive Real Pole to a Growing Exponential

**Example:** Given $X(s)=\frac{2}{s-5}$, find the inverse one-sided time-domain expression $x(t)$.

**Explanation**

The new wrinkle is the pole sign: this term uses $\frac{1}{s-a}$, so identify the pole and then map to a growth template.

$$
X(s)=\frac{2}{s-5}=2\cdot\frac{1}{s-5}
$$

Read the denominator as $s-a$ with $a=5$, so the pole is at $s=5$. This is the positive-real case, corresponding to growth.

$$
\frac{1}{s-a}\;\longleftrightarrow\;e^{at}u(t),\quad a>0
$$

Apply the coefficient by linearity: $x(t)=2\,\mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\frac{1}{s-5}\right\}=2e^{5t}u(t)$.

Final result: $x(t)=2e^{5t}u(t)$.

## Comparing Pole Locations Across Simple Transform Pairs

**Example:** Find $x(t)$ when $X(s)=\frac{6}{s+3}+\frac{1}{s}-\frac{2}{s-4}$.

**Explanation**

This example extends to three terms at once, so classify each denominator separately and reuse the same mapping rule for each pole before recombining.

$$
X(s)=\frac{6}{s+3}+\frac{1}{s}-\frac{2}{s-4}=\frac{6}{s-(-3)}+\frac{1}{s}-\frac{2}{s-4}
$$

Classify each term: $s+3$ gives a pole at $s=-3$ (decay), $s$ gives a pole at $s=0$ (constant), and $s-4$ gives a pole at $s=4$ (growth).

$$
\frac{6}{s+3}\to 6e^{-3t}u(t),\quad \frac{1}{s}\to u(t),\quad -\frac{2}{s-4}\to -2e^{4t}u(t)
$$

Now recombine by linearity and keep one-sided support on every component: $x(t)=6e^{-3t}u(t)+u(t)-2e^{4t}u(t)$.

The final time-domain expression is $x(t)=6e^{-3t}u(t)+u(t)-2e^{4t}u(t)$.
