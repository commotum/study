## Introduction

The **column space** of a matrix $A$ is denoted $\text{Col}(A)$ and is the span of the columns of $A$ (i.e., the set of all possible linear combinations of columns of $A$). For example, consider the matrix

$\begin{bmatrix}1 & 3 \\ 2 & -1\end{bmatrix}$
where we denote its columns by $\color{red}\mathbf{a}_1$ and ${\color{blue}\mathbf{a}_2}$, respectively. The column space of this matrix is given by
$\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 2\end{bmatrix}$

Now, suppose we would like to determine whether the vector $\begin{bmatrix}2 \\ -3\end{bmatrix}$ lies in $\textrm{Col}(A)$. In other words, we need to check if $\mathbf{b} \in \textrm{Span}\{{\color{red}\mathbf{a}_1}, {\color{blue}\mathbf{a}_2} \}$. So, we need to determine whether $\mathbf{b}$ can be written as some linear combination of $\color{red}\mathbf{a}_1$ and ${\color{blue}\mathbf{a}_2}$:

$$
x_{1}a_{1} + x_{2}a_{2} = b
$$

Notice that the equation above can be written as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\begin{bmatrix}a_{1} & a_{2}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}x_{1} \\ x_{2}\end{bmatrix} &= b \\
\begin{bmatrix}1 & 3 \\ 2 & -1\end{bmatrix}_(⏟)_(A)\begin{bmatrix}x_{1} \\ x_{2}\end{bmatrix}_(⏟)_(x) &= \begin{bmatrix}2 \\ -3\end{bmatrix}_(⏟)_(b)
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the equation is equivalent to

$$
A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{b}
$$

To solve the equation, let's find the row echelon form of the augmented matrix $\begin{bmatrix}A \, & \, \mathbf{b}\end{bmatrix}$ as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\begin{bmatrix}A & b\end{bmatrix} &= \begin{bmatrix}1 & 3 & 2 \\ 2 & -1 & -3\end{bmatrix} \mid R_{2} \mid:=R_{2} + (-2)R_{1} \\
&= ∼\begin{bmatrix}1 & 3 & 2 \\ 0 & -7 & -7\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we have the system

$$
{x_{1} + 3x_{2} = 2; - 7x_{2} =-7
$$

From the second equation, we get $x_2=1$. Substituting into the first equation, we get $x_1=-1$.

Since we have a solution, the equation

$$
A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{b}
$$

is consistent, which implies that $\mathbf{b} \in \textrm{Col}(A)$.

Moreover, there is exactly one solution, so we have exactly one way to write $\mathbf{b}$ as a linear combination of $\color{red}\mathbf{a}_1$ and ${\color{blue}\mathbf{a}_2}$:
$\begin{bmatrix}2 \\ -3\end{bmatrix}$

## Determining if a Vector is in the Column Space of a Matrix

**Example:** The matrix $B$ and the vector $\mathbf{b}$ are given below.
Determine if $\mathbf{b} \in \textrm{Col}(B)$.
In the affirmative case, find the values of $x_1$ and $x_2$, where $x_1$ and $x_2$ are the real numbers such that $\mathbf{b}=x_1\mathbf{b}_1+x_2\mathbf{b}_2$, and $\mathbf{b}_1$, $\mathbf{b}_2$ are the respective columns of $B$.

$\begin{bmatrix}5 & -2 \\ 15 & -6 \\ 0 & 1\end{bmatrix}$

**Explanation**

In order to determine whether $\mathbf{b}$ lies in $\textrm{Col}(B)$, we need to check if the equation

$$
B\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{b}
$$

is consistent. So, let's find the row echelon form of the augmented matrix $\begin{bmatrix}B \, & \, \mathbf{b}\end{bmatrix}$ as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\begin{bmatrix}B & b\end{bmatrix} &= \begin{bmatrix}5 & -2 & 7 \\ 15 & -6 & 21 \\ 0 & 1 & 0\end{bmatrix}\begin{vmatrix}R_{2} &:=R_{2} - 3R_{1} \\ ∼\begin{bmatrix}5 & -2 & 7 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0\end{bmatrix} & R_{2}\end{vmatrix}↔R_{3} \\
&= ∼\begin{bmatrix}5 & -2 & 7 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we have the system

$$
{5x_{1} - 2x_{2} = 7; x_{2} = 0; 0 = 0
$$

From the third equation, we get the true statement $0=0$. So the system is equivalent to the following:

$$
{5x_{1} - 2x_{2} = 7; x_{2} = 0
$$

From the second equation, we get $x_2=0$. Substituting into the first equation gives

$$
x_1=\dfrac 7 5
$$

Since we have a solution, the equation

$$
B\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{b}
$$

is consistent, which implies that $\mathbf{b}\in\textrm{Col}(B)$.

Moreover, there is exactly one solution, so we have exactly one way to write $\mathbf{b}$ as a linear combination of $\mathbf{b}_1$ and $\mathbf{b}_2$:
$\begin{bmatrix}7 \\ 21 \\ 0\end{bmatrix}$

## Determining the Value of an Unknown Given a Vector That’s in the Column Space of a Matrix

**Example:** Consider the matrix given by $\begin{bmatrix}1 & 2 \\ 3 & -2 \\ 5 & -1\end{bmatrix}$ Determine the value of $k$ for which the vector $\begin{bmatrix}10 \\ -2 \\ 2 + k\end{bmatrix}$ belongs to $\textrm{Col}(A)$.

**Explanation**

If $\mathbf{b}$ lies in $\textrm{Col}(A)$, then the equation

$$
A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{b}
$$

must be consistent. So, let's find the row echelon form of the augmented matrix $\begin{bmatrix}A \, & \, \mathbf{b}\end{bmatrix}$ as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\begin{bmatrix}A & b\end{bmatrix} &= \begin{bmatrix}1 & 2 & 10 \\ 3 & -2 & -2 \\ 5 & -1 & 2 + k\end{bmatrix}\begin{vmatrix}R_{2} &:=R_{2} - 3R_{1} \\ ∼\begin{bmatrix}1 & 2 & 10 \\ 0 & -8 & -32 \\ 5 & -1 & 2 + k\end{bmatrix} & R_{3}\end{vmatrix}:=R_{3} - 5R_{1} \\
&= ∼\begin{bmatrix}1 & 2 & 10 \\ 0 & -8 & -32 \\ 0 & -11 & k - 48\end{bmatrix}\begin{vmatrix}R_{2} &:=\frac{1}{8}R_{2} \\ ∼\begin{bmatrix}1 & 2 & 10 \\ 0 & 1 & 4 \\ 0 & -11 & k - 48\end{bmatrix} & R_{3}\end{vmatrix}:=R_{3} + 11R_{2} \\
&= ∼\begin{bmatrix}1 & 2 & 10 \\ 0 & 1 & 4 \\ 0 & 0 & k - 4\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

So, we have the system

$$
{x_{1} + 2x_{2} = 10; x_{2} = 4; 0 = k - 4
$$

In order for this system to be consistent, we require $0=k-4$, which means that $k=4$.

<a id="properties-of-the-column-space-of-a-matrix"></a>
## Properties of the Column Space of a Matrix

The column space is defined for matrices of *any* dimensions. In general, if ${\color{black}\mathbf{a}_1}, {\color{black}\mathbf{a}_2}, \ldots, {\color{black}\mathbf{a}_n}$ are the columns of a matrix $A$, that is to say,
$\mid \mid \mid \mid \mid; a_{1}\begin{vmatrix}a_{2} & …\end{vmatrix}a_{n}; \mid \mid \mid \mid \mid$
then

$$
\textrm{Col}(A) = \textrm{Span} \{{\color{black}\mathbf{a}_1}, {\color{black}\mathbf{a}_2}, \ldots, {\color{black}\mathbf{a}_n} \}
$$

The column space satisfies the following properties:

- If $A$ is an ${\color{blue}m}\times n$ matrix, then $\textrm{Col}(A)$ is a subspace of $\mathbb{R}^{\color{blue}m}$. This is because each column has ${\color{blue}m}$ entries.
- $\mathbf{b}\in\textrm{Col}(A)$ if and only if the equation $A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{b}$ is consistent (has a solution).
- If the equation $A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{b}$ is inconsistent (has no solution), then $\mathbf{b}\not\in\textrm{Col}(A)$.

These hold for any $m\times n$ matrix, and the matrix does not necessarily have to be square.

## Determining if a Vector Is in the Column Space of a Larger Matrix

**Example:** The matrix $C$ and the vector $\mathbf{b}$ are given below. Determine if $\mathbf{b} \in \textrm{Col}(C)$. In affirmative case, find the value of $x_1 \cdot x_2 \cdot x_3$, where $x_1$, $x_2$, and $x_3$ are real numbers such that $\mathbf{b}=x_1\mathbf{c}_1+x_2\mathbf{c}_3+x_3\mathbf{c}_3$, and $\mathbf{c}_1$, $\mathbf{c}_2$, and $\mathbf{c}_3$ are the respective columns of $C$.

$\begin{bmatrix}1 & 1 & 4 \\ -1 & 2 & 0 \\ 0 & -3 & 1\end{bmatrix}$

**Explanation**

In order to determine whether $\mathbf{b}$ lies in $\textrm{Col}(C)$, we need to check if the equation

$$
C\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{b}
$$

is consistent. So, let's find the row echelon form of the augmented matrix $\begin{bmatrix}C \, & \, \mathbf{b}\end{bmatrix}$ as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\begin{bmatrix}C & b\end{bmatrix} &= \begin{bmatrix}1 & 1 & 4 & 5 \\ -1 & 2 & 0 & -4 \\ 0 & -3 & 1 & 4\end{bmatrix}\begin{vmatrix}R_{2} &:=R_{2} + R_{1} \\ ∼\begin{bmatrix}1 & 1 & 4 & 5 \\ 0 & 3 & 4 & 1 \\ 0 & -3 & 1 & 4\end{bmatrix} & R_{3}\end{vmatrix}:=R_{3} + R_{2} \\
&= ∼\begin{bmatrix}1 & 1 & 4 & 5 \\ 0 & 3 & 4 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 5 & 5\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we have the system

$$
{x_{1} + x_{2} + 4x_{3} = 5; 3x_{2} + 4x_{3} = 1; 5x_{3} = 5
$$

From the third equation, we get $x_3=1$. Substituting into the second equation gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
3x_{2} + 4(1) &= 1 \\
3x_{2} &= -3 \\
x_{2} &= -1
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, from the first equation, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_{1} &= 5 - x_{2} - 4x_{3} \\
&= 5 - (-1) - 4(1) \\
&= 2
\end{aligned}
$$

Since we have a solution, the equation

$$
C\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{b}
$$

is consistent, which implies that $\mathbf{b}\in\textrm{Col}(C)$.

Moreover, there is exactly one solution, so we have exactly one way to write $\mathbf{b}$ as a linear combination of $\mathbf{c}_1$, $\mathbf{c}_2$, and $\mathbf{c}_3$:
$\begin{bmatrix}5 \\ -4 \\ 4\end{bmatrix}$

Finally, we get

$$
x_1 \cdot x_2 \cdot x_3 = 2 \cdot (-1) \cdot 1 = -2
$$

.
