## Introduction

Consider a vector $\mathbf{u}$ which is given in terms of the vectors $\mathbf{v_1}$ and $\mathbf{v_2}$ as follows:

$$
{\color{red}3}{\color{blue}\mathbf{v_1}}+({\color{red}-2}){\color{blue}\mathbf{v_2}} = \mathbf{u}
$$

Here, we say that $\mathbf{u}$ is a **linear combination** of the vectors ${\color{blue}\mathbf{v_1}}$ and ${\color{blue}\mathbf{v_2}}$ with coefficients ${\color{red}3}$ and ${\color{red}-2}$ respectively.

In general, a linear combination of $n$ vectors $\mathbf{v_1},\mathbf{v_2},\dots,\mathbf{v_n}$ is the *weighted sum*

$$
{\color{red}x_1}\mathbf{\color{blue}v_1}+{\color{red}x_2}\mathbf{\color{blue}v_2}+\cdots + {\color{red}x_n}\mathbf{\color{blue}v_n} = \mathbf{u}
$$

where ${\color{red}x_1},{\color{red}x_2},\dots,{\color{red}x_n}\in\mathbb{R}$. We say that $\mathbf{u}$ is a linear combination of the vectors $\mathbf{\color{blue}v_1},\mathbf{\color{blue}v_2},\dots,\mathbf{\color{blue}v_n}$ with coefficients (or *weights*) ${\color{red}x_1},{\color{red}x_2},\dots,{\color{red}x_n}$, respectively.

For instance,
$\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ -1\end{bmatrix}$
is the linear combination of vectors $\mathbf{\color{blue}v_1},\mathbf{\color{blue}v_2},\mathbf{\color{blue}v_3}$ with coefficients

$$
{\color{red}2}, {\color{red}0}, {\color{red}\dfrac{1}{2}}
$$

respectively.

## Solving for One Coefficient in a Linear Combination

**Example:** Find $s$, if $3\mathbf{a_1}+s\mathbf{a_2} = \mathbf{b}$ and
$\begin{bmatrix}-1 \\ 2 \\ 1\end{bmatrix}$

**Explanation**

Using the properties of addition/subtraction and scalar multiplication of vectors to isolate the $s$ term, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
3a_{1} + sa_{2} &= b \\
3\begin{bmatrix}-1 \\ 2 \\ 1\end{bmatrix} + s\begin{bmatrix}4 \\ 0 \\ -2\end{bmatrix} &= \begin{bmatrix}13 \\ 6 \\ -5\end{bmatrix} \\
s\begin{bmatrix}4 \\ 0 \\ -2\end{bmatrix} &= \begin{bmatrix}13 \\ 6 \\ -5\end{bmatrix} - 3\begin{bmatrix}-1 \\ 2 \\ 1\end{bmatrix} \\
s\begin{bmatrix}4 \\ 0 \\ -2\end{bmatrix} &= \begin{bmatrix}16 \\ 0 \\ -8\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

From the final equation above, we can tell that $s=4$.

## Solving for a Vector in a Linear Combination

**Example:** Find $\mathbf{x}$, if $\mathbf{a}+2\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b}$ and
$\begin{bmatrix}12 \\ -11 \\ -5\end{bmatrix}$

**Explanation**

Using the properties of addition/subtraction and scalar multiplication of vectors to isolate $\mathbf{x}$, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
a + 2x &= b \\
2x &= b - a \\
x &= \frac{1}{2}(b - a) \\
x &= \frac{1}{2}(\begin{bmatrix}24 \\ -9 \\ 13\end{bmatrix} - \begin{bmatrix}12 \\ -11 \\ -5\end{bmatrix}) \\
x &= \frac{1}{2}\begin{bmatrix}12 \\ 2 \\ 18\end{bmatrix} \\
x &= \begin{bmatrix}6 \\ 1 \\ 9\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="solving-for-two-coefficients-in-a-linear-combination"></a>
## Solving for Two Coefficients in a Linear Combination

**Example:** Consider the vectors

$\begin{bmatrix}4 \\ -1 \\ 3\end{bmatrix}$

If $x_1 \mathbf{c}_1 + x_2\mathbf{c}_2 = \mathbf d$, then what is $x_1+x_2$?

**Explanation**

If

$$
x_1 \mathbf{c}_1 + x_2\mathbf{c}_2 = \mathbf d
$$

then we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_{1}\begin{bmatrix}4 \\ -1 \\ 3\end{bmatrix} + x_{2}\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 0 \\ 1\end{bmatrix} &= \begin{bmatrix}5 \\ -5 \\ 0\end{bmatrix} \\
\begin{bmatrix}4x_{1} + x_{2} \\ -x_{1} \\ 3x_{1} + x_{2}\end{bmatrix} &= \begin{bmatrix}5 \\ -5 \\ 0\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Equating the corresponding components, we get the following system:

$$
{4x_{1} + x_{2} = 5; - x_{1} =-5; 3x_{1} + x_{2} = 0
$$

From the second equation, we obtain $x_1=5$, which we substitute into the first equation to get

$$
\begin{aligned}
4x_{1} + x_{2} &= 5 \\
4(5) + x_{2} &= 5 \\
x_{2} &= -15
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we substitute $x_1=5$ and $x_2=-15$ into the third equation to get

$$
\begin{aligned}
3x_{1} + x_{2} &= 0 \\
3(5) + (-15) &= 0 \\
0 &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

which gives a true statement.

So the solution of the system is $x_1=5$ and $x_2=-15$, which means

$$
5 \mathbf{c_1}-15 \mathbf{c_2} = \mathbf{d}
$$

Consequently,

$$
x_1+x_2=5-15=-10
$$

## Solving for Three Coefficients in a Linear Combination

**Example:** Consider the vectors

$\begin{bmatrix}0 \\ 1 \\ 0\end{bmatrix}$

If $x_1 \mathbf{a}_1 + x_2\mathbf{a}_2 + x_3\mathbf{a}_3 = \mathbf b$, then what is $x_1+x_2+x_3$?

**Explanation**

Since $\mathbf{b}$ is a linear combination of vectors $\mathbf{a_1}, \,\mathbf{a_2}$ and $\mathbf{a_3}$ with coefficients $x_1,\, x_2$ and $x_3$, respectively, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_{1}a_{1} + x_{2}a_{2} + x_{3}a_{3} &= b \\
x_{1}\begin{bmatrix}0 \\ 1 \\ 0\end{bmatrix} + x_{2}\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 0 \\ -1\end{bmatrix} + x_{3}\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 2 \\ 3\end{bmatrix} &= \begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 1 \\ 7\end{bmatrix} \\
\begin{bmatrix}0 \\ x_{1} \\ 0\end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix}x_{2} \\ 0 \\ -x_{2}\end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix}x_{3} \\ 2x_{3} \\ 3x_{3}\end{bmatrix} &= \begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 1 \\ 7\end{bmatrix} \\
\begin{bmatrix}x_{2} + x_{3} \\ x_{1} + 2x_{3} \\ -x_{2} + 3x_{3}\end{bmatrix} &= \begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 1 \\ 7\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Equating the corresponding components, we get the following system:

$$
{x_{2} + x_{3} = 1; x_{1} + 2x_{3} = 1; - x_{2} + 3x_{3} = 7
$$

We will solve this system using Gaussian elimination. First, we write the augmented matrix for this system:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}0 & 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 & 2 & 1 \\ 0 & -1 & 3 & 7\end{bmatrix}
$$

Row-reducing the augmented matrix, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
M &= \begin{bmatrix}0 & 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 & 2 & 1 \\ 0 & -1 & 3 & 7\end{bmatrix}\begin{vmatrix}R_{1} & ↔R_{2} \\ ∼\begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 & 2 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & -1 & 3 & 7\end{bmatrix} & R_{3}\end{vmatrix}:=R_{3} + R_{2} \\
&= ∼\begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 & 2 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 4 & 8\end{bmatrix} \mid R_{3} \mid:=\frac{1}{4}R_{3} \\
&= ∼\begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 & 2 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 & 2\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

The system is now

$$
{x_{1} + 2x_{3} = 1; x_{2} + x_{3} = 1; x_{3} = 2
$$

From the third equation, we get $x_3 = 2$. Substituting this into the second equation, we get

$$
x_2+(2) = 1 \quad\Rightarrow\quad x_2 = -1
$$

and substituting $x_3=2$ into the first equation, we get

$$
x_1+2(2) = 1 \quad\Rightarrow\quad x_1 = -3
$$

So the solution of the system is

$$
x_1=-3, x_2=-1, x_3=2
$$

which means

$$
-3 \mathbf{a_1}- \mathbf{a_2} +2 \mathbf{a_3}= \mathbf{b}
$$

Consequently,

$$
x_1+x_2+x_3=-3-1+2=-2
$$
