## Introduction

Remember that the null space $\textrm{Null}(A)$ of a matrix $A$ consists of the solutions to the equation

$$
A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{0}
$$

To find a basis of $\textrm{Null}(A)$, we solve the equation

$$
A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{0}
$$

and express the solution as a linear combination of linearly independent vectors. Those vectors form a basis of $\textrm{Null}(A)$.

To illustrate, consider the matrix $\begin{bmatrix}1 & 2 & 2 \\ 2 & 4 & 4 \\ 1 & 3 & 3\end{bmatrix}$ Computing the reduced row echelon form of $A$, we get

$$
\begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 0\end{bmatrix}
$$

So, the equation

$$
A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{0}
$$

is equivalent to the system

$$
{x_{1} = 0; x_{2} + x_{3} = 0; 0 = 0
$$

where $\color{blue}x_1$ and $\color{blue}x_2$ are basic variables (corresponding to the pivot columns) and $\color{red}x_3$ is a free variable (corresponding to the non-pivot column).

Therefore, any vector $\mathbf{x}$ in $\textrm{Null}(A)$ has the form

$$
\begin{bmatrix}x_{1} \\ x_{2} \\ x_{3}\end{bmatrix}
$$

So, the set $\begin{bmatrix}0 \\ -1 \\ 1\end{bmatrix}$ is a basis of $\textrm{Null}(A)$ since it is a linearly independent set and the vector $\begin{bmatrix}0 \\ -1 \\ 1\end{bmatrix}$ generates the null space of $A$.

**Note:** It might be the case that $\textrm{Null}(A)$ for a particular matrix $A$ equals the so-called zero subspace $\{\mathbf{0} \}$ containing only the zero-vector. The basis of $\{\mathbf{0} \}$ is said to be the *empty set*, which is a set that does not contain any vectors. The empty set is denoted by $\emptyset$ or $\{\,\}$.

## Finding a Basis for a Given Null Space

**Example:** Given that $\begin{bmatrix}3x_{3} - x_{4} \\ 4x_{3} \\ x_{3} \\ x_{4}\end{bmatrix}$ find a basis for $\textrm{Null}(A)$.

**Explanation**

Any vector in $\textrm{Null}(A)$ has the form

$$
\begin{bmatrix}3x_{3} - x_{4} \\ 4x_{3} \\ x_{3} \\ x_{4}\end{bmatrix}
$$

Expanding, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
\begin{bmatrix}3x_{3} - x_{4} \\ 4x_{3} \\ x_{3} \\ x_{4}\end{bmatrix} &= \begin{bmatrix}3x_{3} \\ 4x_{3} \\ x_{3} \\ 0\end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix}-x_{4} \\ 0 \\ 0 \\ x_{4}\end{bmatrix} \\
&= x_{3}\begin{bmatrix}3 \\ 4 \\ 1 \\ 0\end{bmatrix} + x_{4}\begin{bmatrix}-1 \\ 0 \\ 0 \\ 1\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, a basis for $\textrm{Null}(A)$ is

$$
\begin{bmatrix}3 \\ 4 \\ 1 \\ 0\end{bmatrix}
$$

since it is a linearly independent set and its vectors generate $\textrm{Null}(A)$.

## Finding a Basis for the Null Space of a Matrix Given in Reduced Row Echelon Form

**Example:** Find a basis of the null space of the matrix $\begin{bmatrix}1 & 3 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 & -4 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0\end{bmatrix}$

**Explanation**

To find a basis of $\textrm{Null}(A)$, we need to solve the equation

$$
A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{0}
$$

and express the solution as a linear combination of linearly independent vectors. Those vectors form a basis of $\textrm{Null}(A)$.

The given matrix is already in reduced row echelon form. So, the equation

$$
A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{0}
$$

corresponds to the following system:

$$
{x_{1} + 3x_{2} = 0; x_{3} - 4x_{4} = 0; 0 = 0
$$

Here, $x_1$ and $x_3$ are basic variables corresponding to the pivot columns ($1$st and $3$rd) and $x_2,x_4$ are free variables corresponding to the non-pivot columns. Therefore, if $\mathbf{x} \in \textrm{Null}(A)$, then

$$
\begin{aligned}
\begin{bmatrix}x_{1} \\ x_{2} \\ x_{3} \\ x_{4}\end{bmatrix} &= \begin{bmatrix}-3x_{2} \\ x_{2} \\ 4x_{4} \\ x_{4}\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}-3x_{2} \\ x_{2} \\ 0 \\ 0\end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix}0 \\ 0 \\ 4x_{4} \\ x_{4}\end{bmatrix} \\
&= x_{2}\begin{bmatrix}-3 \\ 1 \\ 0 \\ 0\end{bmatrix} + x_{4}\begin{bmatrix}0 \\ 0 \\ 4 \\ 1\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

where $x_2,x_4\in\mathbb{R}$. This implies that

$$
\begin{bmatrix}-3 \\ 1 \\ 0 \\ 0\end{bmatrix}
$$

is a basis of $\textrm{Null}(A)$ since it is a linearly independent set and its vectors generate $\textrm{Null}(A)$.

## Finding a Basis of the Null Space of a Matrix Given a Row Equivalent Matrix

**Example:** The matrices $A$ and $B$ are given below. If $A$ is row equivalent to $B$, then find a basis of $\textrm{Null}(A)$.

$\begin{bmatrix}-9 & 3 & -1 & 2 \\ 1 & 3 & -1 & 2 \\ -5 & 9 & -3 & 6\end{bmatrix}$

**Explanation**

To find a basis of $\textrm{Null}(A)$, we need to solve the equation

$$
A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{0}
$$

and express the solution as a linear combination of linearly independent vectors. Those vectors form a basis of $\textrm{Null}(A)$.

Since $A\sim B$, the equation

$$
A \mathbf{x} = \mathbf{0}
$$

is equivalent to the equation

$$
B\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{0}
$$

which corresponds to the following system:

$$
{x_{1} = 0; x_{2} - \frac{1}{3}x_{3} + \frac{2}{3}x_{4} = 0
$$

So, the general solution is $x_1=0$ and

$$
x_2=\dfrac{1}{3}x_3 - \dfrac{2}{3} x_4
$$

where $x_3$ and $x_4$ are free variables. Therefore, if $\mathbf{x}\in\textrm{Null}(A)$, then

$$
\begin{aligned}
\begin{bmatrix}x_{1} \\ x_{2} \\ x_{3} \\ x_{4}\end{bmatrix} &= \begin{bmatrix}0 \\ \frac{1}{3}x_{3} - \frac{2}{3}x_{4} \\ x_{3} \\ x_{4}\end{bmatrix} = x_{3}\begin{bmatrix}0 \\ \frac{1}{3} \\ 1 \\ 0\end{bmatrix} + x_{4}\begin{bmatrix}0 \\ -\frac{2}{3} \\ 0 \\ 1\end{bmatrix}, x_{3}, x_{4} ∈ R
\end{aligned}
$$

This implies that

$$
\begin{bmatrix}0 \\ \frac{1}{3} \\ 1 \\ 0\end{bmatrix}
$$

is a basis of $\textrm{Null}(A)$ since it is a linearly independent set and its vectors generate $\textrm{Null}(A)$.

## Finding a Basis for the Null Space of a Matrix That Is Not in Reduced Row Echelon Form

**Example:** Given the matrix $\begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 & 2 \\ 0 & 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 2 & 5\end{bmatrix}$ find a basis of $\textrm{Null}(A)$.

**Explanation**

First, we reduce the matrix $A$ to reduced row echelon form (RREF):

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= \begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 & 2 \\ 0 & 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 2 & 5\end{bmatrix}\begin{vmatrix}R_{3} &:=R_{3} + (-2)R_{2} \\ ∼\begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 & 2 \\ 0 & 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 0 & 1\end{bmatrix} & R_{2}\end{vmatrix}:=R_{2} + (-2)R_{3} \\
&= ∼\begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 & 2 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1\end{bmatrix} \mid R_{1} \mid:=R_{1} + (-2)R_{3} \\
&= ∼\begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the equation

$$
A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{0}
$$

is equivalent to the system

$$
{x_{1} = 0; x_{2} = 0; x_{3} = 0
$$

This implies that the only solution of

$$
A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{0}
$$

is

$$
\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{0}
$$

So

$$
\textrm{Null}(A)=\{\mathbf{0} \}
$$

which means that the basis of $\textrm{Null}(A)$ is the empty set, i.e.,

$$
\mathcal{B}=\{\,\}
$$
