## Introduction

Two statements are **logically equivalent** if they have identical truth values in their respective truth tables. In this lesson, we'll use truth tables to show that two complex compound statements are logically equivalent.

If two statements $P$ and $Q$ are logically equivalent, we write
$P\equiv Q$
where the symbol "$\equiv$" means "is logically equivalent to."

For example, let's use a truth table to prove the following logical equivalence:

$$
(A \land \neg A) \lor B \equiv B
$$

First, we construct truth tables with columns $A$ and $B$ containing all possible combinations of truth values. Then, we create another column for

$$
\neg A
$$

which has the opposite truth values to $A$.

| $\,A\,$ | $\,B\,$ | $\, \neg A \,$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| $\bbox[2pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm T}$ | $\textrm T$ | $\bbox[2pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm F}$ |
| $\bbox[2pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm T}$ | $\textrm F$ | $\bbox[2pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm F}$ |
| $\bbox[2pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm F}$ | $\textrm T$ | $\bbox[2pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm T}$ |
| $\bbox[2pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm F}$ | $\textrm F$ | $\bbox[2pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm T}$ |

Now, we add a column for

$$
A \land \neg A
$$

Recall that

$$
A\land \neg A
$$

is true if $A$ is true and

$$
\neg A
$$

is true and is false otherwise. In this case,

$$
A\land \neg A
$$

is always false.

| $\,A\,$ | $\,B\,$ | $\, \neg A \,$ | $\, A \land \neg A \,$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $\bbox[2pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm T}$ | $\textrm T$ | $\bbox[2pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm F}$ | $\bbox[2pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm F}$ |
| $\bbox[2pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm T}$ | $\textrm F$ | $\bbox[2pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm F}$ | $\bbox[2pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm F}$ |
| $\bbox[2pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm F}$ | $\textrm T$ | $\bbox[2pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm T}$ | $\bbox[2pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm F}$ |
| $\bbox[2pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm F}$ | $\textrm F$ | $\bbox[2pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm T}$ | $\bbox[2pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm F}$ |

Finally, we complete the table with a column for

$$
(A \land \neg A) \lor B
$$

Recall that

$$
(A \land \neg A) \lor B
$$

is true if

$$
(A \land \neg A)
$$

is true, or $B$ is true, or both are true.

| $\,A\,$ | $\,B\,$ | $\, \neg A \,$ | $\, A \land \neg A \,$ | $\, (A \land \neg A) \lor B \,$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $\textrm T$ | $\bbox[2pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm T}$ | $\textrm F$ | $\bbox[2pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm F}$ | $\bbox[2pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm T}$ |
| $\textrm T$ | $\bbox[2pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm F}$ | $\textrm F$ | $\bbox[2pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm F}$ | $\bbox[2pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm F}$ |
| $\textrm F$ | $\bbox[2pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm T}$ | $\textrm T$ | $\bbox[2pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm F}$ | $\bbox[2pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm T}$ |
| $\textrm F$ | $\bbox[2pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm F}$ | $\textrm T$ | $\bbox[2pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm F}$ | $\bbox[2pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm F}$ |

We see that the truth values of

$$
(A \land \neg A) \lor B
$$

are the same as the truth values of $B$. This proves that

$$
(A \land \neg A) \lor B
$$

is logically equivalent to $B$.

## Completing a Truth Table

**Example:** What are the missing values in the following truth table?

| $\,X\,$ | $\,Y\,$ | $\,X \land (X \lor \neg Y) \,$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| $\textrm T$ | $\textrm T$ | $\bbox[2pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\phantom{T}}$ |
| $\textrm T$ | $\textrm F$ | $\bbox[2pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\phantom{T}}$ |
| $\textrm F$ | $\textrm T$ | $\bbox[2pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\phantom{F}}$ |
| $\textrm F$ | $\textrm F$ | $\bbox[2pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\phantom{F}}$ |

**Explanation**

First, we add a column for

$$
\neg Y{:}
$$

| $\,X\,$ | $\,Y\,$ | $\, \neg Y \,$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| $\textrm T$ | $\textrm T$ | $\textrm F$ |
| $\textrm T$ | $\textrm F$ | $\textrm T$ |
| $\textrm F$ | $\textrm T$ | $\textrm F$ |
| $\textrm F$ | $\textrm F$ | $\textrm T$ |

Then, we add a column for

$$
X \lor \neg Y{:}
$$

| $\,X\,$ | $\,Y\,$ | $\, \neg Y \,$ | $\, X \lor \neg Y \,$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $\textrm T$ | $\textrm T$ | $\textrm F$ | $\textrm T$ |
| $\textrm T$ | $\textrm F$ | $\textrm T$ | $\textrm T$ |
| $\textrm F$ | $\textrm T$ | $\textrm F$ | $\textrm F$ |
| $\textrm F$ | $\textrm F$ | $\textrm T$ | $\textrm T$ |

Finally, we add a column for

$$
X \land (X \lor \neg Y){:}
$$

| $\,X\,$ | $\,Y\,$ | $\, \neg Y \,$ | $\, X \lor \neg Y \,$ | $\, X \land (X \lor \neg Y) \,$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $\textrm T$ | $\textrm T$ | $\textrm F$ | $\textrm T$ | $\bbox[2pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm T}$ |
| $\textrm T$ | $\textrm F$ | $\textrm T$ | $\textrm T$ | $\bbox[2pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm T}$ |
| $\textrm F$ | $\textrm T$ | $\textrm F$ | $\textrm F$ | $\bbox[2pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm F}$ |
| $\textrm F$ | $\textrm F$ | $\textrm T$ | $\textrm T$ | $\bbox[2pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm F}$ |

## Identifying Equivalent Statements Using Partially Completed Truth Tables

**Example:** Find the missing values in the following truth table:

| $\,A\,$ | $\,B\,$ | $\,\neg A \land \neg B \,$ | $\,\neg (A \lor B) \,$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $\textrm T$ | $\textrm T$ | $\textrm F$ | $\textrm F$ |
| $\textrm T$ | $\textrm F$ | $\bbox[2pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\phantom{F}}$ | $\textrm F$ |
| $\textrm F$ | $\textrm T$ | $\textrm F$ | $\bbox[2pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\phantom{F}}$ |
| $\textrm F$ | $\textrm F$ | $\textrm T$ | $\bbox[2pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\phantom{F}}$ |

Use your results to determine whether $\neg A \land \neg B$ is logically equivalent to $\neg (A \lor B)$.

**Explanation**

First, let's solve for the missing values:

- When $A \equiv \textrm T$ and $B \equiv \textrm F$, we have
$¬A∧¬B \mid ≡¬T∧¬F; ≡F∧T; ≡F$
- When $A \equiv \textrm F$ and $B \equiv \textrm T$, we have
$¬(A∨B) \mid ≡¬(F∨T); ≡¬T; ≡F$
- When $A \equiv \textrm F$ and $B \equiv \textrm F$, we have
$¬(A∨B) \mid ≡¬(F∨F); ≡¬F; ≡T$

Our completed table is shown below.

| $\,A\,$ | $\,B\,$ | $\,\neg A \land \neg B \,$ | $\,\neg (A \lor B) \,$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $\textrm T$ | $\textrm T$ | $\textrm F$ | $\textrm F$ |
| $\textrm T$ | $\textrm F$ | $\bbox[2pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm F}$ | $\textrm F$ |
| $\textrm F$ | $\textrm T$ | $\textrm F$ | $\bbox[2pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm F}$ |
| $\textrm F$ | $\textrm F$ | $\textrm T$ | $\bbox[2pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm T}$ |

To determine whether

$$
\neg A \land \neg B
$$

is logically equivalent to

$$
\neg (A \lor B)
$$

we need to check whether their truth tables are equivalent.

Looking at the last two columns, we see that they are the same $(\textrm{F},\textrm{F},\textrm{F},\textrm{T})$. Therefore, we conclude that

$$
\neg A \land \neg B
$$

is logically equivalent to

$$
\neg (A \lor B)
$$

that is,

$$
\neg A \land \neg B \bbox[2pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\equiv} \neg (A \lor B)
$$

## Identifying Logically Equivalent Statements

**Example:** Which of the following statements are logically equivalent?

1. $B \land \neg A$
2. $\neg (A \lor \neg B)$
3. $\neg (\neg A \lor B)$

**Explanation**

Two statements are logically equivalent if they have the same truth table. So, we construct a truth table for the three given statements:

| $\,A\,$ | $\,B\,$ | $\, B \land \neg A \,$ | $\, \neg (A \lor \neg B) \,$ | $\, \neg (\neg A \lor B) \,$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $\textrm T$ | $\textrm T$ | $\textrm F$ | $\textrm F$ | $\textrm F$ |
| $\textrm T$ | $\textrm F$ | $\textrm F$ | $\textrm F$ | $\textrm T$ |
| $\textrm F$ | $\textrm T$ | $\textrm T$ | $\textrm T$ | $\textrm F$ |
| $\textrm F$ | $\textrm F$ | $\textrm F$ | $\textrm F$ | $\textrm F$ |

We see that only the statements

$$
B \land \neg A
$$

and

$$
\neg (A \lor \neg B)
$$

have the same truth table, so they are logically equivalent.

Therefore, the correct answer is "I and II only."
