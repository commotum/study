## Introduction

Given two random variables $X$ and $Y$, the variance of the sum or difference of these variables is given by

$$
\textrm{Var}[X\pm Y] = \textrm{Var}[X] + \textrm{Var}[Y] \pm 2\textrm{Cov}[X,Y]
$$

where $\textrm{Cov}[X,Y]$ is the covariance of $X$ and $Y$. We'll prove this formula at the end of the lesson.

Note that if $X$ and $Y$ are independent, then

$$
\textrm{Cov}[X,Y]= 0
$$

and we have

$$
\textrm{Var}[X\pm Y] = \textrm{Var}[X] + \textrm{Var}[Y]
$$

---

<a id="calculating-the-variance-of-a-sum-or-difference-of-random-variables"></a>
## Calculating the Variance of a Sum or Difference of Random Variables

**Example:** For two random variables $X$ and $Y$, we have $\textrm{Var}[X] = 3$, $\textrm{Var}[Y] = 0.24$, and $\textrm{Cov}[X,Y] = 0.2$. Determine $\textrm{Var}[X-Y]$.

**Explanation**

Recall that if $X$ and $Y$ are random variables, then

$$
\textrm{Var}[X \pm Y] = \textrm{Var}[X] + \textrm{Var}[Y] \pm 2\textrm{Cov}[X,Y]
$$

Substituting the given data into the formula above, we obtain

$$
\textrm{Var}[X - Y] = 3 + 0.24 - 2 \cdot 0.2 = 2.84
$$

<a id="the-variance-of-a-sum-of-scaled-random-variables"></a>
## The Variance of a Sum of Scaled Random Variables

If $X$ and $Y$ are random variables and $a$ and $b$ are constants, then

$$
\textrm{Var}[aX + bY] = a^2 \textrm{Var}[X] + b^2 \textrm{Var}[Y] + 2ab \textrm{Cov}[X,Y]
$$

If $X$ and $Y$ are independent, then

$$
\textrm{Cov}[X,Y] = 0
$$

and we recover the formula

$$
\textrm{Var}[aX + bY] = a^2 \textrm{Var}[X] + b^2 \textrm{Var}[Y]
$$

---

<a id="calculating-the-variance-of-a-sum-of-scaled-random-variables"></a>
## Calculating the Variance of a Sum of Scaled Random Variables

**Example:** For two random variables $X$ and $Y$, we have $\textrm{Var}[X] = 5$, $\textrm{Var}[Y] = 2$, and $\textrm{Cov}[X,Y] = 3$. Find $\textrm{Var}[2X - 5Y]$.

**Explanation**

Recall that if $X$ and $Y$ are random variables, and $a$ and $b$ are constants, then

$$
\textrm{Var}[aX + bY] = a^2\textrm{Var}[X] + b^2\textrm{Var}[Y] + 2ab\,\textrm{Cov}[X,Y]
$$

Substituting the given data into the formula above, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
Var[2X - 5Y] &= 2^{2} \cdot 5 + (-5)^{2} \cdot 2 + 2 \cdot 2 \cdot (-5) \cdot 3 \\
&= 20 + 50 - 60 \\
&= 10
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="calculating-a-combined-variance-given-some-moments"></a>
## Calculating a Combined Variance Given Some Moments

**Example:** The joint probability mass function of the random variables $X$ and $Y$ is given below.

| $f$ | $Y=0$ | $Y=2$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| $X=0$ | $0.1$ | $0.3$ |
| $X=3$ | $0.4$ | $0.2$ |

Find $\textrm{Var}[5X+2Y]$.

**Explanation**

Recall that if $X$ and $Y$ are random variables and $a$ and $b$ are constants, then

$$
\textrm{Var}[aX+bY] = a^2 \textrm{Var}[X] + b^2 \textrm{Var}[Y] + 2ab\,\textrm{Cov}[X,Y]
$$

Also, the variance of a random variable $X$ is determined by the formula

$$
\textrm{Var}[X] = \textrm{E}[X^2] - \textrm{E}[X]^2
$$

and the covariance of two random variables $X$ and $Y$ is given by the formula

$$
\textrm{Cov}[X,Y] = \textrm{E}[XY] - \textrm{E}[X] \cdot \textrm{E}[Y]
$$

First, let's find the marginal mass functions for $X$ and $Y$. These are given by the row and column totals, respectively.

| $f$ | $Y=0$ | $Y=2$ | $f_X$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $X=0$ | $0.1$ | $0.3$ | $0.4$ |
| $X=3$ | $0.4$ | $0.2$ | $0.6$ |
| $f_Y$ | $0.5$ | $0.5$ | $1$ |

Now, we can find $\textrm{Var}[X]$. If $X$ is defined on a sample space $S_X$, then

$$
\begin{aligned}
E[X] &= ∑_(x ∈ S_{X})x \cdot f_{X}(x) \\
&= 0 \cdot 0.4 + 3 \cdot 0.6 \\
&= 1.8
\end{aligned}
$$

and

$$
\begin{aligned}
E[X^{2}] &= ∑_(x ∈ S_{X})x^{2} \cdot f_{X}(x) \\
&= 0^{2} \cdot 0.4 + 3^{2} \cdot 0.6 \\
&= 5.4
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
Var[X] &= E[X^{2}] - E[X]^{2} \\
&= 5.4 - 1.8^{2} \\
&= 2.16
\end{aligned}
$$

Similarly, if $Y$ is defined on a sample space $S_Y$, then

$$
\begin{aligned}
E[Y] &= ∑_(y ∈ S_{y})y \cdot f_{Y}(y) \\
&= 0 \cdot 0.5 + 2 \cdot 0.5 \\
&= 1
\end{aligned}
$$

and

$$
\begin{aligned}
E[Y^{2}] &= ∑_(y ∈ S_{y})y^{2} \cdot f_{Y}(y) \\
&= 0^{2} \cdot 0.5 + 2^{2} \cdot 0.5 \\
&= 2
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
Var[Y] &= E[Y^{2}] - E[Y]^{2} \\
&= 2 - 1^{2} \\
&= 1
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, let's find $\textrm{Cov}[X,Y]$. We find $\textrm{E}[XY]$ using the rule of the lazy statistician:

$$
\begin{aligned}
E[XY] &= ∑_((x, y) ∈ S)xy \cdot f(x, y) \\
&= 0 \cdot 0 \cdot f(0, 0) + 0 \cdot 2 \cdot f(0, 2) + 3 \cdot 0 \cdot f(3, 0) + 3 \cdot 2 \cdot f(3, 2) \\
&= 0 + 0 + 0 + 6 \cdot 0.2 \\
&= 1.2
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, using the covariance formula, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
Cov[XY] &= E[XY] - E[X] \cdot E[Y] \\
&= 1.2 - 1.8 \cdot 1 \\
&=-0.6
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
Var[5X + 2Y] &= 5^{2} \cdot 2.16 + 2^{2} \cdot 1 + 2 \cdot 5 \cdot 2 \cdot (-0.6) \\
&= 54 + 4 - 12 \\
&= 46
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="generalizing-to-multiple-random-variables"></a>
## Generalizing to Multiple Random Variables

Finally, we have the following generalization for the variance of a sum of $n$ random variables.

$$
\textrm{Var} \left[ \sum\limits_{i=1}^{n} a_i X_i \right] = \sum\limits_{i=1}^{n} a_i^2 \textrm{Var}[X_i] + 2\sum\limits_{i < j} a_i a_j \textrm{Cov}[X_i, X_j]
$$

Let's see an example of how this works in practice.

---

<a id="calculating-the-variance-of-a-sum-of-three-random-variables"></a>
## Calculating the Variance of a Sum of Three Random Variables

**Example:** For three random variables $X_1,X_2$, and $X_3$, we have the following variances and covariances:

$\textrm{Var}[X_1] = 1, \qquad \textrm{Var}[X_2] = 3, \qquad \textrm{Var}[X_3] = 8 \qquad$

$\textrm{Cov}[X_1,X_2] = 2, \qquad \textrm{Cov}[X_1,X_3] = 4, \qquad \textrm{Cov}[X_2,X_3] = -5 \qquad$

Find $\textrm{Var}[5X_1 + 2X_2 + 3X_3]$.

**Explanation**

Recall that if $X_1, \ldots, X_n$ are random variables, and $a_1, \ldots, a_n$ are constants, then

$$
\textrm{Var}\left[\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n} a_i X_i \right] = \sum\limits_{i=1}^{n} a_i^2 \textrm{Var}[X_i] + 2\sum\limits_{i < j} a_i a_j \textrm{Cov}[X_i,X_j]
$$

Hence, for $n=3$ random variables, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
Var[a_{1}X_{1} + a_{2}X_{2} + a_{3}X_{3}] &= a_{1}^{2}Var[X_{1}] + a_{2}^{2}Var[X_{2}] + a_{3}^{2}Var[X_{3}] \\
&= + 2a_{1}a_{2}Cov[X_{1}, X_{2}] + 2a_{1}a_{3}Cov[X_{1}, X_{3}] + 2a_{2}a_{3}Cov[X_{2}, X_{3}]
\end{aligned}
$$

Substituting the given data into the formula above, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
Var[5X_{1} + 2X_{2} + 3X_{3}] &= 5^{2} \cdot 1 + 2^{2} \cdot 3 + 3^{2} \cdot 8 \\
&= + 2 \cdot 5 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 + 2 \cdot 5 \cdot 3 \cdot 4 + 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 3 \cdot (-5) \\
&= 25 + 12 + 72 + 40 + 120 - 60 \\
&= 209
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="proof-of-the-sum-formula"></a>
## Proof of the Sum Formula

Let's prove the following formula:

$$
\textrm{Var}[X+ Y] = \textrm{Var}[X] + \textrm{Var}[Y] + 2\textrm{Cov}[X,Y]
$$

Applying the definition of variance and using the linearity of expectation, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
Var[X + Y] &= E[((X + Y) - E[X + Y])^{2}] \\
&= E[((X - E[X]) + (Y - E[Y]))^{2}] \\
&= E[(X - E[X])^{2} + (Y - E[Y])^{2} + 2(X - E[X])(Y - E[Y])]
\end{aligned}
$$

Distributing the expected value, and using the fact that

$$
\textrm{E}[aX] = a\cdot \textrm{E}[X]
$$

for constant $a$, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
Var[X + Y] &= E[(X - E[X])^{2} + (Y - E[Y])^{2} + 2(X - E[X])(Y - E[Y])] \\
&= E[(X - E[X])^{2}] + E[(Y - E[Y])^{2}] + 2 \cdot E[(X - E[X])(Y - E[Y])] \\
&= Var[X] + Var[Y] + 2Cov[X, Y]
\end{aligned}
$$

as required.

The proof for the difference between two random variables is similar.
