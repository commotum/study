## Introduction

For discrete random variables $X$ and $Y$, the **conditional expected value of $X$ given $Y=y$** is defined by

$$
\textrm E\begin{bmatrix}X & Y=y\end{bmatrix}=\sum_{x}x f_{X\mid Y}(x \mid y)
$$

where $f_{X\mid Y}(x \mid y)$ is the conditional probability mass function of $X$ given $Y$.

This definition is similar to that of $\textrm{E}[X]$. However, we now use the conditional PMF $f_{X\mid Y}(x \mid y)$ instead of the marginal PMF $f_X(x)$ in the summation.

Note the following:

- The conditional expectation $\textrm E\begin{bmatrix}X & Y = y\end{bmatrix}$ is (in general) a function of $y$. This makes intuitive sense. If the distribution of $X$ varies with $Y$, then the expected value of $X$ for a given $Y$ must vary with the value of $Y$.
- If $y$ is replaced with a number, then $\textrm E\begin{bmatrix}X & Y = y\end{bmatrix}$ returns a single value.
- If $X$ and $Y$ are independent, then $\textrm E\begin{bmatrix}X & Y = y\end{bmatrix} = \textrm E[X]$ for all $y$. Again, this makes intuitive sense. If the outcome of $Y$ has no influence on the outcome of $X$, then the distribution of $X$ and, consequently, its mean, is also unaffected by $Y$.
- We sometimes use the notation $\mu_{X \mid y}$ to denote $E\begin{bmatrix}X & Y = y\end{bmatrix}$.

Finally, we have an analogous definition for

$$
\textrm E\begin{bmatrix}Y & X=x\end{bmatrix}
$$

$$
\textrm E\begin{bmatrix}Y & X=x\end{bmatrix}=\sum_{y}y f_{Y\mid X}(y \mid x)
$$

## Calculating a Conditional Expected Value Given a Conditional Mass Function

**Example:** Find the $\textrm{E}\big\begin{bmatrix}Y \, & \, X=2\big\end{bmatrix}$ given that the conditional probability mass function $f_{Y\mid X}(y \, \mid \, 2)$ is shown in the table above.

| $y$ | $1$ | $2$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| $f_{Y\mid X}(y \, \mid \, 2)$ | $\dfrac{2}{3}$ | $\dfrac{1}{3}$ |

**Explanation**

The conditional expected value of $Y$ given $X=x$ is defined by

$$
\textrm{E}\big\begin{bmatrix}Y \, & \, X=x\big\end{bmatrix} = \sum_{y} y \, f_{Y \mid X}(y \, \mid \, x)
$$

Therefore, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
E\begin{bmatrix}Y & X = 2\end{bmatrix} &= ∑_(y)yf_{Y\mid X}(y \mid 2) \\
&= 1 \cdot \frac{2}{3} + 2 \cdot \frac{1}{3} \\
&= \frac{4}{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Calculating a Conditional Expectation Using Row Totals

**Example:** Find $\textrm{E}\big\begin{bmatrix}Y \, & \, X=2\big\end{bmatrix}$ given that the random variables $X$ and $Y$ have the joint probability mass function $f(x,y)$ shown in the table above.

| $f$ | $Y=1$ | $Y=2$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| $X=1$ | $\dfrac{1}{9}$ | $\dfrac{2}{9}$ |
| $X=2$ | $\dfrac{8}{27}$ | $\dfrac{5}{27}$ |
| $X=3$ | $\dfrac{2}{27}$ | $\dfrac{1}{9}$ |

**Explanation**

Recall that the conditional expected value of $Y$ given $X=x$ is defined by

$$
\textrm{E}\big\begin{bmatrix}Y \, & \, X=x\big\end{bmatrix} = \sum_{y} y \, f_{Y \mid X}(y \, \mid \, x)
$$

In order to find $f_{Y \mid X}(y \, \mid \, x)$, let's first find the marginal distribution for $X$, corresponding to the row totals:

| $f$ | $Y=1$ | $Y=2$ | $f_X(x)$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $X=1$ | $\dfrac{1}{9}$ | $\dfrac{2}{9}$ | $\dfrac{1}{3}$ |
| $X=2$ | $\dfrac{8}{27}$ | $\dfrac{5}{27}$ | $\dfrac{13}{27}$ |
| $X=3$ | $\dfrac{2}{27}$ | $\dfrac{1}{9}$ | $\dfrac{5}{27}$ |

The conditional probability mass function of $Y$ given that $X=x$ is

$$
f_{Y \mid X}(y \, \mid \, x) = \dfrac{f(x,y)}{f_X(x)}
$$

Therefore, dividing each joint probability by the corresponding row total, we obtain the conditional probability mass function of $Y$ given that $X=x{:}$

| $f_{Y \mid X}(y \, \mid \, x)$ | $Y=1$ | $Y=2$ |  |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $X=1$ | $\dfrac{1}{3}$ | $\dfrac{2}{3}$ | $1$ |
| $X=2$ | $\dfrac{8}{13}$ | $\dfrac{5}{13}$ | $1$ |
| $X=3$ | $\dfrac{2}{5}$ | $\dfrac{3}{5}$ | $1$ |

Finally, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
E\begin{bmatrix}Y & X = 2\end{bmatrix} &= ∑_(y)yf_{Y\mid X}(y \mid 2) \\
&= 1 \cdot \frac{8}{13} + 2 \cdot \frac{5}{13} \\
&= \frac{18}{13}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Calculating a Conditional Expectation Using Column Totals

**Example:** Find $\textrm{E}\big\begin{bmatrix}X \, & \, Y=1\big\end{bmatrix}$ given that the random variables $X$ and $Y$ have the joint probability mass function $f(x,y)$ shown in the table above.

| $f$ | $Y=1$ | $Y=2$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| $X=2$ | $\dfrac{1}{9}$ | $\dfrac{2}{9}$ |
| $X=4$ | $\dfrac{1}{3}$ | $\dfrac{1}{9}$ |
| $X=6$ | $\dfrac{1}{9}$ | $\dfrac{1}{9}$ |

**Explanation**

Recall that the conditional expected value of $X$ given $Y=y$ is defined by

$$
\textrm{E}\big\begin{bmatrix}X \, & \, Y=y\big\end{bmatrix} = \sum_{x} x \, f_{X \mid Y}(x \, \mid \, y)
$$

In order to find $f_{X \mid Y}(x \, \mid \, y)$, let's first find the marginal distribution for $Y$, corresponding to the column totals:

| $f$ | $Y=1$ | $Y=2$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| $X=2$ | $\dfrac{1}{9}$ | $\dfrac{2}{9}$ |
| $X=4$ | $\dfrac{1}{3}$ | $\dfrac{1}{9}$ |
| $X=6$ | $\dfrac{1}{9}$ | $\dfrac{1}{9}$ |
| $f_Y(y)$ | $\dfrac{5}{9}$ | $\dfrac{4}{9}$ |

The conditional probability mass function of $X$ given that $Y=y$ is

$$
f_{X \mid Y}(x \, \mid \, y) = \dfrac{f(x,y)}{f_Y(y)}
$$

Therefore, dividing each joint probability by the corresponding column total, we obtain the conditional probability mass function of $X$ given that $Y=y{:}$

| $f_{X \mid Y}(x \, \mid \, y)$ | $Y=1$ | $Y=2$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| $X=2$ | $\dfrac{1}{5}$ | $\dfrac{1}{2}$ |
| $X=4$ | $\dfrac{3}{5}$ | $\dfrac{1}{4}$ |
| $X=6$ | $\dfrac{1}{5}$ | $\dfrac{1}{4}$ |
|  | $1$ | $1$ |

Finally, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
E\begin{bmatrix}X & Y = 1\end{bmatrix} &= ∑_(x)xf_{X\mid Y}(x \mid 1) \\
&= 2 \cdot \frac{1}{5} + 4 \cdot \frac{3}{5} + 6 \cdot \frac{1}{5} \\
&= \frac{20}{5} \\
&= 4
\end{aligned}
$$
