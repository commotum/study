## Introduction

To compute the expected value of a discrete random variable $X$ given a joint mass function $f(x,y)$, we must first find its marginal mass function $f_X$. Once the marginal mass function is known, we can then compute $\textrm{E}[X]$ using the formula

$$
\textrm{E}[X] = \sum\limits_{i=1}^n x_i \, f_X(x_i)
$$

For example, consider the joint probability mass function $f(x,y)$ for the discrete random variables $X$ and $Y$, shown below.

| $f$ | $Y=1$ | $Y=2$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| $X=1$ | $0.05$ | $0.15$ |
| $X=2$ | $0.7$ | $0.1$ |

Recall that the marginal distribution for $X$ corresponds to the row totals, and the marginal distribution for $Y$ corresponds to the column totals. Since we're only interested in the expected value of $X$, let's compute the row totals only.

| $f$ | $Y=1$ | $Y=2$ | $f_X$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $X=1$ | $0.05$ | $0.15$ | $\bbox[2pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}0.20}$ |
| $X=2$ | $0.7$ | $0.1$ | $\bbox[2pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}0.80}$ |

Therefore, the marginal mass function of $X$ is as follows:

| $x$ | $1$ | $2$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| $f_X(x)$ | $0.20$ | $0.80$ |

We can now compute the expected value of $X$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
E[X] &= ∑_(i = 1)^(n)x_{i}f_{X}(x_{i}) \\
&= 1 \cdot 0.2 + 2 \cdot 0.8 \\
&= 0.2 + 1.6 \\
&= 1.8
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, we conclude that

$$
\textrm{E}[X] = 1.8
$$

## Finding the Expected Value of a Discrete Random Variable From a Joint Distribution

**Example:** Given the joint probability distribution for two discrete random variables $X$ and $Y$ below, find $\textrm{E}[4X-3Y]$.

| $f$ | $Y=0$ | $Y=1$ | $Y=3$ | $Y=5$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $X=-1$ | $0.1$ | $0.1$ | $0.05$ | $0.1$ |
| $X=2$ | $0.1$ | $0.05$ | $0.1$ | $0.1$ |
| $X=4$ | $0.1$ | $0.05$ | $0.05$ | $0.1$ |

**Explanation**

Recall that the marginal distribution for $X$ corresponds to the row totals, and the marginal distribution for $Y$ corresponds to the column totals.

| $f$ | $Y=0$ | $Y=1$ | $Y=3$ | $Y=5$ | $f_X$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $X=-1$ | $0.1$ | $0.1$ | $0.05$ | $0.1$ | $0.35$ |
| $X=2$ | $0.1$ | $0.05$ | $0.1$ | $0.1$ | $0.35$ |
| $X=4$ | $0.1$ | $0.05$ | $0.05$ | $0.1$ | $0.3$ |
| $f_Y$ | $0.3$ | $0.2$ | $0.2$ | $0.3$ | $1$ |

Therefore, the probability mass functions for $X$ and $Y$ are given by the following tables:

| $x$$-1$$2$$4$$f_X(x)$$0.35$$0.35$$0.3$ |  | $y$$0$$1$$3$$5$$f_Y(y)$$0.3$$0.2$$0.2$$0.3$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: |

Now, we can compute the expected values for $X$ and $Y{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
E[X] &= ∑_(i = 1)^(n)x_{i}f_{X}(x_{i}) \\
&= (-1) \cdot 0.35 + 2 \cdot 0.35 + 4 \cdot 0.3 \\
&=-0.35 + 0.7 + 1.2 \\
&= 1.55
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
E[Y] &= ∑_(i = 1)^(n)y_{i}f_{Y}(y_{i}) \\
&= 0 \cdot 0.3 + 1 \cdot 0.2 + 3 \cdot 0.2 + 5 \cdot 0.3 \\
&= 0 + 0.2 + 0.6 + 1.5 \\
&= 2.3
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, using the properties of expectation, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
E[4X - 3Y] &= 4E[X] - 3E[Y] \\
&= 4(1.55) - 3(2.3) \\
&= 6.2 - 6.9 \\
&=-0.7
\end{aligned}
$$

## Continuous Random Variables

To compute the expected value of a *continuous* random variable $X$ given a joint density function $f(x,y)$, we must first find its marginal density function $f_X$. Once the marginal density function is known, we can then compute $\textrm{E}[X]$ using the formula

$$
\textrm{E}[X] = \int_{-\infty}^\infty x \cdot f_X(x)\,\textrm d x
$$

Let's see an example.

---

<a id="finding-the-expected-value-of-a-continuous-random-variable-from-a-joint-distribution"></a>
## Finding the Expected Value of a Continuous Random Variable From a Joint Distribution

**Example:** Let $X$ and $Y$ be two continuous random variables with the joint probability density function

${\frac{4}{x^{3}y^{3}},\begin{vmatrix}x \ge 1, y \ge 1, \\ 0,\end{vmatrix}otherwise$.

Find the expected value of $Y$.

**Explanation**

The marginal density function for $Y$ when

$$
y \geq 1
$$

is

$$
\begin{aligned}
f_{Y}(y) &= ∫_{1}^{∞}\frac{4}{x^{3}y^{3}}dx \\
&= \frac{4}{y^{3}}lim_(b → ∞)∫_{1}^{b}\frac{1}{x^{3}}dx \\
&= \frac{4}{y^{3}}lim_(b → ∞)[- \frac{1}{2x^{2}}]_{1}^{b} \\
&= \frac{4}{y^{3}}lim_(b → ∞)(\frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{2b^{2}}) \\
&= \frac{4}{y^{3}}(\frac{1}{2} - 0) \\
&= \frac{2}{y^{3}}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the full expression for $f_Y(y)$ is given by

$$
{\frac{2}{y^{3}},\begin{vmatrix}y \ge 1, \\ 0,\end{vmatrix}otherwise
$$

Finally, the expected value of $Y$ is

$$
\begin{aligned}
E[Y] &= ∫_{- ∞}^{∞}y \cdot f_{Y}(y)dy \\
&= ∫_{1}^{∞}y \cdot \frac{2}{y^{3}}dy \\
&= 2lim_(b → ∞)∫_{1}^{b}\frac{1}{y^{2}}dy \\
&= 2lim_(b → ∞)[- \frac{1}{y}]_{1}^{b} \\
&= 2lim_(b → ∞)(1 - \frac{1}{b}) \\
&= 2
\end{aligned}
$$
