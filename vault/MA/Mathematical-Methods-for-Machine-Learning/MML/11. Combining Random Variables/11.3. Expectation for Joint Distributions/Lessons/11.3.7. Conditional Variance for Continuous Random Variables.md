## Introduction

Suppose $X$ and $Y$ are continuous random variables, where the conditional expected value of $X$ given $Y=y$ is given by $\mu_{X \mid y}$. In other words,

$$
\textrm{E}\begin{bmatrix}X\, & \,Y=y\end{bmatrix} = \mu_{X \mid y}
$$

The **conditional variance of $X$ given $Y=y$** is defined as

$$
\textrm{Var}\begin{bmatrix}X\, & \,Y\end{bmatrix} = \textrm{E}\big\begin{bmatrix}(X - \mu_{X \mid y} \big)^2 \, & \, Y=y\end{bmatrix}
$$

As with the discrete case, this definition can be written as

$$
\begin{aligned}
Var\begin{bmatrix}X & Y = y\end{bmatrix} &= E\begin{bmatrix}X^{2} & Y = y\end{bmatrix} - μ_{X \mid y}^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

where

$$
\displaystyle \textrm{E}\big\begin{bmatrix}X^2 \, & \, Y=y \big\end{bmatrix} = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x^2 \, f_{X\mid Y}(x \, \mid \, y) \: \textrm{d}x
$$

and $f_{X\mid Y}(x \, \mid \, y)$ is the conditional probability density function of $X$ given $Y=y$. Note that these formulas are analogous to those for $\textrm{Var}[X]$ for continuous $X$.

When we select a particular value of $y$, the conditional variance of $X$ given $Y=y$ returns a real number. However, if $y$ is not specified, then

$$
\textrm {Var}\begin{bmatrix}X & Y = y\end{bmatrix}
$$

gives us a *function* of $y$.

We have an analogous definition for the **conditional variance of $Y$ given $X=x{:}$**

$$
\begin{aligned}
Var\begin{bmatrix}Y & X = x\end{bmatrix} &= E\begin{bmatrix}(Y - μ_{Y \mid x})^{2} & X = x\end{bmatrix} \\
&= E\begin{bmatrix}Y^{2} & X = x\end{bmatrix} - μ_{Y \mid x}^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

where

- $\mu_{Y \mid x} = \textrm{E}\big\begin{bmatrix}Y \, & \, X=x \big\end{bmatrix}$ is the conditional expected value of $Y$ given $X=x$,
- $\displaystyle \textrm{E}\big\begin{bmatrix}Y^2 \, & \, X=x \big\end{bmatrix} = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} y^2 \, f_{Y\mid X}(y \, \mid \, x) \: \textrm{d}y$, and
- $f_{Y\mid X}(y \, \mid \, x)$ is the conditional probability density function of $Y$ given $X=x$.

<a id="a-worked-example"></a>
## A Worked Example

Suppose that $X$ and $Y$ are continuous random variables. It is known that the conditional probability density function $f_{Y\mid X}(y \, \mid \, 1)$ is given by

$$
{4y^{3},\begin{vmatrix}0 \le y \le 1 \\ 0,\end{vmatrix}otherwise
$$

and that

$$
\mu_{Y \mid 1}=\dfrac{4}{5}
$$

What is the conditional variance

$$
\textrm{Var}\big\begin{bmatrix}Y \, & \, X=1\big\end{bmatrix}
$$

The conditional variance of $Y$ given $X=x$ is defined by

$$
\begin{aligned}
Var\begin{bmatrix}Y & X = x\end{bmatrix} &= E\begin{bmatrix}(Y - μ_{Y \mid x})^{2} & X = x\end{bmatrix} \\
&= E\begin{bmatrix}Y^{2} & X = x\end{bmatrix} - μ_{Y \mid x}^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

where

$$
\textrm{E}\big\begin{bmatrix}Y^2 \, & \, X=x \big\end{bmatrix} = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} y^2 \, f_{Y\mid X}(y \, \mid \, x) \: \textrm{d}y
$$

Therefore, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
E\begin{bmatrix}Y^{2} & X = 1\end{bmatrix} &= ∫_{- ∞}^{∞}y^{2}f_{Y\mid X}(y \mid 1)dy \\
&= ∫_{0}^{1}y^{2} \cdot 4y^{3}dx \\
&= ∫_{0}^{1}4y^{5}dy \\
&= [\frac{2y^{6}}{3}]_{0}^{1} \\
&= \frac{2}{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally,

$$
\begin{aligned}
Var\begin{bmatrix}Y & X = 1\end{bmatrix} &= E\begin{bmatrix}Y^{2} & X = 1\end{bmatrix} - μ_{Y \mid 1}^{2} \\
&= \frac{2}{3} - (\frac{4}{5})^{2} \\
&= \frac{2}{3} - \frac{16}{25} \\
&= \frac{2}{75}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Calculating a Conditional Variance Given a Conditional PDF

**Example:** ${3x^{2},\begin{vmatrix}0 \le x \le 1 \\ 0,\end{vmatrix}otherwise$

Let $X$ and $Y$ be continuous random variables.
Find the conditional variance $\textrm{Var}\big\begin{bmatrix}X \, & \, Y=-2\big\end{bmatrix}$ given that the conditional probability density function $f_{X\mid Y}(x \, \mid \, -2)$ is shown above and the corresponding expected value is $\mu_{X \mid -2}=\dfrac{3}{4}$.

**Explanation**

Recall that the conditional variance of $X$ given $Y=y$ is defined by

$$
\begin{aligned}
Var\begin{bmatrix}X & Y = y\end{bmatrix} &= E\begin{bmatrix}(X - μ_{X \mid y})^{2} & Y = y\end{bmatrix} \\
&= E\begin{bmatrix}X^{2} & Y = y\end{bmatrix} - μ_{X \mid y}^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

where

$$
\textrm{E}\big\begin{bmatrix}X^2 \, & \, Y=y \big\end{bmatrix} = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x^2 \, f_{X\mid Y}(x \, \mid \, y) \: \textrm{d}x
$$

Therefore, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
E\begin{bmatrix}X^{2} & Y =-2\end{bmatrix} &= ∫_{- ∞}^{∞}x^{2}f_{X\mid Y}(x \mid - 2)dx \\
&= ∫_{0}^{1}x^{2} \cdot 3x^{2}dx \\
&= ∫_{0}^{1}3x^{4}dx \\
&= [\frac{3x^{5}}{5}]_{0}^{1} \\
&= \frac{3}{5} - 0 \\
&= \frac{3}{5}
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally,

$$
\begin{aligned}
Var\begin{bmatrix}X & Y =-2\end{bmatrix} &= E\begin{bmatrix}X^{2} & Y =-2\end{bmatrix} - μ_{X \mid - 2}^{2} \\
&= \frac{3}{5} - (\frac{3}{4})^{2} \\
&= \frac{3}{5} - \frac{9}{16} \\
&= \frac{3}{80}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Calculating a Conditional Variance Given a Conditional PDF Using Improper Integrals

**Example:** ${\frac{3}{y^{4}},\begin{vmatrix}y \ge 1 \\ 0,\end{vmatrix}otherwise$

Let $X$ and $Y$ be continuous random variables.
Find the conditional variance $\textrm{Var}\big\begin{bmatrix}Y \, & \, X=3\big\end{bmatrix}$ given that the conditional probability density function $f_{Y\mid X}(y \, \mid \, 3)$ is shown above and the corresponding expected value is $\mu_{Y \mid 3}=\dfrac{3}{2}$.

**Explanation**

Recall that the conditional variance of $Y$ given $X=x$ is defined by

$$
\begin{aligned}
Var\begin{bmatrix}Y & X = x\end{bmatrix} &= E\begin{bmatrix}(Y - μ_{Y \mid x})^{2} & X = x\end{bmatrix} \\
&= E\begin{bmatrix}Y^{2} & X = x\end{bmatrix} - μ_{Y \mid x}^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

where

$$
\textrm{E}\big\begin{bmatrix}Y^2 \, & \, X=x \big\end{bmatrix} = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} y^2 \, f_{Y\mid X}(y \, \mid \, x) \: \textrm{d}y
$$

Therefore, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
E\begin{bmatrix}Y^{2} & X = 3\end{bmatrix} &= ∫_{- ∞}^{∞}y^{2}f_{Y\mid X}(y \mid 3)dy \\
&= ∫_{1}^{∞}y^{2} \cdot \frac{3}{y^{4}}dy \\
&= ∫_{1}^{∞}\frac{3}{y^{2}}dy \\
&= ∫_{1}^{∞}3y^{-2}dy \\
&= [3 \cdot (y^{-1})/((-1))]_{1}^{∞} \\
&= [- \frac{3}{y}]_{1}^{∞} \\
&= 0 - (-3) \\
&= 3
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally,

$$
\begin{aligned}
Var\begin{bmatrix}Y & X = 3\end{bmatrix} &= E\begin{bmatrix}Y^{2} & X = 3\end{bmatrix} - μ_{Y \mid 3}^{2} \\
&= 3 - (\frac{3}{2})^{2} \\
&= 3 - \frac{9}{4} \\
&= \frac{3}{4}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Calculating a Conditional Variance Given Joint and Marginal Density Functions

**Example:** $f(x, y) = {2y\ln (x),\begin{vmatrix}1 \le x \le e, 0 \le y \le 1, \\ 0,\end{vmatrix}otherwisef_{X}(x) = {\ln (x),\begin{vmatrix}1 \le x \le e, \\ 0,\end{vmatrix}otherwise$

Let $X$ and $Y$ be continuous random variables. Their joint probability density function and the marginal probability density function of $X$ are shown above. Find the conditional variance $\textrm{Var}\big\begin{bmatrix}Y \,\big & \, X=1 \big\end{bmatrix}$ given that the corresponding expected value is $\mu_{Y \mid 1}=\dfrac{2}{3}$.

**Explanation**

Recall that the conditional variance of $Y$ given $X=x$ is defined by

$$
\begin{aligned}
Var\begin{bmatrix}Y & X = x\end{bmatrix} &= E\begin{bmatrix}(Y - μ_{Y \mid x})^{2} & X = x\end{bmatrix} \\
&= E\begin{bmatrix}Y^{2} & X = x\end{bmatrix} - μ_{Y \mid x}^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

where

$$
\textrm{E}\big\begin{bmatrix}Y^2 \, & \, X=x \big\end{bmatrix} = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} y^2 \, f_{Y\mid X}(y \, \mid \, x) \: \textrm{d}y
$$

Note that

$$
\textrm E\big\begin{bmatrix}Y \, & \, X = x \big\end{bmatrix}
$$

is a function of $x$.

The conditional density function of $Y$ given $X=x$ can be computed as

$$
\begin{aligned}
f_{Y\mid X}(y \mid x) &= (f(x, y))/(f_{X}(x)) \\
&= (2y\ln (x))/(\ln (x)) \\
&= 2y
\end{aligned}
$$

where

$$
1 \leq x \leq e, \: 0 \leq y \leq 1
$$

Outside this domain, we have

$$
f_{Y \mid X} (y \, \mid \, x) = 0
$$

Notice that $f_{Y \mid X} (y \, \mid \, x)$ does not depend on $x$. Therefore, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
E\begin{bmatrix}Y^{2} & X = 1\end{bmatrix} &= ∫_{- ∞}^{∞}y^{2}f_{Y\mid X}(y \mid 1)dy \\
&= ∫_{0}^{1}y^{2} \cdot 2ydy \\
&= ∫_{0}^{1}2y^{3}dy \\
&= [\frac{y^{4}}{2}]_{0}^{1} \\
&= \frac{1}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally,

$$
\begin{aligned}
Var\begin{bmatrix}Y & X = 1\end{bmatrix} &= E\begin{bmatrix}Y^{2} & X = 1\end{bmatrix} - μ_{Y \mid 1}^{2} \\
&= \frac{1}{2} - (\frac{2}{3})^{2} \\
&= \frac{1}{2} - \frac{4}{9} \\
&= \frac{1}{18}
\end{aligned}
$$
