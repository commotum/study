## Introduction

A partition $P$ of a closed interval $[a,b]$ is a finite sequence of real numbers taken from $[a,b]$ such that

$$
a = x_0 < x_1< x_2 < \ldots < x_n = b\,
$$

For example, given the interval $[0,6]$, one possible partition is

$$
0 < 2 < 5 < 6
$$

We can express this partition using set notation as

$$
P=\{0,2,5,6\}
$$

Note that this partition breaks up the interval $[0,6]$ into the union of the following subintervals:

$$
[0,2] \cup [2,5] \cup [5,6]
$$

## Identifying Partitions of the Real Line

**Example:** Which of the following are partitions of the real interval $[1,9]$?

1. $\{1, 4, 5, 8\}$
2. $\{1, 3, 4, 9\}$
3. $1 < 2 < 9$

**Explanation**

A partition $P$ of a closed interval $[a,b]$ is a finite sequence of real numbers taken from $[a,b]$ such that

$$
a = x_0 < x_1< x_2 < \ldots < x_n = b\,
$$

We can express $P$ using set notation as

$$
P=\{x_0,x_1,\ldots,x_n\}
$$

With that in mind, let's consider each statement:

- Statement I is not a partition of $[1,9]$ since it does not include the right endpoint $b=9$.
- Statement II is a partition of $[1,9]$.
- Statement III is a partition of $[1,9]$.

Therefore, the correct answer is "II and III only."

## Sums Over Partitions

Suppose we have the partition

$$
P=\{0,2,5,6\}
$$

of the interval

$$
[a,b]=[0,6]
$$

Then, we have

$$
a=\underbrace{0}_{\large x_0} < \underbrace{2}_{\large x_1} < \underbrace{5}_{\large x_2} < \underbrace{6}_{\large x_3} =b
$$

This partition breaks up the interval $[0,6]$ into the union of the following subintervals:

$$
[0,2] \cup [2,5] \cup [5,6]
$$

The length of the $i$th subinterval, denoted $\Delta x_i$, is simply the difference of the endpoints:

- The length of the first interval $[0,2]$, denoted $\Delta x_1$, is
$Δx_{1}|= x_{1} - x_{0}; = 2 - 0; = 2$.
- The length of the second interval $[2,5]$, denoted $\Delta x_2$, is
$Δx_{2}|= x_{2} - x_{1}; = 5 - 2; = 3$.
- The length of the third interval $[5,6]$, denoted $\Delta x_3$, is
$Δx_{3}|= x_{3} - x_{2}; = 6 - 5; = 1$.

Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
∑_(i = 1)^(3)Δx_{i} &= Δx_{1} + Δx_{2} + Δx_{3} \\
&= 2 + 3 + 1 \\
&= 6
\end{aligned}
$$

which is just the length of the interval $[0, 6]$.

**Note:** In general, for any partition

$$
P=\{x_0,x_1,\ldots,x_m\}
$$

of an interval $[a,b]$, the sum

$$
\displaystyle \sum_{i=1}^m\Delta x_i
$$

is just the length of the interval $[a, b]$, because

$$
\begin{aligned}
∑_(i = 1)^(m)Δx_{i} &= Δx_{1} + Δx_{2} + Δx_{3} + ⋯ + Δx_{m} \\
&= (x_{1} - x_{0}) + (x_{2} - x_{1}) + (x_{3} - x_{2}) + ⋯ + (x_{m} - x_{m - 1}) \\
&= (x_{1} - x_{0}) + (x_{2} - x_{1}) + (x_{3} - x_{2}) + ⋯ + (x_{m} - x_{m - 1}) \\
&=-x_{0} + x_{m} \\
&= x_{m} - x_{0} \\
&= b - a
\end{aligned}
$$

## Computing Simple Sums Over Partitions of the Real Line

**Example:** Let $P= \left\{y_0, y_1, \ldots, y_n\right\}$ be a partition of the interval $[-3, 5]$. If $\Delta y_j = y_j - y_{j-1}$, calculate $\displaystyle \sum_{j=1}^n {3}\Delta y_j$.

**Explanation**

First, we take the factor $3$ outside the summation:

$$
∑_(j = 1)^(n)3Δy_{j} = 3∑_(j = 1)^(n)Δy_{j}
$$

Note that

$$
\displaystyle \sum_{j=1}^n \Delta y_j
$$

is just the length of the interval $[-3, 5]$, because

$$
\begin{aligned}
∑_(j = 1)^(n)Δy_{j} &= Δy_{1} + Δy_{2} + Δy_{3} + ⋯ + Δy_{n} \\
&= (y_{1} - y_{0}) + (y_{2} - y_{1}) + (y_{3} - y_{2}) + ⋯ + (y_{n} - y_{n - 1}) \\
&= (y_{1} - y_{0}) + (y_{2} - y_{1}) + (y_{3} - y_{2}) + ⋯ + (y_{n} - y_{n - 1}) \\
&=-y_{0} + y_{n} \\
&= y_{n} - y_{0} \\
&= 5 - (-3) \\
&= 5 + 3 \\
&= 8
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
{3}\sum_{j=1}^n \Delta y_j = 3 \cdot 8 = 24
$$

## Computing Sums Over Partitions of the Real Line

**Example:** Let $P = \left\{x_0, x_1, \ldots, x_m\right\}$ be a partition of the interval $[-3, 4]$. If $\Delta x_i = x_i - x_{i-1}$, calculate $\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^m (x_i + x_{i-1})\Delta x_i$.

**Explanation**

We can simplify the given summation as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
∑_(i = 1)^(m)(x_{i} + x_{i - 1})Δx_{i} &= (x_{1} + x_{0})Δx_{1} + (x_{2} + x_{1})Δx_{2} + ⋯ + (x_{m} + x_{m - 1})Δx_{m} \\
&= (x_{1} + x_{0})(x_{1} - x_{0}) + (x_{2} + x_{1})(x_{2} - x_{1}) + ⋯ + (x_{m} + x_{m - 1})(x_{m} - x_{m - 1}) \\
&= (x_{1}^{2} - x_{0}^{2}) + (x_{2}^{2} - x_{1}^{2}) + ⋯ + (x_{m}^{2} - x_{m - 1}^{2}) \\
&= (x_{1}^{2} - x_{0}^{2}) + (x_{2}^{2} - x_{1}^{2}) + ⋯ + (x_{m}^{2} - x_{m - 1}^{2}) \\
&=-x_{0}^{2} + x_{m}^{2} \\
&= x_{m}^{2} - x_{0}^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, since $x_0 = -3$ and $x_m = 4$ are the endpoints of our partition, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
∑_(i = 1)^(m)(x_{i} + x_{i - 1})Δx_{i} &= x_{m}^{2} - x_{0}^{2} \\
&= 4^{2} - (-3)^{2} \\
&= 7
\end{aligned}
$$
