## Introduction

Consider the matrix $\begin{bmatrix}2 & 6 \\ 0 & 3\end{bmatrix}$ Given that $\lambda=3$ is an eigenvalue of $A$, how can we find an eigenvector that corresponds to this eigenvalue?

Recall that a non-zero vector $\color{blue}\mathbf{v}$ is an eigenvector of $A$ with corresponding eigenvalue $\lambda$ if

$$
A{\color{blue}\mathbf{v}}={\color{red}\lambda}{\color{blue}\mathbf{v}}
$$

We can rewrite this equation, substituting

$$
{\color{red}{\lambda = 3}}
$$

as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
Av &= 3v \\
Av - 3v &= 0 \\
Av - 3Iv &= 0 \\
(A - 3I)v &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

So, to find an eigenvector of $A$ that corresponds to

$$
\lambda = {\color{red}3}
$$

we need to find a non-zero solution of the equation

$$
(A- {\color{red}3} I){\color{blue}\mathbf{v}} = \mathbf{0}
$$

In our case, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
A - 3I &= \begin{bmatrix}2 & 6 \\ 0 & 3\end{bmatrix} - 3\begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}-1 & 6 \\ 0 & 0\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the equation

$$
(A- {\color{red}3} I){\color{blue}\mathbf{v}} = \mathbf{0}
$$

corresponds to the following homogeneous system of linear equations:

$$
{-x_{1} + 6x_{2} = 0; 0 = 0
$$

Notice that $A-{\color{red}3}I$ is already in row echelon form, and it has one pivot column (the $1$st one). So, $x_2$ is a free variable. Moreover, from the first equation, we get $x_1=6x_2$, which implies that the general solution is

$$
\begin{bmatrix}6x_{2} \\ x_{2}\end{bmatrix}
$$

We only require one eigenvector corresponding to

$$
\lambda={\color{red}3}
$$

Setting $x_2=1$, we obtain the eigenvector

$$
\begin{bmatrix}6 \\ 1\end{bmatrix}
$$

**Note:** If we set $x_2=2$, we'll get a different eigenvector, namely
$\begin{bmatrix}12 \\ 2\end{bmatrix}$
Note that $\mathbf w$ is also an eigenvector that corresponds to $\lambda=3$. In fact, we can choose any real

$$
x_2 \neq 0
$$

to generate an eigenvector corresponding to

$$
\lambda = 3
$$

**Watch out!** In the case of $x_2=0$, we get the zero vector, but this is not an eigenvector. Remember that eigenvectors must be non-zero vectors.

<a id="calculating-the-components-of-an-eigenvector-corresponding-to-an-eigenvalue"></a>
## Calculating the Components of an Eigenvector Corresponding to an Eigenvalue

**Example:** If $\begin{bmatrix}a \\ b\end{bmatrix}$ is an eigenvector of $\begin{bmatrix}2 & 5 \\ -1 & -4\end{bmatrix}$
that corresponds to the eigenvalue $\,\lambda=1$, then what is the value of $\,\dfrac{b}{a}\,$?

**Explanation**

To compute the eigenvectors of a matrix, we need to find non-zero solutions of the matrix equation

$$
(A - \lambda I) \textbf{x}=\mathbf{0}
$$

In our case, we have

$$
\,\lambda =1
$$

So, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
A - λI &= \begin{bmatrix}2 & 5 \\ -1 & -4\end{bmatrix} - \begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}1 & 5 \\ -1 & -5\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

We have to solve the corresponding homogeneous system of linear equations:

$$
{x_{1} + 5x_{2} = 0; - x_{1} - 5x_{2} = 0
$$

So, we row-reduce the augmented matrix $M$ to row echelon form using Gaussian elimination, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
M &= \begin{bmatrix}1 & 5 & 0 \\ -1 & -5 & 0\end{bmatrix} \mid R_{2}:=R_{2} + R_{1} \\
&= ∼\begin{bmatrix}1 & 5 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

In the reduced matrix above, there is one pivot column (the $1$st one). So, $x_2$ is a free variable.

From the first equation, we obtain

$$
x_1+5x_2=0 \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad x_1=-5x_2
$$

Therefore, the general solution is

$$
\begin{bmatrix}-5x_{2} \\ x_{2}\end{bmatrix}
$$

Setting, for instance, $x_2=-1$, we get the following eigenvector:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}a \\ b\end{bmatrix}
$$

Finally,

$$
\dfrac{b}{a}=\dfrac{-1}{5}=-\dfrac{1}{5}
$$

<a id="calculating-an-eigenvector-corresponding-to-an-eigenvalue"></a>
## Calculating an Eigenvector Corresponding to an Eigenvalue

**Example:** Find an eigenvector of $\begin{bmatrix}7 & -4 \\ 5 & -2\end{bmatrix}$
that corresponds to the eigenvalue $\,\lambda=2$.

**Explanation**

To compute the eigenvectors of a matrix, we need to find non-zero solutions of the matrix equation

$$
(A - \lambda I) \textbf{x}=\mathbf{0}
$$

In our case, we have

$$
\,\lambda =2
$$

So, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
A - λI &= \begin{bmatrix}7 & -4 \\ 5 & -2\end{bmatrix} - 2\begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}5 & -4 \\ 5 & -4\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

We have to solve the corresponding homogeneous system of linear equations:

$$
{5x_{1} - 4x_{2} = 0; 5x_{1} - 4x_{2} = 0
$$

So, we row-reduce the augmented matrix $M$ to row echelon form using Gaussian elimination, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
M &= \begin{bmatrix}5 & -4 & 0 \\ 5 & -4 & 0\end{bmatrix} \mid R_{2}:=R_{2} + (-1)R_{1} \\
&= ∼\begin{bmatrix}5 & -4 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

In the reduced matrix above, there is one pivot column (the $1$st one). So, $x_2$ is a free variable.

From the first equation, we obtain

$$
5x_1-4x_2=0 \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad x_1=\dfrac{4}{5}x_2
$$

Therefore, the general solution is

$$
\begin{bmatrix}\frac{4}{5}x_{2} \\ x_{2}\end{bmatrix}
$$

Finally, setting $x_2=5$, we get the eigenvector

$$
\begin{bmatrix}4 \\ 5\end{bmatrix}
$$

<a id="connection-between-eigenspaces-and-null-spaces"></a>
## Connection Between Eigenspaces and Null Spaces

Given a matrix $A$ with an eigenvalue $\lambda$, the **eigenspace** $V_\lambda$ can be defined as the set of all possible eigenvectors corresponding to $\lambda$ plus the vector $\mathbf{0}{:}$
$eigenvectors$

Note that the eigenspace consists of *all* (not only non-zero) solutions of the equation

$$
(A-\lambda I)\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{0}
$$

So, the eigenspace can also be viewed as the *null space* of the matrix $A-\lambda I$, as follows:

$$
V_{\color{red}\lambda} = \textrm{Null}(A - {\color{red}\lambda} I)
$$

We also have the following theorem:

> *Eigenvectors that correspond to different eigenvalues are always linearly independent.*

For example, the matrix

$$
\begin{bmatrix}1 & -2 \\ 3 & -4\end{bmatrix}
$$

has eigenvalues

$$
\lambda_1=-2
$$

and

$$
\lambda_2=-1
$$

for which the corresponding eigenvectors are

$$
\begin{bmatrix}2 \\ 3\end{bmatrix}
$$

Here, $\mathbf{v}_1$ and $\mathbf{v}_2$ are linearly independent (they are not collinear).

**Watch out!** Eigenvectors that correspond to the *same* eigenvalue *can be* (and very often *will* be) linearly dependent. For example, the vectors

$$
\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 1\end{bmatrix}
$$

are collinear eigenvectors of the matrix $A$ above that correspond to

$$
\lambda_2=-1
$$

<a id="finding-two-linearly-independent-eigenvectors-of-a-2x2-matrix"></a>
## Finding Two Linearly Independent Eigenvectors of a 2x2 Matrix

**Example:** Given that $\lambda=1$ and $\lambda=-4$ are the eigenvalues of the matrix
$\begin{bmatrix}1 & -1 \\ 0 & -4\end{bmatrix}$
find two linearly independent eigenvectors of $A$.

**Explanation**

We can get two linearly independent eigenvectors by simply finding eigenvectors corresponding to the different eigenvalues.

To compute the eigenvectors of a matrix, we need to find non-zero solutions of the matrix equation

$$
(A - \lambda I) \textbf{x}=\mathbf{0}
$$

With that in mind, let's consider each of the eigenvalues separately.

- For $\lambda =1$, we have
$A - λI|= \begin{bmatrix}1 & -1 \\ 0 & -4\end{bmatrix} - 1\begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1\end{bmatrix}; = \begin{bmatrix}0 & -1 \\ 0 & -5\end{bmatrix}$.
We have to solve the corresponding system of linear equations:
${-x_{2} = 0; - 5x_{2} = 0$
So, we row-reduce the augmented matrix $M$ to row echelon form using Gaussian elimination, as follows:
$M|= \begin{bmatrix}0 & -1 & 0 \\ 0 & -5 & 0\end{bmatrix} \mid R_{2}:=R_{2} + (-5)R_{1}; ∼\begin{bmatrix}0 & -1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0\end{bmatrix}$
In the reduced matrix above, there is one pivot column (the $2$nd one). So, $x_1$ is a free variable.
From the first equation, we obtain $x_2=0$. Therefore, the general solution is
$\begin{bmatrix}x_{1} \\ 0\end{bmatrix}$
Setting, for instance, $x_1=1$, we get the eigenvector
$\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 0\end{bmatrix}$

- For $\lambda =-4$, we have
$A - λI|= \begin{bmatrix}1 & -1 \\ 0 & -4\end{bmatrix} - (-4)\begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1\end{bmatrix}; = \begin{bmatrix}5 & -1 \\ 0 & 0\end{bmatrix}$.
We have to solve the corresponding system of linear equations:
${5x_{1} - x_{2} = 0; 0 = 0$
So, we row-reduce the augmented matrix $M$ to row echelon form using Gaussian elimination, as follows:
$M|= \begin{bmatrix}5 & -1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0\end{bmatrix}$
In the matrix above, there is one pivot column (the $1$st one). So, $x_2$ is a free variable.
From the first equation, we obtain $x_1=\dfrac{1}{5}x_2$. Therefore, the general solution is
$\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{5}x_{2} \\ x_{2}\end{bmatrix}$
Setting, for instance, $x_2=5$, we get the eigenvector
$\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 5\end{bmatrix}$

Therefore, two linearly independent eigenvectors of the matrix $A$ are
$\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 0\end{bmatrix}$
