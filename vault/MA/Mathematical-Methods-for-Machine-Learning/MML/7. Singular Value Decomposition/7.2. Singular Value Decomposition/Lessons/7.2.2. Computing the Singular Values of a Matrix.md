## Introduction

Recall that the singular values of a real matrix $A$ are the square roots of the eigenvalues of the matrix $A^T \! A$.

Also, since $A^T \! A$ is a positive semi-definite matrix, the eigenvalues of $A^T \! A$ must be always non-negative real numbers.

Let's compute the singular values of the matrix $A$, given by

$$
\begin{bmatrix}0 & 2 \\ -2 & 0 \\ 2 & 1\end{bmatrix}
$$

Now, since

- $A$ is a $3 \times 2$ matrix, and
- $A^T$ is a $2 \times 3$ matrix,

then $A^T \! A$ must be a $2 \times 2$ matrix:

$$
\underset{\bbox[Gainsboro, 2pt]{\large 2} \times {\large 3}}{A^T} \times \underset{{\large 3} \times \bbox[Gainsboro, 2pt]{\large 2}}{A} = \underset{\bbox[Gainsboro, 2pt]{\large 2} \times \bbox[Gainsboro, 2pt]{\large 2}}{A^T \! A}
$$

Let's now compute our singular values. First, we calculate $A^T \! A$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
A^{T}A &= \begin{bmatrix}0 & 2 \\ -2 & 0 \\ 2 & 1\end{bmatrix}^{T}\begin{bmatrix}0 & 2 \\ -2 & 0 \\ 2 & 1\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}0 & -2 & 2 \\ 2 & 0 & 1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}0 & 2 \\ -2 & 0 \\ 2 & 1\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}8 & 2 \\ 2 & 5\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we compute the eigenvalues of $A^T \! A$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mid A^{T}A - λI \mid &= 0 \\
\begin{vmatrix}8 - λ & 2 \\ 2 & 5 - λ\end{vmatrix} &= 0 \\
(8 - λ)(5 - λ) - 4 &= 0 \\
λ^{2} - 13λ + 36 &= 0 \\
(λ - 9)(λ - 4) &= 0 \\
λ &= 9, 4
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the singular values of $A$ are as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
σ_{1} &= \sqrt{λ_{1}} = \sqrt{9} = 3 \\
σ_{2} &= \sqrt{λ_{2}} = \sqrt{4} = 2
\end{aligned}
$$

Note the following:

- When listing the singular values of a matrix, we usually write them in descending order.
- If $A$ is a $m\times n$ matrix, then $A^T A$ is a $n\times n$ positive semi-definite matrix. This means that $A$ has *at most* $n$ distinct singular values.

## Identifying True Statements Regarding the Singular Values of a Matrix

**Example:** Which of the following statements are true?

1. Any $1\times4$ matrix $A$ has at most $1$ singular value.
2. Any $4\times2$ matrix $A$ has at most $2$ distinct singular values.
3. The matrix $\begin{bmatrix}-1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1\end{bmatrix}$ has one negative singular value.

**Explanation**

The singular values of the matrix $A$ are the square roots of the eigenvalues of the matrix $A^T \! A$.

With that in mind, let's examine our statements.

- Statement I is false. Since $A$ is a $1 \times 4$ matrix and $A^T$ is a $4\times 1$ matrix, $A^T \! A$ must be a $4 \times 4$ matrix:
$\underset{\bbox[Gainsboro, 2pt]{\large 4} \times {\large 1}}{A^T} \times \underset{{\large 1} \times \bbox[Gainsboro, 2pt]{\large 4}}{A} = \underset{\bbox[Gainsboro, 2pt]{\large 4} \times \bbox[Gainsboro, 2pt]{\large 4}}{A^T \! A}$
Hence, $A$ has at most $4$ distinct singular values.
- Statement II is true. Since $A$ is a $4 \times 2$ matrix and $A^T$ is a $2\times 4$ matrix, $A^T \! A$ must be a $2 \times 2$ matrix:
$\underset{\bbox[Gainsboro, 2pt]{\large 2} \times {\large 4}}{A^T} \times \underset{{\large 4} \times \bbox[Gainsboro, 2pt]{\large 2}}{A} = \underset{\bbox[Gainsboro, 2pt]{\large 2} \times \bbox[Gainsboro, 2pt]{\large 2}}{A^T \! A}$
Hence, $A$ has at most $2$ distinct singular values.
- Statement III is false. All singular values of any matrix are non-negative. So, $A$ cannot have a negative singular value.

Therefore, the correct answer is "II only."

## Finding the Singular Values of a Square Matrix

**Example:** What are the singular values of the matrix $\begin{bmatrix}1 & 2 \\ 2 & 1\end{bmatrix}$

**Explanation**

The singular values of the matrix $A$ are the square roots of the eigenvalues of the matrix $A^T \! A$.

First, we calculate $A^T \! A$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
A^{T}A &= \begin{bmatrix}1 & 2 \\ 2 & 1\end{bmatrix}^{T}\begin{bmatrix}1 & 2 \\ 2 & 1\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}1 & 2 \\ 2 & 1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1 & 2 \\ 2 & 1\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}5 & 4 \\ 4 & 5\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we solve the characteristic equation of $A^T \! A$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mid A^{T}A - λI \mid &= 0 \\
\begin{vmatrix}5 - λ & 4 \\ 4 & 5 - λ\end{vmatrix} &= 0 \\
(5 - λ)^{2} - 16 &= 0 \\
λ^{2} - 10λ + 9 &= 0 \\
(λ - 1)(λ - 9) &= 0 \\
λ &= 9, 1
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the singular values of $A$ are as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
σ_{1} &= \sqrt{λ_{1}} = \sqrt{9} = 3 \\
σ_{2} &= \sqrt{λ_{2}} = \sqrt{1} = 1
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding the Singular Values of a Rectangular Matrix

**Example:** What are the singular values of the matrix $\begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 0\end{bmatrix}$

**Explanation**

The singular values of the matrix $A$ are the square roots of the eigenvalues of the matrix $A^T \! A$.

First, we calculate $A^T \! A$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
A^{T}A &= \begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 0\end{bmatrix}^{T}\begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 0\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 0\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 & 1\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we solve the characteristic equation of $A^T \! A$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mid A^{T}A - λI \mid &= 0 \\
\begin{vmatrix}1 - λ & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 - λ & 0 \\ 1 & 0 & 1 - λ\end{vmatrix} &= 0 \\
(1 - λ)\begin{vmatrix}1 - λ & 0 \\ 0 & 1 - λ\end{vmatrix}+\begin{vmatrix}0 & 1 - λ \\ 1 & 0\end{vmatrix} &= 0 \\
(1 - λ)(1 - λ)^{2} - (1 - λ) &= 0 \\
(1 - λ)((1 - λ)^{2} - 1) &= 0 \\
(1 - λ)(λ^{2} - 2λ) &= 0 \\
- λ(λ - 1)(λ - 2) &= 0 \\
λ &= 2, 1, 0
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the singular values of $A$ are as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
σ_{1} &= \sqrt{λ_{1}} = \sqrt{2} \\
σ_{2} &= \sqrt{λ_{2}} = \sqrt{1} = 1 \\
σ_{3} &= \sqrt{λ_{3}} = \sqrt{0} = 0
\end{aligned}
$$
