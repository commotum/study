## Introduction

If $\big\{\mathbf{a}_1, \mathbf{a}_2, \ldots, \mathbf{a}_n \big\}$ is a set of orthogonal vectors, then the **orthogonal projection of a vector $\mathbf{x}$ onto the subspace $S = \textrm{Span}\{\mathbf{a}_1, \mathbf{a}_2, \ldots, \mathbf{a}_n \}$** is given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
proj_{S}x &= proj_{a_{1}}x + proj_{a_{2}}x + ⋯ + proj_{a_{n}}x \\
&= \frac{x \cdot a_{1}}{a_{1} \cdot a_{1}}a_{1} + \frac{x \cdot a_{2}}{a_{2} \cdot a_{2}}a_{2} + ⋯ + \frac{x \cdot a_{n}}{a_{n} \cdot a_{n}}a_{n}
\end{aligned}
$$

In other words, the orthogonal projection of $\mathbf{x}$ onto

$$
S = \textrm{Span}\{\mathbf{a}_1, \mathbf{a}_2, \ldots, \mathbf{a}_n \}
$$

is the sum of the orthogonal projections of $\mathbf{x}$ onto each one-dimensional subspace of $\textrm{Span}\big\{\mathbf{a}_1, \mathbf{a}_2, \ldots, \mathbf{a}_n \big\}$.

**Watch out!** The formula works *only* when the set of vectors $\{\mathbf{a}_1, \mathbf{a}_2, \ldots, \mathbf{a}_n \}$ is orthogonal!

In case of a $2$-dimensional subspace

$$
S=\textrm{Span} \big\{\mathbf{a}_1,\mathbf{a}_2 \big\}
$$

we can visualize the orthogonal projection geometrically, as follows:

$$
{\color{blue}\textrm{proj}_{S} \: \mathbf{x}} = {\color{red}\textrm{proj}_{\mathbf{a}_1}\mathbf{x}} + {\color{red}\textrm{proj}_{\mathbf{a}_2}\mathbf{x}}
$$

![](<../Source/6.3.3. Projecting Vectors Onto Subspaces in Euclidean Spaces (Orthogonal Bases)/Images/d57efb54e33ccf455e8ca9f5ddaf0eb7.png>)

**Note:** A proof of the formula will be provided at the end of the lesson. But first, let's get some practice using the formula.

## Finding the Orthogonal Projection of a Vector Onto a Subspace Spanned by Orthogonal Vectors

**Example:** Consider the vector $\mathbf{x}$ and the subspace $S=\textrm{Span}\{\mathbf{a}_1,\mathbf{a}_2 \}$, where
$\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 1 \\ 1\end{bmatrix}$

Find the orthogonal projection of $\mathbf{x}$ onto the subspace $S$ given that the set $\{\mathbf{a}_1,\mathbf{a}_2\}$ is orthogonal.

**Explanation**

Since the set $\{\mathbf{a}_1,\mathbf{a}_2\}$ is orthogonal, the orthogonal projection of $\mathbf{x}$ onto the subspace $S$ is given by

$$
\textrm{proj}_{S}\,\mathbf{x} = \textrm{proj}_{\mathbf{a}_1}\mathbf{x} + \textrm{proj}_{\mathbf{a}_2}\mathbf{x}
$$

So, we first find the orthogonal projections of $\mathbf{x}$ onto $\textrm{Span}\{\mathbf{a}_1\}$ and $\textrm{Span}\{\mathbf{a}_2\}$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
proj_{a_{1}}x &= \frac{x \cdot a_{1}}{a_{1} \cdot a_{1}}a_{1} = (2 \cdot 1 + 3 \cdot 1 + (-2) \cdot 1)/(1^{2} + 1^{2} + 1^{2})a_{1} = 1\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 1 \\ 1\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 1 \\ 1\end{bmatrix} \\
proj_{a_{2}}x &= \frac{x \cdot a_{2}}{a_{2} \cdot a_{2}}a_{2} = (2 \cdot (-2) + 3 \cdot 1 + (-2) \cdot 1)/((-2)^{2} + 1^{2} + 1^{2})a_{2} =-\frac{1}{2}\begin{bmatrix}-2 \\ 1 \\ 1\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, we have that

$$
\begin{aligned}
proj_{S}x &= proj_{a_{1}}x + proj_{a_{2}}x \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 1 \\ 1\end{bmatrix} + (-\frac{1}{2})\begin{bmatrix}-2 \\ 1 \\ 1\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}2 \\ \frac{1}{2} \\ \frac{1}{2}\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding the Orthogonal Projection of a Vector Onto a Plane

**Example:** Consider the vector $\mathbf{x}$ and the plane $\mathbf{r}=s\mathbf{v}+t\mathbf{w}$, where $s,t\in\mathbb R$, and
$\begin{bmatrix}2 \\ 3 \\ -1\end{bmatrix}$
Find the orthogonal projection of $\mathbf{x}$ onto the plane given that $\mathbf{v} \perp \mathbf{w}$.

**Explanation**

Since our plane passes through the origin, it defines the subspace

$$
S=\text{Span}\{\mathbf{v}, \mathbf{w}\}
$$

in $\mathbb{R}^3$.
And, since the set $\{\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w}\}$ is orthogonal, the orthogonal projection of $\mathbf{x}$ onto the subspace $S$ is given by

$$
\textrm{proj}_{S}\,\mathbf{x} = \textrm{proj}_{\mathbf{v}}\,\mathbf{x} + \textrm{proj}_{\mathbf{w}}\,\mathbf{x}
$$

So, we first find the orthogonal projections of $\mathbf{x}$ onto $\textrm{Span}\{\mathbf{v}\}$ and $\textrm{Span}\{\mathbf{w}\}$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
proj_{v}x &= \frac{x \cdot v}{v \cdot v}v = (13 \cdot 2 + (-2) \cdot 3 + (-1) \cdot (-1))/(2^{2} + 3^{2} + (-1)^{2})v = \frac{3}{2}\begin{bmatrix}2 \\ 3 \\ -1\end{bmatrix} \\
proj_{w}x &= \frac{x \cdot w}{w \cdot w}w = (13 \cdot 5 + (-2) \cdot (-3) + (-1) \cdot 1)/(5^{2} + (-3)^{2} + 1^{2})w = 2\begin{bmatrix}5 \\ -3 \\ 1\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, we have that

$$
\begin{aligned}
proj_{S}x &= proj_{v}x + proj_{w}x \\
&= \frac{3}{2}\begin{bmatrix}2 \\ 3 \\ -1\end{bmatrix} + 2\begin{bmatrix}5 \\ -3 \\ 1\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}3 \\ \frac{9}{2} \\ -\frac{3}{2}\end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix}10 \\ -6 \\ 2\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}13 \\ -\frac{3}{2} \\ \frac{1}{2}\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

## The Distance Between a Vector and a Subspace

Let $n,m$ be positive integers such that

$$
n\leq m
$$

Given an orthogonal set $\big\{\mathbf{a}_1, \mathbf{a}_2, \ldots, \mathbf{a}_n \big\} \subset \Bbb R^m$ and a vector $\mathbf x \in \Bbb R^m$, we define the **distance between the vector $\mathbf{x}$ and the vector space $S=\textrm{Span}\{\mathbf{a}_1,\mathbf{a}_2, \ldots, \mathbf{a}_n \}$** as
$\Vert{\color{purple} \mathbf{x} - \text{proj}_{S}\,\mathbf{x}}\Vert$.

We can also define the **angle between the vector $\mathbf{x}$ and the subspace $S$** to be the acute angle $\theta$ between the vector $\mathbf{x}$ and its orthogonal projection $\text{proj}_{S}\,\mathbf{x}$, as shown in the diagram below.

![](<../Source/6.3.3. Projecting Vectors Onto Subspaces in Euclidean Spaces (Orthogonal Bases)/Images/416ecc062316779973c0a5c1a6921bd6.png>)

Finally, notice that we can represent the vector $\mathbf{x}$ as a sum of two orthogonal vectors:

$$
\mathbf{x} = \underbrace{\textrm{proj}_{S}\mathbf{x}}_{\large \in S} + \underbrace{(\mathbf{x}-\textrm{proj}_{S}\mathbf{x})}_{\large \in S^\perp}
$$

**Note:** The vector $\textbf{x}-\textrm{proj}_{S}\,\mathbf{x}$, whose norm $\| \textbf{x}-\textrm{proj}_{S}\,\mathbf{x} \|$ represents the distance from $\mathbf{x}$ to $S$, is sometimes called the **vector rejection of $\mathbf{x}$ from $S$.**

## Calculating the Distance Between a Vector and a Subspace Spanned by Orthogonal Vectors

**Example:** Consider the vector $\mathbf{x}$ and the subspace $S=\textrm{Span}\{\mathbf{a}_1,\mathbf{a}_2 \}$, where
$\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 2 \\ 1\end{bmatrix}$

Find the distance between $\mathbf{x}$ and the subspace $S$ given that the set $\{\mathbf{a}_1,\mathbf{a}_2\}$ is orthogonal.

**Explanation**

Since the set $\{\mathbf{a}_1,\mathbf{a}_2\}$ is orthogonal, the orthogonal projection of $\mathbf{x}$ onto the subspace $S$ is given by

$$
\textrm{proj}_{S}\,\mathbf{x} = \textrm{proj}_{\mathbf{a}_1}\mathbf{x} + \textrm{proj}_{\mathbf{a}_2}\mathbf{x}
$$

So, we first find the orthogonal projections of $\mathbf{x}$ onto $\textrm{Span}\{\mathbf{a}_1\}$ and $\textrm{Span}\{\mathbf{a}_2\}$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
proj_{a_{1}}x &= \frac{x \cdot a_{1}}{a_{1} \cdot a_{1}}a_{1} = \frac{3 \cdot 1 + 2 \cdot 2 + 1 \cdot 1}{1^{2} + 2^{2} + 1^{2}}a_{1} = \frac{4}{3}\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 2 \\ 1\end{bmatrix} \\
proj_{a_{2}}x &= \frac{x \cdot a_{2}}{a_{2} \cdot a_{2}}a_{2} = (3 \cdot 1 + 2 \cdot (-1) + 1 \cdot 1)/(1^{2} + (-1)^{2} + 1^{2})a_{2} = \frac{2}{3}\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ -1 \\ 1\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, we have that

$$
\begin{aligned}
proj_{S}x &= proj_{a_{1}}x + proj_{a_{2}}x \\
&= \frac{4}{3}\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 2 \\ 1\end{bmatrix} + \frac{2}{3}\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ -1 \\ 1\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \frac{2}{3}(2\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 2 \\ 1\end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix}1 \\ -1 \\ 1\end{bmatrix}) \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}2 \\ 2 \\ 2\end{bmatrix} = x^(^)
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, the distance between $\mathbf{x}$ and the subspace $S$ is given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
d(x, x^(^)) &= ‖x - x^(^)‖ \\
&= \sqrt{(x_{1} - x^(^)_{1})^{2} + (x_{2} - x^(^)_{2})^{2} + (x_{3} - x^(^)_{3})^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{(3 - 2)^{2} + (2 - 2)^{2} + (1 - 2)^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Calculating the Acute Angle Between a Vector and a Subspace Spanned by Orthogonal Vectors

**Example:** Consider the vector $\mathbf{x}$ and the subspace $S=\textrm{Span}\{\mathbf v,\mathbf w\}$ where
$\begin{bmatrix}0 \\ 1 \\ 1\end{bmatrix}$
Find the acute angle between $\mathbf{x}$ and the subspace $S$ given that the set $\{\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w}\}$ is orthogonal.

**Explanation**

Since the set $\{\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w}\}$ is orthogonal, the orthogonal projection of $\mathbf{x}$ onto the subspace $S$ is given by

$$
\textrm{proj}_{S}\,\mathbf{x} = \textrm{proj}_{\mathbf{v}}\mathbf{x} + \textrm{proj}_{\mathbf{w}}\mathbf{x}
$$

So, we first find the orthogonal projections of $\mathbf{x}$ onto $\textrm{Span}\{\mathbf{v}\}$ and $\textrm{Span}\{\mathbf{w}\}$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
proj_{v}x &= \frac{x \cdot v}{v \cdot v}v = (\sqrt{10} \cdot 0 + 3 \cdot 1 + 1 \cdot 1)/(0^{2} + (-1)^{2} + 1^{2})v = 2\begin{bmatrix}0 \\ 1 \\ 1\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}0 \\ 2 \\ 2\end{bmatrix} \\
proj_{w}x &= \frac{x \cdot w}{w \cdot w}w = (\sqrt{10} \cdot 0 + 3 \cdot (-1) + 1 \cdot 1)/(0^{2} + (-1)^{2} + (-1)^{2})w = (-1)\begin{bmatrix}0 \\ -1 \\ 1\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}0 \\ 1 \\ -1\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, we have that

$$
\begin{aligned}
proj_{S}x &= proj_{v}x + proj_{w}x \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}0 \\ 2 \\ 2\end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix}0 \\ 1 \\ -1\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}0 \\ 3 \\ 1\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

The acute angle between $\mathbf{x}$ and $S$ is the acute angle between $\mathbf{x}$ and $\textrm{proj}_S \mathbf{x}$ or any other non-zero vector that is parallel to $\textrm{proj}_S \mathbf{x}$. So, computing the cosine using the dot product, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
\cos θ &= \frac{x \cdot proj_{S}x}{‖x‖‖proj_{S}x‖} \\
&= (\sqrt{10} \cdot 0 + 3 \cdot 3 + 1 \cdot 1)/(\sqrt{(\sqrt{10})^{2} + 3^{2} + 1^{2}}\sqrt{0^{2} + 3^{2} + 1^{2}}) \\
&= \frac{10}{\sqrt{20}\sqrt{10}} \\
&= \frac{10}{10\sqrt{2}} \\
&= \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\theta = \arccos\left(\dfrac{{\sqrt2}}{2}\right) = \dfrac{\pi}{4}
$$

which is already an acute angle.

<a id="justification-of-the-formula"></a>
## Justification of the Formula

Let's now prove the formula for the orthogonal projection of a vector $\mathbf{x}$ onto the subspaces $\textrm{Span}\{\mathbf{a}_1 \}$ and $\textrm{Span}\{\mathbf{a}_2 \}$ given that $\mathbf{a}_1\perp \mathbf{a}_2$.

We start by writing $\mathbf{x}$ as a sum of two vectors

$$
\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{x}_S + \mathbf{x}_{S^\perp}
$$

where

$$
\mathbf{x}_S=\text{proj}_S\,\mathbf{x}
$$

is in $\textrm{Span}\{\mathbf{a}_1,\mathbf{a}_2\}$, and $\mathbf{x}_{S^\perp}$ is in the orthogonal complement of $\textrm{Span}\{\mathbf{a}_1,\mathbf{a}_2\}$. That is,

$$
\mathbf{x}_S=k_1\mathbf{a}_1+k_2\mathbf{a}_2\in S\,
$$

and $\mathbf{x}_{S^\perp} \in S^\perp$.

From the equation above, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_{S^{⊥}} &= x - x_{S} \\
&= x - (k_{1}a_{1} + k_{2}a_{2}) \\
&= x - k_{1}a_{1} - k_{2}a_{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, using the fact that $\mathbf{x}_{S^\perp}$ is orthogonal to $S$, and the fact that

$$
\mathbf{a}_1\cdot\mathbf{a}_2=0
$$

we obtain

$$
{x_{S^{⊥}} \cdot a_{1} = 0; x_{S^{⊥}} \cdot a_{2} = 0 \mid ⟹{(x - k_{1}a_{1} - k_{2}a_{2}) \cdot a_{1} = 0; (x - k_{1}a_{1} - k_{2}a_{2}) \cdot a_{2} = 0; ⟹{x \cdot a_{1} - k_{1}(a_{1} \cdot a_{1}) - k_{2}(a_{2} \cdot a_{1}) = 0; x \cdot a_{2} - k_{1}(a_{1} \cdot a_{2}) - k_{2}(a_{2} \cdot a_{2}) = 0; ⟹{k_{1}(a_{1} \cdot a_{1}) = x \cdot a_{1}; k_{2}(a_{2} \cdot a_{2}) = x \cdot a_{2}; ⟹{k_{1} = \frac{x \cdot a_{1}}{a_{1} \cdot a_{1}}; k_{2} = \frac{x \cdot a_{2}}{a_{2} \cdot a_{2}}
$$

As a result, we obtain our formula

$$
\begin{aligned}
proj_{S}x &= k_{1}a_{1} + k_{2}a_{2} \\
&= \frac{x \cdot a_{1}}{a_{1} \cdot a_{1}}a_{1} + \frac{x \cdot a_{2}}{a_{2} \cdot a_{2}}a_{2} \\
&= proj_{a_{1}}x + proj_{a_{2}}x
\end{aligned}
$$
