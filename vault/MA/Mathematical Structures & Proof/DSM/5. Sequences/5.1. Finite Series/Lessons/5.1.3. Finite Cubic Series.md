## Introduction

If we wish to sum the cubes of the first $N$ integers, an easy way is to use the following formula:

$$
\begin{aligned}
∑_(n = 1)^(N)n^{3} &= 1^{3} + 2^{3} + 3^{3} + ⋯ + (N - 1)^{3} + N^{3} \\
&= \frac{1}{4}N^{2}(N + 1)^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Since we also know that

$$
\begin{aligned}
∑_(n = 1)^(N)n^{2} &= 1^{2} + 2^{2} + 3^{2} + ⋯ + (N - 1)^{2} + N^{2} \\
&= \frac{1}{6}N(N + 1)(2N + 1)
\end{aligned}
$$

and

$$
\begin{aligned}
∑_(n = 1)^(N)n &= 1 + 2 + 3 + ⋯ + (N - 1) + N \\
&= \frac{1}{2}N(N + 1)
\end{aligned}
$$

we can use these formulae to evaluate any series of the form

$$
\displaystyle{\sum_{n=1}^N (an^3+bn^2+cn+d)}
$$

Finally, it's worth noting that

$$
\sum_{n=1}^N n^3 = \frac{1}{4}N^2(N+1)^2 = \left(\frac{1}{2}N(N+1)\right)^2 = \left(\sum_{n=1}^N n\right)^2
$$

In other words, the sum of the first $N$ cube numbers is equal to the square of the sum of the first $N$ natural numbers.

## Calculating the Sum of the First N Cube Numbers

**Example:** Calculate $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{100} n^3$.

**Explanation**

Using the formula

$$
\sum_{n=1}^N n^3 = \frac{1}{4}N^2(N+1)^2
$$

with $N=100$, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
∑_(n = 1)^(100)n^{3} &= \frac{1}{4} \cdot 100^{2}(100 + 1)^{2} \\
&= \frac{1}{4} \cdot 100^{2} \cdot 101^{2} \\
&= \frac{1}{4} \cdot 10000 \cdot 10201 \\
&= 25502500
\end{aligned}
$$

## Calculating the Sum of a Finite Cubic Series with a Constant Factor

**Example:** Calculate
$\displaystyle \sum_{n=1}^{100} 4n^3$.

**Explanation**

First, we take the factor $4$ outside the summation:

$$
\sum_{n=1}^{100} 4n^3 = 4\sum_{n=1}^{100} n^3
$$

Now, using the formula

$$
\sum_{n=1}^N n^3 = \frac{1}{4}N^2(N+1)^2
$$

with $N=100$, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
4∑_(n = 1)^(100)n^{3} &= 4(\frac{1}{4} \cdot 100^{2}(100 + 1)^{2}) \\
&= 4(\frac{1}{4} \cdot 100^{2} \cdot 101^{2}) \\
&= 4(\frac{1}{4} \cdot 10000 \cdot 10201) \\
&= 4 \cdot 25502500 \\
&= 102010000
\end{aligned}
$$

## Calculating the Sum of a Cubic Series with a Quadratic, Linear, or Constant Term

**Example:** Calculate
$\displaystyle \sum_{n=1}^{50} (3n^3+2n)$.

**Explanation**

Notice that we can write the given summation as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
∑_(n = 1)^(50)(3n^{3} + 2n) &= ∑_(n = 1)^(50)3n^{3} + ∑_(n = 1)^(50)2n \\
&= 3∑_(n = 1)^(50)n^{3} + 2∑_(n = 1)^(50)n
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, let's evaluate the two series using our formulas.

- For the first series, we use the formula
$\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^N n^3 = \dfrac1 4 N^2 (N+1)^2$
with $N=50$, and we get
$3∑_(n = 1)^(50)n^{3}|= 3(\frac{1}{4} \cdot 50^{2}(50 + 1)^{2}); = 3(\frac{1}{4} \cdot 50^{2} \cdot 51^{2}); = 3 \cdot 1625625; = 4876875$.
- For the second series, we use the formula
$\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^N n = \dfrac1 2 N (N+1)$
with $N=50$, and we get
$2∑_(n = 1)^(50)n|= 2(\frac{1}{2} \cdot 50(50 + 1)); = 2 \cdot 1275; = 2550$.

Finally, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
∑_(n = 1)^(50)(3n^{3} + 2n) &= 4876875 + 2550 \\
&= 4879425
\end{aligned}
$$
