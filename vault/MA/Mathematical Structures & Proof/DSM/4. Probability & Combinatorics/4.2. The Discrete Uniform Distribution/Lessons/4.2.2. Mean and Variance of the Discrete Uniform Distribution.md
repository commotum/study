## Introduction

We know that if a random variable $X$ follows a *discrete* uniform distribution, then it has the following probability mass function:

$$
{\frac{1}{\begin{vmatrix}S &},\end{vmatrix}x ∈ S; 0, \mid otherwise
$$

Let's now suppose that

$$
X\sim U\{a, a+1, a+2, \ldots, b\}
$$

where $a$ and $b$ are integers. In other words, we're concerned with the special case where the support $S$ of $X$ contains every integer between $a$ and $b$ inclusive.

In this case, it can be shown that the mean value of $X$ is given by

$$
\textrm{E}[X] = \dfrac{a+b}{2}
$$

It can also be shown that

$$
\textrm{Var}[X] = \dfrac{\mid S \mid ^2-1}{12}
$$

We'll prove these results at the end of the lesson.

## Computing Mean Values in Context

**Example:** A fair die has $12$ sides labeled with numbers $1$ through $12$. What is the expected value of a random roll of this die?

**Explanation**

Let $X$ represent the number the die lands on. From the problem statement, we know that $X \sim U \{1, 2, \ldots, 12\}$, and we wish to compute $\textrm E[X]$.

In general, if $X \sim U \{a, a + 1, \ldots, b\}$, then $X$ has the following expected value:

$$
\textrm E[X] = \dfrac{a+b}{2}
$$

So, for our random variable $X \sim U \{1, 2, \ldots, 12\}$, we have the following expected value:

$$
\textrm E[X] = \dfrac{1+12}{2} = 6.5
$$

Therefore, the expected value is $6.5$.

## Computing Variances in Context

**Example:** A fair die has sides labeled with numbers $4$ through $15$. What is the variance of the numbers obtained when rolling this die?

**Explanation**

Let $X$ represent the number the die lands on. From the problem statement, we know that $X \sim U \{4, 5, \ldots, 15 \}$, and we wish to compute $\textrm{Var}[X]$.

In general, if $X \sim U \{a, a + 1, \ldots, b\}$, then the variance of $X$ is

$$
\textrm{Var}[X] = \dfrac{\mid S \mid ^2-1}{12}
$$

where

$$
S = \{a, a+1, \ldots, b\}
$$

Now, we know that

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mid S \mid &= b - a + 1 \\
&= 15 - 4 + 1 \\
&= 12
\end{aligned}
$$

So, for our random variable $X \sim U \{4, 5, \ldots, 15 \}$, we have the following variance:

$$
\textrm{Var}[X] = \dfrac{12^2 - 1}{12} = \dfrac{143}{12}
$$

Therefore, the variance is

$$
\dfrac{143}{12}
$$

## Proving the Formula for the Mean

Here, we will show that for $X\sim \{a, a+1, a+2, \ldots, b\}$, we have

$$
\textrm{E}[X] = \dfrac{a+b}{2}
$$

First, recall that the probability mass function of $X$ is given by

$$
{\frac{1}{\begin{vmatrix}S &},\end{vmatrix}x ∈ S; 0, \mid otherwise
$$

By the definition of expected value for discrete random variables, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
E[X] &= ∑_(x ∈ S)xf(x) \\
&= ∑_(x ∈ S)x \cdot \frac{1}{\mid S\begin{vmatrix}} \\ = \frac{1}{\mid S \mid}∑_(x ∈ S)x \\ = \frac{1}{\mid S\end{vmatrix}}[a + (a + 1) + (a + 2) + ⋯ + b]
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, the term in the square brackets is an arithmetic series with the first term $a$ and the last term $b$. Therefore, using the formula for the sum of an arithmetic series, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
E[X] &= \frac{1}{\mid S\begin{vmatrix}}[a + (a + 1) + (a + 2) + ⋯ + b] \\ = \frac{1}{\begin{vmatrix}S &} \cdot \frac{\mid S\end{vmatrix}}{2} \cdot (a + b) \\ = \frac{1}{\begin{vmatrix}S &} \cdot \frac{\end{vmatrix}S\end{vmatrix}}{2} \cdot (a + b) \\
&= \frac{a + b}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

as required.

## Proving the Formula for the Variance

Let's now show that for $X\sim \{a, a+1, a+2, \ldots, b\}$, we have

$$
\textrm{Var}[X] = \dfrac{\mid S \mid ^2-1}{12}
$$

We will assume, without loss of generality, that the distribution of our random variable $X$ is given by

$$
X\sim\{1, 2, 3, \ldots, \mid S \mid \}
$$

We do not lose generality here because, in general, the variance (i.e., spread) of a random variable is unaffected by translations:

$$
\textrm{Var}[X + c] = \textrm{Var}[X]
$$

By the definition of $\textrm{E}[X^2]$, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
E[X^{2}] &= ∑_(x ∈ S)x^{2}f(x) \\
&= ∑_(x ∈ S)x^{2} \cdot \frac{1}{\mid S\begin{vmatrix}} \\ = \frac{1}{\mid S \mid} \cdot ∑_(x ∈ S)x^{2} \\ = \frac{1}{\mid S\end{vmatrix}} \cdot \begin{bmatrix}1^{2} + 2^{2} + ⋯ + & S & ^{2}\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

We now make use of the following result:

$$
\sum_{i=1}^n i^2 = \dfrac16 n (n+1)(2n+1)
$$

Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
E[X^{2}] &= \frac{1}{\begin{vmatrix}S &} \cdot \begin{bmatrix}1^{2} + 2^{2} + ⋯ + & S & ^{2}\end{bmatrix} \\ = \frac{1}{\mid S \mid} \cdot \frac{1}{6} \cdot & S\end{vmatrix}\cdot (\begin{vmatrix}S & + 1)(2 \mid S \mid + 1) \\ = \frac{1}{\mid S \mid} \cdot \frac{1}{6} \cdot & S\end{vmatrix}\cdot (\begin{vmatrix}S & + 1)(2 \mid S\end{vmatrix}+ 1) \\
&= ((\begin{vmatrix}S & + 1)(2 \mid S\end{vmatrix}+ 1))/(6)
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we use the following results:

$$
\textrm{Var}[X] = \textrm{E}[X^2] - \left(\textrm{E}[X]\right)^2, \qquad \textrm {E}[X] = \dfrac{1+ \mid S \mid}{2}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
Var[X] &= E[X^{2}] - (E[X])^{2} \\
&= ((\begin{vmatrix}S & + 1)(2\begin{vmatrix}S & + 1))/(6) - (\frac{\end{vmatrix}S\end{vmatrix}+ 1}{2})^{2} \\
&= ((\begin{vmatrix}S & + 1)(2\begin{vmatrix}S & + 1))/(6) - ((\end{vmatrix}S\end{vmatrix}+ 1)^{2})/(4) \\
&= (2(\begin{vmatrix}S & + 1)(2\begin{vmatrix}S & + 1))/(12) - (3(\end{vmatrix}S\end{vmatrix}+ 1)^{2})/(12) \\
&= ((\begin{vmatrix}S & + 1))/(12)(2(2\begin{vmatrix}S & + 1) - 3(\end{vmatrix}S\end{vmatrix}+ 1)) \\
&= ((\begin{vmatrix}S & + 1))/(12)(4\begin{vmatrix}S & + 2 - 3\end{vmatrix}S\end{vmatrix}- 3) \\
&= ((\begin{vmatrix}S & + 1))/(12)(\mid S\end{vmatrix}- 1) \\
&= ((\begin{vmatrix}S & + 1)(\mid S\end{vmatrix}- 1))/(12) \\
&= \frac{\mid S \mid ^{2} - 1}{12}
\end{aligned}
$$

as required.
