## Introduction

A discrete random variable $X$ follows a **discrete uniform distribution** over a set $S$ if each value in $S$ is equally likely.

The probability mass function for the discrete uniform distribution on a set $S$ is given by

$$
{\frac{1}{\begin{vmatrix}S &},\end{vmatrix}x ∈ S,; 0, \mid otherwise
$$

When a random variable $X$ follows a discrete uniform distribution over the set

$$
S=\{s_1, s_2, \ldots, s_n\}
$$

we denote it as

$$
X \sim U \left\{s_1, s_2, \ldots, s_n\right\}
$$

and we say that $X$ is **uniformly distributed** over $S$.

For example, consider the random variable $T\sim U\{1,2,4\}$. In this case,

$$
S = \{1,2,4\}
$$

$$
\mid S \mid = 3
$$

and therefore the probability mass function for $T$ is

$$
{\frac{1}{3},\begin{vmatrix}t ∈ {1, 2, 4}, \\ 0,\end{vmatrix}otherwise
$$

A sketch of $f(t)$ is shown below.

**Note:** In this lesson, we will make use of the following formula for computing the cardinality of a set of consecutive numbers:

$$
\left| \{a, a+1, a+2, \ldots, b \} \right| = b - a + 1
$$

Don't forget about the $+1$ at the end of the formula. The $+1$ is needed because we are including both endpoints $a$ and $b$ in the count. (Not convinced? Notice that

$$
\left| \{4, 5 \} \right| = 5 - 4 + 1 = 2
$$

)

## Computing a Probability at a Single Point

**Example:** Given that $X \sim U \left\lbrace 7, 8, 9, \ldots, 21 \right\rbrace$, compute $P(X = 18)$.

**Explanation**

If $X$ follows a discrete uniform distribution over a set $S$, then $X$ has the following probability mass function:

$$
{\frac{1}{\begin{vmatrix}S &},\end{vmatrix}x ∈ S; 0, \mid otherwise
$$

Here, we have

$$
X \sim U \left\lbrace 7, 8, 9, \ldots, 21 \right\rbrace
$$

and the set

$$
S = \left\lbrace 7, 8, 9, \ldots, 21 \right\rbrace
$$

has

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mid S &= 21 - 7 + 1 = 15
\end{aligned}
$$

items. So, we have

$$
{\frac{1}{15},\begin{vmatrix}x = 7, 8, 9, …, 21 \\ 0,\end{vmatrix}otherwise
$$

Therefore,

$$
P(X = 18) = f(18) = \dfrac{1}{15}
$$

## Computing a Probability on an Unbounded Interval

**Example:** Given $X \sim U \left\lbrace 5, 6, 7, \ldots, 14 \right\rbrace$, compute $P(X \geq 9)$.

**Explanation**

If $X$ follows a discrete uniform distribution over a set $S$, then $X$ has the following probability mass function:

$$
{\frac{1}{\begin{vmatrix}S &},\end{vmatrix}x ∈ S; 0, \mid otherwise
$$

Here, we have

$$
X \sim U \left\lbrace 5, 6, 7, \ldots, 14 \right\rbrace
$$

and the set

$$
S = \left\lbrace 5, 6, 7, \ldots, 14 \right\rbrace
$$

has

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mid S &= 14 - 5 + 1 = 10
\end{aligned}
$$

items. So, we have

$$
{\frac{1}{10},\begin{vmatrix}x = 5, 6, 7, …, 14 \\ 0,\end{vmatrix}otherwise
$$

In $S$, the items that satisfy

$$
X \geq 9
$$

are $9, 10, 11, \ldots, 14$. There are

$$
14 - 9 + 1 = 6
$$

of these items. Therefore,

$$
P(X \geq 9) = 6 \cdot \dfrac{1}{10} = \dfrac{3}{5}
$$

## Computing a Probability on a Bounded Interval

**Example:** Given $X \sim U \left\lbrace 7, 8, 9, \ldots, 16\right\rbrace$, compute $P(8 \leq X < 13)$.

**Explanation**

If $X$ follows a discrete uniform distribution over a set $S$, then $X$ has the following probability mass function:

$$
{\frac{1}{\begin{vmatrix}S &},\end{vmatrix}x ∈ S; 0, \mid otherwise
$$

Here, we have

$$
X \sim U \left\lbrace 7, 8, 9, \ldots, 16 \right\rbrace
$$

and the set

$$
S = \left\lbrace 7, 8, 9, \ldots, 16 \right\rbrace
$$

has

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mid S &= 16 - 7 + 1 = 10
\end{aligned}
$$

items. So, we have

$$
{\frac{1}{10},\begin{vmatrix}x = 7, 8, 9, …, 16 \\ 0,\end{vmatrix}otherwise
$$

In $S$, there are $5$ items $(8, 9, 10, 11, 12)$ that satisfy

$$
8 \leq X < 13
$$

Therefore,

$$
P(8 \leq X < 13) = 5 \cdot \dfrac{1}{10} = \dfrac{1}{2}
$$
