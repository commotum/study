## Introduction

**De Morgan's laws** for sets tell us alternative ways to represent the complement of a union or intersection.

- De Morgan's law for unions states that
$\overline{A \cup B} \equiv \overline{A} \cap \overline{B}$.
- De Morgan's law for intersections states that
$\overline{A \cap B} \equiv \overline{A} \cup \overline{B}$.

Intuitively, you can think that the complement "flips" the union into an intersection and vice versa.

To demonstrate De Morgan's laws, consider the sets

$$
A = \{1,2,3 \}
$$

and

$$
B = \{3,4 \}
$$

in the universe

$$
U = \{1,2,3,4,5,6 \}
$$

- Computing $\overline{A \cup B}$, we have
$A∪B^(―)|= {1, 2, 3}∪{3, 4}^(―); = {1, 2, 3, 4}^(―); = {5, 6}$.
Computing $\overline{A} \cap \overline{B}$, we have
$A^(―)∩B^(―)|= {1, 2, 3}^(―)∩{3, 4}^(―); = {4, 5, 6}∩{1, 2, 5, 6}; = {5, 6}$.
So, we have that $\overline{A \cup B} = \overline{A} \cap \overline{B}. \quad \color{green}\checkmark$
- Computing $\overline{A \cap B}$, we have
$A∩B^(―)|= {1, 2, 3}∩{3, 4}^(―); = {3}^(―); = {1, 2, 4, 5, 6}$.
Computing $\overline{A} \cup \overline{B}$, we have
$A^(―)∪B^(―)|= {1, 2, 3}^(―)∪{3, 4}^(―); = {4, 5, 6}∪{1, 2, 5, 6}; = {1, 2, 4, 5, 6}$.
So, we have that $\overline{A \cap B} = \overline{A} \cup \overline{B}. \quad \color{green}\checkmark$

## Finding the Complement of a Union

**Example:** If $\overline{X} = \{a, c, d\}$ and $\overline{Y} = \{a, e, i, o, u \}$, then what is $\overline{X \cup Y}$?

**Explanation**

De Morgan's law for unions states that
$\overline{X \cup Y} \equiv \overline{X} \cap \overline{Y}$.

So, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
X∪Y^(―) &= X^(―)∩Y^(―) \\
&= {a, c, d}∩{a, e, i, o, u} \\
&= {a}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding the Complement of an Intersection

**Example:** If $\overline{P} = \{2, 4, 6 \}$ and $\overline{Q} = \{1, 2, 4 \}$, then what is $\overline{P \cap Q}$?

**Explanation**

De Morgan's law for intersections states that
$\overline{P \cap Q} \equiv \overline{P} \cup \overline{Q}$.

So, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
P∩Q^(―) &= P^(―)∪Q^(―) \\
&= {2, 4, 6}∪{1, 2, 4} \\
&= {1, 2, 4, 6}
\end{aligned}
$$
