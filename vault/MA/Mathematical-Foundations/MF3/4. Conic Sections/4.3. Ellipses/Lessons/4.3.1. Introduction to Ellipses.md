## Introduction

An **ellipse** is a circle that has been stretched. For example, let's look at the ellipse given below:

![](<../Source/4.3.1. Introduction to Ellipses/Images/3a2a913fc89e69e7939f83d4c35ae042.png>)

The longest diameter of an ellipse is called the **major axis**.

So, for the ellipse above, the major axis is the line segment bounded by the points $(1,-2)$ and $(1,4)$, and its length is $4-(-2)=6$.

Half of the major axis is called a **semi-major axis**. For our ellipse above, the length of the semi-major axis is

$$
\dfrac{6}{2} = 3
$$

On the other hand, the **minor axis** is the shortest diameter of an ellipse.
Let's have another look at the same ellipse:

![](<../Source/4.3.1. Introduction to Ellipses/Images/fe45be387514f1aff084879219f783e5.png>)

Here, we see the minor axis of the ellipse. It is the line segment bounded by the points $(-1,1)$ and $(3,1)$, and its length is $3-(-1)=4$.

Half of the minor axis is called a **semi-minor axis**. For our ellipse, the semi-minor axis has a length of

$$
\dfrac{4}{2} = 2
$$

For this particular ellipse:

- Since the major axis is parallel to the $y$-axis, the major axis is said to be "along the $y$-direction".
- Since the minor axis is parallel to the $x$-axis, the minor axis is said to be "along the $x$-direction".

Note that for other ellipses, it may be opposite: the major axis may be along the $x$-direction, and the minor axis may be along the $y$-direction.

## Determining the Lengths of the Major and Minor Axes of an Ellipse

**Example:** What are the lengths of the major and minor axes of the ellipse given below?

![](<../Source/4.3.1. Introduction to Ellipses/Images/f800398f09f7c8e4a7d629ceae4476f1.png>)

**Explanation**

The major axis is the longest diameter of the ellipse, which is the line segment bounded by the points $(-3,1)$ and $(7,1)$. So, the length of the major axis is

$$
7 - (-3) = 10
$$

The minor axis is the shortest diameter of the ellipse, which is the line segment bounded by the points $(2,-1)$ and $(2,3)$. So, the length of the minor axis is

$$
3 - (-1) = 4
$$

## Determining the Lengths of the Semi-Major Axis of an Ellipse

**Example:** What are the lengths of the major and semi-major axes of the ellipse given below?

![](<../Source/4.3.1. Introduction to Ellipses/Images/5c2dc5486dff41e044c362485d5f13d8.png>)

**Explanation**

The major axis is the longest diameter of the ellipse, which is the line segment bounded by the points $(-2,-5)$ and $(-2,3)$. So, the length of the major axis is $3-(-5)=8$.

The length of the semi-major axis is half the length of the major axis. So, the length of the semi-major axis

$$
\dfrac{8}{2} = 4
$$

## Determining the Lengths of the Semi-Minor Axis of an Ellipse

**Example:** What are the lengths of the minor and semi-minor axes of the ellipse given below?

![](<../Source/4.3.1. Introduction to Ellipses/Images/0c1da2b02c8beeffeaf54b2bdd689d3c.png>)

**Explanation**

The major axis is the longest diameter of the ellipse, which is the line segment bounded by the points $(-3, -1)$ and $(7, -1)$. So, the length of the major axis is $7-(-3)=10$.

The length of the semi-minor axis is half the length of the minor axis.

- The minor axis is the shortest diameter of the ellipse, which is the line segment bounded by the points $(2,2)$ and $(2,-4)$. So, the length of the minor axis is $2-(-4)=6$.
- The length of the semi-minor axis, then, is $\dfrac{6}{2}=3$.
