## Introduction

Consider the circle with equation

$$
(x - 2)^2 + (y - 1)^2 = 3^2
$$

as shown below. How can we calculate the points where the circle intercepts the $x$-axis?

![](<../Source/4.1.5. Calculating Circle Intercepts/Images/8da55ccd721d34a5ead3db59768d75e0.png>)

Remember, when the circle intercepts the $x$-axis, we must have $y = 0$.

Therefore, to find the $x$-intercepts of the circle, we need to set $y = 0$ in the equation of the circle and solve for $x$ as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x - 2)^{2} + (y - 1)^{2} &= 3^{2} \\
(x - 2)^{2} + (0 - 1)^{2} &= 3^{2} \\
(x - 2)^{2} + 1 &= 9 \\
(x - 2)^{2} &= 8 \\
x - 2 &= ± \sqrt{8} \\
x - 2 &= ± 2\sqrt{2} \\
x &= 2 ± 2\sqrt{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the circle intercepts the $x$-axis at $(2-2\sqrt{2},0)$ and $(2+2\sqrt{2},0)$.

## Calculating the Intersection of a Circle With the X-Axis

**Example:** Find the $x$-intercepts of the circle $(x + 3)^2 + (y - 2)^2 = 4$.

**Explanation**

We must have $y = 0$ at the $x$-intercepts.

So, we need to substitute $y = 0$ into the equation of the circle and solve for $x$, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x + 3)^{2} + (y - 2)^{2} &= 4 \\
(x + 3)^{2} + (0 - 2)^{2} &= 4 \\
(x + 3)^{2} + 4 &= 4 \\
(x + 3)^{2} &= 0 \\
x + 3 &= 0 \\
x &= -3
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the $x$-intercept occurs at the point $(-3,0)$, as shown below.

![](<../Source/4.1.5. Calculating Circle Intercepts/Images/7b22a1d5c4a7451aec2ac41c916b9eed.png>)

## Calculating the Intersection of a Circle With the Y-Axis

Consider the circle with equation

$$
x^2 + (y-2)^2 = 4^2
$$

as shown below. How can we calculate the points where the circle intercepts the $y$-axis?

![](<../Source/4.1.5. Calculating Circle Intercepts/Images/95bdbcd0899c32a69e54b2a2f4a4a11b.png>)

When the circle intercepts the $y$-axis, we must have $x = 0$.

Therefore, to find the $y$-intercepts of the circle, we need to set $x = 0$ in the equation of the circle and solven for $y$ as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^{2} + (y - 2)^{2} &= 4^{2} \\
0^{2} + (y - 2)^{2} &= 4^{2} \\
0 + (y - 2)^{2} &= 16 \\
(y - 2)^{2} &= 16 \\
y - 2 &= ± 4 \\
y &= 2 ± 4 \\
y &= -2, 6
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the circle intercepts the $y$-axis at $(0,-2)$ and $(0,6)$.

## Calculating the Intersection of a Circle With the Y-Axis

**Example:** Find the $y$-intercepts of the circle $x^2+y^2+2x-2y= 8$, shown below.

![](<../Source/4.1.5. Calculating Circle Intercepts/Images/0ca0fbe99c8a02c276d3fd0a1905e8ae.png>)

**Explanation**

We must have $x = 0$ at the $y$-intercepts.

So, we need to substitute $x = 0$ into the equation of the circle and solve for $y$, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^{2} + y^{2} + 2x - 2y &= 8 \\
(0)^{2} + y^{2} + 2(0) - 2y &= 8 \\
y^{2} - 2y &= 8
\end{aligned}
$$

We can solve this equation by completing the square, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
y^{2} - 2y + 1 &= 8 + 1 \\
(y - 1)^{2} &= 9 \\
y - 1 &= ± 3 \\
y &= 1 ± 3
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the $y$-intercepts occur at $(0,-2)$ and $(0,4)$.

## Identifying When a Circle Does Not Intersect an Axis

**Example:** Calculate the $y$-intercepts of the circle $(x - 3)^2 + (y - 1)^2 = 4$.

**Explanation**

We must have $x = 0$ at the $y$-intercepts.

So, we need to substitute $x = 0$ into the equation of the circle and solve the resulting equation for $y$, if possible:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x - 3)^{2} + (y - 1)^{2} &= 4 \\
(0 - 3)^{2} + (y - 1)^{2} &= 4 \\
9 + (y - 1)^{2} &= 4 \\
(y - 1)^{2} &= -5
\end{aligned}
$$

Since

$$
(y-1)^2 \geq 0
$$

for all values of $y$, there is no $y$ such that

$$
(y-1)^2 = -5
$$

Therefore, the circle has no $y$-intercepts, as shown below.

![](<../Source/4.1.5. Calculating Circle Intercepts/Images/95fe7135f0f55209cacb7cc2b7b423fa.png>)
