## Introduction

The **general standard equation** of a hyperbola centered at $(h, k)$ has two forms.

- The equation for a horizontal hyperbola is given by
$\dfrac {(x - h)^2} {a^2} - \dfrac {(y - k)^2} {b^2} = 1$.![](<../Source/4.4.2. Equations of Hyperbolas Centered at a General Point/Images/fd7ed3be9725c2ffb365d8319a4b33f9.png>)
- The equation for a vertical hyperbola is given by
$\dfrac {(y - k)^2} {a^2} - \dfrac {(x - h)^2} {b^2} = 1$.![](<../Source/4.4.2. Equations of Hyperbolas Centered at a General Point/Images/922e770b72f853bd87c6e8f36bed4f45.png>)

## Finding the Center of a Horizontal Hyperbola

**Example:** What is the center of the hyperbola $\dfrac{(x-1)^2}{9} - \dfrac{(y-2)^2}{4} = 1$?

**Explanation**

The equation of a horizontal hyperbola centered at $(h,k)$ is given by

$$
\dfrac{(x-h)^2}{a^2} - \dfrac{(y-k)^2}{b^2}=1
$$

In this case, we have $h = 1$ and $k = 2$.

Therefore, the center of the hyperbola is $(1,2)$.

## Finding the Center of a Vertical Hyperbola

**Example:** What is the center of the hyperbola $\dfrac{(y+3)^2}{16} - \dfrac{(x-1)^2}{9} = 1$?

**Explanation**

The equation of a vertical hyperbola centered at $(h,k)$ is given by

$$
\dfrac{(y-k)^2}{a^2} - \dfrac{(x-h)^2}{b^2}=1
$$

Note that the given equation can be rewritten as follows:

$$
\dfrac{(y-(-3))^2}{16} - \dfrac{(x-1)^2}{9}=1
$$

In this case, we have $h = 1$ and $k=-3$.

Therefore, the center of the hyperbola is $(1,-3)$.

## Finding the Center of a Horizontal Hyperbola Given an Equation in Expanded Form

**Example:** What is the center of the hyperbola
$x^2 - 4 y^2- 2 x + 16 y = 31$?

**Explanation**

The equation of a horizontal hyperbola centered at $(h,k)$ is given by

$$
\dfrac{(x-h)^2}{a^2} - \dfrac{(y-k)^2}{b^2}=1
$$

To rewrite the equation of the hyperbola in the above form, we need to group $x$ and $y$ terms and complete the squares, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^{2} - 4y^{2} - 2x + 16y &= 31 \\
x^{2} - 2x - 4y^{2} + 16y &= 31 \\
x^{2} - 2x - 4[y^{2} - 4y] &= 31 \\
(x - 1)^{2} - 1 - 4[(y - 2)^{2} - 4] &= 31 \\
(x - 1)^{2} - 1 - 4(y - 2)^{2} + 16 &= 31 \\
(x - 1)^{2} - 4(y - 2)^{2} &= 31 + 1 - 16 \\
(x - 1)^{2} - 4(y - 2)^{2} &= 16 \\
((x - 1)^{2})/(16) - ((y - 2)^{2})/(4) &= 1
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the center is $(1,2)$.

## Finding the Center of a Vertical Hyperbola Given an Equation in Expanded Form

**Example:** What is the center of the hyperbola
$4 y^2 - 10 x^2 - 8 y - 40 x = 76$?

**Explanation**

The equation of a vertical hyperbola centered at $(h,k)$ is given by

$$
\dfrac{(y-k)^2}{a^2} - \dfrac{(x-h)^2}{b^2}=1
$$

To rewrite the equation of the hyperbola in the above form, we need to group $x$ and $y$ terms and complete the squares as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
4y^{2} - 10x^{2} - 8y - 40x &= 76 \\
4y^{2} - 8y - 10x^{2} - 40x &= 76 \\
4[y^{2} - 2y] - 10[x^{2} + 4x] &= 76 \\
4[(y - 1)^{2} - 1] - 10[(x + 2)^{2} - 4] &= 76 \\
4(y - 1)^{2} - 4 - 10(x + 2)^{2} + 40 &= 76 \\
4(y - 1)^{2} - 10(x + 2)^{2} &= 76 - 40 + 4 \\
4(y - 1)^{2} - 10(x + 2)^{2} &= 40 \\
((y - 1)^{2})/(10) - ((x + 2)^{2})/(4) &= 1
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the center is $(-2,1)$.
