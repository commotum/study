## Introduction

When simplifying expressions containing $\cot$ and $\csc$, we can use the following **cotangent-cosecant identity**:

$$
1 + \cot^2 x= \csc^2 x
$$

The identity above can be obtained by starting with the Pythagorean identity:

$$
\sin^{2} x + \cos^{2} x = 1
$$

We divide both sides by $\color{black}\sin^2 x$ as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\sin^{2} x}{\sin^{2} x} + \frac{\cos^{2} x}{\sin^{2} x} &= \frac{1}{\sin^{2} x}
\end{aligned}
$$

Simplifying the above using

$$
\cot x = \dfrac{\cos x}{\sin x}
$$

and

$$
\csc x = \dfrac{1}{\sin x}
$$

gives

$$
1 + \cot^2 x = \csc^2 x
$$

as required.

---

<a id="simplifying-an-expression-using-the-cotangent-cosecant-identity"></a>
## Simplifying an Expression Using the Cotangent-Cosecant Identity

**Example:** Simplify the trigonometric expression $\sin \theta \, (1 + \cot^2 \theta)$.

**Explanation**

First, using the identity

$$
1 + \cot^2 \theta = \csc^2 \theta
$$

we rewrite the expression in the parentheses:

$$
\sin θ(1 + \cot^{2} θ) = \sin θ \cdot \csc^{2} θ
$$

Then, using the fact that

$$
\csc {\theta} = \dfrac{1}{\sin{\theta}}
$$

we can further simplify the expression:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sin θ \cdot \csc^{2} θ &= \sin θ \cdot \frac{1}{\sin^{2} θ} \\
&= \sin θ \cdot \frac{1}{\sin θ \cdot \sin θ} \\
&= \frac{1}{\sin θ} \\
&= \csc θ
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="alternate-forms-of-the-cotangent-cosecant-identity"></a>
## Alternate Forms of the Cotangent-Cosecant Identity

If we subtract $1$ from both sides of the cotangent-cosecant identity

$$
1 + \cot^2\theta = \csc^2\theta
$$

we get

$$
\cot^2\theta = \csc^2\theta - 1
$$

This is an alternate form of the cotangent-cosecant identity.

We can also get a second alternate form if we start with

$$
1 + \cot^2\theta = \csc^2\theta
$$

and subtract $\cot^2\theta$ from both sides. This gives

$$
\csc^2\theta - \cot^2\theta = 1
$$

---

<a id="simplifying-an-expression-using-the-alternate-forms-of-the-cotangent-cosecant-identity"></a>
## Simplifying an Expression Using the Alternate Forms of the Cotangent-Cosecant Identity

**Example:** Simplify $\dfrac{1}{\csc^2{\theta} - 1}$.

**Explanation**

First, let's recall the following identity:

$$
\csc^2{\theta} - 1 = \cot^2{\theta}
$$

Substituting the above identity into the given expression, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{1}{\csc^{2} θ - 1} &= \frac{1}{\cot^{2} θ} = \tan^{2} θ
\end{aligned}
$$

## Simplifying an Expression by Factoring and Using Various Forms of the Cotangent-Cosecant Identity

**Example:** Simplify $\csc x + \csc x \cot^2 x -\csc^3x$.

**Explanation**

First, we factor $\csc x$ from the first two terms, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\csc x + \csc x\cot^{2} x - \csc^{3} x &= \csc x(1 + \cot^{2} x) - \csc^{3} x
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, using the cotangent-cosecant identity,

$$
1+ \cot^2 x=\csc^2 x
$$

we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
\csc x(1 + \cot^{2} x) - \csc^{3} x &= \csc x \cdot (\csc^{2} x) - \csc^{3} x \\
&= \csc^{3} x - \csc^{3} x \\
&= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

## Simplifying an Expression by Factoring the Alternate Forms of the Cotangent-Cosecant Identity

**Example:** Simplify $\dfrac{1}{\csc \theta + \cot \theta} + \cot \theta$.

**Explanation**

First, we recall the following identity:

$$
1= \csc^2{\theta} -\cot^2{\theta}
$$

Substituting the above identity into the given expression, and then factoring it as a difference of squares, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{1}{\csc θ + \cot θ} + \cot θ &= \frac{\csc^{2} θ - \cot^{2} θ}{\csc θ + \cot θ} + \cot θ \\
&= ((\csc θ - \cot θ)(\csc θ + \cot θ))/(\csc θ + \cot θ) + \cot θ \\
&= ((\csc θ - \cot θ)(\csc θ + \cot θ))/(\csc θ + \cot θ) + \cot θ \\
&= (\csc θ - \cot θ) + \cot θ \\
&= \csc θ
\end{aligned}
$$
