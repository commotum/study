## Introduction

The Pythagorean identity is

$$
\cos^2{\theta}+\sin^2{\theta}=1
$$

We can use the Pythagorean identity to simplify trigonometric expressions. For example, let's try to simplify the following:

$$
2\sin \theta \left(\sin \theta + \dfrac {\cos^2 \theta} {\sin \theta}\right)
$$

We first expand the parentheses, and then simplify the resulting expression:

$$
\begin{aligned}
2\sin θ(\sin θ + \frac{\cos^{2} θ}{\sin θ}) &= 2\sin^{2} θ + 2\sin θ \cdot \frac{\cos^{2} θ}{\sin θ} \\
&= 2\sin^{2} θ + 2\sin θ \cdot \frac{\cos^{2} θ}{\sin θ} \\
&= 2\sin^{2} θ + 2\cos^{2} θ \\
&= 2(\sin^{2} θ + \cos^{2} θ)
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we can use the Pythagorean identity to rewrite the expression in the parentheses, as follows:

$$
2(\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta) =2\cdot 1 = 2
$$

Therefore, we conclude that

$$
2\sin \theta \left(\sin \theta + \dfrac {\cos^2 \theta} {\sin \theta}\right) = 2
$$

## Simplifying an Expression by Expanding Parentheses and Using the Pythagorean Identity

**Example:** Simplify the expression $\sin x(2\cos x - \sin x) - \cos^2x$.

**Explanation**

First, we expand the parentheses:

$$
2\sin x\cos x - \sin^{2} x - \cos^{2} x
$$

Next, we group the $\sin^2 x$ and $\cos^2 x$ terms.

$$
2\sin x\cos x -(\sin^2x + \cos^2 x)
$$

Then, we use

$$
\sin^2{x} + \cos^2{x} = 1
$$

to simplify the expression in the parentheses:

$$
2\sin x\cos x - 1
$$

Therefore, we conclude that

$$
\sin x(2\cos x - \sin x) - \cos^2x = 2\sin x\cos x - 1
$$

## Simplifying an Expression by Factoring and Using the Pythagorean Identity

**Example:** Simplify the trigonometric expression $\sin^4{\theta}+\sin^2{\theta}\cos^2{\theta}$.

**Explanation**

First, we factor $\sin^2\theta$ from the expression:

$$
\sin^4{\theta}+\sin^2{\theta}\cos^2{\theta} = \sin^2{\theta}\cdot(\sin^2{\theta}+\cos^2{\theta})
$$

Then, we use

$$
\cos^2\theta + \sin^2\theta = 1
$$

to simplify the expression in the parentheses:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sin^{2} θ \cdot (\sin^{2} θ + \cos^{2}) &= \sin^{2} θ \cdot 1 = \sin^{2} θ
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, we conclude that

$$
\sin^4{\theta}+\sin^2{\theta}\cos^2{\theta} = \sin^2\theta
$$

<a id="simplifying-a-rational-expression-using-the-pythagorean-identity"></a>
## Simplifying a Rational Expression Using the Pythagorean Identity

**Example:** Simplify the trigonometric expression $\dfrac {\cos \theta \sin^2 \theta + \cos^3 \theta}{\sin\theta}$.

**Explanation**

First, we factor the numerator:

$$
\dfrac {\cos \theta \sin^2 \theta + \cos^3 \theta} {\sin \theta} = \dfrac {\cos \theta \cdot (\sin^2 \theta + \cos^2 \theta)} {\sin \theta}
$$

Next, we use the Pythagorean identity to rewrite the expression in the parentheses, and then we simplify the resulting expression as much as possible.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(\cos θ \cdot (\sin^{2} θ + \cos^{2} θ))/(\sin θ) &= (\cos θ \cdot (1))/(\sin θ) \\
&= \frac{\cos θ}{\sin θ} \\
&= \cot θ
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, we conclude that:

$$
\dfrac {\cos \theta \sin^2 \theta + \cos^3 \theta} {\sin \theta} = \cot \theta
$$

## Simplifying a Trigonometric Expression Using a Common Denominator and the Pythagorean Identity

**Example:** Simplify the trigonometric expression $\dfrac{\sin{\theta}}{1+\cos{\theta}}+\cot{\theta}$.

**Explanation**

First, we rewrite the second term using the identity

$$
\cot \theta = \dfrac{\cos\theta}{\sin\theta}
$$

as follows:

$$
\dfrac{\sin{\theta}}{1+\cos{\theta}}+\dfrac{\cos{\theta}}{\sin{\theta}}
$$

Next, we put both terms over a common denominator, and combine the two fractions:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(\sin θ \cdot \sin θ)/(\sin θ(1 + \cos θ)) + (\cos θ \cdot (1 + \cos θ))/(\sin θ(1 + \cos θ)) &=  \\
&= (\sin θ \cdot \sin θ + \cos θ \cdot (1 + \cos θ))/(\sin θ(1 + \cos θ))
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we simplify as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(\sin^{2} θ + \cos θ + \cos^{2} θ)/(\sin θ(1 + \cos θ)) &=  \\
((\sin^{2} θ + \cos^{2} θ) + \cos θ)/(\sin θ(1 + \cos θ)) &=  \\
(1 + \cos θ)/(\sin θ(1 + \cos θ)) &=  \\
\frac{1}{\sin θ} &=  \\
&= \csc θ
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, we conclude that

$$
\dfrac{\sin{\theta}}{1+\cos{\theta}}+\cot{\theta} = \csc\theta
$$
