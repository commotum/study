## Introduction

Suppose we want to find all of the solutions to the equation

$$
0^{∘} \le x < 360^{∘}
$$

We can immediately calculate a solution, called the **principal value**, as follows:

$$
x = \arcsin\left(\frac{1}{2}\right) =30^\circ
$$

Since $30^\circ$ lies inside the required domain, our first solution is

$$
x_1=30^\circ
$$

This angle lies in the first quadrant and has reference angle

$$
\theta_R = 30^\circ
$$

But there is another solution.
Since

$$
\sin x = \dfrac{1}{2}>0
$$

the second solution to the equation will lie in the second quadrant, and its reference angle is also

$$
\theta_R = 30^\circ
$$

To find the second solution, we use a CAST diagram.

![](<../Source/5.2.1. Elementary Trigonometric Equations Containing Sine/Images/4e0ac2eab678db7e109fcdc7fcf555b2.png>)

Since the second solution $x_2$ lies in the $2$nd quadrant, we can find it using the reference angle as follows:

$$
x_2 = 180^\circ - 30^\circ = 150^\circ
$$

So, the solutions are

$$
x=30^\circ, 150^\circ
$$

<a id="solving-a-trigonometric-equation-involving-sine-with-special-angles"></a>
## Solving a Trigonometric Equation Involving Sine with Special Angles

**Example:** Find the solutions to the equation $2\sin x+1=0$ for $0 \leq x < 2\pi$.

**Explanation**

First, we rearrange the equation, isolating $\sin{x}$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
2\sin x + 1 &= 0 \\
2\sin x &= -1 \\
\sin x &= -\frac{1}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we find the principal value:

$$
x = \arcsin (-\frac{1}{2}) =-\frac{π}{6}
$$

Note that

$$
x =-\dfrac{\pi}{6}
$$

is not in the range

$$
0 \leq x < 2\pi
$$

To get the first solution that's within the required range, we need to find an angle that's coterminal with

$$
-\dfrac{\pi}{6}
$$

To do this, we simply add $2 \pi$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_{1} &= -\frac{π}{6} + 2π = \frac{11π}{6}
\end{aligned}
$$

Our first solution lies in the fourth quadrant and has a reference angle of

$$
\theta_R = \dfrac{\pi}{6}
$$

Since $\sin{x} < 0$, the second solution must lie in the third quadrant, and it also has a reference angle

$$
\theta_R = \dfrac{\pi}{6}
$$

![](<../Source/5.2.1. Elementary Trigonometric Equations Containing Sine/Images/b170f4c1e6d9e1c8a09d09c59b5ba53c.png>)

Since the second solution $x_2$ lies in the $3$rd quadrant, we can find it using the reference angle as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_{2} &= π + θ_{R} \\
&= π + \frac{π}{6} \\
&= \frac{7π}{6}
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the solutions are

$$
x = \dfrac{7\pi}{6}, \dfrac{11\pi}{6}
$$

## Solving a Trigonometric Equation Involving Sine with Non-Special Angles

**Example:** Calculate, in radians to $1$ decimal place, the solutions to the equation $\sin x = \dfrac{4}{5}$ for $0 \leq x < 2\pi$.

**Explanation**

First, we find the principal value:

$$
x = \arcsin (\frac{4}{5}) = 0.927295… \approx 0.927
$$

Since $0.927$ lies inside the given domain, our first solution is

$$
x_1 = 0.927
$$

Our first solution lies in the first quadrant and has a reference angle of

$$
\theta_R = 0.927
$$

Since $\sin{x} > 0$, the second solution must lie in the second quadrant, and it also has a reference angle

$$
\theta_R = 0.927
$$

![](<../Source/5.2.1. Elementary Trigonometric Equations Containing Sine/Images/14ea617c25681a19eca6f44a19ff4aa8.png>)

Since the second solution $x_2$ lies in the $2$nd quadrant, we can find it using the reference angle as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_{2} &= π - θ_{R} \\
&= π - 0.927 \\
&= 2.215
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the solutions (rounded to one decimal place) are

$$
x = 0.9, 2.2
$$

<a id="solving-a-trigonometric-equation-involving-sine-under-a-modified-domain"></a>
## Solving a Trigonometric Equation Involving Sine Under a Modified Domain

We can look for solutions to trigonometric equations in domains other than

$$
0 \leq x < 2\pi
$$

or

$$
0^\circ \leq x < 360^\circ
$$

For instance, suppose that we want to find all of the solutions to

$$
- π < x \le π
$$

This time, we are looking for solutions in the domain

$$
-\pi < x \leq \pi
$$

First, we find the principal value:

$$
x = \arcsin\left(-\frac{1}{2}\right) = -\dfrac{\pi}{6}
$$

Since

$$
-\dfrac{\pi}{6}
$$

lies inside the required domain, our first solution is

$$
x_1=-\dfrac{\pi}{6}
$$

Our first solution lies in the fourth quadrant and has a reference angle of

$$
\theta_R=\dfrac{\pi}{6}
$$

Since $\sin x<0$, the second solution must lie in the third quadrant, and it also has a reference angle

$$
\theta_R=\dfrac{\pi}{6}
$$

![](<../Source/5.2.1. Elementary Trigonometric Equations Containing Sine/Images/f3b7a6d51f0d5ccdebaa7b6f3fc60429.png>)

A second solution to the equation, $X$, lies in the $3$rd quadrant, and we can find it using the reference angle as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
X &= π + θ_{R} \\
&= π + \frac{π}{6} \\
&= \frac{7π}{6}
\end{aligned}
$$

However, this value is not in the required domain. To get the second solution $x_2$ that's within the given domain, we simply subtract $2\pi$:

$$
x_{2} = X - 2π = \frac{7π}{6} - 2π =-\frac{5π}{6}
$$

So, the solutions are

$$
x=-\dfrac{5\pi}{6}, -\dfrac{\pi}{6}
$$

<a id="solving-a-trigonometric-equation-involving-sine-under-a-modified-domain"></a>
## Solving a Trigonometric Equation Involving Sine Under a Modified Domain

**Example:** Solve the equation $2\sin x - \sqrt{3}=0$ for $-180^{\circ} < x \leq 180^{\circ}$.

**Explanation**

First, we rearrange the equation, isolating $\sin x$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
2\sin x - \sqrt{3} &= 0 \\
2\sin x &= \sqrt{3} \\
\sin x &= \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we find the principal value:

$$
x = \arcsin (\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}) = 60^{∘}
$$

Since $60^{\circ}$ lies inside the required domain, our first solution is

$$
x_1 = 60^{\circ}
$$

Our first solution lies in the first quadrant and has a reference angle of

$$
\theta_R = 60^{\circ}
$$

Since $\sin{x} > 0$, the second solution must lie in the second quadrant, and it also has a reference angle

$$
\theta_R = 60^{\circ}
$$

![](<../Source/5.2.1. Elementary Trigonometric Equations Containing Sine/Images/2db14a4844cbf6c495543c6905847053.png>)

Since the second solution $x_2$ lies in the $2$nd quadrant, we can find it using the reference angle as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_{2} &= 180^{∘} - θ_{R} \\
&= 180^{∘} - 60^{∘} \\
&= 120^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the solutions are

$$
x = 60^\circ, 120^\circ
$$

## Solving a Trigonometric Equation Involving Sine with Quadrantal Angles

**Example:** Solve the equation $\sin{x} = 1$ for $-180^\circ < x \leq 180^\circ$.

**Explanation**

First, we find the principal value:

$$
x = \arcsin (1) = 90^{∘}
$$

Since $90^\circ$ lies inside the required domain, our first solution is

$$
x_1 = 90^\circ
$$

This solution is a quadrantal angle. To find the remaining solutions, we use our knowledge of the sine function. We know that the sine function has its maximum values at

$$
x = 90^\circ + n (360^\circ)
$$

Since the domain

$$
-180^\circ < x \leq 180^\circ
$$

is one period, and we have already found one maximum value in this period, we conclude that

$$
x = 90^\circ
$$

is the only solution.

<a id="equations-involving-the-sine-function-with-no-solutions"></a>
## Equations Involving the Sine Function With No Solutions

Can we find solutions to the equation

$$
0^{∘} \le x < 360^{∘}
$$

Remember that the sine function always satisfies

$$
-1 \leq \sin{x} \leq 1
$$

The number $2$ is outside of this range. So, there is no solution to the equation above.

In general, when a number $a$ is not in $[-1,1]$, there are no values of $x$ such that $\sin x = a$.
