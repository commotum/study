## Introduction

The $n$th-degree Taylor polynomial of a function $f(x)$ around the point $x=a$ is

$$
P_n(x) = f(a) + f'(a)(x-a) + \dfrac{f''(a)}{2!}(x-a)^2 + \cdots + \dfrac{f^{(n)}(a)}{n!}(x-a)^n
$$

In particular, the coefficient of the $k$th-degree term is

$$
\dfrac{f^{(k)}(a)}{k!}
$$

## Computing a Higher-Degree Taylor Polynomial Of a Given Function About a Given Point

**Example:** Let $f(x) = \sin{x}$. Calculate the fourth-degree Taylor polynomial for $f(x)$ about $x=\dfrac{\pi}{2}$.

**Explanation**

The fourth-degree Taylor polynomial of $f(x)$ about $x=a$ is given by

$$
P_4(x) = f(a) + f'(a)(x-a) + \dfrac{f''(a)}{2!}(x-a)^2 + \dfrac{f'''(a)}{3!}(x-a)^3 + \dfrac{f^{(4)}(a)}{4!}(x-a)^4
$$

Here, we have

$$
a=\dfrac{\pi}{2}
$$

so

$$
\begin{aligned}
P_{4}(x) &= f(\frac{π}{2}) + f^{′}(\frac{π}{2})(x - \frac{π}{2}) + (f^{″}(\frac{π}{2}))/(2!)(x - \frac{π}{2})^{2} \\
&= + (f^{‴}(\frac{π}{2}))/(3!)(x - \frac{π}{2})^{3} + (f^{(4)}(\frac{π}{2}))/(4!)(x - \frac{π}{2})^{4}
\end{aligned}
$$

So we need to work out

$$
f\left(\dfrac{\pi}{2}\right)
$$

$$
f'\left(\dfrac{\pi}{2}\right)
$$

$$
f''\left(\dfrac{\pi}{2}\right)
$$

$$
f'''\left(\dfrac{\pi}{2}\right)
$$

and

$$
f^{(4)}\left(\dfrac{\pi}{2}\right)
$$

as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\begin{bmatrix}f(x) &= \sin x & ⟹ & f(\frac{π}{2})|= 1 \\ f^{′}(x)|= \cos x & ⟹ & f^{′}(\frac{π}{2})|= 0 \\ f^{″}(x)|=-\sin x & ⟹ & f^{″}(\frac{π}{2})|=-1 \\ f^{‴}(x)|=-\cos x & ⟹ & f^{‴}(\frac{π}{2})|= 0 \\ f^{(4)}(x)|= \sin x & ⟹ & f^{(4)}(\frac{π}{2})|= 1\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, $P_4(x)$ is given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
P_{4}(x) &= 1 - \frac{1}{2!}(x - \frac{π}{2})^{2} + \frac{1}{4!}(x - \frac{π}{2})^{4} \\
&= 1 - \frac{1}{2}(x - \frac{π}{2})^{2} + \frac{1}{24}(x - \frac{π}{2})^{4}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Obtaining a Higher-Order Derivative Of a Function Given its Higher-Degree Taylor Polynomial

**Example:** The fourth-degree Taylor polynomial of $f(x)$ about $x=0$ is given by

$P_4(x) = 1+2x-3x^2+4x^3-5x^4$.

What is $f^{(4)}(0)$?

**Explanation**

The coefficient of $x^4$ is given by

$$
\dfrac{f^{(4)}(0)}{4!}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
(f^{(4)}(0))/(4!) &= -5 \\
(f^{(4)}(0))/(24) &= -5 \\
f^{(4)}(0) &= -120
\end{aligned}
$$
