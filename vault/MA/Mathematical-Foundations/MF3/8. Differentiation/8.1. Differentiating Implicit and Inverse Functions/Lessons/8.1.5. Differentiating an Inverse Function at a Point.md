## Introduction

Consider the following function:

$$
f(x)= 4x^2+x, \qquad x \gt 0
$$

The domain restriction $x > 0$ means that $f$ is invertible on its entire domain.

The point $P$ with coordinates $(2,18)$ lies on the curve $y = f(x)$. This means that the point $P'$ with coordinates $(18,2)$ lies on the curve of the inverse function

$$
y=f^{-1}(x)
$$

as shown below.

![](<../Source/8.1.5. Differentiating an Inverse Function at a Point/Images/70602360a39d20dfb6aa8080a847732f.png>)

How do we find

$$
\dfrac{\textrm d y}{\textrm d x}
$$

at the point $P'$?

For this, we will use the fact that the slope of the tangent to the curve $y=f^{-1}(x)$ at $P'$ is the *reciprocal* of the slope of the tangent to the curve $y=f(x)$ at $P$.

$$
\frac{dy}{dx} \mid _{P^{′}} = (1)/((\frac{dy}{dx} \mid _{P}))
$$

Calculating

$$
\dfrac{\textrm d y}{\textrm d x}
$$

at $P$ is straightforward. First, we differentiate $y=f(x)$. This gives

$$
\dfrac{\textrm{d}y}{\textrm{d}x} = 8x + 1
$$

and then plugging in $x=2$ gives

$$
\left.\dfrac{\textrm{d}y}{\textrm{d}x}\right|_P = 8(2)+1 = 17
$$

Finally, we take the reciprocal:

$$
\left.\dfrac{\textrm{d}y}{\textrm{d}x}\right|_{P'}= \dfrac{1}{17}
$$

We can summarize this process using the following formula:

$$
\left(f^{-1}\right)'(x) = \dfrac{1}{f'(f^{-1}(x))}
$$

To help remember this formula, note that it's okay to swap the derivative symbol (the prime) with the inverse symbol (the exponent of $-1$) as long as we take the reciprocal afterward.

## Calculating the Derivative of an Inverse Function From a Graph

**Example:** The figure above shows the graph of a function $y=f(x)$ and its inverse $y=f^{-1}(x)$. The point $A$ has coordinates $(2, 0)$ and lies on $y=f(x)$.
The point $B$ has coordinates $(0,2)$ and lies on $y=f^{-1}(x)$. If $f'(2)=-\dfrac{3}{2}$, what is $(f^{-1})'(0)$?

![](<../Source/8.1.5. Differentiating an Inverse Function at a Point/Images/d6a83b3149c17f55744d206f77f51649.png>)

**Explanation**

Since $B \,(0,2)$ lies on $f^{-1}$, we have

$$
f^{-1}(0) = 2
$$

We're also told that

$$
f'(2)=-\dfrac{3}{2}
$$

The formula for the derivative of the inverse function is

$$
\begin{aligned}
(f^{-1})^{′}(x) &= (1)/(f^{′}(f^{-1}(x)))
\end{aligned}
$$

Evaluating at $x=0$, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
(f^{-1})^{′}(0) &= (1)/(f^{′}(f^{-1}(0))) \\
&= (1)/(f^{′}(2)) \\
&= (1)/((-\frac{3}{2})) \\
&=-\frac{2}{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Calculating the Derivative of an Inverse Function

**Example:** Consider the function $f(x) = \dfrac{2-3x}{4+5x}$ and the point $P\left(-1,-5\right)$ that lies on $y=f(x)$. Given that $f(x)$ is invertible in the vicinity of $P$, determine the slope of $y=f^{-1}(x)$ at the point $\left(-5,-1\right)$.

**Explanation**

We start by calculating the derivative of $f$ at the point $(-1, -5)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
f(x) &= \frac{2 - 3x}{4 + 5x} \\
f^{′}(x) &= - (22)/((4 + 5x)^{2}) \\
f^{′}(-1) &= -22
\end{aligned}
$$

Since $(-1, -5)$ lies on $y=f(x)$, we know that the corresponding point $(-5, -1)$ lies on

$$
y=f^{-1}(x)
$$

Furthermore, the slopes of the tangents to the two respective points are reciprocals. Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
(f^{-1})^{′}(-5) &= (1)/(f^{′}(f^{-1}(-5))) \\
&= (1)/(f^{′}(-1)) \\
&= (1)/((-22)) \\
&=-\frac{1}{22}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Calculating Derivatives Using Tables

The following table gives the values of functions $f(x)$ and $f'(x)$ at some points.

| **$x$** | $f(x)$ | $f'(x)$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| $1$ | $2$ | $3$ |
| $2$ | $1$ | $12$ |

Let's use this data to find $(f^{-1})'(1)$.

Recall that the formula for the derivative of the inverse is

$$
(f^{-1})'(x) = \dfrac{1}{f'(f^{-1}(x))}
$$

Substituting $x=1$, we have

$$
(f^{-1})'(1) = \dfrac{1}{f'(f^{-1}(1))}
$$

From the table, we see that

$$
f(2)=1
$$

, hence $f^{-1}(1)=2$, and also $f'(2)=12$. Therefore

$$
\begin{aligned}
(f^{-1})^{′}(1) &= (1)/(f^{′}(f^{-1}(1))) \\
&= (1)/(f^{′}(2)) \\
&= \frac{1}{12}
\end{aligned}
$$

We can also use the rule for calculating derivatives of inverse functions in conjunction with other rules of differentiation. Let's see an example.

## Calculating the Derivative of an Inverse Function Using a Table

**Example:** The following table gives the values of $f(x), f'(x),g(x)$, and $g'(x)$ at some points. Find $(f^{-1}\cdot g)'(1)$ of the product of functions.

| **$x$** | $f(x)$ | $f'(x)$ | $g(x)$ | $g'(x)$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $1$ | $-1$ | $2$ | $-3$ | $1$ |
| $2$ | $1$ | $-1$ | $3$ | $5$ |

**Explanation**

The formula for the derivative of the inverse $f^{-1}(x)$ is

$$
(f^{-1})'(x) = \dfrac{1}{f'(f^{-1}(x))}
$$

Using this, and the product rule, the derivative of the function $f^{-1}(x)g(x)$ is

$$
(f^{-1}\cdot g)'(x)=\dfrac{g(x)}{f'(f^{-1}(x))}+g'(x)f^{-1}(x)
$$

Substituting $x=1$, we have

$$
(f^{-1}\cdot g)'(1) =\dfrac{g(1)}{f'(f^{-1}(1))}+g'(1)f^{-1}(1)
$$

From the table, we obtain

$$
f^{-1}(1)=2
$$

$g(1)=-3$, $g'(1)=1$, and $f'(2)=-1$. Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
(f^{-1} \cdot g)^{′}(1) &= (g(1))/(f^{′}(f^{-1}(1))) + g^{′}(1)f^{-1}(1) \\
&= (-3)/(f^{′}(2)) + 1 \cdot 2 \\
&= \frac{-3}{-1} + 2 \\
&= 5
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding a Derivative Using Properties of Inverse Functions

**Example:** Consider the function $f(x) =3e^{x}-1$. Given that $y = f(x)$ is invertible throughout its domain, determine $(f^{-1})'(2)$.

**Explanation**

To determine $(f^{-1})'(2)$, we use the following formula:

$$
\left(f^{-1}\right)'(2) = \dfrac{1}{f'(f^{-1}(2))}
$$

First, we calculate the value of $f^{-1}(2)$. To do this, we solve the equation

$$
f^{-1}(2) = x
$$

This gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
f^{-1}(2) &= x \\
f(x) &= 2 \\
3e^{x} - 1 &= 2 \\
3e^{x} &= 3 \\
e^{x} &= 1 \\
x &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
f^{-1}(2) = 0
$$

Now, we calculate $f'(0)$, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
f(x) &= 3e^{x} - 1 \\
f^{′}(x) &= 3e^{x} \\
f^{′}(0) &= 3e^{0} \\
f^{′}(0) &= 3
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
(f^{-1})^{′}(2) &= (1)/(f^{′}(f^{-1}(2))) \\
&= (1)/(f^{′}(0)) \\
&= \frac{1}{3}
\end{aligned}
$$
