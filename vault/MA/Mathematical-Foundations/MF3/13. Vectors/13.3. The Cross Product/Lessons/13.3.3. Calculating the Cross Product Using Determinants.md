## Introduction

Suppose we have two vectors $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$, given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
a &= a_{1}i + a_{2}j + a_{3}k \\
b &= b_{1}i + b_{2}j + b_{3}k
\end{aligned}
$$

The cross product $\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf b$ can be calculated using the following determinant:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}i & j & k \\ a_{1} & a_{2} & a_{3} \\ b_{1} & b_{2} & b_{3}\end{bmatrix}
$$

For instance, suppose that we have the two vectors

$$
\mathbf{a} = \langle 2,-1,5 \rangle
$$

,

$$
\mathbf{b} = \langle -1,4,2 \rangle
$$

. We can find the cross product of $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$ as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
a \cdot b &= \begin{vmatrix}i & j & k \\ 2 & -1 & 5 \\ -1 & 4 & 2\end{vmatrix} \\
&=\begin{vmatrix}-1 & 5 \\ 4 & 2\end{vmatrix}i -\begin{vmatrix}2 & 5 \\ -1 & 2\end{vmatrix}j +\begin{vmatrix}2 & -1 \\ -1 & 4\end{vmatrix}k \\
&= ((-1) \cdot 2 - 5 \cdot 4)i - (2 \cdot 2 - 5 \cdot (-1))j + (2 \cdot 4 - (-1) \cdot (-1))k \\
&=-22i - 9j + 7k \\
&= ⟨ - 22,-9, 7⟩
\end{aligned}
$$

## Calculating the Cross Product Using Determinants

**Example:** Let $\mathbf{a}=\langle 3,-4,1 \rangle$ and $\mathbf{b}=\langle -7,0,2 \rangle$. Find $\mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{b}$.

**Explanation**

Using the determinant formula, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
a \cdot b &= \begin{vmatrix}i & j & k \\ 3 & -4 & 1 \\ -7 & 0 & 2\end{vmatrix} \\
&=\begin{vmatrix}-4 & 1 \\ 0 & 2\end{vmatrix}i -\begin{vmatrix}3 & 1 \\ -7 & 2\end{vmatrix}j +\begin{vmatrix}3 & -4 \\ -7 & 0\end{vmatrix}k \\
&= ((-4) \cdot 2 - 1 \cdot 0)i - (3 \cdot 2 - 1 \cdot (-7))j + (3 \cdot 0 - (-4) \cdot (-7))k \\
&=-8i - 13j - 28k \\
&= ⟨ - 8,-13,-28⟩
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="solving-for-an-unknown-in-an-equation-involving-the-cross-product"></a>
## Solving for an Unknown in an Equation Involving the Cross Product

**Example:** Let $\mathbf{a}=\langle 3, 1, 2x \rangle$, $\mathbf{b}=\langle 0,1,x \rangle$, and $\mid \,\mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{b}\, \mid = \sqrt{19}$. Find $x$.

**Explanation**

Using the determinant formula, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
a \cdot b &= \begin{vmatrix}i & j & k \\ 3 & 1 & 2x \\ 0 & 1 & x\end{vmatrix} \\
&=\begin{vmatrix}1 & 2x \\ 1 & x\end{vmatrix}i -\begin{vmatrix}3 & 2x \\ 0 & x\end{vmatrix}j +\begin{vmatrix}3 & 1 \\ 0 & 1\end{vmatrix}k \\
&=-xi - 3xj + 3k \\
&= ⟨ - x,-3x, 3⟩
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, computing the magnitude, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mid a \cdot b \mid &= \sqrt{(-x)^{2} + (-3x)^{2} + 3^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{10x^{2} + 9}
\end{aligned}
$$

Lastly, we solve for $x$ the following equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mid a \cdot b &= \sqrt{19} \\
&= \sqrt{10x^{2} + 9} = \sqrt{19} \\
&= 10x^{2} + 9 = 19 \\
&= 10x^{2} = 10 \\
&= x^{2} = 1
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the solutions are $x= \pm 1$.

<a id="proof-of-the-formula-for-the-cross-product-using-the-determinant"></a>
## Proof of the Formula for the Cross Product Using the Determinant

We have been using the following formula to compute the cross product of two vectors:

$$
\begin{aligned}
a \cdot b &= \begin{vmatrix}i & j & k \\ a_{1} & a_{2} & a_{3} \\ b_{1} & b_{2} & b_{3}\end{vmatrix} \\
&=\begin{vmatrix}a_{2} & a_{3} \\ b_{2} & b_{3}\end{vmatrix}i -\begin{vmatrix}a_{1} & a_{3} \\ b_{1} & b_{3}\end{vmatrix}j +\begin{vmatrix}a_{1} & a_{2} \\ b_{1} & b_{2}\end{vmatrix}k \\
&= (a_{2}b_{3} - a_{3}b_{2})i - (a_{1}b_{3} - a_{3}b_{1})j + (a_{1}b_{2} - a_{2}b_{1})k
\end{aligned}
$$

But where does this formula come from? Let's find out.

Suppose we want to find the cross product $\mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{b}$ for

$$
\mathbf{a}=a_1\mathbf{i}+a_2\mathbf{j}+a_3\mathbf{k}
$$

and

$$
\mathbf{b}=b_1\mathbf{i}+b_2\mathbf{j}+b_3\mathbf{k}
$$

Using properties of the cross product, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
a \cdot b &= (a_{1}i + a_{2}j + a_{3}k) \cdot (b_{1}i + b_{2}j + b_{3}k) \\
&= (a_{1} \cdot b_{1})i \cdot i + (a_{1} \cdot b_{2})i \cdot j + (a_{1} \cdot b_{3})i \cdot k \\
&= + (a_{2} \cdot b_{1})j \cdot i + (a_{2} \cdot b_{2})j \cdot j + (a_{2} \cdot b_{3})j \cdot k \\
&= + (a_{3} \cdot b_{1})k \cdot i + (a_{3} \cdot b_{2})k \cdot j + (a_{3} \cdot b_{3})k \cdot k
\end{aligned}
$$

Since the cross product of two parallel (collinear) vectors is equal to $\mathbf 0$, we have

$$
\mathbf{i}\times\mathbf{i} = \mathbf{j}\times\mathbf{j} = \mathbf{k}\times\mathbf{k} = \mathbf 0
$$

Also, using the definition of the cross product we obtain

$$
\begin{bmatrix}i \cdot j = k, & j \cdot i =-k, \\ j \cdot k = i, & k \cdot j =-i, \\ k \cdot i = j, & i \cdot k =-j\end{bmatrix}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
a \cdot b &= (a_{1} \cdot b_{1})i \cdot i_(⏟)_(= 0) + (a_{1} \cdot b_{2})i \cdot j_(⏟)_(= k) + (a_{1} \cdot b_{3})i \cdot k_(⏟)_(=-j) \\
&= + (a_{2} \cdot b_{1})j \cdot i_(⏟)_(=-k) + (a_{2} \cdot b_{2})j \cdot j_(⏟)_(= 0) + (a_{2} \cdot b_{3})j \cdot k_(⏟)_(= i) \\
&= + (a_{3} \cdot b_{1})k \cdot i_(⏟)_(= j) + (a_{3} \cdot b_{2})k \cdot j_(⏟)_(=-i) + (a_{3} \cdot b_{3})k \cdot k_(⏟)_(= 0) \\
&= (a_{2}b_{3} - a_{3}b_{2})i - (a_{1}b_{3} - a_{3}b_{1})j + (a_{1}b_{2} - a_{2}b_{1})k
\end{aligned}
$$

The expression above is equal to the formula given for the cross product of two general vectors using the determinant.
