## Introduction

The **dot product** (or **scalar product**) of two vectors $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$ is defined as

$$
\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b} = \begin{vmatrix}\,\mathbf{a}\, & \cdot & \,\mathbf{b}\,\end{vmatrix} \cdot \cos\theta
$$

where $\theta$ is the angle formed between the vectors when their tails are placed at the same point.

![](<../Source/13.2.1. Calculating the Dot Product Using Angle and Magnitude/Images/9a97c3b428ac307511935a4c41a4fce0.png>)

For example, suppose we know that

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mid \mathbf{a} &= 2
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mid \mathbf{b} &= 4
\end{aligned}
$$

and

$$
\theta = \dfrac{2\pi}{3}
$$

We can now define the **dot product** (or **scalar product**) of $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$ as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
a \cdot b &= \begin{vmatrix}a & \cdot & b\end{vmatrix}\cdot \cos θ \\
&= 2 \cdot 4 \cdot \cos (\frac{2π}{3}) \\
&= 2 \cdot 4 \cdot (-\frac{1}{2}) \\
&=-4
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="calculating-the-dot-product-of-two-vectors-given-their-magnitudes-and-angle"></a>
## Calculating the Dot Product of Two Vectors Given Their Magnitudes and Angle

**Example:** For two vectors $\mathbf a$ and $\mathbf b$, we have $\mid \mathbf{a}|=45$, $\mid \mathbf{b}|=12$, and the angle between the vectors is $\theta=77^\circ$. Find $\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b}$ rounded to $3$ decimal places.

**Explanation**

Using the formula for the dot product, we obtain:

$$
\begin{aligned}
a \cdot b &= \begin{vmatrix}a & \cdot & b\end{vmatrix}\cdot \cos θ \\
&= 45 \cdot 12 \cdot \cos 77^{∘} \\
&\approx 45 \cdot 12 \cdot 0.224951 \\
&\approx 121.474
\end{aligned}
$$

## Calculating the Dot Product of Two Vectors Given Their Magnitudes and Directions

**Example:** Suppose that the vectors $\mathbf a$ and $\mathbf b$ are two vectors defined in the $x$-$y$ plane. The vector $\mathbf{a}$ makes a counterclockwise angle of $127^\circ$ with the positive $x$-axis and has $\mid \mathbf{a}|=4$, while the vector $\mathbf{b}$ makes a clockwise angle of $23^\circ$ with the positive $x$-axis and has $\mid \mathbf{b}|=3$. Find $\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b}$.

**Explanation**

If $\mathbf{a}$ makes an angle of $127^\circ$ with the positive $x$-axis and $\mathbf{b}$ makes an angle of $-23^\circ$ with the positive $x$-axis, then the angle between the vectors is

$$
\begin{aligned}
θ &= \mid 127^{∘} - (-23^{∘})|= 150^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

![](<../Source/13.2.1. Calculating the Dot Product Using Angle and Magnitude/Images/89fc54462c93d2e250ce6b87a97bbc8b.png>)

Now, using the formula for the dot product, we obtain:

$$
\begin{aligned}
a \cdot b &= \begin{vmatrix}a & \cdot & b\end{vmatrix}\cdot \cos θ \\
&= 4 \cdot 3 \cdot \cos 150^{∘} \\
&= 4 \cdot 3 \cdot (-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}) \\
&=-6\sqrt{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="important-properties"></a>
## Important Properties

The dot product satisfies many properties that we would normally expect multiplication to satisfy. Given the vectors $\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b},\mathbf{c}$ and any scalar $\lambda$, the following properties hold.

- The dot product distributes over addition:
$(a + b) \cdot c|= a \cdot c + b \cdot c; c \cdot (a + b)|= c \cdot a + c \cdot b$
- The dot product is associative with respect to scalar multiplication:
$(λa) \cdot b|= λ(a \cdot b); a \cdot (λb)|= λ(a \cdot b)$
- The dot product is commutative:
$\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b} = \mathbf{b} \cdot \mathbf{a}$

---

<a id="calculating-dot-product-expressions-using-the-properties-of-the-dot-product"></a>
## Calculating Dot Product Expressions Using the Properties of the Dot Product

**Example:** Find $(2\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b}) \cdot \mathbf{c}$, if $\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{c} = 2$ and $\mathbf{b} \cdot \mathbf{c} = -5$.

**Explanation**

Using the properties of the dot product, we have:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(2a - b) \cdot c &= (2a) \cdot c - b \cdot c \\
&= 2(a \cdot c) - b \cdot c \\
&= 2 \cdot 2 - (-5) \\
&= 9
\end{aligned}
$$

## The Dot Product of Perpendicular Vectors

Two nonzero vectors $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$ are **perpendicular** if the angle between them is

$$
\theta=90^\circ
$$

We can denote that two vectors are perpendicular using the symbol $\perp$, as follows:

$$
\mathbf{a} \perp \mathbf{b}
$$

In general, the dot product of two perpendicular vectors is $0$. If $\mathbf{a} \perp \mathbf{b}$, and

$$
\theta = 90^\circ
$$

then

$$
\begin{aligned}
a \cdot b &= \begin{vmatrix}a & \cdot & b & \cdot \cos 90^{∘} \\ = & a & \cdot & b\end{vmatrix}\cdot 0 \\
&= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

The converse is also true. If $\mathbf a$ and $\mathbf b$ are nonzero vectors, then

$$
\mathbf{a} \perp \mathbf{b} \quad\Leftrightarrow\quad \mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b} = 0
$$

---

<a id="calculating-the-dot-product-of-perpendicular-vectors"></a>
## Calculating the Dot Product of Perpendicular Vectors

**Example:** Let $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$ be two vectors with magnitudes $\mid \mathbf{a}|=13\sqrt{17}$ and $\mid \mathbf{b}|=146$, and let the angle between the vectors be $\theta=\dfrac{\pi}{2}$. Find $\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b}$.

**Explanation**

Since the angle between the vectors is

$$
\theta = \dfrac\pi 2
$$

the vectors are perpendicular and thus

$$
\mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b} = 0
$$

We can confirm this. Using the formula for the dot product, we obtain:

$$
\begin{aligned}
a \cdot b &= \begin{vmatrix}a & \cdot & b\end{vmatrix}\cdot \cos θ \\
&= 13\sqrt{17} \cdot 146 \cdot \cos (\frac{π}{2}) \\
&= 13\sqrt{17} \cdot 146 \cdot 0 \\
&= 0
\end{aligned}
$$
