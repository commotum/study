## Introduction

If the $2\times 2$ matrix $A$ is invertible, then we can find a solution to the system

$$
A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b}
$$

by "pre-multiplying" both sides of the equation by $A^{-1}$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
Ax &= b \\
A^{-1}Ax &= A^{-1}b \\
I_{2}x &= A^{-1}b \\
x &= A^{-1}b
\end{aligned}
$$

So, to find the solution of

$$
A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b}
$$

we simply calculate

$$
\mathbf{x}=A^{-1}\mathbf{b}
$$

For instance, to find the solution to the system of equations
$\begin{bmatrix}1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1\end{bmatrix}$
we first calculate the inverse of the coefficient matrix $\begin{bmatrix}1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1\end{bmatrix}$
This gives
$\begin{bmatrix}1 & -1 \\ 0 & 1\end{bmatrix}$

Then, the solution to this system is

$$
\begin{aligned}
x &= A^{-1}b \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}1 & -1 \\ 0 & 1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}5 \\ 2\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}3 \\ 2\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

So the solution is $x=3$ and $y=2$.

## Solving a 2x2 System of Equations Using an Inverse Matrix

**Example:** Find a solution to the system of linear equations

${2x + y = 2; 4x + 3y = 6$.

**Explanation**

This system can be written as

$$
\begin{bmatrix}2 & 1 \\ 4 & 3\end{bmatrix}
$$

To find the solution, we need to calculate the inverse of the coefficient matrix $\begin{bmatrix}2 & 1 \\ 4 & 3\end{bmatrix}$ This gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
A^{-1} &= \frac{1}{3 \cdot 2 - 4 \cdot 1}\begin{bmatrix}3 & -1 \\ -4 & 2\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \frac{1}{2}\begin{bmatrix}3 & -1 \\ -4 & 2\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

We can now find the solution to this system:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x &= A^{-1}b \\
&= \frac{1}{2}\begin{bmatrix}3 & -1 \\ -4 & 2\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}2 \\ 6\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \frac{1}{2}\begin{bmatrix}0 \\ 4\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}0 \\ 2\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

So the solution is $x=0$ and $y=2$.

## Solving a 2x2 System of Equations Using an Inverse Matrix With Reorganization

**Example:** Solve the system of linear equations

${2x - 3y = 5; 6y - 5x =-8$.

**Explanation**

First, we reorganize the equations so that they are in the right order, to get

$$
{2x - 3y = 5; - 5x + 6y =-8
$$

Now, we can represent this system of equations in the form

$$
A\mathbf{x} =\mathbf{b}
$$

$$
\begin{bmatrix}2 & -3 \\ -5 & 6\end{bmatrix}
$$

To find the solution, we need to calculate the inverse of the coefficient matrix $\begin{bmatrix}2 & -3 \\ -5 & 6\end{bmatrix}$ This gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
A^{-1} &= \frac{1}{6 \cdot 2 - 3 \cdot 5}\begin{bmatrix}6 & 3 \\ 5 & 2\end{bmatrix} \\
&=-\frac{1}{3}\begin{bmatrix}6 & 3 \\ 5 & 2\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

We can now find the solution to this system:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x &= A^{-1}b \\
&=-\frac{1}{3}\begin{bmatrix}6 & 3 \\ 5 & 2\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}5 \\ -8\end{bmatrix} \\
&=-\frac{1}{3}\begin{bmatrix}6 \\ 9\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}-2 \\ -3\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the solution is $x=-2$ and $y=-3$.

## Systems of Equations Where the Coefficient Matrix is Singular

Consider the system of equations

$$
{2x + y = 3; 6x + 3y = 9
$$

Can we use the matrix method of

$$
\mathbf{x} = A^{-1}\mathbf{b}
$$

to find a solution to this system?

This system can be represented in the form

$$
A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{b}
$$

as
$\begin{bmatrix}2 & 1 \\ 6 & 3\end{bmatrix}$

To use this matrix method, we need to find $A^{-1}$. However

$$
\det (A) = 2\cdot 3 - 1\cdot 6 =0
$$

Therefore, $A$ is not invertible. So we cannot use the matrix method to find a solution to the system.

In general, if the coefficient matrix $A$ is not invertible, there is no unique solution. This means that either

- a solution may not exist, or
- there may be infinitely many solutions.
