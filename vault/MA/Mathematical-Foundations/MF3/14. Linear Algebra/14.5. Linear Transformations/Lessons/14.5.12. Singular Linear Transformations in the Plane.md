## Introduction

Consider the triangle below with vertices $(0,0)$, $(2,1)$ and $(2,3)$.

![](<../Source/14.5.12. Singular Linear Transformations in the Plane/Images/99fccef4611b8fcb6b3961cf37d73e71.png>)

What is the image of this triangle under the action of the transformation $\mathbf{T}$ represented by the matrix
$\begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0\end{bmatrix}$

To find the image of the triangle under the action of $\mathbf T$, we first create a matrix $X$ containing all vertices of our triangle written as columns:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}0 & 2 & 2 \\ 0 & 1 & 3\end{bmatrix}
$$

Now, we compute the image of $X$ under the action of $\mathbf T$ by calculating the matrix product $TX$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
TX &= \begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}0 & 2 & 2 \\ 0 & 1 & 3\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}0 & 2 & 2 \\ 0 & 0 & 0\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the coordinates of the image's vertices are $(0,0)$, $(2,0)$, $(2,0)$. Notice that these points lie on the same line. Two of them even coincide!

So, the image of our triangle under the action of $\mathbf T$ is the following line segment:

![](<../Source/14.5.12. Singular Linear Transformations in the Plane/Images/942c0ed880bd56c0318d7fbf0588981e.png>)

Note the following:

- Recall that for any linear transformation $\mathbf T$ and region $D$, we have
$\text{Area}(\mathbf{T}(D)) = \mid \det(T) \mid \cdot \text{Area}(D)$.
Since $\mid \det(T) \mid = 0$ in this case, any object applied to $\mathbf T$ collapses to an object with zero area.
- Also, the fact that $\mid \det(T) \mid = 0$ tells us that $\mathbf T$ has no inverse transformation. Therefore, it is impossible to recover the original triangle knowing only its image and the matrix $T$.

## Calculating the Coordinates of an Image Formed by a Singular Transformation

**Example:** Consider the linear transformation $\mathbf T$ with matrix representation $T$, given by
$\begin{bmatrix}2 & 0 \\ 0 & 0\end{bmatrix}$

A triangle $S$ has vertices at $(-1,2), (0,1)$ and $(1,1)$. Which of the following are coordinates of the vertices of $S'$, the image of $S$ under the action of $\mathbf T$?

1. $(-2,0)$
2. $(0,0)$
3. $(2,0)$

**Explanation**

To find the image of $S$ under the action of $\mathbf T$, we first create a matrix $X$ containing all of the vertices of $S$:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}-1 & 0 & 1 \\ 2 & 1 & 1\end{bmatrix}
$$

Now, we compute the image of $X$ under $\mathbf T$ by calculating the matrix product $TX$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
TX &= \begin{bmatrix}2 & 0 \\ 0 & 0\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}-1 & 0 & 1 \\ 2 & 1 & 1\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}-2 & 0 & 2 \\ 0 & 0 & 0\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the coordinates of the respective vertices of $S'$ are $(-2,0)$, $(0,0)$, and $(2,0)$.

So, the correct answer is "I, II, and III."

## Determining the Image of an Object Formed by a Singular Transformation

**Example:** Consider the linear transformation $\mathbf T$ with matrix representation $T$, given by
$\begin{bmatrix}3 & -2 \\ -6 & 4\end{bmatrix}$ Find the image of the triangle shown below under the action of $\mathbf T$.

![](<../Source/14.5.12. Singular Linear Transformations in the Plane/Images/71b4c00c3f3f2354e52c4ddadc41fdd1.png>)

**Explanation**

To find the image of the triangle under the action of $\mathbf T$, we first create a matrix $X$ containing all of the vertices of the triangle:

$\begin{bmatrix}1 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 1\end{bmatrix}$
Now, we compute the image of $X$ under the action of $\mathbf T$ by calculating the matrix product $TX$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
TX &= \begin{bmatrix}3 & -2 \\ -6 & 4\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 1\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}3 & 1 & -2 \\ -6 & -2 & 4\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the coordinates of the image's vertices are $(3,-6), (1,-2)$, and $(-2,4)$. Notice that all three points lie on the same line.

![](<../Source/14.5.12. Singular Linear Transformations in the Plane/Images/90a937e06df1fa3177ee4608a88110e5.png>)

<a id="image-types-formed-by-linear-transformations"></a>
## Image Types Formed by Linear Transformations

Given a linear transformation $\mathbf T$ with an associated $2\times 2$ matrix $T$, there are three possibilities regarding the type of object formed under the action of $\mathbf{T}$ by a two-dimensional preimage.

To demonstrate, consider the three-sided polygon $\mathcal P$ with vertices $(0,1)$, $(2,0)$ and $(2,1)$, as shown below.

![](<../Source/14.5.12. Singular Linear Transformations in the Plane/Images/ae508c15cb767d68db89d7463f09a17f.png>)

Let's describe the three possible types of object formed by the image of $\mathcal P{:}$

- Case where $T$ is non-singular:Let $\mathbf T$ be the linear transformation with the following matrix representation:
$\begin{bmatrix}0 & 1 \\ 1 & 2\end{bmatrix}$
We can easily check that $T$ is non-singular by computing its determinant:
$det(T)|=\begin{vmatrix}0 & 1 \\ 1 & 2\end{vmatrix}; = 2 \cdot 0 - 1 \cdot 1; =-1$
To find the image of $\mathcal P$ under the action of $\mathbf T$, we create a matrix $X$ containing all of the vertices of $\mathcal P$ and then compute the product $TX{:}$
$TX|= \begin{bmatrix}0 & 1 \\ 1 & 2\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}0 & 2 & 2 \\ 1 & 0 & 1\end{bmatrix}; = \begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 & 1 \\ 2 & 2 & 4\end{bmatrix}$
Therefore, the image of $\mathcal P$ under the action of $\mathbf{T}$ is a triangle.![](<../Source/14.5.12. Singular Linear Transformations in the Plane/Images/b95ce660f76f3cb28bf70c9af98ae9fe.png>)

- Case where $\mathbf{T}$ is singular and has at least one nonzero entry:Let $\mathbf T$ be the linear transformation with the following matrix representation:
$\begin{bmatrix}2 & 6 \\ 1 & 3\end{bmatrix}$
We can easily check that $T$ is singular by computing its determinant:
$det(T)|=\begin{vmatrix}2 & 6 \\ 1 & 3\end{vmatrix}; = 2 \cdot 3 - 6 \cdot 1; = 0$
Now, computing the image of $\mathcal P$ under the action of $\mathbf T$, we get
$TX|= \begin{bmatrix}2 & 6 \\ 1 & 3\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}0 & 2 & 2 \\ 1 & 0 & 1\end{bmatrix}; = \begin{bmatrix}6 & 4 & 10 \\ 3 & 2 & 5\end{bmatrix}$.
Consequently, the image of $\mathcal P$ under the action of $\mathbf{T}$ is a line segment.![](<../Source/14.5.12. Singular Linear Transformations in the Plane/Images/2de537c0c5f9e5bf868067dcd8dcbdb7.png>)
- Case where $T$ is the zero matrix:Let $\mathbf T$ be the linear transformation represented by the zero matrix:
$\begin{bmatrix}0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0\end{bmatrix}$
Clearly, $T$ is singular. Now, computing the image of $\mathcal P$ under the action of $\mathbf T$, we get
$TX|= \begin{bmatrix}0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}0 & 2 & 2 \\ 1 & 0 & 1\end{bmatrix}; = \begin{bmatrix}0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0\end{bmatrix}$.
Therefore, the coordinates of the image's vertices are all $(0,0)$, the origin $O$ of the coordinate system.![](<../Source/14.5.12. Singular Linear Transformations in the Plane/Images/9b0cfda3c64dd9acf06c0c9b5ceb6c0e.png>)

<a id="a-summary-of-image-types"></a>
## A Summary of Image Types

In general, for any linear transformation $\mathbf{T}$ with associated $2 \times 2$ matrix $T$, we have one of the following three possibilities:

- If $\mathbf{T}$ is non-singular, i.e., $\det(T) \neq 0$, then the image of a two-dimensional object is a two-dimensional object. In particular, the image of a polygon with $n$ vertices is a polygon with $n$ vertices.
- If $\mathbf{T}$ is singular, i.e., $\det(T) = 0$, we have two additional subcases:If $T$ has at least one nonzero entry, then the image of a two-dimensional object is a one-dimensional line segment.If $T$ is the zero matrix, then the image of a two-dimensional object is the point $(0,0)$, the origin of the coordinate system.

## Identifying the Type of Image Formed by a Linear Transformation

**Example:** Consider the linear transformation $\mathbf T$ with matrix representation $T$, given by
$\begin{bmatrix}0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0\end{bmatrix}$
What would be the image of the triangle shown above under the action of $\mathbf{T}$?

![](<../Source/14.5.12. Singular Linear Transformations in the Plane/Images/66b3a70a542c03540cf219bdce0d024d.png>)

**Explanation**

Recall that for any linear transformation $\mathbf{T}$ with associated $2 \times 2$ matrix $T$, we have one of the following three possibilities:

- If $\mathbf{T}$ is non-singular, i.e., $\det(T) \neq 0$, then the image of a two-dimensional object is a two-dimensional object. In particular, the image of a polygon with $n$ vertices is a polygon with $n$ vertices.
- If $\mathbf{T}$ is singular, i.e., $\det(T) = 0$, we have two additional subcases:If $T$ has at least one nonzero entry, then the image of a two-dimensional object is a one-dimensional line segment.If $T$ is the zero matrix, then the image of a two-dimensional object is the point $(0,0)$, the origin of the coordinate system.

Since $T$ is the zero matrix, the image of our object under the action of $\mathbf{T}$ is the origin.
