## Introduction

When multiplying two matrices in general, we apply the same approach as usual:

1. First, we verify that the two matrices are conformable for multiplication.
2. Then, we multiply each row in the first matrix by each column in the second.

For example, let's find $AB$, where
$\begin{bmatrix}-2 & 2 & 1 \\ 1 & -1 & 3\end{bmatrix}$
and
$\begin{bmatrix}-5 & 2 \\ 2 & -2 \\ -4 & 1\end{bmatrix}$

First, we verify the conformability for multiplication:

$$
\underset{\bbox[lightgray, 2pt]{2} \times \color{green}{\fbox{3}}}{A} \underset{\color{green}\checkmark}{\cdot} \underset{{\color{green}\fbox{3}} \times \bbox[lightgray, 2pt]{2}}{B} = \underset{\bbox[lightgray, 2pt]{2} \times \bbox[lightgray, 2pt]{2}}{C}
$$

So the matrices are conformable for multiplication, and the product is a $2 \times 2$ matrix. Let's set up the multiplication:

$$
\eqalign {\underbrace{\left\lbrack \matrix {-2 & 2 & 1 \ 1 & -1 & 3} \right\rbrack}_{A} \underbrace{\left\lbrack \matrix {-5 & 2 \ 2 & -2 \ -4 & 1} \right\rbrack}_{B} &= \underbrace{\left\lbrack \matrix {\ast & \ast \ \ast & \ast} \right\rbrack}_{C}}
$$

Now, we calculate the entries of the product matrix (shown as $\ast$'s in the equation above).

To find $c_{11}$, the entry at the intersection of the $1$st row and $1$st column, we calculate the dot product of the $1$st row of $A$ and the $1$st column of $B$:

$$
\eqalign {\left\lbrack \matrix {\color{blue}-2 & \color{blue}2 & \color{blue}1 \ \color{lightgray}1 & \color{lightgray}-1 & \color{lightgray}3} \right\rbrack \left\lbrack \matrix {\color{red}-5 & \color{lightgray}2 \ \color{red}2 & \color{lightgray}-2 \ \color{red}-4 & \color{lightgray}1} \right\rbrack % &= % \left\lbrack % \matrix {% ({\color{blue}{-2}}) \cdot ({\color{red}{-5}}) + {\color{blue}{2}} \cdot {\color{red}{2}} + {\color{blue}{1}} \cdot ({\color{red}{-4}}) & \ast \ % \ast & \ast %} % \right\rbrack = \left\lbrack \matrix {\bbox[lightgray, 2pt]{10} & \ast \ \ast & \ast} \right\rbrack, && \quad \bbox[lightgray, 2pt]{10} = ({\color{blue}{-2}}) \cdot ({\color{red}{-5}}) + {\color{blue}{2}} \cdot {\color{red}{2}} + {\color{blue}{1}} \cdot ({\color{red}{-4}})}
$$

To find $c_{12}$, we calculate the dot product of the $1$st row of $A$ and the $2$nd column of $B$:

$$
\eqalign {\left\lbrack \matrix {\color{blue}-2 & \color{blue}2 & \color{blue}1 \ \color{lightgray}1 & \color{lightgray}-1 & \color{lightgray}3} \right\rbrack \left\lbrack \matrix {\color{lightgray}-5 & \color{red}2 \ \color{lightgray}2 & \color{red}-2 \ \color{lightgray}-4 & \color{red}1} \right\rbrack % &= % \left\lbrack % \matrix {% 10 & ({\color{blue}{-2}}) \cdot {\color{red}{2}} + {\color{blue}{2}} \cdot ({\color{red}{-2}}) + {\color{blue}{1}} \cdot {\color{red}{1}} \ % \ast & \ast %} % \right\rbrack = \left\lbrack \matrix {10 & \bbox[lightgray, 2pt]{-7} \ \ast & \ast} \right\rbrack, && \quad \bbox[lightgray, 2pt]{-7} = ({\color{blue}{-2}}) \cdot {\color{red}{2}} + {\color{blue}{2}} \cdot ({\color{red}{-2}}) + {\color{blue}{1}} \cdot {\color{red}{1}}}
$$

To find $c_{21}$, we calculate the dot product of the $2$nd row of $A$ and the $1$st column of $B$:

$$
\eqalign {\left\lbrack \matrix {\color{lightgray}-2 & \color{lightgray}2 & \color{lightgray}1 \ \color{blue}1 & \color{blue}-1 & \color{blue}3} \right\rbrack \left\lbrack \matrix {\color{red}-5 & \color{lightgray}2 \ \color{red}2 & \color{lightgray}-2 \ \color{red}-4 & \color{lightgray}1} \right\rbrack % &= % \left\lbrack % \matrix {% 10 & -7 \ % {\color{blue}{1}} \cdot ({\color{red}{-5}}) + ({\color{blue}{-1}}) \cdot {\color{red}{2}} + {\color{blue}{3}} \cdot ({\color{red}{-4}}) & \ast %} % \right\rbrack = \left\lbrack \matrix {10 & -7 \ \bbox[lightgray, 2pt]{-19} & \ast} \right\rbrack, && \quad \bbox[lightgray, 2pt]{-19} = {\color{blue}{1}} \cdot ({\color{red}{-5}}) + ({\color{blue}{-1}}) \cdot {\color{red}{2}} + {\color{blue}{3}} \cdot ({\color{red}{-4}})}
$$

Finally, to find $c_{22}$, we calculate the dot product of the $2$nd row of $A$ and the $2$nd column of $B$:

$$
\eqalign {\left\lbrack \matrix {\color{lightgray}-2 & \color{lightgray}2 & \color{lightgray}1 \ \color{blue}1 & \color{blue}-1 & \color{blue}3} \right\rbrack \left\lbrack \matrix {\color{lightgray}-5 & \color{red}2 \ \color{lightgray}2 & \color{red}-2 \ \color{lightgray}-4 & \color{red}1} \right\rbrack % &= % \left\lbrack % \matrix {% 10 & -7 \ % -19 & {\color{blue}{1}} \cdot {\color{red}{2}} + ({\color{blue}{-1}}) \cdot ({\color{red}{-2}}) + {\color{blue}{3}} \cdot {\color{red}{1}} %} % \right\rbrack = \left\lbrack \matrix {10 & -7 \ -19 & \bbox[lightgray, 2pt]{7}} \right\rbrack, && \quad \bbox[lightgray, 2pt]{7} = {\color{blue}{1}} \cdot {\color{red}{2}} + ({\color{blue}{-1}}) \cdot ({\color{red}{-2}}) + {\color{blue}{3}} \cdot {\color{red}{1}}}
$$

Therefore, we have
$\begin{bmatrix}10 & -7 \\ -19 & 7\end{bmatrix}$

## Multiplying Two Matrices When They Are Conformable

**Example:** Find $CD$, if
$\begin{bmatrix}1 & -2 & 0 \\ 2 & 1 & 1\end{bmatrix}$
and
$\begin{bmatrix}0 & 1 \\ 2 & 0 \\ -3 & 2\end{bmatrix}$

**Explanation**

Here, $C$ is a $2 \times 3$ matrix and $D$ is a $3 \times 2$ matrix.

There are $3$ columns in $C$ and $3$ rows in $D$, so $C$ and $D$ are conformable for multiplication:

$$
\underset{\bbox[lightgray, 2pt]{2} \times \color{green}{\fbox{3}}}{C} \underset{\color{green}\checkmark}{\cdot} \underset{{\color{green}\fbox{3}} \times \bbox[lightgray, 2pt]{2}}{D} = \underset{\bbox[lightgray, 2pt]{2} \times \bbox[lightgray, 2pt]{2}}{CD}
$$

Let's compute $CD$, one element at a time:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1 & -2 & 0 \\ 2 & 1 & 1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}0 & 1 \\ 2 & 0 \\ -3 & 2\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}-4 & ∗ \\ ∗ & ∗\end{bmatrix}, & -4 = 1 \cdot 0 + (-2) \cdot 2 + 0 \cdot (-3) \\ \begin{bmatrix}1 & -2 & 0 \\ 2 & 1 & 1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}0 & 1 \\ 2 & 0 \\ -3 & 2\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}-4 & 1 \\ ∗ & ∗\end{bmatrix}, & 1 = 1 \cdot 1 + (-2) \cdot 0 + 0 \cdot 2 \\ \begin{bmatrix}1 & -2 & 0 \\ 2 & 1 & 1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}0 & 1 \\ 2 & 0 \\ -3 & 2\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}-4 & 1 \\ -1 & ∗\end{bmatrix}, & -1 = 2 \cdot 0 + 1 \cdot 2 + 1 \cdot (-3) \\ \begin{bmatrix}1 & -2 & 0 \\ 2 & 1 & 1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}0 & 1 \\ 2 & 0 \\ -3 & 2\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}-4 & 1 \\ -1 & 4\end{bmatrix}, & 4 = 2 \cdot 1 + 1 \cdot 0 + 1 \cdot 2\end{bmatrix}
$$

Therefore, $\begin{bmatrix}-4 & 1 \\ -1 & 4\end{bmatrix}$

## Finding an Entry of the Product of Two Matrices

**Example:** Let $C=AB$. What is the value of $c_{32}$, if
$\begin{bmatrix}1 & 2 \\ -2 & 0 \\ 1 & 2\end{bmatrix}$

**Explanation**

Multiplying the $3$rd row in $A$ by the $2$nd column in $B$, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
c_{32} &= \begin{bmatrix}1 & 2 \\ -2 & 0 \\ 1 & 2\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}3 & 4 & -1 \\ 1 & -2 & 2\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}1 & 2\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}4 \\ -2\end{bmatrix} \\
&= 1 \cdot 4 + 2 \cdot (-2) \\
&= 4 - 4 \\
&= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

## Multiplying a Column-Vector By a Row-Vector

**Example:** Compute the product
$\left\lbrack \matrix {-1 \ 0 \ -2} \right\rbrack \left\lbrack \matrix {2 & -5} \right\rbrack$.

**Explanation**

Here, the first factor is a $3 \times 1$ matrix and the second factor is a $1 \times 2$ matrix.

There is $1$ column in the first matrix and $1$ row in the second matrix, so the product is well defined:

$$
\bbox[lightgray, 2pt]{3} \times {\color{green}\fbox{1}} {\color{green}\checkmark} {\color{green}\fbox{1}} \times \bbox[lightgray, 2pt]{2} = \bbox[lightgray, 2pt]{3} \times \bbox[lightgray, 2pt]{2}
$$

Computing the product, we have

$$
\eqalign {\left\lbrack \matrix {-1 \ 0 \ -2} \right\rbrack \left\lbrack \matrix {2 & -5} \right\rbrack &= \left\lbrack \matrix {(-1)\cdot 2 & (-1)\cdot (-5) \ 0\cdot 2 & 0\cdot (-5)\ (-2)\cdot 2 & (-2)\cdot (-5)} \right\rbrack \[5pt] &= \left\lbrack \matrix {-2 & 5 \ 0 & 0 \ -4 & 10} \right\rbrack}
$$

## Index Notation of Matrix Multiplication

It's possible to express matrix multiplication using index notation.

To see how, first let $C=AB$, where $A$ and $B$ are two matrices that are conformable for multiplication.

Then $c_{\color{red}{i}\color{blue}{j}}$ represents the entry at the intersection of the $\color{red}i$th row and $\color{blue}j$th column in $C$.

To compute $c_{\color{red}{i}\color{blue}{j}}$, we take the dot product of the $\color{red}i$th row of $\color{red}A$ and the $\color{blue}j$th column of ${\color{blue}B}$, as follows:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}a_{i1} & a_{i2} & ⋯ & a_{in}\end{bmatrix}
$$
