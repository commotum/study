## Introduction

Matrices can be used to represent systems of equations, such as the following:

$$
{2x + 3y = 5; 4x-5y =-1
$$

For a $2\times 2$ system like the one above, we put the coefficients of the system into a $2\times 2$ matrix, called the **coefficient matrix**:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}2 & 3 \\ 4 & -5\end{bmatrix}
$$

Each row has the coefficients of one of the equations in the system, and each column consists of the coefficients of one particular variable.
We also have the $2\times 1$ column vectors

$$
\begin{bmatrix}5 \\ -1\end{bmatrix}
$$

where $\mathbf{b}$ consists of each constant on the right side of the system and $\mathbf{x}$ contains the variables.
With these matrices, we can write our system as

$$
A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b}
$$

or
$\begin{bmatrix}2 & 3 \\ 4 & -5\end{bmatrix}$

We can write any system of two equations with two variables in this form.
Just to check that the above equation is true, let's multiply out the left-hand side to see if it equals the right-hand side:

$$
\begin{aligned}
Ax &= \begin{bmatrix}2 & 3 \\ 4 & -5\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}x \\ y\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}2x + 3y \\ 4x-5y\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}5 \\ -1\end{bmatrix} \\
&= b✓
\end{aligned}
$$

## Representing a 2x2 System of Equations Using a Matrix Product

**Example:** Represent the following system of equations using matrices:
${2x = 5; - 5x + 6y =-8$

**Explanation**

First, let's write the system so that both $x$ and $y$ appear in each equation. The variable $y$ does not appear in the first equation, but this just means its coefficient is $0$. So, we get

$$
{2x + 0y = 5; - 5x + 6y =-8
$$

Now, we can write the system in the form

$$
A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b}
$$

where

$$
\begin{bmatrix}2 & 0 \\ -5 & 6\end{bmatrix}
$$

is the coefficient matrix,

$$
\begin{bmatrix}5 \\ -8\end{bmatrix}
$$

is the column vector of constants, and

$$
\begin{bmatrix}x \\ y\end{bmatrix}
$$

is the column vector of variables.

So, the equation

$$
A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b}
$$

becomes

$$
\begin{bmatrix}2 & 0 \\ -5 & 6\end{bmatrix}
$$

## Representing a 2x2 System of Equations Using a Matrix Product by Reorganizing

**Example:** Represent the following system of equations using matrices:
${-5y =-3x; 6 + x =-2 + y$

**Explanation**

First, we organize the system so that it’s in the correct order:

$$
{3x - 5y = 0; x - y =-8
$$

Now, we can write the system in the form

$$
A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b}
$$

where

$$
\begin{bmatrix}3 & -5 \\ 1 & -1\end{bmatrix}
$$

is the coefficient matrix,

$$
\begin{bmatrix}0 \\ -8\end{bmatrix}
$$

is the column vector of constants, and

$$
\begin{bmatrix}x \\ y\end{bmatrix}
$$

is the column vector of variables.

So, the equation

$$
A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b}
$$

becomes

$$
\begin{bmatrix}3 & -5 \\ 1 & -1\end{bmatrix}
$$

## Expressing a Matrix Product as a 2x2 System of Equations

**Example:** What is the system of equations represented by
$\begin{bmatrix}10 & -15 \\ 1 & 0\end{bmatrix}$

**Explanation**

The $2\times 2$ matrix represents the coefficients of $x$ and $y$, while the $2\times 1$ column vector on the right-hand side represents the constants of the equations. So, we get the following system of equations:

$$
{10x - 15y = 5; 1x + 0y =-2
$$

Simplifying both equations in the system, we get

$$
{2x - 3y = 1; x =-2
$$
