## Introduction

Given an equation involving complex numbers, we can always equate real and imaginary parts to give two separate equations.

For example, suppose we're given the equation

$$
2 + 9 \textrm{i} = 2x + 3y \textrm{i}
$$

The real part of $2 + 9 \textrm{i}$ is $2$, and the real part of $2x + 3y \textrm{i}$ is $2x$. Equating the real parts, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
2 &= 2x \\
x &= 1
\end{aligned}
$$

The imaginary part of $2 + 9 \textrm{i}$ is $9$, and the imaginary part of $2x + 3y \textrm{i}$ is $3y$. Equating the imaginary parts, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
9 &= 3y \\
y &= 3
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, $x=1$ and $y=3$.

## Equating Real and Imaginary Parts of a Complex Number to Solve For an Unknown Quantity

**Example:** Given that $3+2\textrm{i} = a + \textrm{i}b$, calculate the value of $a$ and the value of $b$.

**Explanation**

First, we equate the real parts:
$3 = a$
Then, we equate the imaginary parts:
$2 = b$
Therefore, $a=3$ and $b=2$.

## Solving for Unknowns By Computing a Product and Equating Real and Imaginary Parts

**Example:** Given that $2 - 3 \textrm{i} = \dfrac{p - 2q \textrm{i}}{1 + 2 \textrm{i}}$, calculate $p$ and $q$.

**Explanation**

First, we multiply both sides of the equation by $1+2\textrm{i}$ to get rid of the fraction and isolate the variables:

$$
\begin{aligned}
2 - 3\textrm{i} &= \frac{p - 2q\textrm{i}}{1 + 2\textrm{i}} \\
(2 - 3\textrm{i})(1 + 2\textrm{i}) &= p - 2q\textrm{i} \\
2 + 4\textrm{i} - 3\textrm{i} - 6\textrm{i}^{2} &= p - 2q\textrm{i} \\
2 + \textrm{i} - 6(-1) &= p - 2q\textrm{i} \\
8 + \textrm{i} &= p - 2q\textrm{i}
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we can equate the real parts and the imaginary parts separately.

- Equating the real parts, we get
$8 = p$.
- Equating the imaginary parts, we get
$1|=-2q; q|=-\frac{1}{2}$.

Therefore, $p=8$ and

$$
q = -\dfrac{1}{2}
$$

## Solving for Unknowns By Computing a Quotient and Equating Real and Imaginary Parts

**Example:** Given that $3+5\textrm{i} = (p+\textrm{i}q)(1+\textrm{i})$, calculate $p$ and $q$.

**Explanation**

First, we divide both sides of the equation by $1+\textrm{i}$ to isolate the variables:

$$
\begin{aligned}
3 + 5\textrm{i} &= (p + \textrm{i}q)(1 + \textrm{i}) \\
\frac{3 + 5\textrm{i}}{1 + \textrm{i}} &= p + \textrm{i}q
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, to simplify the fraction on the left-hand side, we multiply the numerator and denominator by the conjugate of the denominator:

$$
\begin{aligned}
((3 + 5\textrm{i})(1 - \textrm{i}))/((1 + \textrm{i})(1 - \textrm{i})) &= p + \textrm{i}q \\
\frac{3 - 3\textrm{i} + 5\textrm{i} - 5\textrm{i}^{2}}{1^{2} - \textrm{i}^{2}} &= p + \textrm{i}q \\
(3 + 2\textrm{i} - 5(-1))/(1 - (-1)) &= p + \textrm{i}q \\
\frac{8 + 2\textrm{i}}{2} &= p + \textrm{i}q \\
4 + \textrm{i} &= p + \textrm{i}q
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we can equate the real parts and the imaginary parts separately.

- Equating the real parts, we get $4=p$.
- Equating the imaginary parts, we get $1=q$.

Therefore, $p=4$ and $q=1$.
