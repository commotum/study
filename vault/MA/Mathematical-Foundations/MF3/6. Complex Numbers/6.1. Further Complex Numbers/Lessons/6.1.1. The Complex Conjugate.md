## Introduction

To find the **complex conjugate** of a complex number, we flip the sign of the imaginary part.

More precisely, for a complex number

$$
z=x+\textrm{i}y
$$

the **complex conjugate** is denoted $\overline{z}$ and is given by

$$
\overline{z} = x-\textrm{i}y
$$

Although the complex conjugate is very simple to compute, it has a surprising number of interesting properties that we will learn about in the future.

<a id="finding-the-complex-conjugate-of-a-complex-number-with-positive-imaginary-part"></a>
## Finding the Complex Conjugate of a Complex Number With Positive Imaginary Part

**Example:** What is the complex conjugate of $z=3+6\textrm{i}$?

**Explanation**

To find the complex conjugate, we flip the sign of the imaginary part.

In the given number

$$
z=3+6\textrm{i}
$$

the imaginary part is $6\textrm{i}$. Flipping the sign, we get $-6\textrm{i}$.

Therefore, the complex conjugate is given by

$$
\overline{z} = 3-6\textrm{i}
$$

## Finding the Complex Conjugate of a Complex Number With Negative Imaginary Part

**Example:** What is the complex conjugate of $z=-4-6\textrm{i}$?

**Explanation**

To find the complex conjugate, we flip the sign of the imaginary part.

In the given number

$$
z=-4-6\textrm{i}
$$

the imaginary part is $-6\textrm{i}$. Flipping the sign, we get $6\textrm{i}$.

Therefore, the complex conjugate is given by

$$
\overline{z} = -4+6\textrm{i}
$$

## Finding the Complex Conjugate of an Imaginary Number

**Example:** What is the complex conjugate of $z=7\textrm{i}$?

**Explanation**

To find the complex conjugate, we flip the sign of the imaginary part.

In the given number

$$
z=7\textrm{i}
$$

the imaginary part is $7\textrm{i}$ (there is no real part, just an imaginary part). Flipping the sign, we get $-7\textrm{i}$.

Therefore, the complex conjugate is given by

$$
\overline{z} = -7\textrm{i}
$$

<a id="the-complex-conjugate-of-a-real-number"></a>
## The Complex Conjugate of a Real Number

For a real number, its complex conjugate is always the same as the original number itself.

To understand why, first notice that any real number $a$ can be expressed as a complex number with an imaginary part of $0\textrm{i}$:

$$
a = a + 0 \textrm{i}
$$

To find the complex conjugate, we need to flip the sign of the imaginary part. So, we get

$$
\overline{a} = a - 0 \textrm{i} = a
$$

Therefore,

$$
\overline{a} = a
$$

---

<a id="finding-the-complex-conjugate-of-a-real-number"></a>
## Finding the Complex Conjugate of a Real Number

**Example:** What is the complex conjugate of $z=-6$?

**Explanation**

The number $z=-6$ is a real number, and the complex conjugate of any real number is just the number itself.

So here, the complex conjugate is

$$
\overline{z} = z = -6
$$
