## Introduction

**De Moivre's theorem** provides a simple way to compute powers of complex numbers in polar form. It states that

$$
\left[ \cos (\varphi) + \textrm{i}\sin(\varphi) \right]^n = \cos(n\varphi) + \textrm{i}\sin{(n\varphi)}
$$

where $n$ is an integer.

For example, let's use De Moivre's theorem to compute

$$
\left[ \cos (3\theta) +\textrm{i}\sin(3\theta) \right]^5
$$

We get

$$
\begin{aligned}
[\cos (3θ) + \textrm{i}\sin (3θ)]^{5} &= \cos (5 \cdot 3θ) + \textrm{i}\sin (5 \cdot 3θ) \\
&= \cos (15θ) + \textrm{i}\sin (15θ)
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="applying-de-moivres-theorem"></a>
## Applying De Moivre's Theorem

**Example:** Simplify the expression $\left[\cos \left(\dfrac{\theta}{10} \right) + \textrm{i}\sin \left(\dfrac{\theta}{10}\right) \right]^4$.

**Explanation**

De Moivre's Theorem states that

$$
\left[ \cos(\varphi) + \textrm{i}\sin(\varphi) \right]^n = \cos(n\varphi) + \textrm{i}\sin{(n\varphi)}
$$

In our expression, we have $n=4$ and

$$
\varphi=\dfrac{\theta}{10}
$$

Substituting in these values, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
[\cos (\frac{θ}{10}) + \textrm{i}\sin (\frac{θ}{10})]^{4} &= \cos (4 \cdot \frac{θ}{10}) + \textrm{i}\sin (4 \cdot \frac{θ}{10}) \\
&= \cos (\frac{2θ}{5}) + \textrm{i}\sin (\frac{2θ}{5})
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="applying-de-moivres-theorem-to-fractions"></a>
## Applying De Moivre's Theorem to Fractions

**Example:** Simplify the expression $\dfrac{1}{\left[\cos (\theta) +\textrm{i}\sin(\theta) \right]^2}$.

**Explanation**

First, note that

$$
\dfrac{1}{\left[ \cos(\theta) +\textrm{i}\sin(\theta) \right]^2} = \left[ \cos(\theta) +\textrm{i}\sin(\theta) \right]^{-2}
$$

De Moivre's Theorem states that

$$
\left[ \cos(\varphi) + \textrm{i}\sin(\varphi) \right]^n = \cos(n\varphi) + \textrm{i}\sin{(n\varphi)}
$$

In our expression, we have $n=-2$ and

$$
\varphi=\theta
$$

Substituting in these values, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
(1)/([\cos (θ) + \textrm{i}\sin (θ)]^{2}) &= [\cos (θ) + \textrm{i}\sin (θ)]^{-2} \\
&= \cos (-2θ) + \textrm{i}\sin (-2θ)
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, using the fact that

$$
\cos(-\varphi) = \cos (\varphi)
$$

and

$$
\sin(-\varphi) = -\sin(\varphi)
$$

for any input $\varphi$, we have

$$
\cos(-2\theta) + \textrm{i}\sin(-2\theta) = \cos(2\theta) - \textrm{i}\sin{(2\theta)}
$$

<a id="de-moivres-theorem-with-a-negative-sine"></a>
## De Moivre's Theorem With a Negative Sine

We can also use De Moivre's theorem for an expression with a subtraction:

$$
\left[ \cos (\varphi) - \textrm{i}\sin(\varphi) \right]^n = \cos(n\varphi) - \textrm{i}\sin{(n\varphi)}
$$

For example, with $n=5$ and

$$
\varphi=3\theta
$$

we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
[\cos (3θ) - \textrm{i}\sin (3θ)]^{5} &= \cos (5 \cdot 3θ) - \textrm{i}\sin (5 \cdot 3θ) \\
&= \cos (15θ) - \textrm{i}\sin (15θ)
\end{aligned}
$$

To understand why we can also use De Moivre's theorem with subtraction, remember that

$$
\cos\varphi = \cos(-\varphi)
$$

and

$$
-\sin(\varphi) = \sin(-\varphi)
$$

Then, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
[\cos (φ) - \textrm{i}\sin (φ)]^{n} &= [\cos (- φ) + \textrm{i}\sin (- φ)]^{n} \\
&= \cos (-nφ) + \textrm{i}\sin (-nφ) \\
&= \cos (nφ) - \textrm{i}\sin (nφ)
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="applying-de-moivres-theorem-with-a-negative-sine"></a>
## Applying De Moivre's Theorem With a Negative Sine

**Example:** Simplify the expression $\dfrac{1}{\left[\cos (2\theta) -\textrm{i}\sin (2\theta) \right]^4}$.

**Explanation**

First, note that

$$
\begin{aligned}
(1)/([\cos (2θ) - \textrm{i}\sin (2θ)]^{4}) &= [\cos (2θ) - \textrm{i}\sin (2θ)]^{-4}
\end{aligned}
$$

For expressions involving subtraction, De Moivre's theorem states that

$$
\left[ \cos (\varphi) - \textrm{i}\sin(\varphi) \right]^n = \cos(n\varphi) - \textrm{i}\sin{(n\varphi)}
$$

In our expression, we have $n=-4$ and

$$
\varphi=2\theta
$$

Substituting in these values, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
[\cos (2θ) - \textrm{i}\sin (2θ)]^{-4} &= \cos (-4 \cdot 2θ) - \textrm{i}\sin (-4 \cdot 2θ) \\
&= \cos (-8θ) - \textrm{i}\sin (-8θ)
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, using the fact that

$$
\cos(-\varphi) = \cos (\varphi)
$$

and

$$
\sin(-\varphi) = -\sin(\varphi)
$$

for any input $\varphi$, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
\cos (-8θ) - \textrm{i}\sin (-8θ) &= \cos (8θ) - [- \textrm{i}\sin (8θ)] \\
&= \cos (8θ) + \textrm{i}\sin (8θ)
\end{aligned}
$$
