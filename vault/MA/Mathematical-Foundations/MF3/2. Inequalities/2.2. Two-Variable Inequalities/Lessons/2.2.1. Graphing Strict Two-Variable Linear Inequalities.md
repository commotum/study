## Introduction

Let's think about how to graph the solutions to the following two-variable inequality:

$$
y \gt x
$$

Since this is a two-variable inequality, we must represent its solutions in the $xy$-plane.

We start by replacing the inequality symbol $(>)$ with an equals sign $(=)$ and plot the resulting line. In this case, we get the line $y=x$.

![](<../Source/2.2.1. Graphing Strict Two-Variable Linear Inequalities/Images/745a55d73f9d7774b7bc6d0d84445908.png>)

Notice that we have a "*greater than*" $(>)$ inequality. Therefore, we draw a *dashed* line to denote that points on the line are *not* solutions to our inequality.

Now, since we're solving $y > x$, we need to shade all points whose $y$-value is *greater than* its $x$-value. This is given by all of the points that lie *above* our dashed line. So, let's shade these in.

![](<../Source/2.2.1. Graphing Strict Two-Variable Linear Inequalities/Images/90a6a6338454f7b4d44250780ba7a942.png>)

And we're done! All of the points in the shaded region satisfy the inequality $y > x$.

---

<a id="testing-the-solution"></a>
## Testing the Solution

When solving two-variable inequalities, it's often helpful to test to ensure we've shaded the correct region.

To demonstrate, let's go back to the following inequality:

$$
y > x
$$

The solution to this inequality is indicated by the shaded region below.

![](<../Source/2.2.1. Graphing Strict Two-Variable Linear Inequalities/Images/10569b99db244ebd1661273c8ef240ec.png>)

To check that we've shaded the correct region, we pick a point inside the shaded region and verify that it satisfies the original inequality. Let's pick the point $(0,1)$.

![](<../Source/2.2.1. Graphing Strict Two-Variable Linear Inequalities/Images/78a2962aa72d25074a6b0a20617b18ce.png>)

When we substitute the coordinates $(0,1)$ into our inequality, we get a true statement:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}y & > x \\ 1 & > 0✓\end{bmatrix}
$$

This indicates that we have shaded the correct region.

<a id="sketching-the-solution-to-a-strictly-greater-than-inequality"></a>
## Sketching the Solution to a “Strictly Greater Than” Inequality

**Example:** Sketch the solution to the inequality $y \gt -x+2$.

**Explanation**

First, we draw the corresponding straight line

$$
y=-x+2
$$

Since the inequality involves a 'strictly greater than' sign, the line

$$
y=-x+2
$$

itself is *not* part of the solution. Therefore, we draw a **dashed line.**

![](<../Source/2.2.1. Graphing Strict Two-Variable Linear Inequalities/Images/0dbac4559129443b377ebb33f3e68e80.png>)

Then, we must decide whether to shade above or below the line. The inequality $y > -x+2$ refers to the $y$-values that are *greater than* those on the line

$$
y=-x+2
$$

so we shade *above* the line.

![](<../Source/2.2.1. Graphing Strict Two-Variable Linear Inequalities/Images/12547eeb9ea450055ffabc766106cde6.png>)

Let's now check that we've shaded the correct part. First, we consider a point in the shaded region, say $(3,0)$.

![](<../Source/2.2.1. Graphing Strict Two-Variable Linear Inequalities/Images/17ca8f4ea53250c6e1e962075fd63867.png>)

When we substitute the coordinates of this point into our inequality, we get a true statement:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}y & > - x + 2 \\ (0) & > - (3) + 2 \\ 0 & > - 1✓\end{bmatrix}
$$

This indicates that we have shaded the correct region.

<a id="solving-strictly-less-than-inequalities"></a>
## Solving "Strictly Less Than" Inequalities

Let's now think about how to graph the solutions to the inequality

$$
y \lt x
$$

As before, we start by replacing the inequality symbol $(\lt)$ with an equals sign $(=)$ and plot the resulting line $y=x$.

![](<../Source/2.2.1. Graphing Strict Two-Variable Linear Inequalities/Images/e98a9d386041ef0fea8f2ef320b4576f.png>)

Notice that we have a "*less than*" $(\lt)$ inequality. Therefore, we draw a *dashed* line.

Now, since we're solving $y \lt x$, we need to shade all points whose $y$-value is *smaller than* its $x$-value. This is given by all of the points that lie *below* our dashed line.

![](<../Source/2.2.1. Graphing Strict Two-Variable Linear Inequalities/Images/36ee572eb71debae6fd0b01a88aaff80.png>)

And we're done! All of the points in the shaded region satisfy the inequality $y \lt x$.

To check that we've shaded the correct region, we pick a point inside the shaded region and check that it satisfies the original inequality. Let's pick the point $(1,0)$.

![](<../Source/2.2.1. Graphing Strict Two-Variable Linear Inequalities/Images/d832434a0a4947dc0e07519ea0c57d37.png>)

When we substitute the coordinates $(1,0)$ into our inequality, we get a true statement:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}y & < x \\ 0 & < 1✓\end{bmatrix}
$$

This indicates that we have shaded the correct region.

## Sketching the Solution to a “Strictly Less Than” Inequality

**Example:** Sketch the solution to the inequality $y < -2x+4$.

**Explanation**

First, we draw the corresponding straight line

$$
y=-2x+4
$$

Since the inequality involves a 'strictly less than' sign, the line

$$
y=-2x+4
$$

itself is *not* part of the solution. Therefore, we draw a **dashed line.**

![](<../Source/2.2.1. Graphing Strict Two-Variable Linear Inequalities/Images/c83b53bca56a3ae465a3be3a22a9dbf9.png>)

Then, we must decide whether to shade above or below the line. The inequality $y < -2x+4$ refers to the $y$-values that are *less than* those on the line

$$
y=-2x+4
$$

so we shade *below* the line.

![](<../Source/2.2.1. Graphing Strict Two-Variable Linear Inequalities/Images/10f9f29d7a458d954b95bd14e102f149.png>)

Let's now check that we've shaded the correct part. First, we consider a point in the shaded region, say $(-1,0)$.

![](<../Source/2.2.1. Graphing Strict Two-Variable Linear Inequalities/Images/083bef3c8b6cb08df1e410d92d3ec3b8.png>)

When we substitute the coordinates of this point into our inequality, we get a true statement:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}y & < - 2x + 4 \\ (0) & < - 2(-1) + 4 \\ 0 & < 6✓\end{bmatrix}
$$

This indicates that we have shaded the correct region.

<a id="finding-an-inequality-corresponding-to-a-given-graph"></a>
## Finding an Inequality Corresponding to a Given Graph

**Example:** Which inequality generates the shaded region shown below?

![](<../Source/2.2.1. Graphing Strict Two-Variable Linear Inequalities/Images/a67fd563d388910f2a0293214587b562.png>)

**Explanation**

First, we find the equation of the corresponding line in the slope-intercept form

$$
y=mx+b
$$

- The line intersects the $y$-axis at $(0,4)$, so the $y$-intercept is $b=4$.
- The line passes through the points $(0,4)$ and $(2,0)$, so its slope is
$m = \frac{y_{2} - y_{1}}{x_{2} - x_{1}} = \frac{0 - 4}{2 - 0} = \frac{-4}{2} =-2$.

Therefore, the equation of the line is

$$
y=-2x+4
$$

Finally, we need to decide which inequality symbol to use. Since the line is dashed, we will use a strict inequality. Since the shaded region is *below* the line, the inequality refers to the $y$-values that are *less than* those on the line.

So, the corresponding inequality is
$y < -2x+4$.
