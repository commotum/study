## Introduction

To calculate the area $A$ bounded by a curve $y=y(x)$, the $x$-axis, and the vertical lines $x=a$ and $x=b$, as shown below, we compute the integral

$$
A = \int_{a}^{b} y(x) \:\textrm{d}x
$$

![](<../Source/9.3.1. The Area Bounded by a Curve and the X-Axis/Images/9688fdbba99c631ff86b8e20b98c0e67.png>)

<a id="calculating-the-area-bounded-below-a-curve-and-above-the-x-axis"></a>
## Calculating the Area Bounded Below a Curve and Above the X-Axis

**Example:** Find the area between the curve $y=x^2-2x+2$, the $x$-axis, and the lines $x=0$ and $x=2$.

![](<../Source/9.3.1. The Area Bounded by a Curve and the X-Axis/Images/cd089b9532fe77a9dea0d813bf425efc.png>)

**Explanation**

To find the area, we just need to compute the corresponding definite integral:

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= ∫_{a}^{b}y(x)dx \\
&= ∫_{0}^{2}(x^{2} - 2x + 2)dx \\
&= ∫_{0}^{2}x^{2}dx - 2∫_{0}^{2}xdx + 2∫_{0}^{2}dx \\
&= \left.\frac{x^3}{3}\right|_0^2 - 2\left.\frac{x^2}{2}\right|_0^2 + \left.2x\right|_0^2 \\
&= (\frac{8}{3} - 0) - 2(2 - 0) + 2(2 - 0) \\
&= \frac{8}{3} - 4 + 4 \\
&= \frac{8}{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Signed Areas Bounded by a Curve and the x-Axis

When we compute the area under a curve using integration, we must bear the following in mind:

- definite integrals that correspond to areas above the $x$-axis are positive, while
- definite integrals that correspond to areas below the $x$-axis are negative.

![](<../Source/9.3.1. The Area Bounded by a Curve and the X-Axis/Images/d656ddea6e23f093317adc6ba576f51d.png>)

Let's see an example where the area comes out to be negative.

<a id="calculating-the-area-bounded-above-a-curve-and-below-the-x-axis"></a>
## Calculating the Area Bounded Above a Curve and Below the X-Axis

**Example:** Find the area of the closed region between the curve $y=x^2-1$ and the $x$-axis.

**Explanation**

First, we find the intersections of the curve with the $x$-axis:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^{2} - 1 &= 0 \\
x^{2} &= 1 \\
x &= ± 1
\end{aligned}
$$

We draw a sketch of the region, as follows:

![](<../Source/9.3.1. The Area Bounded by a Curve and the X-Axis/Images/92bd9521485f93f43b1f96172bd73ba5.png>)

We want the area between the curve $y=x^2-1$ and the $x$-axis, from $x=-1$ to $x=1$. To find the area, we just need to compute the corresponding definite integral:

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= ∫_{a}^{b}y(x)dx \\
&= ∫_{-1}^{1}(x^{2} - 1)dx \\
&= ∫_{-1}^{1}x^{2}dx - ∫_{-1}^{1}dx \\
&= \frac{x^{3}}{3} \mid _{-1}^{1} - x \mid _{-1}^{1} \\
&= (\frac{1}{3} - (-\frac{1}{3})) - (1 - (-1)) \\
&= \frac{2}{3} - 2 \\
&=-\frac{4}{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

Since the region lies below the $x$-axis, we obtained a negative number. So the area that we want is

$$
\dfrac{4}{3}
$$
