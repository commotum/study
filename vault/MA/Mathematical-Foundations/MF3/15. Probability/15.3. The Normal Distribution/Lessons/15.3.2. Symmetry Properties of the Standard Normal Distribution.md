## Introduction

Suppose that the continuous random variable $Z$ follows a standard normal distribution:

$$
Z\sim N(0,1)
$$

The standard normal distribution is *symmetric* about the vertical axis. We can use this fact to help us calculate probabilities.

For example, the two shaded areas below are equal.

![](<../Source/15.3.2. Symmetry Properties of the Standard Normal Distribution/Images/0cb4db7bd66551fad0b2f5b811e48d2a.png>)

Note that

- the shaded area on the left corresponds to $P(Z \lt -a)$, and
- the shaded area on the right corresponds to $P(Z \gt a)$.

![](<../Source/15.3.2. Symmetry Properties of the Standard Normal Distribution/Images/750d46685f52f34c6ed909816f011a81.png>)

Since these two areas are equal by symmetry, we have the following rule:

$$
P(Z \gt a) = P(Z \lt -a)
$$

Similarly, we have that

$$
P(Z \gt -a) = P(Z \lt a) = \Phi(a)
$$

as shown below. Recall that $\Phi(z)$ is the cumulative distribution function of the standard normal distribution.

![](<../Source/15.3.2. Symmetry Properties of the Standard Normal Distribution/Images/896a96349cab4d47e25199d59ea3bcb8.png>)

Let's take a look at some concrete examples.

## Computing a Probability on an Unbounded Interval

**Example:** The data below is taken from the table of values of the cumulative distribution function $\Phi(z)$ for the standard normal distribution. Given that $Z \sim N(0,1)$, compute $P(Z \gt -0.76)$.

| $z$ | $\, 0.05 \,$ | $\, 0.06 \,$ | $\, 0.07 \,$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $\, 0.6 \,$ | $\, 0.7422 \,$ | $\, 0.7454 \,$ | $\, 0.7486 \,$ |
| $\, 0.7 \,$ | $\, 0.7734 \,$ | $\, 0.7764 \,$ | $\, 0.7794 \,$ |
| $\, 0.8 \,$ | $\, 0.8023 \,$ | $\, 0.8051 \,$ | $\, 0.8078 \,$ |

**Explanation**

![](<../Source/15.3.2. Symmetry Properties of the Standard Normal Distribution/Images/66fefa957dd168d1589cdb34bce3bced.png>)

For a random variable $Z \sim N(0,1)$ we have the following symmetry property for $a > 0{:}$

$$
P(Z \gt -a) = P(Z \lt a) = \Phi(a)
$$

We're given that $Z\sim N(0,1)$, and we wish to compute $P(Z > -0.76)$. The required probability is represented by the area shown below.

![](<../Source/15.3.2. Symmetry Properties of the Standard Normal Distribution/Images/1bc0f3dd71234ca7671872a937519a38.png>)

By symmetry, this equals the area corresponding to the probability $P(Z \lt 0.76)$.

![](<../Source/15.3.2. Symmetry Properties of the Standard Normal Distribution/Images/bd8ed379c32619927570445941878c4f.png>)

Therefore,

$$
P(Z > -0.76) = P(Z < 0.76) = \Phi(0.76) = 0.7764
$$

## Computing a Probability on an Unbounded Interval Using the Complement

**Example:** The data below is taken from the table of values of the cumulative distribution function $\Phi(z)$ for the standard normal distribution. Given that $Z \sim N(0,1)$, compute $P(Z \lt -1.54)$.

| $z$ | $\, 0.02 \,$ | $\, 0.03 \,$ | $\, 0.04 \,$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $\, 1.3 \,$ | $\, 0.9066 \,$ | $\, 0.9082 \,$ | $\, 0.9099 \,$ |
| $\, 1.4 \,$ | $\, 0.9222 \,$ | $\, 0.9236 \,$ | $\, 0.9251 \,$ |
| $\, 1.5 \,$ | $\, 0.9357 \,$ | $\, 0.9370 \,$ | $\, 0.9382 \,$ |

**Explanation**

![](<../Source/15.3.2. Symmetry Properties of the Standard Normal Distribution/Images/95c129d15152dcce16214fa0613e8fb6.png>)

For a random variable $Z \sim N(0,1)$, we have the following symmetry property for $a > 0{:}$

$$
P(Z \gt a) = P(Z \lt -a) = \Phi(-a)
$$

We're given that $Z\sim N(0,1)$, and we wish to compute $P(Z \lt -1.54)$. The required probability is represented by the area shown below.

![](<../Source/15.3.2. Symmetry Properties of the Standard Normal Distribution/Images/aa5d3354086f3622c4f7c5920b1753b8.png>)

By symmetry, this equals the area corresponding to the probability $P(Z \gt 1.54)$.

![](<../Source/15.3.2. Symmetry Properties of the Standard Normal Distribution/Images/3e0d56a379a7fecf3403b9750a7133ab.png>)

Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(Z < - 1.54) &= P(Z > 1.54) \\
&= 1 - P(Z < 1.54) \\
&= 1 - Φ(1.54) \\
&= 1 - 0.9382 \\
&= 0.0618
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="computing-a-probability-on-a-bounded-interval"></a>
## Computing a Probability on a Bounded Interval

**Example:** The data below is taken from the table of values of the cumulative distribution function $\Phi(z)$ for the standard normal distribution. Given that $Z \sim N(0,1)$, compute $P(-0.53 < Z < 0.53)$.

| $z$ | $\, 0.02 \,$ | $\, 0.03 \,$ | $\, 0.04 \,$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $\, 0.4 \,$ | $\, 0.6628 \,$ | $\, 0.6664 \,$ | $\, 0.67 \,$ |
| $\, 0.5 \,$ | $\, 0.6985 \,$ | $\, 0.7019 \,$ | $\, 0.7054 \,$ |
| $\, 0.6 \,$ | $\, 0.7324 \,$ | $\, 0.7357 \,$ | $\, 0.7389 \,$ |

**Explanation**

We're given that $Z\sim N(0,1)$, and we wish to compute $P(-0.53 < Z < 0.53)$. The required probability is represented by the area shown below.

![](<../Source/15.3.2. Symmetry Properties of the Standard Normal Distribution/Images/0b30d484a778ffede28d541415b21e58.png>)

By the definition of the cumulative distribution function $\Phi(z)$,

$$
P(Z \leq z) = \Phi(z)
$$

So, we express the required probability in terms of $\Phi(z)$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(-0.53 < Z < 0.53) &= P(Z < 0.53) - P(Z \le - 0.53) \\
&= P(Z \le 0.53) - P(Z \le - 0.53) \\
&= Φ(0.53) - Φ(-0.53)
\end{aligned}
$$

From the table, we know that

$$
\Phi(0.53) = 0.7019
$$

but we are not given $\Phi(-0.53)$. However, we can compute this using symmetry:

$$
\begin{aligned}
Φ(-0.53) &= P(Z \le - 0.53) \\
&= P(Z \ge 0.53) \\
&= 1 - P(Z < 0.53) \\
&= 1 - P(Z \le 0.53) \\
&= 1 - Φ(0.53) \\
&= 1 - 0.7019 \\
&= 0.2981
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(-0.53 < Z < 0.53) &= Φ(0.53) - Φ(-0.53) \\
&= 0.7019 - 0.2981 \\
&= 0.4038
\end{aligned}
$$
