## Introduction

Given a data set $x_1, x_2, \ldots, x_n$, we know that its variance, which we may denote as $\sigma_x^2$, is given by

$$
\sigma_x^2 = \dfrac1n \sum_{i=1}^n (x_i - \overline x)^2
$$

where $\overline x$ is the mean of $x$.

The summation term

$$
S_{xx} = \sum_{i=1}^n (x_i - \overline x)^2
$$

is known as a **sum of squares** and is denoted $S_{xx}$. As we'll see, this quantity often appears in statistical analysis, so giving it a separate notation is useful.

Similarly, if we have a data set $y_1, y_2, \ldots, y_n$, then we define

$$
S_{yy} = \sum_{i=1}^n (y_i - \overline y)^2
$$

Now, suppose we have the bivariate data set
$(x_1, y_1), \qquad (x_2, y_2),\qquad \ldots, \qquad (x_n, y_n)$.
Then, the covariance is given by

$$
\textrm{Cov}(x,y) = \dfrac1n\sum_{i=1}^n (x_i - \overline x)(y_i - \overline y)
$$

and we may define $S_{xy}$ as

$$
S_{xy} = \sum_{i=1}^n (x_i - \overline{x})(y_i - \overline{y})
$$

Finally, we can define the variance and covariance in terms of $S_{xx}, S_{yy}$, and $S_{xy}$ as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
σ_{x}^{2} &= \frac{S_{xx}}{n}, σ_{y}^{2} = \frac{S_{yy}}{n}, Cov(x, y) = \frac{S_{xy}}{n}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Calculating Sums of Squares Given a Dataset or Summary Statistics

**Example:** Consider the two sets of $5$ paired observations shown in the table below.

| **$\, x_i \,$** | $15$ | $12$ | $13$ | $16$ | $19$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| **$\, y_i \,$** | $8$ | $13$ | $6$ | $15$ | $13$ |

Calculate $S_{xy}$.

**Explanation**

Given a set of $n$ paired observations $x_{1}, x_2, \ldots, x_{n}$ and $y_{1}, y_2, \ldots, y_{n}$, $S_{xy}$ is the sum of products of the deviations of $x_i$ and $y_i$ from their respective means, that is

$$
\begin{aligned}
S_{xy} &= ∑_(i = 1)^(n)(x_{i} - x^(―))(y_{i} - y^(―))
\end{aligned}
$$

First, we calculate the sample means $\overline{x}$ and $\overline{y}{:}$

$$
\overline{x} = \dfrac{15+12+13+16+19}{5} = 15
$$

$$
\overline{y} = \dfrac{8+13+6+15+13}{5} = 11
$$

Next, we calculate the differences between the $x_i$ and $\overline{x}$, $y_i$ and $\overline{y}$, and their product.

| **$\, x_i \,$** | $15$ | $12$ | $13$ | $16$ | $19$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| **$\, y_i \,$** | $8$ | $13$ | $6$ | $15$ | $13$ |
| **$\, (x_i - \overline{x}) \,$** | $0$ | $-3$ | $-2$ | $1$ | $4$ |
| **$\, (y_i - \overline{y}) \,$** | $-3$ | $2$ | $-5$ | $4$ | $2$ |
| **$\, (x_i - \overline{x})(y_i - \overline{y}) \,$** | $0$ | $-6$ | $10$ | $4$ | $8$ |

Therefore, $S_{xy}$ is

$$
S_{xy} = 0 + (-6) + 10 + 4 + 8 = 16
$$

## Calculating Variance and Covariance

**Example:** Consider two sets of $35$ paired observations $x_{1}, x_2, \ldots, x_{35}$, and $y_{1}, y_2, \ldots, y_{35}$. You are given that
$S_{xx} = 35, S_{yy} = 133, S_{xy} = 105$.

What is the value of $\mathrm{Cov}(x, y)$?

**Explanation**

Given a set of $n$ paired observations $x_{1}, x_2, \ldots, x_{n}$ and $y_{1}, y_2, \ldots, y_{n}$, their covariance can be calculated as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
Cov(x, y) &= \frac{S_{xy}}{n}
\end{aligned}
$$

where $S_{xy}$ is the sum of products of the deviations of $x_i$ and $y_i$ from their means.

We are given that

$$
S_{xy} = 105
$$

Substituting the given statistic in the above formula, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
Cov(x, y) &= \frac{105}{35} \\
&= 3
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="shortcut-formulas"></a>
## Shortcut Formulas

Calculating $S_{xx}, S_{yy}$, and $S_{xy}$ using their definitions can be cumbersome. Thankfully, we have the following shortcut formulas:

$$
\begin{aligned}
S_{xx} &= ∑_(i = 1)^(n)x_{i}^{2} - \frac{1}{n}(∑_(i = 1)^(n)x_{i})^{2} \\
S_{yy} &= ∑_(i = 1)^(n)y_{i}^{2} - \frac{1}{n}(∑_(i = 1)^(n)y_{i})^{2} \\
S_{xy} &= ∑_(i = 1)^(n)x_{i}y_{i} - \frac{1}{n}(∑_(i = 1)^(n)x_{i})(∑_(i = 1)^(n)y_{i})
\end{aligned}
$$

We'll prove these at the end of the lesson.

## Calculating Sums of Squares Using the Shortcut Formula

**Example:** Consider the two sets of $5$ paired observations shown in the table below.

| **$\, x_i \,$** | $10$ | $9$ | $8$ | $8$ | $5$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| **$\, y_i \,$** | $15$ | $12$ | $14$ | $12$ | $8$ |

Given that $\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^5 x_i^2 = 334$, calculate $S_{xx}$.

**Explanation**

Given a set of $n$ paired observations $x_{1}, x_2, \ldots, x_{n}$ and $y_{1}, y_2, \ldots, y_{n}$, the sum of squared deviations from the mean for the data $x_i$ can be calculated as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
S_{xx} &= ∑_(i = 1)^(n)(x_{i} - x^(―))^{2} \\
&= ∑_(i = 1)^(n)x_{i}^{2} - \frac{1}{n}(∑_(i = 1)^(n)x_{i})^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

First, we calculate the sum of the $x_i$ data:

$$
\sum_{i=1}^n x_i = 10 + 9 + 8 + 8 + 5 = 40
$$

We are given that

$$
\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^5 x_i^2 = 334
$$

Finally, by substituting into the above formula, we get

$$
S_{xx} =334 - \dfrac{1}{5}(40)^2 = 334- 320 = 14
$$

## Calculating S(x,y) Using the Shortcut Formula

**Example:** Consider two sets of $30$ paired observations $x_{1}, x_2, \ldots, x_{30}$, and $y_{1}, y_2, \ldots, y_{30}$. You are given that
$∑_(i = 1)^(30)x_{i} = 315,; ∑_(i = 1)^(30)y_{i} = 278,; ∑_(i = 1)^(30)x_{i}y_{i} = 2964$.
Determine $S_{xy}$.

**Explanation**

Given a set of $n$ paired observations $x_{1}, x_2, \ldots, x_{n}$ and $y_{1}, y_2, \ldots, y_{n}$, the statistic $S_{xy}$ be calculated as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
S_{xy} &= ∑_(i = 1)^(n)(x_{i} - x^(―))(y_{i} - y^(―)) \\
&= ∑_(i = 1)^(n)x_{i}y_{i} - \frac{1}{n}(∑_(i = 1)^(n)x_{i})(∑_(i = 1)^(n)y_{i})
\end{aligned}
$$

Substituting the given statistics in the above formula, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
S_{xy} &= 2964 - \frac{1}{30}(315)(278) \\
&= 2964 - 2919 \\
&= 45
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="proving-the-shortcut-formulas"></a>
## Proving the Shortcut Formulas

We wish to prove that

$$
S_{xx} = ∑_(i)x_{i}^{2} - \frac{1}{n}(∑_(i = 1)^(n)x_{i})^{2}
$$

We start with the definition of $S_{xx}{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
S_{xx} &= ∑_(i = 1)^(n)(x_{i} - x^(―))^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

First, we expand the binomial:

$$
∑_(i = 1)^(n)(x_{i}^{2} - 2x_{i} \cdot x^(―) + x^(―)^{2})
$$

Distributing the summation, we have

$$
∑_(i = 1)^(n)x_{i}^{2} - ∑_(i = 1)^(n)2x_{i} \cdot x^(―) + ∑_(i = 1)^(n)x^(―)^{2}
$$

Now, since $2, \overline x$, and $\overline{x}^2$ are constants, we can write this as

$$
∑_(i = 1)^(n)x_{i}^{2} - 2x^(―) \cdot ∑_(i = 1)^(n)x_{i} + x^(―)^{2} \cdot ∑_(i = 1)^(n)1
$$

Next, we use the following results:

$$
\sum_{i=1}^n x_i = n\overline x, \qquad \sum_{i=1}^n 1 = n
$$

This gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
S_{xx} &= ∑_(i = 1)^(n)x_{i}^{2} - 2x^(―) \cdot nx^(―) + x^(―)^{2} \cdot n \\
&= ∑_(i = 1)^(n)x_{i}^{2} - 2nx^(―)^{2} + nx^(―)^{2} \\
&= ∑_(i = 1)^(n)x_{i}^{2} - nx^(―)^{2} \\
&= ∑_(i = 1)^(n)x_{i}^{2} - n \cdot (\frac{1}{n}∑_(i = 1)^(n)x_{i})^{2} \\
&= ∑_(i = 1)^(n)x_{i}^{2} - n \cdot \frac{1}{n^{2}} \cdot (∑_(i = 1)^(n)x_{i})^{2} \\
&= ∑_(i)x_{i}^{2} - \frac{1}{n}(∑_(i = 1)^(n)x_{i})^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

as required.

The proofs for $S_{yy}$ and $S_{xy}$ are similar.
