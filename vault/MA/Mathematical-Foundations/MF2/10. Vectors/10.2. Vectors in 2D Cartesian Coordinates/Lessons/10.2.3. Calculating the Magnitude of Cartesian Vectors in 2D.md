## Introduction

Consider the vector

$$
\mathbf{a}=\langle -2,4 \rangle
$$

shown below.

![](<../Source/10.2.3. Calculating the Magnitude of Cartesian Vectors in 2D/Images/8f7288d7d92b213ba3a51c852a40b21c.png>)

Expressed in component form, our vector is

$$
\mathbf{a}=-2\mathbf{i} + 4 \mathbf{j}
$$

To find the magnitude (length) of $\mathbf{a}$, we can use the Pythagorean theorem on $\triangle OXA$.

The legs of the triangle have lengths

$$
\begin{aligned}
\begin{vmatrix}OX^(→) & &= \end{vmatrix}- 2|= 2
\end{aligned}
$$

and

$$
\begin{aligned}
\begin{vmatrix}XA^(→) & &= \end{vmatrix}4|= 4
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, using the Pythagorean theorem, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mid a \mid &= \sqrt{\begin{vmatrix}OX^(→) & ^{2} + & XA^(→)\end{vmatrix}^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{2^{2} + 4^{2}} \\
&= 2\sqrt{5}
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="general-formula"></a>
## General Formula

We just saw that the magnitude of the vector

$$
\mathbf{a}=\langle -2,4 \rangle
$$

was given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mid a \mid &= \sqrt{(-2)^{2} + 4^{2}} \\
&= 2\sqrt{5}
\end{aligned}
$$

In general, given a vector
$\begin{bmatrix}a_{x} \\ a_{y}\end{bmatrix}$
the magnitude of the vector can always be computed as

$$
\mid \,\mathbf{a}\, \mid = \sqrt{a_x^2+a_y^2}
$$

**Note**

We sometimes use the notation $\mid \mid \mathbf a \mid \mid$ to denote the magnitude of a vector.

<a id="calculating-the-magnitude-of-a-vector-given-in-component-form"></a>
## Calculating the Magnitude of a Vector Given in Component Form

**Example:** Let $\mathbf{a}=\langle -4, 6 \rangle$. Find $\mid \,\mathbf{a}\, \mid$.

**Explanation**

Using the formula, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mid a \mid &= \sqrt{a_{x}^{2} + a_{y}^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{(-4)^{2} + (6)^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{16 + 36} \\
&= 2\sqrt{13}
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="calculating-a-vector-from-position-vectors-and-then-computing-its-magnitude"></a>
## Calculating a Vector From Position Vectors and then Computing its Magnitude

**Example:** Let $A(1,3)$ and $B(2,-2)$. Find $\mid \overrightarrow{AB} \mid$.

![](<../Source/10.2.3. Calculating the Magnitude of Cartesian Vectors in 2D/Images/dec4b359e985f4d1cec149579ce8c082.png>)

**Explanation**

We have

$$
OA^(→) = a = i + 3j,; OB^(→) = b = 2i - 2j
$$

Expressing $\overrightarrow{AB}$ as the difference of position vectors, we obtain:

$$
\begin{aligned}
AB^(→) &= OB^(→) - OA^(→) \\
&= b - a \\
&= (2i - 2j) - (i + 3j) \\
&= i - 5j
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, using the formula for the magnitude of a vector, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mid AB^(→) \mid &= \sqrt{1^{2} + (-5)^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{1 + 25} \\
&= \sqrt{26}
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="calculating-a-unit-vector-parallel-to-a-given-vector"></a>
## Calculating a Unit Vector Parallel to a Given Vector

**Example:** Let $\begin{bmatrix}-2 \\ 0\end{bmatrix}$ and $\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ -1\end{bmatrix}$ Find a unit vector that is parallel to $\mathbf{a}+2\mathbf{b}$.

**Explanation**

Remember that to find a unit vector that is parallel to a given vector, we need to divide the given vector by its length:

$$
\begin{aligned}
u &= \frac{a + 2b}{\mid a + 2b \mid}
\end{aligned}
$$

First, let's compute the sum:

$$
\begin{aligned}
a + 2b &= \begin{bmatrix}-2 \\ 0\end{bmatrix} + 2 \cdot \begin{bmatrix}1 \\ -1\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}-2 \\ 0\end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix}2 \\ -2\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}-2 + 2 \\ 0 + (-2)\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}0 \\ -2\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Using the formula for the magnitude of a vector, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mid a + 2b \mid &= \sqrt{0^{2} + (-2)^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{4} \\
&= 2
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we divide $\mathbf{a}+2\mathbf{b}$ by its length and get the following unit vector:

$$
\begin{aligned}
u &= \frac{a + 2b}{\mid a + 2b \mid} \\
&= \frac{1}{2}(a + 2b) \\
&= \frac{1}{2} \cdot \begin{bmatrix}0 \\ -2\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}0 \\ -1\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$
