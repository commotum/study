## Introduction

Suppose we are given the equation

$$
\log_3 x = 4
$$

Can we determine $x$?

Recall that the inverse operation of the logarithm is exponentiation. So, to isolate $x$, we must exponentiate both sides of this equation.

The base of the logarithm is $3$, so we use a base of $3$ for the exponentiation as well:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\log_{3} x &= 4 \\
3^{(\log_{3} x)} &= 3^{4}
\end{aligned}
$$

The equation above may look complicated, but since exponentiation is the inverse function of the logarithm, we know that

$$
3^{(\log_3{x})}=x
$$

Therefore, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
x &= 3^{4} \\
x &= 81
\end{aligned}
$$

We conclude that $x = 81$ is the solution.

## Solving Logarithmic Equations

**Example:** If $\log_2 x = 5$, then what is the value of $x$?

**Explanation**

To remove the logarithm, we exponentiate both sides of the equation. The base of the logarithm is $2$, so we use a base of $2$ for the exponentiation as well:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\log_{2} x &= 5 \\
2^{\log_{2} x} &= 2^{5}
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, since exponentiation is the inverse function of the logarithm, we know that

$$
2^{\log_2 x} = x
$$

Therefore, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
x &= 2^{5} \\
x &= 32
\end{aligned}
$$

## Solving Logarithmic Equations With Linear Arguments

**Example:** If $\log_7 (2x + 1) = 1$, then what is $x$?

**Explanation**

To remove the logarithm, we exponentiate both sides of the equation. The base of the logarithm is $7$, so we use this base for the exponentiation as well:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\log_{7} (2x + 1) &= 1 \\
7^{\log_{7} (2x + 1)} &= 7^{1}
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, since exponentiation is the inverse function of the logarithm, we know that

$$
7^{\log_7 (2x + 1)} = 2x+1
$$

Therefore, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
2x + 1 &= 7 \\
2x &= 6 \\
x &= 3
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="solving-logarithmic-equations-by-first-rearranging-the-equation"></a>
## Solving Logarithmic Equations by First Rearranging the Equation

**Example:** Given that $2\log_5 (x-3)+1 = 5$, then what is the value of $x$?

**Explanation**

First, we make $\log_5 (x-3)$ the subject of the equation by moving everything else to the right-hand side. This gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
2\log_{5} (x - 3) + 1 &= 5 \\
2\log_{5} (x - 3) &= 4 \\
\log_{5} (x - 3) &= 2
\end{aligned}
$$

To remove the logarithm, we exponentiate both sides of the equation. The base of the logarithm is $5$, so we use a base of $5$ for the exponentiation as well:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\log_{5} (x - 3) &= 2 \\
5^{\log_{5} (x - 3)} &= 5^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, since exponentiation is the inverse function of the logarithm, we know that

$$
5^{\log_5 (x - 3)} = x-3
$$

Therefore, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
x - 3 &= 5^{2} \\
x - 3 &= 25 \\
x &= 28
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="solving-logarithmic-equations-by-combining-logarithmic-terms"></a>
## Solving Logarithmic Equations by Combining Logarithmic Terms

**Example:** If $7\log_6{x} - 3\log_6{x} = 2$, then what is the value of $x$?

**Explanation**

First, we simplify our equation by collecting the like logarithmic terms, and isolating the logarithm, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
7\log_{6} x - 3\log_{6} x &= 2 \\
(7 - 3)\log_{6} x &= 2 \\
4\log_{6} x &= 2 \\
\log_{6} x &= \frac{1}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

To remove the logarithm, we exponentiate both sides of the equation. The base of the logarithm is $6$, so we use a base of $6$ for the exponentiation as well:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\log_{6} x &= \frac{1}{2} \\
6^{\log_{6} x} &= 6^{1/2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, since exponentiation is the inverse function of the logarithm, we know that

$$
6^{\log_6 x} = x
$$

Therefore, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
x &= 6^{1/2} \\
x &= \sqrt{6}
\end{aligned}
$$
