## Introduction

When we transform a logarithmic function

$$
y = \log_a (x)
$$

its domain might be modified as well.

For example, the diagram below shows the graph of the transformed logarithmic function

$$
f(x) = \log_2(x-1)
$$

What is the domain of $f(x)$?

![](<../Source/5.6.3. Properties of Transformed Logarithmic Functions/Images/1b2655341e0798fee369b17aa1fbd9f6.png>)

First, recall that the domain of

$$
y=\log_a (x)
$$

is
$x > 0$.

To find the domain of $f(x)$, we take the above inequality and replace $x$ with the argument (or input) of the logarithm function contained in $f(x)$. In this case, the argument is $x-1$, so we get
$x-1 > 0$.

Solving this inequality, we obtain
$\begin{bmatrix}x - 1 & > 0 \\ x & > 1\end{bmatrix}$.

Therefore, the domain of $y=f(x)$ is $x\in(1, \infty)$.

<a id="the-domain-of-a-transformed-logarithmic-function"></a>
## The Domain of a Transformed Logarithmic Function

**Example:** What is the domain of $f(x)=\ln (2x+4)-2$?

**Explanation**

The domain of $y = \ln x$ is
$x \gt 0$.

To find the domain of $f(x)$, we replace $x$ with $2x+4$ in the above inequality. This gives
$2x+4 > 0$.

Solving this inequality, we obtain
$\begin{bmatrix}2x + 4 & > 0 \\ 2x & > - 4 \\ x & > - 2\end{bmatrix}$.

Therefore, the domain of $y = f(x)$ is

$$
x\in\left(-2, \infty \right)
$$

## The Vertical Asymptote of a Transformed Logarithmic Function

**Example:** Find the equation of the vertical asymptote of the function $f(x) = -\ln(4x)$.

**Explanation**

The equation of the vertical asymptote of the function $y = \ln{x}$ is $x=0$.

To find the equation of the vertical asymptote of $f(x)$, we set the argument of the logarithmic term equal to zero, and solve for $x$. This gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
4x &= 0 \\
x &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the equation of the vertical asymptote is $x = 0$.

<a id="the-root-of-a-transformed-logarithmic-function"></a>
## The Root of a Transformed Logarithmic Function

**Example:** Find the root of $f(x) = \log(2x) - 2$.

**Explanation**

To find the root of $f(x)$, we must solve

$$
f(x)=0
$$

$$
\log(2x) - 2 = 0
$$

We solve this equation by making $x$ the subject, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\log (2x) - 2 &= 0 \\
\log (2x) &= 2 \\
10^{\log (2x)} &= 10^{2} \\
2x &= 100 \\
x &= 50
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the root of $f(x)$ is $x=50$.

<a id="the-range-of-a-transformed-logarithmic-function"></a>
## The Range of a Transformed Logarithmic Function

When we transform a logarithmic function

$$
y = \log_a (x)
$$

its range is not affected.

For example, the diagram below shows the graph of the transformed logarithmic function

$$
f(x) = 3\log_2(x-1)+1
$$

![](<../Source/5.6.3. Properties of Transformed Logarithmic Functions/Images/0c826cdd6d7f58ee1a3d4877ba72aee8.png>)

The range of $\log_2 x$ is
$\log_2 x \in (-\infty,\infty)$.

Because the range of the logarithm function is the interval containing all real numbers, horizontal and vertical shifts and stretches do not affect its range. Therefore, the range of $f(x)$ is

$$
f(x) \in (-\infty,\infty)
$$

---

<a id="the-range-of-a-transformed-logarithmic-function"></a>
## The Range of a Transformed Logarithmic Function

**Example:** What is the range of $f(x) = 5\log_6(x-1)-4$?

**Explanation**

The range of the function

$$
y = \log_{6}(x)
$$

is $y \in (-\infty,\infty)$.

For a logarithmic function, horizontal and vertical shifts and stretches do not affect the range.

Therefore, the range of the given function is $f(x) \in (-\infty,\infty)$.
