## Introduction

Suppose we are given the function

$$
y=5\log_6(x-2)
$$

How do we plot its graph?

As usual, we can do this by combining transformations of the curve $y=\log_6 x$.

- We start with the graph of $y=\log_6 x{:}$![](<../Source/5.6.2. Combining Graph Transformations of Logarithmic Functions/Images/bdb0c6b259d1a9aa682f127b1f1aa0cf.png>)
- Translate it by $2$ units to the right to get the graph of $y=\log_6{(x-2)}$.![](<../Source/5.6.2. Combining Graph Transformations of Logarithmic Functions/Images/aa3a3e5426fe73d31f40282bb5142c17.png>)
Notice that points $(1,0)$ and $(6,1)$ on the graph of $y=\log_6 x$ map to the points $(3,0)$ and $(8,1)$ on the graph of $y=\log_6 (x-2)$, respectively.
- Then, stretch the result by a scale factor of $5$ parallel to the $y$-axis to get the graph of $y=5\log_6{(x-2)}$.![](<../Source/5.6.2. Combining Graph Transformations of Logarithmic Functions/Images/15cfecf1f7090b9961119eb5dfb0902c.png>)

So, to plot the curve of a transformed logarithmic function, we apply subsequent transformations presented in the given function, as usual.

## Finding Unknown Parameters Given a Stretched Logarithmic Function

**Example:** Given the diagram above, what is the value of $k$?

![](<../Source/5.6.2. Combining Graph Transformations of Logarithmic Functions/Images/74619885577fcd4e8f51e3eccbc9f3af.png>)

**Explanation**

The graph of

$$
y=k\log_2 {x}
$$

can be obtained from

$$
y=\log_2 {x}
$$

by a vertical stretch of scale factor $k$.

From the diagram, we deduce that under such a transformation, the point $(4,2)$ that lies on

$$
y=\log_2 {x}
$$

becomes

$$
(4,k \cdot 2)=\left(4,3\right)
$$

which lies on

$$
y=k\log_2 {x}
$$

Therefore,

$$
k=\dfrac 32
$$

## Combining Translations and Stretches of Logarithmic Functions

**Example:** Which of the following is the graph of $y=4\log_3 (x-2)+1$?

**Explanation**

To plot

$$
y=4\log_3 (x-2)+1
$$

we apply the following steps:

- Take the curve $y=\log_3 x$.![](<../Source/5.6.2. Combining Graph Transformations of Logarithmic Functions/Images/fc3cf6e2e540996b4e8a004d4acd9a04.png>)
- Translate it by $2$ units to the right to get the graph of $y=\log_3 (x-2)$.![](<../Source/5.6.2. Combining Graph Transformations of Logarithmic Functions/Images/ca49fe3eb2ff1acd76d70865f5c01f73.png>)
- Then, stretch the result by a scale factor of $4$ parallel to the $y$-axis to get the graph of $y=4\log_3 (x-2)$.![](<../Source/5.6.2. Combining Graph Transformations of Logarithmic Functions/Images/15730a7255657ae1a538fc117502c537.png>)
- Finally, translate the result by $1$ unit up to get the graph of $y=4\log_3 (x-2)+1$.![](<../Source/5.6.2. Combining Graph Transformations of Logarithmic Functions/Images/d9ff94e25ab7fcff901c7f9fc503fa2b.png>)

<a id="finding-unknown-parameters-given-a-transformed-logarithmic-function"></a>
## Finding Unknown Parameters Given a Transformed Logarithmic Function

**Example:** The diagram below shows the graph of $y=k \log_5 (bx)$. Find the value of $b\cdot k$.

![](<../Source/5.6.2. Combining Graph Transformations of Logarithmic Functions/Images/9c65b77cb64a1cbf796399b4bf72797a.png>)

**Explanation**

Clearly,

$$
b \neq 0
$$

Substituting

$$
x=\dfrac1b
$$

into the equation of the curve, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
y &= k\log_{5} (b \cdot \frac{1}{b}) \\
&= k\log_{5} 1 \\
&= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the point

$$
\left(\dfrac1b, 0\right)
$$

lies on the curve. But from the diagram, we know that $(2,0)$ lies on the curve. Hence,

$$
(2,0) = \left(\dfrac1b,0\right)\quad\Rightarrow\quad b=\dfrac12
$$

Now, we substitute coordinates of the point $(10,3)$ into the equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
k\log_{5} (bx) &= y \\
k\log_{5} (\frac{1}{2}x) &= y \\
k\log_{5} (\frac{1}{2} \cdot 10) &= 3 \\
k\log_{5} 5 &= 3 \\
k &= 3
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally,

$$
b\cdot k = \dfrac12 \cdot 3 = \dfrac32
$$
