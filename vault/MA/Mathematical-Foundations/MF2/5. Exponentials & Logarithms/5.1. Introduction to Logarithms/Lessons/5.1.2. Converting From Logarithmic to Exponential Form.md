## Introduction

So far, we have expressed an exponential equation in logarithm form. We can also go the other way, and express a logarithmic equation in exponential form.

Remember, the base of the logarithm is always the base of the exponential expression.
For instance, if we know that

$$
\log_{\color{blue}{2}}(8) = {\color{red}{3}}
$$

we could write this using an exponent as

$$
{\color{blue}{2}}^{\color{red}{3}} = 8
$$

In general, the logarithm of $a$ with base $b$ is defined as follows:

> $\log_{\color{blue}b}{a} = {\color{red}x}$ is equivalent to $a = {\color{blue}b}^{\color{red}x}$.

## Converting Equations From Logarithmic Form to Exponential Form

**Example:** The equation $\log_3{9} = 2$ is equivalent to $b^2 = 9$. What is the value of $b$?

**Explanation**

The logarithm of $a$ with base $b$ is defined as follows:

> $\log_{b}{a} = x$ is equivalent to $a = b^x$

If we compare

$$
\log_3{9} = 2
$$

with

$$
\log_{b}{a} = x
$$

we get $a = 9$, $b = 3$, and $x = 2$.

Therefore, the given equation is equivalent to $3^2 = 9$. Consequently, $b = 3$.

## Converting Equations From Logarithmic Form to Exponential Form Using Fractional Powers

**Example:** The equation $\log_{16}{4} = \dfrac{1}{2}$ is equivalent to $\sqrt{b} = 4$. What is the value of $b$?

**Explanation**

The logarithm of $a$ with base $b$ is defined as follows:

> $\log_{b}{a} = x$ is equivalent to $a = b^x$

If we compare

$$
\log_{16}{4} = \dfrac{1}{2}
$$

with

$$
\log_{b}{a} = x
$$

we get $a = 4$, $b = 16$, and

$$
x = \dfrac{1}{2}
$$

Therefore, the given equation is equivalent to

$$
16^{1/2} = 4
$$

which is also equivalent to

$$
\sqrt{16} = 4
$$

Consequently, $b = 16$.

## Converting Equations From Logarithmic Form to Exponential Form Using Negative Powers

**Example:** The equation $\log_{2}\left(\dfrac{1}{8} \right) = -3$ is equivalent to $b^{-3} = \dfrac{1}{8}$. What is the value of $b$?

**Explanation**

The logarithm of $a$ with base $b$ is defined as follows:

> $\log_{b}{a} = x$ is equivalent to $a = b^x$

If we compare

$$
\log_{2}\left(\dfrac{1}{8} \right) = -3
$$

with

$$
\log_{b}{a} = x
$$

we get

$$
a = \dfrac{1}{8}
$$

$b = 2$, and $x = -3$.

Therefore, the given equation is equivalent to

$$
2^{-3} = \dfrac{1}{8}
$$

Consequently, $b = 2$.
