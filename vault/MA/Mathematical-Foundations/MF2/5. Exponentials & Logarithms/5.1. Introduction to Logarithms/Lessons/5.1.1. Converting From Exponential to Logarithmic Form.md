## Introduction

A **logarithm** (or log) is an operation that's the *inverse* of exponentiation.

For example, we know that

$$
{\color{blue}{2}}^{\color{red}{3}} = 8
$$

But we can also write this using a logarithm as

$$
\log_{\color{blue}{2}}(8) = {\color{red}{3}}
$$

In words, we would say "log of $8$ with base ${\color{blue}{2}}$ equals ${\color{red}{3}}$". The **base** of the logarithm is always the base of the exponential expression.

Since exponents and logarithms are opposite operations, an exponential equation can always be expressed in logarithmic form, and vice versa.

In general, the logarithm of $a$ with positive base

$$
b \neq 1
$$

is defined as follows:

> $a = {\color{blue}b}^{\color{red}x}$ is equivalent to $\log_{\color{blue}b}{a} = {\color{red}x}$.

## Converting Equations From Exponential Form to Logarithmic Form

**Example:** Express $2^4 = 16$ in logarithmic form.

**Explanation**

The logarithm of $a$ with base $b$ is defined as follows:

> $a = b^x$ is equivalent to $\log_{b}{a} = x$

If we compare $2^4 = 16$ with $a = b^x$, we get $a = 16$, $b = 2$, and $x = 4$.

Therefore, the equation $2^4 = 16$ can be expressed as

$$
\log_{2}{16} = 4
$$

## Converting Equations From Exponential Form to Logarithmic Form with Exponents of Zero or One

**Example:** Express $7^0 = 1$ in logarithmic form.

**Explanation**

The logarithm of $a$ with base $b$ is defined as follows:

> $a = b^x$ is equivalent to $\log_{b}{a} = x$

If we compare $7^0 = 1$ with $a = b^x$, we get

$$
a = 1, b = 7
$$

and $x=0$.

Therefore, the equation $7^0 = 1$ is equivalent to

$$
\log_{7}{1}= 0
$$

In general,

$$
\log_n(1) = 0
$$

for any

$$
n>0, n\neq 1
$$

## Converting Equations With Negative Powers From Exponential Form to Logarithmic Form

**Example:** Express $2^{-5} = \dfrac{1}{32}$ in logarithmic form.

**Explanation**

The logarithm of $a$ with base $b$ is defined as follows:

> $a = b^x$ is equivalent to $\log_{b}{a} = x$

If we compare

$$
2^{-5} = \dfrac{1}{32}
$$

with $a = b^x$, we get

$$
a = \dfrac{1}{32}, b = 2
$$

and $x=-5$.

Therefore, the given equation can be expressed as

$$
\log_{2}\left(\dfrac{1}{32} \right)= -5
$$

<a id="converting-equations-with-radicals-into-logarithmic-form"></a>
## Converting Equations With Radicals Into Logarithmic Form

What logarithmic equation is equivalent to the equation

$$
\sqrt{4} = 2
$$

Notice that the given equation can be written using exponents as

$$
4^{1/2} = 2
$$

The logarithm of $a$ with base $b$ is defined as follows:

> $a = b^x$ is equivalent to $\log_{b}{a} = x$

If we compare

$$
4^{1/2} = 2
$$

with $a = b^x$, we get

$$
a = 2, b = 4
$$

and

$$
x=\dfrac{1}{2}
$$

Therefore, the equation

$$
\sqrt{4} = 2
$$

is equivalent to

$$
\log_{4}{2}= \dfrac{1}{2}
$$

## Converting Equations With Radicals Into Logarithmic Form

**Example:** Express $\sqrt[3]{5} \approx 1.709$ in an equivalent logarithmic form.

**Explanation**

Notice that the given approximation can be written using exponents:

$$
5^{1/3} \approx 1.709
$$

The logarithm of $a$ with base $b$ is defined as follows:

> $a = b^x$ is equivalent to $\log_{b}{a} = x$

If we compare

$$
5^{1/3} \approx 1.709
$$

with $a = b^x$, we get

$$
a = 1.709, b = 5
$$

and

$$
x=\dfrac{1}{3}
$$

Therefore, the approximation

$$
\sqrt[3]{5} \approx 1.709
$$

is equivalent to

$$
\log_{5}(1.709) \approx \dfrac{1}{3}
$$
