## Introduction

When two logarithmic terms in an expression have the same base and argument, we can combine them by adding their coefficients.

To demonstrate, let's look at the expression
$5\log_{\color{blue}4}{\color{red}7}+3\log_{\color{blue}4}{\color{red}7}$.

Both logarithmic terms have a base of $\color{blue}4$ and an argument of ${\color{red}7}$. Therefore, we can combine them by adding their coefficients:

$$
\begin{aligned}
5\log_{4} 7 + 3\log_{4} 7 &= (5 + 3)\log_{4} 7 = 8\log_{4} 7
\end{aligned}
$$

## Simplifying Logarithmic Expressions Containing Two Terms

**Example:** Simplify $2\log_{2}{5}+4\log_{2}{5}$.

**Explanation**

Both logarithmic terms have a base of $2$ and an argument of $5$. Therefore, we can combine them by adding their coefficients:

$$
\begin{aligned}
2\log_{2} 5 + 4\log_{2} 5 &= (2 + 4)\log_{2} 5 = 6\log_{2} 5
\end{aligned}
$$

## Simplifying Logarithmic Expressions Containing More Than Two Terms

**Example:** Simplify $2\log_4(9)-3\log_3(9)+5\log_4(9)$.

**Explanation**

Both the first and the third logarithmic terms have a base of ${\color{blue}4}$ and an argument of ${\color{red}9}$. Therefore, we can combine them by adding their coefficients:

$$
\begin{aligned}
2\log_{4} (9) - 3\log_{3} (9) + 5\log_{4} (9) &= (2 + 5)\log_{4} (9) - 3\log_{3} (9) \\
&= 7\log_{4} (9) - 3\log_{3} (9)
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="simplifying-logarithmic-expressions-containing-variable-expressions"></a>
## Simplifying Logarithmic Expressions Containing Variable Expressions

**Example:** Simplify $9\log_6(2x)-2\log_4(2x)-3\log_4(2x)$.

**Explanation**

Both the second and the third logarithmic terms have a base of ${\color{blue}4}$ and an argument of ${\color{red}2x}$. Therefore, we can combine them by adding their coefficients:

$$
\begin{aligned}
9\log_{6} (2x) - 2\log_{4} (2x) - 3\log_{4} (2x) &= 9\log_{6} (2x) + (-2 - 3)\log_{4} (2x) \\
&= 9\log_{6} (2x) - 5\log_{4} (2x)
\end{aligned}
$$
