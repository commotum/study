## Introduction

The **magnitude** of a complex number is its distance from the origin. It's denoted as $\mid z \mid$.

For example, consider the complex number

$$
z=-4+5\textrm{i}
$$

shown in the complex plane below.

![](<../Source/3.3.2. The Magnitude of a Complex Number/Images/8db46a9aac50b0c9b767f7883c71e174.png>)

Using the Pythagorean theorem, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mid z \mid &= \sqrt{(-4)^{2} + (5)^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{16 + 25} \\
&= \sqrt{41}
\end{aligned}
$$

In general, for a complex number

$$
z=a+b\textrm{i}
$$

the magnitude of $z$ can be found as follows:

$$
\mid z \mid = \sqrt{a^2+b^2}
$$

## Finding the Magnitude of a Complex Number

**Example:** Find $\left|z\right|$ if $z=3 - 4\textrm{i}$.

**Explanation**

For a complex number

$$
z=a+b\textrm{i}
$$

the magnitude of $z$ is given by

$$
\mid z \mid = \sqrt{a^2+b^2}
$$

For the complex number

$$
z=3-4\textrm{i}
$$

we have $a=3$ and $b=-4$. So, the magnitude is given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mid z \mid &= \sqrt{(3)^{2} + (-4)^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{9 + 16} \\
&= \sqrt{25} \\
&= 5
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding the Magnitude of a Complex Number With No Real Part

**Example:** What is the magnitude of the number $\textrm{i}\sqrt{2}$?

**Explanation**

The imaginary number $\textrm{i} \sqrt{2}$ is equivalent to the complex number

$$
z = 0 + \sqrt{2} \textrm{i}
$$

We know that for a complex number

$$
z=a+b\textrm{i}
$$

the magnitude of $z$ is given by

$$
\mid z \mid = \sqrt{a^2+b^2}
$$

So, its magnitude of

$$
z=0 + \sqrt{2} \textrm{i}
$$

is given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mid z \mid &= \sqrt{a^{2} + b^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{(0)^{2} + (\sqrt{2})^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{0 + 2} \\
&= \sqrt{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding the Magnitude of a Complex Number With Variable Coefficients

**Example:** Find $\left|z\right|$ if $z=5c - 9d\textrm{i}$.

**Explanation**

For a complex number

$$
z=a+b\textrm{i}
$$

the magnitude of $z$ is given by

$$
\mid z \mid = \sqrt{a^2+b^2}
$$

For the complex number

$$
z=5c - 9d\textrm{i}
$$

we have $a=5c$ and $b=-9d$. So, the magnitude is given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mid z \mid &= \sqrt{(5c)^{2} + (-9d)^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{25c^{2} + 81d^{2}}
\end{aligned}
$$
