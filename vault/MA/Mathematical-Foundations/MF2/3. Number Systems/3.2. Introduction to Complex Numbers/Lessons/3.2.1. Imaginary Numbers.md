## Introduction

Up until now, you've been told that you can't take the square root of a negative number. Well, this is actually not entirely true.

To take the square root of a negative number, we need to expand our domain beyond the real numbers (integers, rationals, and irrationals) to the realm of **imaginary numbers**.

The fundamental imaginary number is $\textrm{i}$, which is defined to be the square root of $-1$:

$$
\textrm{i} =\sqrt{-1}
$$

Following this, we can find the square root of any other negative number, such as $-9$, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{-9} &= \sqrt{9 \cdot (-1)} \\
&= \sqrt{9} \cdot \sqrt{-1} \\
&= 3 \cdot \textrm{i} \\
&= 3\textrm{i}
\end{aligned}
$$

All other imaginary numbers are multiples of $\textrm{i}$.

<a id="finding-the-square-root-of-a-negative-number"></a>
## Finding the Square Root of a Negative Number

**Example:** Evaluate $\sqrt{-36}$.

**Explanation**

The imaginary unit $\textrm{i}$ is defined as

$$
\textrm{i} = \sqrt{-1}
$$

Therefore, the given number can be rewritten as

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{-36} &= \sqrt{36 \cdot (-1)} \\
&= \sqrt{36} \cdot \sqrt{-1} \\
&= 6\textrm{i}
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="finding-the-negative-square-root-of-a-negative-number"></a>
## Finding the Negative Square Root of a Negative Number

**Example:** Find the value of $-\sqrt{-9}$.

**Explanation**

The imaginary unit $\textrm{i}$ is defined as

$$
\textrm{i} = \sqrt{-1}
$$

Therefore, the given number can be rewritten as

$$
\begin{aligned}
-\sqrt{-9} &= -\sqrt{9 \cdot (-1)} \\
&=-\sqrt{9} \cdot \sqrt{-1} \\
&=-3\textrm{i}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding the Square Root of a Negative Number With Simplifications

**Example:** Evaluate $\sqrt{-18}$.

**Explanation**

The imaginary unit $\textrm{i}$ is defined as

$$
\textrm{i} = \sqrt{-1}
$$

Since

$$
18 = 9 \cdot 2
$$

the given number can be rewritten as

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{-18} &= \sqrt{18 \cdot (-1)} \\
&= \sqrt{9 \cdot 2 \cdot (-1)} \\
&= \sqrt{9} \cdot \sqrt{2} \cdot \sqrt{-1} \\
&= 3\sqrt{2}\textrm{i}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding the Square Root of a Negative Fraction

**Example:** Find the value of $\sqrt{-\dfrac{25}{16}}$.

**Explanation**

The imaginary unit $\textrm{i}$ is defined as

$$
\textrm{i} = \sqrt{-1}
$$

Therefore, the given number can be rewritten as

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{-\frac{25}{16}} &= \sqrt{\frac{25}{16} \cdot (-1)} \\
&= \sqrt{\frac{25}{16}} \cdot \sqrt{-1} \\
&= \frac{\sqrt{25}}{\sqrt{16}} \cdot \sqrt{-1} \\
&= \frac{5}{4}\textrm{i}
\end{aligned}
$$
