## Introduction

Multiplying a purely imaginary number by a real number is similar to multiplying two monomials.

For example, we can multiply the purely imaginary number $3 \textrm{i}$ by the real number $6$ as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
6 \cdot 3\textrm{i} &= (6 \cdot 3)\textrm{i} = 18\textrm{i}
\end{aligned}
$$

Similarly, we can multiply a complex number by a real number using the distributive property.

For example, we can multiply the complex number $2-3\textrm{i}$ by the real number $3$ as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned} 3(2-3\textrm{i}) &= 3\cdot2 + 3\cdot(-3\textrm{i}) \\[3pt] &= 6 + 3\cdot (-3)\textrm{i} \\[3pt] &= 6 -9\textrm{i} \end{aligned}
$$

## Multiplying a Complex Number by a Real Number

**Example:** Evaluate $2 \cdot 3\,\textrm{i}$.

**Explanation**

Multiplying a purely imaginary number by a real number is similar to multiplying two monomials.

For the given problem, we have

$$
2\cdot 3\,\textrm{i} = (2\cdot 3) \,\textrm{i} = 6\,\textrm{i}
$$

<a id="multiplying-by-a-purely-imaginary-number"></a>
## Multiplying by a Purely Imaginary Number

Multiplying two purely imaginary numbers is similar to multiplying two monomials. But we also need to use the fact that

$$
\textrm{i}^2=-1
$$

For example, to multiply $2\textrm{i}$ and $3\textrm{i}$, we proceed as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(2\textrm{i})(3\textrm{i}) &= 2 \cdot 3 \cdot \textrm{i}^{2} \\
&= 6\textrm{i}^{2} \\
&= 6(-1) \\
&=-6
\end{aligned}
$$

Knowing this, we can also multiply a complex number by a purely imaginary number.
For instance, to multiply $2\textrm{i}$ and $4-3\textrm{i}$, we use the distributive property and the fact that

$$
\textrm{i}^2=-1
$$

$$
\begin{aligned} 2\textrm{i}(4-3\textrm{i}) &= 2\textrm{i}\cdot 4+2\textrm{i}\cdot(-3\textrm{i}) \\[3pt] &=2\cdot 4\textrm{i}+2\cdot(-3)\textrm{i}^2 \\[3pt] &= 8\textrm{i}+(-6)(-1) \\[3pt] &= 8\textrm{i}+6 \\[3pt] &=6+8\textrm{i} \end{aligned}
$$

---

<a id="multiplying-two-purely-imaginary-numbers"></a>
## Multiplying Two Purely Imaginary Numbers

**Example:** Evaluate $2\,\textrm{i}\cdot (-3\,\textrm{i})$.

**Explanation**

Multiplying two purely imaginary numbers is similar to multiplying two monomials. We also use the fact that

$$
\textrm{i}^2=-1
$$

For the given problem, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
2\textrm{i} \cdot (-3\textrm{i}) &= 2 \cdot (-3) \cdot \textrm{i}^{2} \\
&=-6\textrm{i}^{2} \\
&=-6 \cdot (-1) \\
&= 6
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="multiplying-a-complex-number-by-a-purely-imaginary-number"></a>
## Multiplying a Complex Number by a Purely Imaginary Number

**Example:** Evaluate $6\,\textrm{i}\cdot(4 + 2\,\textrm{i})$.

**Explanation**

To multiply a complex number by a purely imaginary number, we use the distributive property and the fact that

$$
\textrm{i}^2 = -1
$$

For the given problem, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
6\textrm{i} \cdot (4 + 2\textrm{i}) &= 6\textrm{i} \cdot 4 + 6\textrm{i} \cdot 2\textrm{i} \\
&= 4 \cdot 6\textrm{i} + 6 \cdot 2 \cdot \textrm{i}^{2} \\
&= 24\textrm{i} + 12 \cdot (-1) \\
&= 24\textrm{i} - 12 \\
&=-12 + 24\textrm{i}
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="multiplying-two-complex-numbers"></a>
## Multiplying Two Complex Numbers

Finally, we can multiply two complex numbers just like multiplying two binomials. Again, we use the fact that

$$
\mathrm{i}^2=-1
$$

So, to multiply $2 + \textrm{i}$ and $5 + 3\textrm{i}$, we proceed as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned} (2+\textrm{i})(5+3\textrm{i}) &= 2 (5+3\textrm{i}) + \textrm{i} (5+3\textrm{i}) \\ &= 2\cdot5 + 2\cdot(3\textrm{i}) +\textrm{i}\cdot 5+ \textrm{i} \cdot (3\textrm{i}) \\ &= 10 + 6\textrm{i} + 5\textrm{i} +3\textrm{i}^2 \\ &= 10 + 11\textrm{i} + 3(-1) \\ &= 10 + 11\textrm{i} - 3 \\ &= 7 + 11\textrm{i} \end{aligned}
$$

---

<a id="multiplying-two-complex-numbers"></a>
## Multiplying Two Complex Numbers

**Example:** Evaluate $(1 - 3\,\textrm{i})(2 + 4\, \textrm{i})$.

**Explanation**

Multiplying two complex numbers is similar to multiplying two binomials. We also use the fact that

$$
\textrm{i}^2 = -1
$$

For the given problem, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
(1 - 3\textrm{i})(2 + 4\textrm{i}) &= 1 \cdot 2 + 1 \cdot 4\textrm{i} + (-3\textrm{i}) \cdot 2 + (-3\textrm{i}) \cdot 4\textrm{i} \\
&= 2 + 4\textrm{i} - 6\textrm{i} - 12\textrm{i}^{2} \\
&= 2 - 2\textrm{i} - 12 \cdot (-1) \\
&= 2 - 2\textrm{i} + 12 \\
&= 14 - 2\textrm{i}
\end{aligned}
$$
