## Introduction

In the coordinate plane, the **midpoint** of a line segment with endpoints $(x_1,y_1)$ and $(x_2,y_2)$ lies halfway between the $x$-coordinates $x_1$ and $x_2$, and halfway between the $y$-coordinates $y_1$ and $y_2$.

The coordinates of the midpoint can be computed using the formula

$$
(x_m,y_m) = \left(\dfrac{x_1 +x_2}2,\dfrac{y_1+y_2}2\right)
$$

For example, the coordinates of the midpoint of a line segment with endpoints $(2,1)$ and $(6,13)$ can be found as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x_{m}, y_{m}) &= (\frac{x_{1} + x_{2}}{2}, \frac{y_{1} + y_{2}}{2}) \\
&= (\frac{2 + 6}{2}, \frac{1 + 13}{2}) \\
&= (\frac{8}{2}, \frac{14}{2}) \\
&= (4, 7)
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding the Midpoint of a Segment

**Example:** Find the midpoint of the line segment shown below.

![](<../Source/8.1.4. Midpoints in the Coordinate Plane/Images/3a11ff422e440cb39cc5ab7cb6ccdb50.png>)

**Explanation**

Let $(x_m,y_m)$ be the midpoint between the given points $x_{1}, y_{1})$ and $x_{2}, y_{2})$

We use the midpoint formula as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x_{m}, y_{m}) &= (\frac{x_{1} + x_{2}}{2}, \frac{y_{1} + y_{2}}{2}) \\
&= (\frac{-7 + 5}{2}, (3 + (-3))/(2)) \\
&= (\frac{-2}{2}, \frac{0}{2}) \\
&= (-1, 0)
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding the Midpoint of a Segment With Variable Coordinates

**Example:** Find the midpoint of the line segment with endpoints $(5a,0)$ and $(-a,8b)$.

**Explanation**

Let $(x_m,y_m)$ be the midpoint between the given points $x_{1}, y_{1})$ and $x_{2}, y_{2})$

We use the midpoint formula as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x_{m}, y_{m}) &= (\frac{x_{1} + x_{2}}{2}, \frac{y_{1} + y_{2}}{2}) \\
&= ((5a + (-a))/(2), \frac{0 + 8b}{2}) \\
&= (\frac{4a}{2}, \frac{8b}{2}) \\
&= (2a, 4b)
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding the Coordinates of Endpoints

Suppose $M(4,2)$ is the midpoint between $A(1,1)$ and $B(a,b)$. What are the values of $a$ and $b$?

![](<../Source/8.1.4. Midpoints in the Coordinate Plane/Images/4944c95dfef380ca92bd606c4b2380c6.png>)

Again, we use the midpoint formula:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x_{m}, y_{m}) &= (\frac{x_{1} + x_{2}}{2}, \frac{y_{1} + y_{2}}{2})
\end{aligned}
$$

We have

$$
\begin{aligned}
\begin{bmatrix}x_{1} &= 1, & y_{1}|= 1, \\ x_{2}|= a, & y_{2}|= b, \\ x_{m}|= 4, & y_{m}|= 2\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Substituting the known values in the formula, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
(4, 2) &= (\frac{1 + a}{2}, \frac{1 + b}{2})
\end{aligned}
$$

Setting the $x$-coordinates equal to each other and the $y$-coordinates equal to each other, we have the following two equations:

$$
{4 = \frac{1 + a}{2}; 2 = \frac{1 + b}{2}
$$

Multiplying both sides of the equations by $2$, we have:

$$
{8 = 1 + a; 4 = 1 + b
$$

Finally, we solve both equations independently:

$$
{a = 8 - 1 = 7; b = 4 - 1 = 3
$$

Therefore, $a=7$ and $b=3$.

## Finding the Coordinates of an Endpoint Given the Midpoint

**Example:** Find $x+y$ if the midpoint of the line segment with endpoints $(-1,3)$ and $(x,y)$ is $(-2,7)$.

![](<../Source/8.1.4. Midpoints in the Coordinate Plane/Images/d48cc5a027c30aa09b921b01069fe9d9.png>)

**Explanation**

The given points are $x_{1}, y_{1})$ $x_{2}, y_{2})$ and $x_{m}, y_{m})$

According to the midpoint formula, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x_{m}, y_{m}) &= (\frac{x_{1} + x_{2}}{2}, \frac{y_{1} + y_{2}}{2}) \\
(-2, 7) &= (\frac{-1 + x}{2}, \frac{3 + y}{2})
\end{aligned}
$$

Equating the corresponding $x$- and $y$-coordinates in the equation above gives us

$$
{-2 = \frac{-1 + x}{2}; 7 = \frac{3 + y}{2}⇒{x =-3; y = 11
$$

Therefore, the second endpoint is

$$
(x,y)=(-3,11)
$$

Consequently,

$$
x+y=8
$$
