## Introduction

We can use a protractor to **rotate** a point by a certain angle about another point.

To demonstrate, let's rotate the point $A$ below by an angle of $80^\circ$ **counterclockwise** around the point $O$.

![](<../Source/8.2.2. Rotations of Geometric Figures/Images/4814f94604f4e33d93d7ac7c4feb4344.png>)

**Step 1.** We draw a ray $\overset{\longrightarrow}{OA}$

![](<../Source/8.2.2. Rotations of Geometric Figures/Images/d684b4f25eb1c0ab0218f232d0f350a6.png>)

**Step 2.** Using a protractor, draw a **counterclockwise** angle $\angle {BOA}$ which measures $80^\circ$.

![](<../Source/8.2.2. Rotations of Geometric Figures/Images/f88048acdd8ddbf7f433c7a9f6c6aeea.png>)

**Step 3.** Using a compass, we mark a point $A'$ on $\overset{\longrightarrow}{OB}$ such that the segment $\overline{OA'}$ is congruent to $\overline{OA}$.

![](<../Source/8.2.2. Rotations of Geometric Figures/Images/2aed782518da09a3f8ab509a0b94a45d.png>)

The point $A'$ is the image of $A$ under a rotation of $80^\circ$ counterclockwise around the point $O$. We're done!

## Rotating Polygons

To rotate a polygon about a point, we rotate each of its vertices and then connect the vertices with line segments.

For example, suppose the triangle $\triangle ABC$ below is rotated by $135^\circ$ clockwise about the point $C$. What is the resulting polygon?

![](<../Source/8.2.2. Rotations of Geometric Figures/Images/1c460c6ca84f04aa83f09cacdca6a946.png>)

To rotate the triangle, we rotate each vertex and connect the resulting vertices using line segments.

First, let's rotate the point $B$. Placing a protractor on the ray $\overset{\longrightarrow}{CB}$, we draw an angle of $135^\circ$.

![](<../Source/8.2.2. Rotations of Geometric Figures/Images/cfcf471997e98c4efe1d76d4570851a9.png>)

Using a compass on the second side of the angle, we mark a point $B'$ such that $\overline{CB} \cong \overline{CB'}$.

![](<../Source/8.2.2. Rotations of Geometric Figures/Images/44a5ac6b3f294bae031116ba4cb32788.png>)

We use the same method to rotate $A$, which gives $A'$ as the resulting point.

![](<../Source/8.2.2. Rotations of Geometric Figures/Images/f66c7522d81798252088ecfd47f7dde6.png>)

Finally, we note that the position of $C$ doesn't change as it's the center of the rotation.

Our rotated triangle $\triangle A'B'C$ is shown below.

![](<../Source/8.2.2. Rotations of Geometric Figures/Images/e857cc648753ffe6ce5b73d444741153.png>)
