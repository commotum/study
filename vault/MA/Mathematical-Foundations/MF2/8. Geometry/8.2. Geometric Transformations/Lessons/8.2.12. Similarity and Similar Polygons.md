## Introduction

Two polygons are **similar** if they have the same shape (though they may have different sizes).

More precisely, two polygons are similar if the following conditions hold:

1. Their corresponding angles are congruent.
2. The ratios of the lengths of their corresponding sides are all equal.

To demonstrate, let's consider the triangles $\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle EFG$ below.

![](<../Source/8.2.12. Similarity and Similar Polygons/Images/3d52e7a8ed9c3af8e7698eac4dc56862.png>)

From the figure above, we see that the corresponding angles are congruent:

$$
\angle A \cong \angle E, \qquad \angle B \cong \angle F, \qquad \angle C \cong \angle G
$$

We also need to check whether the ratios of the corresponding sides are the same. That is, we have to check whether

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{AB}{EF} &= \frac{BC}{FG} = \frac{AC}{EG}
\end{aligned}
$$

Substituting the lengths of the sides, we get the following:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{AB}{EF} &= \frac{6}{12} = \frac{1}{2} \\
\frac{BC}{FG} &= \frac{4}{8} = \frac{1}{2} \\
\frac{AC}{EG} &= \frac{5}{10} = \frac{1}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

The ratios are the same. Therefore, we conclude that the triangles $\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle EFG$ are similar. The common ratio of the sides,

$$
\dfrac{1}{2}
$$

is called the **similarity ratio**.

To express mathematically that the triangles $\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle EFG$ are similar, we write

$$
\triangle ABC \sim \triangle EFG
$$

## Determining the Similarity Ratio Between Two Similar Polygons

**Example:** Given that the two polygons shown below are similar, determine the similarity ratio.

*Hint: Consider the ratio of the **larger** polygon to the **smaller** polygon.*

![](<../Source/8.2.12. Similarity and Similar Polygons/Images/a53c21dce9cd73bd6857eb63571d1436.png>)

**Explanation**

Given two similar polygons, the similarity ratio is the ratio of any two corresponding sides.

Notice that, for example, $\overline{LM}$ is the longest side of $NLMP$. Hence, it corresponds to the longest side of $ABCD$, which is $\overline{BC}$.

Therefore, the similarity ratio is

$$
\begin{aligned}
k &= \frac{BC}{LM} \\
&= \frac{33}{22} \\
&= \frac{3}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

**Note:** The ratio of the smaller polygon to the larger polygon would be

$$
\dfrac{1}{\left(\dfrac{3}{2}\right)}=\dfrac{2}{3}
$$

<a id="identifying-shapes-similar-to-a-given-polygon"></a>
## Identifying Shapes Similar to a Given Polygon

**Example:** Which of the following quadrilaterals are similar to $\mathcal{R}$ (other than $\mathcal R$ itself)?

![](<../Source/8.2.12. Similarity and Similar Polygons/Images/aa402ee836ac878ab164f59e3001f0e5.png>)

**Explanation**

First, notice that $\mathcal{R}$ is a rectangle, meaning all the angles measure $90^\circ$, and it has $2$ pairs of parallel congruent sides.

With that in mind, let's examine each of the given polygons.

- $\mathcal{Q}$ is *not* similar to $\mathcal{R}$. The polygon $\mathcal{Q}$ is a rectangle, and the corresponding angles are congruent, but the ratios of the lengths of the corresponding sides are not the same:
$\dfrac{8}{6}\neq\dfrac{3}{3}$
- $\mathcal{P}$ is similar to $\mathcal{R}$. Indeed, $\mathcal{P}$ is a rectangle. Furthermore, the corresponding angles are congruent, and the ratios of the lengths of the corresponding sides are the same:
$\dfrac{4}{6}=\dfrac{2}{3}$
- $\mathcal{S}$ is *not* similar to $\mathcal{R}$ because $\mathcal{S}$ is not a rectangle (it's a parallelogram).

Therefore, the correct answer is "$\mathcal{P}$ only."

<a id="correctly-writing-similarity-statements"></a>
## Correctly Writing Similarity Statements

When writing a similarity statement, corresponding vertices should appear in the same position in the statement. For example, if we're given that

$$
ABC \sim PQR
$$

for two polygons $ABC$ and $PQR$, we can immediately deduce that

$$
\angle A \cong \angle P, \qquad \angle B \cong \angle Q, \qquad \angle C \cong \angle R
$$

We can also deduce that

$$
k = \dfrac{AB}{PQ}= \dfrac{BC}{QR} = \dfrac{AC}{PR}
$$

where $k$ is the similarity ratio.

<a id="determining-the-correct-similarity-statement-between-two-similar-polygons"></a>
## Determining the Correct Similarity Statement Between Two Similar Polygons

**Example:** Given that the two quadrilaterals below are similar, write down a correct similarity statement.

![](<../Source/8.2.12. Similarity and Similar Polygons/Images/a11d56e4ead1bf76ae58717704a70251.png>)

**Explanation**

First, let's find the measures of unknown angles. Since the sum of the measures of internal angles of a quadrilateral is $360^\circ$, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
m∠A + m∠B + m∠C + m∠D &= 360^{∘} \\
55^{∘} + m∠B + 90^{∘} + 125^{∘} &= 360^{∘} \\
m∠B + 270^{∘} &= 360^{∘} \\
m∠B &= 90^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

Similarly,

$$
\begin{aligned}
m∠K + m∠L + m∠M + m∠N &= 360^{∘} \\
55^{∘} + 90^{∘} + m∠M + 125^{∘} &= 360^{∘} \\
m∠M + 270^{∘} &= 360^{∘} \\
m∠M &= 90^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

Hence, the quadrilaterals are similar, and

$$
∠A≅∠K, ∠D≅∠N, ∠C≅∠L≅∠B≅∠M
$$

So far, we have that

- $A$ corresponds to $K$, and
- $D$ corresponds to $N$.

From the angle measurements, it's unclear whether $C$ corresponds to $L$ or $M$ (these are all right angles). However, $C$ sits between a $125^\circ$ angle and a $90^\circ$ angle, and so does $M$ (whereas $L$ does not). So, we also have that

- $C$ corresponds to $M$, and
- $B$ corresponds to $L$.

Therefore, we conclude that a correct similarity statement is
$ABCD \sim KLMN$.

<a id="identifying-possible-lengths-of-sides-for-a-polygon-given-dimensions-of-a-similar-polygon"></a>
## Identifying Possible Lengths of Sides for a Polygon Given Dimensions of a Similar Polygon

**Example:** Given that $ABCD \sim A'B'C'D'$, $AB = 3\,\mathrm{ft}$, and $BC = 4\,\mathrm{ft}$,
which of the following could be the values of $A'B'$ and $B'C'$?

1. ${A'B'} = 15\, \mathrm{ft}, \, {B'C'} = 20\, \mathrm{ft}$
2. ${A'B'} = 30\, \mathrm{ft}, \, {B'C'} = 44\, \mathrm{ft}$
3. ${A'B'} = 18\, \mathrm{ft}, \, {B'C'} = 24\, \mathrm{ft}$

**Explanation**

For similar polygons, the ratios of lengths of the corresponding sides should be the same.

- For option I, the ratios are the same:
$\frac{AB}{A^{′}B^{′}}|= \frac{3}{15} = \frac{1}{5}; \frac{BC}{B^{′}C^{′}}|= \frac{4}{20} = \frac{1}{5}$
So, option I indeed gives possible values of $A'B'$ and $B'C'$.
- For option II, the ratios are different:
$\frac{AB}{A^{′}B^{′}}|= \frac{3}{30} = \frac{1}{10}; \frac{BC}{B^{′}C^{′}}|= \frac{4}{44} = \frac{1}{11}$
So, option II does not give possible values of $A'B'$ and $B'C'$.
- For option III, the ratios are the same:
$\frac{AB}{A^{′}B^{′}}|= \frac{3}{18} = \frac{1}{6}; \frac{BC}{B^{′}C^{′}}|= \frac{4}{24} = \frac{1}{6}$
So, option III indeed gives possible values of $A'B'$ and $B'C'$.

Therefore, the correct answer is "I and III only."
