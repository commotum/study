## Introduction

Many quadratic equations have two solutions. However, it's also possible for a quadratic equation to have only one solution or no real solutions at all.

For example, consider the following quadratic equation:

$$
(3x-6)^2=0
$$

We can solve this equation using the square root method:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(3x - 6)^{2} &= 0 \\
\sqrt{(3x - 6)^{2}} &= \sqrt{0} \\
\mid 3x - 6 \mid &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

Normally, at this stage, we would obtain two solutions by setting the inside of the absolute value equal to positive or negative $(\pm)$ the right-hand side. However, in this case, the right-hand side is zero, and $+0$ and $-0$ are both just $0$.

So, we have only one solution:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mid 3x - 6 \mid &= 0 \\
3x - 6 &= 0 \\
3x &= 6 \\
x &= 2
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="cases-when-the-perfect-square-is-isolated"></a>
## Cases When the Perfect Square is Isolated

**Example:** Solve $-2(3k-12)^2 = 0$.

**Explanation**

We can solve this equation using the square root method:

$$
\begin{aligned}
-2(3k - 12)^{2} &= 0 \\
(3k - 12)^{2} &= 0 \\
\sqrt{(3k - 12)^{2}} &= \sqrt{0} \\
\mid 3k - 12 \mid &= 0 \\
3k - 12 &= 0 \\
3k &= 12 \\
k &= 4
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="finding-an-unknown-parameter"></a>
## Finding an Unknown Parameter

**Example:** Which value of $k$ gives precisely one solution to the equation $(x+3)^2+10=k$?

**Explanation**

First, we solve for $x$ as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x + 3)^{2} + 10 &= k \\
(x + 3)^{2} &= k - 10 \\
\sqrt{(x + 3)^{2}} &= \sqrt{k - 10} \\
\mid x + 3 \mid &= \sqrt{k - 10}
\end{aligned}
$$

The equation has only one solution if the right-hand side is equal to zero. So, we solve for the value of $k$ that makes the right-hand side equal to zero:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{k - 10} &= 0 \\
k - 10 &= 0 \\
k &= 10
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, $k=10$ will give only one solution to the equation.

<a id="factoring-a-perfect-square"></a>
## Factoring a Perfect Square

**Example:** Given that $g^2 -18g + 81 = 0$, what are the values of $g$?

**Explanation**

First, notice that the left-hand side is a perfect square. It just needs to be factored:

$$
\begin{aligned}
g^{2} - 18g + 81 &= 0 \\
g^{2} - 2 \cdot 9 \cdot g + 9^{2} &= 0 \\
(g - 9)^{2} &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

From here, we solve the equation using the square root method:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{(g - 9)^{2}} &= \sqrt{0} \\
\mid g - 9 \mid &= 0 \\
g - 9 &= 0 \\
g &= 9
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="perfect-square-quadratic-equations-with-no-real-solutions"></a>
## Perfect Square Quadratic Equations With No Real Solutions

Let's try and solve the equation

$$
(x+1)^2=-1
$$

using the square root method.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x + 1)^{2} &= -1 \\
\sqrt{(x + 1)^{2}} &= \sqrt{-1} \\
\mid x + 1 \mid &= \sqrt{-1}
\end{aligned}
$$

We have a problem here. We cannot take the square root of a negative number. For that reason, the equation above has *no real solutions*.

---

<a id="equations-with-no-real-solutions"></a>
## Equations With No Real Solutions

**Example:** Consider the equation $(4x+1)^2 +2=3k$. For which values of $k$ does this equation have no real solutions?

**Explanation**

Let's try and solve using the square root method:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(4x + 1)^{2} + 2 &= 3k \\
(4x + 1)^{2} &= 3k - 2 \\
\sqrt{(4x + 1)^{2}} &= \sqrt{3k - 2} \\
\mid 4x + 1 \mid &= \sqrt{3k - 2}
\end{aligned}
$$

The equation will have no real solutions if the expression under the square root is negative, which gives the following condition:
$3k-2 < 0$

We solve this inequality for $k$:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}3k - 2 & < 0 \\ 3k & < 2 \\ k & < \frac{2}{3}\end{bmatrix}
$$

Therefore, any value of $k$ that is less than

$$
\dfrac 2 3
$$

will give an equation with no real solutions.
