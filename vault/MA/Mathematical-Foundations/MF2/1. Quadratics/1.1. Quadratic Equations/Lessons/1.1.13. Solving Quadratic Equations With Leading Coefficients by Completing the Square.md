## Introduction

We can use the method of completing the square to solve quadratic equations with leading coefficients.

As an example, let's solve the following quadratic equation by completing the square:

$$
4x^2-8x+3 = 0
$$

First, we move the constant term to the right-hand side and complete the square on the left-hand side.

$$
\begin{aligned}
4x^{2} - 8x + 3 &= 0 \\
4x^{2} - 8x &= -3 \\
4(x^{2} - 2x) &= -3 \\
4(x^{2} - 2 \cdot 1 \cdot x + 1^{2} - 1^{2}) &= -3 \\
4[(x - 1)^{2} - 1] &= -3
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, expanding the brackets, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
4[(x - 1)^{2} - 1] &= -3 \\
4(x - 1)^{2} - 4 &= -3
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we isolate the perfect square by adding $4$ to both sides and then dividing by $4$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
4(x - 1)^{2} - 4 &= -3 \\
4(x - 1)^{2} - 4 + 4 &= -3 + 4 \\
4(x - 1)^{2} &= 1 \\
(x - 1)^{2} &= \frac{1}{4}
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we solve the equation using the square root method:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x - 1)^{2} &= \frac{1}{4} \\
\sqrt{(x - 1)^{2}} &= \sqrt{\frac{1}{4}} \\
\mid x - 1 \mid &= \frac{1}{2} \\
x - 1 &= ± \frac{1}{2} \\
x &= 1 ± \frac{1}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we simplify the solutions:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_{1} &= 1 - \frac{1}{2} = \frac{1}{2} \\
x_{2} &= 1 + \frac{1}{2} = \frac{3}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the solutions are

$$
x=\dfrac12
$$

and

$$
x=\dfrac32
$$

## Solving a Quadratic Equation Given a Corresponding Perfect Square

**Example:** Solve the equation $-2x^2 - 4x = 1$.

*Hint: Use the fact that $x^2 + 2x + 1$ is a perfect square.*

**Explanation**

Using the hint, we have

$$
x^2 + 2x + 1 = (x + 1)^2
$$

First, we complete the square on the left-hand side:

$$
\begin{aligned}
-2x^{2} - 4x &= 1 \\
- 2(x^{2} + 2x) &= 1 \\
- 2(x^{2} + 2 \cdot 1x) &= 1 \\
- 2(x^{2} + 2 \cdot 1x + 1^{2} - 1^{2}) &= 1 \\
- 2[(x + 1)^{2} - 1^{2}] &= 1 \\
- 2[(x + 1)^{2} - 1] &= 1 \\
- 2(x + 1)^{2} + (-2)(-1) &= 1 \\
- 2(x + 1)^{2} + 2 &= 1
\end{aligned}
$$

Isolating the perfect square by subtracting $2$ from both sides and then dividing by $(-2)$, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
-2(x + 1)^{2} + 2 &= 1 \\
- 2(x + 1)^{2} + 2 - 2 &= 1 - 2 \\
- 2(x + 1)^{2} &= -1 \\
(x + 1)^{2} &= ((-1))/((-2)) \\
(x + 1)^{2} &= \frac{1}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

We now solve the equation using the square root method:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x + 1)^{2} &= \frac{1}{2} \\
\sqrt{(x + 1)^{2}} &= \sqrt{\frac{1}{2}} \\
\mid x + 1 \mid &= \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \\
x + 1 &= ± \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \\
x &= -1 ± \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Solving a Quadratic Equation With a Positive Leading Coefficient

**Example:** Write the equation $2x^2 - 6x = 1$ in the form $(x+p)^2 = q$, and hence solve the equation.

**Explanation**

First, we divide out by the coefficient of the quadratic term, and complete the square on the left-hand side.

$$
\begin{aligned}
2x^{2} - 6x &= 1 \\
2(x^{2} - 3x) &= 1 \\
x^{2} - 3x &= \frac{1}{2} \\
x^{2} - 2 \cdot \frac{3}{2} \cdot x &= \frac{1}{2} \\
x^{2} - 2 \cdot (\frac{3}{2}) \cdot x + (\frac{3}{2})^{2} - (\frac{3}{2})^{2} &= \frac{1}{2} \\
(x - \frac{3}{2})^{2} - (\frac{3}{2})^{2} &= \frac{1}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we isolate the perfect square:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x - \frac{3}{2})^{2} - (\frac{3}{2})^{2} &= \frac{1}{2} \\
(x - \frac{3}{2})^{2} - \frac{9}{4} &= \frac{2}{4} \\
(x - \frac{3}{2})^{2} &= \frac{11}{4}
\end{aligned}
$$

So,

$$
p=-\dfrac32
$$

and

$$
q=\dfrac{11}{4}
$$

Now, we solve the equation using the square root method:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{(x - \frac{3}{2})^{2}} &= \sqrt{\frac{11}{4}} \\
\mid x - \frac{3}{2} \mid &= \frac{\sqrt{11}}{2} \\
x - \frac{3}{2} &= ± \frac{\sqrt{11}}{2} \\
x &= \frac{3}{2} ± \frac{\sqrt{11}}{2} \\
x &= \frac{3 ± \sqrt{11}}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Solving a Quadratic Equation With a Negative Leading Coefficient

**Example:** Write the equation $-2x^2+2x+1 = 0$ in the form $2(x+p)^2=q$, and hence solve the equation.

**Explanation**

First, we multiply the equation by $-1$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(-1) \cdot (-2x^{2} + 2x + 1) &= (-1) \cdot 0 \\
2x^{2} - 2x - 1 &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we move the constant term to the right-hand side, factor the coefficient of the quadratic term, and complete the square on the left-hand side.

$$
\begin{aligned}
2x^{2} - 2x - 1 &= 0 \\
2x^{2} - 2x &= 1 \\
2(x^{2} - x) &= 1 \\
2[x^{2} - 2 \cdot \frac{1}{2}x + (\frac{1}{2})^{2} - (\frac{1}{2})^{2}] &= 1 \\
2[(x - \frac{1}{2})^{2} - \frac{1}{4}] &= 1
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we expand the brackets, and write the equation in the required form:

$$
\begin{aligned}
2[(x - \frac{1}{2})^{2} - \frac{1}{4}] &= 1 \\
2(x - \frac{1}{2})^{2} - \frac{1}{2} &= 1 \\
2(x - \frac{1}{2})^{2} &= \frac{3}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
p=-\dfrac12
$$

and

$$
q=\dfrac32
$$

We now solve the equation using the square root method:

$$
\begin{aligned}
2(x - \frac{1}{2})^{2} &= \frac{3}{2} \\
(x - \frac{1}{2})^{2} &= \frac{3}{4} \\
\sqrt{(x - \frac{1}{2})^{2}} &= \sqrt{\frac{3}{4}} \\
\mid x - \frac{1}{2} \mid &= \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \\
x - \frac{1}{2} &= ± \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \\
x &= \frac{1}{2} ± \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Deriving the Quadratic Formula

Consider the quadratic equation

$$
ax^2+bx+c = 0
$$

where

$$
a\neq 0
$$

We can derive a general solution to this equation by completing the square.

First, let's subtract $c$ from both sides to isolate the variable terms:

$$
ax^2+bx = -c
$$

Since

$$
a\neq 0
$$

dividing by $a$ gives

$$
x^2 + \dfrac{b}{a}x = -\dfrac{c}{a}
$$

Next, we complete the square on the left-hand side and move the constant terms to the right-hand side:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^{2} + 2 \cdot \frac{b}{2a}x =-\frac{c}{a} \\
(x + \frac{b}{2a})^{2} - (\frac{b}{2a})^{2} &= -\frac{c}{a} \\
(x + \frac{b}{2a})^{2} - \frac{b^{2}}{4a^{2}} &= -\frac{c}{a} \\
(x + \frac{b}{2a})^{2} &= \frac{b^{2}}{4a^{2}} - \frac{c}{a}
\end{aligned}
$$

Putting the terms on the right-hand side over a common factor of $4a^2$, we get

$$
\left(x + \dfrac{b}{2a}\right)^2 = \dfrac{b^2 - 4ac}{4a^2}
$$

Taking the square root of both sides yields

$$
x + \dfrac{b}{2a} = \pm \sqrt{\dfrac{b^2 - 4ac}{4a^2}}
$$

which can be simplified as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x + \frac{b}{2a} &= ± \frac{\sqrt{b^{2} - 4ac}}{2a}
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we isolate $x{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
x &= -\frac{b}{2a} ± \frac{\sqrt{b^{2} - 4ac}}{2a}
\end{aligned}
$$

which simplifies as

$$
x = \dfrac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}
$$

This formula is called the **quadratic formula**. You'll learn more about how to apply the quadratic formula in future lessons.
