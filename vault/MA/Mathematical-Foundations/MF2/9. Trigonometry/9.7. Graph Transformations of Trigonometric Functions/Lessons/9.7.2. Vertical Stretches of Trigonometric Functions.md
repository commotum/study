## Introduction

Let's recall the graph of the function $y=\sin{x}$, shown below.

![](<../Source/9.7.2. Vertical Stretches of Trigonometric Functions/Images/6eef6544708e5ea153567a7223ef7ed6.png>)

We can stretch the graph of the sine function in the $y$-direction by multiplying the right-hand-side of the function by a number. This type of transformation is a **vertical stretch**.

For instance, the graph

$$
y= {\color{blue}2}\sin x
$$

stretches the sine curve by a stretch factor of ${\color{blue}2}$:

![](<../Source/9.7.2. Vertical Stretches of Trigonometric Functions/Images/96ccae9500fecac7a4f1c0ab3715f8e3.png>)

Notice that the minimum and maximum values have changed to

$$
y=-{\color{blue}2}
$$

and

$$
y={\color{blue}2}
$$

respectively.

Alternatively, multiplying by a positive number less than $1$ has the effect of shrinking the graph.
For instance, the graph

$$
y= {\color{red}\dfrac{1}{2}}\sin x
$$

shrinks the original sine curve by a stretch factor of

$$
{\color{red}\dfrac{1}{2}}
$$

![](<../Source/9.7.2. Vertical Stretches of Trigonometric Functions/Images/347b694dd4c0a7d2db9ad670fb3a67c5.png>)

Again, the minimum and maximum values have changed to

$$
y=-{\color{red}\dfrac{1}{2}}
$$

and

$$
y={\color{red}\dfrac{1}{2}}
$$

respectively.

<a id="determining-the-maximum-and-minimum-values-of-a-vertically-stretched-sine-or-cosine-function"></a>
## Determining the Maximum and Minimum Values of a Vertically Stretched Sine or Cosine Function

**Example:** What is the minimum value of $y=2\sin x$?

**Explanation**

**Method 1**

To plot the given curve, we follow these steps:

- Start with the graph of $y=\sin x$.
- Then, stretch it parallel to the $y$-axis by a stretch factor of $2$.

The resulting graph is shown below.

![](<../Source/9.7.2. Vertical Stretches of Trigonometric Functions/Images/376ef1e1c1b18ebc1970e8019692e0f8.png>)

From the graph, we see that the minimum value is $y=-2$

**Method 2**

We know that that $y = \sin{x}$ has a minimum value of $-1$.

$$
\sin{x} \geq -1
$$

Multiplying both sides of the above inequality by $2$ gives:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}2 \cdot \sin x & \ge 2 \cdot (-1) \\ 2\sin x & \ge - 2\end{bmatrix}
$$

Therefore, the minimum value of $2\sin{x}$ is $-2$.

<a id="the-amplitude-of-trigonometric-functions"></a>
## The Amplitude of Trigonometric Functions

The **amplitude** of a sine function or cosine function represents half the distance between the maximum and minimum values of the function.

![](<../Source/9.7.2. Vertical Stretches of Trigonometric Functions/Images/4d787c7d56cfa4b65d4038768fee1aa4.png>)

We can calculate the amplitude using the minimum and maximum values $y_\min$ and $y_\max$:

$$
\textrm{Amplitude} =\dfrac{y_\max - y_\min}2
$$

For example, the amplitude of the function $y=\sin x$ shown above, where $y_\min =-1$ and $y_\max =1$, is

$$
\begin{aligned}
Amplitude &= (1 - (-1))/(2) \\
&= \frac{1 + 1}{2} \\
&= \frac{2}{2} \\
&= 1
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="determining-the-amplitude-given-the-equation-of-a-vertically-stretched-sine-or-cosine-function"></a>
## Determining the Amplitude Given the Equation of a Vertically Stretched Sine or Cosine Function

**Example:** What is the amplitude of $f(x)=2\cos{x}$?

**Explanation**

To plot the given curve, we follow these steps:

- Start with the graph of $y=\cos x$.
- Then, stretch it parallel to the $y$-axis by a stretch factor of $2$.

The resulting graph is shown below.

![](<../Source/9.7.2. Vertical Stretches of Trigonometric Functions/Images/7e4eba4e3e4fb7907c44977b4a96d51f.png>)

From the graph, we see that the maximum value is $y_\max=2$ and the minimum value is $y_\min=-2$. Therefore, the amplitude is

$$
\begin{aligned}
Amplitude &= \frac{y_{max} - y_{min}}{2} \\
&= (2 - (-2))/(2) \\
&= \frac{2 + 2}{2} \\
&= \frac{4}{2} \\
&= 2
\end{aligned}
$$

## Vertical Stretches of the Tangent Function

Now, let's recall the graph of $y=\tan x$, shown below.

![](<../Source/9.7.2. Vertical Stretches of Trigonometric Functions/Images/e950075e7d707fefceea65a4c89d6935.png>)

We can also apply vertical stretches to the graph of $y=\tan{x}$ in the same way. The function

$$
y={\color{blue}2}\tan x
$$

is the result of stretching the tangent by a stretch factor of ${\color{blue}2}$:

![](<../Source/9.7.2. Vertical Stretches of Trigonometric Functions/Images/f3ce855ab348a2e0bfd3b583fba5f380.png>)

Likewise, the function

$$
y={\color{red}\dfrac{1}{4}}\tan x
$$

is the result of stretching the tangent by a stretch factor of

$$
{\color{red}\dfrac{1}{4}}
$$

![](<../Source/9.7.2. Vertical Stretches of Trigonometric Functions/Images/e7533f6d9a96bc6b94dd50ef20f3ebc7.png>)

Note that we can't define an amplitude for the tangent function, as it has no minimum or maximum value.
