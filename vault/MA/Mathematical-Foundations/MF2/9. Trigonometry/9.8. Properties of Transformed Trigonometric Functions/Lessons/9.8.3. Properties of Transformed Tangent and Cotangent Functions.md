## Introduction

Given the equation of a general transformed tangent function

$$
y={\color{red}A}\tan\left({\color{blue}B} x + C\right) +{\color{green}D}
$$

some properties of the graph of the function are as follows:

- The period is $\dfrac{\pi}{\color{blue}B}$.
- The vertical shift is ${\color{green}D}$.

We obtain the horizontal shift by factoring the argument of the function, as follows:

$$
y={\color{red}A}\tan\left({\color{blue}B}\left[ x + \dfrac{C}{{\color{blue}B}} \right]\right) +{\color{green}D}
$$

From here, we can see that the phase (horizontal shift) is

$$
-\dfrac{C}{{\color{blue}B}}
$$

We can apply the same ideas to work out the properties of a transformed cotangent function of the form

$$
y={\color{red}A}\cot\left({\color{blue}B} x + C\right) +{\color{green}D}
$$

## Determining the Period of a Transformed Tangent or Cotangent Function

**Example:** Calculate the period of the function $y = \cot\left(2x-\dfrac\pi 6\right)$.

**Explanation**

Given a function of the form

$$
y = A\cot(Bx +C) + D
$$

the period is

$$
\dfrac{\pi}{B}
$$

In our case, we have that $B=2$. Therefore, the period is

$$
\dfrac{\pi}{2}
$$

## Finding a General Expression for the Zeros of a Transformed Tangent or Cotangent Function

**Example:** Which expression gives all of the zeros of the function $f(x) = \cot \left(2x + \dfrac \pi 6\right)$?

**Explanation**

The zeros of $y=\cot x$ are given by

$$
x = \dfrac{\pi}{2} + n\pi
$$

where $n$ is an integer.

To find the zeros of

$$
f(x) = \cot \left(2x + \dfrac \pi 6\right)
$$

we replace the $x$ on the left-hand side of the above with

$$
\left(2x + \dfrac \pi 6\right)
$$

This gives

$$
2x + \dfrac \pi 6 = \dfrac{\pi}{2} + n\pi
$$

Solving for $x$, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
2x + \frac{π}{6} &= \frac{π}{2} + nπ \\
2x &= \frac{π}{2} + nπ - \frac{π}{6} \\
2x &= \frac{π}{3} + nπ \\
x &= \frac{π}{6} + \frac{nπ}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, we conclude that the zeros are

$$
x = \dfrac{\pi}{6} + \dfrac{n\pi}{2}
$$

where $n$ is an integer.

## Finding a General Expression for the Asymptotes of a Transformed Tangent or Cotangent Function

**Example:** What expression gives all of the vertical asymptotes of the function $f(x)=\tan\left(2x\right)$?

**Explanation**

The asymptotes of $y=\tan x$ are given by the expression

$$
x = \dfrac{\pi}{2} + n \pi
$$

where $n$ is an integer.

To find the asymptotes of

$$
\tan\left(2x\right)
$$

we replace the $x$ on the left-hand side of the above with $2x$. This gives

$$
2x = \dfrac{\pi}{2} + n\pi
$$

Solving for $x$, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
2x &= \frac{π}{2} + nπ \\
x &= \frac{π}{4} + \frac{nπ}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, we conclude that the expression giving all the vertical asymptotes is

$$
x= \dfrac{\pi}{4} + \dfrac{n\pi}{2}
$$

where $n$ is an integer.

## Finding a General Expression for the Domain of a Transformed Tangent or Cotangent Function

**Example:** What is the domain of the function $y=\tan\left(\dfrac x 2\right)$?

**Explanation**

The domain of $y=\tan x$ is given by the expression

$$
x \neq \dfrac\pi 2 + n\pi
$$

where $n$ is an integer.

To find the domain of

$$
\tan\left(\dfrac x 2\right)
$$

we replace the $x$ on the left-hand side of the above with

$$
\left(\dfrac x 2\right)
$$

This gives

$$
\dfrac x 2 \neq \dfrac\pi 2 + n\pi
$$

Solving for $x$, we get

$$
\begin{bmatrix}\frac{x}{2} & \ne \frac{π}{2} + nπ \\ x & \ne π + 2nπ\end{bmatrix}
$$

Therefore, the domain of the function is

$$
x \neq \pi + 2n\pi
$$

where $n$ is an integer.
