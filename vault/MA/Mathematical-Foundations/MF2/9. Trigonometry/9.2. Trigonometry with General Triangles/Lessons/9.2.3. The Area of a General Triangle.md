## Introduction

The formula for the area of a general triangle is

$$
\mathcal{A}=\frac{1}{2}{\color{red}{a}}{\color{blue}{b}}\sin{C}
$$

where $\color{red}a$ and $\color{blue}b$ are two sides of the triangle and $C$ is the measure of the angle between those sides (called the **included angle**).

We'll prove this result at the end of the lesson.

To demonstrate how this works, suppose that we want to calculate the area of the non-right-angled triangle below:

![](<../Source/9.2.3. The Area of a General Triangle/Images/4c41cbb9d027469659dd8ba71061c10e.png>)

Let's call the lengths of the sides

$$
BC = \color{red}{a}
$$

and

$$
AC ={\color{blue}{b}}
$$

For the triangle above we have

$$
{\color{red}a=10}
$$

$$
{\color{blue}b=12}
$$

and

$$
m\angle C=45^{\circ}
$$

Therefore, the area of our triangle is

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= \frac{1}{2} \cdot 10 \cdot 12 \cdot \sin 45^{∘} \\
&= 60 \cdot \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \\
&= 30\sqrt{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Calculating the Area of a Triangle

**Example:** Find the area of $\triangle{ABC}$ below. Round your answer to two decimal places.

![](<../Source/9.2.3. The Area of a General Triangle/Images/f15aebd48fba864b6b804273ece662d0.png>)

**Explanation**

The formula for the area of the triangle is

$$
\mathcal{A}=\dfrac{1}{2}ab\sin{C}
$$

where $\angle{C}$ is between the sides of lengths $a$ and $b$.

Here, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
a &= CB = 58 \\
b &= CA = 40 \\
m∠C &= 31^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

Since

$$
\sin{31^{\circ}} = 0.515\,038
$$

rounded to six decimal places, we calculate the area as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= \frac{1}{2} \cdot 58 \cdot 40\sin 31^{∘} \\
&= 1160 \cdot (0.515038) \\
&\approx 597.44
\end{aligned}
$$

rounded to two decimal places.

<a id="calculating-an-angle-or-side-of-a-triangle-given-the-area-of-a-triangle"></a>
## Calculating an Angle or Side of a Triangle Given the Area of a Triangle

**Example:** The area of ​​the triangle $\triangle ABC$ is $24\sqrt{2} \text{cm}^2$. Find $AB$.

![](<../Source/9.2.3. The Area of a General Triangle/Images/baf9217d37cc64a0caa8560240c0a729.png>)

**Explanation**

The formula for the area of the triangle is

$$
\mathcal{A}=\dfrac{1}{2}bc\sin{A}
$$

where $\angle{A}$ is between the sides of lengths $b$ and $c$.

Here

$$
\begin{aligned}
c &= AB, \\
b &= AC = 12cm, \\
m∠A &= 45^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

Since

$$
\sin{45^{\circ}} = \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}
$$

we can calculate $c=AB$, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= \frac{1}{2} \cdot b \cdot c \cdot \sin A \\
24\sqrt{2} &= \frac{1}{2} \cdot 12 \cdot c \cdot \sin 45^{∘} \\
24\sqrt{2} &= \frac{1}{2} \cdot 12 \cdot c \cdot (\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}) \\
24\sqrt{2} &= 3\sqrt{2}c \\
24 &= 3c \\
c &= 8cm
\end{aligned}
$$

We conclude that

$$
AB = 8\,\textrm{cm}
$$

## Calculating the Area of a Quadrilateral

**Example:** Rounded to the nearest integer, what is the area of the quadrilateral $ABCD$?

![](<../Source/9.2.3. The Area of a General Triangle/Images/df17bd0e04a4bf54f78403799e52df63.png>)

**Explanation**

The area of the quadrilateral $ABCD$ is the sum of the areas of the triangles $\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle ADC$ (see the illustration below).

![](<../Source/9.2.3. The Area of a General Triangle/Images/43f655045f30483b6371dd630b70d121.png>)

First, we find the area for $\triangle ABC$, and get

$$
\begin{aligned}
A_{ABC} &= \frac{1}{2}(42)(35)\sin 36^{∘} \\
&= 735\sin 36^{∘} \\
&\approx 432.022
\end{aligned}
$$

rounded to three decimal places.

Now we find the area for $\triangle ADC$, and get

$$
\begin{aligned}
A_{ADC} &= \frac{1}{2}(20)(18)\sin 80^{∘} \\
&= 180\sin 80^{∘} \\
&\approx 177.265
\end{aligned}
$$

rounded to three decimal places.

Finally, we add together the areas of our two triangles to determine the area of $ABCD$. We get

$$
\begin{aligned}
A_{ABCD} &= A_{ABC} + A_{ADC} \\
&= 432.022 + 177.265 \\
&\approx 609
\end{aligned}
$$

Consequently, to the nearest integer, the area of the quadrilateral $ABCD$ is $609$.

<a id="finding-the-area-of-a-triangle-given-the-area-of-a-quadrilateral"></a>
## Finding the Area of a Triangle Given the Area of a Quadrilateral

**Example:** The area of $ABCD$ is $300\, \text{cm}^2$. What is the area of $\triangle ABC$ rounded to the nearest integer?

![](<../Source/9.2.3. The Area of a General Triangle/Images/082daac33f9eacc4721718b0450a9b3d.png>)

**Explanation**

Let's start by finding the area of $\triangle ACD$. We get

$$
\begin{aligned}
A_{ACD} &= \frac{1}{2} \cdot DA \cdot DC \cdot \sin D \\
&= \frac{1}{2}(25)(32)\sin 70^{∘} \\
&= 400\sin 70^{∘} \\
&= 375.877... \\
&\approx 376cm^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

rounded to the nearest integer.

The difference of the areas of the quadrilateral $ABCD$ and $\triangle ACD$ gives the area of $\triangle ABC$. Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
A_{ABC} &= A_{ACD} - A_{ABCD} \\
&= 376 - 300 \\
&= 76cm^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="deriving-the-formula-for-the-area-of-a-general-triangle"></a>
## Deriving the Formula for the Area of a General Triangle

Consider $\triangle ABC$ with sides of lengths $a,b$, and $c$, as shown below.

![](<../Source/9.2.3. The Area of a General Triangle/Images/85cb06d9cd067b372393114bddfa976d.png>)

Let's now add the height of length $h$ perpendicular to $\overline{BC}$, as shown.

![](<../Source/9.2.3. The Area of a General Triangle/Images/5b6c83c07b7266920d24646cfb6f22af.png>)

Using the usual formula for the area $\mathcal A$ of a triangle in terms of its base and height, we have

$$
\mathcal A = \dfrac12 ah
$$

Using elementary trigonometry, we have that

$$
\dfrac{h}{b} = \sin C
$$

which means that

$$
h = b\sin C
$$

Therefore, by substituting this into our expression for $\mathcal A$, we have

$$
\mathcal A = \dfrac12ab\sin C
$$

as required.

We can use a similar approach to prove the following formulas:

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= \frac{1}{2}bc\sin A \\
A &= \frac{1}{2}ac\sin B
\end{aligned}
$$
