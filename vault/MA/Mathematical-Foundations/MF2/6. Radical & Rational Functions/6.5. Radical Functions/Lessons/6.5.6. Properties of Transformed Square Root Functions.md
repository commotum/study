## Introduction

The domain of a radical function is limited to the set of $x$-values that make the expression under the square root positive or zero.

For example, the domain of $\sqrt{x}$ is

$$
x \geq 0
$$

or $[MATH: x \in [0,\infty)]$ in interval notation.

On the other hand, the domain of $\sqrt{-x}$ is given by

$$
-x \geq 0
$$

which simplifies to

$$
x \leq 0
$$

Thus, the domain of $\sqrt{-x}$ is $[MATH: x \in (-\infty, 0].]$

As another example, the domain of $\sqrt{x-1}$ is given by

$$
x -1 \geq 0
$$

which simplifies to

$$
x \geq 1
$$

or $[MATH: x \in [1, \infty)]$ in interval notation.

We can also see that this is true from the graph of the function. The graph of

$$
y=\sqrt{x-1}
$$

is shown below.

![](<../Source/6.5.6. Properties of Transformed Square Root Functions/Images/063bdfb30ca255be6fafa8867c5b0412.png>)

In general, to find the domain of a radical function of the form

$$
y = \sqrt{g(x)}
$$

we need to solve the inequality

$$
g(x) \geq 0
$$

<a id="finding-the-domain-of-a-transformed-square-root-function"></a>
## Finding the Domain of a Transformed Square Root Function

**Example:** What is the domain of $f(x) = \sqrt{3x+5}-2$?

**Explanation**

The domain of

$$
y = \sqrt{x}
$$

is

$$
x \ge 0
$$

To find the domain of

$$
g(x) = \sqrt{3x+5} -2
$$

we replace $x$ with $3x+5$ in the above inequality. This gives

$$
{3x+5} \ge 0
$$

Solving this inequality, we get

$$
\begin{bmatrix}3x + 5 & \ge 0 \\ 3x & \ge - 5 \\ x & \ge - \frac{5}{3}\end{bmatrix}
$$

Therefore, the domain of $f(x)$ is $[MATH: x\in \left[-\dfrac{5}{3}, \infty\right).]$

We can also see that this is true from the graph of the function. The graph of $y=f(x)$ is shown below.

![](<../Source/6.5.6. Properties of Transformed Square Root Functions/Images/008f1ee87ab90aefe6b453e268dd745e.png>)

<a id="the-range-of-a-transformed-square-root-function"></a>
## The Range of a Transformed Square Root Function

Now, let's examine the range of a transformed radical function. For example, what is the range of

$$
f(x) = -3\sqrt{5x-2}+7
$$

The range of $\sqrt x$ is

$$
\sqrt x \ge 0
$$

Horizontal shifts and stretches do not affect the range. So, we have

$$
\sqrt{5x-2} \ge 0
$$

Multiplying the above inequality by $-3$ and then adding $7$, we get

$$
\begin{bmatrix}\sqrt{5x - 2} & \ge 0 \\ -3\sqrt{5x - 2} & \le 0 \\ -3\sqrt{5x - 2} + 7 & \le 7\end{bmatrix}
$$

Therefore, the range of the given function is $[MATH: f(x) \in (-\infty,7].]$

We can also see that this is true from the graph of the function. The graph of $y=f(x)$ is shown below.

![](<../Source/6.5.6. Properties of Transformed Square Root Functions/Images/c640b041d12f59ff87a484d7ce18c80d.png>)

<a id="finding-the-range-of-a-transformed-square-root-function"></a>
## Finding the Range of a Transformed Square Root Function

**Example:** Which of the following values are possible outputs of $f(x) = 2 \sqrt{x- 2} + 2$?

1. $1$
2. $2$
3. $6$

**Explanation**

The range of $\sqrt x$ is

$$
\sqrt x \ge 0
$$

Horizontal shifts do not affect the range. So, we have

$$
\sqrt{x-2} \ge 0
$$

Multiplying the above inequality by $2$ and then adding $2$, we get

$$
\begin{bmatrix}\sqrt{x - 2} & \ge 0 \\ 2\sqrt{x - 2} & \ge 0 \\ 2\sqrt{x - 2} + 2 & \ge 2\end{bmatrix}
$$

Therefore, the range of the given function is $[MATH: f(x) \in [2,\infty).]$

Among the given values, only

$$
f(x)=2
$$

and

$$
f(x)=6
$$

lie in the range. So, the correct answer is "II and III only."

We can also see that this is true from the graph of the function. The graph of $y=f(x)$ is shown below.

![](<../Source/6.5.6. Properties of Transformed Square Root Functions/Images/839d75b8fe1695115836a2d2d7d3f2d6.png>)

<a id="the-roots-of-a-transformed-square-root-function"></a>
## The Roots of a Transformed Square Root Function

To find the roots of a transformed radical function, we must set the function expression equal to zero and solve for $x$.

For instance, what are the roots of

$$
f(x) = \sqrt{2x+1} + 5
$$

In this case, we set

$$
\sqrt{2x+1} + 5 = 0
$$

We solve this equation by making $x$ the subject, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{2x + 1} + 5 &= 0 \\
\sqrt{2x + 1} &= -5
\end{aligned}
$$

This equation does not have any solutions since its left-hand side is always nonnegative, while the right-hand side is negative.

Therefore, $f(x)$ has no roots.

---

<a id="finding-the-roots-of-a-transformed-square-root-function"></a>
## Finding the Roots of a Transformed Square Root Function

**Example:** Find the root of $f(x) = \sqrt{6x+7} - 5$.

**Explanation**

To find the root of $f(x)$, we must solve

$$
f(x)=0
$$

$$
\sqrt{6x+7} - 5 = 0
$$

We solve this equation by making $x$ the subject, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{6x + 7} - 5 &= 0 \\
\sqrt{6x + 7} &= 5 \\
(\sqrt{6x + 7})^{2} &= 5^{2} \\
6x + 7 &= 25 \\
6x &= 18 \\
x &= 3
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we must check the answer by substituting it back into the initial equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{6 \cdot 3 + 7} - 5 &= 0 \\
\sqrt{25} &= 5 \\
5 &= 5✓
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the root of $f(x)$ is $x=3$.

<a id="finding-a-transformed-square-root-function-given-some-properties"></a>
## Finding a Transformed Square Root Function Given Some Properties

**Example:** Let $f(x)=a\sqrt{b-x}+c$. Now consider the following properties of $f(x)$:

- The domain of $f(x)$ is $[MATH: x \in (-\infty, 2].]$
- The range is $[MATH: f(x) \in [2, \infty).]$
- The point $(1,4)$ lies on the graph of $y=f(x)$.

Find the value of $a+b+c$.

**Explanation**

Let's consider each property in turn.

- The domain of $f(x)=a\sqrt{b-x}+c\,$ is determined by the following inequality:
$\begin{bmatrix}b - x & \ge 0 \\ x & \le b\end{bmatrix}$
We are given that the domain is $[MATH: x\in \left(-\infty, 2\right],]$ which is equivalent to $x\le 2$. Hence, we obtain
$b=2$.
- Since the range is unbounded on the right-hand side, we know that $a>0$. Furthermore, since we are given that the lowest point in the range is $2$, we must have
$a\sqrt{2 - x}_(⏟)_(\ge 0) + c \ge 2$.
This means that $c=2$.
- Finally, since the point $(1,4)$ lies on the graph of $y=f(x)$, we have
$f(1)|= a\sqrt{2 - 1} + 2; 4|= a + 2; a|= 2$.

Therefore, our function is

$$
f(x)=2\sqrt{2-x}+2
$$

and

$$
a+b+c = 2 + 2 + 2 = 6
$$
