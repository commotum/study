## Introduction

Suppose we wish to plot the graph of

$$
f(x)=\sqrt{x}
$$

First, we create a table of values.

| **$x$** | $0$ | $1$ | $4$ | $9$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $f(x)$ | $0$ | $1$ | $2$ | $3$ |

We do not consider negative values for the input because the square root of a negative number is not a real number.

Now, we can plot the graph:

![](<../Source/6.5.3. Graphing the Square Root Function/Images/5ed56e18425f052ae47bc211213e9e16.png>)

Some key points to note:

- The graph passes through the points $(1,1)$ and $(0,0)$.
- The domain is $[MATH: x\in [0,\infty).]$
- The range is $[MATH: y\in [0,\infty).]$
- The function is increasing over its entire domain.
- As $x \rightarrow \infty$, we have $y\rightarrow \infty$, though the function increases more slowly as $x$ gets large.

## Identifying the Graph of the Square Root Function

**Example:** Which of the following could be the graph of $y = \sqrt{x}$?

![](<../Source/6.5.3. Graphing the Square Root Function/Images/ffaf9b03943c62a4dc06e9425dc434c0.png>)

**Explanation**

Let's recall some of the basic properties of

$$
y=\sqrt x{:}
$$

- The point $(0,0)$ lies on the graph.
- Its domain is $[MATH: x\in [0,\infty).]$
- Its range is $[MATH: y\in [0,\infty).]$
- The function is increasing over its entire domain.
- As $x \rightarrow \infty$, we have $y\rightarrow \infty$, though the function increases more slowly as $x$ gets large.

Of the given options, only graph II has all of the above properties.

Therefore, the correct answer is only II.

## Identifying Properties of the Square Root Function

**Example:** Which of the following statements are true regarding the function $f(x) =\sqrt{x}$?

1. $f(x)$ is always positive.
2. The range of $f(x)$ is $(-\infty,0)\cup (0,\infty)$.
3. $f(x) \rightarrow \infty$ as $x \rightarrow \infty$

**Explanation**

Let's start by recalling the graph of

$$
y=\sqrt x
$$

![](<../Source/6.5.3. Graphing the Square Root Function/Images/8971d09c88e8f2ae46a2c894741a27f2.png>)

Now, let's examine each statement in turn.

- Statement I is false since $f(0)=0 \not\gt 0$.
- Statement II is false since the range of $f(x)$ is $[MATH: [0,\infty).]$
- Statement III is true. Indeed, $f(x) \rightarrow \infty$ as $x \rightarrow \infty$.
