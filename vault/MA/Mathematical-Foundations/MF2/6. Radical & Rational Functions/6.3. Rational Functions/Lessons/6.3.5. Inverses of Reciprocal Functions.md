## Introduction

In this lesson, we'll learn how to compute inverses of reciprocal functions.

As an example, let's consider the function $f(x)$, given by

$$
f(x) = \dfrac{7}{2x+4}
$$

To find the inverse $f^{-1}(x)$, we start by replacing $f(x)$ with $y{:}$

$$
y = \dfrac{7}{2x+4}
$$

Now, interchanging $x$ and $y$ gives

$$
x = \dfrac{7}{2y+4}
$$

Then, we make $y$ the subject of the equation as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x &= \frac{7}{2y + 4} \\
x(2y + 4) &= 7 \\
2xy + 4x &= 7 \\
2xy &= 7 - 4x \\
y &= \frac{7 - 4x}{2x}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the inverse function is

$$
f^{-1}(x) = \dfrac{7 - 4x}{2x}
$$

## Finding the Inverse of a Reciprocal Function

**Example:** The function $f(x) = \dfrac{4}{(x-1)^2}$ is defined for $x >1$. Find $f^{-1}(x)$.

**Explanation**

To find $f^{-1}(x)$, we start by replacing $f(x)$ with $y{:}$

$$
y = \dfrac{4}{(x-1)^2}
$$

Now, interchanging $x$ and $y$ gives

$$
x = \dfrac{4}{(y-1)^2}
$$

Then, we make $y$ the subject of the equation, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x &= (4)/((y - 1)^{2}) \\
x \cdot (y - 1)^{2} &= 4 \\
(y - 1)^{2} &= \frac{4}{x} \\
\sqrt{(y - 1)^{2}} &= \sqrt{\frac{4}{x}} \\
\mid y - 1 \mid &= \frac{2}{\sqrt{x}} \\
y - 1 &= \frac{2}{\sqrt{x}} \\
y &= \frac{2}{\sqrt{x}} + 1
\end{aligned}
$$

Note that we used the fact that for $y > 1$, we have

$$
\mid y-1 \mid = y-1
$$

Therefore, the inverse function is

$$
f^{-1}(x) = \dfrac{2}{\sqrt{x}} + 1
$$

## Finding the Inverse of a Simple Rational Function

**Example:** Find the inverse of the function $f(x) = \dfrac{2-x}{x+3}$.

**Explanation**

To find $f^{-1}(x)$, we start by replacing $f(x)$ with $y{:}$

$$
y = \dfrac{2-x}{x+3}
$$

Now, interchanging $x$ and $y$ gives

$$
x = \dfrac{2-y}{y+3}
$$

Then, we make $y$ the subject of the equation, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x &= \frac{2 - y}{y + 3} \\
x(y + 3) &= 2 - y \\
xy + 3x &= 2 - y \\
xy + y &= 2 - 3x \\
y(x + 1) &= 2 - 3x \\
y &= \frac{2 - 3x}{x + 1}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the inverse function is

$$
f^{-1}(x) = \dfrac{2-3x}{x+1}
$$

## Determining an Unknown Value Given the Inverse of a Rational Function

**Example:** The function $f(x)$, defined as
$f(x) = \dfrac{kx + 6}{x-4}, \qquad x\neq 4$
has an inverse function given by
$f^{-1}(x) = \dfrac{4x+6}{x-2}, \qquad x\neq 2$.
What is the value of $k$?

**Explanation**

To find $f^{-1}(x)$, we start by replacing $f(x)$ with $y{:}$

$$
y = \dfrac{kx + 6}{x-4}
$$

Now, interchanging $x$ and $y$ gives

$$
x = \dfrac{ky+6}{y-4}
$$

Then, we make $y$ the subject of the equation, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x &= \frac{ky + 6}{y - 4} \\
x(y - 4) &= ky + 6 \\
xy - 4x &= ky + 6 \\
xy - ky &= 4x + 6 \\
y(x - k) &= 4x + 6 \\
y &= \frac{4x + 6}{x - k}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the inverse function is

$$
f^{-1}(x) = \dfrac{4x+6}{x-k}
$$

Comparing our answer with the given expression, we conclude that $k=2$.
