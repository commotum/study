## Introduction

To find the zeros of a rational function, we first need to factor the numerator and denominator and cancel any common factors. Then, we set the numerator equal to zero and solve.

For example, consider the function

$$
f(x) = \dfrac{x-3}{x^2-25}
$$

Factoring the numerator and denominator, we get

$$
f(x) = \dfrac{x-3}{(x+5)(x-5)}
$$

There are no common factors to cancel, so we set the numerator equal to zero and solve:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x - 3 &= 0 \\
x &= 3
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the solution to

$$
f(x)=0
$$

is $x=3$.

## Finding the Roots of a Rational Function With a Linear Numerator and No Common Factors

**Example:** Solve $f(x)=0$, where $f(x) = \dfrac{2x + 1}{x^2 + x - 6}$.

**Explanation**

First, we factor $f(x)$, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
f(x) &= \frac{2x + 1}{x^{2} + x - 6} \\
&= (2x + 1)/((x + 3)(x - 2))
\end{aligned}
$$

There are no common factors in the numerator and denominator, so we set the numerator equal to zero and solve for $x$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
2x + 1 &= 0 \\
2x &= -1 \\
x &= -\frac{1}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the solution to

$$
f(x)=0
$$

is

$$
x=-\dfrac{1}{2}
$$

## Finding the Roots of a Rational Function With a Quadratic Numerator and No Common Factors

**Example:** Solve $f(x)=0$, where $f(x) = \dfrac{x^2-4x}{x^2+5x+6}$.

**Explanation**

First, we factor $f(x)$, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
f(x) &= \frac{x^{2} - 4x}{x^{2} + 5x + 6} \\
&= (x(x - 4))/((x + 2)(x + 3))
\end{aligned}
$$

There are no common factors in the numerator and denominator, so we set the numerator equal to zero and solve for $x$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x(x - 4) &= 0 \\
x &= 0, 4
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the solutions to

$$
f(x)=0
$$

are $x=0$ and $x=4$.

## Finding the Roots of a Rational Function With Common Factors

**Example:** Solve $f(x)=0$, where $f(x) = \dfrac{x+1}{x^2-x-2}$.

**Explanation**

First, we factor the numerator and denominator and cancel any common factors, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
f(x) &= \frac{x + 1}{x^{2} - x - 2} \\
&= (x + 1)/((x + 1)(x - 2)) \\
&= ((x + 1))/((x + 1)(x - 2)) \\
&= \frac{1}{x - 2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Setting the numerator equal to zero, we get $1=0$, which is a false statement. Therefore,

$$
f(x)=0
$$

has no solution.

## Finding the Roots of a Rational Function Containing a Cubic Expression

**Example:** Solve $f(x)=0$, where $f(x) = \dfrac{x^3-2x^2-9x+18}{x^2-x-2}$.

**Explanation**

First, we factor the numerator and denominator and cancel any common factors, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
f(x) &= \frac{x^{3} - 2x^{2} - 9x + 18}{x^{2} - x - 2} \\
&= (x^{2}(x - 2) - 9(x - 2))/((x - 2)(x + 1)) \\
&= ((x - 2)(x^{2} - 9))/((x - 2)(x + 1)) \\
&= ((x - 2)(x - 3)(x + 3))/((x - 2)(x + 1)) \\
&= ((x - 2)(x - 3)(x + 3))/((x - 2)(x + 1)) \\
&= ((x - 3)(x + 3))/(x + 1)
\end{aligned}
$$

Setting the numerator equal to zero and solving for $x$ gives

$$
(x-3)(x+3) = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad x = \pm3
$$

Therefore, the solutions to

$$
f(x)=0
$$

are $x=3$ and $x=-3$.
