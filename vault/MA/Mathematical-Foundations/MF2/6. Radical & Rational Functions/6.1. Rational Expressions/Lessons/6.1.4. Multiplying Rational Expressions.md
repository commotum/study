## Introduction

Multiplying rational expressions is just like multiplying fractions. After all, fractions are just rational expressions with constants!

For example, to multiply the rational expressions

$$
\dfrac{3}{x}
$$

and

$$
\dfrac{y}{2}
$$

we multiply the numerators and the denominators as follows:

$$
\dfrac{3}{x} \cdot \dfrac{y}{2} = \dfrac{3 \cdot y}{x \cdot 2} = \dfrac{3y}{2x}
$$

<a id="multiplying-rational-expressions-with-no-common-factor"></a>
## Multiplying Rational Expressions With No Common Factor

**Example:** Simplify $\dfrac 2 x \cdot \dfrac 3 {y^2}$.

**Explanation**

We multiply the numerators and the denominators as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned} \dfrac 2 x \cdot \dfrac 3 {y^2} &=\dfrac {2 \cdot 3} {x \cdot y^2} &=\dfrac {6} {xy^2} \end{aligned}
$$

<a id="multiplying-rational-expressions-and-simplifying-the-result"></a>
## Multiplying Rational Expressions and Simplifying the Result

**Example:** Given that $\dfrac {6a} {b^2} = x$ and $\dfrac {3b} {2a}= y$, what is $x\cdot y$?

**Explanation**

We multiply the numerators and the denominators as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x \cdot y &= \frac{6a}{b^{2}} \cdot \frac{3b}{2a} \\
&= \frac{6a \cdot 3b}{b^{2} \cdot 2a} \\
&= \frac{18ab}{2ab^{2}} \\
&= \frac{9}{b}
\end{aligned}
$$

**Note:** In the above example, we performed some cancellations after multiplying. However, with more complex expressions, it's often easier to cancel *before* multiplying.

<a id="multiplying-rational-expressions-by-canceling-common-terms"></a>
## Multiplying Rational Expressions by Canceling Common Terms

**Example:** Simplify $\dfrac {(n + 1)(n + 3)} {4n^2} \cdot \dfrac {8n}{(n + 3)(n - 2)}$.

**Explanation**

First, we cancel any terms that appear in both a numerator and a denominator. Then, we multiply the numerators and the denominators as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
((n + 1)(n + 3))/(4n^{2}) \cdot (8n)/((n + 3)(n - 2)) &= ((n + 1)(n + 3))/(4n \cdot n) \cdot (2 \cdot 4n)/((n + 3)(n - 2)) \\
&= \frac{n + 1}{n} \cdot \frac{2}{n - 2} \\
&= \frac{2n + 2}{n^{2} - 2n}
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="multiplying-rational-expressions-by-factoring-and-canceling-common-terms"></a>
## Multiplying Rational Expressions by Factoring and Canceling Common Terms

**Example:** Simplify $\dfrac {3p - 12}{p^2 - 9} \cdot \dfrac {p + 3} {p - 4}$.

**Explanation**

First, we factor all of the expressions and cancel any terms that appear in both a numerator and a denominator. Then, we multiply the numerators and the denominators as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned} \dfrac {3p - 12}{p^2 - 9} \cdot \dfrac {p + 3} {p - 4} &=\dfrac {3(p - 4)} {(p + 3)(p - 3)} \cdot \dfrac {p + 3} {p - 4} \\[5pt] &=\dfrac {3\cancel{(p - 4)}} {\cancel{(p + 3)}(p - 3)} \cdot \dfrac {\cancel{p + 3}} {\cancel{p - 4}} \\[5pt] &=\dfrac {3}{p - 3} \end{aligned}
$$
