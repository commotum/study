## Introduction

Remember that in order to add fractions with different denominators, like

$$
\dfrac 1 4 + \dfrac 1 8
$$

we need to put the fractions over a **common denominator**, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{1}{4} + \frac{1}{8} &=  \\
\frac{2}{8} + \frac{1}{8} &=  \\
&= \frac{3}{8}
\end{aligned}
$$

It works the same in algebra! To add two fractions, we need to put each fraction over a common denominator. Then, we add the numerators.

## Adding and Subtracting a Number and a Rational Expression

**Example:** Write $2 + \dfrac{2}{k}$ as a single fraction.

**Explanation**

Using the fact that

$$
2=\dfrac 2 1
$$

we can write the expression as

$$
\dfrac 2 1 + \dfrac{2}{k}
$$

In order to add the two fractions, we need them to have a common denominator. So, we put both fractions over a common denominator of $k$ by multiplying the top and bottom of the first fraction by $k$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{2}{1} + \frac{2}{k} &=  \\
\frac{k}{k} \cdot \frac{2}{1} + \frac{2}{k} &=  \\
&= \frac{2k}{k} + \frac{2}{k}
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we add the fractions and simplify the result:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{2k}{k} + \frac{2}{k} &=  \\
\frac{2k + 2}{k} &=  \\
&= (2(k + 1))/(k)
\end{aligned}
$$

## Adding and Subtracting Rational Expressions With No Common Factors in the Denominators

**Example:** Write $\dfrac{1}{q} + \dfrac{1}{p+1}$ as a single fraction.

**Explanation**

In this case, we need to put the fractions over a common denominator of $(p+1)q$, since there are no common factors in either denominator that we can exploit.

To do this, we multiply the top and bottom of the first fraction by $p+1$, and the top and bottom of the second fraction by $q$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{1}{q} + \frac{1}{p + 1} &=  \\
\frac{p + 1}{p + 1} \cdot \frac{1}{q} + \frac{q}{q} \cdot \frac{1}{p + 1} &=  \\
(p + 1)/((p + 1)q) + (q)/((p + 1)q) &=  \\
&= (p + q + 1)/((p + 1)q)
\end{aligned}
$$
