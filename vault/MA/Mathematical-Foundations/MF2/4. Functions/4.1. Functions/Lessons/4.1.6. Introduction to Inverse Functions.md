## Introduction

Consider the mapping diagram of the function $f(x)$, shown below.

![](<../Source/4.1.6. Introduction to Inverse Functions/Images/b075c5e399f2f0b3a3c7ae417ab52323.png>)

Let's now reverse the arrows, as follows:

![](<../Source/4.1.6. Introduction to Inverse Functions/Images/da0f7c4a868c8dde679865455ec74d77.png>)

Now, we have a mapping diagram that "reverses" the action of the function $f$. The diagram itself represents a new function, which we call the **inverse of the function $f$,** and denote by $f^{-1}$.

We can evaluate the inverse function $f^{-1}$ at any point in the range of $f$. For example, to find the value of $f^{-1}(2)$, we notice that we have an arrow from ${\color{black}2}$ (on the right) to ${\color{black}0}$ (on the left). Therefore,

$$
f^{-1}({\color{black}2}) = {\color{black}0}
$$

In the original function, $f$, that same arrow went from ${\color{black}0}$ (on the left) to ${\color{black}2}$ (on the right). So, we have that

$$
f({\color{black}0})={\color{black}2}
$$

and as a result, we obtain

$$
f^{-1}(f(0)) = 0
$$

So, applying the function $f$ and then "reversing" the action using $f^{-1}$ will get us back to $0$.

In general, this is true for every $x$ in the domain of the function $f$:

$$
f^{-1}(f(x)) = x
$$

<a id="evaluating-an-inverse-function-from-a-mapping-diagram"></a>
## Evaluating an Inverse Function From a Mapping Diagram

**Example:** The function $f(x)$ is represented by the mapping diagram above. What is $f^{-1}(-1)$?

![](<../Source/4.1.6. Introduction to Inverse Functions/Images/70abac9cf00204ab68516f21c84ac94c.png>)

**Explanation**

**Method 1**

The function $f^{-1}(x)$ reverses the action of $f(x)$, as depicted below.

![](<../Source/4.1.6. Introduction to Inverse Functions/Images/1df2bc501dc85c96976727ba0c4e2546.png>)

From the above, we see that

$$
f^{-1}(-1) = 4
$$

**Method 2**

From the given mapping diagram, we have

$$
f(4) = -1
$$

Applying $f^{-1}$ to both sides of the above equation, and using the fact that

$$
f^{-1}\left(f(x)\right) = x
$$

we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
f^{-1}(f(4)) &= f^{-1}(-1) \\
4 &= f^{-1}(-1)
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, we conclude that

$$
f^{-1}(-1) = 4
$$

## Evaluating an Inverse Function Given Function Data

**Example:** Given the function $f(x)$ defined by the table above, what is the value of $f^{-1}(3)$?

| **$x$** | $-8$ | $-4$ | $-1$ | $1$ | $2$ | $3$ | $5$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $f(x)$ | $7$ | $3$ | $0$ | $-2$ | $-3$ | $-4$ | $-6$ |

**Explanation**

From the table, we have

$$
f(-4) = 3
$$

Applying $f^{-1}$ to both sides of the above equation, and using the fact that

$$
f^{-1}\left(f(x)\right) = x
$$

we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
f^{-1}(f(-4)) &= f^{-1}(3) \\
- 4 &= f^{-1}(3)
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, we conclude that

$$
f^{-1}(3) = -4
$$

## Evaluating an Inverse Function Using a Graph

**Example:** The graph of $y = f(x)$ is given above. The points $A,B,C$, and $D$ lie on the graph, as shown. What is the value of $f^{-1}(-3)$?

![](<../Source/4.1.6. Introduction to Inverse Functions/Images/d77575ccdfbefe893ce638352499b534.png>)

**Explanation**

The point $D$ on the graph has coordinates $(2,-3)$. Therefore,

$$
f(2) = -3
$$

Applying $f^{-1}$ to both sides of the above equation, and using the fact that

$$
f^{-1}\left(f(x)\right) = x
$$

we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
f^{-1}(f(2)) &= f^{-1}(-3) \\
2 &= f^{-1}(-3)
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, we conclude that

$$
f^{-1}(-3) = 2
$$

<a id="the-definition-of-the-inverse-function"></a>
## The Definition of the Inverse Function

Recall that the $f^{-1}$ "reverses" the action of $f$ so that

$$
f^{-1}(f(x)) = x
$$

which can be visualized using the mapping diagram, as shown below.

![](<../Source/4.1.6. Introduction to Inverse Functions/Images/fb37131843cf52b9e2738d93f97a5590.png>)

Since $f^{-1}(f(x))$ is just the composition $(f^{-1} \circ f)(x)$, we can now give the algebraic definition of the inverse.

The function $f^{-1}$ is called the **inverse of the function $f$** if

$$
(f^{-1} \circ f)(x) = x
$$

for each $x$ in the domain of $f$.

In other words, to show that two functions $f(x)$ and $g(x)$ are inverses of each other, we need to check the following condition:

$$
(g\circ f)(x) = x
$$

---

<a id="computing-an-inverse-function-using-the-definition"></a>
## Computing an Inverse Function Using the Definition

**Example:** Suppose that $f(x)=\dfrac{x}{4}-2$ and $g(x)=4x-2k$. If $g$ is the inverse of $f$, then what is the value of $k$?

**Explanation**

If $g$ is the inverse of $f$, then we must have

$$
g(f(x)) = x
$$

To find $g(f(x))$, we write down the function $g$ and replace every occurrence of $x$ with $f(x)$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
g(x) &= 4x - 2k \\
g(f(x)) &= 4f(x) - 2k
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we substitute

$$
{\color{blue}f(x)} = {\color{blue}\dfrac{x}{4}-2}
$$

into the right-hand side of our expression and simplify, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
g(f(x)) &= 4f(x) - 2k \\
&= 4(\frac{x}{4} - 2) - 2k \\
&= x - 8 - 2k
\end{aligned}
$$

Because $g$ is the inverse of $f$, we also know that

$$
g(f(x)) = x
$$

Equating the above expression with $x$ and solving for $k$, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
x - 8 - 2k &= x \\
x - 8 - 2k &= x \\
- 8 - 2k &= 0 \\
2k &= -8 \\
k &= -4
\end{aligned}
$$
