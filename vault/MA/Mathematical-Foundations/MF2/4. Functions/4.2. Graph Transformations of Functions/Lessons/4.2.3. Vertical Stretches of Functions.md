## Introduction

When we multiply a function by a constant, the graph either stretches out or compresses. Transforming a function in this way is called **scaling**.

To illustrate, consider the graph of $y=f(x)$ shown below.

![](<../Source/4.2.3. Vertical Stretches of Functions/Images/f7485248b3f5658e5bb5d3ded2eb4785.png>)

The following table lists four sets of points. The set of points in the left-hand column correspond to the graph of $y=f(x)$, and the set on the right-hand side corresponds to $y=2f(x)$.

$$
\begin{bmatrix}Points on [math]y=f(x)[/math] & Points on [math]y=2f(x)[/math] \\ (1, 2) & (1, 4) \\ (4,-3) & (4,-6) \\ (0, 2) & (0, 4) \\ (-2, 0) & (-2, 0)\end{bmatrix}
$$

For each point on the graph of $y=f(x)$, there is a point on the graph of $y=2f(x)$ with the same $x$-value and *double* the $y$-value.

Now that we have four points on the graph of $y=2f(x)$, let's plot the full graph.

![](<../Source/4.2.3. Vertical Stretches of Functions/Images/9b5840dcf6d1d828021ae25843ddd186.png>)

We say that the graph of $y=2f(x)$ is the graph of $y=f(x)$ **vertically stretched** by a **scale factor** of $2$.

We can also say that the graph of $y=2f(x)$ is the graph of $y=f(x)$ stretched by a factor of $2$ **parallel to the $\mathbf y$-axis.**

## Plotting the Graph of a Vertically Stretched Function

**Example:** The graph of $y=f(x)$ is shown below. Sketch the graph of $y=4f(x)$.

![](<../Source/4.2.3. Vertical Stretches of Functions/Images/92cd312c6f65be1e2876ce4d25b8a108.png>)

**Explanation**

To graph the function $y = 4f(x)$, we take the graph of $y = f(x)$ and stretch it by factor of $4$ parallel to the $y$-axis. This has the effect of multiplying the $y$-coordinates of all points on $y = f(x)$ by $4$.

The resulting graph is the solid blue curve shown below.

![](<../Source/4.2.3. Vertical Stretches of Functions/Images/204d690040ddcd205cda0a9908221f9a.png>)

## Plotting the Graph of a Vertically Compressed Function

**Example:** The graph of $y=f(x)$ is shown above. Sketch the graph of $y = \dfrac{1}{2}f(x)$.

![](<../Source/4.2.3. Vertical Stretches of Functions/Images/3a6a490dd4c7c2ab46912cf5603b2b75.png>)

**Explanation**

To graph the function

$$
y = \dfrac{1}{2}f(x)
$$

we take the graph of $y = f(x)$ and compress it by factor of $2$ parallel to the $y$-axis. This has the effect of dividing the $y$-coordinates of all points on $y = f(x)$ by $2$.

The resulting graph is the solid blue curve shown below.

## Finding a Point that Lies on a Vertically Scaled Function

**Example:** The point $P$ with coordinates $(-2, 2)$ lies on the curve $y=f(x)$. Find a point that must lie on the graph of $y= 7f(x)$.

**Explanation**

The function $y = 7f(x)$ represents a stretch of the function $y = f(x)$ by a factor of $7$ parallel to the $y$-axis. This has the effect of multiplying the $y$-coordinates of all points on $y=f(x)$ by $7$.

Therefore, the point $(-2,2)$ on $f(x)$ is mapped to the point

$$
\left(-2, 7 \cdot 2\right) = (-2, 14)
$$

and this point must lie on the graph of $y = 7f(x)$.
