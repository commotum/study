## Introduction

Sometimes, perfect square trinomials have a leading coefficient that is something other than $1$. For example, the expression

$$
4x^2+12x+9
$$

has a leading coefficient of $4$, and it is a perfect square trinomial because it is the square of the binomial $(2x+3)$ as we can see below:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(2x + 3)^{2} &=  \\
(2x)^{2} + 2(2x)(3) + (3)^{2} &=  \\
&= 4x^{2} + 12x + 9
\end{aligned}
$$

To factor a perfect square trinomial with a leading coefficient other than $1$, we use the square of sum formula:

$$
\begin{aligned}
a^{2} + 2ab + b^{2} &= (a + b)^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

To illustrate, let's factor the expression $4x^2+12x+9$ using the square of sum formula. We perform the following steps:

1. Write both the quadratic term and the constant term as perfect squares:
$4x^{2} + 12x + 9|=; (2x)^{2} + 12x + (3)^{2}$
2. Factor a $2$ out of the linear term:
$(2x)^{2} + 2(6x) + (3)^{2}$
3. Write the middle parentheses $(6x)$ as a product of $2x$ and ${\color{blue}3}$:
$(2x)^{2} + 2(2x \cdot 3) + (3)^{2}$
4. Factor the trinomial using the square of sum formula. In this case, we have $a=2x$ and $b=3$:
$(2x + 3)^{2}$

**Note:** This method works *only* when the trinomial is a perfect square! It will *not* work for every trinomial. But we will learn more general methods in the future.

## Factoring a Perfect Square Trinomial Generated by the Square of a Sum

**Example:** Factor $9x^2+30x+25$.

**Explanation**

The "square of a sum" formula states that

$$
a^2 + {\color{red}{2}}a{\color{blue}{b}} + {\color{blue}b}^2 = (a+{\color{blue}{b}})^2
$$

We will use this formula to factor the given trinomial. Our trinomial is

$$
9x^2+30x+25
$$

First, we write both the quadratic term and the constant term as perfect squares:

$$
(3x)^{2} + 30x + (5)^{2}
$$

Next, we factor a $2$ out of the linear term:
$(3x)^{2} + 2(15x) + (5)^{2}$

Then, we write the middle parentheses $(15x)$ as a product of $3x$ and ${\color{blue}5}$:
$(3x)^{2} + 2(3x \cdot 5) + (5)^{2}$

Finally, we factor the trinomial using the square of sum formula with $a=3x$ and

$$
{\color{blue}{b}} = {\color{blue}{5}}
$$

This gives
$(3x + 5)^{2}$

## Factoring a Perfect Square Trinomial Generated by the Square of a Difference

Other perfect square trinomials can be factored as the square of a difference:

$$
\begin{aligned}
a^{2} - 2ab + b^{2} &= (a - b)^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

To illustrate, let's factor the expression $9x^2-24x+16$ using the square of difference formula. We perform the following steps:

1. Write both the quadratic term and the constant term as perfect squares:
$9x^{2} - 24x + 16|=; (3x)^{2} - 24x + (4)^{2}$
2. Factor a $2$ out of the linear term:
$(3x)^{2} - 24x + (4)^{2}|=; (3x)^{2} - 2(12x) + (4)^{2}$
3. Write the middle parentheses $(12x)$ as a product of $3x$ and ${\color{blue}4}$:
$(3x)^{2} - 2(12x) + (4)^{2}|=; (3x)^{2} - 2(3x \cdot 4) + (4)^{2}$
4. Factor the trinomial using the square of difference formula. In this case, we have $a=3x$ and $b=4$:
$(3x)^{2} - 2(3x \cdot 4) + (4)^{2}|=; (3x - 4)^{2}$

**Note:** Once again, we note that this method works *only* when the trinomial is a perfect square! It will *not* work for every trinomial.

<a id="factoring-a-perfect-square-trinomial-generated-by-the-square-of-a-difference"></a>
## Factoring a Perfect Square Trinomial Generated by the Square of a Difference

**Example:** Factor $9y^2-12y+4$.

**Explanation**

The "square of a difference" formula states that

$$
a^2 - {\color{red}{2}}a{\color{blue}{b}} + {\color{blue}b}^2 = (a-{\color{blue}{b}})^2
$$

We will use this formula to factor the given trinomial. Our trinomial is

$$
9y^2-12y+4
$$

First, we write both the quadratic term and the constant term as perfect squares:
$(3y)^{2} - 12y + (2)^{2}$

Next, we factor a $2$ out of the linear term:
$(3y)^{2} - 2(6y) + (2)^{2}$

Then, we write the middle parentheses $(6y)$ as a product of $3y$ and ${\color{blue}2}$:
$(3y)^{2} - 2(3y \cdot 2) + (2)^{2}$

Finally, we factor the trinomial using the square of difference formula with $a=3y$ and

$$
{\color{blue}{b}} = {\color{blue}{2}}
$$

This gives
$(3y - 2)^{2}$

## Factoring a Perfect Square Trinomial Whose Terms Have a Common Factor

**Example:** Factor the expression $7a^2+28a+28$.

**Explanation**

Before we start our normal process of factoring, notice that all of the terms have a common factor of $7$. Therefore, we can simplify the expression by factoring out $7$, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
7a^{2} + 28a + 28 &=  \\
&= 7(a^{2} + 4a + 4)
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, let's factor the expression inside parentheses using the square of sum formula:

$$
\begin{aligned}
a^{2} + 4a + 4 &=  \\
&= a^{2} + 4a + 2^{2} \\
&= a^{2} + 2 \cdot 2a + 2^{2} \\
&= (a + 2)^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, our original expression factors as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
7(a^{2} + 4a + 4) &=  \\
&= 7(a + 2)^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Factoring a Perfect Square Trinomial With a Negative Leading Coefficient

How can we factor a trinomial that has a negative leading coefficient, like
$-25y^2 + 20y - 4\,$?
We can reduce this to a more familiar problem by factoring out $-1$ from the entire expression:
$-(25y^2 - 20y + 4)$

Now, we factor the quadratic expression inside the parentheses using the square of a difference formula:

$$
\begin{aligned}
25y^{2} - 20y + 4 &=  \\
(5y)^{2} - 20y + 2^{2} &=  \\
(5y)^{2} - 2(10y) + 2^{2} &=  \\
(5y)^{2} - 2(5y \cdot 2) + 2^{2} &=  \\
&= (5y - 2)^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, our original expression factors as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
- (25y^{2} - 20y + 4) &=  \\
&= - (5y - 2)^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="factoring-a-perfect-square-trinomial-with-a-negative-leading-coefficient"></a>
## Factoring a Perfect Square Trinomial With a Negative Leading Coefficient

**Example:** Factor $-16y^2 + 4y - \dfrac{1}{4}$.

**Explanation**

Notice that the leading coefficient is negative. To reduce the expression to a more familiar form, we can factor out $-1$ as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
-16y^{2} + 4y - \frac{1}{4} &=  \\
&= - (16y^{2} - 4y + \frac{1}{4})
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we factor the quadratic expression inside the parentheses using the sum of difference formula:

$$
\begin{aligned}
16y^{2} - 4y + \frac{1}{4} &=  \\
(4y)^{2} - 4y + (\frac{1}{2})^{2} &=  \\
(4y)^{2} - 2 \cdot (2y) + (\frac{1}{2})^{2} &=  \\
(4y)^{2} - 2 \cdot (4y \cdot \frac{1}{2}) + (\frac{1}{2})^{2} &=  \\
&= (4y - \frac{1}{2})^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, our original expression factors as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
- (16y^{2} - 4y + \frac{1}{4}) &=  \\
&= - (4y - \frac{1}{2})^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$
