## Introduction

The **greatest common factor** (or $\textrm{GCF}$) of the two monomials $12x^2$ and $54x$ is the largest expression that will divide *both* $12x^2$ and $54x$ with no remainder.

To find the greatest common factor, we follow three steps:

1. First, decompose each expression into its separate factors. We decompose each constant into its prime factors, and we treat each variable in the product as a separate factor.
$12x^{2}|= 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 3 \cdot x \cdot x; 54x|= 2 \cdot 3 \cdot 3 \cdot 3 \cdot x$
2. Now, we pair off the individual factors as much as possible:
$12x^{2}|= 2_(―) \cdot 2 \cdot 3_(―) \cdot x_(―) \cdot x; 54x|= 2_(―) \cdot 3_(―) \cdot 3 \cdot 3 \cdot x_(―)$
3. The greatest common factor is the product of these paired factors:

$$
\textrm{GCF}(12x^2, 54x) = {\color{red}2} \cdot {\color{blue}3} \cdot {\color{green}x} = 6x
$$

## Finding the Greatest Common Factor With One Variable

**Example:** What is the greatest common factor of $10y^2$ and $25y^4$?

**Explanation**

First, we decompose each expression into its separate factors. We express each constant as a product of its prime factors, and we treat each variable in the product as a separate factor.

$$
\begin{aligned}
10y^{2} &= 2 \cdot 5 \cdot y \cdot y \\
25y^{4} &= 5 \cdot 5 \cdot y \cdot y \cdot y \cdot y
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we pair off the individual factors as much as possible:

$$
\begin{aligned}
10y^{2} &= 2 \cdot 5_(―) \cdot y_(―) \cdot y_(―) \\
25y^{4} &= 5_(―) \cdot 5 \cdot y_(―) \cdot y_(―) \cdot y \cdot y
\end{aligned}
$$

The greatest common factor is the product of these paired factors:

$$
\textrm{GCF}(10y^2, 25y^4) = {\color{red}5} \cdot {\color{blue}y} \cdot {\color{green}y} = 5y^2
$$

## Finding the Greatest Common Factor With Two Variables

**Example:** What is the greatest common factor of $6x^2y^2$ and $3xy$?

**Explanation**

First, we decompose each expression into its separate factors. We express each constant as a product of its prime factors, and we treat each variable in the product as a separate factor.

$$
\begin{aligned}
6x^{2}y^{2} &= 2 \cdot 3 \cdot x \cdot x \cdot y \cdot y \\
3xy &= 3 \cdot x \cdot y
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we pair off the individual factors as much as possible:

$$
\begin{aligned}
6x^{2}y^{2} &= 2 \cdot 3_(―) \cdot x_(―) \cdot x \cdot y_(―) \cdot y \\
3xy &= 3_(―) \cdot x_(―) \cdot y_(―)
\end{aligned}
$$

The greatest common factor is the product of these paired factors:

$$
\textrm{GCF}(6x^2y^2, 3xy) = {\color{red}3} \cdot {\color{blue}x} \cdot {\color{green}y} = 3xy
$$

## Finding the Greatest Common Factor With Three Variables

**Example:** What is the greatest common factor of $-18p^2qr$ and $24p^3qr^2$?

**Explanation**

The terms are $-18p^2qr$ and $24p^3qr^2$. When finding the greatest common factor, we ignore the negative sign.

First, we decompose each expression into its separate factors. We express each constant as a product of its prime factors, and we treat each variable in the product as a separate factor.

$$
\begin{aligned}
18p^{2}qr &= 2 \cdot 3 \cdot 3 \cdot p \cdot p \cdot q \cdot r \\
24p^{3}qr^{2} &= 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 3 \cdot p \cdot p \cdot p \cdot q \cdot r \cdot r
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we pair off the individual factors as much as possible:

$$
\begin{aligned}
18p^{2}qr &= 2_(―) \cdot 3_(―) \cdot 3 \cdot p_(―) \cdot p_(―) \cdot q_(―) \cdot r_(―) \\
24p^{3}qr^{2} &= 2_(―) \cdot 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 3_(―) \cdot p_(―) \cdot p_(―) \cdot p \cdot q_(―) \cdot r_(―) \cdot r
\end{aligned}
$$

The greatest common factor is the product of these paired factors:

$$
\textrm{GCF}(-18p^2qr, 24p^3qr^2) = {\color{red}2} \cdot {\color{blue}3} \cdot {\color{green}p} \cdot {\color{green}p} \cdot {\color{YellowOrange}q} \cdot {\color{Magenta}r} = 6p^2qr
$$

## Finding the Greatest Common Factor of Three Monomials

**Example:** What is the greatest common factor of $30ab^3$, $10a^3b$, and $15a^2b^2$?

**Explanation**

First, we decompose each expression into its separate factors. We express each constant as a product of its prime factors, and we treat each variable in the product as a separate factor.

$$
\begin{aligned}
30ab^{3} &= 2 \cdot 3 \cdot 5 \cdot a \cdot b \cdot b \cdot b \\
10a^{3}b &= 2 \cdot 5 \cdot a \cdot a \cdot a \cdot b \\
15a^{2}b^{2} &= 3 \cdot 5 \cdot a \cdot a \cdot b \cdot b
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we pair off the individual factors as much as possible:

$$
\begin{aligned}
30ab^{3} &= 2 \cdot 3 \cdot 5_(―) \cdot a_(―) \cdot b_(―) \cdot b \cdot b \\
10a^{3}b &= 2 \cdot 5_(―) \cdot a_(―) \cdot a \cdot a \cdot b_(―) \\
15a^{2}b^{2} &= 3 \cdot 5_(―) \cdot a_(―) \cdot a \cdot b_(―) \cdot b
\end{aligned}
$$

The greatest common factor is the product of these paired factors:

$$
\textrm{GCF}\left(30ab^3, 10a^3b, 15a^2b^2\right) = {\color{red}5} \cdot {\color{blue}a} \cdot {\color{violet}b} = 5ab
$$
