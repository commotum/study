## Introduction

Let's think about how to factor the following trinomial:

$$
x^2 + 7x + 12
$$

This trinomial is neither a perfect square nor a difference of squares. In such cases, the factoring process requires a little trial and error.

We can factor this trinomial in four steps:

- **Step 1:** Find the factor pairs of the constant term $(12)$:The factor pairs of $12$ are
$(1, 12), (2, 6), (3, 4)$.
- **Step 2:** Determine which of these factor pairs add up to the coefficient of the linear term $(7)$:
$\begin{bmatrix}1 + 12|= 13 & \cdot \\ 2 + 6|= 8 & \cdot \\ 3 + 4|= 7 & ✓\end{bmatrix}$
- **Step 3:** Using the factor pair identified in the previous step, write the linear term as $7x=3x+4x$:
$x^{2} + 7x + 12|=; x^{2} + 3x + 4x_(⏟)_(7x) + 12$
- **Step 4:** Factor the first two terms and the last two terms separately and then factor the expression completely:
$x^{2} + 3x + 4x + 12|=; (x^{2} + 3x) + (4x + 12)|=; x(x + 3) + 4(x + 3)|=; (x + 4)(x + 3)$

And we're done!

Before we continue, note the following:

- The process carried out in step 4 is sometimes called **factoring by grouping.** This is because we group two sets of terms and factor them individually before factoring the entire expression.
- We can check that our answer is correct by multiplying out the factors:
$(x + 4)(x + 3)|= x(x + 3) + 4(x + 3); = x^{2} + 3x + 4x + 12; = x^{2} + 7x + 12✓$

## Factoring Trinomials With Positive Coefficients

**Example:** Factor the expression $z^2+13z+36$.

**Explanation**

We must factor the quadratic expression
$z^2+13z+36$.

First, we find the factor pairs of the constant term $(36)$:

$$
(1, 36), (2, 18), (3, 12), (4, 9), (6, 6)
$$

Now, we find which factor pair adds up to the coefficient of the linear term $(13)$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\begin{bmatrix}1 + 36 &= 37 & \cdot \\ 2 + 18|= 20 & \cdot \\ 3 + 12|= 15 & \cdot \\ 4 + 9|= 13 & ✓ \\ 6 + 6|= 12 & \cdot\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Using the factor pair identified in the previous step, write the linear term as

$$
13z=4z + 9z
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
z^{2} + 13z + 36 &=  \\
&= z^{2} + 4z + 9z + 36
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we factor the first two terms and the last two terms separately, and then factor the expression completely:

$$
\begin{aligned}
z^{2} + 4z + 9z + 36 &=  \\
(z^{2} + 4z) + (9z + 36) &=  \\
z(z + 4) + 9(z + 4) &=  \\
&= (z + 9)(z + 4)
\end{aligned}
$$

## Trinomials With Negative Constants

Let's now consider the following trinomial:

$$
x^2 - 2x - 8
$$

Notice that the constant term $(-8)$ is *negative*. So, when writing down our factor pairs, one number will always be positive, and the other will always be negative.

So, first, we write down all factor pairs of $-8{:}$

$$
(1,-8), (-1, 8), (2,-4), (-2, 4)
$$

Now, we find which factor pair adds up to the coefficient of the linear term $(-2)$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\begin{bmatrix}1 + (-8) &= -7 & \cdot \\ (-1) + 8|= 7 & \cdot \\ 2 + (-4)|=-2 & ✓ \\ (-2) + 4|= 2 & \cdot\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Using the factor pair identified above, write the linear term as

$$
-2x = 2x-4x
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^{2} - 2x - 8 &=  \\
&= x^{2} + 2x - 4x - 8
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we factor the first two terms and the last two terms separately, and then factor the expression completely:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^{2} + 2x - 4x - 8 &=  \\
(x^{2} + 2x) + (-4x - 8) &=  \\
x(x + 2) - 4(x + 2) &=  \\
&= (x - 4)(x + 2)
\end{aligned}
$$

Let's see another example.

## Factoring Trinomials With Negative Constants

**Example:** Factor the expression $a^2+a-20$.

**Explanation**

We must factor the quadratic expression
$a^2+a-20$.

First, we find the factor pairs of the constant term $(-20)$:

$$
(1,-20), (-1, 20), (2,-10), (-2, 10), (4,-5), (-4, 5)
$$

Now, we find which factor pair adds up to the coefficient of the linear term $(1)$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\begin{bmatrix}1 + (-20) &= -19 & \cdot \\ -1 + 20|= 19 & \cdot \\ 2 + (-10)|=-8 & \cdot \\ -2 + 10|= 8 & \cdot \\ 4 + (-5)|=-1 & \cdot \\ -4 + 5|= 1 & ✓\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Using the factor pair identified above, write the linear term as

$$
a=-4a+5a
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
a^{2} + a - 20 &=  \\
&= a^{2} - 4a + 5a - 20
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we factor the first two terms and the last two terms separately, and then factor the expression completely:

$$
\begin{aligned}
a^{2} - 4a + 5a - 20 &=  \\
(a^{2} - 4a) + (5a - 20) &=  \\
a(a - 4) + 5(a - 4) &=  \\
&= (a + 5)(a - 4)
\end{aligned}
$$

## Trinomials With Negative Linear Coefficients and Positive Constants

Remember that to factor a quadratic expression, we must find a factor pair of the constant term that adds to the coefficient of the linear term.

With that in mind, let's factor the following trinomial:

$$
x^2 - 7x+12
$$

Notice that,

- the constant term $(12)$ is *positive*, while
- the coefficient of the linear term $(-7)$ is *negative*.

Since the constant is *positive* and the coefficient of the linear term is *negative*, we must first consider the *negative* factor pairs of the constant term $(12)$. These are as follows:

$$
(-1,-12),\qquad (-2,-6),\qquad (-3,-4)
$$

For each pair, their *product* is positive, and their *sum* is negative, which is what we want.

Now, we find which factor pair adds up to the coefficient of the linear term $(-7)$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\begin{bmatrix}(-1) + (-12) &= -13 & \cdot \\ (-2) + (-6)|=-8 & \cdot \\ (-3) + (-4)|=-7 & ✓\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Using the factor pair identified in the previous step, write the linear term as

$$
-7x = -3x - 4x
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^{2} - 7x + 12 &=  \\
&= x^{2} - 3x - 4x + 12
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we factor the first two terms and the last two terms separately:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^{2} - 3x - 4x + 12 &=  \\
(x^{2} - 3x) + (-4x + 12) &=  \\
&= x(x - 3) + 4(-x + 3)
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, to get a *common* factor in both terms, we take an additional factor of $-1$ from the second term:

$$
x(x - 3) - 4(x - 3)
$$

Now that we have a common factor of $(x-3)$, we factor the expression fully in the usual way:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x(x - 3) - 4(x - 3) &=  \\
&= (x - 3)(x - 4)
\end{aligned}
$$

## Factoring Trinomials With Negative Linear Coefficients and Positive Constants

**Example:** Factor the expression $n^2 - 6n + 8$.

**Explanation**

We must factor the quadratic expression
$n^2 - 6n + 8$.

Notice that,

- the constant term $(8)$ is *positive*, while
- the coefficient of the linear term $(-6)$ is *negative*.

Therefore, we first consider the *negative* factor pairs of the constant term $(8)$:

$$
(-1,-8), \quad (-2,-4)
$$

Now we find which factor pair adds up to the coefficient of the linear term $(-6)$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\begin{bmatrix}(-1) + (-8) &= -9 & \cdot \\ (-2) + (-4)|=-6 & ✓\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Using the factor pair identified in the previous step, write the linear term as $-6n=-2n-4n$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
n^{2} - 6n + 8 &=  \\
&= n^{2} - 2n - 4n + 8
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we factor the first two terms and the last two terms separately and then factor the expression completely:

$$
\begin{aligned}
n^{2} - 2n - 4n + 8 &=  \\
(n^{2} - 2n) + (-4n + 8) &=  \\
n(n - 2) - 4(n - 2) &=  \\
&= (n - 4)(n - 2)
\end{aligned}
$$
