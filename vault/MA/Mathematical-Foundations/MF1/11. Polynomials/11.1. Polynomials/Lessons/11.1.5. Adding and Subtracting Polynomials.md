## Introduction

Suppose that we have the two polynomials $6x^2 + x +1$ and $2x^2 - 3x + 6$, and we want to add them. To do that, we put a plus sign between the two, collect like terms, and then simplify:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(6x^{2} + x + 1) + (2x^{2} - 3x + 6) &=  \\
6x^{2} + x + 1 + 2x^{2} - 3x + 6 &=  \\
6x^{2} + 2x^{2}_(⏟)_(x^{2}terms) + x - 3x^(⏞)^(xterms) + 1 + 6_(⏟)_(constants) &=  \\
(6 + 2)x^{2} + (1 - 3)x + 7 &=  \\
&= 8x^{2} - 2x + 7
\end{aligned}
$$

## Adding Polynomials

**Example:** Add the polynomials $x^3-x^2 - 2$ and $-2x^3 + 3x^2-x$.

**Explanation**

We put a plus sign between the two polynomials, collect like terms, and then simplify:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x^{3} - x^{2} - 2) + (-2x^{3} + 3x^{2} - x) &=  \\
x^{3} - x^{2} - 2 - 2x^{3} + 3x^{2} - x &=  \\
x^{3} - 2x^{3} - x^{2} + 3x^{2} - x - 2 &=  \\
(1 - 2)x^{3} + (-1 + 3)x^{2} - x - 2 &=  \\
(-1)x^{3} + (2)x^{2} - x - 2 &=  \\
&= - x^{3} + 2x^{2} - x - 2
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="adding-polynomials-expressed-as-functions"></a>
## Adding Polynomials Expressed as Functions

**Example:** If $f(x) = 1-2x^3$ and $g(x)=1+3x^3$, then what is $f(x) + g(x)$?

**Explanation**

We put a plus sign between the two polynomials, collect like terms, and then simplify:

$$
\begin{aligned}
f(x) + g(x) &=  \\
(1 - 2x^{3}) + (1 + 3x^{3}) &=  \\
- 2x^{3} + 3x^{3} + 1 + 1 &=  \\
(-2 + 3)x^{3} + (1 + 1) &=  \\
(1)x^{3} + (2) &=  \\
&= x^{3} + 2
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="the-difference-of-two-polynomials"></a>
## The Difference of Two Polynomials

Suppose that we want to *subtract* the polynomial $2x^2 + x$ from the polynomial $6x^2 - 3x +1$. To do that, we start by putting a minus sign between the two:

$$
(6x^2 - 3x +1) - (2x^2 + x)
$$

Here, we need to remember to distribute the minus sign. Once we've done that, we can collect like terms and then simplify as usual:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(6x^{2} - 3x + 1) - (2x^{2} + x) &=  \\
6x^{2} - 3x + 1 - 2x^{2} - x &=  \\
6x^{2} - 2x^{2} - 3x - x + 1 &=  \\
(6 - 2)x^{2} + (-3 - 1)x + 1 &=  \\
&= 4x^{2} - 4x + 1
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="subtracting-polynomials"></a>
## Subtracting Polynomials

**Example:** Subtract the polynomial $1-x-2x^2$ from $x^2+x+6$.

**Explanation**

We put a minus sign between the two polynomials, distribute it over the second polynomial, collect like terms, and then simplify:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x^{2} + x + 6) - (1 - x - 2x^{2}) &=  \\
x^{2} + x + 6 - 1 + x + 2x^{2} &=  \\
(1 + 2)x^{2} + (1 + 1)x + (6 - 1) &=  \\
&= 3x^{2} + 2x + 5
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="subtracting-polynomials-expressed-as-functions"></a>
## Subtracting Polynomials Expressed as Functions

**Example:** If $f(x) = x^2-6$ and $g(x) =4 - x^2$, what is $f(x)-g(x)$?

**Explanation**

We put a minus sign between the two polynomials, distribute the minus sign, collect like terms, and then simplify:

$$
\begin{aligned}
f(x) - g(x) &=  \\
(x^{2} - 6) - (4 - x^{2}) &=  \\
x^{2} - 6 - 4 + x^{2} &=  \\
(x^{2} + x^{2}) + (-6 - 4) &=  \\
&= 2x^{2} - 10
\end{aligned}
$$
