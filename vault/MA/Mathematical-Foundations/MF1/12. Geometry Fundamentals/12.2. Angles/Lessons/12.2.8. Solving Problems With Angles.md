## Introduction

When solving problems with angles, it is helpful to remember the following classifications:

- a **null** angle has a measure of $0^\circ$,
- an **acute** angle has a measure between $0^\circ$ and $90^\circ$,
- a **right** angle has a measure of $90^\circ$,
- an **obtuse** angle has a measure between $90^\circ$ and $180^\circ$,
- a **straight** angle has a measure of $180^\circ$,
- a **reflex** angle has a measure between $180^\circ$ and $360^\circ$, and
- a **full** angle has a measure of $360^\circ$.

It is also helpful to remember that when two lines meet at a point, the opposite angles are called **vertical angles**. Vertical angles are congruent, and consequently they have the same measure.

## Identifying Angle Types From a Diagram

**Example:** Consider the diagram above. Which of the following statements is true?

![](<../Source/12.2.8. Solving Problems With Angles/Images/7a256493d1611876231c4bd89ba72805.png>)

1. $\angle A$ is obtuse
2. $\angle B$ is a right angle
3. $\angle A$ and $\angle B$ are vertical angles

**Explanation**

Let's examine our statements in turn.

- Statement I is true. From the diagram, $\angle A$ and the angle with measure $130^\circ$ are vertical angles. Therefore,
$m\angle A = 130^\circ > 90^\circ$.
Consequently, $\angle A$ is an obtuse angle.
- Statement II is false. From the diagram, $\angle B$ and the angle with measure $50^\circ$ are vertical angles. Therefore,
$m\angle B = 50^\circ \neq 90^\circ$.
Consequently, $\angle B$ is not a right angle.
- Statement III is false. Angles $\angle A$ and $\angle B$ are not vertical since they have a common side.

So, the correct answer is "I only."

<a id="identifying-an-angle-type-from-a-description"></a>
## Identifying an Angle Type From a Description

**Example:** Let $\angle S$ and $\angle T$ be two adjacent angles. Given that $\angle S$ is a straight angle and $\angle T$ is right, what type of angle is their sum?

**Explanation**

Since $\angle S$ is a straight angle, we have

$$
m\angle S=180^\circ
$$

and since $\angle T$ is a right angle, we have

$$
m\angle T=90^{\circ}
$$

So, their sum is

$$
m\angle S + m\angle T=180^{\circ} + 90^{\circ}=270^{\circ}
$$

Consequently, the sum of $\angle S$ and $\angle T$ is a reflex angle.

## Calculating Angles From Right, Straight, and Full Angles Using Algebra

**Example:** Given that $\angle DOA$ is a straight angle, what is the value of $x$?

![](<../Source/12.2.8. Solving Problems With Angles/Images/024efbfcba9b764b6a65dafe47665693.png>)

**Explanation**

Note that $\angle AOD$ is a straight angle and $\angle BOC$ is a right angle. Hence,

$$
m∠AOD = 180^{∘}, m∠BOC = 90^{∘}
$$

Therefore, we have:

$$
\begin{aligned}
m∠AOB + m∠BOC + m∠COD &= m∠AOD \\
x + 90^{∘} + (2x - 30^{∘}) &= 180^{∘} \\
3x + 60^{∘} &= 180^{∘} \\
3x &= 120^{∘} \\
x &= 40^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="finding-the-measure-of-unknown-angles-on-a-diagram"></a>
## Finding the Measure of Unknown Angles on a Diagram

**Example:** In the figure shown below, determine the measure of $\angle EOA$.

![](<../Source/12.2.8. Solving Problems With Angles/Images/0dedb3faf4277792f61ed915e3c725d1.png>)

**Explanation**

From the diagram, $\angle COD$ is a right angle, so

$$
m\angle COD = 90^\circ
$$

Also, since $\angle COD$ and $\angle EOB$ are vertical angles, they have the same measure:

$$
m\angle EOB=m\angle COD=90^\circ
$$

Now, since we know that

$$
m\angle AOB=40^{\circ}
$$

and

$$
m\angle EOB = 90^\circ
$$

we can use the angle addition postulate to solve for $m\angle EOA$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
m∠EOA + m∠AOB &= m∠EOB \\
m∠EOA + 40^{∘} &= 90^{∘} \\
m∠EOA &= 50^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$
