## Introduction

Two angles are **congruent** if their measures are equal.

For example, if

$$
m\angle X =50^\circ \quad \text{and} \ \quad m\angle Y=50^\circ
$$

then $\angle X$ and $\angle Y$ are congruent. We can express this as

$$
\angle X \cong \angle Y
$$

However, if

$$
m\angle X =50^\circ \quad \text{and} \ \quad m\angle Z = 110^\circ
$$

then $\angle X$ and $\angle Z$ are *not* congruent, and we write

$$
\angle X \ncong \angle Z
$$

We represent congruent angles by marking the arcs with the same symbol. For example, three pairs of congruent angles are show below:

## Identifying Congruent Angles Given Measures of Angles

**Example:** From the information given below, find all of the angles that are congruent.
$m∠A|= 10^{∘},; m∠B|= 40^{∘},; m∠C|= 80^{∘},; m∠D|= 90^{∘} - m∠C,; m∠E|= m∠B$.

**Explanation**

First, let's find the measures of $\angle D$ and $\angle E$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
m∠D &= 90^{∘} - m∠C \\
&= 90^{∘} - 80^{∘} \\
&= 10^{∘} \\
m∠E &= m∠B \\
&= 40^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

So, we have:

$$
\begin{aligned}
m∠A &= 10^{∘} \\
m∠B &= 40^{∘} \\
m∠C &= 80^{∘} \\
m∠D &= 10^{∘} \\
m∠E &= 40^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

We have that

$$
m \angle A = m \angle D
$$

and

$$
m \angle B = m \angle E
$$

Therefore, $\angle A \cong \angle D$ and $\angle B \cong \angle E$.

## Using Markings to Denote Congruent Angles

**Example:** Which of the following statements are true regarding the angles shown below?

1. $\angle AOB \cong \angle COD$
2. $\angle COD \cong \angle BOC$
3. $m\angle BOC = m\angle DOE$

![](<../Source/12.2.6. Congruent Angles/Images/04aa672fd921e36d20ed92c462e6985c.png>)

**Explanation**

From the diagram, we see that

- $\angle AOB$ & $\angle COD$ are each marked with a single hash mark "$\mid$," while
- $\angle BOC$ & $\angle DOE$ are each marked with a double hash mark "$\mid \mid$."

Therefore,
$\begin{bmatrix}∠AOB & ≅∠COD \\ ∠BOC & ≅∠DOE\end{bmatrix}$.

Consequently, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
m∠AOB &= m∠COD \\
m∠BOC &= m∠DOE
\end{aligned}
$$

So statements I and III are true, while statement II is false.

<a id="solving-problems-involving-congruent-angles"></a>
## Solving Problems Involving Congruent Angles

**Example:** In the figure above, $m \angle CBD = 5x - 5^\circ$ and $m \angle ABD =2x + 10^\circ$. If $\angle CBD \cong \angle ABD$, find $m \angle ABC$.

![](<../Source/12.2.6. Congruent Angles/Images/471575dfacb48d1dfd85105920a5514c.png>)

**Explanation**

Since $\angle CBD \cong \angle ABD$, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
m∠CBD &= m∠ABD \\
5x - 5^{∘} &= 2x + 10^{∘} \\
5x &= 2x + 15^{∘} \\
3x &= 15^{∘} \\
x &= 5^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the measure of $\angle CBD$ is

$$
m \angle CBD = 5x - 5^\circ = 5 \cdot 5^\circ - 5^\circ = 20^\circ
$$

So, by the angle addition postulate,

$$
\begin{aligned}
m∠ABC &= m∠CBD + m∠ABD \\
&= m∠CBD + m∠CBD \\
&= 2 \cdot m∠CBD \\
&= 2 \cdot 20^{∘} \\
&= 40^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$
