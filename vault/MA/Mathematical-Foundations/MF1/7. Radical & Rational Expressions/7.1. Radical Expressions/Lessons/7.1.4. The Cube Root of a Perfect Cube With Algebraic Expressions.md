## Introduction

To find the **cube root** of a number $x$, we must ask ourselves, "what number *cubed* equals $x$?"

For example, the cube root of $8$ is $2$ since

$$
2^3 = 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 = 8
$$

and the cube root of $-8$ is $-2$ since

$$
(-2)^3 = (-2) \cdot (-2) \cdot (-2) = -8
$$

In general, the cube root of $x^3$ is $x$.

Like the square root, the cube root is signified by a radical symbol, but it also has a little $3$ next to it, i.e., $\sqrt[3\!\phantom{p}]{\phantom{2}}$. Using this notation, we can express the above statements mathematically:

- "The cube root of $8$ is $2$" can be expressed as $\sqrt[3]{8}=2$.
- "The cube root of $-8$ is $-2$" can be expressed as $\sqrt[3]{-8}=-2$.
- "The cube root of $x^3$ is $x$" can be expressed as $\sqrt[3]{x^3}=x$.

**Note:** For square roots, we have

$$
\sqrt{x^2} = \mid x \mid
$$

but for cube roots, we have

$$
\sqrt[3]{x^3} = x
$$

No absolute value is needed for cube roots, because

- the cube root of a positive number is a positive number, and
- the cube root of a negative number is a negative number.

## Finding the Cube Root of a Monomial

**Example:** Find $\sqrt[3]{-8x^6}$.

**Explanation**

We write the term under the radical as a perfect cube, and simplify:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt[3]{-8x^{6}} &=  \\
\sqrt[3]{(-2x^{2}) \cdot (-2x^{2}) \cdot (-2x^{2})} &=  \\
\sqrt[3]{(-2x^{2})^{3}} &=  \\
&= - 2x^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding Cube Roots of Terms Containing Binomials

**Example:** Simplify $\sqrt[3]{(5x - 4)^3}$.

**Explanation**

The expression underneath the radical is already a perfect cube. Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt[3]{(5x - 4)^{3}} &= 5x - 4
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding the Cube Root of a Fraction

**Example:** Simplify $\sqrt[3]{\dfrac{a^3}{(b-1)^3}}$.

**Explanation**

We write the term under the radical as a perfect cube, and simplify:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt[3]{(a^{3})/((b - 1)^{3})} &=  \\
\sqrt[3]{(a)/((b - 1)) \cdot (a)/((b - 1)) \cdot (a)/((b - 1))} &=  \\
\sqrt[3]{(\frac{a}{b - 1})^{3}} &=  \\
&= \frac{a}{b - 1}
\end{aligned}
$$
