## Introduction

A radical expression like $\sqrt{{\color{black}x}}$ can also be written using a fractional exponent as $x^{1/2}$. They mean the same thing.

Similarly,

- a cube root such as $\sqrt[{\color{blue}3\!\phantom{p}}]{x}$ is equivalent to $x^{1/\color{blue}{3}}$,
- a tenth root $\sqrt[{\color{blue}10\!\phantom{p}}]{x}$ is equivalent to $x^{1/\color{blue}{10}}$,

and so on. In general, we have the following formula:

$$
\sqrt[n]{x} = x^{1/n}
$$

---

<a id="converting-a-fractional-exponent-to-a-radical"></a>
## Converting a Fractional Exponent to a Radical

**Example:** Write $(2a)^{1/3}$ using radical notation.

**Explanation**

In general, the fractional exponent $x^{1/n}$ can also be written as the $n$th root $\sqrt[n]{x}$. In this particular case, $n=3$, $x=2a$, and we have

$$
(2a)^{1/3} = \sqrt[3]{2a}
$$

## Converting a Radical to a Fractional Exponent

**Example:** Write $\sqrt[4]{2y}$ using power notation.

**Explanation**

In general, the $n$th root $\sqrt[n]{x}$ can also be written using a fractional exponent as $x^{1/n}$. In this particular case, $n=4$, $x=2y$, and we have

$$
\sqrt[4]{2y} = (2y)^{1/4}
$$

## Converting a Negative Fractional Exponent to a Radical

**Example:** Write $x^{-1/2}$ using radical notation.

**Explanation**

Remember that, in general, a negative exponent can be converted to a positive exponent if we take the reciprocal. Here, we have

$$
x^{-1/2} = \dfrac{1}{x^{1/2}}
$$

Now, we can proceed as usual to convert the fractional exponent to a radical

$$
\dfrac{1}{x^{1/2}} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x}}
$$

Therefore, we have

$$
x^{-1/2} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x}}
$$

<a id="converting-a-radical-to-a-negative-fractional-exponent"></a>
## Converting a Radical to a Negative Fractional Exponent

**Example:** Express $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt[3\phantom{\mid}]{2y}}$ using power notation.

**Explanation**

First, we convert the radical to a fractional exponent:

$$
\dfrac{1}{\sqrt[3\phantom{\mid}]{2y}} = \dfrac{1}{(2y)^{1/3}}
$$

Then, we take the reciprocal and convert the exponent to a negative exponent:

$$
\dfrac{1}{(2y)^{1/3}} = (2y)^{-1/3}
$$

Therefore, we have:

$$
\dfrac{1}{\sqrt[3\phantom{\mid}]{2y}} = (2y)^{-1/3}
$$
