## Introduction

Some linear equations have **infinitely many solutions.**

For example, consider the following equation:

$$
x+5 = x + 5
$$

Let's attempt to solve this equation by subtracting $x$ from both sides:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x + 5 &= x + 5 \\
x + 5 - x &= x + 5 - x \\
5 &= 5
\end{aligned}
$$

So, what does $x$ equal? The statement $5=5$ is *always* true, irrespective of the value of $x$. This tells us that *any* value of $x$ is a solution to the equation!

Don't believe it? If we substitute any value, say $x=3$, into the original equation, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
x + 5 &= x + 5 \\
3 + 5 &= 3 + 5 \\
8 &= 8.✓
\end{aligned}
$$

It's important to note that substituting *any* value for $x$ into the equation will lead to a true statement. For example, the values

$$
x=42,\qquad x=-\dfrac 1 4, \qquad x=1.2345
$$

are *all* solutions to the original equation.

To summarize:

- Our original equation has *infinitely many solutions.*
- The solution of the equation is "*any (real) value of* $x$".

<a id="identifying-equations-with-infinitely-many-solutions"></a>
## Identifying Equations With Infinitely Many Solutions

**Example:** Which of the following equations has infinitely many solutions?

1. $4x + 1 = 4x + 1$
2. $-3x = -4x + 6$
3. $2x + 7 = 2x - 1$

**Explanation**

Let's attempt to solve each equation in turn:

- For the first equation, we have
$4x + 1|= 4x + 1; 4x + 1 - 4x|= 4x + 1 - 4x; 1|= 1$.
This equation is true for all values of $x$. Therefore, the equation $4x + 1 = 4x + 1$ has infinitely many solutions.
- For the second equation, we have
$-3x|=-4x + 6; - 3x + 4x|=-4x + 6 + 4x; x|= 6$.
Therefore, the equation $-3x = -4x + 6$ has the unique solution $x = 6$.
- For the third equation, we have
$2x + 7|= 2x - 1; 2x + 7 - 2x|= 2x - 1 - 2x; 7|=-1$.
This equation is false for all values of $x$.

Therefore, the correct answer is "I only."

<a id="conditions-for-an-equation-to-have-infinitely-many-solutions"></a>
## Conditions for an Equation To Have Infinitely Many Solutions

Suppose we have an equation of the form

$$
ax = bx
$$

where $a$ and $b$ are constants. If $a = b$, then the equation has infinitely many solutions.

For example, each of the following equations has infinitely many solutions:

$$
2x = 2x, \frac{1}{3}x = \frac{1}{3}x - 2.16x =-2.16x
$$

To show that the general equation $ax = bx$ has infinitely many solutions whenever $a=b$, we can substitute $a$ for $b$ in our equation as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
ax &= bx \\
ax &= (a)x \\
ax &= ax
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we simplify this equation by subtracting $ax$ from both sides of the equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
ax &= ax \\
ax - ax &= ax - ax \\
0 &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

The equation, $0=0$, is always true, regardless of the value of $x$. Therefore, the equation $ax = bx$ has infinitely many solutions whenever $a=b$, and any value of $x$ is a solution to the equation.

<a id="problems-involving-unknown-parameters"></a>
## Problems Involving Unknown Parameters

Consider the equation
$2qx = 10x$,

where $q$ is a constant. If we're given that this equation has infinitely many solutions, how can we find the value of $q$?

Notice that this equation is in the form $ax = bx{:}$

$$
\underbrace{2q}_{\large a} \ x = \underbrace{10}_{\large b} \ x
$$

In this case,

$$
a = 2q, \qquad b = 10
$$

We know that an equation of the form $ax = bx$ has infinitely many solutions whenever $a=b$. Therefore, the equation has infinitely many solutions if
$2q = 10$.

Solving this for $q$, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
2q &= 10 \\
\frac{2q}{2} &= \frac{10}{2} \\
q &= 5
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the equation $2qx = 10x$ has infinitely many solutions if $q = 5$.

We can check that this answer is correct by substituting $q = 5$ into our original equation. Doing this, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
2qx &= 10x \\
2(5)x &= 10x \\
10x &= 10x
\end{aligned}
$$

and simplifying this equation by subtracting $10x$ from both sides yields

$$
\begin{aligned}
10x &= 10x \\
10x - 10x &= 10x - 10x \\
0 &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

which is true for all values of $x$.

**Note:** The constant $q$ in this problem is sometimes called a **parameter.**

<a id="solving-for-an-unknown-parameter"></a>
## Solving For an Unknown Parameter

**Example:** Consider the equation
$(5s + 4)x = 9x$
where $s$ is a constant. Given that this equation has infinitely many solutions, what is the value of $s$?

**Explanation**

Suppose we have an equation of the form
$ax = bx$
where $a$ and $b$ are constants. If $a = b$, then the equation has infinitely many solutions.

Notice that this equation is in the form $ax = bx$. In this case,

$$
a = 5s + 4, \qquad b = 9
$$

Therefore, the equation has infinitely many solutions if

$$
5s + 4 = 9
$$

Solving this for $s$, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
5s &= 5 \\
s &= 1
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="conditions-for-a-general-linear-equation-to-have-infinitely-many-solutions"></a>
## Conditions for a General Linear Equation To Have Infinitely Many Solutions

Each of the following equations has infinitely many solutions:

$$
2x + 1 = 2x + 1, \qquad \dfrac13x -2 = \dfrac13x -2, \qquad 1-x = 1 - x
$$

More generally, suppose we have an equation of the form

$$
ax + b = cx + d
$$

where $a, b, c$, and $d$ are constants. Then, this equation has infinitely many solutions if

$$
a = c\quad \text{and}\quad b = d
$$

Note that if we substitute $c=a$ and $d=b$ into this equation, we get the following:

$$
\begin{aligned}
ax + b &= cx + d \\
ax + b &= (a)x + (b) \\
ax + b &= ax + b
\end{aligned}
$$

Simplifying this equation by subtracting $ax$ from both sides of the equation gives the following:

$$
\begin{aligned}
ax + b &= ax + b \\
ax + b - ax &= ax + b - ax \\
b &= b
\end{aligned}
$$

The final equation, $b = b$, is always true. Therefore, the equation has infinitely many solutions, and any real value of $x$ is a solution.

<a id="problems-involving-unknown-parameters-in-general-linear-equations"></a>
## Problems Involving Unknown Parameters in General Linear Equations

Consider the equation

$$
(s - 2)x + t = 6x + 9
$$

where $s$ and $t$ are constants. Given that this equation has infinitely many solutions, how can we find the values of $s$ and $t$?

Notice that this equation is in the form

$$
ax+b = cx+d{:}
$$

$$
\underbrace{(s-2)}_{\large a} \ x + \underbrace{t}_{\large b} = \underbrace{6}_{\large c} \ x + \underbrace{9}_{\large d}
$$

We know that an equation of the form

$$
ax+b = cx+d
$$

has infinitely many solutions when $a=c$ and $b=d$.

So, to determine the values of $s$ and $t$ such that the equation

$$
({\color{blue}{{s - 2}}})x + {\color{red}{{t}}} = {\color{blue}{{6}}}x + {\color{red}{{9}}}
$$

has infinitely many solutions, we **equate the coefficients** of $x$, and we **equate the constants:**

- Equating the coefficients of $x$, we have
${\color{blue}{{s - 2}}} = {\color{blue}{{6}}}$.
Solving this equation for $s$, we get
$s|= 8$.
- Equating the constants, we get
${\color{red}{{t}}} = {\color{red}{{9}}}$.

Therefore, if $s = 8$ and $t=9$, our original equation has infinitely many solutions.

<a id="solving-a-general-equation-with-infinitely-many-solutions-for-unknown-parameters"></a>
## Solving a General Equation With Infinitely Many Solutions For Unknown Parameters

**Example:** Consider the equation
$(k+4)x + 3r = 6x + 12$
where $k$ and $r$ are constants. Given that this equation has infinitely many solutions, what is the value of $k+r$?

**Explanation**

Suppose we have an equation of the form

$$
ax + b = cx + d
$$

where $a, b, c$, and $d$ are constants. This equation has infinitely many solutions if

$$
a = c\quad \text{and}\quad b = d
$$

To determine the values of $k$ and $r$ such that the equation

$$
(k+4)x + 3r = 6x + 12
$$

has infinitely many solutions, we equate the coefficients of $x$, and we equate the constants:

- Equating the coefficients of $x$, we have
$k + 4 = 6$.
Solving this equation for $k$, we get
$k|= 2$.
- Equating the constants, we have
$3r = 12$.
Solving this equation for $r$, we get
$r|= 4$.

Finally, since $k = 2$ and $r = 4$, we have

$$
k + r = 2+4 = 6
$$
