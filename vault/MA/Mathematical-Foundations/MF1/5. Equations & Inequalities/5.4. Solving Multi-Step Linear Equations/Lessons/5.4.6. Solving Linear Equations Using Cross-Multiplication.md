## Introduction

If we have rational expressions on both sides of an equation, say,

$$
\dfrac{{\color{red}x-1}}{{\color{blue}2}} = \dfrac{{\color{blue}2x-1}}{{\color{red}5}}
$$

then we can eliminate the rational expressions by applying **cross-multiplication**:

- we cross-multiply the left-hand side numerator $({\color{red}x-1})$ with the right-hand side denominator $({\color{red}5})$, and
- we cross-multiply the left-hand side denominator $({\color{blue}2})$ with the right-hand side numerator $({\color{blue}2x-1})$.

Doing this, we get the following equation:

$$
({\color{red}x-1}) \cdot ({\color{red}5}) = ({\color{blue}2}) \cdot ({\color{blue}2x - 1})
$$

After we've cross-multiplied, we proceed as usual to solve the equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x - 1) \cdot 5 &= 2 \cdot (2x - 1) \\
5x - 5 &= 4x - 2 \\
5x - 5 + 5 &= 4x - 2 + 5 \\
5x &= 4x + 3 \\
5x - 4x &= 4x + 3 - 4x \\
x &= 3
\end{aligned}
$$

**Note:** Cross-multiplication might seem magical, but it is really just a shortcut for multiplying both sides of the equation by each denominator:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{x - 1}{2} &= \frac{2x - 1}{5} \\
2 \cdot \frac{x - 1}{2} &= 2 \cdot \frac{2x - 1}{5} \\
2 \cdot \frac{x - 1}{2} &= 2 \cdot \frac{2x - 1}{5} \\
x - 1 &= 2 \cdot \frac{2x - 1}{5} \\
(x - 1) \cdot 5 &= 2 \cdot \frac{2x - 1}{5} \cdot 5 \\
(x - 1) \cdot 5 &= 2 \cdot \frac{2x - 1}{5} \cdot 5 \\
(x - 1) \cdot (5) &= (2) \cdot (2x - 1)
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="cross-multiplying-with-one-binomial"></a>
## Cross-Multiplying with One Binomial

**Example:** Solve the equation $\dfrac{2x+5}{30} = \dfrac{1}{2}$.

**Explanation**

First, we cross-multiply:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{2x + 5}{30} &= \frac{1}{2} \\
(2x + 5) \cdot 2 &= 30 \cdot 1
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we proceed as usual to solve the equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(2x + 5) \cdot 2 &= 30 \cdot 1 \\
4x + 10 &= 30 \\
4x + 10 - 10 &= 30 - 10 \\
4x &= 20 \\
\frac{4x}{4} &= \frac{20}{4} \\
x &= 5
\end{aligned}
$$

## Cross-Multiplying with Two Binomials

**Example:** Solve for $x$, where $\dfrac{6-2x}{2} = \dfrac{6-x}{3}$.

**Explanation**

First, we cross-multiply:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{6 - 2x}{2} &= \frac{6 - x}{3} \\
(6 - 2x) \cdot 3 &= 2 \cdot (6 - x)
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we proceed as usual to solve the equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(6 - 2x) \cdot 3 &= 2 \cdot (6 - x) \\
18 - 6x &= 12 - 2x \\
18 - 6x - 18 &= 12 - 2x - 18 \\
- 6x &= -2x - 6 \\
- 6x + 2x &= -2x - 6 + 2x \\
- 4x &= -6 \\
\frac{-4x}{-4} &= \frac{-6}{-4} \\
x &= \frac{6}{4} \\
x &= \frac{3}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="cross-multiplying-with-a-negative-sign"></a>
## Cross-Multiplying with a Negative Sign

Consider the following equation:

$$
\dfrac{x+1}{2} = -\dfrac{x}{3}
$$

Even though one of the fractions is negative, we can still use cross-multiplication. All we have to do is transfer the negative into the numerator of the fraction, and proceed with cross-multiplication:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{x + 1}{2} &= -\frac{x}{3} \\
\frac{x + 1}{2} &= \frac{-x}{3} \\
(x + 1) \cdot 3 &= 2 \cdot (-x)
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we proceed as usual to solve the equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x + 1) \cdot 3 &= 2 \cdot (-x) \\
3x + 3 &= -2x \\
3x + 3 - 3x &= -2x - 3x \\
3 &= -5x \\
\frac{3}{-5} &= \frac{-5x}{-5} \\
- \frac{3}{5} &= x
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
x = -\dfrac{3}{5}
$$

<a id="cross-multiplying-with-a-negative-sign"></a>
## Cross-Multiplying with a Negative Sign

**Example:** Solve for $u$, where $\dfrac{u+2}{3} =-\dfrac{3-u}{2}$.

**Explanation**

We transfer the negative into the numerator of the fraction, and proceed with cross-multiplication:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{u + 2}{3} &= -\frac{3 - u}{2} \\
\frac{u + 2}{3} &= (- (3 - u))/(2) \\
\frac{u + 2}{3} &= \frac{-3 + u}{2} \\
(u + 2) \cdot 2 &= 3 \cdot (-3 + u)
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we proceed as usual to solve the equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(u + 2) \cdot 2 &= 3 \cdot (-3 + u) \\
2u + 4 &= -9 + 3u \\
2u + 4 - 2u &= -9 + 3u - 2u \\
4 &= -9 + u \\
4 + 9 &= -9 + u + 9 \\
13 &= u
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, $u=13$.
