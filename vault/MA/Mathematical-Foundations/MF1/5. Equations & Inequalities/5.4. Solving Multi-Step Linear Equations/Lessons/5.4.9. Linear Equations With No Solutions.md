## Introduction

Some linear equations can have **no solutions.** This means there is no value of $x$ that can be substituted into the equation to give a true statement.

For example, let's consider the following equation:

$$
x+1 = x
$$

Let's attempt to solve this equation. We start by subtracting $x$ from both sides.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x + 1 &= x \\
x + 1- x &= x- x \\
(x - x) + 1 &= (x - x) \\
0 + 1 &= 0 \\
1 &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

Notice that the final equation, $1=0$, is always **false**. In other words, there is no value of $x$ that we can substitute to make the equation true.

Therefore, our equation has no solutions.

<a id="identifying-an-equation-with-no-solutions"></a>
## Identifying an Equation With No Solutions

**Example:** Which of the following equations has no solutions?

1. $4z + 3 = 4z + 9$
2. $2z - 5 = z + 1$
3. $3z - 2 = 3z - 2$

**Explanation**

Let's attempt to solve each equation in turn:

- For the first equation, we have
$4z + 3|= 4z + 9; 4z + 3 - 4z|= 4z + 9 - 4z; 3|= 9$.
This equation is false for all values of $z$. Therefore, the equation $4z + 3 = 4z + 9$ has no solutions.
- For the second equation, we have
$2z - 5|= z + 1; 2z - 5 - z + 5|= z + 1 - z + 5; z|= 6$.
Therefore, the equation $2z - 5 = z + 1$ has the unique solution $z = 6$.
- For the third equation, we have
$3z - 2|= 3z - 2; 3z - 2 - 3z|= 3z - 2 - 3z; - 2|=-2$.
This equation is true for all values of $z$.

Therefore, the correct answer is "I only."

<a id="conditions-for-an-equation-to-have-no-solutions"></a>
## Conditions for an Equation To Have No Solutions

In general, suppose we have an equation in the variable $x$ of the form

$$
ax = b
$$

where

- $a$ and $b$ are constants, and
- $b$ is *not* equal to zero, which we write as $b \neq 0$.

Then, if $a = 0$, the equation has **no solutions.**

Indeed, by substituting $a = 0$ into $ax=b$, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
ax &= b \\
(0)x &= b \\
0 &= b
\end{aligned}
$$

which is a *contradiction* (i.e., a statement that's always false) because we were told that

$$
b \neq 0
$$

Therefore, the equation has no solution.

<a id="problems-involving-unknown-parameters"></a>
## Problems Involving Unknown Parameters

Consider the equation in the variable $x$, given by

$$
7qx = -5
$$

where $q$ is a constant. Given that this equation has no solutions, what is the value of $q$?

Notice that this equation is in the form $ax = b{:}$

$$
\underbrace{7q}_{\large a} \ x = \underbrace{-5}_{\large b}
$$

In this case,

$$
a = 7q, \qquad b = -5
$$

We know that an equation of the form $ax=b$ with

$$
b \neq 0
$$

has no solutions when $a = 0$.
Therefore, the equation has no solution if

$$
7q = 0
$$

Solving this for $q$, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
7q &= 0 \\
\frac{7q}{7} &= \frac{0}{7} \\
q &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, we conclude that the equation $7qx = -5$ has no solutions if $q = 0$.

**Note:** The constant $q$ in this problem is sometimes called a **parameter.**

<a id="solving-for-an-unknown-parameter"></a>
## Solving For an Unknown Parameter

**Example:** Consider the equation in the variable $y$, given by
$4(r - 2)y = -8$
where $r$ is a constant. Given that this equation has no solution, what is the value of $r$?

**Explanation**

Suppose we have an equation of the form
$ax = b$
where $a$ and $b$ are constants, and

$$
b\neq 0
$$

If $a = 0$, then the equation has no solution.

Notice that this equation is in the form $ay = b$. In this case,

$$
a = 4(r - 2), \qquad b = -8
$$

Therefore, the equation has no solution if

$$
4(r - 2) = 0
$$

Solving this for $r$, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
4(r - 2) &= 0 \\
(4(r - 2))/(4) &= \frac{0}{4} \\
r - 2 &= 0 \\
r &= 2
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="solving-for-an-unknown-parameter-constants-on-both-sides"></a>
## Solving For an Unknown Parameter - Constants on Both Sides

**Example:** Consider the equation in the variable $z$, given by
$(r - 9)z + 2 = -1$
where $r$ is a constant. Given that this equation has no solution, what is the value of $r$?

**Explanation**

Suppose we have an equation of the form
$ax = b$
where $a$ and $b$ are constants, and

$$
b\neq 0
$$

If $a = 0$, then the equation has no solution.

First, let's write our equation in the form $az = b{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
(r - 9)z + 2 &= -1 \\
(r - 9)z + 2 - 2 &= -1 - 2 \\
(r - 9)_(⏟)_(a)z &= -3_(⏟)_(b)
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the equation has no solution if
$r - 9 = 0$.

Solving this for $r$, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
r - 9 &= 0 \\
r &= 9
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="solving-for-an-unknown-parameter-variables-and-constants-on-both-sides"></a>
## Solving For an Unknown Parameter - Variables and Constants on Both Sides

**Example:** Consider the equation in the variable $x$, given by
$2nx + 7 = -4x + 5$
where $n$ is a constant. Given that this equation has no solution, what is the value of $n$?

**Explanation**

Suppose we have an equation of the form
$ax = b$
where $a$ and $b$ are constants, and

$$
b\neq 0
$$

If $a = 0$, then the equation has no solution.

First, we rewrite the equation in the form $ax = b$.

We start by adding $4x$ to both sides of the equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
2nx + 7 &= -4x + 5 \\
2nx + 4x + 7 &= -4x + 5 + 4x \\
2nx + 4x + 7 &= 5
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we subtract $7$ from both sides:

$$
\begin{aligned}
2nx + 4x + 7 &= 5 \\
2nx + 4x + 7 - 7 &= 5 - 7 \\
2nx + 4x &= -2
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we factor $x$ on the left-hand side. This gives

$$
(2n + 4)x = -2
$$

Notice that this equation is now in the form $ax = b{:}$

$$
\underbrace{(2n + 4)}_{a}x = \underbrace{-2}_{b}
$$

Therefore, the equation has no solution if

$$
2n + 4= 0
$$

Solving this for $n$, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
2n + 4 &= 0 \\
2n + 4 - 4 &= 0 - 4 \\
2n &= -4 \\
\frac{2n}{2} &= \frac{-4}{2} \\
n &= -2
\end{aligned}
$$
