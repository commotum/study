## Introduction

Let's consider the following inequality:

$$
7x -2 < 5x - 18
$$

As usual, we solve the inequality just like we would solve an equation.

First, we apply the addition principle. We start by subtracting $5x$ from both sides:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}7x - 2 & < 5x - 18 \\ 7x - 2 - 5x & < 5x - 18 - 5x \\ 2x - 2 & < - 18\end{bmatrix}
$$

Then, we add $2$ to both sides:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}2x - 2 & < - 18 \\ 2x - 2 + 2 & < - 18 + 2 \\ 2x & < - 16\end{bmatrix}
$$

Finally, we use the multiplication principle, dividing both sides by $2{:}$

$$
\begin{bmatrix}2x & < - 16 \\ \frac{2x}{2} & < \frac{-16}{2} \\ x & < - 8\end{bmatrix}
$$

Therefore, the solution is $x < -8$.

## Solving Linear Inequalities With Variables on Both Sides

**Example:** Solve the inequality $5x+13 \ge 3x-7$ and sketch the solution on the number line.

**Explanation**

We solve the inequality just like we would solve an equation.

First, we apply the addition principle:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}5x + 13 & \ge 3x - 7 \\ 5x + 13 - 3x & \ge 3x - 7 - 3x \\ 2x + 13 & \ge - 7 \\ 2x + 13 - 13 & \ge - 7 - 13 \\ 2x & \ge - 20\end{bmatrix}
$$

Then, we use the multiplication principle:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}2x & \ge - 20 \\ \frac{2x}{2} & \ge \frac{-20}{2} \\ x & \ge - 10\end{bmatrix}
$$

Therefore, the solution is

$$
x \ge -10
$$

## Clearing Rational Expressions in Linear Inequalities

Now, let's solve an inequality that contains a rational expression:

$$
\dfrac{2y-1}{3} \lt 5
$$

First, we clear the rational expression by multiplying both sides by $3{:}$

$$
\begin{bmatrix}\frac{2y - 1}{3} & < 5 \\ 3 \cdot (\frac{2y - 1}{3}) & < 3 \cdot 5 \\ 3 \cdot (\frac{2y - 1}{3}) & < 15 \\ 2y - 1 & < 15\end{bmatrix}
$$

Then, we apply the addition and multiplication principles:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}2y - 1 & < 15 \\ 2y - 1 + 1 & < 15 + 1 \\ 2y & < 16 \\ \frac{2y}{2} & < \frac{16}{2} \\ \frac{2y}{2} & < \frac{16}{2} \\ y & < 8\end{bmatrix}
$$

Therefore, the solution is $y < 8$.

<a id="solving-inequalities-by-clearing-a-rational-expression"></a>
## Solving Inequalities by Clearing a Rational Expression

**Example:** Solve the inequality $\dfrac{2-5y}{3} \le 4$.

**Explanation**

First, we clear the rational expression by multiplying both sides by $3{:}$

$$
\begin{bmatrix}\frac{2 - 5y}{3} & \le 4 \\ 3 \cdot (\frac{2 - 5y}{3}) & \le 3 \cdot 4 \\ 3 \cdot (\frac{2 - 5y}{3}) & \le 3 \cdot 4 \\ 2 - 5y & \le 12\end{bmatrix}
$$

Then, we apply the addition and multiplication principles.

Remember, when we divide an inequality by a negative number, we need to flip the inequality sign:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}2 - 5y & \le 12 \\ 2 - 5y - 2 & \le 12 - 2 \\ -5y & \le 10 \\ \frac{-5y}{-5} & \ge\frac{10}{-5} \\ \frac{-5y}{-5} & \ge - 2 \\ y & \ge - 2\end{bmatrix}
$$

Therefore, the solution is

$$
y \ge -2
$$

## Inequalities With Rational Expressions and Variables on Both Sides

**Example:** Solve the inequality $4y -1 > \dfrac {5y - 2} {3}$.

**Explanation**

First, we clear the rational expression by multiplying both sides by $3{:}$

$$
\begin{bmatrix}4y - 1 & > \frac{5y - 2}{3} \\ 3 \cdot (4y - 1) & > 3 \cdot (\frac{5y - 2}{3}) \\ 3 \cdot (4y - 1) & > 3 \cdot (\frac{5y - 2}{3}) \\ 3 \cdot (4y - 1) & > 5y - 2 \\ 12y - 3 & > 5y - 2\end{bmatrix}
$$

Next, we use the addition principle:

$$
\begin{aligned} 12y-3 &> 5y-2 \\[5pt] 12y-3-5y &> 5y-2-5y \\[5pt] 7y-3 &> {-2} \\[5pt] 7y-3 +3 &> {-2} +3 \\[5pt] 7y &> {1} \end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we use the multiplication principle:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}7y & > 1 \\ \frac{7y}{7} & > \frac{1}{7} \\ \frac{7y}{7} & > \frac{1}{7} \\ y & > \frac{1}{7}\end{bmatrix}
$$

Therefore, the solution is

$$
y > \dfrac17
$$

## Cross-Multiplication in Linear Inequalities

Finally, let's consider an inequality that contains rational expressions on both sides:

$$
\dfrac{x+1}{4} \leq \dfrac{x}{2}
$$

First, we clear the rational expressions by cross-multiplying:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}\frac{x + 1}{4} & \le \frac{x}{2} \\ 2 \cdot (x + 1) & \le 4 \cdot x \\ 2x + 2 & \le 4x\end{bmatrix}
$$

Next, we use the addition principle:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}2x + 2 & \le 4x \\ 2x + 2 - 2x & \le 4x - 2x \\ 2 & \le 2x\end{bmatrix}
$$

Finally, we use the multiplication principle:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}2 & \le 2x \\ \frac{2}{2} & \le \frac{2x}{2} \\ \frac{2}{2} & \le \frac{2x}{2} \\ 1 & \le x \\ x & \ge 1\end{bmatrix}
$$

Therefore, the solution is

$$
x\geq 1
$$

<a id="solving-linear-inequalities-using-cross-multiplication"></a>
## Solving Linear Inequalities Using Cross-Multiplication

**Example:** Solve the inequality $\dfrac{3a-2}{5} > \dfrac{4+6a}{2}$.

**Explanation**

First, we clear the rational expressions by cross-multiplying:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}\frac{3a - 2}{5} & > \frac{4 + 6a}{2} \\ 2 \cdot (3a - 2) & > 5 \cdot (4 + 6a) \\ 6a - 4 & > 20 + 30a\end{bmatrix}
$$

Next, we use the addition principle:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}6a - 4 & > 20 + 30a \\ 6a - 4 - 6a & > 20 + 30a - 6a \\ -4 & > 20 + 24a \\ -4 - 20 & > 20 + 24a - 20 \\ -24 & > 24a\end{bmatrix}
$$

Finally, we use the multiplication principle:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}-24 & > 24a \\ \frac{-24}{24} & > \frac{24a}{24} \\ -1 & > \frac{24a}{24} \\ -1 & > a \\ a & < - 1\end{bmatrix}
$$

Therefore, the solution is $a < -1$.
