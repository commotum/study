## Introduction

In a previous lesson, we saw how to apply a percentage increase to a number to get a new, larger number. In this lesson, we’ll work backwards, finding the *original value* when we know the percentage increase and the new value.

For example, suppose that after a $35\%$ increase, a conference registration fee is $\[math]405$. What was the original fee?

Increasing a number by $35\%$ means that we must apply a new percentage of

$$
100\% + 35\% = 135\%
$$

Converting this percentage to a decimal, we have

$$
135\%=\frac{135}{100}=1.35
$$

Now, let the original fee be $x$. Then, after a $35\%$ increase, the new value is

$$
1.35x
$$

We are told that the increased value is $\[/math]405$. So, we set them equal and solve for $x{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
1.35x &= 405 \\
x &= \frac{405}{1.35} \\
x &= 300
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the original fee was $\[math]300$.

We can check that this answer is correct by applying a $35\%$ increase to $\[/math]300{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\[math]300 \cdot (100% + 35%) &= \[/math]300 \cdot 135% \\
&= \[math]300 \cdot 1.35 \\
&= \[/math]405✓
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding an Original Value Given a Percentage Increase

**Example:** After increasing a quantity $x$ by $20\%$, the result is $102$. What is the value of $x$?

**Explanation**

Increasing a number by $20\%$ means that we must apply a percentage of

$$
100\% + 20\% = 120\%
$$

Converting this percentage to a decimal, we have

$$
120\% = \dfrac{120}{100} = 1.20
$$

We're told that by increasing a number $x$ by $20\%$, we get the number $102$. So, we have the equation

$$
1.20x = 102
$$

Now, we solve for $x{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
x &= \frac{102}{1.20} \\
&= 85
\end{aligned}
$$

So, we have $x= 85$.

## Finding an Original Value Given a Percentage Increase Greater Than 100%

**Example:** After increasing a number $x$ by $1{,}900\%$, the result is $740$. What is the value of $x$?

**Explanation**

Increasing a number by $1{,}900\%$ means that we must apply a percentage of

$$
100\% + 1{,}900\% = 2{,}000\%
$$

Converting this percentage to a decimal, we have

$$
2{,}000\% = \dfrac{2{,}000}{100} = 20
$$

Let $x$ be the original number. Since the increased number equals $740$, we have the equation
$20x = 740$.

Now, we solve for $x{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
x &= \frac{740}{20} \\
&= 37
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the value of $x$ is $37$.

## Finding Original Values Given a Percentage Increase in Context

**Example:** A farmer counted crates of harvested produce. In week $2$, there were $1{,}044$ crates. The percent increase from week $1$ to week $2$ was $45\%$. How many crates were there in week $1$?

**Explanation**

Increasing a number by $45\%$ means that we must apply a new percentage of

$$
100\% + 45\% = 145\%
$$

Converting this percentage to a decimal, we have

$$
145\% = \dfrac{145}{100} = 1.45
$$

Let $x$ represent the number of crates in week $1$. So, after a $45\%$ increase, the new value is

$$
1.45x
$$

We are told the increased value is $1{,}044$. So, we set them equal and solve for $x{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
1.45x &= 1,044 \\
x &= \frac{1,044}{1.45} \\
x &= 720
\end{aligned}
$$

So, there were $720$ crates in week $1$.
