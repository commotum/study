## Introduction

Sometimes, we are given a ratio and want to find an amount. In these cases, it's helpful to use equivalent fractions.

For example, if the ratio of cows to sheep on Margaret's farm is $11:7$, and she has $22$ cows, how many sheep are there on the farm?

To solve this, we start with the ratio ${\color{blue}11}:{\color{red}7}$ and write it as a fraction:

$$
\dfrac{\color{blue}11}{\color{red}7}
$$

In this fraction, the numerator represents the number of cows and the denominator represents the number of sheep.

Since there are $22$ cows on the farm, we would like a numerator of $22$. We make this by multiplying the numerator and denominator by $2$:

$$
\dfrac{{\color{blue}11}\times 2}{{\color{red}7}\times 2} = \dfrac{{\color{blue}22}}{{\color{red}14}}
$$

The denominator is now the number of sheep on the farm if there are $22$ cows. Therefore, on Margaret's farm, there are $\color{red}14$ sheep.

## Using a Ratio to Compute a Denominator

**Example:** In Jennifer's garden, there are $60$ roses. The ratio of roses to orchids is $\dfrac{3}{5}$. How many orchids are there in the garden?

**Explanation**

The ratio of roses to orchids is

$$
\dfrac{3}{5}
$$

In this fraction, the numerator represents the number of roses and the denominator represents the number of orchids.

We can make a numerator of $60$ by multiplying the numerator and denominator by $20$:

$$
\dfrac{{\color{blue}3}\times 20}{{\color{red}5}\times 20} = \dfrac{{\color{blue}60}}{{\color{red}100}}
$$

Therefore, in Jennifer's garden, there are $100$ orchids.

## Using a Ratio to Compute a Numerator

**Example:** The ratio of sand to crushed stone in concrete is $\dfrac{3}{2}$. If Daniel has $80$ kilograms of crushed stone available to make concrete, what amount of sand should he use?

**Explanation**

We are given that the ratio of sand to crushed stone in concrete is

$$
\dfrac{3}{2}
$$

In this fraction, the numerator represents the amount of sand and the denominator represents the amount of crushed stone.

We can make a denominator of $80$ by multiplying the numerator and denominator by $40$:

$$
\dfrac{{\color{blue}3}\times 40}{{\color{red}2}\times 40} = \dfrac{{\color{blue}120}}{{\color{red}80}}
$$

Therefore, for $80$ kilograms of crushed stone, Daniel must use $120$ kilograms of sand.
