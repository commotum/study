## Introduction

If we start with the formula

$$
\textrm{percentage}\times \textrm{whole} = \textrm{part}
$$

we can use the relationship between multiplication and division to write down a formula to calculate a percentage:

$$
\textrm{percentage} = \textrm{part} \div \textrm{whole}
$$

Let's demonstrate how to apply this formula using a real-world example.

Suppose that $54$ out of $300$ boxes of grapes in a supermarket contain red grapes. What percentage of the boxes contain red grapes?

In this case, the part is the number of red grapes, which is $54$, and the whole is the total number of grapes, which is $300$.

Substituting our numbers into the above formula, we get

$$
\textrm{percentage} = 54 \div 300 = \dfrac{54}{300}
$$

To write this fraction as a percentage, we create an equivalent fraction with $100$ as the denominator:

$$
\dfrac{54}{300} = \dfrac{54 \div 3}{300 \div 3} = \dfrac{18}{100}
$$

Now we can write this fraction as a percentage:

$$
\dfrac{18}{100} = 18 \%
$$

Therefore, $18 \%$ of the boxes contain red grapes.

## Finding a Missing Percent Given Two Numbers Without Simplification

**Example:** Winston drove $200$ kilometers in two days. If he drove $110$ kilometers on the first day, what percentage of the total distance did he drive on the first day?

**Explanation**

We're given a part and a whole, and we must find a percentage. So, we can use the following formula:

$$
\textrm{percentage} = \textrm{part} \div \textrm{whole}
$$

In this case,

- the part is the number of kilometers Winston drove the first day, which is $110$, and
- the whole is the total number of kilometers he drove, which is $200$.

Substituting our numbers into the above formula, we get

$$
\textrm{percentage} = 110 \div 200 = \dfrac{110}{200}
$$

To write this as a percentage, we create an equivalent fraction with $100$ as the denominator:

$$
\dfrac{110}{200} = \dfrac{110 \div 2}{200 \div 2} = \dfrac{55}{100}
$$

Now we can write this fraction as a percentage:

$$
\dfrac{55}{100} = 55 \%
$$

Therefore, Winston drove $55 \%$ of the total distance on the first day.

## Finding a Missing Percent Given Two Numbers Using Simplification

**Example:** In his previous youth baseball season, Matt hit $75$ extra-bases, $9$ of which were home runs. What percentage of extra-base hits were home runs?

**Explanation**

We're given a part and a whole, and we must find a percentage. So, we can use the following formula:

$$
\textrm{percentage} = \textrm{part} \div \textrm{whole}
$$

In this case,

- the part is the number of home runs, which is $9$, and
- the whole is the total number of extra-base hits Matt connected, which is $75$.

Substituting our numbers into the above formula, we get

$$
\textrm{percentage} = 9 \div 75 = \dfrac{9}{75}
$$

We can simplify this fraction as follows:

$$
\dfrac{9}{75}=\dfrac{9 \div 3}{75 \div 3}=\dfrac{3}{25}
$$

To write this as a percentage, we create an equivalent fraction with $100$ as the denominator:

$$
\dfrac{3}{25} = \dfrac{3 \times 4}{25 \times 4} = \dfrac{12}{100}
$$

Now we can write this fraction as a percentage:

$$
\dfrac{12}{100} = 12 \%
$$

Therefore, $12 \%$ of extra-base hits were home runs.
