## Introduction

In many real-world situations, such as in retail or finance, it is necessary to apply multiple percentages in succession.

To do so, we perform one percentage calculation and then apply another percentage to the *result* of the previous calculation.
This means each new percentage is based on a *new amount*, not on the original number.

To demonstrate, let's find $40\%$ of $150$, and then $25\%$ of the result.

First, we find $40\%$ of $150{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
40% \cdot 150 &= \frac{40}{100} \cdot 150 \\
&= 0.4 \cdot 150 \\
&= 60
\end{aligned}
$$

So, after the first step, the result is $60$.
Then, we find $25\%$ of the result:

$$
\begin{aligned}
25% \cdot 60 &= \frac{25}{100} \cdot 60 \\
&= 0.25 \cdot 60 \\
&= 15
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, after applying both percentages in succession, the final answer is $15$.

<a id="applying-subsequent-percentages"></a>
## Applying Subsequent Percentages

**Example:** Find $160\%$ of $75$, and then $150\%$ of the result.

**Explanation**

First, we find $160\%$ of $75{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
160% \cdot 75 &= \frac{160}{100} \cdot 75 \\
&= 1.6 \cdot 75 \\
&= 120
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we find $150\%$ of the result $(120){:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
150% \cdot 120 &= \frac{150}{100} \cdot 120 \\
&= 1.5 \cdot 120 \\
&= 180
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the final answer is $180$.

<a id="applying-subsequent-percentages-in-real-world-situations"></a>
## Applying Subsequent Percentages in Real-World Situations

**Example:** A service adjusted a subscription fee to $90\%$ of its original cost. A surcharge of $18\%$ was applied to the adjusted fee. If the subscription originally cost $\[math]180$, what is the surcharge?

**Explanation**

First, the adjusted fee is $90\%$ of the original cost $(\[math]180){:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
90% \cdot 180 &= \frac{90}{100} \cdot 180 \\
&= 0.9 \cdot 180 \\
&= 162
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the adjusted fee is $\[/math]162$. Then, the surcharge is $18\%$ of this fee:

$$
\begin{aligned}
18% \cdot 162 &= \frac{18}{100} \cdot 162 \\
&= 0.18 \cdot 162 \\
&= 29.16
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the surcharge is $\[math]29.16$.

<a id="subsequent-percentage-applications-as-a-single-percentage"></a>
## Subsequent Percentage Applications as a Single Percentage

When percentages are applied in succession, it is useful to represent their combined effect as a single percentage of the original amount.

This means that, instead of applying one percentage and then another, we can multiply their decimal multipliers to find an overall multiplier (or percentage) for the total change.

For example, suppose that a city’s library membership rose to $150\%$ of the previous year’s total. Of these members, $40\%$ borrowed digital books. What percentage of last year’s members borrowed digital books?

First, let $x$ be the number of members in the previous year.

We're told that membership rose to $150\%$ of the previous year’s total. Thus, the new number of members is

$$
1.5x
$$

We are also told that of those members, $40\%$ borrowed digital books. So, the number of members who borrowed digital books is

$$
0.4(1.5x) = 0.6x
$$

The expression $0.6x$ represents $60\%$ of $x$. Therefore, $60\%$ of last year’s members borrowed digital books.

<a id="expressing-subsequent-percentages-as-a-single-percentage"></a>
## Expressing Subsequent Percentages as a Single Percentage

**Example:** According to a recent report, $40\%$ of viewers said they enjoyed documentaries. Of those, $50\%$ preferred documentaries about nature. What percentage of all viewers preferred nature documentaries?

**Explanation**

Let $x$ be the total number of viewers.

We're told that $40\%$ of viewers enjoyed documentaries. Thus, the number of viewers who enjoy documentaries is
$0.4x$.

We are also told that of those viewers who enjoy documentaries, $50\%$ preferred nature topics. So, the number of viewers who preferred nature documentaries is

$$
0.50(0.4x) = 0.20x
$$

The expression $0.20x$ represents $20\%$ of $x$. Therefore, $20\%$ of all viewers preferred nature documentaries.
