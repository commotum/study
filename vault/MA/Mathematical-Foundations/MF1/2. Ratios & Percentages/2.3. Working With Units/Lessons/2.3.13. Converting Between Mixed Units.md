## Introduction

We can use unit conversions to convert from one set of units to another.

For instance, the speed of sound is approximately $0.34$ kilometers per second. What is this in meters per second? We can find out using the following two steps.

**Step 1: Create a conversion formula.**

We are given the units

$$
\dfrac{\textrm{km}}{\textrm{s}}
$$

and we want to convert this into

$$
\dfrac{\textrm{m}}{\textrm{s}}
$$

To figure out what we need to multiply by, we can set up an equation to help us:

$$
\dfrac{\textrm{km}}{s}\cdot\, \dfrac{?}{?} = \dfrac{\textrm{m}}{\textrm{s}}
$$

We want to replace $\textrm{km}$ with $\textrm{m}$. To get rid of $\textrm{km}$, we can put $\textrm{km}$ in the denominator so that it cancels out:

$$
\require{cancel} \dfrac{\cancel{\textrm{km}}}{s}\cdot \dfrac{?}{\cancel{\textrm{km}}} = \dfrac{\textrm{m}}{\textrm{s}}
$$

Then, to introduce $\textrm{m}$, we can put $\textrm{m}$ in the numerator:

$$
\require{cancel} \dfrac{\cancel{\textrm{km}}}{s}\cdot \dfrac{\textrm{m}}{\cancel{\textrm{km}}} = \dfrac{\textrm{m}}{\textrm{s}}
$$

So, our conversion formula is

$$
\dfrac{\textrm{km}}{s}\cdot \dfrac{\textrm{m}}{\textrm{km}} = \dfrac{\textrm{m}}{\textrm{s}}
$$

**Step 2: Use the conversion formula.**

Now that we have our conversion formula, we need to determine the correct numbers to use, and apply it.

- The speed that we wish to convert is $0.34 \, \dfrac{\textrm{km}}{\textrm{s}}$.
- There are $1\,000$ meters per kilometer.

Applying our conversion formula, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
0.34\frac{km}{s} &= 0.34\frac{km}{s} \cdot 1000\frac{m}{km} \\
&= (0.34 \cdot 1000)\frac{km}{s} \cdot \frac{m}{km} \\
&= 340\frac{m}{s}
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus, the speed of sound is $340$ meters per second.

## Performing a Unit Conversion: Converting a Numerator

**Example:** A guitar string, made of carbon steel, has a linear mass density of $3\,950$ milligrams per meter. What is the linear mass density in grams per meter?

**Explanation**

We are given the units

$$
\dfrac{\textrm{mg}}{\textrm{m}}
$$

and we want to convert this into

$$
\dfrac{\textrm{g}}{\textrm{m}}
$$

To figure out what we need to multiply by, we can set up an equation to help us:

$$
\dfrac{\textrm{mg}}{\textrm{m}}\cdot\, \dfrac{?}{?} = \dfrac{\textrm{g}}{\textrm{m}}
$$

We want to replace $\textrm{mg}$ with $\textrm{g}$. To get rid of $\textrm{mg}$, we can put $\textrm{mg}$ in the denominator so that it cancels out. To introduce $\textrm{g}$, we can put $\textrm{g}$ in the numerator.

$$
\require{cancel} \dfrac{\cancel{\textrm{mg}}}{\textrm{m}}\cdot \dfrac{\textrm{g}}{\cancel{\textrm{mg}}} = \dfrac{\textrm{g}}{\textrm{m}}
$$

So, our conversion formula is

$$
\require{cancel} \dfrac{\textrm{mg}}{\textrm{m}}\cdot \dfrac{\textrm{g}}{\textrm{mg}} = \dfrac{\textrm{g}}{\textrm{m}}
$$

Now that we have our conversion formula, we need to determine the correct numbers to use, and apply it.

- The density given in the problem statement is $3\,950 \, \dfrac{\textrm{mg}}{\textrm{m}}$.
- There are $1\,000$ milligrams per gram. Taking the reciprocal, there are $\dfrac{1}{1\,000}$ grams per milligram.

Applying our conversion formula, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
3950\frac{mg}{m} &= 3950\frac{mg}{m} \cdot \frac{1}{1000}\frac{g}{mg} \\
&= (3950 \cdot \frac{1}{1000})\frac{mg}{m} \cdot \frac{g}{mg} \\
&= 3.95\frac{g}{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus, the linear mass density of the string is $3.95\,\textrm{g}/\textrm{m}$.

## Performing a Unit Conversion: Converting a Denominator

**Example:** Sand runs through a sand timer at a rate of $120$ grams per minute. What is this rate in grams per second?

**Explanation**

We are given the units

$$
\dfrac{\textrm{g}}{\textrm{min}}
$$

and we want to convert this into

$$
\dfrac{\textrm{g}}{\textrm{s}}
$$

To figure out what we need to multiply by, we can set up an equation to help us:

$$
\dfrac{\textrm{g}}{\textrm{min}}\cdot\, \dfrac{?}{?} = \dfrac{\textrm{g}}{\textrm{s}}
$$

We want to replace $\textrm{min}$ with $\textrm{s}$. To get rid of $\textrm{min}$, we can put $\textrm{min}$ in the numerator so that it cancels out. To introduce $\textrm{s}$, we can put $\textrm{s}$ in the denominator.

$$
\require{cancel} \dfrac{\textrm{g}}{\cancel{\textrm{min}}}\cdot \dfrac{\cancel{\textrm{min}}}{\textrm{s}} = \dfrac{\textrm{g}}{\textrm{s}}
$$

Therefore, our conversion formula is

$$
\dfrac{\textrm{g}}{\textrm{min}}\cdot \dfrac{\textrm{min}}{\textrm{s}} = \dfrac{\textrm{g}}{\textrm{s}}
$$

Now that we have our conversion formula, we need to determine the correct numbers to use, and apply it.

- The rate given in the problem statement is $120 \, \dfrac{\textrm{g}}{\textrm{min}}$.
- There are $60$ seconds per minute. Taking the reciprocal, there are $\dfrac{1}{60}$ minutes per second.

Applying our conversion formula, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
120\frac{g}{min} &= 120\frac{g}{min} \cdot \frac{1}{60}\frac{min}{s} \\
&= (120 \cdot \frac{1}{60})\frac{g}{min} \cdot \frac{min}{s} \\
&= 2\frac{g}{s}
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus, the sand runs through the sand timer at a rate of $2 \,\textrm{g}/\textrm{s}$.

## Performing a Unit Conversion: Converting a Numerator and a Denominator

**Example:** A peregrine falcon can reach a speed of $360$ kilometers per hour during flight while diving. What is this speed in meters per second?

**Explanation**

We are given the units

$$
\dfrac{\textrm{km}}{\textrm{hour}}
$$

and we want to convert this into

$$
\dfrac{\textrm{m}}{\textrm{s}}
$$

We're converting two units instead of just one, so we're going to have to multiply by two conversion factors:

$$
\dfrac{\textrm{km}}{\textrm{hour}}\cdot \,\dfrac{?}{?} \cdot \,\dfrac{?}{?} = \dfrac{\textrm{m}}{\textrm{s}}
$$

One of the conversion factors should change $\textrm{km}$ to $\textrm{m}$.

$$
\dfrac{\cancel{\color{blue}\textrm{km}}}{\textrm{hour}}\cdot \dfrac{\textrm{m}}{\cancel{\color{blue}\textrm{km}}} \cdot \,\dfrac{?}{?} = \dfrac{\textrm{m}}{\textrm{s}}
$$

The other conversion factor should change $\textrm{hour}$ into $\textrm{s}$.

$$
\dfrac{\cancel{\color{blue}\textrm{km}}}{\cancel{\color{red}\textrm{hour}}}\cdot \dfrac{\textrm{m}}{\cancel{\color{blue}\textrm{km}}} \cdot \dfrac{\cancel{\color{red}\textrm{hour}}}{\textrm{s}} = \dfrac{\textrm{m}}{\textrm{s}}
$$

So, our conversion formula is

$$
\dfrac{\color{black}\textrm{km}}{\color{black}\textrm{hour}}\cdot \dfrac{\textrm{m}}{\color{black}\textrm{km}} \cdot \dfrac{\color{black}\textrm{hour}}{\textrm{s}} = \dfrac{\textrm{m}}{\textrm{s}}
$$

Now that we have our conversion formula, we need to determine the correct numbers to use, and apply it.

- The rate given in the problem statement is $360 \, \dfrac{\textrm{km}}{\textrm{hour}}$.
- There are $1\,000$ meters per kilometer.
- The number of seconds in one hour is given by
$1hour|= 60minutes; = 60 \cdot 1minute; = 60 \cdot 60s; = 3600s$.
Taking the reciprocal, we find that there are $\dfrac{1}{3\,600}$ hours per second.

Applying our conversion formula, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
360\frac{km}{hour} &= 360\frac{km}{hour} \cdot 1000\frac{m}{km} \cdot \frac{1}{3600}\frac{hour}{s} \\
&= (360 \cdot 1000 \cdot \frac{1}{3600})\frac{km}{hour} \cdot \frac{m}{km} \cdot \frac{hour}{s} \\
&= 100\frac{m}{s}
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus, the speed of the falcon is $100$ meters per second.
