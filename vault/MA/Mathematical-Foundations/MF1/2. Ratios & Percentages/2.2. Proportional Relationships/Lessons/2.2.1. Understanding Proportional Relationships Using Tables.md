## Introduction

Two variables, $x$ and $y$, are said to have a **proportional relationship** if their ratio is constant. That is, for any $(x,y)$ pair, we have

$$
k = \dfrac{y}{x}
$$

where $k$ is a fixed number called the **constant of proportionality** or **constant of variation.**

As a concrete example, let's consider the table below.

| $x$ | $y$ |
| --- | ---: |
| $1$ | $2$ |
| $2$ | $4$ |
| $4$ | $8$ |

Do $x$ and $y$ have a proportional relationship?

To determine whether $x$ and $y$ have a proportional relationship, we first add a new

$$
\dfrac yx
$$

column:

| $x$ | $y$ | $\dfrac{y}{x}$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| $1$ | $2$ |  |
| $2$ | $4$ |  |
| $4$ | $8$ |  |

Next, we compute

$$
\dfrac yx
$$

for each row:

| $x$ | $y$ | $\dfrac{y}{x}$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| $1$ | $2$ | $2\div 1 = {\color{blue}{2}}$ |
| $2$ | $4$ | $4\div 2 = {\color{blue}{2}}$ |
| $4$ | $8$ | $8\div 4 = {\color{blue}{2}}$ |

Since all the entries in the third column have the same value, this table does indeed represent a proportional relationship. In this case, the constant of proportionality

$$
k = {\color{blue}{2}}
$$

---

<a id="computing-a-constant-of-proportionality"></a>
## Computing a Constant of Proportionality

**Example:** The table above shows a proportional relationship. What is the constant of proportionality?

| $x$ | $y$ |
| --- | ---: |
| $6$ | $4$ |
| $9$ | $6$ |
| $15$ | $10$ |

**Explanation**

We're told that $x$ and $y$ have a proportional relationship.

To find the constant of proportionality, we first need to choose a point $(x,y)$ from the table. Let's choose the point $(6,4)$, which corresponds to the first row of the table.

Now, we can find the constant of proportionality by computing the ratio of the $y$-coordinate to the $x$-coordinate, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
k &= \frac{y}{x} \\
&= \frac{4}{6} \\
&= \frac{4 \div 2}{6 \div 2} \\
&= \frac{2}{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the constant of proportionality is

$$
k=\dfrac{2}{3}
$$

## Determining Whether a Table Represents a Proportional Relationship

**Example:** Martha is running low on cupcakes, so she decides to make some more. The amount of time $h$ (in hours) she spends baking and the number of cupcakes $c$ she makes are shown in the table below.

| Hours $(h)$ | Cupcakes $(c)$ |
| --- | ---: |
| $2$ | $5$ |
| $3$ | $7$ |
| $4$ | $9$ |

Which of the following statements are true?

1. There is a proportional relationship between $c$ and $h$.
2. There is **not** a proportional relationship between $c$ and $h$.
3. The constant of proportionality is $\dfrac{9}{2}$.

**Explanation**

First, let's add a new column to our table and compute the ratio of cupcakes per hour for each row.

| Hours $(h)$ | Cupcakes $(c)$ | Cupcakes / Hours |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| $2$ | $5$ | $\dfrac 5 2$ |
| $3$ | $7$ | $\dfrac 7 3$ |
| $4$ | $9$ | $\dfrac 9 4$ |

The ratio of cupcakes per hour is not the same for each row. Therefore, there is *not* a proportional relationship between $c$ and $h$. Consequently, there is no constant of proportionality.

Therefore, the correct answer is "II only."

<a id="finding-the-equation-of-a-proportional-relationship"></a>
## Finding the Equation of a Proportional Relationship

When the variables $x$ and $y$ form a proportional relationship, the ratio of any $(x,y)$ pair is constant:

$$
\dfrac y x = k
$$

Using the relationship between multiplication and division, we can rewrite this equation above as follows:

$$
y = kx
$$

---

<a id="finding-a-proportional-relationship-equation"></a>
## Finding a Proportional Relationship Equation

**Example:** Merlin is making sandwiches for a party. There is a proportional relationship between the time $x$ (in minutes) he spends preparing sandwiches and the number of sandwiches $y$ he makes.

| Time $(x)$ | Sandwiches $(y)$ |
| --- | ---: |
| $5$ | $4$ |
| $10$ | $8$ |
| $15$ | $12$ |

What is the equation for the relationship between $x$ and $y$?

**Explanation**

We're told that $x$ and $y$ have a proportional relationship.

Every proportional relationship can be written in the form $y=kx$, where $k$ is the constant of proportionality.

To compute the constant of proportionality, we first need to choose a point $(x,y)$ from the table. Let's choose the point $(5,4)$, which corresponds to the first row of the table.

Now, we can find the constant of proportionality as follows:

$$
k = \dfrac{y}{x} = \dfrac{4}{5}
$$

Therefore, the equation relating $x$ and $y$ is

$$
y = \dfrac{4}{5} x
$$

<a id="interpreting-data-from-proportional-relationships"></a>
## Interpreting Data From Proportional Relationships

**Example:** Jill likes to listen to music while she runs. The following table shows a proportional relationship between the distance $d$ (in kilometers) she runs and the number of songs $s$ she listens to.

| Kilometers ($d$) | Songs ($s$) |
| --- | ---: |
| $2$ | $3$ |
| $4$ | $6$ |
| $6$ | $9$ |

Which of the following statements are true?

1. The unit rate is $\dfrac32$ songs per kilometer.
2. If Jill runs for $1$ kilometer, she'll listen to $1.5$ songs.
3. If Jill runs for $12$ kilometers, she'll listen to $15$ songs.

**Explanation**

We're told that $d$ and $s$ have a proportional relationship.

To find the constant of proportionality, we first need to choose a point $(d,s)$ from the table. Let's choose the point $(2,3)$, which corresponds to the first row of the table.

We can find the constant of proportionality as follows:

$$
k = \dfrac{s}{d} = \dfrac{3}{2}
$$

The units of $k$ are "songs per kilometer" since we divided the number of songs $s$ by the distance $d$. Therefore, the relationship between $d$ and $s$ is

$$
s=\dfrac32 d
$$

With that in mind, let's look at each statement.

- Statement I is true. We found above that $k =\dfrac32$ songs per kilometer.
- Statement II is true. We know that $k=\dfrac32$, which means that if Jill runs for $1$ kilometer, she'll listen to $\dfrac32 = 1.5$ songs.
- Statement III is false. If we substitute $d=12$ into the relationship $s=\dfrac32 d$, we get
$s=\dfrac32\cdot 12=18$
which means that she'll listen to $18$ songs if she runs for $12$ kilometers.

Therefore, the correct answer is "I and II only."
