## Introduction

Recall that two variables, $x$ and $y$, are said to have a proportional relationship if their ratio is constant. The constant of proportionality $k$ is given by

$$
k = \dfrac{y}{x}
$$

We can rearrange the equation above as follows:

$$
y = kx
$$

The following statements are all equivalent:

- $y = kx$
- $y$ **varies directly** with $x$
- $y$ is **directly proportional** to $x$
- $y \propto x$

Proportional relationships frequently occur in real-world problems. Let's see an example.

---

<a id="computing-a-constant-of-proportionality"></a>
## Computing a Constant of Proportionality

**Example:** Adam started writing a book. There is a proportional relationship between the number of pages $x$ he writes and the book's thickness $y$ (in millimeters). Adam measured the thickness of his book to be $3$ millimeters after he'd written $18$ pages.

What is the constant of proportionality for the relationship between $x$ and $y$?

**Explanation**

We are told that after Adam wrote $x=18$ pages, the book's thickness was $y=3$ millimeters.

We can find the constant of proportionality by computing the ratio of the $y$-value to the $x$-value, as follows:

$$
k = \dfrac{y}{x} = \dfrac{3}{18} = \dfrac{3 \div 3}{18 \div 3} = \dfrac{1}{6}
$$

Therefore, the constant of proportionality is

$$
\dfrac{1}{6}
$$

and its units are "millimeters per page."

<a id="identifying-the-equation-of-a-proportional-relationship"></a>
## Identifying the Equation of a Proportional Relationship

**Example:** Beatrice needs to clean the garage. So, to motivate herself, she allows herself $10$ minutes of video game time for every $25$ minutes she spends cleaning.

There is a proportional relationship between the time $x$ (in minutes) Beatrice spends cleaning the garage and the time $y$ (in minutes) she plays video games. What equation represents the relationship between $x$ and $y$?

**Explanation**

Every proportional relationship takes the form $y=kx$, where $k$ is the constant of proportionality.

We are told that for every $x=25$ minutes Beatrice spends cleaning, she will play $y=10$ minutes of video games.

We can find the constant of proportionality by computing the ratio of the $y$-value to the $x$-value, as follows:

$$
k = \dfrac{y}{x} = \dfrac{10}{25} = \dfrac{10 \div 5}{25 \div 5} = \dfrac{2}{5}
$$

Therefore, the equation is

$$
y = \dfrac{2}{5} x
$$

## Solving for an Unknown Amount

**Example:** Amy is baking a loaf of bread. If the loaf weighs $12$ ounces, then Amy needs to bake the loaf for $40$ minutes. There is a proportional relationship between the loaf's weight (in ounces) and the time (in minutes) the loaf needs to bake.

If Amy leaves the house for $60$ minutes and wants the loaf to be fully baked the moment she returns, how much should the loaf of bread weigh?

**Explanation**

Let $x$ be the loaf's weight (in ounces), and $y$ be the time (in minutes) the loaf needs to bake.

First, we need to construct the equation that represents the proportional relationship. Every proportional relationship takes the form $y=kx$, where $k$ is the constant of proportionality.

We are told that if the loaf weighs $x=12$ ounces, then Amy needs to bake it for $y=40$ minutes. We can find the constant of proportionality by computing the ratio of the $y$-value to the $x$-value, as follows:

$$
k = \dfrac{y}{x} = \dfrac{40}{12} = \dfrac{40 \div 4}{12 \div 4} = \dfrac{10}{3}
$$

Therefore, the equation is

$$
y = \dfrac{10}{3} x
$$

Now, we want to find the value of $x$ so that the loaf will bake for exactly $y=60$ minutes. So, we substitute $y=60$ into the equation above:

$$
\begin{aligned}
60 &= \frac{10}{3}x \\
\frac{10}{3}x &= 60
\end{aligned}
$$

To solve for $x$, we multiply both sides of the equation by the reciprocal of

$$
\dfrac{10}{3}
$$

which is

$$
\dfrac{3}{10}
$$

We get

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{3}{10} \cdot \frac{10}{3}x &= \frac{3}{10} \cdot 60 \\
1x &= \frac{3 \cdot 60}{10} \\
x &= \frac{180}{10} \\
x &= 18
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the loaf of bread should weigh $18$ ounces.
