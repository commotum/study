## Introduction

Let's take a number, say, $-3$. We can depict it on a number line as follows:

![](<../Source/9.1.1. Absolute Value Expressions/Images/639fbaffa133fb983eef5d2453eeedba.png>)

The *distance* between the number $-3$ and $0$ is known as the **absolute value** of $-3$. We write it as

$$
\mid -3 \mid = {\color{red}3}
$$

Similarly, $\mid \,2\, \mid$, the absolute value of the number $2$, is the distance between $2$ and $0$:

![](<../Source/9.1.1. Absolute Value Expressions/Images/197c5c28024cc300e7086bfff10e313a.png>)

We write

$$
\mid \,2\, \mid = {\color{red}2}
$$

So, the absolute value simply

- *removes the minus sign*, if the number is negative, or
- does nothing, if the number is non-negative.

Sometimes, we can be asked to evaluate an expression involving the absolute value function, like $\mid x +1 \mid + 5$ when $x=-3$. In this case, we evaluate the expression inside the absolute value before simplifying the rest of the expression:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mid x + 1 \mid + 5 &=  \\
\mid (-3) + 1 \mid + 5 &=  \\
\mid - 2 \mid + 5 &=  \\
2 + 5 &=  \\
&= 7
\end{aligned}
$$

## Evaluating an Expression With One Absolute Value

**Example:** Evaluate $\mid x - 6 \mid + 2 x +3$ for $x = -3$.

**Explanation**

We substitute $x = -3$ and then evaluate the absolute value before we simplify the rest of the expression:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mid x - 6 \mid + 2x + 3 &=  \\
\mid - 3 - 6 \mid + 2(-3) + 3 &=  \\
\mid - 9 \mid + 2(-3) + 3 &=  \\
9 + 2(-3) + 3 &=  \\
9 - 6 + 3 &=  \\
&= 6
\end{aligned}
$$

## Evaluating an Expression With Two Absolute Values

**Example:** Evaluate $\begin{vmatrix}2 - x & + 1 + 5 & x\end{vmatrix}$ for $x = 5$.

**Explanation**

We substitute $x = 5$ and then evaluate the absolute values before we simplify the rest of the expression:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\begin{vmatrix}2 - x & + 1 + 5 & x\end{vmatrix} &=  \\
\begin{vmatrix}2 - 5 & + 1 + 5 & 5\end{vmatrix} &=  \\
\mid - 3 \mid + 1 + 5(5) &=  \\
3 + 1 + 25 &=  \\
4 + 25 &=  \\
&= 29
\end{aligned}
$$
