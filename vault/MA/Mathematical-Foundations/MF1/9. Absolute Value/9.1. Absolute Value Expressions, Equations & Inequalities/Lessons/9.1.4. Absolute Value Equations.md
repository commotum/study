<a id="introduction"></a>
## Introduction

In this lesson, we'll learn how to solve equations that contain absolute value expressions.

For example, let's consider the following equation:

$$
\mid x+1 \mid = 4
$$

For this equation to be true, either the *positive or negative* of the expression inside the absolute value must equal the right-hand side. That is,

$$
x+1 = 4 \qquad\qquad \text{or} \qquad\qquad -(x+1)=4
$$

So, let's solve these two equations:

- First, we solve the equation $x+1 = 4{:}$
$x + 1|= 4; x + 1 - 1|= 4 - 1; x|= 3$

- Then, we solve the equation $-(x+1) = 4{:}$
$- (x + 1)|= 4; - x - 1|= 4; - x - 1 + 1|= 4 + 1; - x|= 5; x|=-5$

Therefore, the solutions to our original absolute value equation are

$$
x={\color{blue}3}
$$

and

$$
x={\color{red}{-5}}
$$

---

<a id="checking-the-solution"></a>
## Checking the Solution

So, we've seen that the solutions to the equation

$$
\mid x+1 \mid = 4
$$

are

$$
x={\color{blue}3}
$$

and

$$
x={\color{red}{-5}}
$$

We can verify that these solutions are correct by substituting them back into the original equation and checking we get a true statement:

- Substituting $x={\color{blue}3}$ into our equation, we get
$\mid x + 1 \mid |= 4; \mid 3 + 1 \mid |= 4; \mid 4 \mid |= 4; 4|= 4.✓$
Therefore, $x={\color{blue}3}$ is indeed a solution to the absolute value equation.
- Substituting $x={\color{red}-5}$ into our equation, we get
$\mid x + 1 \mid |= 4; \mid - 5 + 1 \mid |= 4; \mid - 4 \mid |= 4; 4|= 4.✓$
Therefore, $x={\color{red}-5}$ is indeed a solution to the absolute value equation.

Therefore,

$$
x={\color{blue}3}
$$

and

$$
x={\color{red}{-5}}
$$

are both solutions to our absolute value equation.

<a id="solving-one-step-absolute-value-equations"></a>
## Solving One-Step Absolute Value Equations

**Example:** Solve the equation $\left\vert 7x \right\vert = 21$.

**Explanation**

Either the positive or negative of the expression inside the absolute value must be equal to the right-hand side of the equation.

So, we set up the two corresponding equations and solve them independently:

- If $\mid 7x \mid = 7x$, we obtain:
$\mid 7x \mid |= 21; 7x|= 21; \frac{7x}{7}|= \frac{21}{7}; x|= 3$
- If $\mid 7x \mid = -7x$, we obtain:
$\mid 7x \mid |= 21; - 7x|= 21; \frac{-7x}{-7}|= \frac{21}{-7}; x|=-3$

Therefore, the solutions are $x=3$ and $x=-3$. We can write this more compactly as $x = \pm 3$.

## Solving Two-Step Absolute Value Equations

**Example:** Solve the equation $\mid 2x+9 \mid = 15$.

**Explanation**

Either the positive or negative of the expression inside the absolute value must be equal to the right-hand side of the equation.

So, we set up the two corresponding equations and solve them independently:

- If $\mid 2x+9 \mid = 2x+9$, we obtain:
$\mid 2x + 9 \mid |= 15; 2x + 9|= 15; 2x|= 6; \frac{2x}{2}|= \frac{6}{2}; x|= 3$
- If $\mid 2x+9 \mid = -(2x+9)$, we obtain:
$\mid 2x + 9 \mid |= 15; - (2x + 9)|= 15; - 2x - 9|= 15; - 2x|= 24; \frac{-2x}{-2}|= \frac{24}{-2}; x|=-12$

Therefore, the solutions are $x=-12$ and $x=3$.

## Solving Absolute Value Equations With Negative Variable Coefficients

**Example:** Solve the equation $\mid 5-8y \mid = 11$.

**Explanation**

Either the positive or negative of the expression inside the absolute value must be equal to the right-hand side of the equation.

So, we set up the two corresponding equations and solve them independently:

- If $\mid 5-8y \mid = 5-8y$, we obtain:
$\mid 5 - 8y \mid |= 11; 5 - 8y|= 11; - 8y|= 6; \frac{-8y}{-8}|= \frac{6}{-8}; y|=-\frac{3}{4}$
- If $\mid 5-8y \mid = -(5-8y)$, we obtain:
$\mid 5 - 8y \mid |= 11; - (5 - 8y)|= 11; - 5 + 8y|= 11; 8y|= 16; \frac{8y}{8}|= \frac{16}{8}; y|= 2$

Therefore, the solutions are

$$
y=-\dfrac34
$$

and $y=2$.

<a id="solving-two-step-absolute-value-equations-containing-fractions"></a>
## Solving Two-Step Absolute Value Equations Containing Fractions

**Example:** Solve the equation $\left| \dfrac{7}{2}z-2 \right| = 3$.

**Explanation**

Either the positive or negative of the expression inside the absolute value must be equal to the right-hand side of the equation.

So, we set up the two corresponding equations and solve them independently:

- If $\left| \dfrac{7}{2}z-2 \right| = \dfrac{7}{2}z-2$, we obtain:
$\mid \frac{7}{2}z - 2 \mid |= 3; \frac{7}{2}z - 2|= 3; \frac{7}{2}z|= 5$
Multiplying both sides of the equation by $\dfrac{2}{7}$ gives
$\frac{7}{2}z|= 5; (\frac{2}{7}) \cdot (\frac{7}{2}z)|= (\frac{2}{7}) \cdot (5); (\frac{2}{7}) \cdot (\frac{7}{2}z)|= \frac{10}{7}; z|= \frac{10}{7}$.
- If $\left| \dfrac{7}{2}z-2 \right| = -\left(\dfrac{7}{2}z-2\right)$, we obtain:
$\mid \frac{7}{2}z - 2 \mid |= 3; - (\frac{7}{2}z - 2)|= 3; - \frac{7}{2}z + 2|= 3; - \frac{7}{2}z|= 1$
Multiplying both sides of the equation by $-\dfrac{2}{7}$ gives
$-\frac{7}{2}z|= 1; (-\frac{2}{7}) \cdot (-\frac{7}{2}z)|= (-\frac{2}{7}) \cdot (1); (-\frac{2}{7}) \cdot (-\frac{7}{2}z)|=-\frac{2}{7}; z|=-\frac{2}{7}$.

Therefore, the solutions are

$$
z = -\dfrac{2}{7}
$$

and

$$
z = \dfrac{10}{7}
$$
