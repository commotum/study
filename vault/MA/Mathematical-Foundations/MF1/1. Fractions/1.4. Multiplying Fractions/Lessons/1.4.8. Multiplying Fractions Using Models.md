## Introduction

We can use fraction models to multiply any two fractions.

Let's figure out how to use models to compute the following product:

$$
\dfrac{3}{5} \times \dfrac{2}{3}
$$

We start with the model of

$$
\dfrac{3}{5}
$$

![](<../Source/1.4.8. Multiplying Fractions Using Models/Images/8658f23cb6ae437311982a1bae016940.png>)

Since we need to multiply by

$$
\dfrac{\color{red}2}{\color{blue}3}
$$

, we split the whole into $\color{blue}3$ equal parts *horizontally*. But this time, we keep the shaded pieces in the bottom $\color{red}2$ rows.

![](<../Source/1.4.8. Multiplying Fractions Using Models/Images/d4c8d9300f65e6c7dffdb971fcf99cc3.png>)

From the picture on the right, we see that the shaded part represents

$$
\dfrac{6}{15}
$$

of the whole.

Therefore,

$$
\dfrac{3}{5} \times \dfrac{2}{3} = \dfrac{6}{15}
$$

## Identifying a Multiplication Problem Corresponding to a Model

**Example:** What multiplication problem is represented by the model above?

![](<../Source/1.4.8. Multiplying Fractions Using Models/Images/38a54298d946af17dadbe7e1a2a92e6e.png>)

**Explanation**

We have

$$
\dfrac{2}{5}
$$

on the left and

$$
\dfrac{6}{20}
$$

on the right.

![](<../Source/1.4.8. Multiplying Fractions Using Models/Images/6a921de3214b6962fe300b018fe4a4b6.png>)

Therefore, the multiplication problem shown is:

$$
\dfrac{2}{5} \times \dfrac{3}{4} = \dfrac{6}{20}
$$

<a id="identifying-a-missing-number"></a>
## Identifying a Missing Number

**Example:** What number is missing from the multiplication problem above?

![](<../Source/1.4.8. Multiplying Fractions Using Models/Images/19cd0e18fac2630a47c7bae13f89c3ea.png>)

**Explanation**

We have

$$
\dfrac{3}{4}
$$

on the left and

$$
\dfrac{9}{20}
$$

on the right.

![](<../Source/1.4.8. Multiplying Fractions Using Models/Images/fc891d4459735f0108b2aea6959cb35b.png>)

The shape on the right-hand side is split into $5$ equal parts by horizontal lines.
So, the shaded part on the right is

$$
\dfrac{\color{blue}3}{5}
$$

of the shaded part on the left.

Therefore, the multiplication problem shown is

$$
\dfrac{3}{4} \times \dfrac{\color{blue}3}{5} = \dfrac{9}{20}
$$

So, the missing number is $\color{blue}3$.

## Multiplying Two Fractions Using Fraction Models

**Example:** Use the model above to calculate $\dfrac{2}{6} \times \dfrac{3}{4}$.

![](<../Source/1.4.8. Multiplying Fractions Using Models/Images/634a6220b38cb608a85be4d185a130de.png>)

**Explanation**

Since we need to multiply by

$$
\dfrac{\color{red}3}{\color{blue}4}
$$

, we split the whole into $\color{blue}4$ equal parts horizontally and ensure that $\color{red}3$ of the parts remain shaded.

![](<../Source/1.4.8. Multiplying Fractions Using Models/Images/aebb8cb487034e8355d0919c7ea985f5.png>)

From the picture on the right, we see that the shaded part represents

$$
\dfrac{6}{24}
$$

of the whole.

Therefore,

$$
\dfrac{2}{6} \times \dfrac{3}{4} = \dfrac{6}{24}
$$
