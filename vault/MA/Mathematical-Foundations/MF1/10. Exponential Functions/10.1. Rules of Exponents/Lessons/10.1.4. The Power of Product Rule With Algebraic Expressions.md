## Introduction

The **power of product rule** states that if we want to raise the product $xy$ to some power $n$, we must raise each factor to the power $n$ and then multiply:

$$
(xy)^n=x^n y^n
$$

For example,

$$
(xy)^4=x^4y^4
$$

To see why the power of product rule is true, let's take the above example and work it out again the long way, changing the order of factors in the multiplication. We get the same result:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(xy)^{4} &=  \\
(xy)(xy)(xy)(xy) &=  \\
(x \cdot x \cdot x \cdot x)(y \cdot y \cdot y \cdot y) &=  \\
&= x^{4}y^{4}
\end{aligned}
$$

The power of product rule is just a shortcut for the process above.

<a id="using-the-power-of-product-rule-with-a-positive-power"></a>
## Using the Power of Product Rule with a Positive Power

**Example:** Simplify $\left(2x\right)^3$.

**Explanation**

We apply the power of product rule and then simplify:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(2x)^{3} &=  \\
(2)^{3} \cdot (x)^{3} &=  \\
(8) \cdot (x^{3}) &=  \\
&= 8x^{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Using the Power of Product Rule with a Negative Power

**Example:** Simplify the expression $\left(pq^{-2}\right)^3$.

**Explanation**

We apply the power of product rule and then simplify:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(pq^{-2})^{3} &=  \\
p^{3} \cdot (q^{-2})^{3} &=  \\
p^{3} \cdot q^{(-2)(3)} &=  \\
p^{3}q^{-6} &=  \\
&= \frac{p^{3}}{q^{6}}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Using the Power of Product Rule with Three Factors

**Example:** Simplify $(2x^2y^{-1})^{-4}$.

**Explanation**

We apply the power of product rule and then simplify:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(2x^{2}y^{-1})^{-4} &=  \\
2^{-4} \cdot (x^{2})^{-4} \cdot (y^{-1})^{-4} &=  \\
\frac{1}{2^{4}} \cdot x^{(2)(-4)} \cdot y^{(-1)(-4)} &=  \\
\frac{1}{16} \cdot x^{-8} \cdot y^{4} &=  \\
\frac{1}{16} \cdot \frac{1}{x^{8}} \cdot y^{4} &=  \\
&= \frac{y^{4}}{16x^{8}}
\end{aligned}
$$
