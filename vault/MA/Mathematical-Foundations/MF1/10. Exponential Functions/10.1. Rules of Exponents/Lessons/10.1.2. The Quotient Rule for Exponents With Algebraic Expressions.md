## Introduction

The **quotient rule for exponents** states that when we divide two exponents that have the same base, we take the difference of the exponents in the numerator and denominator:

$$
\dfrac{a^n}{a^m}=a^{n-m}
$$

For example,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{a^{5}}{a^{2}} &=  \\
a^{(5 - 2)} &=  \\
&= a^{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

To see why the quotient rule is true, let's take the above example and work it out again, the long way.

For the two terms $a^5$ and $a^2$ we have

$$
a^5 = a \cdot a \cdot a \cdot a \cdot a
$$

and

$$
a^2 = a \cdot a
$$

If we now substitute these two expressions into

$$
\dfrac{a^5}{a^2}
$$

we get the same result:

$$
\require{cancel} \begin{aligned} \dfrac{a^{5}}{a^2}&=\ \dfrac {a \cdot a \cdot a \cdot a \cdot a} {a \cdot a}&=\ \dfrac {\cancel{a} \cdot \cancel{a} \cdot a \cdot a \cdot a} {\cancel{a} \cdot \cancel{a}} \\ &= a \cdot a \cdot a \\ &= a^3 \end{aligned}
$$

The quotient rule is just a shortcut for the process above.

<a id="applying-the-quotient-rule-for-exponents"></a>
## Applying the Quotient Rule for Exponents

**Example:** Simplify $\dfrac{x^2}{x^3}$.

**Explanation**

We subtract the exponents, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{x^{2}}{x^{3}} &=  \\
x^{(2 - 3)} &=  \\
&= x^{-1}
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we convert the negative exponent to a positive exponent by taking the reciprocal:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^{-1} &= \frac{1}{x}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\dfrac{x^2}{x^3} = \dfrac 1 x
$$

## Applying the Quotient Rule with Negative Exponents

**Example:** Simplify $\dfrac{z^5}{z^{-3}}$.

**Explanation**

We subtract the exponents, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{z^{5}}{z^{-3}} &=  \\
z^{5 - (-3)} &=  \\
z^{5 + 3} &=  \\
&= z^{8}
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="applying-the-quotient-rule-and-product-rule"></a>
## Applying the Quotient Rule and Product Rule

**Example:** Simplify the expression $\dfrac {p^7} {p^2} \cdot p^3$.

**Explanation**

First we apply the quotient rule, subtracting exponents in the fraction:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{p^{7}}{p^{2}} \cdot p^{3} &=  \\
p^{(7 - 2)} \cdot p^{3} &=  \\
&= p^{5} \cdot p^{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

Then we apply the product rule, adding exponents in the product:

$$
\begin{aligned}
p^{5} \cdot p^{3} &=  \\
p^{(5 + 3)} &=  \\
&= p^{8}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\frac{p^{7}}{p^{2}} \cdot p^{3} = p^{8}
$$
