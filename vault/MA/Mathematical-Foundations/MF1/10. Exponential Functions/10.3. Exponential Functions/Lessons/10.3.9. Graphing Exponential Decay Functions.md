## Introduction

Suppose we wish to plot the graph of $y=0.5^x$. First, we create a table of values:

| $x$ | $-3$ | $-2$ | $-1$ | $0$ | $1$ | $2$ | $3$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $y = 0.5^x$ | $8$ | $4$ | $2$ | $1$ | $0.5$ | $0.25$ | $0.125$ |

Now, we can plot the graph:

![](<../Source/10.3.9. Graphing Exponential Decay Functions/Images/a7a2a31ad238f4339a1e650284efd343.png>)

Note the following key points:

1. The graph has a $y$-intercept at $(0,1)$.
2. The graph is decreasing.
3. For $x<0$, the graph grows very rapidly as $x$ decreases. Symbolically, we can write $y\rightarrow \infty$ as $x\rightarrow -\infty$.
4. For $x>0$, the graph gets very close to zero as $x$ increases. However, it never actually reaches zero, so the graph has a horizontal asymptote $y=0$. Symbolically, we can write $y\rightarrow 0$ as $x\rightarrow \infty$.
5. The domain is $x\in\left(-\infty, \infty\right)$, and the range is $y>0$.

These key points apply to *any* exponential curve $y=a^x$ with $0 < a < 1$. These graphs always have the same general shape:

![](<../Source/10.3.9. Graphing Exponential Decay Functions/Images/cbb955f60141d280a5d25ccb1b71ca7e.png>)

## Sketching the Graph of an Exponential Decay Function

**Example:** Plot the graph of $y=\left(\dfrac{1}{4} \right)^x$.

**Explanation**

The graph of

$$
y=\left(\dfrac{1}{4}\right)^x
$$

has a $y$-intercept at $(0,1)$, grows rapidly for $x<0$, and approaches the asymptote $y=0$ for $x>0$.

When $x=-1$, we have

$$
y=\left(\dfrac{1}{4}\right)^{-1} =4
$$

Therefore, another point on the graph is

$$
\left(-1, 4\right)
$$

We draw the graph through these points as follows:

![](<../Source/10.3.9. Graphing Exponential Decay Functions/Images/e22081e2b2700465942e6efd103b2d56.png>)

<a id="identifying-true-statements-regarding-an-exponential-decay-curve"></a>
## Identifying True Statements Regarding an Exponential Decay Curve

**Example:** Which of the following statements are true in relation to the function $f(x) = (0.2)^x$?

1. The domain is $x\in(-\infty,\infty)$ and the range is $y\gt 0$.
2. The graph of $y=f(x)$ passes through the point $\left(1,0.2\right)$.
3. $f(x) \to -\infty$ as $x \to -\infty$.

**Explanation**

Let's go through the statements one by one.

- Statement I is true. The function $f(x) = (0.2)^x$ is defined for all input values $x$, and, the set of possible outputs consists of all the positive numbers.
- Statement II is true. When we evaluate the function at $x=1$, we have
$f(1) = (0.2)^1 = 0.2$.
- Statement III is false. As $x$ decreases, the function value gets larger and larger, approaching *positive* infinity. Therefore $f(x) \to \infty$ as $x \to -\infty$.

In conclusion, only statements I and II are true.

<a id="vertical-stretching-and-compression"></a>
## Vertical Stretching and Compression

Multiplying an exponential decay function by a positive number **scales** the graph in the vertical direction.

For example, the curve

$$
y=2\left(\dfrac{1}{3}\right)^x
$$

represents a vertical scaling of

$$
y=\left(\dfrac{1}{3}\right)^x
$$

by a factor of $2$.
It takes all of the $y$-coordinates on

$$
y=\left(\dfrac{1}{3}\right)^x
$$

and multiplies them by $2$. This has the effect of **stretching** the curve upwards.

![](<../Source/10.3.9. Graphing Exponential Decay Functions/Images/4b1acf1d3289550192a36fecefc9a8e9.png>)

Notice that the horizontal asymptote at $y=0$ remained the same, and the $y$-intercept moved from $(0,1)$ to $(0,2)$.

On the other hand, the curve

$$
y = \dfrac{1}{5}\left(2^x\right)
$$

represents a vertical scaling of
$y = 2^x$
by a factor of

$$
\dfrac{1}{5}
$$

It takes all of the $y$-coordinates on $y = 2^x$ and multiplies them by

$$
\dfrac{1}{5}
$$

This has the effect of **compressing** the curve downwards.

In general, for *any* vertically stretched or compressed exponential curve $y=k(a^x)$,

- the horizontal asymptote at $y=0$ remains the same, and
- the $y$-intercept moves from $(0,1)$ to $(0,k)$.

<a id="identifying-the-scaling-constant-from-a-graph"></a>
## Identifying the Scaling Constant From a Graph

**Example:** The graph below shows $y=k\left(\dfrac{2}{5}\right)^x$. What is the value of $k$?

![](<../Source/10.3.9. Graphing Exponential Decay Functions/Images/3690a60e3884515fadd832bdbe5b9efa.png>)

**Explanation**

To plot the graph of

$$
y=k\left(\dfrac25\right)^x
$$

we take the graph of

$$
y=\left(\dfrac 25\right)^x
$$

and stretch it vertically by a scale factor of $k$. Under the action of this transformation, the $y$-intercept is mapped from $(0,1)$ to $(0,k)$.

Comparing the point $(0,1.5)$ on the graph to $(0,k)$, we see that we must have $k=1.5$.
