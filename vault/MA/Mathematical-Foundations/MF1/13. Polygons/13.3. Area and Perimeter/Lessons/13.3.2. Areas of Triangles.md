## Introduction

The **area of a triangle** can be computed using the formula

$$
\mathcal{A} = \dfrac{b \cdot h}{2}
$$

where $b$ is a *base* of the triangle and $h$ is the corresponding *height*.

To demonstrate, let's compute the area of the triangle with base
$b = 4$
and the corresponding height is
$h = 8$,
as shown below.

![](<../Source/13.3.2. Areas of Triangles/Images/fc113e09eff8eb7443efb28ada750225.png>)

The area is

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= \frac{b \cdot h}{2} \\
&= \frac{4 \cdot 8}{2} \\
&= \frac{32}{2} \\
&= 16
\end{aligned}
$$

**Note:** At the end of the lesson, we'll demonstrate where the formula comes from. But first, let's get some practice using it.

## Calculating the Area of a Triangle

**Example:** Find the area of the triangle $\triangle ABC$.

![](<../Source/13.3.2. Areas of Triangles/Images/3b4d9c9c491ffbfc0029c8dc8fbdec5f.png>)

**Explanation**

The formula for the area of the triangle is

$$
\mathcal{A} = \dfrac{b \cdot h}{2}
$$

where $b$ is a base and $h$ is the corresponding height.

From the diagram, we see that the base is

$$
\begin{aligned}
b &= AB \\
&= AH + HB \\
&= 6 + 3 \\
&= 9
\end{aligned}
$$

and the corresponding height is
$h={CH} = 4$.

Therefore, the area is

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= \frac{b \cdot h}{2} \\
&= \frac{9 \cdot 4}{2} \\
&= \frac{36}{2} \\
&= 18
\end{aligned}
$$

## Calculating the Area of an Isosceles Right Triangle

**Example:** What is the area of the right triangle $ABC$ shown below?

![](<../Source/13.3.2. Areas of Triangles/Images/6e33b27de00dd277467a671bfd437fe1.png>)

**Explanation**

Since

$$
m\angle{A}=m\angle{C}
$$

we have an isosceles triangle, where $AB=BC$.

Therefore, the area of the triangle is

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= \frac{1}{2}AB \cdot BC \\
&= \frac{1}{2} \cdot 8 \cdot 8 \\
&= \frac{1}{2} \cdot 8^{2} \\
&= \frac{1}{2} \cdot 64 \\
&= 32
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="finding-the-base-or-the-height-of-a-triangle-given-its-area"></a>
## Finding the Base or the Height of a Triangle Given Its Area

**Example:** Consider the triangles $\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle CDE$ shown below. If the area of $\triangle CDE$ is $25 \textrm{cm}^2$, find the area of $\triangle ABC$.

![](<../Source/13.3.2. Areas of Triangles/Images/da9f32aef805e352321a281de43fe6ef.png>)

**Explanation**

Let's consider $\triangle CDE$. We can see that $\overline{CD}$ is the height corresponding to the base $\overline{DE}$ and that

$$
DE=10\textrm{cm}
$$

Since the area of $\triangle CDE$ is $25\textrm{cm}^2$, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= \frac{b \cdot h}{2} \\
25 &= \frac{10 \cdot CD}{2} \\
25 &= 5 \cdot CD \\
CD &= 5cm
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, notice that $\overline{CD}$ is also the height of $\triangle ABC$ corresponding to the base $\overline{AB}$.

![](<../Source/13.3.2. Areas of Triangles/Images/007cd77422021914a9dee871680fdecb.png>)

Therefore, we find the area of $\triangle ABC$ as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= \frac{b \cdot h}{2} \\
&= \frac{AB \cdot CD}{2} \\
&= \frac{6 \cdot 5}{2} \\
&= 15cm^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Proof of the Formula

We know that the area formula for a triangle is

$$
\mathcal{A} = \dfrac{b \cdot h}{2}
$$

where $b$ is a base and $h$ is the corresponding height.

But where does this formula come from?

To understand where the formula comes from, consider $\triangle{ABC}$ below.

![](<../Source/13.3.2. Areas of Triangles/Images/ca6f0f235d553fa3278d1726334102fc.png>)

Let's draw a rectangle around $\triangle{ABC}$, as follows:

![](<../Source/13.3.2. Areas of Triangles/Images/2ccb505e1fe94085d7ed416d4fcfedd6.png>)

We have two pairs of right triangles with equal areas (each pair is indicated with arrows).

So, the area of $\triangle{ABC}$ is exactly half of the area of the rectangle. This gives

$$
\mathcal{A}_{ABC} = \dfrac{AB \cdot CH}{2}
$$
