## Introduction

A **rectangle** is a parallelogram where all angles are right. It has all the properties of parallelograms: its opposite sides are parallel and congruent.

![](<../Source/13.2.8. Properties of Rectangles and Squares/Images/2086348cfb41ee2338739562729b3069.png>)

If all $4$ sides of a rectangle are congruent, then it is a **square**.

![](<../Source/13.2.8. Properties of Rectangles and Squares/Images/8b1fab7e95a453af67a3a1c2b9f4c05a.png>)

## Finding the Value of a Variable That Makes a Rectangle a Square

**Example:** For which value of $t$ is the rectangle below also a square?

![](<../Source/13.2.8. Properties of Rectangles and Squares/Images/43b8fde472f8e294f553191dc400b17e.png>)

**Explanation**

In order for the rectangle to be a square, all of its sides must be congruent.

So, we must have

$$
\begin{aligned}
BC &= CD \\
2t + 6 &= 4t \\
2t &= 6 \\
t &= 3
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="finding-the-measures-of-the-sides-of-a-rectangle"></a>
## Finding the Measures of the Sides of a Rectangle

**Example:** The perimeter of the rectangle $PQRS$ below equals $25\, \mathrm{cm}$. What is the length of $\overline{PQ}$?

![](<../Source/13.2.8. Properties of Rectangles and Squares/Images/50e16746e371910fac024a6da095bcc0.png>)

**Explanation**

The opposite sides of a rectangle are congruent, so we have

$$
PS=QR=5\, \mathrm{cm}
$$

For the same reason, we also have

$$
PQ = RS
$$

Now, since the perimeter is

$$
p=25 \, \text{cm}
$$

we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
p &= PQ + RS + PS + QR \\
25 &= PQ + PQ + 5 + 5 \\
25 &= 2PQ + 10 \\
15 &= 2PQ \\
7.5 &= PQ
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
PQ = 7.5 \, \text{cm}
$$

## The Diagonals of Rectangles and Squares

The diagonals of a rectangle are equal and bisect each other. Consequently, they divide a rectangle into four isosceles triangles, as shown below.

![](<../Source/13.2.8. Properties of Rectangles and Squares/Images/4a921330a2961b3df5147e83ddcdd9f4.png>)

In a square, the diagonals also bisect the angles.

![](<../Source/13.2.8. Properties of Rectangles and Squares/Images/2c2cb3b508fcd9344f6a2162da6ff9b2.png>)

<a id="finding-the-measure-of-a-diagonal-of-a-square"></a>
## Finding the Measure of a Diagonal of a Square

**Example:** In the square below, $NO=3\,\mathrm{mm}$. Find $KM$.

![](<../Source/13.2.8. Properties of Rectangles and Squares/Images/26e270081c071cc1dd57fd06d6eaee62.png>)

**Explanation**

The diagonals of a square are equal and bisect each other. So we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
KM &= LN \\
&= 2NO \\
&= 2 \cdot 3mm \\
&= 6mm
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding the Value of a Variable That Makes a Given Quadrilateral a Rectangle

**Example:** For which value of $x$ is the parallelogram below also a rectangle?

![](<../Source/13.2.8. Properties of Rectangles and Squares/Images/9190f6f338f2c204c7c3ff263f30d17e.png>)

**Explanation**

If $ABCD$ is a rectangle, then

$$
m\angle A = 90^\circ
$$

Now, since the interior angles of $\triangle ABD$ must sum to $180^\circ$, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
m∠ADB + m∠ABD + m∠BAD &= 180^{∘} \\
2x^{∘} + (x + 15)^{∘} + 90^{∘} &= 180^{∘} \\
3x + 15 &= 90 \\
3x &= 75 \\
x &= 25
\end{aligned}
$$
