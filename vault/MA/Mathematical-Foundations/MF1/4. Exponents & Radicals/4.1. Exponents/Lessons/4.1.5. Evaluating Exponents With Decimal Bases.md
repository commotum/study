## Introduction

We can use our knowledge of decimal multiplication to evaluate decimal exponents.

To illustrate, let's evaluate $(0.4)^2$.

First, by writing the expression with multiplication signs, we get the following:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(0.4)^{2} &= 0.4 \cdot 0.4_(⏟)_([math]{\color{blue}{2}}[/math] times)
\end{aligned}
$$

To compute this product, we first ignore any decimal points and multiply as if both numbers were whole numbers. We also ignore any leading zeros during the multiplication. This gives

$$
2
$$

We now count the number of decimal places in the $\color{blue}2$ original factors.

There is ${\color{purple}{1}}$ decimal place in $0.4$.
Therefore, the exponent $(0.4)^{\color{blue}2}$ will have

$$
{\color{purple}{1}}\times {\color{blue}{2}} = \color{red}2
$$

decimal places.

So, we take our value of $16$ and insert a decimal point to make a number with $\color{red}2$ decimal places:

$$
2
$$

Therefore, we conclude that

$$
(0.4)^2 = 0.16
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
(0.5)^{3} &= 0.5 \cdot 0.5 \cdot 0.5_(⏟)_([math]{\color{blue}{3}}[/math] times)
\end{aligned}
$$

To compute this product, we first ignore any decimal points and multiply the factors just as we would with whole numbers. We also ignore any leading zeros during the multiplication:

$$
3
$$

We now count the number of decimal places in the $\color{blue}3$ factors.

There is ${\color{purple}{1}}$ decimal place in $0.5$.
Therefore, the exponent $(0.5)^{\color{blue}3}$ will have

$$
{\color{purple}{1}}\times {\color{blue}{3}} = \color{red}3
$$

decimal places.

So, we take our value of $125$ and insert a decimal point to make a number with $\color{red}3$ decimal places:

$$
3
$$

Therefore, we conclude that

$$
(0.5)^3 = 0.125
$$

## Evaluating Two-Digit Decimal Exponents

**Example:** Find the value of $(4.2)^2$.

**Explanation**

First, by writing the expression with multiplication signs, we get the following:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(4.2)^{2} &= 4.2 \cdot 4.2_(⏟)_([math]{\color{blue}{2}}[/math] times)
\end{aligned}
$$

We proceed by multiplying the two numbers just as we would with whole numbers:

$$
[math]\color{blue}\phantom{0}[/math]; [math]\color{blue}\phantom{0}[/math]; 4\begin{vmatrix}2 \\ \cdot\end{vmatrix}0\begin{vmatrix}0 & 4\end{vmatrix}2; 8\begin{vmatrix}4 \\ +\end{vmatrix}1\begin{vmatrix}6 & 8 & 0 \\ 1 & 7 & 6\end{vmatrix}4
$$

We now count the total number of decimal places in the $\color{blue}2$ factors.

There is ${\color{purple}{1}}$ decimal place in $4.2$. Therefore, the exponent $(4.2)^{\color{blue}2}$ will have

$$
{\color{purple}{1}}\times {\color{blue}{2}} = \color{red}2
$$

decimal places.

So, we take our value of $1764$ and insert a decimal point to make a number with $\color{red}2$ decimal places:

$$
2
$$

Therefore, we conclude that

$$
(4.2)^2 = 17.64
$$

\begin{aligned}
