## Introduction

To raise a fraction to a power, we must raise both the numerator and the denominator to that power. This is known as the **power of quotient rule for exponents**.

To demonstrate, let's simplify the expression

$$
\left(\dfrac{2}{5}\right)^3
$$

by applying the power of quotient rule:

$$
\left(\dfrac{2}{5}\right)^3=\dfrac{2^3}{5^3}
$$

**Note:** The power of quotient rule for exponents is really just a quicker alternative to using repeated multiplication, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(\frac{2}{5})^{3} &= (\frac{2}{5}) \cdot (\frac{2}{5}) \cdot (\frac{2}{5}) \\
&= \frac{2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2}{5 \cdot 5 \cdot 5} \\
&= \frac{2^{3}}{5^{3}}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Applying the Power of Quotient Rule

**Example:** Express $\left(\dfrac{3}{4}\right)^2$ as the quotient of two powers.

**Explanation**

To raise the fraction to the power of $2$, we must raise both the numerator and the denominator to the power of $2$:

$$
\left(\dfrac{3}{4}\right)^2=\dfrac{3^2}{4^2}
$$

## Evaluating Powers of Fractions Using Power of Quotient Rule

**Example:** Evaluate $\left(\dfrac{4}{5}\right)^3$.

**Explanation**

To raise the fraction to the power of $3$, we must raise both the numerator and the denominator to the power of $3$:

$$
\left(\dfrac{4}{5}\right)^3=\dfrac{4^3}{5^3}
$$

Now, computing $4^3=64$ and $5^3=125$, we get

$$
\dfrac{4^3}{5^3}= \dfrac{64}{125}
$$

## Evaluating Powers of Negative Fractions Using the Power of Quotient Rule

**Example:** Evaluate $\left(-\dfrac{3}{2}\right)^3$.

**Explanation**

First, notice that

$$
-\dfrac{3}{2}=\dfrac{(-3)}{2}
$$

To raise the fraction to the power of $3$, we must raise both the numerator and the denominator to the power of $3$:

$$
\left(-\dfrac{3}{2}\right)^3=\left(\dfrac{(-3)}{2}\right)^3=\dfrac{(-3)^3}{2^3}
$$

Now, computing $(-3)^3=-27$ and $2^3=8$, we get

$$
\dfrac{(-3)^3}{2^3}= \dfrac{-27}{8}=-\dfrac{27}{8}
$$
