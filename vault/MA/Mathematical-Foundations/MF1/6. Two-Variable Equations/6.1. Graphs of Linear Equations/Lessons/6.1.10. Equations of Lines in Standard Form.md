## Introduction

The **standard form** equation of a line is

$$
ax+by=c
$$

where

- $a,b$, and $c$ are all integers,
- $a \geq 0$, and
- $a,b$, and $c$ have no common factors.

Note that we always write $x$ *before* $y$ on the left-hand side!

Let's see some examples:

- The equation $2x - y= -5$ is in standard form. Indeed, the variables are on the left-hand side, while the constant term is on the right-hand side:
$\underbrace{2x - y}_{\textrm{variables}} \quad = \underbrace{-5\phantom{y}}_{\textrm{constant}}$
All coefficients are integers (namely, $2$, $-1$, and $-5$) and don't have common factors greater than $1$. Also, the coefficient of $x$ is non-negative ($2$).
- The equation $-3x + y = 1$ is *not* in standard form because the coefficient of $x$ is negative ($-3$).
- The equation $2x+4y = -10$ is *not* in standard form because the coefficients $(2,4$, and $-10)$ have a common factor of $2$.
- The equation $\dfrac12x+\dfrac13y = 6$ is *not* in standard form because the coefficients of $x$ and $y$ are not integers.

## Identifying Equations Written in Standard Form

**Example:** Consider the equations of the following lines.

1. $-5x + y=3$
2. $3x - y= -2$
3. $3x= 5$
4. $2x- 6y= 4$

Which of these lines is written in standard form?

**Explanation**

The *standard form* equation of a line is

$$
ax+by=c
$$

where

- $a,b$, and $c$ are all integers,
- $a\geq 0$, and
- $a,b$, and $c$ have no common factors greater than $1$.

With that in mind, let's examine our equations.

- Equation I is *not* in standard form because the coefficient of $x$ is negative ($-5$).
- Equation II is in standard form. Indeed, the variables are on the left-hand side, while the constant term is on the right-hand side:
$\underbrace{3x- y}_{\textrm{variables}} \quad = \underbrace{\phantom{y}-2\phantom{y}}_{\textrm{constant}}$
All coefficients are integers (namely, $3$, $-1$, and $-2$) and don't have common factors greater than $1$. Also, the coefficient of $x$ is positive ($3$).
- Equation III is in standard form. Indeed, the variables are on the left-hand side, while the constant term is on the right-hand side:
$\underbrace{3x}_{\textrm{variables}} \quad = \underbrace{\phantom{y}5\phantom{y}}_{\textrm{constant}}$
All coefficients are integers (namely, $3$ and $5$) and don't have common factors greater than $1$. Also, the coefficient of $x$ is positive ($3$).
- Equation IV is *not* in standard form because the coefficients $(2,-6$, and $4)$ have a common factor of $2$.

Therefore, the correct answer is "II and III only."

<a id="converting-a-line-to-standard-form"></a>
## Converting a Line to Standard Form

Let's now discuss how to convert the equation of a line to standard form.

To illustrate, we will take the line $y = 2x-1$ and convert it into standard form. We begin by moving all of the variables to the left-hand side and all of the constants to the right-hand side.

$$
\begin{aligned}
y &= 2x - 1 \\
y - 2x &= -1 \\
- 2x + y &= -1
\end{aligned}
$$

Now the equation is in the form

$$
ax+by=c
$$

However, it violates the rule

$$
a \geq 0
$$

We can fix this by multiplying the equation by $-1$ as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
-2x + y &= -1 \\
(-1) \cdot (-2x + y) &= (-1) \cdot (-1) \\
2x - y &= 1
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, $a,b$, and $c$ are integers with no common factors, and

$$
a \geq 0
$$

So, we are done!

The standard form of the line is $2x-y=1$.

<a id="converting-from-slope-intercept-form-to-standard-form"></a>
## Converting from Slope-Intercept Form to Standard Form

**Example:** Write $y = 7x+\dfrac{2}{5}$ in standard form.

**Explanation**

Our goal is to write the equation in the form

$$
ax+by = c
$$

where $a,b$, and $c$ are integers with no common factors, and

$$
a\geq 0
$$

We begin by moving all of the variables to the left-hand side and all of the constants to the right-hand side.

$$
\begin{aligned}
y &= 7x + \frac{2}{5} \\
y - 7x &= \frac{2}{5} \\
- 7x + y &= \frac{2}{5}
\end{aligned}
$$

Now the equation is in the form

$$
ax+by=c
$$

However, it violates the rule

$$
a \geq 0
$$

We can fix this by multiplying the equation by $-1$ as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
-7x + y &= \frac{2}{5} \\
(-1) \cdot (-7x + y) &= (-1) \cdot (\frac{2}{5}) \\
7x - y &= -\frac{2}{5}
\end{aligned}
$$

However, $c$ is a fraction, not an integer. To fix this, we multiply by $5$ to get rid of the fraction:

$$
\begin{aligned}
7x - y &= -\frac{2}{5} \\
5 \cdot (7x - y) &= 5 \cdot (-\frac{2}{5}) \\
35x - 5y &= -2
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, $a,b$, and $c$ are integers with no common factors, and

$$
a\geq 0
$$

So we are done!

The standard form of the line is $35x-5y=-2$.

## Converting an Arbitrary Line to Standard Form

**Example:** Write $y-1=x$ in standard form.

**Explanation**

Our goal is to write the equation in the form

$$
ax+by = c
$$

where $a,b$, and $c$ are integers with no common factors and

$$
a\geq 0
$$

We begin by moving all of the variables to the left-hand side and all of the constants to the right-hand side. This gives

$$
-x+y=1
$$

Then, we multiply by $-1$ to make the $x$-coefficient positive:

$$
\begin{aligned}
-x + y &= 1 \\
(-1)(-x + y) &= -1 \\
x - y &= -1
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, $a,b$, and $c$ are integers with no common factors, and

$$
a \geq 0
$$

So we are done!

The standard form of the line is $x-y=-1$.

<a id="finding-the-standard-equation-of-a-line-passing-through-two-points"></a>
## Finding the Standard Equation of a Line Passing Through Two Points

**Example:** Calculate the equation of the straight line that passes through the points $(-1,3)$ and $(2,4)$. Write the answer in standard form.

**Explanation**

First, we will construct the point-slope form of the line. Then, we will simplify it into standard form.

To construct the point-slope form of the line, we need a point and a slope. We already have two points, so we calculate the slope $m$ using the given points:

$$
m = \frac{y_{2} - y_{1}}{x_{2} - x_{1}} = (4 - 3)/(2 - (-1)) = \frac{1}{3}
$$

Then, we use this slope along with one of the points to write the point-slope form equation of the line. It doesn't matter which point we choose, but let's choose $(2,4)$. The point-slope equation is as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
y - y_{1} &= m(x - x_{1}) \\
y - 4 &= \frac{1}{3}(x - 2)
\end{aligned}
$$

To simplify the point-slope formula into standard form, we first multiply by $3$ to get rid of the fraction:

$$
\begin{aligned}
y - 4 &= \frac{1}{3}(x - 2) \\
3 \cdot (y - 4) &= 3 \cdot \frac{1}{3}(x - 2) \\
3y - 12 &= x - 2
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we move the variables to the left side and the constant to the right side:

$$
\begin{aligned}
3y - 12 &= x - 2 \\
3y &= x + 10 \\
- x + 3y &= 10
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we multiply by $-1$ to make the $x$-coefficient positive:

$$
\begin{aligned}
-x + 3y &= 10 \\
(-1) \cdot (-x + 3y) &= (-1) \cdot 10 \\
x - 3y &= -10
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the standard form of the equation is $x-3y = -10$.
