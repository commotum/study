## Introduction

Sometimes, we might have a system of equations with fractional coefficients, such as the following:

$$
{\frac{1}{2}x + \frac{5}{4}y =-4; 7x - 5y =-2
$$

We can turn the system into an equivalent system involving only integer coefficients. This will make it easier to solve.

In general, we multiply each equation by its lowest common denominator. In this instance, multiplying the first equation by the lowest common denominator of the fractions $(4)$ will do the job:

$$
{\frac{1}{2}x + \frac{5}{4}y =-4 \mid \mid \cdot 4; 7x - 5y =-2
$$

Now, we can solve the system using, for example, the elimination method. Notice that the $5y$ term in the first equation can cancel with the $-5y$ term in the second equation.

Adding the two equations eliminates the $y$-variable, and allows us to solve for $x$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
2x + 5y &= -16 \\
7x - 5y &= -2 \\
9x &= -18 \\
x &= -2
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we can substitute $x=-2$ into the equation $7x - 5y= -2$ to solve for $y$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
7x - 5y &= -2 \\
7(-2) - 5y &= -2 \\
- 14 - 5y &= -2 \\
- 5y &= 12 \\
y &= -\frac{12}{5}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the solution to the system of equations is

$$
x=-2, \,y=-\dfrac{12}{5}
$$

## Solving a System Containing Fractions in One Equation Using Elimination

**Example:** ${-x - 5y =-1; \frac{1}{4}x + 3y = 2$

Find the solution to the system of equations given above.

**Explanation**

To get rid of the fractions in the second equation, we must multiply the second equation by the lowest common denominator of the fractions, which is $4$.

$$
{-x - 5y =-1; \frac{1}{4}x + 3y = 2 \mid \mid \cdot 4
$$

We will use the elimination method to solve the system. Notice that the $-x$ term in the first equation can cancel with the $x$ term in the second equation.

Adding the two equations eliminates the $x$-variable, and allows us to solve for $y$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
-x - 5y &= -1 \\
x + 12y &= 8 \\
7y &= 7 \\
y &= 1
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we can substitute $y=1$ into the equation $-x-5y=-1$ to solve for $x$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
-x - 5y &= -1 \\
- x - 5 \cdot 1 &= -1 \\
- x - 5 &= -1 \\
- x &= 4 \\
x &= -4
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus, the solution to the system of equations is

$$
x=-4, \, y=1
$$

## Solving a System Containing Fractions in Both Equations Using Elimination

**Example:** Find the solution to the following system of equations.
${\frac{5}{2}x + 2y = 3; \frac{1}{6}x - \frac{2}{3}y =-7$

**Explanation**

To get rid of fractions, we multiply the first equation by $2$ and the second equation by $6$, as follows:

$$
{\frac{5}{2}x + 2y = 3 \mid \mid \cdot 2; \frac{1}{6}x - \frac{2}{3}y =-7 \mid \mid \cdot 6
$$

We will use the elimination method to solve the system. We can eliminate $y$ by adding the two equations:

$$
\begin{aligned}
5x + 4y &= 6 \\
x - 4y &= -42 \\
(5x + x) &= (6 - 42) \\
6x &= -36 \\
x &= -6
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we substitute $x=-6$ into the second equation to solve for $y$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x - 4y &= -42 \\
- 6 - 4y &= -42 \\
- 4y &= -36 \\
y &= 9
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the solution to the system is $x=-6$ and $y=9$.

## Using Substitution

We can also use the substitution method to solve systems of equations containing fractional coefficients.

We proceed as follows:

- First, we multiply both equations by the lowest common denominator of the fractions in the system.
- Then, we apply the substitution method as usual.

Let's see an example.

---

<a id="solving-a-system-containing-fractions-in-one-equation-using-substitution"></a>
## Solving a System Containing Fractions in One Equation Using Substitution

**Example:** Solve the following system of equations:

${x + 2y =-4; x - \frac{y}{6} = \frac{5}{2}$

**Explanation**

To get rid of the fractions in the second equation, we must multiply the second equation by the lowest common denominator of the fractions, which is $6$.

$$
{x + 2y =-4; x - \frac{y}{6} = \frac{5}{2} \mid \mid \cdot 6
$$

We will use the substitution method to solve the system. Let's solve for $x$ in the first equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x + 2y &= -4 \\
x &= -4 - 2y
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we can substitute $x=-4-2y$ into the second equation and solve for $y$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
6x - y &= 15 \\
6(-4 - 2y) - y &= 15 \\
- 24 - 12y - y &= 15 \\
- 24 - 13y &= 15 \\
- 13y &= 39 \\
y &= -3
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, to find the value of $x$, we substitute $y=-3$ into the equation $x=-4-2y$, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x &= -4 - 2y \\
&=-4 - 2(-3) \\
&=-4 + 6 \\
&= 2
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the solution to the system of equations is $x = 2$ and $y = -3$.

<a id="solving-a-system-containing-fractions-in-both-equations"></a>
## Solving a System Containing Fractions in Both Equations

**Example:** Find the solution to the following system of equations:

${5a + \frac{3b}{2} = 2; \frac{4a}{3} + 2b = 0$

**Explanation**

To get rid of the fractions, we multiply the first equation by $2$ and the second equation by $3$, as follows:

$$
{5a + \frac{3b}{2} = 2 \mid \mid \cdot 2; \frac{4a}{3} + 2b = 0 \mid \mid \cdot 3
$$

We will use the elimination method to solve the system. Notice that if we multiply the first equation by $-2$, then we will get a $-6b$ term that can cancel with the $6b$ term in the second equation:

$$
{10a + 3b = 4 \mid \mid \cdot (-2); 4a + 6b = 0
$$

Adding the two equations eliminates the $b$-variable, and allows us to solve for $a$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
-20a - 6b &= -8 \\
4a + 6b &= 0 \\
(-20a + 4a) &= (-8 + 0) \\
- 16a &= -8 \\
a &= \frac{-8}{-16} \\
a &= \frac{1}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we can substitute

$$
a=\dfrac{1}{2}
$$

into the equation

$$
4a+6b=0
$$

to solve for $b$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
4a + 6b &= 0 \\
4(\frac{1}{2}) + 6b &= 0 \\
2 + 6b &= 0 \\
6b &= -2 \\
b &= -\frac{2}{6} \\
b &= -\frac{1}{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus, the solution to the system of equations is

$$
a = \dfrac{1}{2}, \,b =-\dfrac{1}{3}
$$
