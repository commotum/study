## Introduction

A **logic circuit** is an electrical circuit that performs logical operations on one or more binary inputs to produce a binary output.
These circuits are fundamental to digital systems and can be represented mathematically using Boolean functions, which define their behavior.

One such logic circuit is a **switch circuit**. A Boolean variable $A$ (or a negation $\overline{A}$) represents a switch in the circuit.

![](<../Source/2.3.1. Introduction to Logic Circuits/Images/46323ab7946f2ad32aec37fe8b1245ed.png>)

Conjunction $A \land B$ can be represented by switches $A$ and $B$ connected "in series," i.e., consecutively. For an electrical current to flow through the circuit, it must pass through switches $A$ *and* $B$ (i.e., both must be true).

![](<../Source/2.3.1. Introduction to Logic Circuits/Images/fddcd5159644955820ceda5c2ac4d283.png>)

A disjunction $A \lor B$ can be represented by switches $A$ and $B$ connected "in parallel." For current to flow through the circuit, it must pass either through either switch $A$ *or* switch $B$ (i.e., at least one must be true).

![](<../Source/2.3.1. Introduction to Logic Circuits/Images/1f819d5a6f86054ec1eee7d099480461.png>)

To create larger circuits, we can combine "in series" and "in parallel" switches. For example, let's construct the logic circuit represented by the expression $A \lor (\overline{B} \land C)$.

Considering the expression from right to left and taking into account the parentheses, we get the following:

- The conjunction of $\overline{B}$ and $C$ corresponds to a series connection, as shown below.

![](<../Source/2.3.1. Introduction to Logic Circuits/Images/473e032566f4411112735c263dd24da2.png>)

- Now, the disjunction of $A$ and $\overline{B} \land C$ corresponds to a parallel connection. Therefore, the given expression represents the following circuit:![](<../Source/2.3.1. Introduction to Logic Circuits/Images/a692771bed450405298afa51bdd1604d.png>)

## Constructing a Logical Expression Given a Switching Circuit

**Example:** Find an expression representing the switching circuit above.

![](<../Source/2.3.1. Introduction to Logic Circuits/Images/37e637cd9c6e961cb38b3f28bf30e105.png>)

**Explanation**

Recall that:

- The conjunction $X \land Y$ represents switches $X$ and $Y$ connected "in series" (i.e., consecutively):

![](<../Source/2.3.1. Introduction to Logic Circuits/Images/4b60d89f515ad6fb3effaeed33417473.png>)

- The disjunction $X \lor Y$ represents switches $X$ and $Y$ connected "in parallel":

![](<../Source/2.3.1. Introduction to Logic Circuits/Images/da9710819ab96426ddc8d7a7f5c53175.png>)

Considering the given circuit from right to left, we get the following:

- The switches $A$, $B$, and $C$ are connected in parallel. So, this part can be represented as $A \lor B \lor C$.
- Now, the part corresponding to $A \lor B \lor C$ and the switch $\overline{C}$ are connected in series. Therefore, the whole circuit can be represented by the expression
$(A \lor B \lor C)\land \overline{C}$.

## Constructing a Logical Expression Given a Switching Circuit: Advanced Cases

**Example:** Write the expressions represented by the switching circuit below.

![](<../Source/2.3.1. Introduction to Logic Circuits/Images/88d5852f0818b267dd314e6997097778.png>)

**Explanation**

Recall that:

- The conjunction $X \land Y$ represents switches $X$ and $Y$ connected "in series" (i.e., consecutively):

![](<../Source/2.3.1. Introduction to Logic Circuits/Images/58b46119b9b43de2215bf68f11ac85c7.png>)

- The disjunction $X \lor Y$ represents switches $X$ and $Y$ connected "in parallel":

![](<../Source/2.3.1. Introduction to Logic Circuits/Images/1e0bd6850ebb21773de121549c537c24.png>)

The left part of the circuit represents
$A \lor B\lor C$

and the right part represents
$\ \overline {A}\land (C\lor D)$.

These parts are connected in series. Therefore, the given circuit represents the following expression:
$(A \lor B\lor C)\land \overline {A}\land (C\lor D)$
