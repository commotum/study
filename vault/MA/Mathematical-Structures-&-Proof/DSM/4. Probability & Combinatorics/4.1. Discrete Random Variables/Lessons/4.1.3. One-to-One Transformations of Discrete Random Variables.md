## Introduction

Suppose that the random variable $X$ has the following probability distribution.

| $x$ | $1$ | $2$ | $3$ | $4$ | $5$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $f_X(x)$ | $0.25$ | $0.2$ | $0.1$ | $0.15$ | $0.3$ |

Note that $f_X(x)$ denotes the probability mass function of $X$.

Now, imagine that we define the variable $Y$ as follows:

$$
Y = 2X
$$

Since $Y$ is a function of $X$, it must also be a random variable. What is the probability distribution of $Y$?

We start by computing all possible values of $Y$, so let's add a $Y=2X$ row to our table.

| $x$ | $1$ | $2$ | $3$ | $4$ | $5$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $y$ | $2$ | $4$ | $6$ | $8$ | $10$ |
| $f_X(x)$ | $0.25$ | $0.2$ | $0.1$ | $0.15$ | $0.3$ |

So, the possible values of $Y$ are $2,4,6,8$, and $10$.

We now need to calculate the probability mass function $f_Y(y)$ for each possible value of $Y$.

The crucial thing to realize is that

$$
f_X(x) = f_Y(y)
$$

for corresponding values of $X$ and $Y$. So, for example, we have the following:

$$
\begin{aligned}
f_{X}(1) = P(X = 1) &= P(Y = 2) = f_{Y}(2) = 0.25 \\
f_{X}(2) = P(X = 2) &= P(Y = 4) = f_{Y}(4) = 0.2 \\
&= ⋮
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the distribution of $Y$ is as follows:

| $y$ | $2$ | $4$ | $6$ | $8$ | $10$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $f_Y(y)$ | $0.25$ | $0.2$ | $0.1$ | $0.15$ | $0.3$ |

## Finding the Distribution of a Transformed Random Variable

**Example:** The random variable $X$ has the following probability distribution.

| $x$ | $-0.5$ | $-0.25$ | $0$ | $0.25$ | $0.5$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $f_X(x)$ | $0.25$ | $0.2$ | $0.1$ | $0.15$ | $0.3$ |

Let $Y=2-4X$. The distribution of $Y$ is given below.

| $y$ | $a$ | $3$ | $c$ | $1$ | $0$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $f_Y(y)$ | $0.25$ | $b$ | $0.1$ | $d$ | $0.3$ |

What is the value of $a+b+c$?

**Explanation**

First, let's add a $Y = 2-4X$ row to our table.

| $x$ | $-0.5$ | $-0.25$ | $0$ | $0.25$ | $0.5$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $y$ | $4$ | $3$ | $2$ | $1$ | $0$ |
| $f_X(x)$ | $0.25$ | $0.2$ | $0.1$ | $0.15$ | $0.3$ |

Now,

$$
f_X(x) = f_Y(y)
$$

for corresponding values of $X$ and $Y$. Therefore, the distribution of $Y$ is as follows:

| $y$ | $4$ | $3$ | $2$ | $1$ | $0$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $f_Y(y)$ | $0.25$ | $0.2$ | $0.1$ | $0.15$ | $0.3$ |

So, we have

$$
a=4, b=0.2, c=2
$$

and therefore

$$
a+b+c =6.2
$$

## Calculating a Probability Involving a Transformed Random Variable

**Example:** The random variable $X$ has the following probability distribution.

| $x$ | $-0.5$ | $-0.25$ | $0$ | $0.25$ | $0.5$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $f_X(x)$ | $0.25$ | $0.2$ | $0.1$ | $0.15$ | $0.3$ |

Let $Y=2-4X$. Calculate $P(Y \gt 2)$.

**Explanation**

First, let's add a $Y = 2-4X$ row to our table.

| $x$ | $-0.5$ | $-0.25$ | $0$ | $0.25$ | $0.5$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $y$ | $4$ | $3$ | $2$ | $1$ | $0$ |
| $f_X(x)$ | $0.25$ | $0.2$ | $0.1$ | $0.15$ | $0.3$ |

Now,

$$
f_X(x) = f_Y(y)
$$

for corresponding values of $X$ and $Y$. So, the distribution of $Y$ is as follows:

| $y$ | $4$ | $3$ | $2$ | $1$ | $0$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $f_Y(y)$ | $0.25$ | $0.2$ | $0.1$ | $0.15$ | $0.3$ |

Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(Y > 2) &= P(Y = 3) + P(Y = 4) \\
&= 0.2 + 0.25 \\
&= 0.45
\end{aligned}
$$
