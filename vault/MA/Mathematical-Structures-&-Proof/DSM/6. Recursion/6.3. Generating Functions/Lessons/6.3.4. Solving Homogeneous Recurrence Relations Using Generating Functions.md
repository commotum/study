## Introduction

Recall that a relation $a_n$ is **homogeneous** if the corresponding expression contains only preceding terms of the relation.

In other words, the linear recurrence relation

$$
a_n = s_1a_{n-1} + s_2a_{n-2} + \ldots + s_ka_{n-k} + g(n)
$$

is homogeneous if $g(n) = 0$ for all $n$, and $s_i$ is constant for each $i$.

Given a sequence $a_n$ for

$$
n\geq 0
$$

the corresponding **generating series** is an infinite power series such that the coefficient of $x^i$ is equal to $a_i$ for all

$$
i\geq 0
$$

For example, suppose that we have the following sequence of numbers:

$$
1,\quad -2,\quad 4,\quad -8, \quad\ldots
$$

The corresponding generating series for this sequence is

$$
1-2x+4x^2-8x^3+\cdots
$$

A generating series is simply an alternative way of representing a sequence.

## Homogeneous Recurrence Relations Using Generating Functions

Suppose the general term $a_n$ for

$$
n\geq 0
$$

of the sequence $1, \: -1, \: -1, \: 11, \: \ldots$ satisfies the recurrence relation

$$
a_n=-5a_{n-1}-6a_{n-2}
$$

We can use generating functions to find the explicit expression for $a_n$. Let's see how.

Notice that for

$$
n \geq 2
$$

our sequence satisfies the recurrence relation

$$
a_n + 5a_{n-1} + 6a_{n-2} = 0
$$

Let $A$ denote the generating function of our sequence:

$$
A = 1-x-x^2+11x^3+\cdots
$$

Adding $A$, $5xA$, and $6x^2A$, we obtain the following:

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= 1 - 0x - 0x^{2} + 11x^{3} + ⋯ \\
5xA &= 0 + 5x - 5x^{2} - 05x^{3} + ⋯ \\
6x^{2}A &= 0 + 0x + 6x^{2} - 06x^{3} + ⋯ \\
A + 5xA + 6x^{2}A &= 1 + 4x + 0x^{2} + 00x^{3} + ⋯ \\
(1 + 5x + 6x^{2})A &= 1 + 4x \\
A &= \frac{1 + 4x}{1 + 5x + 6x^{2}} \\
&= (1 + 4x)/((3x + 1)(2x + 1))
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we re-write our fraction as a sum of partial fractions:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(1 + 4x)/((3x + 1)(2x + 1)) &= - (1)/((3x + 1)) + (2)/((2x + 1))
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we expand each of the partial fractions as a geometric series.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{1}{3x + 1} &= 1 + (-3)x + (-3)^{2}x^{2} + (-3)^{3}x^{3} + ⋯ \\
\frac{2}{2x + 1} &= 2 + 2(-2)x + 2(-2)^{2}x^{2} + 2(-2)^{3}x^{3} + ⋯
\end{aligned}
$$

The general term of the first sequence is

$$
b_n = \left(-3\right)^n
$$

while the general term of the second sequence is

$$
c_n = 2 \cdot (-2)^n
$$

Therefore, the general term of our original sequence is

$$
\begin{aligned}
a_{n} &= -b_{n} + c_{n} \\
&= - (-3)^{n} + 2 \cdot (-2)^{n} \\
&= (-1)^{n}(2^{n + 1} - 3^{n})
\end{aligned}
$$

## Completing a Solution Template

**Example:** Suppose we wish to determine the general term $a_n$ for $n \geq 0$ of the sequence
$3, \: -9, \: 21, \: -45, \: \ldots$
that satisfies the recurrence relation $a_n=-3a_{n-1}-2a_{n-2}$.

Fill the blanks in the template for the solution below.

**Step 1.** Let $A$ denote the generating function of our sequence:
$A = \bbox[3pt,border:solid 1pt]{\phantom{\textrm{3-9x+21x^2-45x^3+...}}}$

**Step 2.** Find a closed expression for $A$ by adding
$\bbox[3pt,border:solid 1pt]{\phantom{\textrm{odd}}}$,
$\bbox[3pt,border:solid 1pt]{\phantom{\textrm{odd}}}$, and
$\bbox[3pt,border:solid 1pt]{\phantom{\textrm{odd}}}$:

$A = \dfrac{3}{(1+3x+2x^2)}$

**Step 3.** We express $A$ as a
$\bbox[3pt,border:solid 1pt]{\phantom{\textrm{sum of partial fractions}}}$:

$A = \dfrac{6}{1+2x} - \dfrac{3}{1+x}$

**Step 4.** We determine the general terms for the coefficients $b_n$ and $c_n$ in the
$\bbox[3pt,border:solid 1pt]{\phantom{\textrm{geometric}}}$
series corresponding to the first and the second partial fractions:
$[MATH: b_n = 6 \cdot \big(\bbox[3pt,border:solid 1pt]{\phantom{\textrm{odd}}}] \big)^n \qquad\text{and}\qquad c_n = 3 \cdot \big(\bbox[3pt,border:solid 1pt]{\phantom{\textrm{odd}}}\big)^n]$

**Step 5.** We write down the general term of our original sequence:
$a_n = \bbox[3pt,border:solid 1pt]{\phantom{\textrm{b_n-c_n}}} =6 \cdot (-2)^n - 3\cdot (-1)^{n}$

**Explanation**

The correct solution is the following:

**Step 1.** Let $A$ denote the generating function of our sequence:

$$
A = \bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}3 - 9x + 21x^2 - 45x^3+\cdots}
$$

**Step 2.** Find a closed expression for $A$ by adding
$\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}A}$,
$\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}3xA}$, and
$\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}2x^2A}{:}$

$$
A = \dfrac{3}{(1+3x+2x^2)}
$$

**Step 3.** We express $A$ as a
$\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm{sum of partial fractions}}{:}$

$$
A = \dfrac{6}{1+2x} - \dfrac{3}{1+x}
$$

**Step 4.** We determine the general terms for the coefficients $b_n$ and $c_n$ in the
$\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm{geometric}}$
series corresponding to the first and the second partial fractions:

$$
b_n =6 \cdot \big(\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}-2} \big)^n \qquad\text{and}\qquad c_n =3 \cdot \big(\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}{-1}} \big)^n
$$

Indeed, notice the following:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{6}{1 + 2x} &= 6 \cdot (1)/(1 - (-2x)) \\
&= 6[1 + (-2x) + (-2x)^{2} + (-2x)^{3} + ⋯] \\
&= ∑_(n = 0)^(∞)[6 \cdot (-2)^{n}x^{n}] \\
\frac{3}{1 + x} &= 3 \cdot (1)/(1 - (-x)) \\
&= 3[1 + (-x) + (-x)^{2} + (-x)^{3} + ⋯] \\
&= ∑_(n = 0)^(∞)[3 \cdot (-1)^{n}x^{n}]
\end{aligned}
$$

**Step 5.** We write down the general term of our original sequence:

$$
a_n = \bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}b_n-c_n} = 6 \cdot (-2)^n - 3\cdot (-1)^{n}
$$

## Second-Order Homogeneous Recurrence Relations: Finding Partial Solutions Using Generating Functions

**Example:** The general term $a_n$ for $n\geq 0$ of the sequence
$1, \: -4, \: 10, \: -22, \: \ldots$
that satisfies the recurrence relation $a_n=-3a_{n-1}-2a_{n-2}$ is given by
$a_n = 3\cdot (-2)^{n} - 2 \cdot (-1)^n$.
Fill in the blanks below to obtain the correct reasoning.

Let $A$ denote the generating function of our sequence:

$A =\bbox[3pt,border:solid 1pt]{\phantom{\textrm{1-4x +10x^2-22x^3}}}$

First, we find a closed expression for $A$ by adding
$\bbox[3pt,border:solid 1pt]{\phantom{\textrm{A}}}$,
$\bbox[3pt,border:solid 1pt]{\phantom{\textrm{3xA}}}$, and
$\bbox[3pt,border:solid 1pt]{\phantom{\textrm{2x^2A}}}$.

By doing so, we obtain

| $A=\,\,$ | $\bbox[3pt,border:solid 1pt]{\phantom{\textrm{1-x}}}$ |
| --- | ---: |
| $\big(1\,+\bbox[3pt,border:solid 1pt]{\phantom{\textrm{2x}}}\big)$<br>$\big(1\,+\bbox[3pt,border:solid 1pt]{\phantom{\textrm{0x}}}\big)$ |  |

Now, we rewrite our fraction as the sum of the partial fractions:

| $A=\,\,$ | $\bbox[3pt,border:solid 1pt]{\phantom{\textrm{3}}}$ | $-$ | $\bbox[3pt,border:solid 1pt]{\phantom{\textrm{2}}}$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $1+2x$ | $1+x$ |  |  |

The general terms for the coefficients in the geometric series corresponding to the first and second partial fractions, respectively, are
$b_n = \bbox[3pt,border:solid 1pt]{\phantom{\textrm{3*(-2)^n}}} \qquad\text{and}\qquad c_n = \bbox[3pt,border:solid 1pt]{\phantom{\textrm{(-1)^n}}}$.

Therefore, the general term of our original sequence is
$a_n = \bbox[3pt,border:solid 1pt]{\phantom{\textrm{b_n-c_n}}} = 3\cdot (-2)^{n} - 2\cdot (-1)^n$.

**Explanation**

Notice that for

$$
n \geq 2
$$

our sequence satisfies the recurrence relation

$$
a_n + 3a_{n-1} + 2a_{n-2} = 0
$$

Let $A$ denote the generating function of our sequence:

$$
A = \bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}1-4x+10x^2-22x^3+\cdots}
$$

Adding $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}A}$, $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}3xA}$, and $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}2x^2A}$, we obtain the following:

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= 1 - 4x + 10x^{2} - 22x^{3} + ⋯ \\
3xA &= 0 + 3x - 12x^{2} + 30x^{3} + ⋯ \\
2x^{2}A &= 0 + 0x + 02x^{2} - 08x^{3} + ⋯ \\
A + 3xA + 2x^{2}A &= 1 - 0x + 00x^{2} + 00x^{3} + ⋯ \\
A + 3xA + 2x^{2}A &= 1 - x \\
(1 + 3x + 2x^{2})A &= 1 - x \\
A &= \frac{1 - x}{1 + 3x + 2x^{2}} \\
&= (1 - x)/((1 + 2 x)(1 + x))
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we rewrite our fraction as a sum of partial fractions:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(1 - x)/((1 + 2x)(1 + x)) &= (B)/((1 + 2x)) + (C)/((1 + x))
\end{aligned}
$$

Multiplying both sides by $(1+2x)(1+x)$ gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
1 - x &= B(1 + x) + C(1 + 2x)
\end{aligned}
$$

- Substituting $x=-\dfrac12$, we get $B = 3$.
- Substituting $x=-1$, we get $C = -2$.

Hence,

$$
\dfrac{1-x}{(1+2x)(1+x)} = \dfrac{3}{1+2x} + \dfrac{-2}{1+x} = \dfrac{\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}3}}{1+2x} - \dfrac{\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}2}}{1+x}
$$

Next, we expand each of the partial fractions as a geometric series.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{3}{1 + 2x} &= 3 \cdot (1)/(1 - (-2x)) \\
&= 3[1 + (-2x) + (-2x)^{2} + (-2x)^{3} + ⋯] \\
&= 3 + 3(-2)x + 3(-2)^{2}x^{2} + 3(-2)^{3}x^{3} + ⋯ \\
\frac{2}{1 + x} &= 2 \cdot (1)/(1 - (-x)) \\
&= 2[1 + (-x) + (-x)^{2} + (-x)^{3} + ⋯] \\
&= 2 + 2(-1)x + 2(-1)^{2}x^{2} + 2(-1)^{3}x^{3} + ⋯
\end{aligned}
$$

The general term for the coefficients in the first series is

$$
b_n = \bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}3 \cdot (-2)^n}
$$

while the general term for the coefficients in the second series is

$$
c_n = \bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}2 \cdot (-1)^n}
$$

Therefore, the general term of our original sequence is

$$
\begin{aligned}
a_{n} &= b n - c n \\
&= 3 \cdot (-2)^{n} - 2 \cdot (-1)^{n}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Solving Second-Order Homogeneous Recurrence Relation Using Generating Functions

**Example:** Find the general term $a_n$ for $n\geq 0$ of the sequence
$2, \: 2, \: -2, \: -22, \: \ldots$
that satisfies the recurrence relation $a_n=5a_{n-1}-6a_{n-2}$.

**Explanation**

Notice that for

$$
n \geq 2
$$

our sequence satisfies the recurrence relation

$$
a_n - 5a_{n-1} + 6a_{n-2} = 0
$$

Let $A$ denote the generating function of our sequence:

$$
A = 2+2x-2x^2-22x^3+\cdots
$$

Adding $A$, $-5xA$, and $6x^2A$, we obtain the following:

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= 2 + 02x - 02x^{2} - 22x^{3} + ⋯ \\
- 5xA &= 0 - 10x - 10x^{2} + 10x^{3} + ⋯ \\
6x^{2}A &= 0 + 00x + 12x^{2} + 12x^{3} + ⋯ \\
A - 5xA + 6x^{2}A &= 2 - 08x + 00x^{2} + 00x^{3} + ⋯ \\
A - 5xA + 6x^{2}A &= 2 - 8x \\
(1 - 5x + 6x^{2})A &= 2 - 8x \\
A &= \frac{2 - 8x}{1 - 5x + 6x^{2}} \\
&= (2 - 8x)/((1 - 3x)(1 - 2x))
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we re-write our fraction as a sum of partial fractions:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(2 - 8x)/((1 - 3x)(1 - 2x)) &= (B)/((1 - 3x)) + (C)/((1 - 2x))
\end{aligned}
$$

Multiplying both sides by $(1-3x)(1-2x)$ gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
2 - 8x &= B(1 - 2x) + C(1 - 3x)
\end{aligned}
$$

- Substituting $x=\dfrac 1 3$, we get $B =-2$.
- Substituting $x=\dfrac{1}{2}$, we get $C = 4$.

Hence,

$$
\dfrac{2-8x}{(1-3x)(1-2x)} = \dfrac{4}{1-2x}-\dfrac{2}{1-3x}
$$

Next, we expand each of the partial fractions as a geometric series.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{4}{1 - 2x} &= 4 \cdot (1)/(1 - (2x)) \\
&= 4[1 + (2x) + (2x)^{2} + (2x)^{3} + ⋯] \\
&= 4 + 4(2)x + 4(2)^{2}x^{2} + 4(2)^{3}x^{3} + ⋯ \\
\frac{2}{1 - 3x} &= 2 \cdot (1)/(1 - (3x)) \\
&= 2[1 + (3x) + (3x)^{2} + (3x)^{3} + ⋯] \\
&= 2 + 2(3)x + 2(3)^{2}x^{2} + 2(3)^{3}x^{3} + ⋯
\end{aligned}
$$

The general term for the coefficients in the first series is

$$
b_n = 4 \cdot 2^n
$$

while the general term for the coefficients in the second series is

$$
c_n = 2 \cdot 3^n
$$

Therefore, the general term of our original sequence is

$$
\begin{aligned}
a_{n} &= b_{n} - c_{n} \\
&= 4 \cdot 2^{n} - 2 \cdot 3^{n}
\end{aligned}
$$
