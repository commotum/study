## Introduction

If we wish to sum the squares of the first $N$ integers, an easy way is to use the following formula:

$$
\begin{aligned}
∑_(n = 1)^(N)n^{2} &= 1^{2} + 2^{2} + 3^{2} + ⋯ + (N - 1)^{2} + N^{2} \\
&= 1 + 4 + 9 + ⋯ + (N - 1)^{2} + N^{2} \\
&= \frac{1}{6}N(N + 1)(2N + 1)
\end{aligned}
$$

Since we also know that

$$
\begin{aligned}
∑_(n = 1)^(N)n &= 1 + 2 + 3 + ⋯ + (N - 1) + N \\
&= \frac{1}{2}N(N + 1)
\end{aligned}
$$

we can use these formulas to evaluate any series of the form

$$
\displaystyle{\sum_{n=1}^N (an^2+bn+c)}
$$

<a id="calculating-the-sum-of-the-first-n-square-numbers"></a>
## Calculating the Sum of the First N Square Numbers

**Example:** Calculate $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{100} n^2$.

**Explanation**

Using the formula

$$
\sum_{n=1}^N n^2 = \frac{1}{6}N(N+1)(2N+1)
$$

with $N=100$, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
∑_(n = 1)^(100)n^{2} &= \frac{1}{6} \cdot 100(100 + 1)(2 \cdot 100 + 1) \\
&= \frac{1}{6} \cdot 100 \cdot 101 \cdot 201 \\
&= 338350
\end{aligned}
$$

## Calculating the Sum of a Finite Quadratic Series with a Constant Factor

**Example:** Calculate
$\displaystyle \sum_{n=1}^{100} 4n^2$.

**Explanation**

First, we take the factor $4$ outside the summation:

$$
\sum_{n=1}^{100} 4n^2 = 4\sum_{n=1}^{100} n^2
$$

Now, using the formula

$$
\sum_{n=1}^N n^2 = \frac{1}{6}N(N+1)(2N+1)
$$

with $N=100$, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
4∑_(n = 1)^(100)n^{2} &= 4(\frac{1}{6} \cdot 100(100 + 1)(2 \cdot 100 + 1)) \\
&= 4 \cdot \frac{1}{6} \cdot 100 \cdot 101 \cdot 201 \\
&= 4 \cdot 338350 \\
&= 1353400
\end{aligned}
$$

## Calculating the Sum of a Quadratic Series with a Linear or Constant Term

**Example:** Calculate
$\displaystyle \sum_{n=1}^{50} (3n^2+2n)$.

**Explanation**

Notice that we can write the given summation as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
∑_(n = 1)^(50)(3n^{2} + 2n) &= ∑_(n = 1)^(50)3n^{2} + ∑_(n = 1)^(50)2n \\
&= 3∑_(n = 1)^(50)n^{2} + 2∑_(n = 1)^(50)n
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, let's evaluate the two series using our formulas.

- For the first series, we use the formula
$\displaystyle \sum_{n=1}^N n^2 = \dfrac1 6 N (N+1)(2N+1)$
with $N=50$, and we get
$3∑_(n = 1)^(50)n^{2}|= 3(\frac{1}{6} \cdot 50(50 + 1)(2 \cdot 50 + 1)); = 3 \cdot \frac{1}{6} \cdot 50 \cdot 51 \cdot 101; = 3 \cdot 42925; = 128775$.
- For the second series, we use the formula
$\displaystyle \sum_{n=1}^N n = \dfrac1 2 N (N+1)$
with $N=50$, and we get
$2∑_(n = 1)^(50)n|= 2(\frac{1}{2} \cdot 50 \cdot (50 + 1)); = 2 \cdot 1275; = 2550$.

Finally, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
∑_(n = 1)^(50)(3n^{2} + 2n) &= 128775 + 2550 \\
&= 131325
\end{aligned}
$$
