## Introduction

If we wish to sum the first $N$ integers, an easy way is to use the following formula:

$$
\begin{aligned}
∑_(n = 1)^(N)n &= 1 + 2 + 3 + ⋯ + (N - 1) + N \\
&= \frac{1}{2}N(N + 1)
\end{aligned}
$$

As we will see, we can use this simple formula to evaluate any series of the form

$$
\displaystyle{\sum_{n=1}^N (an+b)}
$$

---

<a id="calculating-the-sum-of-the-first-n-integers"></a>
## Calculating the Sum of the First N Integers

**Example:** Calculate $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{100} n$.

**Explanation**

Using the formula

$$
\begin{aligned}
∑_(n = 1)^(N)n &= \frac{1}{2}N(N + 1)
\end{aligned}
$$

with $N=100$, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
∑_(n = 1)^(100)n &= \frac{1}{2} \cdot 100 \cdot (100 + 1) \\
&= \frac{1}{2} \cdot 100 \cdot 101 \\
&= 5050
\end{aligned}
$$

## Calculating the Sum of a Finite Linear Series with a Constant Factor

**Example:** Calculate $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{100} 4n$.

**Explanation**

First, we take the factor $4$ outside the summation:

$$
\sum_{n=1}^{100} 4n = 4\sum_{n=1}^{100} n
$$

Now, using the formula

$$
\begin{aligned}
∑_(n = 1)^(N)n &= \frac{1}{2}N(N + 1)
\end{aligned}
$$

with $N=100$, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
4∑_(n = 1)^(100)n &= 4(\frac{1}{2} \cdot 100 \cdot 101) \\
&= 4 \cdot 5050 \\
&= 20200
\end{aligned}
$$

## Calculating the Sum of a Finite Linear Series with a Constant Term

**Example:** Calculate $\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{200} (3n+2)$.

**Explanation**

We can write the summation as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
∑_(n = 1)^(200)(3n + 2) &= ∑_(n = 1)^(200)3n + ∑_(n = 1)^(200)2 \\
&= 3∑_(n = 1)^(200)n + 2∑_(n = 1)^(200)1
\end{aligned}
$$

Let's consider the two series separately.

- For the first series, we use the formula
$\displaystyle \sum_{n=1}^N n = \dfrac 1 2 N (N+1)$
with $N=200$, and we get
$3∑_(n = 1)^(200)n|= 3(\frac{1}{2} \cdot 200 \cdot 201); = 3 \cdot 20100; = 60300$.
- In the second series, $\displaystyle{\sum_{n=1}^{200} 1}$ is just $1$ added together $200$ times, which is $200 \cdot 1 = 200$. So, we have
$2\sum_{n=1}^{200} 1 =2 \cdot 200 = 400$.

Finally, we have

$$
\sum_{n=1}^{200} (3n+2) = 60\,300 + 400 = 60\,700
$$

## Derivation of the Formula

Here, we'll derive the formula

$$
\sum_{n=1}^N n = \dfrac 1 2 N (N+1)
$$

First, let's represent the sum with the letter $S$. This way, it will be easier to manipulate the sum algebraically.

$$
S = \sum_{n=1}^N n
$$

Next, let's write out $S$ twice. The first time, we'll write the numbers in ascending order, and the second time in descending order:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}S = 1 & + 2 + ⋯ + (N - 1) + N \\ S = N & + (N - 1) + ⋯ + 2 + 1\end{bmatrix}
$$

Now, if we add the two sums above, we get the following:

$$
\begin{aligned}
2S &= (N + 1) + (2 + N - 1) + ⋯ + (N - 1 + 2) + (N + 1) \\
&= (N + 1) + (N + 1) + ⋯ + (N + 1) + (N + 1)_(⏟)_(Ntimes) \\
&= N(N + 1)
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, solving for $S$, we reach the desired result:

$$
S = \dfrac 1 2 N (N+1)
$$
