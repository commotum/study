## Introduction

Just like we can distribute multiplication over addition, we can also distribute conjunctions over disjunctions (and vice versa).

- We can distribute a conjunction over a disjunction, as follows:
$A \land (B \lor C) \equiv (A \land B) \lor (A \land C)$
- Likewise, we can distribute a disjunction over a conjunction, as follows:
$A \lor (B \land C) \equiv (A \lor B) \land (A \lor C)$

The rules above are called the **distributive laws** for propositional logic. To prove that these laws hold, we can construct truth tables.

- The following truth table shows that $A \land (B \lor C) \equiv (A \land B) \lor (A \land C){:}$

| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $\textrm T$ | $\textrm T$ | $\textrm T$ | $\color{blue} \textrm T$ | $\color{blue} \textrm T$ |
| $\textrm T$ | $\textrm T$ | $\textrm F$ | $\color{blue} \textrm T$ | $\color{blue} \textrm T$ |
| $\textrm T$ | $\textrm F$ | $\textrm T$ | $\color{blue} \textrm T$ | $\color{blue} \textrm T$ |

## Expanding a Statement by Distributing a Conjunction

**Example:** Expand the statement $P \land (Q \lor R)$ by distributing the conjunction.

**Explanation**

We distribute the conjunction over the disjunction, as follows:

$$
P \land (Q \lor R) \equiv (P \land Q) \lor (P \land R)
$$

## Expanding a Statement by Distributing a Disjunction

**Example:** Expand the statement $(C \land B) \lor A$ by distributing the disjunction.

**Explanation**

We distribute the disjunction over the conjunction, as follows:

$$
(C \land B) \lor A \equiv (C \lor A) \land (B \lor A)
$$

## Simplifying a Statement Using the Distributive Property for Conjunctions

**Example:** Simplify the statement $(A \land C) \lor (A \land B)$ using the distributive property for conjunctions.

**Explanation**

We can factor out the conjunction with $A$, as follows:

$$
(A \land C) \lor (A \land B) \equiv A \land (C \lor B)
$$

## Simplifying a Statement Using the Distributive Property for Disjunctions

**Example:** Simplify the statement $(P \lor Q) \land (P \lor R)$ using the distributive property for disjunctions.

**Explanation**

We can factor out the disjunction with $P$, as follows:

$$
(P \lor Q) \land (P \lor R) \equiv P \lor (Q \land R)
$$
