## Introduction

An expression is a **tautology** if it is always true, regardless of the truth values of inputs.

For example, let's consider the expression

$$
A \lor \neg A
$$

and fill out the corresponding truth table:

| $\,A\,$ | $\,\lnot A\,$ | $\,A \lor \neg A\,$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| $\textrm T$ | $\textrm F$ | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm T}$ |
| $\textrm F$ | $\textrm T$ | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm T}$ |

According to the table,

$$
A \lor \neg A
$$

is always true. Therefore, it is a tautology.

Tautologies are represented by the symbol $\textbf t$, so we write

$$
A \lor \neg A = \textbf t
$$

## Contradictions

An expression is a **contradiction** if it is always false, regardless of the truth values of inputs.

For example, let's consider the expression

$$
A \land \neg A
$$

and fill out the corresponding truth table:

| $\,A\,$ | $\,\lnot A\,$ | $\,A \land \neg A\,$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| $\textrm T$ | $\textrm F$ | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm F}$ |
| $\textrm F$ | $\textrm T$ | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm F}$ |

According to the table,

$$
A \land \neg A
$$

is always false. Therefore, it is a contradiction.

Contradictions are represented by the symbol $\textbf c$, so we write

$$
A \land \neg A = \textbf c
$$

## Properties of Tautologies and Contradictions

Tautologies have the following properties:

- The disjunction of an expression and a tautology gives a tautology:
$A \lor \textbf{t} = \textbf{t}$
- The conjunction of an expression and a tautology gives the initial expression:
$A \land \textbf{t} = A$

Contradictions have the following properties:

- The disjunction of an expression and a contradiction gives the initial expression:
$A \lor \textbf{c} = A$
- The conjunction of an expression and a contradiction gives a contradiction:
$A \land \textbf{c} = \textbf{c}$

To prove these properties, we need to fill out the corresponding truth tables. For example, the truth table for the first property is shown below.

| $\,A\,$ | $\textbf{t}$ | $A \lor \textbf{t}$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| $\textrm T$ | $\textrm T$ | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm T}$ |
| $\textrm F$ | $\textrm T$ | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm T}$ |

$$
A \lor \textbf{t}
$$

Finally, note the following:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\begin{bmatrix}t∨t = t & t∧t &= t \\ t∨c = t & t∧c|= c \\ c∨t = t & c∧t|= c \\ c∨c = c & c∧c|= c\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Again, all of these properties can be proved using truth tables.

## Applying Properties of Tautologies and Contradictions

**Example:** If $P$ is a statement, $\textbf t$ is a tautology, and $\textbf c$ is a contradiction, complete the following table of logical equivalences.

| Statement | Is Logically Equivalent To |
| --- | ---: |
| $\textbf t \land \textbf c$ | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\phantom{\color{blue}P}}$ |
| $\textbf t \lor \textbf c$ | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\phantom{\color{blue}P}}$ |
| $P \land \textbf c$ | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\phantom{\color{blue}P}}$ |
| $P \lor \textbf c$ | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\phantom{\color{blue}P}}$ |

**Explanation**

Recall the following properties of tautologies and contradictions:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\begin{bmatrix}A∨t = t & A∧t &= A \\ A∨c = A & A∧c|= c \\ t∨t = t & t∧t|= t \\ t∨c = t & t∧c|= c \\ c∨t = t & c∧t|= c \\ c∨c = c & c∧c|= c\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

The completed table is shown below.

| Statement | Is Logically Equivalent To |
| --- | ---: |
| $\textbf t \land \textbf c$ | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textbf c}$ |
| $\textbf t \lor \textbf c$ | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textbf t}$ |
| $P \land \textbf c$ | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textbf c}$ |
| $P \lor \textbf c$ | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}P}$ |

## Simplifying Expressions Involving Tautologies and Contradictions

**Example:** Simplify the expression $(A \land \neg B)\Rightarrow \neg B$.

**Explanation**

Let's simplify the expression using the properties of logical operation

- First, we use that $X \Rightarrow Y \equiv \neg X \lor Y {:}$
$(A \land \neg B)\Rightarrow \neg B \equiv \neg (A \land \neg B) \lor \neg B$
- Next, we use a De Morgan's law:
${\color{blue}\neg (A \land \neg B)} \lor \neg B \equiv {\color{blue}(\neg A \lor \neg \neg B)} \lor \neg B$
- Then, we simplify the double negation:
$(\neg A \lor {\color{blue}\neg \neg} B) \lor \neg B \equiv (\neg A \lor B) \lor \neg B$
- Next, we use the associative law:
$(\neg A \lor B) \lor \neg B \equiv \neg A \lor (B \lor \neg B)$
- Then, we use the fact that the disjunction of an expression and its negation is a tautology:
$\neg A \lor {\color{blue} (B \lor \neg B)} \equiv \neg A \lor {\color{blue} \textrm{t}}$
- Finally, we use the fact that the disjunction of an expression and a tautology gives a tautology:
$\neg A \lor \textrm{t} \equiv \textrm{t}$

Therefore, we conclude that

$$
(A \land \neg B) \Rightarrow \neg B \equiv \textrm{t}
$$
