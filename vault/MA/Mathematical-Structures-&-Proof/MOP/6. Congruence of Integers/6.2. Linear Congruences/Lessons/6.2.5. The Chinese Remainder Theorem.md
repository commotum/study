## Introduction

The Chinese remainder theorem states that if the natural numbers $n_1,n_2,\ldots,n_k$ are pairwise coprime, meaning that

$$
\text{gcd}(n_i,n_j)=1
$$

whenever

$$
i \neq j
$$

then for any integers $a_1,a_2,\ldots,a_k$ the system

$$
{x≡a_{1}\begin{vmatrix}(modn_{1}) \\ x≡a_{2}\end{vmatrix}(modn_{2}); ⋮; x≡a_{k} \mid (modn_{k})
$$

has a unique solution modulo $n_1 n_2 \cdots n_k$.

For example, suppose that we want to find the smallest natural number solution to the following system of linear congruences:
${x≡4\begin{vmatrix}(mod5) \\ x≡7\end{vmatrix}(mod9)$

This system can be interpreted as follows. Suppose we have $x$ identical "bricks" and we know that:

- if we stack them in piles with $5$ bricks each, then the last remaining pile will contain $4$ bricks.
- if we stack them in piles with $9$ bricks each, then the last remaining pile will contain $7$ bricks.

How can we find the smallest possible number $x$?

![](<../Source/6.2.5. The Chinese Remainder Theorem/Images/fb1a16658b2287fd548ce71128df08e5.png>)

First, we note that $5$ and $9$ are coprime. Therefore, according to the Chinese remainder theorem, the system has a unique solution modulo

$$
5 \cdot 9 = 45
$$

From the first congruence, we get

$$
x \equiv 4 \: (\text{mod}\:5) \qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad x = 4 + 5t, \quad t \in \mathbb{Z}
$$

Substituting this into the second congruence, we obtain the following:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}x & ≡7 & (mod9) \\ 4 + 5t & ≡7 & (mod9) \\ 5t & ≡3 & (mod9)\end{bmatrix}
$$

Since $9$ is a relatively small modulus, we can find the multiplicative inverse of $5$ by trial and error. We find that $2 \cdot 5 \equiv 1 \: (\text{mod}\:9)$. So, we multiply both sides of the above congruence by $2$:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}5t & ≡3 & (mod9) \\ 2 \cdot 5t & ≡2 \cdot 3 & (mod9) \\ t & ≡6 & (mod9)\end{bmatrix}
$$

As a result,

$$
t = 6 + 9s
$$

where $s \in \mathbb{Z}$. Substituting this into the expression for $x$, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
x &= 4 + 5t \\
&= 4 + 5(6 + 9s) \\
&= 34 + 45s
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, $x \equiv 34 \: (\text{mod}\:45)$ is the unique solution modulo $45$. The solution $x=34$ is the smallest natural number solution to the system, and all other solutions are obtained by adding or subtracting multiples of $45$ to this solution.

Finally, let's check that this is indeed correct. Substituting $x=34$ into the first congruence, we get

$$
\begin{bmatrix}34 & ≡4 & (mod5) \\ 34 - 6(5) & ≡4 & (mod5) \\ 4 & ≡4 & (mod5), ✓\end{bmatrix}
$$

and substituting $x=34$ into the second congruence, we get

$$
\begin{bmatrix}34 & ≡7 & (mod9) \\ 34 - 3(9) & ≡7 & (mod9) \\ 7 & ≡7 & (mod9).✓\end{bmatrix}
$$

## Identifying True Statements About the Chinese Remainder Theorem

**Example:** According to the Chinese remainder theorem, which of the following systems of congruences has a unique solution modulo $140$?

${x≡2\begin{vmatrix}(mod4) \\ x≡3\end{vmatrix}(mod5); x≡5 \mid (mod7)$

**Explanation**

The Chinese remainder theorem states that if the natural numbers $n_1,n_2,\ldots,n_k$ are pairwise coprime, meaning that

$$
\text{gcd}(n_i,n_j)=1
$$

whenever

$$
i \neq j
$$

then for any integers $a_1,a_2,\ldots,a_k$ the system

$$
{x≡a_{1}\begin{vmatrix}(modn_{1}) \\ x≡a_{2}\end{vmatrix}(modn_{2}); ⋮; x≡a_{k} \mid (modn_{k})
$$

has a unique solution modulo $n_1 n_2 \cdots n_k$.

Let's now inspect each of the given systems in turn.

- The theorem applies to system I. The numbers $4$, $5$, and $7$ are pairwise coprime natural numbers. Therefore, according to the Chinese remainder theorem, the system has a unique solution modulo $4 \cdot 5 \cdot 7 = 140$.
- The theorem does *not* apply to system II since $10$ and $14$ are not coprime: $\text{gcd}(10,14)=2 > 1$
- The theorem applies to system III. The numbers $7$ and $20$ are pairwise coprime natural numbers. Therefore, according to the Chinese remainder theorem, the system has a unique solution modulo $7 \cdot 20 = 140$.

So, the correct answer is "I and III only."

## Solving a System of Two Linear Congruences

**Example:** Find the smallest natural number solution to the system of linear congruences below.
${x≡1\begin{vmatrix}(mod2) \\ x≡5\end{vmatrix}(mod7)$

**Explanation**

Since $2$ and $7$ are coprime, then according to the Chinese remainder theorem, the system has a unique solution modulo

$$
2 \cdot 7 = 14
$$

From the first congruence, we get

$$
x \equiv 1 \: (\text{mod}\:2) \qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad x = 1 + 2t, \quad t \in \mathbb{Z}
$$

Substituting this into the second congruence, we obtain the following:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}x & ≡5 & (mod7) \\ 1 + 2t & ≡5 & (mod7) \\ 2t & ≡4 & (mod7)\end{bmatrix}
$$

Since $7$ is a relatively small modulus, by trial and error, we have that $4 \cdot 2 \equiv 1 \: (\text{mod}\:7)$. So, we multiply both sides by $4$:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}2t & ≡4 & (mod7) \\ 4 \cdot 2t & ≡16 & (mod7) \\ t & ≡2 & (mod7)\end{bmatrix}
$$

As a result,

$$
t = 2 + 7s
$$

where $s \in \mathbb{Z}$. Substituting this into the expression for $x$, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
x &= 1 + 2t \\
&= 1 + 2(2 + 7s) \\
&= 5 + 14s
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, $x \equiv 5 \: (\text{mod}\:14)$ is the unique solution modulo $14$, and $x=5$ is the smallest natural number solution to the system.

## Solving a System of Three Linear Congruences

**Example:** Find the smallest natural number solution to the system of linear congruences below.
${x≡1\begin{vmatrix}(mod2) \\ x≡2\end{vmatrix}(mod3); x≡3 \mid (mod5)$

**Explanation**

Since $2$, $3$, and $5$ are coprime, then according to the Chinese remainder theorem, the system has a unique solution modulo

$$
2 \cdot 3 \cdot 5 = 30
$$

We will solve the system in two steps.

**Step 1.** Find the common solution for the first and second congruences of the system.

From the first congruence, we get

$$
x \equiv 1 \: (\text{mod}\:2) \qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad x = 1 + 2t, \quad t \in \mathbb{Z}
$$

Substituting this into the second congruence, we obtain the following:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}x & ≡2 & (mod3) \\ 1 + 2t & ≡2 & (mod3) \\ 2t & ≡1 & (mod3)\end{bmatrix}
$$

Since $3$ is a relatively small modulus, we can solve it by trial and error. We have that $2 \cdot 2 \equiv 1 \: (\text{mod}\:3)$. So, we multiply both sides by $2$:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}2t & ≡1 & (mod3) \\ 2 \cdot 2t & ≡2 & (mod3) \\ t & ≡2 & (mod3)\end{bmatrix}
$$

As a result,

$$
t = 2 + 3s
$$

where $s \in \mathbb{Z}$. Substituting this into the expression for $x$, we get the common solution for the first and second congruences:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x &= 1 + 2t \\
&= 1 + 2(2 + 3s) \\
&= 5 + 6s
\end{aligned}
$$

**Step 2.** Find the common solution of all three congruences.

Now, substituting the common solution of the first two congruences into the third congruence, we obtain the following:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}x & ≡3 & (mod5) \\ 5 + 6s & ≡3 & (mod5) \\ 6s & ≡ - 2 & (mod5) \\ s & ≡3 & (mod5)\end{bmatrix}
$$

As a result,

$$
s = 3 + 5u
$$

where $u \in \mathbb{Z}$. Substituting this into the expression for the common solution of the first two congruences, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
x &= 5 + 6s \\
&= 5 + 6(3 + 5u) \\
&= 23 + 30u
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, $x \equiv 23 \: (\text{mod}\:30)$ is the unique solution modulo $30$, and $x=23$ is the smallest natural number solution to the system.
