## Introduction

When attempting to construct a mathematical proof, it's often a good idea to start with some specific cases. Proving results for particular cases usually gives valuable insights into how to construct the proof of a more general result.

In this lesson, we'll prove specific cases of the addition, scalar multiplication, and division properties of modular arithmetic.

We start with our definition of modular congruence:

> *Let $a$ and $b$ be integers and let $n$ be a positive integer. We say that $\boldsymbol a$ **is congruent to** $\boldsymbol b$ **modulo** $\boldsymbol n$ if and only if $n\mid (a-b)$. If $a$ is congruent to $b$ modulo $n$, we write*
> $a\equiv b\quad (\textrm{mod}n)$.

Since the definition of congruence uses the concept of divisibility, let's also state this definition:

> *Let $c$ and $d$ be integers with $d\neq 0$. We say that $\boldsymbol d$ **divides** $\boldsymbol c$ if there exists an integer $k$ such that $c=d\cdot k$.*

Putting these two definitions together, we have the following chain of equivalences:

$$
a \equiv b \: (\textrm{mod} \: n) \qquad\Leftrightarrow\qquad n \mid (a-b) \qquad\Leftrightarrow\qquad a-b = nk
$$

for some integer $k$.

<a id="proving-a-congruence-statement"></a>
## Proving a Congruence Statement

Let's construct a direct proof of the following statement:

$$
\underbrace{a \equiv b}_{P(a,b)} \quad\Rightarrow\quad \underbrace{a+2 \equiv b+2}_{Q(a,b)} \qquad (\textrm{mod} \: 5)
$$

We start by assuming the antecedent $P$ is true.

> *Assume that* $a \equiv b \: (\textrm{mod} \: 5)$.

Next, we state what this means using the definition of congruence.

> *By the definition of modular congruence,*
> $5 \mid (a-b) \qquad\Leftrightarrow\qquad a-b = 5k$
> *for some integer $k$.*

Now, the goal is to show that

$$
(a+2) \equiv (b+2) \quad \Leftrightarrow\quad 5 \mid \big((a+2) - (b+2)\big)
$$

So, we write down the difference between $(a+2)$ and $(b+2)$ and show that it is a multiple of $5$ using our assumption regarding the difference between $a$ and $b{:}$

> *Hence, we get*
> $(a + 2) - (b + 2)|= a + 2 - b - 2; = a - b; = 5k$.

Since $(a+2) - (b+2)$ is a multiple of $5$, this shows that $(a+2) \equiv (b+2)$ modulo $5$. So, our proof is complete, and we write our conclusion.

> *Therefore, according to the definition of congruence, we have*
> $a+2 \equiv b+2 \quad (\textrm{mod} \: 5)$.

Now that we've discussed all the details, let's write down our final proof.

---

<a id="stating-the-full-proof"></a>
## Stating the Full Proof

Proposition:

$$
a \equiv b \quad\Rightarrow\quad a+2 \equiv b+2 \qquad (\textrm{mod} \: 5)
$$

Proof:

> *Assume that* $a \equiv b \: (\textrm{mod} \: 5)$. *By the definition of modular congruence,*
> $5 \mid (a-b) \qquad\Leftrightarrow\qquad a-b = 5k$
> *for some integer $k$. Hence, we get*
> $(a + 2) - (b + 2)|= a + 2 - b - 2; = a - b; = 5k$.
> *Therefore, according to the definition of congruence, we have*
> $a+2 \equiv b+2 \qquad (\textrm{mod} \: 5)$.

## Proving the Addition and Subtraction Properties

**Example:** Let $a,b \in \mathbb{Z}$. Prove that if $a \equiv b \: (\textrm{mod} \: 7)$, then $a-4 \equiv b-4 \: (\textrm{mod} \: 7)$.

**Explanation**

We start by using the definition of congruence modulo $n$. Recall that

$$
a \equiv b \: (\textrm{mod} \: n) \quad\Leftrightarrow\quad n \mid (a-b) \quad\Leftrightarrow\quad a-b = nk
$$

for some integer $k$.

In this case, we need to show the following:
$a \equiv b \: (\textrm{mod} \: 7) \quad\Rightarrow\quad a-4 \equiv b-4 \qquad (\textrm{mod} \: 7)$

So, we can reason as follows:

> Since $a \equiv b \: (\textrm{mod} \: 7)$, by the definition of modular congruence,
> $7 \mid (a-b) \qquad\Leftrightarrow\qquad a-b = 7k$
> for some integer $k$.

Now, we express the difference between $a-4$ and $b-4$ through the difference between $a$ and $b{:}$

> Now, notice that
> $(a - 4) - (b - 4)|= a - 4 - b + 4; = a - b; = 7k$.

Finally, we use the definition of congruence in reverse:

> Therefore, according to the definition of congruence, we have
> $a-4 \equiv b-4 \: (\textrm{mod} \: 7)$.

## Proving the Scalar Multiplication Property

**Example:** Let $a, b \in \mathbb{Z}$. Prove that $8a \equiv 8b \: (\textrm{mod} \: 10)$ if $a \equiv b \: (\textrm{mod} \: 10)$.

**Explanation**

We start by using the definition of congruent numbers. Recall that

$$
a \equiv b \: (\textrm{mod} \: n) \quad\Leftrightarrow\quad n \mid (a-b) \quad\Leftrightarrow\quad a-b = nk
$$

for some integer $k$.

In this case, we need to show the following:
$a \equiv b \: \quad\Rightarrow\quad 8a \equiv 8b \quad (\textrm{mod} \: 10)$

So, we can reason as follows:

> Since $a \equiv b \: (\textrm{mod} \: 10)$, by the definition of modular congruence,
> $10 \mid (a-b) \qquad\Leftrightarrow\qquad a - b = 10k$
> for some integer $k$.

The equation above involves the difference between $a$ and $b$. But we want a relationship between $8a$ and $8b$. To get an expression involving $8a$ and $8b$, we can multiply the above equation by $8{:}$

> Now, multiplying both sides of $a - b = 10k$ by $8$, we get
> $8 \cdot (a - b)|= 8 \cdot 10k; 8a - 8b|= 10(8k); 8a - 8b|= 10m$,
> where $m = 8k$ is an integer.

Finally, we use the definition of modular congruence in reverse:

> According to the definition of modular congruence, the final equation implies that
> $8a \equiv 8b \: (\textrm{mod} \: 10)$.

## The Division Properties

Recall the following division properties of modular arithmetic for

$$
k\neq 0{:}
$$

- The first division property states that
$ka \equiv kb \quad(\text{mod}\,kn) \quad \Rightarrow \quad a \equiv b \quad(\textrm{mod}\,n)$.

- The second division property states that
$\left(ka\equiv kb\:(\textrm{mod} \,n)\right) \:\land\: \left(\textrm{gcd}(k,n)=1\right) \quad \Rightarrow\quad a \equiv b \quad(\textrm{mod}\, n)$.

Let's prove that the second property holds in the following case:

$$
\underbrace{3a \equiv 3b}_{P(a,b)} \quad\Rightarrow\quad \underbrace{a \equiv b}_{Q(a,b)} \qquad (\textrm{mod} \: 4)
$$

We begin our proof by assuming $P$ is true.

> *Assume* $3a \equiv 3b\:(\textrm{mod} \: 4)$.
> *Then, by the definition of modular congruence, we have*
> $3a≡3b(mod4) \mid ⇔4∣(3a - 3b); ⇔4∣3(a - b)$.

Now, we recall the following divisibility property of integers:

$$
(c \mid ab) \:\land\: (\textrm{gcd}(a,c)=1) \quad\Rightarrow\quad (c \mid b)
$$

Using this fact, we have the following:

> *Now, since $3(a-b)$ is divisible by $4$ and $\textrm{gcd}(4,3)=1$, the difference $(a-b)$ must be divisible by $4$, i.e.,*
> $4 \mid (a-b)$.

Finally, we use the definition of modular congruence one more:

> *Therefore, according to the definition of modular congruence, we have*
> $a \equiv b \qquad (\textrm{mod} \: 4)$.

And we're done.

Let's restate the full proof.

---

<a id="stating-the-full-proof"></a>
## Stating the Full Proof

Proposition:

$$
3a \equiv 3b\quad\Rightarrow\quad a \equiv b \qquad (\textrm{mod} \: 4)
$$

Proof:

> *Assume* $3a \equiv 3b\:(\textrm{mod} \: 4)$. *Then, by the definition of modular congruence, we have*
> $3a≡3b(mod4) \mid ⇔4∣(3a - 3b); ⇔4∣3(a - b)$.
> *Now, since $3(a-b)$ is divisible by $4$ and $\textrm{gcd}(4,3)=1$, the difference $(a-b)$ must be divisible by $4$, i.e.,*
> $4 \mid (a-b)$.
> *Therefore, according to the definition of modular congruence, we have*
> $a \equiv b \qquad (\textrm{mod} \: 4)$.

## Proving the Division Properties

**Example:** Let $a,b \in \mathbb{Z}$. Prove that if $2a \equiv 2b \: (\textrm{mod} \: 14)$, then $a \equiv b \: (\textrm{mod} \: 7)$.

**Explanation**

We start by using the definition of modular congruence. Recall that

$$
a \equiv b \: (\textrm{mod} \: n) \quad\Leftrightarrow\quad n \mid (a-b) \quad\Leftrightarrow\quad a-b = nk
$$

for some integer $k$.

In this case, we need to show the following:

$$
2a \equiv 2b \: (\textrm{mod} \: 14) \quad\Rightarrow\quad a \equiv b \: (\textrm{mod} \: 7)
$$

So, we can reason as follows:

> By the definition of modular congruence,
> $2a≡2b(mod14) \mid ⇔14∣(2a - 2b); ⇔14∣2(a - b)$

Next, we use the definition of divisibility:

> By the definition of divisibility in $\mathbb{Z}$, $14 \mid 2(a-b)$ means that there exists an integer $k$ such that
> $2(a-b) = 14k$.

Now, we can cancel out the factor of $2{:}$

> Dividing this equation by $2$, we obtain
> $a-b = 7k$,
> which in turn implies
> $7 \mid (a-b)$.

Finally, we use the definition of modular congruence again:

> Therefore, according to the definition of modular congruence, we have
> $a \equiv b \: (\textrm{mod} \: 7)$.
