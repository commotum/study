## Introduction

Let $f(x,y)$ be a function of two variables and $C$ be a path in the $xy$-plane. Recall that the line integral
$\int\limits_C f(x,y) \, \textrm dx + f(x,y)\,\textrm d y$

can be evaluated by parameterizing $C$ using the parameter $t\in [a,b]$ and then applying a change of variables, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
∫_(C)f(x, y)dx + f(x, y)dy &= ∫_{a}^{b}f(x, y)\frac{dx}{dt}dt + f(x, y)\frac{dy}{dt}dt \\
&= ∫_{a}^{b}(f(x, y)\frac{dx}{dt} + f(x, y)\frac{dy}{dt})dt
\end{aligned}
$$

The hardest part is often finding a suitable parameterization for curve $C$. We'll discuss a number of different cases in this lesson.

## Evaluating Line Integrals With Respect to X and Y Over Segments

**Example:** If the path $C$ is the line segment $\overline{AB}$ traversed from the point $A(0,0)$ to the point $B(1, 1)$, calculate the line integral
$\displaystyle \int\limits_C x^2y\,\mathrm{d}x + xy\,\mathrm{d}y$.

**Explanation**

The position vectors of the endpoints of our line segment are

$$
\mathbf{a} = 0\,\mathbf{i} + 0\,\mathbf{j}
$$

and

$$
\mathbf{b} = 1\,\mathbf{i} + 1\,\mathbf{j}
$$

So, the parametrization of the line segment is given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
r(t) &= a + t(b - a) \\
&= ti + tj
\end{aligned}
$$

where

$$
0 \le t \le 1
$$

So, we have that $x(t) = t$ and $y(t) = t$ along the segment.

The derivative of $\mathbf r(t)$ is given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
r^{′}(t) &= \frac{dx}{dt}i + \frac{dy}{dt}j = i + j
\end{aligned}
$$

We can now evaluate the line integral:

$$
\begin{aligned}
∫_(C)x^{2}ydx + xydy &= ∫_{0}^{1}x^{2}y\frac{dx}{dt}dt + xy\frac{dy}{dt}dt \\
&= ∫_{0}^{1}(x^{2}y\frac{dx}{dt} + xy\frac{dy}{dt})dt \\
&= ∫_{0}^{1}t^{2} \cdot t \cdot 1 + t \cdot t \cdot 1dt \\
&= ∫_{0}^{1}t^{3} + t^{2}dt \\
&= [\frac{t^{4}}{4} + \frac{t^{3}}{3}]_{0}^{1} \\
&= \frac{1}{4} + \frac{1}{3} \\
&= \frac{7}{12}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Evaluating Line Integrals With Respect to X and Y Along Cartesian Curves

**Example:** If the path $C$ is a part of the parabola $y=x^2$ traversed from the point $A(0,0)$ to the point $B(1,1)$, evaluate the line integral
$\displaystyle \int\limits_C (x+y)\,\mathrm{d}x + (x-y)\,\mathrm{d}y$.

**Explanation**

Notice that our curve is given by the explicit Cartesian equation $y = x^2$. As a result, we obtain the following parametrization of the curve:

$$
r(t) = x(t)i + y(t)j = ti + t^{2}j, t ∈ [0, 1]
$$

The derivative of $\mathbf r(t)$ is given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
r^{′}(t) &= \frac{dx}{dt}i + \frac{dy}{dt}j = i + 2tj
\end{aligned}
$$

We can now evaluate the line integral:

$$
\begin{aligned}
∫_(C)(x + y)dx + (x - y)dy &= ∫_{0}^{1}(x + y)\frac{dx}{dt}dt + (x - y)\frac{dy}{dt}dt \\
&= ∫_{0}^{1}((x + y)\frac{dx}{dt} + (x - y)\frac{dy}{dt})dt \\
&= ∫_{0}^{1}(t + t^{2}) \cdot 1 + (t - t^{2}) \cdot 2tdt \\
&= ∫_{0}^{1}t + 3t^{2} - 2t^{3}dt \\
&= [\frac{t^{2}}{2} + t^{3} - \frac{t^{4}}{2}]_{0}^{1} \\
&= [\frac{1}{2} + 1 - \frac{1}{2}] - [0] \\
&= 1
\end{aligned}
$$

## Computing Line Integrals With Respect to X and Y Along Circles and Ellipses

**Example:** Evaluate $\displaystyle \int\limits_C \dfrac{y\,\mathrm{d}x - x\,\mathrm{d}y}{x^2+y^2}$, where $C$ is a part of the circle $x^2+y^2=4$ traversed in the counterclockwise direction from the point $(2,0)$ to the point $\left(0,2\right)$.

![](<../Source/5.3.4. Sums of Line Integrals With Respect to X and Y/Images/3b681a72da2d77baebf38cb87698a572.png>)

**Explanation**

First, notice that a parametrization for the given path is

$$
\begin{aligned}
r(t) &= x(t)i + y(t)j = 2\cos ti + 2\sin tj, t ∈ [0, \frac{π}{2}]
\end{aligned}
$$

The derivative of $\mathbf r(t)$ is

$$
\begin{aligned}
r^{′}(t) &= x^{′}(t)i + y^{′}(t)j =-2\sin ti + 2\cos tj
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, we can calculate the line integral as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
∫_(C)\frac{ydx - xdy}{x^{2} + y^{2}} &= ∫_{0}^{π/2}\frac{y \cdot x^{′} - x \cdot y^{′}}{x^{2} + y^{2}}dt \\
&= ∫_{0}^{π/2}((2\sin t)(-2\sin t) - (2\cos t)(2\cos t))/((2\cos t)^{2} + (2\sin t)^{2}), dt \\
&= ∫_{0}^{π/2}(-4\sin^{2} t - 4\cos^{2} t)/(4(\cos^{2} t + \sin^{2} t))dt \\
&= ∫_{0}^{π/2}(-4(\sin^{2} t + \cos^{2} t))/(4(\cos^{2} t + \sin^{2} t))dt \\
&= - ∫_{0}^{π/2}dt \\
&=-\frac{π}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$
