## Introduction

In this topic, we'll study phase portraits of linear systems
$\begin{bmatrix}x(t) \\ y(t)\end{bmatrix}$
where the eigenvalues of a $2 \times 2$ matrix $A$ are

$$
\lambda_{1,2}=a\pm b\text{i}, \qquad b\neq 0
$$

Let

$$
\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{q}\text{i}
$$

where $\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q} \in \mathbb{R}^2$, be an eigenvector corresponding to the eigenvalue

$$
\lambda = a + b\text{i}
$$

Then, a complex solution of the system is given by

$$
\mathbf{x}_{\text{complex}}(t) = \mathbf{v} e^{\lambda t}
$$

Using Euler's formula, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_{complex}(t) &= ve^{(a + b\textrm{i})t} \\
&= e^{at}(\cos (bt) + \textrm{i}\sin (bt))(p + q\textrm{i}) \\
&= e^{at}(\cos (bt)p - \sin (bt)q) + \textrm{i}e^{at}(\sin (bt)p + \cos (bt)q)
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the general solution of the system is

$$
\mathbf{x}(t) = c_1 e^{at}\big(\cos(bt)\mathbf{p}-\sin(bt)\mathbf{q}\big) + c_2 e^{at}\big(\sin(bt)\mathbf{p}+\cos(bt)\mathbf{q}\big)
$$

Notice that $\cos(bt)$ and $\sin(bt)$ are bounded, so the factor $e^{at}$ controls the long-term behavior:

- If $a>0$, then $e^{at}\to\infty$ as $t\to\infty$. So, trajectories are *unbounded*, and we have a *spiral* that is traversed toward infinity as $t \to \infty$.
- If $a<0$, then $e^{at}\to 0$ as $t\to\infty$. So, trajectories *approach the origin*, and we have a *spiral* that is traversed toward the origin as $t \to \infty$.
- If $a=0$, then $e^{at}=1$ for all $t$. So, trajectories are *bounded*, and we have an *ellipse*.

Let's see some concrete examples.

<a id="systems-with-complex-eigenvalues"></a>
## Systems With Complex Eigenvalues

Consider the system of linear differential equations below.

$$
{x^{′}(t) = \frac{1}{2}x(t) - 4y(t); y^{′}(t) = 4x(t) + \frac{1}{2}y(t)
$$

The eigenvalues of the system's matrix are

$$
\lambda_{1,2} = \dfrac{1}{2}\pm4\text{i}
$$

for which the corresponding eigenvectors are

$$
\mathbf{v}_{1,2} = [\pm \text{i}, 1]^T
$$

respectively. Given that the general solution of the system is
$\begin{bmatrix}x(t) \\ y(t)\end{bmatrix}$
we are interested in sketching the phase portrait of the system of differential equations given above.

If we set $c_1 = 1$ and $c_2 = 0$ in the general solution of the system, we obtain the particular solution:

$$
{x = e^{t/2}\cos (4t); y = e^{t/2}\sin (4t)
$$

Computing the coordinates (rounded to two decimal places) of several points on the curve, we get the table below.

| $t$ | $0$ | $\dfrac{\pi}{16}$ | $\dfrac{\pi}{8}$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $x$ | $1$ | $0.78$ | $0$ |
| $y$ | $0$ | $0.78$ | $1.22$ |

Let's sketch this solution in the phase space.

![](<../Source/8.3.7. Phase Portraits for Linear Systems With Complex Eigenvalues/Images/f61bc7723bc855463e9d8018cd58eab0.png>)

Notice that

$$
\mid \sin(4t) \mid \leq 1
$$

and

$$
\mid \cos(4t) \mid \leq 1
$$

but $e^{t/2} \to \infty$ as $t \to \infty$. Thus, the obtained curve is unbounded. So, we have a spiral that is traversed counterclockwise.

Again, since $e^{t/2} \to \infty$ as $t \to \infty$, the spiral is traversed toward infinity as $t \to \infty$.

The phase portrait of our system looks as follows:

![](<../Source/8.3.7. Phase Portraits for Linear Systems With Complex Eigenvalues/Images/e9090ee7001be8621605ddcbdc6d6958.png>)

<a id="classification-of-equilibria"></a>
## Classification of Equilibria

Finally, let’s summarize what we’ve learned so far.

Again, we are considering a linear system
$\begin{bmatrix}x(t) \\ y(t)\end{bmatrix}$
where the eigenvalues of a $2 \times 2$ matrix $A$ are

$$
\lambda_{1,2}=a\pm bi, \qquad b\neq 0
$$

As for the classification of the equilibrium at the origin, we get the following:

- The type of the curve is determined by the parameter $a{:}$If $a=0$, we have bounded closed curves (ellipses/circles).If $a \neq 0$, we have spirals.
- The stability is determined by the sign of $a{:}$If $a>0$, we have an **unstable spiral** (source).If $a<0$, we have a **stable spiral** (sink).If $a=0$, we have a **center**.

**Note:** The center is a *stable* equilibrium but not *asymptotically stable* since trajectories do not converge to it.

Let's see an example.

---

## Classifying Systems of Linear Differential Equations With Complex Eigenvalues

**Example:** ${x^{′}(t) =-2y(t); y^{′}(t) = 2x(t)$
Consider the system of linear differential equations above. The eigenvalues of the system's matrix are $\lambda=\pm2\text{i}$. Provide an interpretation of the equilibrium point (origin) for the system of differential equations given above.

**Explanation**

Let

$$
\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{q}\text{i}
$$

where $\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q} \in \mathbb{R}^2$, be an eigenvector corresponding to the eigenvalue

$$
\lambda = a + b\text{i}
$$

Then, a complex solution of the system is given by

$$
\mathbf{x}_{\text{complex}}(t) = \mathbf{v} e^{\lambda t}
$$

Using Euler's formula, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_{complex}(t) &= ve^{(a + b\textrm{i})t} \\
&= e^{at}(\cos (bt) + \textrm{i}\sin (bt))(p + q\textrm{i}) \\
&= e^{at}(\cos (bt)p - \sin (bt)q) + \textrm{i}e^{at}(\sin (bt)p + \cos (bt)q)
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the general solution of the system is

$$
\textbf{x}(t) = c_1 e^{at}\big(\cos(bt)\mathbf{p}-\sin(bt)\mathbf{q}\big) + c_2 e^{at}\big(\sin(bt)\mathbf{p}+\cos(bt)\mathbf{q}\big)
$$

where the parts $\big(\cos(bt)\mathbf{p}-\sin(bt)\mathbf{q}\big)$ and $\big(\sin(bt)\mathbf{p}+\cos(bt)\mathbf{q}\big)$ are bounded.

Now, notice that

$$
a = \text{Re}(\lambda) = 0
$$

and

$$
e^{at} = e^{0\cdot t} = 1
$$

for all $t$. Thus, we have an ellipse that curves around the origin.

Therefore, since

$$
\text{Re}(\lambda) = 0
$$

the equilibrium point (origin) is a center.
