## Introduction

Let $\mathbf{x}$ and $\mathbf{y}$ be vectors in a vector space $V$ equipped with an inner product $\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle$.

Two vectors $\mathbf{x}$ and $\mathbf{y}$ are **orthogonal with respect to the inner product** $\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle$ in $V$ if their inner product is zero:

$$
\langle \mathbf{x}, \mathbf{y} \rangle = 0
$$

If the two vectors are orthogonal, then we denote this by $\mathbf{x} \perp \mathbf{y}$. For instance, the vectors

$$
\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 1 \\ 0\end{bmatrix}
$$

in $\mathbb{R}^3$ equipped with the inner product

$$
\langle \mathbf{x}, \mathbf{y} \rangle = x_1y_1 + 2x_2y_2 + x_3y_3
$$

are orthogonal, since

$$
\begin{aligned}
⟨x, y⟩ &= 1 \cdot 2 + 2 \cdot (1 \cdot (-1)) + 0 \cdot 3 \\
&= 2 - 2 + 0 \\
&= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

**Note:** The zero vector $\mathbf{0}$ in a vector space $V$ equipped with an inner product $\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle$ is orthogonal to any vector $\mathbf{x} \in V$ since

$$
\langle \mathbf{0}, \mathbf{x} \rangle = 0
$$

## Determining Components of Orthogonal Vectors in Inner Product Spaces

**Example:** Let $V = \mathbb{R}^3$ be the vector space equipped with the inner product
$\langle \mathbf{x}, \mathbf{y} \rangle = 3 x_1 y_1 + x_2 y_2 + 2 x_3 y_3$.

Given that $\begin{bmatrix}2 \\ 1 \\ 2\end{bmatrix}$ and $\begin{bmatrix}k \\ -2 \\ -1\end{bmatrix}$ are orthogonal with respect to the inner product $\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle$ in $V$, find the value of $k$.

**Explanation**

Since $\mathbf x$ and $\mathbf y$ are orthogonal, their inner product must be zero.

First, we compute the inner product using our definition:

$$
\begin{aligned}
⟨x, y⟩ &= 3x_{1}y_{1} + x_{2}y_{2} + 2x_{3}y_{3} \\
&= 3 \cdot (2 \cdot k) + 1 \cdot (-2) + 2 \cdot (2 \cdot (-1)) \\
&= 6k - 2 - 4 \\
&= 6k - 6
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we set

$$
\langle \mathbf{x}, \mathbf{y} \rangle = 0
$$

and solve for $k$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
⟨x, y⟩ &= 0 \\
6k - 6 &= 0 \\
6k &= 6 \\
k &= 1
\end{aligned}
$$

## Determining Components of Orthogonal Vectors in Inner Product Spaces of Polynomials

**Example:** Let $V=\mathbb{R}_2[t]$ be the vector space of all polynomials of degree at most $2$, equipped with the inner product
$\langle {x(t)}, {y(t)} \rangle = x(0)y(0) + x(1)y(1) + x(-1)y(-1)$.
Given that $p(t) = 1 + t + t^2$ and $q(t) = kt - t^2$ are orthogonal with respect to the inner product $\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle$ in $V$, find the value of $k$.

**Explanation**

Since $p(t)$ and $q(t)$ are orthogonal, their inner product must be zero.

First, we compute the inner product using our definition:

$$
\begin{aligned}
⟨p(t), q(t)⟩ &= p(0)q(0) + p(1)q(1) + p(-1)q(-1) \\
&= (1)(0) + (3)(k - 1) + (1)(-k - 1) \\
&= 0 + (3k - 3) + (-k - 1) \\
&= 2k - 4
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we set

$$
\langle p(t), q(t) \rangle = 0
$$

and solve for $k$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
⟨p(t), q(t)⟩ &= 0 \\
2k - 4 &= 0 \\
2k &= 4 \\
k &= 2
\end{aligned}
$$

## Determining Components of Orthogonal Vectors in Inner Product Spaces of Continuous Functions

**Example:** Let $V=\mathcal{C}\left[0,1\right]$ be the vector space of all functions that are continuous on $\left[0,1\right]$, equipped with the inner product
$\langle x(t),y(t) \rangle = \displaystyle\int_{0}^{1} x(t) y(t) \, \textrm{d}t$.
Given that $x(t) = kt^2+3t$ and $y(t) = t$ are orthogonal with respect to the inner product $\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle$ in $V$, find the value of $k$.

**Explanation**

Since $x(t)$ and $y(t)$ are orthogonal, their inner product must be zero.

First, we compute the inner product using our definition:

$$
\begin{aligned}
⟨x(t), y(t)⟩ &= ∫_{0}^{1}x(t)y(t)dt \\
&= ∫_{0}^{1}(kt^{2} + 3t)(t)dt \\
&= ∫_{0}^{1}(kt^{3} + 3t^{2})dt \\
&= (\frac{kt^{4}}{4} + t^{3}) \mid _{0}^{1} \\
&= \frac{k}{4} + 1
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we set

$$
\langle x(t), y(t) \rangle = 0
$$

and solve for $k$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
⟨x(t), y(t)⟩ &= 0 \\
\frac{k}{4} + 1 &= 0 \\
\frac{k}{4} &= -1 \\
k &= -4
\end{aligned}
$$

## Identifying True Statements About Orthogonal Vectors in Inner Product Spaces

**Example:** Let $V=\mathcal{C}\left[0,2\pi\right]$ be the vector space of all functions that are continuous on $\left[0,2\pi\right]$, equipped with the inner product

$\langle f(t),g(t) \rangle = \displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi} f(t) g(t) \, \textrm{d}t$.

Given the functions $f(t) = \cos t$ and $g(t)=\sin 2t$, which of the following statements are true?

1. $\langle f(t), g(t) \rangle = 0$
2. $\langle f(t), g(t) \rangle = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$
3. $f(t) \perp g(t)$ with respect to the inner product $\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle$ in $V$

*Hint: You may wish to make use of the identity* $\sin\alpha\cos\beta=\dfrac12(\sin(\alpha-\beta)+\sin(\alpha+\beta))$.

**Explanation**

We need to evaluate

$$
\langle f(t), g(t) \rangle = \int_{0}^{2\pi}\cos{t} \sin2t \,\textrm{d}t
$$

We can evaluate this integral using the given identity, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
∫_{0}^{2π}\sin 2t\cos tdt &= \frac{1}{2}∫_{0}^{2π}(\sin (2t - t) + \sin (2t + t))dt \\
&= \frac{1}{2}∫_{0}^{2π}(\sin t + \sin 3t)dt \\
&= \frac{1}{2}(-\cos t - \frac{\cos 3t}{3}) \mid _{0}^{2π} \\
&= \frac{1}{2}[- \cos (2π) - (\cos (6π))/(3)] - \frac{1}{2}[- \cos 0 - \frac{\cos 0}{3}] \\
&= \frac{1}{2}[- 1 - \frac{1}{3}] - \frac{1}{2}[- 1 - \frac{1}{3}] \\
&= \frac{1}{2} \cdot (-\frac{4}{3}) - \frac{1}{2} \cdot (-\frac{4}{3}) \\
&=-\frac{2}{3} + \frac{2}{3} \\
&= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

Let's now examine the statements:

- Statement I is true, while statement II is false.
- Statement III is true. Since the inner product is zero, our vectors are orthogonal.

Therefore, the correct answer is "I and III only."
