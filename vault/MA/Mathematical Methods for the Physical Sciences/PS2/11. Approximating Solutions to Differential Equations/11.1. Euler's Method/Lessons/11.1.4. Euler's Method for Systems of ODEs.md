## Introduction

Consider the following initial value problem:

$$
{y^{′} = 2x + y - z,\begin{vmatrix}y(0) = 1 \\ z^{′} = x + 2y + z,\end{vmatrix}z(0) = 2
$$

Is it possible to approximate solutions to initial value problems involving a system of equations, such as the one above, using Euler's method?

Indeed, for a system of ODEs with initial values,

$$
{y^{′} = f(x, y, z),\begin{vmatrix}y(a) = c, \\ z^{′} = g(x, y, z),\end{vmatrix}z(a) = d
$$

where

$$
y = y(x), z = z(x)
$$

we can use Euler's method to approximate a solution by applying it to each ODE simultaneously, but separately:

$$
{Δy = y^{′} \cdot Δx; Δz = z^{′} \cdot Δx
$$

With this in mind, let's approximate the values of $y(1)$ and $z(1)$ for our initial value problem above using Euler's method with step size

$$
\Delta x = 1
$$

We start by constructing a table that contains all relevant quantities, including slopes and increments for *both* variables.
Then, because the initial conditions are $y(0)=1$ and $z(0)=2$, we place $x=0$, $y=1$, and $z=2$ in the first row.

| $x$ | $y$ | $y'$ | $\Delta y$ | $z$ | $z'$ | $\Delta z$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $0$ | $1$ |  |  | $2$ |  |  |

Next, we compute *both $y'$ and $z'$* according to the given rules using the current values of $x$, $y$, and $z{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
y^{′} &= 2x + y - z \mid z^{′}|= x + 2y + z \\
= 2 \cdot 0 + 1 - 2 &= 0 + 2 \cdot 1 + 2 \\
=-1 &= 4
\end{aligned}
$$

So, we add these to our table.

| $x$ | $y$ | $y'$ | $\Delta y$ | $z$ | $z'$ | $\Delta z$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $0$ | $1$ | $-1$ |  | $2$ | $4$ |  |

Finally, we compute $\Delta y$ and $\Delta z$ using Euler's method:

$$
\begin{aligned}
Δy &= y^{′} \cdot Δx \mid Δz|= z^{′} \cdot Δx \\
= (-1) \cdot 1 &= 4 \cdot 1 \\
=-1 &= 4
\end{aligned}
$$

We add these to our table.

| $x$ | $y$ | $y'$ | $\Delta y$ | $z$ | $z'$ | $\Delta z$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $0$ | $1$ | $-1$ | $-1$ | $2$ | $4$ | $4$ |

To get the new values of $x$, $y$, and $z$ in the *next* row, we add $\Delta x$ to $x$, $\Delta y$ to $y$, and $\Delta z$ to $z$, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_{new} &= x + Δx = 0 + 1 = 1 \\
y_{new} &= y + Δy = 1 + (-1) = 0 \\
z_{new} &= z + Δz = 2 + 4 = 6
\end{aligned}
$$

Adding these values to our table in a new row gives the following:

| $x$ | $y$ | $y'$ | $\Delta y$ | $z$ | $z'$ | $\Delta z$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $0$ | $1$ | $-1$ | $-1$ | $2$ | $4$ | $4$ |
| $1$ | $0$ |  |  | $6$ |  |  |

Therefore, we conclude that

$$
y(1) \approx 0
$$

and

$$
z(1) \approx 6
$$

Notice that this component-wise approach is not specific to Euler's method. Indeed, we could apply any numerical method component-wise to approximate the solution to a system of ODEs!

## Approximating the Solution to a System of ODEs Using Euler's Method With One Step

**Example:** Consider the following initial value problem for the functions $y = y(x), z = z(x){:}$
${y^{′} = 4x + 5y - z,\begin{vmatrix}y(0) = 3 \\ z^{′} = 5x - 4y + 2z,\end{vmatrix}z(0) = 4$
Use Euler's Method with one step to approximate $y(1)$ and $z(1)$.

**Explanation**

For an initial value problem

$$
{y^{′} = f(x, y, z),\begin{vmatrix}y(a) = c, \\ z^{′} = g(x, y, z),\end{vmatrix}z(a) = d
$$

Euler's method approximates the solution by applying the update rule component-wise to each equation. More precisely, the increments on $y$ and $z$ are

$$
{Δy = y^{′} \cdot Δx,; Δz = z^{′} \cdot Δx
$$

The new values are then given by

$$
y_\text{new} = y + \Delta y
$$

and

$$
z_\text{new} = z + \Delta z
$$

Since we want to find the value of $y$ and $z$ at $x=1$, we will use a step size of

$$
\Delta x = 1 - 0 = 1
$$

First, we place the initial condition data in a table.

| $x$ | $y$ | $y'$ | $\Delta y$ | $z$ | $z'$ | $\Delta z$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $0$ | $3$ |  |  | $4$ |  |  |

Next, we compute both $y'$ and $z'$ according to the given rules:

$$
\begin{aligned}
y^{′} &= 4x + 5y - z \\
&= 4 \cdot 0 + 5 \cdot 3 - 4 \\
&= 0 + 15 - 4 \\
&= 11 \\
z^{′} &= 5x - 4y + 2z \\
&= 5 \cdot 0 - 4 \cdot 3 + 2 \cdot 4 \\
&= 0 - 12 + 8 \\
&=-4
\end{aligned}
$$

So, we add these to our table.

| $x$ | $y$ | $y'$ | $\Delta y$ | $z$ | $z'$ | $\Delta z$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $0$ | $3$ | $11$ |  | $4$ | $-4$ |  |

Next, we compute $\Delta y$ and $\Delta z$ using Euler's method:

$$
\begin{aligned}
Δy &= y^{′} \cdot Δx \\
&= 11 \cdot 1 \\
&= 11 \\
Δz &= z^{′} \cdot Δx \\
&= (-4) \cdot 1 \\
&=-4
\end{aligned}
$$

We add these to our table.

| $x$ | $y$ | $y'$ | $\Delta y$ | $z$ | $z'$ | $\Delta z$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $0$ | $3$ | $11$ | $11$ | $4$ | $-4$ | $-4$ |

To get the new values of $x$, $y$, and $z$ in the *next* row, we add $\Delta x$ to $x$, $\Delta y$ to $y$, and $\Delta z$ to $z$, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_{new} &= x + Δx = 0 + 1 = 1 \\
y_{new} &= y + Δy = 3 + 11 = 14 \\
z_{new} &= z + Δz = 4 + (-4) = 0
\end{aligned}
$$

Adding these values to our table in a new row gives the following:

| $x$ | $y$ | $y'$ | $\Delta y$ | $z$ | $z'$ | $\Delta z$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $0$ | $3$ | $11$ | $11$ | $4$ | $-4$ | $-4$ |
| $1$ | $14$ |  |  | $0$ |  |  |

Therefore, we conclude that

$$
y(1) \approx 14
$$

and

$$
z(1) \approx 0
$$

## Approximating the Solution to a System of ODEs Using Euler's Method With Multiple Steps

**Example:** Consider the following initial value problem for the functions $y = y(x), z = z(x){:}$
${y^{′} = x - y + 2z,\begin{vmatrix}y(1) = 1 \\ z^{′} = 3x + y - z,\end{vmatrix}z(1) = 0$
Use Euler's method with two steps of equal size to approximate $y(3)$ and $z(3)$.

**Explanation**

For an initial value problem

$$
{y^{′} = f(x, y, z),\begin{vmatrix}y(a) = c, \\ z^{′} = g(x, y, z),\end{vmatrix}z(a) = d
$$

Euler's method approximates the solution by applying the update rule component-wise to each equation. More precisely, the increments on $y$ and $z$ are

$$
{Δy = y^{′} \cdot Δx,; Δz = z^{′} \cdot Δx
$$

The new values are then given by

$$
y_\text{new} = y + \Delta y
$$

and

$$
z_\text{new} = z + \Delta z
$$

First, note that we are given the values of $y$ and $z$ at $x=1$ and asked to find their values at $x=3$. The distance from $x=1$ to $x=3$ is $2$, and since we are asked to use two steps of equal size, our step size must be

$$
\Delta x = \dfrac{3 - 1}2 = \dfrac22 = 1
$$

Now, let's proceed with Euler's method. We'll start by creating a table that includes the initial condition data $y(1)=1$ and $z(1)=0$.

| $x$ | $y$ | $y'$ | $\Delta y$ | $z$ | $z'$ | $\Delta z$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $1$ | $1$ |  |  | $0$ |  |  |

**Step 1**.

We compute $y'$ and $\Delta y$, $z'$ and $\Delta z$ (using the values of $x$, $y$, and $z$ in the first row), and the values of $x_\text{new}$, $y_\text{new}$, and $z_\text{new}$ using Euler's method:

$$
\begin{aligned}
y^{′} &= x - y + 2z = 1 - 1 + 2 \cdot 0 = 0 \\
z^{′} &= 3x + y - z = 3 \cdot 1 + 1 - 0 = 4 \\
Δy &= y^{′} \cdot Δx = 0 \cdot 1 = 0 \\
Δz &= z^{′} \cdot Δx = 4 \cdot 1 = 4 \\
x_{new} &= x + Δx = 1 + 1 = 2 \\
y_{new} &= y + Δy = 1 + 0 = 1 \\
z_{new} &= z + Δz = 0 + 4 = 4
\end{aligned}
$$

We add these values to our table.

| $x$ | $y$ | $y'$ | $\Delta y$ | $z$ | $z'$ | $\Delta z$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $1$ | $1$ | $0$ | $0$ | $0$ | $4$ | $4$ |
| $2$ | $1$ |  |  | $4$ |  |  |

**Step 2**.

We compute $y'$ and $\Delta y$, $z'$ and $\Delta z$ (using the values of $x$, $y$, and $z$ in the second row), and the values of $x_\text{new}$, $y_\text{new}$, and $z_\text{new}$ using Euler's method:

$$
\begin{aligned}
y^{′} &= x - y + 2z = 2 - 1 + 2 \cdot 4 = 9 \\
z^{′} &= 3x + y - z = 3 \cdot 2 + 1 - 4 = 3 \\
Δy &= y^{′} \cdot Δx = 9 \cdot 1 = 9 \\
Δz &= z^{′} \cdot Δx = 3 \cdot 1 = 3 \\
x_{new} &= x + Δx = 2 + 1 = 3 \\
y_{new} &= y + Δy = 1 + 9 = 10 \\
z_{new} &= z + Δz = 4 + 3 = 7
\end{aligned}
$$

We add these values to our table.

| $x$ | $y$ | $y'$ | $\Delta y$ | $z$ | $z'$ | $\Delta z$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $1$ | $1$ | $0$ | $0$ | $0$ | $4$ | $4$ |
| $2$ | $1$ | $9$ | $9$ | $4$ | $3$ | $3$ |
| $3$ | $10$ |  |  | $7$ |  |  |

Therefore, we conclude that

$$
y(3) \approx 10
$$

and

$$
z(3) \approx 7
$$

## Euler's Method for Larger Systems of ODEs

We can apply Euler's method to a system of *any number* of simultaneous first-order ODEs. That is, for an initial value problem

$$
{y_{1}^{′} = f_{1}(x, y_{1}, y_{2}, …, y_{m}),\begin{vmatrix}y_{1}(a) = c_{1}, \\ y_{2}^{′} = f_{2}(x, y_{1}, y_{2}, …, y_{m}),\end{vmatrix}y_{2}(a) = c_{2},; ⋮; y_{m}^{′} = f_{m}(x, y_{1}, y_{2}, …, y_{m}), \mid y_{m}(a) = c_{m}
$$

Euler's method approximates a solution by applying the update rule component-wise. The increments are

$$
{Δy_{1} = y_{1}^{′} \cdot Δx; Δy_{2} = y_{2}^{′} \cdot Δx; ⋮; Δy_{m} = y_{m}^{′} \cdot Δx
$$

The new values are then given by

$$
y_{i,\text{new}} = y_i + \Delta y_i
$$

for

$$
i=1,2,\ldots,m
$$

For convenience, we can express large systems of ODEs in vector form. Let
$\begin{bmatrix}y_{1} \\ y_{2} \\ ⋮ \\ y_{m}\end{bmatrix}$

Then, the initial value problem

$$
\mathbf{y}' = \mathbf{f}(x, \mathbf{y}), \qquad \mathbf{y}(a) = \mathbf{c}
$$

has Euler update

$$
\Delta \mathbf{y} = \mathbf{y}' \cdot \Delta x \qquad \mathbf{y}_\text{new} = \mathbf{y} + \Delta \mathbf{y}
$$

Next, we will apply this vector formulation to a specific numerical example.

## Approximating the Solution to a System of Three ODEs Using Euler's Method

**Example:** Consider the following initial value problem for the functions $y = y(x), z = z(x), w=w(x){:}$
${y^{′} = 2y + z + x,\begin{vmatrix}y(0) = 1 \\ z^{′} = y + z + 2x,\end{vmatrix}z(0) = 0; w^{′} = wy - x, \mid w(0) = 2$

Approximate values of $y$, $z$ and $w$ at $x = 1$ using Euler's method if the step size is $\Delta x = 1$.

**Explanation**

Euler's method approximates the solution of an initial value problem containing a system of ODEs by applying the update rule component-wise to each equation.

First, because the initial conditions are $y(0)=1$, $z(0)=0$, and $w(0)=2$, we place $y=1$, $z=0$, and $w=2$ in the table.

| $x$ | $y$ | $y'$ | $\Delta y$ | $z$ | $z'$ | $\Delta z$ | $w$ | $w'$ | $\Delta w$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $0$ | $1$ |  |  | $0$ |  |  | $2$ |  |  |

Next, we compute $y', z'$, and $w'$ according to the given rules:

$$
\begin{aligned}
y^{′} &= 2y + z + x \\
&= 2 \cdot 1 + 0 + 0 \\
&= 2 \\
z^{′} &= y + z + 2x \\
&= 1 + 0 + 2 \cdot 0 \\
&= 1 \\
w^{′} &= wy - x \\
&= 2 \cdot 1 - 0 \\
&= 2
\end{aligned}
$$

So, we add these to our table.

| $x$ | $y$ | $y'$ | $\Delta y$ | $z$ | $z'$ | $\Delta z$ | $w$ | $w'$ | $\Delta w$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $0$ | $1$ | $2$ |  | $0$ | $1$ |  | $2$ | $2$ |  |

Finally, we compute $\Delta y$, $\Delta z$, and $\Delta w$ using Euler's method:

$$
\begin{aligned}
Δy &= y^{′} \cdot Δx = 2 \cdot 1 = 2 \\
Δz &= z^{′} \cdot Δx = 1 \cdot 1 = 1 \\
Δw &= w^{′} \cdot Δx = 2 \cdot 1 = 2
\end{aligned}
$$

We add these to our table.

| $x$ | $y$ | $y'$ | $\Delta y$ | $z$ | $z'$ | $\Delta z$ | $w$ | $w'$ | $\Delta w$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $0$ | $1$ | $2$ | $\bbox[4pt,border: 1px solid gainsboro]{2}$ | $0$ | $1$ | $\bbox[4pt,border: 1px solid gainsboro]{1}$ | $2$ | $2$ | $\bbox[4pt,border: 1px solid gainsboro]{2}$ |

To get the new values of $x$, $y$, $z$, and $w$ in the *next* row, we add $\Delta x$ to $x$, $\Delta y$ to $y$, $\Delta z$ to $z$, and $\Delta w$ to $w$, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_{new} &= x + Δx = 0 + 1 = 1 \\
y_{new} &= y + Δy = 1 + 2 = 3 \\
z_{new} &= z + Δz = 0 + 1 = 1 \\
w_{new} &= w + Δw = 2 + 2 = 4
\end{aligned}
$$

Adding these values to our table in a new row gives the following:

| $x$ | $y$ | $y'$ | $\Delta y$ | $z$ | $z'$ | $\Delta z$ | $w$ | $w'$ | $\Delta w$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $0$ | $1$ | $2$ | $\bbox[4pt,border: 1px solid gainsboro]{2}$ | $0$ | $1$ | $\bbox[4pt,border: 1px solid gainsboro]{1}$ | $2$ | $2$ | $\bbox[4pt,border: 1px solid gainsboro]{2}$ |
| $1$ | $3$ |  |  | $1$ |  |  | $4$ |  |  |

Therefore, we conclude that

$$
y(1) \approx \bbox[4pt,border: 1px solid gainsboro]{3}
$$

$$
z(1) \approx \bbox[4pt,border: 1px solid gainsboro]{1}
$$

and

$$
w(1) \approx \bbox[4pt,border: 1px solid gainsboro]{4}
$$
