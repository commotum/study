## Introduction

Suppose we want to evaluate the double integral

$\displaystyle \iint\limits_{D} 2(x+y)\,\textrm{d}A$,
where $D$ is the finite region bounded by the lines

$$
x=0, \, y=1, \, y=2, \, y=3-x
$$

and $y=x$. How do we proceed?

To evaluate an integral like this, we carry out the following steps:

**Step 1.** Sketch the region $D$. This allows us to determine the precise set-up of our integral.

In this example, we obtain the following:

![](<MA/Mathematical Methods for the Physical Sciences/PS1/8. Multiple Integrals/8.1. Double Integrals/Source/8.1.7. Double Integrals Over Partitioned Regions/Images/a64f0d6e7fc9604a146826a9c4d44ce3.png>)

**Step 2.** Split (if necessary) the region $D$ into several type I or II regions.

From the diagram above, we see that our region $D$ can be partitioned into two type I regions $D_1$ and $D_2$, as follows.

![](<MA/Mathematical Methods for the Physical Sciences/PS1/8. Multiple Integrals/8.1. Double Integrals/Source/8.1.7. Double Integrals Over Partitioned Regions/Images/00452a48ba8d7e85e9bd6854e50e5882.png>)

So, we get

$$
D_1 = \big\{(x,y) \:: \: 0 \leq x \leq 1, \quad 1 \leq y \leq 2\big\}
$$

and

$$
D_2= \{(x,y)\:: \: 1 \leq x \leq 3/2, \quad x \leq y \leq 3-x\}
$$

**Step 3.** Set up and evaluate the integral using the additivity property of double integrals. We recall the following fact:

> *If $D$ is the union of two sub-regions, $D_1, D_2$, that don't overlap then,*
> $\iint\limits_{D} f(x,y)\ \textrm d A = \iint\limits_{D_1} f(x,y)\ \textrm d A + \iint\limits_{D_2} f(x,y)\ \textrm d A$.

So, finally, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
∬_(D)2(x + y)dA &= ∬_(D_{1})2(x + y)dA + ∬_(D_{2})2(x + y)dA \\
&= ∫_{0}^{1}∫_{1}^{2}2(x + y)dydx + ∫_{1}^{3/2}∫_{x}^{3 - x}2(x + y)dydx
\end{aligned}
$$

## Representing a Double Integral as a Repeated Integral Over a Union of Type I Regions

**Example:** Consider the finite region $D$ in the $xy$-plane enclosed by the curves $y= 2^x$, $y=\sqrt{x}$ and lines $x=0$ and $y=2$, as shown in the picture. Express the double integral of a function $f(x,y)$ over the region $D$ as a sum of repeated integrals over type I regions.

![](<MA/Mathematical Methods for the Physical Sciences/PS1/8. Multiple Integrals/8.1. Double Integrals/Source/8.1.7. Double Integrals Over Partitioned Regions/Images/44c00a18090dd9acfb716d4de0839aed.png>)

**Explanation**

The region $D$ can be partitioned into two type I regions $D_1$ and $D_2$, as shown below.

![](<MA/Mathematical Methods for the Physical Sciences/PS1/8. Multiple Integrals/8.1. Double Integrals/Source/8.1.7. Double Integrals Over Partitioned Regions/Images/dfed9d77548799c30e813d5415b96807.png>)

As a result, we get

$$
D_1 = \big\{(x,y) \:: \: 0 \leq x \leq 1, \quad \sqrt x \leq y \leq 2^x \big\}
$$

and

$$
D_2 = \big\{(x,y) \:: \: 1 \leq x \leq 4, \quad \sqrt x \leq y \leq 2 \big\}
$$

Therefore, using the additivity property of double integrals, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
∬_(D)f(x, y)dA &= ∬_(D_{1})f(x, y)dA + ∬_(D_{2})f(x, y)dA \\
&= ∫_{0}^{1}∫_{\sqrt{x}}^{2^{x}}f(x, y)dydx + ∫_{1}^{4}∫_{\sqrt{x}}^{2}f(x, y)dydx
\end{aligned}
$$

## Representing a Double Integral as a Repeated Integral Over a Union of Type II Regions

**Example:** Consider the finite region $D$ in the $xy$-plane enclosed by the curve $y=2+\ln x$, and the lines $y=1$, $x=1$, and $x=e$, as shown in the picture. Express the double integral of the function $f(x,y)$ over the region $D$ as a sum of repeated integrals over type II regions.

![](<MA/Mathematical Methods for the Physical Sciences/PS1/8. Multiple Integrals/8.1. Double Integrals/Source/8.1.7. Double Integrals Over Partitioned Regions/Images/90badcdff8d1723816783ecee432d5a9.png>)

**Explanation**

First, notice that:

$$
y = 2 + \ln x \mid ⟹x = e^{y - 2}
$$

The region $D$ can be partitioned into two type II regions $D_1$ and $D_2$, as shown below.

![](<MA/Mathematical Methods for the Physical Sciences/PS1/8. Multiple Integrals/8.1. Double Integrals/Source/8.1.7. Double Integrals Over Partitioned Regions/Images/97aa046fce141be1472a9103f7079cae.png>)

As a result, we get

$$
D_1 = \bigg\{(x,y) \:: \: 1 \leq y \leq 2, \quad 1 \leq x \leq e \bigg\}
$$

and

$$
D_2 = \bigg\{(x,y) \:: \: 2 \leq y \leq 3, \quad e^{y-2} \leq x \leq e\bigg\}
$$

Therefore, using the additivity property of double integrals, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
∬_(D)f(x, y)dA &= ∬_(D_{1})f(x, y)dA + ∬_(D_{2})f(x, y)dA \\
&= ∫_{1}^{2}∫_{1}^{e}f(x, y)dxdy + ∫_{2}^{3}∫_{e^{y - 2}}^{e}f(x, y)dxdy
\end{aligned}
$$

## Evaluating a Double Integral Over a Partitioned Region

**Example:** The region $D$ is enclosed by the curves $x=2y^3-2y$ and $x=y^3-4y$, and the $y$-axis, as shown in the picture. Evaluate the double integral $\displaystyle \iint\limits_{D} \mathrm{d}A$.

![](<MA/Mathematical Methods for the Physical Sciences/PS1/8. Multiple Integrals/8.1. Double Integrals/Source/8.1.7. Double Integrals Over Partitioned Regions/Images/ad479776abe1b64937bf9c79c413a827.png>)

**Explanation**

The region $D$ can be partitioned into two type II regions $D_1$ and $D_2$, as shown below.

![](<MA/Mathematical Methods for the Physical Sciences/PS1/8. Multiple Integrals/8.1. Double Integrals/Source/8.1.7. Double Integrals Over Partitioned Regions/Images/f0e34c7f4bb3ec1fc5266af37fe73361.png>)

As a result, we get

$$
D_1 = \bigg\{(x,y) \:: \: -2 \leq y \leq 0, \quad 0 \leq x \leq y^3\!-\!4y \bigg\}
$$

and

$$
D_2 = \bigg\{(x,y) \:: \: 0 \leq y \leq 1, \quad 2y^3\!-\!2y \leq x \leq 0 \bigg\}
$$

Therefore, using the additivity property of double integrals, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= ∬_(D)dA \\
&= ∬_(D_{1})dA + ∬_(D_{2})dA \\
&= ∫_{-2}^{0}∫_{0}^{y^{3} - 4y}dxdy + ∫_{0}^{1}∫_{2y^{3} - 2y}^{0}dxdy \\
&= ∫_{-2}^{0}[x]_{0}^{y^{3} - 4y}dy + ∫_{0}^{1}[x]_{2y^{3} - 2y}^{0}dy \\
&= ∫_{-2}^{0}(y^{3} - 4y)dy + ∫_{0}^{1}(2y - 2y^{3})dy \\
&= [\frac{y^{4}}{4} - 2y^{2}]_{-2}^{0} + [y^{2} - \frac{y^{4}}{2}]_{0}^{1} \\
&= 4 + \frac{1}{2} \\
&= \frac{9}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$
