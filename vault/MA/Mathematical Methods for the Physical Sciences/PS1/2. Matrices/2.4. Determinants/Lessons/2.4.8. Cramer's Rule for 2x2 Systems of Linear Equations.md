## Introduction

**Cramer's Rule** is a method for solving a linear system of equations using determinants.

Suppose we have a square system of linear equations

$$
{a_{11}x + a_{12}y = b_{1}; a_{21}x + a_{22}y = b_{2}
$$

where $\begin{vmatrix}a_{11} & a_{12} \\ a_{21} & a_{22}\end{vmatrix}$ Then Cramer's rule states that

$$
x = \dfrac{D_x}{D}, \qquad y = \dfrac{D_y}{D}
$$

is the unique solution of the system, where
$\begin{vmatrix}b_{1} & a_{12} \\ b_{2} & a_{22}\end{vmatrix}$

For example, let's use Cramer's rule to solve the following system of equations:

$$
{3x- 2y =-1; - 1x + 2y = 3
$$

We start by finding the **determinant of the coefficient matrix**:

$$
\begin{aligned}
D &= \begin{vmatrix}3 & -2 \\ -1 & 2\end{vmatrix} \\
&= 3 \cdot 2 - (-1) \cdot (-2) \\
&= 4
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we compute $D_x$ and $D_y$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
D_{x} &= \begin{vmatrix}-1 & -2 \\ 3 & 2\end{vmatrix} \\
&= (-1) \cdot 2 - 3 \cdot (-2) \\
&= 4 \\
D_{y} &= \begin{vmatrix}3 & -1 \\ -1 & 3\end{vmatrix} \\
&= 3 \cdot 3 - (-1) \cdot (-1) \\
&= 8
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, the solution to the system is given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
x &= \frac{D_{x}}{D} = \frac{4}{4} = 1, \\
y &= \frac{D_{y}}{D} = \frac{8}{4} = 2
\end{aligned}
$$

It is common to write the solutions to a linear system as a vector. So, in this case, we have

$$
\left< x,y \right> = \left< 1,2 \right>
$$

## Identifying the Parts of Cramer's Rule Formula

**Example:** Consider the system of linear equations
${x - y = 2; 2x + y = 1$.
According to Cramer's rule, the unknown $x$ can be written as
$\begin{vmatrix}1 & -1 \\ 2 & 1\end{vmatrix}$
Express $D_x$ in terms of a $2 \times 2$ determinant.

**Explanation**

We are given the system

$$
{x - y = 2; 2x + y = 1
$$

The determinant of the coefficient matrix is

$$
\begin{aligned}
D &= \begin{vmatrix}1 & -1 \\ 2 & 1\end{vmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, to construct $D_x$, we replace the first column of the coefficient matrix by the right-hand side column of the given system:

$$
\begin{aligned}
D_{x} &= \begin{vmatrix}2 & -1 \\ 1 & 1\end{vmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Expressing an Unknown in Terms of 2x2 Determinants

**Example:** Consider the system of equations
${5x + 2y =-1; - 3x + 3y = 9$.
Express $y$ in terms of $2 \times 2$ determinants.

**Explanation**

We are given the system

$$
{5x + 2y =-1; - 3x + 3y = 9
$$

The determinant of the coefficient matrix is

$$
\begin{aligned}
D &= \begin{vmatrix}5 & 2 \\ -3 & 3\end{vmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, to construct $D_y$, we replace the second column of the coefficient matrix by the right-hand side column of the given system:

$$
\begin{aligned}
D_{y} &= \begin{vmatrix}5 & -1 \\ -3 & 9\end{vmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, according to Cramer's rule, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
y &= \frac{D_{y}}{D} = \frac{\begin{vmatrix}5 & -1 \\ -3 & 9\end{vmatrix}}{\begin{vmatrix}5 & 2 \\ -3 & 3\end{vmatrix}}
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="solving-for-a-coefficient-in-a-2x2-linear-system-using-cramers-rule"></a>
## Solving for a Coefficient in a 2x2 Linear System Using Cramer's Rule

**Example:** Let $\langle x,3 \rangle$ be the solution of the system of equations below.
${5x - 2y = 4; x + ky = 8$
Which of the following equations could be solved to find the value of $k$?

1. $\begin{vmatrix}5 & -2 \\ 1 & k\end{vmatrix}$
2. $\begin{vmatrix}5 & -2 \\ 1 & k\end{vmatrix}$
3. $\begin{vmatrix}5 & -2 \\ 1 & k\end{vmatrix}$

**Explanation**

The determinant of the coefficient matrix is

$$
\begin{aligned}
D &= \begin{vmatrix}5 & -2 \\ 1 & k\end{vmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

According to Cramer's rule, the value of $y$ is given by

$$
y=\dfrac{D_y}{D}
$$

where

$$
\begin{aligned}
D_{y} &= \begin{vmatrix}5 & 4 \\ 1 & 8\end{vmatrix} \\
&= 5 \cdot 8 - 4 \cdot 1 \\
&= 36
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore:

$$
\begin{aligned}
y &= \frac{D_{y}}{D} \\
3 &= \frac{36}{\begin{vmatrix}5 & -2 \\ 1 & k\end{vmatrix}} \\
\begin{vmatrix}5 & -2 \\ 1 & k\end{vmatrix} &= 12
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the correct answer is "II only".
