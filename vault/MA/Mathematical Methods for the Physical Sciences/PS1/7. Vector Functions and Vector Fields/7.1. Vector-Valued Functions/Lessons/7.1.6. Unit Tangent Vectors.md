## Introduction

Consider the curve $C$ that's defined parametrically by

$$
\mathbf f(t) = \left\langle t, \: t^3 +1 \right\rangle, \qquad t \in (-\infty, \infty)
$$

The point $P$ corresponding to $t=1$ on the curve has coordinates $(1,2)$. We know that a tangent vector to $C$ at $P$ is given by $\mathbf f'(1)$, as shown below.

![](<MA/Mathematical Methods for the Physical Sciences/PS1/7. Vector Functions and Vector Fields/7.1. Vector-Valued Functions/Source/7.1.6. Unit Tangent Vectors/Images/c1aacd1e97e202b7bd588e160e7cb4bf.png>)

We compute $\mathbf f'(t)$ as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
f^{′}(t) &= ⟨\frac{d}{dt}(t), \frac{d}{dt}(t^{3})⟩ \\
&= ⟨1, 3t^{2}⟩
\end{aligned}
$$

Substituting $t=1$ into the above gives a tangent vector to $C$ at $P$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
f^{′}(1) &= ⟨1, 3(1)^{2}⟩ \\
&= ⟨1, 3⟩
\end{aligned}
$$

As we'll see, it's more helpful to work with unit vectors. Notice that $\mathbf f'(1)$ is not a unit vector because

$$
\begin{aligned}
‖f^{′}(1)‖ &= \sqrt{1^{2} + 3^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{10}
\end{aligned}
$$

However, we can create a unit vector, which we'll call $\mathbf{T}'(1)$, by dividing $\mathbf{f}'(1)$ by its magnitude:

$$
\begin{aligned}
T(1) &= (f^{′}(1))/(‖f^{′}(1)‖) \\
&= \frac{⟨1, 3⟩}{\sqrt{10}} \\
&= ⟨\frac{\sqrt{10}}{10}, \frac{3\sqrt{10}}{10}⟩
\end{aligned}
$$

In general, for a vector valued function $\mathbf f(t)$, the **unit tangent vector** $\mathbf T(t)$ to the curve traced by $\mathbf f$ can be computed using the following formula:

$$
\mathbf T(t)= \dfrac{\mathbf f'(t)}{\|\mathbf f'(t) \|}
$$

**Note:** When we take a vector $\mathbf v$ and creating a unit vector that's parallel to it, we say that we have **normalized** the vector $\mathbf v$.

## Finding the Unit Tangent Vector at an Arbitrary Point

**Example:** Find the unit tangent vector to the curve $\mathbf r(t)=\left\langle \sin^3{t}, \:\cos^3{t} \right\rangle$ at an arbitrary point where $0 < t < \dfrac{\pi}{2}$.

**Explanation**

For a curve

$$
\mathbf r(t)= x(t) \,\mathbf i + y(t) \,\mathbf j
$$

the unit tangent vector $\mathbf T(t)$ at the point $P(x(t),y(t))$ is given by

$$
\mathbf T(t)=\dfrac{\mathbf r'(t)}{\left\| \mathbf r'(t) \right\|}
$$

First, we find the derivative of $\mathbf r(t)$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
r^{′}(t) &= ⟨\frac{d}{dt}(\sin^{3} t), \frac{d}{dt}(\cos^{3} t)⟩ \\
&= ⟨3\sin^{2} t\cos t, 3\cos^{2} t(-\sin t)⟩ \\
&= 3\sin t\cos t⟨\sin t,-\cos t⟩
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we calculate the magnitude of $\mathbf r'(t)$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
‖r^{′}(t)‖ &= \begin{vmatrix}3\sin t\cos t & \sqrt{\sin^{2} t + (-\cos t)^{2}} \\ = & 3\sin t\cos t\end{vmatrix}\sqrt{\sin^{2} t + \cos^{2} t} \\
&= \mid 3\sin t\cos t \mid \sqrt{1} \\
&= 3\sin t\cos t
\end{aligned}
$$

In the last step, we were able to remove the absolute value bars because $\sin{t}>0$ and $\cos{t}>0$ for

$$
0 < t < \dfrac{\pi}{2}
$$

Finally, we normalize $\mathbf r'(t)$ and obtain the unit tangent vector to the curve:

$$
\begin{aligned}
T(t) &= (r^{′}(t))/(‖r^{′}(t)‖) \\
&= \frac{3\sin t\cos t⟨\sin t,-\cos t⟩}{3\sin t\cos t} \\
&= ⟨\sin t,-\cos t⟩
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding the Unit Tangent Vector at a Given Point

**Example:** What is the unit tangent vector to the curve $\mathbf r(t)=\mathbf i+3t\,\mathbf j+2t^2\,\mathbf k$ at the point $P(1,3,2)$?

**Explanation**

For a curve

$$
\mathbf r(t)= x(t) \,\mathbf j + y(t)\,\mathbf j + z(t)\,\mathbf k
$$

the unit tangent vector $\mathbf T(t)$ at the point $P(x(t),y(t),z(t))$ is given by

$$
\mathbf T(t)=\dfrac{\mathbf r'(t)}{\left\| \mathbf r'(t) \right\|}
$$

First, we find the value of $t$ that corresponds to the point $P(1,3,2)$ on the given curve. We know that

$$
\begin{aligned}
r(t) &= i + 3j + 2k \\
i + 3tj + 2t^{2}k &= i + 3j + 2k
\end{aligned}
$$

In the above equation, the first component in each of the two vectors is equal to $1$. Equating the other two components gives the system

$$
{3t = 3; 2t^{2} = 2
$$

From the first equation, $t=1$. This value satisfies the second equation too. Therefore, the point $P(1,3,2)$ is at the tip of $\mathbf r(1)$.

Now, we calculate $\mathbf r'(t)$ and evaluate it at $t=1$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
r^{′}(t) &= \frac{d}{dt}(1)i + \frac{d}{dt}(3t)j + \frac{d}{dt}(2t^{2})k \\
&= 0i + 3j + 4tk \\
&= 3j + 4tk \\
r^{′}(1) &= 3j + 4(1)k \\
&= 3j + 4k
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we calculate the magnitude of $\mathbf r'(1)$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
‖r^{′}(1)‖ &= \sqrt{0^{2} + 3^{2} + 4^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{25} \\
&= 5
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we normalize $\mathbf r'(1)$ and obtain the unit tangent vector to the given curve at $P(1,3,2)$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
T(1) &= (r^{′}(1))/(‖r^{′}(1)‖) \\
&= \frac{3j + 4k}{5} \\
&= \frac{3}{5}j + \frac{4}{5}k
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding Unit Tangent Vectors Using the Rules of Differentiation For Vector Functions

**Example:** Find the unit tangent vector to the curve $\mathbf r(t)=e^{2t}\mathbf p(t)$ at the point where $t=0$, if $\mathbf p(0)=\mathbf i-\,\mathbf j-\mathbf k$ and $\mathbf p'(0)=\mathbf i - \mathbf j+ 2\mathbf k$.

**Explanation**

For a curve

$$
\mathbf r(t)=x(t)\mathbf i+y(t)\mathbf j+z(t)\mathbf k
$$

the unit tangent vector $\mathbf T(t)$ at the point $P(x(t),y(t),z(t))$ is given by

$$
\mathbf T(t)=\dfrac{\mathbf r'(t)}{\left\| \mathbf r'(t) \right\|}
$$

First, we find $\mathbf r'(t)$ and evaluate it at $t=0$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
r^{′}(t) &= \frac{d}{dt}(e^{2t}p(t)) \\
&= \frac{d}{dt}(e^{2t})p(t) + e^{2t}\frac{d}{dt}(p(t)) \\
&= 2e^{2t}p(t) + e^{2t}p^{′}(t) \\
r^{′}(0) &= 2e^{2(0)}p(0) + e^{2(0)}p^{′}(0) \\
&= 2(i - j - k) + 1(i - j + 2k) \\
&= 3i - 3j
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we calculate the magnitude of $\mathbf r'(0)$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
‖r^{′}(0)‖ &= \sqrt{(3)^{2} + (-3)^{2} + (0)^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{2 \cdot 3^{2}} \\
&= 3\sqrt{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we normalize $\mathbf r'(0)$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
T(0) &= (r^{′}(0))/(‖r^{′}(0)‖) \\
&= \frac{1}{3\sqrt{2}}(3i - 3j) \\
&= \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(i - j) \\
&= \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}(i - j)
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the unit tangent vector to the curve at the point that corresponds to $t=0$ is

$$
\mathbf T(0) = \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}(\mathbf i - \mathbf j)
$$
