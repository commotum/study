## Introduction

Consider the graph of $y=\sec(x)$ as shown below. We will use this graph to work out limits involving $\sec(x)$.

![](<../Source/11.3.7. Limits of Reciprocal Trigonometric Functions/Images/aa65e19553ec4160e52f5737f006dd09.png>)

From the graph, we see that for any point that is not an asymptote, the limit of $\sec(x)$ is equal to the value of $\sec(x)$ at that point.

$$
lim_(x → a)\sec (x) = \sec (a), a \ne ± \frac{π}{2}, ± \frac{3π}{2}, …
$$

At each asymptote, the one-sided infinite limits exist. For example:

$$
\lim_\limits{x\to (\pi/2)^{-}}\sec(x)=\infty,\qquad \lim_\limits{x\to (\pi/2)^{+}}\sec(x)=-\infty
$$

However, because the left and right-sided limits are not equal, the overall limit at each asymptote does not exist.

$$
\lim_\limits {x \to a} \sec \left(x\right)=\text{DNE}, \qquad a= \pm\dfrac{\pi}{2}, \pm \dfrac{3\pi}{2},\ldots
$$

<a id="computing-the-limit-of-a-secant-function"></a>
## Computing the Limit of a Secant Function

**Example:** Calculate $\lim_\limits {x\to (\pi/4)} 2\sec \left(2x\right)$.

**Explanation**

First, let's attempt to evaluate the limit by direct substitution:

$$
\begin{aligned}
lim_(x → (π/4))2\sec (2x) &= 2\sec (2 \cdot (\frac{π}{4})) \\
&= 2\sec (\frac{π}{2}) \\
&= (2)/(\cos (\frac{π}{2})) \\
&= \frac{2}{0}
\end{aligned}
$$

which is undefined.

Therefore, to find the limit, let's sketch the graph of

$$
y = 2\sec\left(2x\right)
$$

![](<../Source/11.3.7. Limits of Reciprocal Trigonometric Functions/Images/eb3646314d2530b73d73364a405b3377.png>)

As $x$ approaches

$$
\dfrac\pi {4}
$$

from the left, the values of $y=2\sec(2x)$ are positive and grow without bound. Therefore

$$
\lim_\limits {x \to (\pi/4)^-} 2\sec(2x) = \infty
$$

Similarly, as $x$ approaches

$$
\dfrac\pi {4}
$$

from the right, the values of

$$
y=2\sec (2x)
$$

are negative and decrease without bound. Therefore

$$
\lim_\limits {x \to (\pi/4)^+} 2\sec (2x) = -\infty
$$

Therefore, since the two limits are not equal, we conclude that

$$
\lim_\limits {x \to (\pi/4)} 2\sec (2x) = \text{DNE}
$$

<a id="computing-the-limit-of-a-cosecant-function"></a>
## Computing the Limit of a Cosecant Function

**Example:** Calculate $\lim_\limits {x \to \pi} \csc x$

**Explanation**

First, let's attempt to evaluate the limit by direct substitution:

$$
\begin{aligned}
lim_(x → π)\csc x &= \csc π \\
&= \frac{1}{\sin π} \\
&= \frac{1}{0}
\end{aligned}
$$

which is undefined.

Therefore, to find the limit, let's sketch the graph of

$$
y = \csc\left(x\right)
$$

![](<../Source/11.3.7. Limits of Reciprocal Trigonometric Functions/Images/94ccc14e47fec101b082c73f6ba748e6.png>)

As $x$ approaches $\pi$ from the left, the values of $y=\csc x$ are positive and grow without bound.

Therefore

$$
\lim_\limits {x \to \pi^-}\csc x = \infty
$$

However, as $x$ approaches $\pi$ from the right, the values of $y=\csc x$ are negative and decrease without bound.Therefore,

$$
\lim_\limits {x \to \pi^+}\csc x = -\infty
$$

Since the two limits are not equal, we conclude that

$$
\lim_\limits {x \to \pi}\csc x = \text{DNE}
$$

<a id="computing-the-limit-of-cotangent-function"></a>
## Computing the Limit of Cotangent Function

**Example:** Calculate $\lim_\limits {x \to \pi/3} \cot \left(3x\right)$.

**Explanation**

First, let's attempt to evaluate the limit by direct substitution:

$$
\begin{aligned}
lim_(x → π/3)\cot (3x) &= \cot (3 \cdot \frac{π}{3}) \\
&= \cot (π) \\
&= (\cos (π))/(\sin (π)) \\
&= \frac{-1}{0}
\end{aligned}
$$

which is undefined.

Therefore, to find the limit, let's sketch the graph of

$$
y = \cot\left(3x\right)
$$

![](<../Source/11.3.7. Limits of Reciprocal Trigonometric Functions/Images/3b037fdf23eee2b888f6ae78dbbabc72.png>)

As $x$ approaches

$$
\dfrac\pi 3
$$

from the left, the values of $y=\cot(3x)$ are negative and decrease without bound. Therefore

$$
\lim_\limits {x \to (\pi/3)^-} \cot(3x) = -\infty
$$

As $x$ approaches

$$
\dfrac\pi 3
$$

from the right, the values of $y=\cot(3x)$ are positive and increase without bound. Therefore

$$
\lim_\limits {x \to (\pi/3)^+} \cot(3x) = +\infty
$$

Since the left and right-sided limits are not equal, we get

$$
\lim_\limits {x \to \pi/3} \cot \left(3x\right) = \textrm{DNE}
$$
