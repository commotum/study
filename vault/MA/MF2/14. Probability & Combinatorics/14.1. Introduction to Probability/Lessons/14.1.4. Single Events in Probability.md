## Introduction

We can use probability to predict the chance of something happening in the future.

Any future **event** can be assigned a probability. The probability of an event is a number between $0$ and $1$, where $0$ means impossible and $1$ means certain. Probabilities can be expressed as decimals, fractions, or percentages.

For example,

$$
P(\textrm{rain tomorrow}) = 50\%
$$

means that there is a $50\%$ chance that it will rain tomorrow. This is the same as saying

$$
P(\textrm{rain tomorrow}) = 0.5 \quad \textrm{or} \quad P(\textrm{rain tomorrow}) = \frac{1}{2}
$$

As another example,

$$
P(\textrm{rolling a 7 on a fair six-sided die}) = 0
$$

means that it is impossible to score $7$ on the roll of a six-sided die.

<a id="calculating-probabilities-of-equally-likely-events"></a>
## Calculating Probabilities of Equally Likely Events

Suppose we wanted to work out the probability of scoring a $4$ on the roll of a fair six-sided die. How would we do it?

First, notice that we're told that the die is *fair.* This means that every possible outcome (i.e., every possible event in the sample space) is equally likely.

Now, since the die is fair, we can use the following formula to figure out the probability:

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(scoring a4) &= \frac{Number of ways that the desired outcome can occur}{Total number of outcomes}
\end{aligned}
$$

There are six outcomes in total, which are $1,2,3,4,5,6$. Of these there is only one $4$. Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(scoring a4) &= \frac{1}{6}
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="computing-the-probability-of-a-die-roll"></a>
## Computing the Probability of a Die Roll

**Example:** Suppose that you throw a fair six-sided die once. What is the probability of scoring an even number?

**Explanation**

We can use the formula

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(even number) &= \frac{Number of ways that the desired outcome can occur}{Total number of outcomes}
\end{aligned}
$$

In our case, there are $6$ possible outcomes:

$$
1,2,3,4,5,6
$$

Of these outcomes, $3$ of them are even numbers $(2,4,6)$. Therefore,

$$
P(\textrm{even}) = \frac{3}{6} = \frac{1}{2}
$$

## Computing the Probability of Drawing an Item From a Bag

**Example:** What is the probability of drawing a red sock from a drawer that contains $10$ black socks, $15$ white socks, and $20$ red socks?

**Explanation**

Using the standard formula, we get

$$
\begin{aligned} P(\text{red})&=\dfrac{\text{Number of red socks}}{\text{Total number of socks}} \\[3pt] &=\dfrac{20}{10+15+20} \\[3pt] &= \dfrac{20}{45} \\[3pt] &= \dfrac{4}{9}. \end{aligned}
$$

## The Structure of a Deck of Standard Playing Cards

Probability problems often involve the use of playing cards. A standard deck of playing cards is composed of $52$ cards, divided into $4$ suits.

- There are two red suits (diamonds and hearts) and two black suits (clubs and spades).
- Each suit has $13$ cards: ace, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, jack, queen, king.

Jacks, queens, and kings are also called face cards.

<a id="computing-the-probability-of-drawing-a-particular-card"></a>
## Computing the Probability of Drawing a Particular Card

**Example:** What is the probability of drawing a red jack from a shuffled deck of standard playing cards?

**Explanation**

There are $52$ cards in a standard deck. There are two red suits (hearts and diamonds), each containing a single jack, so we have $2$ red jacks.

Now, we can apply the standard formula:

$$
\begin{aligned} P(\text{red jack}) &=\dfrac{\text{Number of red jacks}}{\text{Total number of cards}} \\[3pt] &=\dfrac{2}{52} \\[3pt] &=\dfrac 1{26} \end{aligned}
$$

<a id="computing-probabilities-using-or-statements"></a>
## Computing Probabilities Using Or Statements

**Example:** You're asked to pick one card from a deck of $52$. What is the probability that it's a heart or a king?

**Explanation**

There are $52$ cards in total. Of these, there are $13$ hearts (including the king of hearts). Aside from the king of hearts, there are $3$ more kings in the deck. Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(heart or king) &= \frac{13 + 3}{52} \\
&= \frac{16}{52} \\
&= \frac{4}{13}
\end{aligned}
$$
