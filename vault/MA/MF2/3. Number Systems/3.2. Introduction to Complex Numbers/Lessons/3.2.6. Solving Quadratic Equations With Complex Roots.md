## Introduction

Suppose that we want to solve the quadratic equation

$$
x^2 -2x +2=0
$$

This quadratic is written in standard form with $a=1$, $b=-2$, and $c=2$. However, its discriminant is negative:

$$
\begin{aligned}
b^{2} - 4ac &= (-2)^{2} - 4(1)(2) \\
&= 4 - 8 \\
&=-4 \\
&< 0
\end{aligned}
$$

This means that the quadratic equation has no real solutions. However, if we allow complex solutions, we may solve the equation as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x &= \frac{-b ± \sqrt{b^{2} - 4ac}}{2a} \\
&= (- (-2) ± \sqrt{(-2)^{2} - 4(1)(2)})/(2 \cdot 1) \\
&= \frac{2 ± \sqrt{-4}}{2} \\
&= \frac{2 ± 2\textrm{i}}{2} \\
&= 1 ± \textrm{i}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the equation

$$
x^2 -2x +2=0
$$

has two solutions,

$$
x=1+\textrm{i}
$$

and

$$
x=1-\textrm{i}
$$

## Solving a Quadratic Equation With Complex Roots Using the Quadratic Formula

**Example:** Solve the equation $\, x^2-4x+5=0$.

**Explanation**

We can find the roots of a quadratic equation using the quadratic formula:

$$
x = \frac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^2-4ac}}{2a}
$$

In this case, the coefficients are $a=1, b=-4$, and $c=5$. Substituting these values into the quadratic formula, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
x &= (- (-4) ± \sqrt{(-4)^{2} - 4(1)(5)})/(2(1)) \\
&= \frac{4 ± \sqrt{16 - 20}}{2} \\
&= \frac{4 ± \sqrt{-4}}{2} \\
&= \frac{4 ± 2\textrm{i}}{2} \\
&= 2 ± \textrm{i}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the equation has two solutions,

$$
x=2+\textrm{i}
$$

and

$$
x=2-\textrm{i}
$$

## Rearranging and Solving a Quadratic Equation With Complex Roots

**Example:** Find the solutions of the equation $4x^2= 4x-13$.

**Explanation**

First, we write the equation in standard form by bringing all of the terms to the left-hand side. This gives the equation

$$
4x^2 - 4x + 13=0
$$

We can find the roots of a quadratic equation using the quadratic formula:

$$
x = \frac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^2-4ac}}{2a}
$$

In this case, the coefficients are $a=4, b=-4$, and $c=13$. Substituting these values into the quadratic formula, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
x &= (- (-4) ± \sqrt{(-4)^{2} - 4(4)(13)})/(2(4)) \\
&= \frac{4 ± \sqrt{16 - 208}}{8} \\
&= \frac{4 ± \sqrt{-192}}{8} \\
&= \frac{4 ± 8\sqrt{-3}}{8} \\
&= \frac{4 ± 8\sqrt{3}\textrm{i}}{8} \\
&= \frac{1}{2} ± \sqrt{3}\textrm{i}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the equation has two solutions,

$$
x=\dfrac{1}{2}+ \sqrt{3} \, \textrm{i}
$$

and

$$
x=\dfrac{1}{2}- \sqrt{3} \, \textrm{i}
$$

## Solving a Quadratic Equation With Complex Roots by Completing the Square

We can also solve a quadratic equation with complex solutions using the square root method.

To demonstrate, let's find the solutions of the equation

$$
(x-2)^2=-16
$$

The left-hand side is a perfect square. Therefore, we can solve the equation as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x - 2)^{2} &= -16 \\
x - 2 &= ± \sqrt{-16} \\
x - 2 &= ± 4\textrm{i} \\
x &= 2 ± 4\textrm{i}
\end{aligned}
$$

Sometimes, we need to complete the square first before applying the square root method. Let's see how.

---

<a id="solving-a-quadratic-equation-with-complex-roots-by-completing-the-square"></a>
## Solving a Quadratic Equation With Complex Roots by Completing the Square

**Example:** The equation $x^2+8x+80=0$ can be written in the form $(x+p)^2=q$. Find $p+q$, and solve the equation.

**Explanation**

We first move the constant to the right-hand side:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^{2} + 8x + 80 &= 0 \\
x^{2} + 8x &= -80
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we complete the square on the left-hand side:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^{2} + 8x &= -80 \\
x^{2} + 2 \cdot 4x + 4^{2} - 4^{2} &= -80 \\
(x + 4)^{2} - 16 &= -80 \\
(x + 4)^{2} &= -64
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, $p=4$, $q=-64$, and

$$
p+q = -60
$$

Finally, we solve the equation using the square root method:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x + 4)^{2} &= -64 \\
x + 4 &= ± \sqrt{-64} \\
x + 4 &= ± 8\textrm{i} \\
x &= -4 ± 8\textrm{i}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the equation has two solutions,

$$
x=-4+8\,\textrm{i}
$$

and

$$
x=-4-8\,\textrm{i}
$$
