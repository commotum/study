## Introduction

We often use

$$
z=x+\textrm{i}y
$$

to denote a complex number $z$, where $x$ and $y$ are the real and imaginary parts of $z$ respectively.

We can also depict complex numbers using $xy$-coordinates. For example, the complex number $4+6\textrm{i}$ can be represented by the point $(4,6)$, as follows:

![](<../Source/3.3.1. The Complex Plane/Images/e01b84a888874faf1317d370f8f5a6e1.png>)

In the context of complex numbers, the $x$-axis is called the **real axis**, and the $y$-axis is called the **imaginary axis**.

When using a two-dimensional plane to represent complex numbers, we call this the **complex plane** (another name for it is the **Argand diagram**). The complex plane is often denoted as $\mathbb{C}$.

## Identifying a Complex Number Represented by an Argand Diagram

**Example:** What complex number is represented in the Argand diagram below?

![](<../Source/3.3.1. The Complex Plane/Images/a398e70774f8baf4d12c200edfc8d28c.png>)

**Explanation**

The coordinates of the point are $(-2,3)$.

- The real part of this complex number is $x=-2$, and
- the imaginary part is $y=3$.

Therefore, the number depicted is

$$
z=-2+3\textrm{i}
$$

## Identifying a Complex Number With No Real or No Imaginary Part Represented by an Argand Diagram

**Example:** What complex number is represented in the Argand diagram below?

![](<../Source/3.3.1. The Complex Plane/Images/96b2302a93c679607a0ea501bc4f3a7f.png>)

**Explanation**

The coordinates of the point are $(-4,0)$.

- The real part of this complex number is $x=-4$, and
- the imaginary part is $y=0$.

Therefore, the number depicted is

$$
z=-4+0\textrm{i}
$$

which can be simplified to $z= -4$.

$$

- The real part of this complex number is $x=4$, and
- the imaginary part is $y=1$.
