## Introduction

When writing out a sum, we often have to write a lot of terms. For example, the following sum has five terms:

$$
S = 2+4+6+8+10
$$

We could just as easily encounter a sum with ten terms, or a hundred terms, or a thousand terms, or even more!

Thankfully, there is a notation called **sigma notation** that allows us to write sums more compactly, without writing every term.

To express the given sum $S$ using sigma notation, we first need to come up with the general term $a_n$ in our sum. Here, the $n$th term of our sum has the form $a_n = 2n$, where $n$ ranges from ${\color{red}1}$ to ${\color{blue}5}$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
a_{1} &= 2(1) = 2 \\
a_{2} &= 2(2) = 4 \\
a_{3} &= 2(3) = 6 \\
a_{4} &= 2(4) = 8 \\
a_{5} &= 2(5) = 10
\end{aligned}
$$

So, our sum can be written as

$$
S = a_{\color{red}1} + a_2 + a_3 + a_4 + a_{\color{blue}5}
$$

Finally, we use the Greek letter $\displaystyle\sum$ (called "sigma") to denote our summation:

$$
S = ∑_(n = 1)^(5)(a_{n}) = ∑_(n = 1)^(5)(2n)
$$

We write the $n$th term

$$
({\color{black}a_n=2n})
$$

to the right of $\displaystyle\sum$, we put the first index $({\color{red}1})$ below the sigma as

$$
n={\color{red}1}
$$

and we put the last index $({\color{blue}5})$ above the sigma.

We can retrieve the expanded sum by substituting all values between $\color{red}1$ and $\color{blue}5$ into the $n$th term, and separating each result with a "$+$" sign, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
∑_(n = 1)^(5)(2n) &= 2(1) + 2(2) + 2(3) + 2(4) + 2(5) \\
&= 2 + 4 + 6 + 8 + 10
\end{aligned}
$$

**Note:** The sum

$$
\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{5} 2n = 2+4+6+8+10
$$

is an example of a **series**. A series is what we get when we add the terms of a *sequence* together. In this case, we're summing the terms of the sequence $a_n = 2n$.

## Expanding Sums Written in Sigma Notation

**Example:** Express $\displaystyle\sum_{n=2}^{4} (2n-3)$ as a sum of three terms.

**Explanation**

The sum takes integer values of $n$ from $2$ to $4$. So, we compute the terms for $n=2,3,4$ and write the sum, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned} \displaystyle\sum_{n=2}^{4}(2n-3)&= \overbrace{\big(2(2)-3\big)}^{\large n\,=\,2}+\overbrace{\big(2(3)-3\big)}^{\large n\,=\,3}+\overbrace{\big(2(4)-3\big)}^{\large n\,=\,4} \\[-5pt] &=(4-3)+(6-3)+(8-3) \\[5pt] &=1+3+5 \end{aligned}
$$

## Evaluating Expressions Written in Sigma Notation

**Example:** What is the value of $\displaystyle{\sum_{n=1}^3 5^n}$?

**Explanation**

The sum takes integer values of $n$ from $1$ to $3$. So, computing the terms for $n=1,2,3$ and then adding them, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
∑_(n = 1)^(3)5^{n} &= 5^{1} + 5^{2} + 5^{3} \\
&= 5 + 25 + 125 \\
&= 155
\end{aligned}
$$

## Expressing Sums in Sigma Notation

Suppose we want to express the following series in sigma notation.

$$
S = \dfrac{\color{red}1}{\color{blue}2} + \dfrac{\color{red}2}{\color{blue}3} + \dfrac{\color{red}3}{\color{blue}4} + \cdots + \dfrac{\color{red}19}{\color{blue}20} + \dfrac{\color{red}20}{\color{blue}21}
$$

To do this, we first examine the numerators and denominators of the corresponding sequence.

$$
\dfrac{\color{red}1}{\color{blue}2}, \quad \dfrac{\color{red}2}{\color{blue}3}, \quad \dfrac{\color{red}3}{\color{blue}4}, \quad \ldots, \qquad \dfrac{\color{red}19}{\color{blue}20}, \quad \dfrac{\color{red}20}{\color{blue}21}
$$

Our goal is to find a pattern in the terms. To do this, we note the following:

- The numerators form the following sequence:
${\color{red}1}, \quad {\color{red}2}, \quad {\color{red}3}, \quad \dots, \quad {\color{red}19}, \quad {\color{red}20}$
Notice that we have $20$ terms in total. The $n$th term of this sequence is given by
${\color{red}a_n} = {\color{red}n}, \qquad 1\leq n \leq 20$.
- The denominators form the following sequence:
${\color{blue}2}, \quad {\color{blue}3}, \quad \dots, \quad {\color{blue}20}, \quad {\color{blue}21}$
Since the value in the denominator is always *one more than* the numerator, the $n$th term of this sequence is given by
${\color{blue}b_n} = {\color{blue}n + 1}, \qquad 1\leq n \leq 20$.

Therefore, the $n$th term of the sequence

$$
\dfrac{\color{red}1}{\color{blue}2}, \quad \dfrac{\color{red}2}{\color{blue}3}, \quad \dfrac{\color{red}3}{\color{blue}4}, \quad \ldots, \qquad \dfrac{\color{red}19}{\color{blue}20}, \quad \dfrac{\color{red}20}{\color{blue}21}
$$

is

$$
\dfrac{\color{red}a_n}{\color{blue}b_n} =\dfrac{\color{red}n}{\color{blue}n+1}
$$

where $n$ ranges from $n=1$ to $n=20$.

Therefore, we can express our series using sigma notation as follows:

$$
S = ∑_(n = 1)^(20)\frac{n}{n + 1}
$$

We can check that this answer is correct by writing out the summation in full and checking that we recover the original sequence:

$$
\begin{aligned}
∑_(n = 1)^(20)\frac{n}{n + 1} &= \frac{1}{1 + 1} + \frac{2}{2 + 1} + ⋯ + \frac{19}{19 + 1} + \frac{20}{20 + 1} \\
&= \frac{1}{2} + \frac{2}{3} + ⋯ + \frac{19}{20} + \frac{20}{21}✓
\end{aligned}
$$

## Writing Sums Using Sigma Notation

**Example:** Express $S=4+8+12+\cdots+56+60$ in sigma notation.

**Explanation**

Notice that the terms of the corresponding sequence

$4, \quad 8, \quad 12, \quad, \ldots, \quad 56, \quad 60$.
are multiples of $4$, and since

$$
60\div 4 = 15
$$

there are $15$ terms in total.

Therefore, the $n$th term of the sequence has the form
$a_n =4n$,
where $n$ ranges from $n=1$ to

$$
n=15\mathbin{.}
$$

Indeed:

$$
\begin{aligned}
a_{1} &= 4(1) = 4 \\
a_{2} &= 4(2) = 8 \\
a_{3} &= 4(3) = 12 \\
&= ⋮ \\
a_{14} &= 4(14) = 56 \\
a_{15} &= 4(15) = 60
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned} S=4+8+12+\cdots+56+60=\sum_{n=1}^{15} 4n. \end{aligned}
$$
