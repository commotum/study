## Introduction

To divide one rational expression by another, we multiply by the reciprocal of the divisor. Just like with fractions!

For example, let's divide $x$ by

$$
\dfrac{3}{y}
$$

$$
x \div \dfrac{3}{y}
$$

To do this, we just need to multiply by the reciprocal of the divisor. The divisor is

$$
\dfrac{3}{y}
$$

and to get the reciprocal, we just flip the fraction upside down:

$$
\dfrac{y}{3}
$$

Multiplying by the reciprocal of the divisor, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
x \div \frac{3}{y} &=  \\
x \cdot \frac{y}{3} &=  \\
&= \frac{xy}{3} \mid
\end{aligned}
$$

Let's see some more examples!

<a id="dividing-rational-expressions-with-no-common-factors"></a>
## Dividing Rational Expressions with No Common Factors

**Example:** What is the result of dividing $\dfrac 3 x$ by $\dfrac {5}{y}$?

**Explanation**

To compute

$$
\dfrac{3}{x} \div \dfrac{5}{y}
$$

we just need to multiply by the reciprocal of the divisor:

$$
\begin{aligned} \dfrac 3 x \div \dfrac 5 y&=\ \dfrac 3 x \cdot \dfrac y 5&=\ \dfrac {3y} {5x} \end{aligned}
$$

<a id="dividing-rational-expressions-with-a-single-common-factor"></a>
## Dividing Rational Expressions with a Single Common Factor

**Example:** What is the result of dividing $\dfrac 3 x$ by $\dfrac y {6x^2}$?

**Explanation**

To compute

$$
\dfrac{3}{x} \div \dfrac{y}{6x^2}
$$

we just need to multiply by the reciprocal of the divisor:

$$
\require{cancel} \begin{aligned} \dfrac 3 x \div \dfrac y {6x^2} &=\ \dfrac 3 x \cdot \dfrac {6x^2} y &=\ \dfrac {18x^2} {xy}&=\ \dfrac {18x^\cancel{2}} {\cancel{x}y}&=\ \dfrac {18x} {y}& \end{aligned}
$$

Note that in the final step, we canceled out a common factor of $x$ in the numerator and denominator.

<a id="dividing-rational-expressions-with-multiple-common-factors"></a>
## Dividing Rational Expressions with Multiple Common Factors

**Example:** Given that $\dfrac {2a^2(1+b)} {3b} =x$ and $\dfrac {1+b}{5b^2} =y$, then what is $x \div y$?

**Explanation**

To compute $x \div y$, i.e.,

$$
(2a^{2}(1 + b))/(3b) \mid \div \frac{1 + b}{5b^{2}}
$$

we just need to multiply by the reciprocal of the divisor:

$$
\require{cancel} \begin{aligned} \dfrac {2a^2(1+b)} {3b} \div \dfrac{1+b}{5b^2} \\[5pt] &= \dfrac {2a^2(1+b)} {3b} \cdot \dfrac {5b^2} {1+b} \\[5pt] &= \dfrac {2a^2\cancel{(1+b)}} {3\cancel{b}} \cdot \dfrac {5b^{\cancel{2}}} {\cancel{1+b}} \\[5pt] &= \dfrac {10a^2b} {3} \end{aligned}
$$

## Dividing Rational Expressions and Simplifying the Result by Factoring

**Example:** Given that $\dfrac {2a^3 - 10a} {7b} =x$ and $\dfrac {2a} {14b^2}=y$, then what is $x \div y$?

**Explanation**

To compute

$$
\require{cancel} \begin{aligned} \dfrac {2a^3 - 10a} {7b} &\div \dfrac {2a} {14b^2}, \end{aligned}
$$

we just need to multiply by the reciprocal of the divisor:

$$
\require{cancel} \begin{aligned} \dfrac {2a^3 - 10a} {7b} \div \dfrac {2a} {14b^2}&=\ \dfrac {2a^3 - 10a} {7b} \cdot \dfrac {14b^2} {2a}&=\ \dfrac {2a(a^2 - 5)} {7b} \cdot \dfrac {2b \cdot 7b} {2a}&=\ \dfrac {\cancel{2a}(a^2 - 5)} {\cancel{7b}} \cdot \dfrac {2b \cdot \cancel{7b}} {\cancel{2a}}&=\ \dfrac {2b(a^2 - 5)} 1 \\ &= 2a^2b - 10b & \end{aligned}
$$
