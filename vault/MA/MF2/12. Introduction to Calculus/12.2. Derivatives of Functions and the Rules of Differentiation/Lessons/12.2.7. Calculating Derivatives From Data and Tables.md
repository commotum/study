## Introduction

Let's suppose we only know the values of the functions $f(x)$ and $g(x)$ at $x=0$ along with their derivatives, as given in the table below.

| $x$ | $f(x)$ | $g(x)$ | $f'(x)$ | $g'(x)$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $0$ | $2$ | $3$ | $4$ | $5$ |

Can we calculate

$$
\dfrac{\textrm{d}}{\textrm{d}x}\Big(f(x)g(x)\Big)
$$

at $x=0$?

Using the product rule, we have

$$
\dfrac{\textrm{d}}{\textrm{d}x}\Big(f(x)g(x)\Big) = f'(x)g(x)+f(x)g'(x)
$$

At the point where $x=0$, this gives

$$
\left.\dfrac{\textrm{d}}{\textrm{d}x}\Big(f(x)g(x)\Big) \right|_{x=0} = f'(0)g(0)+f(0)g'(0)
$$

All of these values are given in the table! So, substituting the values from the table into the formula, we conclude that

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{d}{dx}(f(x)g(x)) \mid _{x = 0} &= f^{′}(0)g(0) + f(0)g^{′}(0) \\
&= 4 \cdot 3 + 2 \cdot 5 \\
&= 22
\end{aligned}
$$

## Computing the Derivative of a Product Given the Necessary Data

**Example:** The values of functions $f(x)$ and $g(x)$ and their derivatives at $x=0$ are given in the table below.

| $x$ | $f(x)$ | $g(x)$ | $f'(x)$ | $g'(x)$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $0$ | $1$ | $3$ | $-2$ | $0$ |

Compute $\dfrac{\textrm{d}}{\textrm{d}x}\big(f(x)g(x)\big)$ at $x=0$.

**Explanation**

Using the product rule, we get

$$
\dfrac{\textrm{d}}{\textrm{d}x}\big(f(x)g(x)\big) = f(x)g'(x)+f'(x)g(x)
$$

At the point where $x=0$, this gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{d}{dx}(f(x)g(x)) \mid _{x = 0} &= f(0)g^{′}(0) + f^{′}(0)g(0) \\
&= 1 \cdot 0 + 3 \cdot (-2) \\
&=-6
\end{aligned}
$$

## Computing the Derivative of a Quotient Given the Necessary Data

**Example:** The values of functions $h(x)$ and $z(x)$ and their derivatives at $x=-1$ are given in the table below.

| $x$ | $h(x)$ | $z(x)$ | $h'(x)$ | $z'(x)$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $-1$ | $-4$ | $2$ | $6$ | $3$ |

Compute $\dfrac{\textrm{d}}{\textrm{d}x}\left(\dfrac{h(x)}{z(x)}\right)$ at $x=-1$.

**Explanation**

Using the quotient rule, we get

$$
\dfrac{\textrm{d}}{\textrm{d}x}\left(\dfrac{h(x)}{z(x)}\right) = \dfrac{h'(x)z(x)-h(x)z'(x)}{(z(x))^2}
$$

At the point where $x=-1$, this gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{d}{dx}((h(x))/(z(x))) \mid _{x =-1} &= (h^{′}(-1)z(-1) - h(-1)z^{′}(-1))/((z(-1))^{2}) \\
&= (6 \cdot 2 - (-4) \cdot 3)/(2^{2}) \\
&= \frac{24}{4} \\
&= 6
\end{aligned}
$$

## Computing the Derivative of a Linear Combination of Functions Given the Necessary Data

**Example:** The values of functions $p(x)$ and $q(x)$ and their derivatives at $x=-3$ are given in the table below.

| $x$ | $p(x)$ | $q(x)$ | $p'(x)$ | $q'(x)$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $-3$ | $1$ | $8$ | $0$ | $-2$ |

Compute $\dfrac{\textrm{d}}{\textrm{d}x}\big(p(x) + 5q(x) \big)$ at $x=-3$.

**Explanation**

Using the sum and constant multiple rules, we get

$$
\dfrac{\textrm{d}}{\textrm{d}x}\left(p(x) + 5q(x) \right) = p'(x) + 5q'(x)
$$

At the point where $x=-3$, this gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{d}{dx}(p(x) + 5q(x)) \mid _{x =-3} &= p^{′}(-3) + 5q^{′}(-3) \\
&= 0 + 5 \cdot (-2) \\
&=-10
\end{aligned}
$$

## Computing the Derivative of the Product or Quotient of a Composition of Functions Given the Necessary Data

**Example:** For the functions $f(x)$ and $g(x)$, we have
$f(1)=2, \quad f'(1)=2, \quad g(1)=-1, \quad g'(1)=3$.
If $h(x) = \left(f(x)-1 \right)\left(2g(x)+1\right)$, compute $h'(1)$.

**Explanation**

The product rule states that

$$
\dfrac{\textrm{d}}{\textrm{d}x}\Big(uv\Big) = u \dfrac{\textrm d v}{\textrm d x} +v \dfrac{\textrm du}{\textrm d x}
$$

Let $u=f(x)-1$ and

$$
v=2g(x)+1
$$

Differentiating each, we get

$$
\dfrac{\textrm d u}{\textrm d x} = f'(x) \qquad \text{and}\qquad \dfrac{\textrm d v}{\textrm d x} = 2g'(x)
$$

Substituting this into the product rule gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
h^{′}(x) &= u\frac{dv}{dx} + v\frac{du}{dx} \\
&= (f(x) - 1) \cdot 2g^{′}(x) + (2g(x) + 1) \cdot f^{′}(x) \\
&= 2g^{′}(x)(f(x) - 1) + f^{′}(x)(2g(x) + 1)
\end{aligned}
$$

At $x=1$, this gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
h^{′}(1) &= 2g^{′}(1)(f(1) - 1) + f^{′}(1)(2g(1) + 1) \\
&= 2 \cdot 3 \cdot (2 - 1) + 2 \cdot (2 \cdot (-1) + 1) \\
&= 4
\end{aligned}
$$
