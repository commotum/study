## Introduction

Let's think about how to complete the square on the following quadratic expression:

$$
-\left(x^2 + 6x+4\right)
$$

We begin by completing the square for the expression inside the parentheses:

$$
x^2 + 6x + {\color{purple}{4}}
$$

First, we rewrite the coefficient of the linear term as a product with ${\color{red}{2}}$ as a factor:

$$
x^2 + {\color{red}{2}}\cdot {\color{blue}{3}}x + {\color{purple}{4}}
$$

Then, we add and then subtract ${\color{blue}{3}}^2$, writing the constant term ${\color{purple}{4}}$ last:

$$
\underbrace{x^2 + {\color{red}{2}}\cdot {\color{blue}{3}}x + {\color{blue}{3}}^2} - {\color{blue}{3}}^2 + {\color{purple}{4}}
$$

The first three terms form a perfect square, which we factor as follows:

$$
(x+{\color{blue}{3}})^2 - {\color{blue}{3}}^2+ {\color{purple}{4}}
$$

Evaluating the last two terms, we get

$$
(x+{\color{blue}{3}})^2 - 5
$$

Finally, we substitute this result back into our original expression:

$$
\begin{aligned}
- (x^{2} + 6x + 4) &= - ((x + 3)^{2} - 5) \\
&= - (x + 3)^{2} + 5 \\
&= 5 - (x + 3)^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Completing the Square When the Leading Coefficient Is Negative One

**Example:** Rewrite $-x^2+6x+1$ by completing the square.

**Explanation**

Notice that the leading coefficient is $-1$. So, we take out a factor of $-1$ from the entire expression:
$-(x^2 - 6x -1)$

Let's now complete the square for the expression inside the parentheses:

$$
x^2 - 6x - {\color{purple}{1}}
$$

First, we rewrite the coefficient of the linear term as a product with ${\color{red}{2}}$ as a factor:

$$
x^2 - {\color{red}{2}}\cdot {\color{blue}{3}}x - {\color{purple}{1}}
$$

Then, we add and then subtract ${\color{blue}{3}}^2$, writing the constant term ${\color{purple}{1}}$ last:

$$
\underbrace{x^2 - {\color{red}{2}}\cdot {\color{blue}{3}}x + {\color{blue}{3}}^2} - {\color{blue}{3}}^2 - {\color{purple}{1}}
$$

The first three terms form a perfect square, which we factor as follows:

$$
(x-{\color{blue}{3}})^2 - {\color{blue}{3}}^2- {\color{purple}{1}}
$$

Evaluating the last two terms, we get

$$
(x-{\color{blue}{3}})^2 - 10
$$

Finally, we substitute this result back into our original expression:

$$
\begin{aligned}
-x^{2} + 6x + 1 &= - ((x - 3)^{2} - 10) \\
&= - (x - 3)^{2} + 10 \\
&= 10 - (x - 3)^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Completing the Square When the Leading Coefficient is Negative One: Odd Linear Terms

**Example:** Rewrite $-x^2 +7x -13$ by completing the square.

**Explanation**

Notice that the leading coefficient is $-1$. So, we take out a factor of $-1$ from the entire expression:
$-(x^2 -7x +13)$

Let's now complete the square for the expression inside the parentheses:

$$
x^2 -7x+13
$$

First, we rewrite the coefficient of the linear term as a product with ${\color{red}{2}}$ as a factor:

$$
x^2 -{\color{red}{2}}\cdot {\color{blue}{\dfrac72}}x +{\color{purple}{13}}
$$

Then, we *add* and *then subtract*

$$
\left({\color{blue}{\dfrac72}}\right)^2
$$

writing the constant term ${\color{purple}{13}}$ last:

$$
\underbrace{x^2 -{\color{red}{2}}\cdot {\color{blue}{\dfrac72}}x + \left({\color{blue}{\dfrac72}}\right)^2} -\left({\color{blue}{\dfrac72}}\right)^2+ {\color{purple}{13}}
$$

The first three terms form a perfect square, which we factor as follows:

$$
\left(x- {\color{blue}{\dfrac72}}\right)^2 -\left({\color{blue}{\dfrac72}}\right)^2 + {\color{purple}{13}}
$$

Evaluating the last two terms, we get

$$
\left(x- {\color{blue}{\dfrac72}}\right)^2 +\dfrac{3}4
$$

Finally, we substitute this result back into our original expression:

$$
\begin{aligned}
-x^{2} + 7x - 13 &= - ((x - \frac{7}{2})^{2} + \frac{3}{4}) \\
&=-\frac{3}{4} - (x - \frac{7}{2})^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Completing the Square on an Expression With a Positive Leading Coefficient

**Example:** Rewrite $2x^2 - 6x+1$ by completing the square.

**Explanation**

Notice that the leading coefficient is $2$. So, we take out a factor of $2$ from the entire expression:

$$
2\left(x^2 - 3x + \dfrac{1}{2} \right)
$$

Let's now complete the square for the expression inside the parentheses:

$$
x^2 - 3x + {\color{purple} \dfrac{1}{2}}
$$

First, we rewrite the coefficient of the linear term as a product with ${\color{red}{2}}$ as a factor:

$$
x^2-{\color{red}{2}}\cdot {\color{blue}\dfrac{3}{2}}x + {\color{purple}\dfrac{1}{2}}
$$

Then, we *add* and *then subtract*

$$
\left({\color{blue}\dfrac{3}{2}}\right)^2
$$

writing the constant term

$$
{\color{purple} \dfrac{1}{2}}
$$

last:

$$
\underbrace{x^2-{\color{red}{2}}\cdot {\color{blue}\dfrac{3}{2}}x + \left({\color{blue}\dfrac{3}{2}}\right)^2} - \left({\color{blue}\dfrac{3}{2}}\right)^2+ {\color{purple}\dfrac{1}{2}}
$$

The first three terms form a perfect square, which we factor as follows:

$$
\left(x - \dfrac{3}{2} \right)^2 - \left({\color{blue}\dfrac{3}{2}}\right)^2+ {\color{purple}\dfrac{1}{2}}
$$

Evaluating the last two terms, we get

$$
\left(x - \dfrac{3}{2} \right)^2 - \dfrac{7}{4}
$$

Finally, we substitute this result back into our original expression:

$$
\begin{aligned}
2(x^{2} - 3x + \frac{1}{2}) &= 2((x - \frac{3}{2})^{2} - \frac{7}{4}) \\
&= 2(x - \frac{3}{2})^{2} - 2 \cdot \frac{7}{4} \\
&= 2(x - \frac{3}{2})^{2} - \frac{7}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Completing the Square on an Expression With a Negative Leading Coefficient

**Example:** Rewrite $-2x^2+20x - 40$ by completing the square.

**Explanation**

Notice that the leading coefficient is $-2$. So, we take out a factor of $-2$ from the entire expression:

$$
-2\left(x^2 - 10x + 20\right)
$$

Let's now complete the square for the expression inside the parentheses:

$$
x^2 - 10x + {\color{purple}20}
$$

First, we rewrite the coefficient of the linear term as a product with ${\color{red}2}$ as a factor:

$$
x^2- {\color{red}{2}}\cdot {\color{blue} 5}\cdot x + {\color{purple}20}
$$

Then, we *add* and *then subtract* ${\color{blue} 5}^2$, writing the constant term ${\color{purple}20}$ last:

$$
\underbrace{x^2- {\color{red}{2}}\cdot {\color{blue} 5}\cdot x + {\color{blue} 5}^2} - {\color{blue} 5}^2 + {\color{purple}20}
$$

The first three terms form a perfect square, which we factor as follows:

$$
\left(x-5\right)^2 -{\color{blue} 5}^2 + {\color{purple}20}
$$

Evaluating the last two terms, we get

$$
\left(x-5\right)^2 - 5
$$

Finally, we substitute this result back into our original expression:

$$
\begin{aligned}
-2(x^{2} - 10x + 20) &= -2((x - 5)^{2} - 5) \\
&=-2(x - 5)^{2} + (-2) \cdot (-5) \\
&=-2(x - 5)^{2} + 10
\end{aligned}
$$
