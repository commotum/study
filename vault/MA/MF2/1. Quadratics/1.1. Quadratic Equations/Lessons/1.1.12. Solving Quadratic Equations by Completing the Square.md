## Introduction

We can use the method of completing the square to solve *any* quadratic equation, even if the equation does not factor.

To illustrate, suppose we want to solve the following equation:

$$
x^2+2x-8=0
$$

We can do so by following the five steps below:

**Step 1.** Move the constant term to the right-hand side:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^{2} + 2x - 8 &= 0 \\
x^{2} + 2x &= 8
\end{aligned}
$$

**Step 2.** Complete the square on the left-hand side:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^{2} + 2x &= 8 \\
x^{2} + 2 \cdot 1 \cdot x &= 8 \\
x^{2} + 2 \cdot 1 \cdot x + 1^{2} - 1^{2} &= 8 \\
(x^{2} + 2 \cdot 1 \cdot x + 1^{2}) - 1 &= 8 \\
(x + 1)^{2} - 1 &= 8
\end{aligned}
$$

**Step 3.** Isolate the perfect square by adding $1$ to both sides. This gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x + 1)^{2} &= 9
\end{aligned}
$$

**Step 4.** We now solve the equation using the square root method:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x + 1)^{2} &= 9 \\
\sqrt{(x + 1)^{2}} &= \sqrt{9} \\
\mid x + 1 \mid &= 3 \\
x + 1 &= ± 3 \\
x &= -1 ± 3
\end{aligned}
$$

**Step 5.** Finally, we simplify the two solutions:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_{1} &= -1 - 3 =-4 \\
x_{2} &= -1 + 3 = 2
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the solutions are $x=-4$ and $x=2$.

## Solving a Quadratic Equation Given a Corresponding Perfect Square

**Example:** Solve the equation $x^2 -10x = -24$.

*Hint: Use the fact that $x^2 -10x +25$ is a perfect square.*

**Explanation**

Using the hint, we have

$$
x^2 -10x +25 = (x-5)^2
$$

First, we complete the square on the left-hand side:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^{2} - 10x &= -24 \\
x^{2} - 2 \cdot 5 \cdot x &= -24 \\
x^{2} - 2 \cdot 5 \cdot x + 5^{2} - 5^{2} &= -24 \\
(x - 5)^{2} - 25 &= -24
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we isolate the perfect square by adding $25$ to both sides:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x - 5)^{2} - 25 + 25 &= -24 + 25 \\
(x - 5)^{2} &= 1
\end{aligned}
$$

We now solve the equation using the square root method:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x - 5)^{2} &= 1 \\
\sqrt{(x - 5)^{2}} &= \sqrt{1} \\
\mid x - 5 \mid &= 1 \\
x - 5 &= ± 1 \\
x &= 5 ± 1
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we simplify the two solutions:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_{1} &= 5 - 1 = 4 \\
x_{2} &= 5 + 1 = 6
\end{aligned}
$$

## Solving a Quadratic Equation When the Solutions Are Rational

**Example:** Write the equation $x^2 + 6x +5= 0$ in the form $(x+p)^2=q$, and hence solve the equation.

**Explanation**

First, we move the constant term to the right-hand side and complete the square on the left-hand side:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^{2} + 6x + 5 &= 0 \\
x^{2} + 6x &= -5 \\
x^{2} + 2 \cdot 3 \cdot x &= -5 \\
x^{2} + 2 \cdot 3 \cdot x + 3^{2} - 3^{2} &= -5 \\
(x^{2} + 2 \cdot 3 \cdot x + 3^{2}) - 9 &= -5 \\
(x + 3)^{2} - 9 &= -5
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we isolate the perfect square:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x + 3)^{2} - 9 &= -5 \\
(x + 3)^{2} &= -5 + 9 \\
(x + 3)^{2} &= 4
\end{aligned}
$$

So, $p=3$ and $q=4$. Now, we solve the equation using the square-root method:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x + 3)^{2} &= 4 \\
\sqrt{(x + 3)^{2}} &= \sqrt{4} \\
\mid x + 3 \mid &= 2 \\
x + 3 &= ± 2 \\
x &= -3 ± 2
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we simplify the two solutions:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_{1} &= -3 + 2 =-1 \\
x_{2} &= -3 - 2 =-5
\end{aligned}
$$

## Solving a Quadratic Equation When the Solutions Are Irrational

**Example:** Solve the equation $x^2-2x-1=0$.

**Explanation**

First, we move the constant term to the right-hand side and complete the square on the left-hand side:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^{2} - 2x - 1 &= 0 \\
x^{2} - 2x &= 1 \\
x^{2} - 2 \cdot 1 \cdot x &= 1 \\
x^{2} - 2 \cdot 1 \cdot x + 1^{2} - 1^{2} &= 1 \\
(x^{2} - 2x + 1) - 1 &= 1 \\
(x - 1)^{2} - 1 &= 1
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we isolate the perfect square and solve the equation using the square-root method:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x - 1)^{2} - 1 &= 1 \\
(x - 1)^{2} &= 2 \\
\sqrt{(x - 1)^{2}} &= \sqrt{2} \\
\mid x - 1 \mid &= \sqrt{2} \\
x - 1 &= ± \sqrt{2} \\
x &= 1 ± \sqrt{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, our solutions are

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_{1} &= 1 + \sqrt{2}, \\
x_{2} &= 1 - \sqrt{2}
\end{aligned}
$$
