## Introduction

If we want to plot the graph of $y=-x^2$, we reflect the curve $y=x^2$ in the $x$-axis, as shown below:

![](<../Source/1.2.2. Vertical Reflections of Quadratic Functions/Images/8b05781f3ccba9da5c5cd71796bccc66.png>)

This transformation is called a **reflection in the $x$-axis**, and it occurs because the output $y$-values of the function are made negative.

As with the curve $y=x^2$, the vertex of $y=-x^2$ is still at $(0,0)$. However, in the curve $y=-x^2$, the vertex is the **maximum point**, because the curve does not go above that point.

If we combine vertical and horizontal translations with reflection, we get a general form

$$
y=-(x-h)^2 +k
$$

This is the equation of a reflected graph $y=-x^2$ that is translated

- $h$ units right and
- $k$ units up.

<a id="combining-reflection-with-vertical-translation"></a>
## Combining Reflection with Vertical Translation

**Example:** Plot the graph of $y=-x^2-3$.

**Explanation**

Remember that

$$
y=-x^2+k
$$

is a reflected parabola $y=-x^2$ that is translated $k$ units up.

The given function $y=-x^2-3$ has $k=-3$. Therefore, it is a reflected parabola $y=-x^2$ that is translated $3$ units *down*.

![](<../Source/1.2.2. Vertical Reflections of Quadratic Functions/Images/c6c47202f825cb9a4555bfc0d1597889.png>)

<a id="combining-reflection-with-horizontal-translation"></a>
## Combining Reflection with Horizontal Translation

**Example:** Plot the graph of $y=-(x+2)^2$.

**Explanation**

Remember that $y=-(x-h)^2$ is a reflected parabola $y=-x^2$ that is translated $h$ units right.

The given function

$$
y=-(x+2)^2
$$

has $h=-2$. To see this clearly, we can write the curve as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
y &= - (x + 2)^{2} \\
y &= - (x - (-2))^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the given curve is a reflected parabola $y=-x^2$ that is translated $2$ units *left*.

<a id="combining-reflection-with-vertical-and-horizontal-translation"></a>
## Combining Reflection with Vertical and Horizontal Translation

**Example:** Identify the equation of the parabola shown below.

![](<../Source/1.2.2. Vertical Reflections of Quadratic Functions/Images/39656ba2514fbec8f4be1096a2ca1634.png>)

**Explanation**

Remember that the graph of

$$
y=-(x-h)^2+k
$$

is a translation of the reflected parabola $y=-x^2$ by

- $h$ units right, and
- $k$ units up.

The vertex of

$$
y=-(x-h)^2+k
$$

is located at $(h,k)$.

Here, the parabola is translated $2$ units right, so we have $h=2$. The parabola is also translated $1$ unit up, so we have $k=1$. We can also see that the vertex is at $(2,1)$, so again we must have $h=2$ and $k=1$.

Therefore, the equation of the graph is:

$$
\begin{aligned}
y &= - (x - 2)^{2} + 1
\end{aligned}
$$
