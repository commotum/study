## Introduction

To determine the axis of symmetry of the parabola

$$
y=ax^2+bx+c
$$

we can use the following formula:

$$
x=-\dfrac{b}{2a}
$$

We'll derive this result in a separate lesson. For now, let's get some practice working with it.

Suppose we want to find the axis of symmetry of the function

$$
y=3x^2-6x+1
$$

To do this, we substitute $a=3$ and $b=-6$ into the above formula, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x &= -\frac{b}{2a} \\
&= - ((-6))/(2(3)) \\
&= 1
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, $x=1$ is the equation of the axis of symmetry.

![](<../Source/1.2.6. The Axis of Symmetry of a Parabola/Images/2d1fe776447a2f4b42ba05f4cd56a891.png>)

The vertex of the parabola lies on the axis of symmetry, so $x=1$ also represents the $x$-coordinate of the vertex. To find the $y$-coordinate of the vertex, we can substitute $x=1$ into the original equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
y &= 3x^{2} - 6x + 1 \\
&= 3(1)^{2} - 6(1) + 1 \\
&= 3 - 6 + 1 \\
&=-2
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the vertex is at $(1,-2)$.

![](<../Source/1.2.6. The Axis of Symmetry of a Parabola/Images/3449179fe7c977b9ebdc1f909022c6fb.png>)

## Finding the Axis of Symmetry of a Parabola

**Example:** Find the axis of symmetry for the parabola $y=2x^2-12x+3$.

**Explanation**

The equation of the axis of symmetry of the parabola

$$
y=ax^2+bx+c
$$

is given by

$$
x = -\dfrac{b}{2a}
$$

We substitute $a=2$ and $b=-12$ into the formula for the axis of symmetry. This gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
x &= - ((-12))/(2(2)) \\
&= - ((-12))/(4) \\
&= 3
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the equation of the axis of symmetry is $x=3$.

## Finding the Vertex of a Parabola

**Example:** Find the coordinates of the vertex of the parabola $y=-x^2+4x+1$.

**Explanation**

The vertex of the parabola lies on the axis of symmetry, so we will start by computing the axis of symmetry.

The equation of the axis of symmetry of the parabola

$$
y=ax^2+bx+c
$$

is given by

$$
x = -\dfrac{b}{2a}
$$

Substituting $a=-1$ and $b=4$ into the formula for the axis of symmetry, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
x &= - (4)/(2(-1)) \\
&= - (4)/((-2)) \\
&= 2
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the equation of the axis of symmetry is $x=2$. This also represents the $x$-coordinate of the vertex. To find the $y$-coordinate of the vertex, we can substitute $x=2$ into the original equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
y &= -x^{2} + 4x + 1 \\
&= - (2)^{2} + 4(2) + 1 \\
&=-4 + 8 + 1 \\
&= 5
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the vertex is at $(2,5)$.

## Finding an Unknown Coefficient Given the Axis of Symmetry

**Example:** The axis of symmetry of the parabola $y=0.25x^2+kx+2$ is $x=-6$. Find the value of $k$.

**Explanation**

First, we will calculate the axis of symmetry in terms of $k$. Then, we will set our result equal to $-6$.

The equation of the axis of symmetry of the parabola

$$
y=ax^2+bx+c
$$

is given by

$$
x = -\dfrac{b}{2a}
$$

Substituting $a=0.25$ and $b=k$ into the formula for the axis of symmetry, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
x &= - (k)/(2(0.25)) \\
&=-\frac{k}{0.5} \\
&=-2k
\end{aligned}
$$

We already know that the axis of symmetry is $x=-6$, so we must have

$$
\begin{aligned}
-2k &= -6 \\
k &= 3
\end{aligned}
$$
