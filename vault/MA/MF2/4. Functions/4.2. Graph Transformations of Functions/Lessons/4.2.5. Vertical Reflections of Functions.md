## Introduction

The graph of $y=f(x)$ shown below. How can we plot the graph of $y=-f(x)$?

![](<../Source/4.2.5. Vertical Reflections of Functions/Images/1cce897f80efeeaa84dd719f8e6016c8.png>)

As usual, It's helpful to first find a few points on the graph of $y=-f(x)$. You can use the points on the graph of $y=f(x)$ to identify corresponding points on the graph of $y=-f(x)$:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}Points ony = f(x) & Points ony =-f(x) \\ (-1, 2) & (-1,-2) \\ (0,-1) & (0, 1) \\ (2, 0) & (2, 0) \\ (3, 4) & (3,-4)\end{bmatrix}
$$

For each point on the graph of $y=f(x)$ there is a point on the graph of $y=-f(x)$ with the *same* $x$-value and the *opposite* $y$-value.

Let's plot our new points, and draw $y=-f(x)$.

![](<../Source/4.2.5. Vertical Reflections of Functions/Images/6e54372496589de45d4a0f01d31782ff.png>)

The graph of $y=-f(x)$ is a **reflection across the $x$-axis** of the graph of $y=f(x)$.

<a id="identifying-a-point-on-a-vertically-reflected-function"></a>
## Identifying a Point on a Vertically Reflected Function

**Example:** The point $P$ with coordinates $(2, -1)$ lies on the curve $y=f(x)$. Which point must lie on the graph of $y= -f(x)$?

**Explanation**

The transformation $y=-f(x)$ reflects the curve $y=f(x)$ in the $x$-axis.

First, notice that the point $P(2, -1)$ lies $1$ unit *below* the $x$-axis. So, under the action of the transformation, this point will be reflected $1$ unit *above* the $x$-axis.

![](<../Source/4.2.5. Vertical Reflections of Functions/Images/c4105ff195b571150972216deab7d412.png>)

We conclude that the point $(2,1)$ must lie on the graph of $y=-f(x)$.
