## Introduction

Given the graph of a curve $y= f(x)$, there are two types of reflections we can apply to it:

1. Reflection across the $y$-axis. This is a transformation of the form $y=f(-x)$.
2. Reflection across the $x$-axis. This is a transformation of the form $y=-f(x)$.

For example, let's consider the function $y=f(x)$ below.

![](<../Source/4.2.10. Combining Reflections With Other Graph Transformations/Images/3c2ea2b8cd72df68390166677ae5a073.png>)

The graph of $y=f(-x)$ is a reflection of $y=f(x)$ in the $y$-axis, and it looks as follows:

![](<../Source/4.2.10. Combining Reflections With Other Graph Transformations/Images/37ade5c76c7d8a5708374711cee6a170.png>)

The graph of $y=-f(x)$ is a reflection of $y=f(x)$ in the $x$-axis, and it looks as follows:

![](<../Source/4.2.10. Combining Reflections With Other Graph Transformations/Images/d034e55ee5dde79372b9e1e466ac23f4.png>)

We can also combine reflections with other graph transformations. Let's see how.

## Determining the Correct Order of Transformations Given a Transformed Function Expression

**Example:** The graph of $y=-f(x-2)+3$, can be obtained from the graph of $y=f(x)$ by applying **some** of the following transformations:

- $(\textrm{T}1)$ - Translation by $3$ units up
- $(\textrm{T}2)$ - Reflection across the $x$-axis
- $(\textrm{T}3)$ - Translation by $2$ units to the right
- $(\textrm{T}4)$ - Translation by $2$ units to the left

What could be a possible ordering of the appropriate transformations?

**Explanation**

To perform transformations of functions with multiple operations, we must remember the following rule:

1. First, we perform the transformations *inside* the argument of $f$. We first carry out horizontal stretches and reflections, followed by horizontal translations.
2. Then, we perform the transformations *outside* the argument of $f$. We first carry out vertical stretches and reflections, followed by vertical translations.

To get

$$
y=-f(x-2)+3
$$

we could apply the following steps:

- Take the curve $y=f(x)$.
- Apply transformation $(\textrm{T}3)$: Translate $y=f(x)$ by $2$ units to the right to get the graph of $y=f(x-2)$.
- Apply transformation $(\textrm{T}2)$: Reflect $y=f(x-2)$ across the $x$-axis to get the graph of $y=-f(x-2)$.
- Apply transformation $(\textrm{T}1)$: Translate $y=-f(x-2)$ by $3$ units up to get the graph of $y=-f(x-2)+3$.

Therefore, a possible order of transformations is

$\qquad$ $(\textrm{T}3) \to (\textrm{T}2) \to (\textrm{T}1)$.

## Identifying Graph Transformations Resulting From Reflections With Shifting

**Example:** The diagram below shows the function $y=f(x)$. Find the graph of $y=-f(x+3)$.

![](<../Source/4.2.10. Combining Reflections With Other Graph Transformations/Images/99a689ccbda2f6ad06f9aceaf17207a4.png>)

**Explanation**

To perform transformations of functions with multiple operations, we must remember the following rule:

1. First, we perform the transformations *inside* the argument of $f$. We first carry out horizontal stretches and reflections, followed by horizontal translations.
2. Then, we perform the transformations *outside* the argument of $f$. We first carry out vertical stretches and reflections, followed by vertical translations.

To plot

$$
y=-f(x+3)
$$

we apply the following steps:

- Take the curve $y=f(x)$.
- Translate it to the left by $3$ units $y=f(x+3)$.

![](<../Source/4.2.10. Combining Reflections With Other Graph Transformations/Images/af7f809b90dd8fbb925a6d806bf40654.png>)

- Then, reflect the result across the $x$- axis to get the graph of $y=-f(x+3)$.

![](<../Source/4.2.10. Combining Reflections With Other Graph Transformations/Images/e5e97c1dc9e548ce0e81574df3854c46.png>)

## Identifying Graph Transformations Resulting From Reflections With Scaling

**Example:** The diagram below shows the function $y=f(x)$. Find the graph of $y=f\left(-3x\right)$.

![](<../Source/4.2.10. Combining Reflections With Other Graph Transformations/Images/3bdd6f612bf243d516ef1b43738f4240.png>)

**Explanation**

To perform transformations of functions with multiple operations, we must remember the following rule:

1. First, we perform the transformations *inside* the argument of $f$. We first carry out horizontal stretches and reflections, followed by horizontal translations.
2. Then, we perform the transformations *outside* the argument of $f$. We first carry out vertical stretches and reflections, followed by vertical translations.

First, let's factor out the minus sign next to the $x$ inside the function:

$$
y=f\left(-\left(3x\right)\right)
$$

To plot

$$
y=f\left(-\left(3x\right)\right)
$$

we apply the following steps:

- Take the curve $y=f(x)$.
- Stretch it by a scale factor of $\dfrac{1}{3}$ parallel to the $x$-axis to get the graph of $y=f\left(3x\right)$.

![](<../Source/4.2.10. Combining Reflections With Other Graph Transformations/Images/09be1c2416acc5d85272d91a4f41a9be.png>)

- Then, reflect it across the $y$-axis to get the graph of $y=f\left(-\left(3x\right)\right)$.

![](<../Source/4.2.10. Combining Reflections With Other Graph Transformations/Images/040bc1cca1c42fd6ceeb37d9c53d177a.png>)

## Identifying Graph Transformations Resulting From Reflections With Other Transformations

**Example:** The diagram below shows the function $y=f(x)$. Find the graph of $y=3f\left(-x+1\right)$?

![](<../Source/4.2.10. Combining Reflections With Other Graph Transformations/Images/bc4edd25452d9d3420b26515f2c658c7.png>)

**Explanation**

To perform transformations of functions with multiple operations, we must remember the following rule:

1. First, we perform the transformations *inside* the argument of $f$. We first carry out horizontal stretches and reflections, followed by horizontal translations.
2. Then, we perform the transformations *outside* the argument of $f$. We first carry out vertical stretches and reflections, followed by vertical translations.

First, let's factor out the negative sign next to the $x$ inside the function:

$$
y=3f\left(-(x-1)\right)
$$

To plot

$$
y=3f\left(-(x-1)\right)
$$

we apply the following steps:

- Take the curve $y=f(x)$.
- Reflect it across the $y$-axis to get the graph of $y=f\left(-x\right)$ (remember that multiplication comes *before* subtraction in the order of operations).

![](<../Source/4.2.10. Combining Reflections With Other Graph Transformations/Images/dfc36c10f52e75c1c9f8e7131063ac11.png>)

- Then, translate the result by $1$ unit to the right to get the graph of $y=f\left(-(x-1)\right)$.

![](<../Source/4.2.10. Combining Reflections With Other Graph Transformations/Images/290b1282b03482cffce9a9d529800427.png>)

- Finally, stretch the result by a scale factor of $3$ parallel to the $y$-axis to get the graph of $y=3f\left(-(x-1)\right)$.

![](<../Source/4.2.10. Combining Reflections With Other Graph Transformations/Images/a24b10f1b0ee88e93d23ff1769c8ffd1.png>)
