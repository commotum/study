## Introduction

Suppose we are given the graph of $y=f(x)$, shown below.

![](<../Source/4.2.8. Combining Graph Transformations- Three or More Operations/Images/959569c0ed26a3e3cf9dca94c30a1bf8.png>)

Given the graph of $y=f(x)$, how can we use graph transformations to plot the graph of

$$
y=f\left(2x-4\right)+1 \,
$$

In this particular situation, we have to combine several operations.

To perform transformations of functions with multiple operations, we must apply the following rule:

1. First, we perform the transformations *inside* the argument of $f$. We first carry out horizontal stretches, followed by horizontal translations.
2. Then, we perform the transformations *outside* the argument of $f$. We first carry out vertical stretches, followed by vertical translations.

In this case, we begin by factoring out the coefficient next to the $x$ inside the function:

$$
y = f\left(2x-4\right) +1 = f\left(2(x-2)\right) + 1
$$

Now, to plot

$$
y=f\left(2(x-2)\right) +1
$$

we apply the following steps:

- Take the curve $y=f(x)$.
- Stretch it by a scale factor of $\dfrac{1}{2}$ parallel to the $x$-axis to get the graph of $y=f(2x)$.

![](<../Source/4.2.8. Combining Graph Transformations- Three or More Operations/Images/5da3ee21b747ff3a66334e943a80af53.png>)

- Then, translate it to the right by $2$ units to get the graph of $y=f\left(2(x-2)\right)$.

![](<../Source/4.2.8. Combining Graph Transformations- Three or More Operations/Images/edfabdc0ce3aa55003c5a89b2e58a0f2.png>)

- There is no vertical scaling, so we omit this step.
- Finally, translate it up by $1$ unit to get the graph of $y=f\left(2(x-2)\right)+1$.

![](<../Source/4.2.8. Combining Graph Transformations- Three or More Operations/Images/d72618fd6b79e64857d9f0c6276696a3.png>)

## Identifying Graph Transformations Resulting From Three Operations

**Example:** The diagram below shows the function $y=f(x)$. Plot the graph of $y=f\left(2x+2\right)-1$.

![](<../Source/4.2.8. Combining Graph Transformations- Three or More Operations/Images/f75434e551ffb2fb0ffb4770bf107ab7.png>)

**Explanation**

To perform transformations of functions with multiple operations, we must remember the following rule:

1. First, we perform the transformations *inside* the argument of $f$. We first carry out horizontal stretches, followed by horizontal translations.
2. Then, we perform the transformations *outside* the argument of $f$. We first carry out vertical stretches, followed by vertical translations.

First, let's factor out the coefficient next to the $x$ inside the function:

$$
y = f\left(2x+2\right) -1 = f\left(2(x+1)\right) - 1
$$

To plot

$$
y=f\left(2(x+1)\right) - 1
$$

we apply the following steps:

- Take the curve $y=f(x)$.
- Stretch it by a scale factor of $\dfrac{1}{2}$ parallel to the $x$-axis to get the graph of $y=f(2x)$.

![](<../Source/4.2.8. Combining Graph Transformations- Three or More Operations/Images/7c816022b7167abef0fd9a1fa9eee326.png>)

- Then, translate it to the left by $1$ unit to get the graph of $y=f\left(2(x+1)\right)$.

![](<../Source/4.2.8. Combining Graph Transformations- Three or More Operations/Images/ef647b9d606659a96fb9c74d990db477.png>)

- Finally, translate it down by $1$ unit to get the graph of $y=f\left(2(x+1)\right)-1$.

![](<../Source/4.2.8. Combining Graph Transformations- Three or More Operations/Images/f574d096ed67e794d664275b3fb24b20.png>)

![](<../Source/4.2.8. Combining Graph Transformations- Three or More Operations/Images/f153d93ec3ad6b8142f54426c4b9ab15.png>)

**Explanation**

To perform transformations of functions with multiple operations, we must remember the following rule:

1. First, we perform the transformations *inside* the argument of $f$. We first carry out horizontal stretches, followed by horizontal translations.
2. Then, we perform the transformations *outside* the argument of $f$. We first carry out vertical stretches, followed by vertical translations.

To plot

$$
y=2f\left({3}(x+3)\right)+5

we apply the following steps:

- Take the curve $y=f(x)$.

![](<../Source/4.2.8. Combining Graph Transformations- Three or More Operations/Images/924a5125fa65f5a4ea9a44b12bb8f9ea.png>)

![](<../Source/4.2.8. Combining Graph Transformations- Three or More Operations/Images/bdf17bc966f4c54255125a8f24ae4ac9.png>)

## Determining the Correct Order of Transformations Given a Transformed Function Expression

**Example:** The graph of $y=f\left(\dfrac x2-3\right)+5$ can be obtained from the graph of $y=f(x)$ by applying **some** of the following transformations:

- $(\textrm{T}1)$ - Stretch by a scale factor of $2$ parallel to the $x$-axis
- $(\textrm{T}2)$ - Translate up by $5$ units
- $(\textrm{T}3)$ - Translate right by $6$ units
- $(\textrm{T}4)$ - Stretch by a scale factor of $\dfrac 12$ parallel to the $x$-axis

What is a possible ordering of the appropriate transformations?

**Explanation**

To perform transformations of functions with multiple operations, we must remember the following rule:

1. First, we perform the transformations *inside* the argument of $f$. We first carry out horizontal stretches, followed by horizontal translations.
2. Then, we perform the transformations *outside* the argument of $f$. We first carry out vertical stretches, followed by vertical translations.

First, let's factor out the coefficient next to the $x$ inside the function:

$$
y=f\left(\dfrac x2-3\right)+5 = f\left(\dfrac 12(x-6)\right)+5
$$

To get

$$
y=f\left(\dfrac 12(x-6)\right)+5
$$

we could apply the following steps:

- Take the curve $y=f(x)$.
- Apply transformation $(\textrm{T}1)$: Stretch $y=f(x)$ by a scale factor of $2$ parallel to the $x$-axis to get the graph of $y=f\left(\dfrac x2 \right)$.
- Apply transformation $(\textrm{T}3)$: Translate $y=f\left(\dfrac x2 \right)$ right by $6$ units to get the graph of $y=f\left(\dfrac12{(x-6)}\right)$.
- Apply transformation $(\textrm{T}2)$: Translate $y=f\left(\dfrac12{(x-6)}\right)$ up by $5$ units to get the graph of $y=f\left(\dfrac12{(x-6)}\right)+5$.

Therefore, a possible order of transformations is

$$
(\textrm{T}1) \to (\textrm{T}3) \to (\textrm{T}2)
$$
