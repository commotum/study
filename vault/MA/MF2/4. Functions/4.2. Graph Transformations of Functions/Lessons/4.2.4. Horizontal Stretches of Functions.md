## Introduction

Consider the function $y=f(x)$ whose graph is shown below. How can we plot the graph of $y=f(2x)$?

![](<../Source/4.2.4. Horizontal Stretches of Functions/Images/54b34afb66b90c1c47a9fade7c956a7b.png>)

We can use some points on the graph of $y=f(x)$ to find the corresponding points on the graphs of $y=f(2x)$.

For example, let's take the point $(-2,0)$. The fact that it lies on the graph of $y=f(x)$ means

$$
f(-2)=0
$$

Then, if

$$
x={\color{red}-1}
$$

we have

$$
f(2x) = f(2\cdot({\color{red}-1})) = f(-2) = 0
$$

This means the point $({\color{red}-2},0)$ that lies on $y=f(x)$ has a corresponding point $({\color{red}-1},0)$ that lies on $y=f(2x)$.

So, for each point on the graph of $y=f(x)$, there will be a point on the graph of $y=f(2x)$ with the *same* $y$-value and *one half* the $x$-value:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}Points on [math]y=f(x)[/math] & Points on [math]y=f({\color{red}2}x)[/math] \\ (-2, 0) & (-1, 0) \\ (0, 2) & (0, 2) \\ (1, 2) & (0.5, 2) \\ (4,-3) & (2,-3)\end{bmatrix}
$$

Finally, we can plot the points and draw the graph of

$$
y = f(2x){:}
$$

![](<../Source/4.2.4. Horizontal Stretches of Functions/Images/93bd9f97cc2b5bd149b961e7c0d7ad47.png>)

The graph of $y=f(2x)$ is the graph of $y=f(x)$ **horizontally scaled** by a factor of

$$
\dfrac{1}{2}
$$

Equivalently, we can say that the original graph is **compressed** by a scale factor of $2$.

In general, the transformation $y=f(kx)$ scales the graph of $y=f(x)$ horizontally by a factor of

$$
\dfrac{1}{k}
$$

- If $k$ is greater than $1$, the graph is compressed (or squished) towards the $y$-axis by a scale factor of $k$.
- If $k$ is between $0$ and $1$, the graph is stretched away from the $y$-axis by a scale factor of $k$.

## Plotting the Graph of a Horizontally Compressed Function

**Example:** The graph of $y=f(x)$ is shown below. Sketch the graph of $y=f(3x)$.

![](<../Source/4.2.4. Horizontal Stretches of Functions/Images/4b70db7109e9fcf360f4b5a63be06c86.png>)

**Explanation**

To graph the function $y=f(3x)$, we take the graph of $y=f(x)$ and compress it by factor of $3$ parallel to the $x$-axis. This has the effect of dividing the $x$-coordinates of all points on $y=f(x)$ by $3$.

The resulting graph is shown below.

![](<../Source/4.2.4. Horizontal Stretches of Functions/Images/b21ee1167c222f069d7c0174ecb36d74.png>)

## Finding a Point that Lies on a Horizontally Stretched Function

**Example:** The point $P$ with coordinates $(6,7)$ lies on the curve $y=f(x)$. Find a point that must lie on the graph of $y= f\left(\dfrac{1}{4}x\right)$.

**Explanation**

The function

$$
y = f\left(\dfrac{1}{4}x\right)
$$

represents a stretch of the function $y = f(x)$ by a factor of $4$ parallel to the $x$-axis. This has the effect of multiplying the $x$-coordinates of all points on $y=f(x)$ by $4$.

Therefore, the point $(6,7)$ on $f(x)$ is mapped to the point

$$
\left(4\cdot 6, 7\right) = (24,7)
$$

and this point must lie on the graph of

$$
y = f\left(\dfrac{1}{4}x\right)
$$
