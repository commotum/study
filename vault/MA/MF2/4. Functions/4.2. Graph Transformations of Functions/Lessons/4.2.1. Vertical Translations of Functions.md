## Introduction

Suppose that we have the graph of $y=f(x)$ shown below. What would the graph of

$$
y=f(x)+2
$$

look like, and how does it relate to the original curve?

![](<../Source/4.2.1. Vertical Translations of Functions/Images/0803df1c5fdec75f3223a4e522e4d6fc.png>)

For each point on the graph of $y=f(x)$ there is a corresponding point on the graph of

$$
y=f(x)+2
$$

with the same $x$-value and a $y$-value that is two units greater. So let's create a table of points.

$$
\begin{bmatrix}Points on [math]y=f(x)[/math] & Points on [math]y=f(x)+2[/math] \\ (-2, 0) & (-2, 2) \\ (0, 1.5) & (0, 3.5) \\ (1, 2) & (1, 4) \\ (3,-3) & (3,-1)\end{bmatrix}
$$

Every point on the graph of

$$
y=f(x)+2
$$

is precisely two units above a point on the graph of $y=f(x)$. The graph of

$$
y=f(x)+2
$$

is an **upward vertical translation** of the graph of $y=f(x)$ by two units.

![](<../Source/4.2.1. Vertical Translations of Functions/Images/6e651a73afdcc2c9e4efd34e9e012678.png>)

On the other hand, for each point on the graph of $y=f(x)$ there is a corresponding point on the graph of $y=f(x)-2$ with the same $x$-value and a $y$-value that is two units smaller. So let's create a table of points.

$$
\begin{bmatrix}Points on [math]y=f(x)[/math] & Points on [math]y=f(x)-2[/math] \\ (-2, 0) & (-2,-2) \\ (0, 1.5) & (0,-0.5) \\ (1, 2) & (1, 0) \\ (3,-3) & (3,-5)\end{bmatrix}
$$

Every point on the graph of $y=f(x)-2$ is two units below a point on the graph of $y=f(x)$. The graph of $y=f(x)-2$ is a **downward vertical translation** of the graph of $y=f(x)$ by two units.

![](<../Source/4.2.1. Vertical Translations of Functions/Images/be14d8e55b8224e03fd2fd33c70e0171.png>)

## Identifying the Graph of a Translated Function

**Example:** The graph of $y=f(x)$ is shown below and the point $(0,9)$ lies on the curve. Sketch the graph of $y=f(x)-2$.

![](<../Source/4.2.1. Vertical Translations of Functions/Images/c3fd1fe4349f3b792bfad2c0d3f703e2.png>)

**Explanation**

The graph of $y = f(x)$ passes through the point $(0, 9)$ as shown in the diagram.

The graph of $y=f(x)-2$ can be obtained from $y=f(x)$ by translating it by $2$ units *down*. So, it must pass through the point $(0,7)$.

The correct graph is shown below.

![](<../Source/4.2.1. Vertical Translations of Functions/Images/bc6ca5bf30c368776084befb23a3709c.png>)

## Identifying the Graph of an Original Function Given a Translated Function

**Example:** The graph of $y=f(x) +2$ is shown below. Sketch the graph of $y=f(x)$?

![](<../Source/4.2.1. Vertical Translations of Functions/Images/939ec13b29fa8621a970b056c8f81da5.png>)

**Explanation**

The graph of

$$
y = f(x) + 2
$$

can be obtained from the graph of $y = f(x)$ by translating $2$ units *up*.

So, the graph $y = f(x)$ would be $2$ units *below* the graph of

$$
y = f(x) + 2
$$

![](<../Source/4.2.1. Vertical Translations of Functions/Images/64ed58b375a181aea791fb3b34ab7e55.png>)

## Identifying the Shift

Consider the solid curve $y=f(x)$ and the dashed curve

$$
y=f(x)+b
$$

shown below. What is the value of $b$?

![](<../Source/4.2.1. Vertical Translations of Functions/Images/3166fc9920361d6ff20f4cb7f0710e82.png>)

The graph of

$$
y=f(x)+b
$$

can be obtained from the graph of $y=f(x)$ by translating it $6$ units down. So, $b=-6$.

## Identifying the Vertical Shift of a Translated Function

**Example:** The point $A$ with coordinates $(2,-1)$ lies on the curve $y = f(x)$ and the point $B$ with coordinates $(2,-4)$ lies on the curve $y = g(x)$. If the curve $y = g(x)$ is related to $y = f(x)$ via the transformation $g(x) = f(x) + k$, then what is the value of $k$?

**Explanation**

First, notice that the point $B(2, -4)$ lies $3$ units *below* the point $A(2, -1)$. So, the graph of $y = g(x)$ can be obtained from the graph of $y = f(x)$ by shifting it $3$ units *down*.

![](<../Source/4.2.1. Vertical Translations of Functions/Images/4571777d9831506b7114451f0e191d57.png>)

As a result, $k = -3$.

## Identifying Points that Must Lie on a Translated Curve

**Example:** The point $P$ with coordinates $(3,4)$ lies on the curve $y=f(x)$. Find a point that must lie on $y=f(x)+3$?

**Explanation**

**Method 1**

The graph of

$$
y = f(x)+3
$$

is obtained by translating the graph of $y=f(x)$ by $3$ units *up*.

Hence, if the point $(3,4)$ lies on $y=f(x)$, then the point

$$
(3,4+3) = (3,7)
$$

lies on

$$
y=f(x) + 3
$$

![](<../Source/4.2.1. Vertical Translations of Functions/Images/2d13d5b55d2715d77269b05d39c1a00e.png>)

**Method 2**

As $(3,4)$ lies on $y = f(x)$, we have that

$$
f(3) = 4
$$

Now, if $x = 3$ then

$$
\begin{aligned}
y &= f(3) + 3 \\
&= 4 + 3 \\
&= 7
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the point $(3,7)$ must lie on

$$
y = f(x) + 3
$$
