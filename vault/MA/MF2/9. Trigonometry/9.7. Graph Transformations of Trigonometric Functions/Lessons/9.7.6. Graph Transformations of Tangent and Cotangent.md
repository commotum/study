## Introduction

Let's consider the graph below that shows a section of the function

$$
y=\cot(2x)+D
$$

where $D$ is a constant.

![](<../Source/9.7.6. Graph Transformations of Tangent and Cotangent/Images/7a532fe194dca744bb63b1e0db92aad4.png>)

Let's determine the value of $D$ using our knowledge of graph transformations.

The constant $D$ represents a vertical shift of the function

$$
y=\cot\left(2x\right)
$$

From the graph, the given curve is a cotangent function $\cot(2x)$ that's been shifted **up** by $3$ units, as shown below.

![](<../Source/9.7.6. Graph Transformations of Tangent and Cotangent/Images/8f71ffe415bf5e43015e3480b2806089.png>)

So, the equation of the curve is

$$
y=\cot\left(2x\right)+3
$$

Therefore, $D =3$.

## Identifying the Vertical Shift of a Transformed Tangent or Cotangent Curve

**Example:** Finding the vertical shift $D$ of the function $y=\tan\left(\dfrac{x}{2}\right) + D$.

![](<../Source/9.7.6. Graph Transformations of Tangent and Cotangent/Images/ab2c131a3236009028a83f467f06267c.png>)

**Explanation**

The constant $D$ represents a vertical shift of the function

$$
y=\tan\left(\dfrac{x}{2}\right)
$$

From the graph, we see that the given curve shifted **down** by $1$ unit. So, the equation of the curve is

$$
y=\tan\left(\dfrac{x}{2}\right)-1
$$

Therefore, $D =-1$.

## Horizontal Shifts

Consider the graph below that shows a section of the function

$$
y=\dfrac{1}{2}\tan{(x+C)}, \quad -\dfrac{\pi}{2} < C < \dfrac{\pi}{2}
$$

where $C$ is a constant.

![](<../Source/9.7.6. Graph Transformations of Tangent and Cotangent/Images/f323e46220e43118d10e7662592e6acf.png>)

The constant $C$ represents a horizontal shift of the function

$$
y=\dfrac 1 2\tan x
$$

Recall that, for the graph of a general function $y=f(x){:}$

- a shift to the *right* by $c$ units results in the graph of the function $y=f(x-c)$, and
- a shift to the *left* by $c$ units results in the graph of the function $y=f(x+c)$.

The given graph shows a tangent function that's been shifted *right* by $1$ unit. We know this because the left asymptote is shifted $1$ unit to the right, as shown below.

![](<../Source/9.7.6. Graph Transformations of Tangent and Cotangent/Images/91fd2d339d7d9e078ca2499431954903.png>)

So, the equation of the curve is

$$
y=\frac 1 2\tan(x-1)
$$

Therefore, $C =-1$.

## Identifying the Horizontal Shift of a Transformed Tangent or Cotangent Curve

**Example:** The graph below shows a section of the function $y=\dfrac{1}{3}\cot{(x+C)}$, where $C$ is a constant. Given that $-\dfrac{\pi}{2} < C < \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, what is the value of $C$?

![](<../Source/9.7.6. Graph Transformations of Tangent and Cotangent/Images/0a7fd9d2a848b0d3bcc5822b49a9f41f.png>)

**Explanation**

The constant $C$ represents a horizontal shift of the function

$$
y=\dfrac 1 3\cot x
$$

From the graph, we see that the given curve shifted **left** by

$$
\dfrac{1}{2}
$$

units. So, the equation of the curve is

$$
y=\frac 1 3\cot\left(x+\dfrac{1}{2}\right)
$$

Therefore,

$$
C = \dfrac{1}{2}
$$

## Identifying the Horizontal Stretch of a Transformed Tangent or Cotangent Curve

**Example:** The graph below shows a section of the function $y=\tan{\left(Bx-1\right)}$, where $B$ is a constant. What is the value of $B$?

![](<../Source/9.7.6. Graph Transformations of Tangent and Cotangent/Images/21988811af1034953edcf94efd4f3762.png>)

**Explanation**

We can calculate the period $T$ by computing the difference in the $x$-values between two consecutive asymptotes. Therefore,

$$
T = (2+\pi) - \left(2-\pi\right) = 2\pi
$$

The formula for the period $T$ of the function

$$
y=A\tan(Bx + C) + D
$$

is given by

$$
T = \dfrac{\pi}{B}
$$

Applying the formula for the period, we get

$$
2\pi = \dfrac{\pi}{B} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad B = \dfrac{\pi}{2\pi} = \dfrac 1 2
$$

Therefore,

$$
B=\dfrac 1 2
$$
