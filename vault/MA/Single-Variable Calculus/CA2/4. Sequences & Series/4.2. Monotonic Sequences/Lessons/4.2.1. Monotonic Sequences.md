## Introduction

Let's consider the following sequence:

$$
a_{n} = \dfrac{1}{n}, \qquad n \geq 1
$$

This is a **decreasing sequence** because every term is less than (or equal to) the previous term. We can see this by plotting the first few terms.

![](<../Source/4.2.1. Monotonic Sequences/Images/2fcd8098a6bf534b5110e0fbc0ec74fb.png>)

Similarly, the sequence

$$
b_n = n, \qquad n \geq 1
$$

is an **increasing sequence** because every term is greater than (or equal to) the previous term.

![](<../Source/4.2.1. Monotonic Sequences/Images/3a93837edcccff116264d4287bd7e7b6.png>)

If a sequence is *either* increasing *or* decreasing, then we say that the sequence is **monotonic**.

Finally, let's consider the sequence

$$
c_n = (n-3)^2, \qquad n \geq 1
$$

![](<../Source/4.2.1. Monotonic Sequences/Images/5d4f1efe12966dc875343286758340fd.png>)

This sequence is **neither increasing nor decreasing**, because it is not increasing *for all*

$$
n\geq 1
$$

nor decreasing *for all*

$$
n\geq 1
$$

<a id="constant-sequences"></a>
## Constant Sequences

Constant sequences are *both* increasing *and* decreasing!

As an example, let's consider the following constant sequence:

$$
a_{n} = 1, \qquad n \geq 1
$$

![](<../Source/4.2.1. Monotonic Sequences/Images/59b21562965cfa7ba447ec2090963e82.png>)

Notice that

- $a_n$ is *increasing* because every term is greater than *or equal to* the previous term, and
- $a_n$ is *decreasing* because every term is less than *or equal to* the previous term.

Therefore, $a_n$ is both increasing and decreasing.

<a id="determining-whether-a-simple-sequence-is-monotonic"></a>
## Determining Whether a Simple Sequence Is Monotonic

**Example:** Which of the following sequences, defined for $n\geq 1$, are decreasing?

1. $a_n = -2^n$
2. $b_n = n$!
3. $c_n = \dfrac{2}{\sqrt[3]n}$

**Explanation**

Suppose the sequence $a_n$ is defined for

$$
n\geq 1
$$

- $a_n$ is **increasing** if every term is greater than or equal to the previous term.
- $a_n$ is **decreasing** if every term is less than or equal to the previous term.

With that in mind, let's consider each sequence in turn.

- The sequence $a_n$ is decreasing. Computing the first few terms, we have
$-2, \quad -4, \quad -8, \quad -16, \quad \ldots$
From here, we see that every term is less than the previous term.
- The sequence $b_n$ is *increasing*. Computing the first few terms, we have
$1, \quad 2, \quad 6, \quad 24, \quad \ldots$
From here, we see that every term is greater than the previous term.
- The sequence $c_n$ is decreasing. Computing the first few terms, we have
$\dfrac{2}{\sqrt[3]1}, \quad \dfrac{2}{\sqrt[3]2}, \quad \dfrac{2}{\sqrt[3]3}, \quad \dfrac{2}{\sqrt[3]4}, \quad \ldots$
From here, we see that as $n$ increases, the numerator remains constant while the denominator increases. So every term is less than the previous term.

Therefore, the correct answer is "$a_n$ and $c_n$ only."

<a id="using-the-difference-of-terms-to-check-whether-a-sequence-is-monotonic"></a>
## Using the Difference of Terms To Check Whether a Sequence Is Monotonic

Up to now, we've determined whether a sequence is increasing or decreasing by looking at the first few terms and observing the general trend. This approach is fine for very simple sequences.

For more complex sequences, we need a more rigorous approach.

Suppose the sequence $a_n$ is defined for

$$
n\geq 1
$$

To determine whether $a_n$ is increasing or decreasing, we can use the following definition:

- A sequence $a_n$ is *increasing* if every term is greater than or equal to the previous term. Therefore, for all $n\geq 1$, we have
$a_{n+1} \geq a_{n} \qquad \Longrightarrow\qquad a_{n+1} - a_n \geq 0$.
- A sequence $a_n$ is *decreasing* if every term is less than or equal to the previous term. Therefore, for all $n\geq 1$, we have
$a_{n+1} \leq a_{n} \qquad \Longrightarrow\qquad a_{n+1} - a_n \leq 0$.
- If neither of these conditions holds, the sequence is *neither increasing nor decreasing*.

Let's use this definition to show that the following sequence is increasing:

$$
a_n = n^2, \qquad n \geq 1
$$

Taking a general term and subtracting the previous term, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
a_{n + 1} - a_{n} &= (n + 1)^{2} - n^{2} \\
&= n^{2} + 2n + 1 - n^{2} \\
&= 2n + 1
\end{aligned}
$$

Notice that this is *positive* for

$$
n\geq 1
$$

To see this concretely, set

$$
b_n = 2n+1
$$

Computing the first few terms of $b_n$, we get
$3, \quad 5, \quad 7, \quad 9, \quad \ldots\,$
which is clearly positive for

$$
n\geq 1
$$

Therefore, we have shown that for

$$
n\geq 1
$$

we have

$$
b_n = a_{n+1} - a_n \geq 0
$$

This shows that $a_n = n^2$ for

$$
n\geq 1
$$

is increasing.

<a id="determining-whether-a-quadratic-sequence-is-monotonic"></a>
## Determining Whether a Quadratic Sequence Is Monotonic

**Example:** Given $a_n =3-2n -n^2$ for $n\geq 1$, which of the following statements are true?

1. $a_{n+1} - a_n \geq 0$ for all $n\geq 1$
2. $a_n$ is an increasing sequence
3. $a_n$ is a decreasing sequence

**Explanation**

Suppose the sequence $a_n$ is defined for

$$
n\geq 1
$$

To determine whether $a_n$ is increasing or decreasing, we can use the following test:

- If a sequence $a_n$ is *increasing,* then for all $n\geq 1$, we have
$a_{n+1} \geq a_{n} \qquad \Longrightarrow\qquad a_{n+1} - a_n \geq 0$.
- If a sequence $a_n$ is *decreasing,* then for all $n\geq 1$, we have
$a_{n+1} \leq a_{n} \qquad \Longrightarrow\qquad a_{n+1} - a_n \leq 0$.
- If neither of these conditions holds, the sequence is *neither increasing nor decreasing*.

With that in mind, let's analyze each statement.

- Statement I is false. Computing the difference $a_{n+1} - a_n$, we get
$a_{n + 1} - a_{n}|= 3 - 2(n + 1) - (n + 1)^{2} - (3 - 2n - n^{2}); = 3 - 2n - 2 - (n^{2} + 2n + 1) - 3 + 2n + n^{2}; = 3 - 2n - 2 - n^{2} - 2n - 1 - 3 + 2n + n^{2}; =-3 - 2n$
which is *negative* for all $n\geq 1$. To see this concretely, set $b_n = -3-2n$. Computing the first few terms of $b_n$ we get
$-5, \quad -7, \quad -9, \quad -11, \quad \ldots\,$.

- Statement II is false, whereas statement III is true. Since $a_{n+1} - a_n \leq 0$ for all $n\geq 1$, we can conclude that $a_n$ is a decreasing sequence.

Therefore, the correct answer is "III only."
