## Introduction

The region enclosed by the curves $y=x^4$ and $y=x$ is shown below. Suppose that this region is rotated $2\pi$ radians about the horizontal line $y=-1$, generating a solid of revolution. How can we write an integral expression that gives the volume of the solid?

![](<../Source/2.3.4. Volumes of Revolution Using the Washer Method- Rotation About Other Axes/Images/31197dd523b62157c140539a6c7bb69a.png>)

First, we want to determine the limits of integration. We find the intersections of the curves by solving the following equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^{4} &= x \\
x^{4} - x &= 0 \\
x(x^{3} - 1) &= 0 \\
x &= 0, 1
\end{aligned}
$$

Hence, our limits of integration are from $x=0$ to $x=1$.

Now, let's draw our vertical arrow through the given region. The arrow should be pointing away from the axis of rotation.

![](<../Source/2.3.4. Volumes of Revolution Using the Washer Method- Rotation About Other Axes/Images/d19fa225d96305c254c7fda3d1928585.png>)

The vertical arrow

- *enters* the region through the inner curve $y=x^4$, and
- *leaves* though the outer curve $y=x$.

Now, we can express the volume of our solid as the difference between the volumes of the solids generated by the outer and inner curves, as follows:

- **Step I.** Figure out the volume generated by the outer curve $y=x$.![](<../Source/2.3.4. Volumes of Revolution Using the Washer Method- Rotation About Other Axes/Images/d1cee24f623c577a2d488ff8bd022294.png>)

The corresponding volume is given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
V_{outer} &= π∫_{0}^{1}[(upper) - (lower)]^{2}dx \\
&= π∫_{0}^{1}[(x) - (-1)]^{2}dx \\
&= π∫_{0}^{1}[x + 1]^{2}dx
\end{aligned}
$$

- **Step II.** Figure out the volume generated by the inner curve $y=x^4$.![](<../Source/2.3.4. Volumes of Revolution Using the Washer Method- Rotation About Other Axes/Images/21c7edba167c8a95ae98dcb97788e78f.png>)

The corresponding volume is given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
V_{inner} &= π∫_{0}^{1}[(upper) - (lower)]^{2}dx \\
&= π∫_{0}^{1}[(x^{4}) - (-1)]^{2}dx \\
&= π∫_{0}^{1}[x^{4} + 1]^{2}dx
\end{aligned}
$$

- **Step III.** Find the difference.
Finally, we can write the volume of the original solid as
$V|= V_{outer} - V_{inner}; = π∫_{0}^{1}[x + 1]^{2}dx - π∫_{0}^{1}[x^{4} + 1]^{2}dx; = π∫_{0}^{1}([x + 1]^{2} - [x^{4} + 1]^{2})dx$.

## Identifying an Integral Representing the Volume of a Solid Generated by Rotation About a Horizontal Line

**Example:**

![](<../Source/2.3.4. Volumes of Revolution Using the Washer Method- Rotation About Other Axes/Images/c6029cbadb68e1c7a7ba64da6cc16573.png>)

**Explanation**

First, let's draw our vertical arrow through the given region. The arrow should be pointing away from the axis of rotation.

![](<../Source/2.3.4. Volumes of Revolution Using the Washer Method- Rotation About Other Axes/Images/26d52e22b6284d5f166889cd0f35f3ba.png>)

The vertical arrow

- *enters* the region through the inner curve $y=-2x$, and
- *leaves* though the outer curver $y=2x^2$.

Now, we can find the volume of our solid as the difference between the volumes of the solids generated by the outer and inner curves.

- **Step I.** Figure out the volume generated by the outer curve $y=2x^2$.![](<../Source/2.3.4. Volumes of Revolution Using the Washer Method- Rotation About Other Axes/Images/f9f05120045a6cb1ab54a5d1f513735c.png>)

The corresponding volume is given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
V_{outer} &= π∫_{-1}^{0}[(upper) - (lower)]^{2}dx \\
&= π∫_{-1}^{0}[(3) - (2x^{2})]^{2}dx \\
&= π∫_{-1}^{0}[3 - 2x^{2}]^{2}dx
\end{aligned}
$$

- **Step II.** Figure out the volume generated by the inner curve $y=-2x$.

![](<../Source/2.3.4. Volumes of Revolution Using the Washer Method- Rotation About Other Axes/Images/c397de2c500adcfa747a095cbd9033a8.png>)

The corresponding volume is given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
V_{inner} &= π∫_{-1}^{0}[(upper) - (lower)]^{2}dx \\
&= π∫_{-1}^{0}[(3) - (-2x)]^{2}dx \\
&= π∫_{-1}^{0}[3 + 2x]^{2}dx
\end{aligned}
$$

- **Step III.** Find the difference.Finally, we can write the volume of the original solid as
$V|= V_{outer} - V_{inner}; = π∫_{-1}^{0}[3 - 2x^{2}]^{2}dx - π∫_{-1}^{0}[3 + 2x]^{2}dx; = π∫_{-1}^{0}([3 - 2x^{2}]^{2} - [3 + 2x]^{2})dx$.

## Computing the Volume of a Solid Generated by Rotation About a Horizontal Line

**Example:** The region below enclosed by the curves $y=\sqrt x$ and $y=x$ is rotated $2\pi$ radians about the line $y=1$. Find the volume of the resulting solid.

![](<../Source/2.3.4. Volumes of Revolution Using the Washer Method- Rotation About Other Axes/Images/fd2a465e5383ce2e54d3ee87f7eb4128.png>)

**Explanation**

First, we want to determine the limits of integration. We find the intersections of the curves by solving the following equation:

$$
\begin{aligned} x & =\sqrt x \\[3pt] x-\sqrt x & = 0 \\[3pt] \sqrt x \left(\sqrt x - 1\right) &= 0 \\[3pt] x& = 0, 1 \end{aligned}
$$

Hence, our limits of integration are from $x=0$ to $x=1$.

Now, let's draw our vertical arrow through the given region. The arrow should be pointing away from the axis of rotation.

![](<../Source/2.3.4. Volumes of Revolution Using the Washer Method- Rotation About Other Axes/Images/216e98318140f5524c172e89271e836a.png>)

The vertical arrow

- *enters* the region through the inner curve $y=\sqrt{x}$, and
- *leaves* though the outer curver $y=x$.

Next, we can find the volume of our solid as the difference between the volumes of the solids generated by the outer and inner curves.

- **Step I.** Figure out the volume generated by the outer curve $y=x$.![](<../Source/2.3.4. Volumes of Revolution Using the Washer Method- Rotation About Other Axes/Images/e2d2e4b6607e0f4e076535b6ff651b03.png>)

The corresponding volume is given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
V_{outer} &= π∫_{0}^{1}[(upper) - (lower)]^{2}dx \\
&= π∫_{0}^{1}[(1) - (x)]^{2}dx \\
&= π∫_{0}^{1}[1 - x]^{2}dx
\end{aligned}
$$

- **Step II.** Figure out the volume generated by the inner curve $y=\sqrt{x}$.![](<../Source/2.3.4. Volumes of Revolution Using the Washer Method- Rotation About Other Axes/Images/ae9f20b7511c5ffe0d37ae0e1e41b9e2.png>)
The corresponding volume is given by
$V_{inner}|= π∫_{0}^{1}[(upper) - (lower)]^{2}dx; = π∫_{0}^{1}[(1) - (\sqrt{x})]^{2}dx; = π∫_{0}^{1}[1 - \sqrt{x}]^{2}dx$.
- **Step III.** Find the difference.Finally, we can write the volume of the original solid as
$\begin{aligned} V &= {\color{blue}V_{\text{outer}}} - {\color{red}V_{\text{inner}}} \\[5pt] & = \pi \int_0^1 [1-x]^2 - \pi \int_0^1 [1-\sqrt x]^2\,\textrm d x \\[5pt] & = \pi \int_0^1 \left((1-x)^2 - (1-\sqrt x)^2\right) \,\textrm d x \\[5pt] & = \pi \int_0^1 (x^2-3x+2\sqrt x) \,\,\textrm d x \\[5pt] & = \pi\left. \left(\dfrac {1} 3 x^3 - \dfrac{3}{2} x^2 + \dfrac{4}{3} x^{3/2} \ \right)\right|_0^1 \\[5pt] & = \pi \left[\left(\dfrac {1}3 (1)^3 - \dfrac{3}{2}(1)^2 + \dfrac{4}{3}(1)^{3/2} \right) - (0) \right] \\[5pt] & = \pi \left(\dfrac{1}3 - \dfrac{3}{2} +\dfrac{4}{3} \right) \\[5pt] & = \dfrac {\pi}{6}. \end{aligned}$

## Rotation About a Vertical Line

Now, suppose that the region enclosed by the curves

$$
y=\dfrac x2
$$

and

$$
y=\sqrt x
$$

is rotated $360^\circ$ about the vertical line $x=-2$. Let's find the volume of the resulting solid.

![](<../Source/2.3.4. Volumes of Revolution Using the Washer Method- Rotation About Other Axes/Images/98b0223052c8f1a102bc48e8b469a832.png>)

First, since we are rotating about a vertical line, we need to solve the equations of our curves for $x$:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}y = \frac{x}{2} & ⟹x = 2y \\ y = \sqrt{x} & ⟹x = y^{2}\end{bmatrix}
$$

Now, we want to determine the limits of integration. We find the intersections of the curves by solving the following equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
2y &= y^{2} \\
y^{2} - 2y &= 0 \\
y(y - 2) &= 0 \\
y &= 0, 2
\end{aligned}
$$

Hence, our limits of integration are from $y=0$ to $y={2}$.

Now, let's draw a horizontal arrow through the given region. The arrow should be pointing away from the axis of rotation.

![](<../Source/2.3.4. Volumes of Revolution Using the Washer Method- Rotation About Other Axes/Images/14120b0298bab645bb807b52babbe446.png>)

The horizontal arrow

- *enters* the region through the inner curve $x={y^2}$, and
- *leaves* though the outer curve $x=2y$.

Next, we can find the volume of our solid as the difference between the volumes of the solids generated by the outer and inner curves.

- **Step I.** Figure out the volume generated by the outer curve $x=2y$.![](<../Source/2.3.4. Volumes of Revolution Using the Washer Method- Rotation About Other Axes/Images/f37b8660aa6a29b391b900526d61d7f1.png>)

The corresponding volume is given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
V_{outer} &= π∫_{0}^{2}[(right) - (left)]^{2}dy \\
&= π∫_{0}^{2}[(2y) - (-2)]^{2}dy \\
&= π∫_{0}^{2}[2y + 2]^{2}dy
\end{aligned}
$$

- **Step II.** Figure out the volume generated by the inner curve $x=y^2$.![](<../Source/2.3.4. Volumes of Revolution Using the Washer Method- Rotation About Other Axes/Images/63a7a2618c8c9cd79c6d16b7c9a6a80f.png>)

The corresponding volume is given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
V_{inner} &= π∫_{0}^{2}[(right) - (left)]^{2}dy \\
&= π∫_{0}^{2}[(y^{2}) - (-2)]^{2}dy \\
&= π∫_{0}^{2}[y^{2} + 2]^{2}dy
\end{aligned}
$$

- **Step III.** Find the difference.Finally, we can write the volume of the original solid as
$V|= V_{outer} - V_{inner}; = π∫_{0}^{2}[2y + 2]^{2}dy - π∫_{0}^{2}[y^{2} + 2]^{2}dy; = π∫_{0}^{2}([2y + 2]^{2} - [y^{2} + 2]^{2})dy; = π∫_{0}^{2}(-y^{4} + 8y)dy; = π(-\frac{y^{5}}{5} + 4y^{2}) \mid _{0}^{2}; = π[(-\frac{32}{5} + 16) - 0]; = \frac{48π}{5}$.

## Identifying an Integral Representing the Volume of a Solid Generated by Rotation About a Vertical Line

**Example:** The region enclosed by the curves $x=-y^2$ and $x = -y$ is rotated $360^\circ$ about the line $x={1}$. Find an integral that gives the volume of the resulting solid.

![](<../Source/2.3.4. Volumes of Revolution Using the Washer Method- Rotation About Other Axes/Images/17004c969dc6d172be8adda2f1bb5ed4.png>)

**Explanation**

First, we want to determine the limits of integration. We find the intersections of the curves by solving the following equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
-y^{2} &= -y \\
y^{2} - y &= 0 \\
y(y - 1) &= 0 \\
y &= 0, 1
\end{aligned}
$$

Hence, our limits of integration are from $y=0$ to $y=1$.

Now, let's draw a horizontal arrow through the given region. The arrow should be pointing away from the axis of rotation.

![](<../Source/2.3.4. Volumes of Revolution Using the Washer Method- Rotation About Other Axes/Images/b361a2a80ddd940e3279d2705a30d50f.png>)

The horizontal arrow

- *enters* the region through the inner curve $x=-{y}^2$, and
- *leaves* though the outer curve $x=-y$.

Next, we can find the volume of our solid as the difference between the volumes of the solids generated by the outer and inner curves.

- **Step I.** Figure out the volume generated by the outer curve $x=-y$.![](<../Source/2.3.4. Volumes of Revolution Using the Washer Method- Rotation About Other Axes/Images/84922146f426e6452d16eeb9e4227e2c.png>)

The corresponding volume is given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
V_{outer} &= π∫_{0}^{1}[(right) - (left)]^{2}dy \\
&= π∫_{0}^{1}[(1) - (-y)]^{2}dy \\
&= π∫_{0}^{1}[1 + y]^{2}dy
\end{aligned}
$$

- **Step II.** Figure out the volume generated by the inner curve $x=-{y}^2$.![](<../Source/2.3.4. Volumes of Revolution Using the Washer Method- Rotation About Other Axes/Images/7e9862a23d595f6d574104bf6d76f1af.png>)
The corresponding volume is given by
$V_{inner}|= π∫_{0}^{1}[(right) - (left)]^{2}dy; = π∫_{0}^{1}[(1) - (-y^{2})]^{2}dy; = π∫_{0}^{1}[1 + y^{2}]^{2}dy$.
- **Step III.** Find the difference.Finally, we can write the volume of the original solid as
$V|= V_{outer} - V_{inner}; = π∫_{0}^{1}[1 + y]^{2}dy - π∫_{0}^{1}[1 + y^{2}]^{2}dy; = π∫_{0}^{1}([1 + y]^{2} - [1 + y^{2}]^{2})dy$.

## Computing the Volume of a Solid Generated by Rotation About a Vertical Line

**Example:** The region enclosed by the curves $x=y^2$ and $x=2-y^2$ is rotated $2\pi$ radians about the line $x=-1$. Find the volume of the resulting solid.

![](<../Source/2.3.4. Volumes of Revolution Using the Washer Method- Rotation About Other Axes/Images/36f70d6dcd168a3ad0e72624444b487f.png>)

**Explanation**

First, to determine the limits of integration, we find the intersections of the curves by solving the following equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
y^{2} &= 2 - y^{2} \\
2y^{2} &= 2 \\
y^{2} &= 1 \\
y &= ± 1
\end{aligned}
$$

Hence, our limits of integration are from $y=-1$ to $y=1$.

Next, let's draw a horizontal arrow through the given region. The arrow should be pointing away from the axis of rotation.

![](<../Source/2.3.4. Volumes of Revolution Using the Washer Method- Rotation About Other Axes/Images/47807a09452165fc286ec5ca7b7d5db5.png>)

The horizontal arrow

- *enters* the region through the inner curve $x=y^2$, and
- *leaves* though the outer curve $x={2-y^2}$.

Next, we can find the volume of our solid as the difference between the volumes of outer and inner solids.

- **Step I.** Figure out the volume generated by the outer curve $x={2-y^2}$.![](<../Source/2.3.4. Volumes of Revolution Using the Washer Method- Rotation About Other Axes/Images/e316b962c107bdc757117251c126cedf.png>)
The corresponding volume is given by
$V_{outer}|= π∫_{-1}^{1}[(right) - (left)]^{2}dy; = π∫_{-1}^{1}[(2 - y^{2}) - (-1)]^{2}dy; = π∫_{-1}^{1}[3 - y^{2}]^{2}dy$.
- **Step II.** Figure out the volume generated by the inner curve $x=y^2$.![](<../Source/2.3.4. Volumes of Revolution Using the Washer Method- Rotation About Other Axes/Images/95f6918d6ec3592d8a6e05998dc28886.png>)
The corresponding volume is given by
$V_{inner}|= π∫_{-1}^{1}[(right) - (left)]^{2}dy; = π∫_{-1}^{1}[(y^{2}) - (-1)]^{2}dy; = π∫_{-1}^{1}[y^{2} + 1]^{2}dy$.
- **Step III.** Find the difference.Finally, we can write the volume of the original solid as
$V|= V_{outer} - V_{inner}; = π∫_{-1}^{1}[3 - y^{2}]^{2}dx - π∫_{-1}^{1}[y^{2} + 1]^{2}dy; = π∫_{-1}^{1}([3 - y^{2}]^{2} - [y^{2} + 1]^{2})dy; = π∫_{-1}^{1}(8 - 8y^{2})dy; = π(8y - \frac{8y^{3}}{3}) \mid _{-1}^{1}; = π[(8 - \frac{8}{3}) - (-8 + \frac{8}{3})]; = π(\frac{32}{3}); = \frac{32π}{3}$.
