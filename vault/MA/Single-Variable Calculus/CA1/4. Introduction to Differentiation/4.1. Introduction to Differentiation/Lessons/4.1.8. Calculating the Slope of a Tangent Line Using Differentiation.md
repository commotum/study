## Introduction

Remember that the derivative of a function $f(x)$ represents the slope of the tangent line to the curve $y=f(x)$.

For example, consider the curve $y=x^2$, shown below. If we want to compute the slope of the tangent to the curve at the point $A(1,1)$, then we compute $f'(x)$ and evaluate $f'(1)$.

![](<../Source/4.1.8. Calculating the Slope of a Tangent Line Using Differentiation/Images/84a150396a9b51534ed1019a310090eb.png>)

If

$$
f(x)=x^2
$$

then $f'(x) = 2 x$ by the power rule. Substituting $x = 1$ into $f'(x)$ gives

$$
f'(1) = 2 (1) = 2
$$

Therefore, the slope of the tangent to the curve $y=x^2$ at the point $A$ is $2$.

## Calculating the Slope of a Tangent Line of a Curve at a Given Point

**Example:** Find the slope of the tangent to the curve $y = x^2 + \dfrac {1}{x}$ at the point where $x=2$.

**Explanation**

Let

$$
f(x)=x^2+\dfrac 1 x
$$

The slope of the tangent at $x=2$ is equal to $f'(2)$. Computing the derivative, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
f^{′}(x) &= \frac{d}{dx}(x^{2} + \frac{1}{x}) \\
&= \frac{d}{dx}(x^{2} + x^{-1}) \\
&= 2 \cdot x - x^{-2} \\
&= 2x - x^{-2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we evaluate at $x=2$ and get

$$
\begin{aligned}
f^{′}(2) &= 2(2) - (2)^{-2} \\
&= 4 - \frac{1}{4} \\
&= \frac{15}{4}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the slope of the tangent to the curve is

$$
\dfrac{15}{4}
$$

## Calculating the Slope of a Tangent Line of a Curve With Fractional Powers at a Given Point

**Example:** What is the slope of the tangent to the curve $y = x^4 + 3\sqrt [3]{x^2}$ at the point $(1,4)$?

**Explanation**

This time, we'll use

$$
\dfrac{\textrm d y}{\textrm d x}
$$

notation. First, we compute the derivative, which gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{dy}{dx} &= \frac{d}{dx}(x^{4} + 3\sqrt[3]{x^{2}}) \\
&= \frac{d}{dx}(x^{4} + 3x^{2/3}) \\
&= 4 \cdot x^{3} + 3 \cdot \frac{2}{3} \cdot x^{-1/3} \\
&= 4x^{3} + 2x^{-1/3}
\end{aligned}
$$

Evaluating at $x=1$ gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\left.\frac{dy}{dx}\right|_{x = 1} &= 4(1)^{3} + 2(1)^{-1/3} \\
&= 4 + 2 \\
&= 6
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the slope of the tangent to the curve is $6$.

## Finding a Point on a Curve With a Certain Slope

**Example:** Find the $x$-coordinate of the point on the curve $y=3x^2+1$ where the tangent line has a slope of $-1$.

**Explanation**

Let

$$
f(x)=3x^2+1
$$

The slope of the tangent at the point $x$ is equal to $f'(x)$. Computing the derivative, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
f^{′}(x) &= \frac{d}{dx}(3x^{2} + 1) \\
&= 3\frac{d}{dx}(x^{2}) + \frac{d}{dx}(1) \\
&= 3 \cdot 2x + 0 \\
&= 6x + 0 \\
&= 6x
\end{aligned}
$$

We now set $f'(x) = -1$ and solve for $x$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
f^{′}(x) &= -1 \\
6x &= -1 \\
x &= -\frac{1}{6}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the slope of the tangent is equal to $-1$ at the point where

$$
x=-\dfrac 1 6
$$
