## Introduction

We can calculate the derivatives of the inverse reciprocal trigonometric functions by using their definition and the chain rule, similar to how we did for the inverse trigonometric functions. When we do so, we get the following results:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{d}{dx}(arcsecx) &= \frac{1}{\mid x\begin{vmatrix}\sqrt{x^{2} - 1}} \\ \frac{d}{dx}(arccscx)|=-\frac{1}{\mid x\end{vmatrix}\sqrt{x^{2} - 1}} \\
\frac{d}{dx}(arccotx) &= -\frac{1}{1 + x^{2}}
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="differentiating-inverse-reciprocal-trigonometric-functions"></a>
## Differentiating Inverse Reciprocal Trigonometric Functions

**Example:** Calculate $\dfrac {\textrm{d}y}{\textrm{d}x}$ if $y = 2 \,\textrm {arccot} x$.

**Explanation**

The formula for the derivative of $\textrm{arccot}$ is

$$
\dfrac{\textrm d}{\textrm d x}(\textrm {arccot} x) = -\dfrac 1 {1+x^2}
$$

Using the formula above, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{dy}{dx} &= \frac{d}{dx}(2arccotx) \\
&= 2 \cdot \frac{d}{dx}(arccotx) \\
&= 2 \cdot (-\frac{1}{1 + x^{2}}) \\
&=-\frac{2}{1 + x^{2}}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Differentiating Inverse Reciprocal Trigonometric Functions Using the Chain Rule

**Example:** Find $f'(x)$ if $f(x) =2 \, \textrm {arccot} (x^2 + 5)$.

**Explanation**

The formula for the derivative of $\textrm{arccot}$ is

$$
\dfrac{\textrm d}{\textrm d x}(\textrm {arccot} x) = -\dfrac 1 {1+x^2}
$$

Using the chain rule and the formula above, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
f^{′}(x) &= \frac{d}{dx}(2arccot(x^{2} + 5)) \\
&= 2 \cdot (- (1)/(1 + (x^{2} + 5)^{2})) \cdot \frac{d}{dx}(x^{2} + 5) \\
&= - (2)/(1 + (x^{2} + 5)^{2}) \cdot 2x \\
&= - (4x)/(1 + (x^{2} + 5)^{2}) \\
&=-\frac{4x}{x^{4} + 10x^{2} + 26}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Differentiating Inverse Reciprocal Trigonometric Functions Using the Product Rule

**Example:** If $f(x) =3 x\, \textrm {arccsc}x$, calculate $f'(x)$.

**Explanation**

The formula for the derivative of $\textrm{arccsc}$ is

$$
\dfrac{\textrm d}{\textrm d x}(\textrm {arccsc} x) = -\dfrac 1 {\mid x \mid \sqrt {x^2 - 1}} \,
$$

Using the product rule and the formula above, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
f^{′}(x) &= \frac{d}{dx}(3xarccscx) \\
&= 3\frac{d}{dx}(xarccscx) \\
&= 3(x \cdot \frac{d}{dx}(arccscx) + arccscx \cdot \frac{d}{dx}(x)) \\
&= 3x(-\frac{1}{\mid x\begin{vmatrix}\sqrt{x^{2} - 1}}) + 3arccscx \cdot 1 \\ =-\frac{3x}{\mid x\end{vmatrix}\sqrt{x^{2} - 1}} + 3arccscx
\end{aligned}
$$

## Differentiating Inverse Reciprocal Trigonometric Functions Using the Quotient Rule

**Example:** If $f(x) = \dfrac{\textrm{arcsec} x}{\textrm{arccsc} x}$, calculate $f'(x)$.

**Explanation**

Here, we let

$$
u(x) = \textrm{arcsec} x
$$

and

$$
v(x) = \textrm{arccsc} x
$$

, and apply the quotient rule.

We use the formulae

$$
u'(x) = \dfrac 1 {\begin{vmatrix}x & \sqrt {x^2 - 1}} \quad \text{and} \quad v'(x) = -\dfrac 1 {\mid x\end{vmatrix} \sqrt {x^2 - 1}}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
f^{′}(x) &= (u^{′}(x) \cdot v(x) - u(x) \cdot v^{′}(x))/([v(x)]^{2}) \\
&= (\frac{1}{\begin{vmatrix}x & \sqrt{x^{2} - 1}} \cdot arccscx - arcsecx \cdot (-\frac{1}{\mid x\end{vmatrix}\sqrt{x^{2} - 1}}))/(arccsc^{2}x) \\
&= (\frac{1}{\mid x\begin{vmatrix}\sqrt{x^{2} - 1}})\frac{arccscx + arcsecx}{arccsc^{2}x} \\ = \frac{arccscx + arcsecx}{\mid x\end{vmatrix}\sqrt{x^{2} - 1}arccsc^{2}x}
\end{aligned}
$$
