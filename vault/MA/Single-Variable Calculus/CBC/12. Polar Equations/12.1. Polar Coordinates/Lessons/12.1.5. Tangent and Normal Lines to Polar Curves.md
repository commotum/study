## Introduction

![](<../Source/12.1.5. Tangent and Normal Lines to Polar Curves/Images/1265bc68fc54501abac991d267434ac6.png>)

The polar curve shown above is defined as

$$
r(\theta) = 4 - 3\cos \theta, \qquad 0\leq \theta < 2\pi
$$

We have

$$
\theta = \dfrac{\pi}{2}
$$

at the point $P$. The tangent to the curve at $P$ is shown in the diagram.

To find the equation of the tangent line to the curve at $P$, we follow four steps.

**Step 1**: Calculate the derivative

$$
\dfrac{\textrm{d}y}{\textrm{d}x}
$$

Using techniques that we've previously discussed, we can show that

$$
y^{′}(θ) = 4\cos θ - 3\cos 2θ, x^{′}(θ) = 3\sin 2θ - 4\sin θ
$$

Therefore, we can find

$$
\dfrac{\textrm{d}y}{\textrm{d}x}
$$

as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{dy}{dx} &= (y^{′}(θ))/(x^{′}(θ)) \\
&= \frac{4\cos θ - 3\cos 2θ}{3\sin 2θ - 4\sin θ}
\end{aligned}
$$

**Step 2**: Find the slope of the tangent at $P$.

The slope of the tangent line is

$$
\begin{aligned}
m &= \frac{dy}{dx} \mid _{θ = π/2} \\
&= (4\cos (\frac{π}{2}) - 3\cos (2 \cdot \frac{π}{2}))/(3\sin (2 \cdot \frac{π}{2}) - 4\sin (\frac{π}{2})) \\
&= (4\cos (\frac{π}{2}) - 3\cos (π))/(3\sin (π) - 4\sin (\frac{π}{2})) \\
&= \frac{0 + 3}{0 - 4} \\
&=-\frac{3}{4}
\end{aligned}
$$

**Step 3**: Find the Cartesian coordinates of $P$.

We compute the $xy$-coordinates of $P$ as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_{1} &= r\cos θ \\
&= (4 - 3\cos θ)\cos θ \\
&= (4 - 3\cos (\frac{π}{2}))\cos (\frac{π}{2}) \\
&= 0 \\
y_{1} &= r\sin θ \\
&= (4 - 3\cos θ)\sin θ \\
&= (4 - 3\cos (\frac{π}{2}))\sin (\frac{π}{2}) \\
&= 4
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the coordinates of $P$ are

$$
\left(0, 4 \right)
$$

**Step 4**: Using point-slope form, find the equation of the tangent.

We find the equation of the tangent as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
y - y_{1} &= m(x - x_{1}) \\
y - 4 &= -\frac{3}{4} \cdot (x - 0) \\
y &= -\frac{3}{4}x + 4
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding Tangent Lines to Polar Curves

**Example:** Find the equation of the tangent line to the polar curve $r = 1+2\cos\theta$ at $\theta = \dfrac{\pi}{2}$, given that

$\dfrac{\textrm d y}{\textrm d x} = -\dfrac{\cos\theta + 2\cos(2\theta)}{\sin\theta + 2\sin(2\theta)}$.

**Explanation**

To find the equation of the tangent line to a polar curve at a point $P$, we follow four steps:

**Step 1**: Calculate the derivative

$$
\dfrac{\textrm{d}y}{\textrm{d}x}
$$

In this case, we're given that

$$
\dfrac{\textrm d y}{\textrm d x} = -\dfrac{\cos\theta + 2\cos(2\theta)}{\sin\theta + 2\sin(2\theta)}
$$

**Step 2**: Find the slope of the tangent at $P$.

The slope of the tangent line is

$$
\begin{aligned}
m &= \frac{dy}{dx} \mid _{θ = π/2} \\
&= - (\cos (\frac{π}{2}) + 2\cos (π))/(\sin (\frac{π}{2}) + 2\sin (π)) \\
&= - (0 + 2(-1))/(1 + 2(0)) \\
&= 2
\end{aligned}
$$

**Step 3**: Find the Cartesian coordinates of $P$.

We compute the $xy$-coordinates of $P$ as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_{1} &= r\cos θ \\
&= (1 + 2\cos θ)\cos θ \\
&= (1 + 2\cos (\frac{π}{2}))\cos (\frac{π}{2}) \\
&= (1 + 2 \cdot 0) \cdot 0 \\
&= 0 \\
y_{1} &= r\sin θ \\
&= (1 + 2\cos θ)\sin θ \\
&= (1 + 2\cos (\frac{π}{2}))\sin (\frac{π}{2}) \\
&= (1 + 2 \cdot 0) \cdot 1 \\
&= 1 \cdot 1 \\
&= 1
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the coordinates of $P$ are $(0,1)$.

**Step 4**: Using point-slope form, find the equation of the tangent.

We find the equation of the tangent as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
y - y_{1} &= m(x - x_{1}) \\
y - 1 &= 2(x - 0) \\
y &= 2x + 1
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding Normal Lines to Polar Curves

**Example:** Find the equation of the **normal** line to the polar curve $r = 1 - 2\cos\theta$ at $\theta = \dfrac{3\pi}{2}$, given that
$y'(\theta) = \cos\theta-2\cos(2\theta), \qquad x'(\theta) = 2\sin(2\theta) - \sin\theta$.

![](<../Source/12.1.5. Tangent and Normal Lines to Polar Curves/Images/cedada86002832a7b352cc49dec2ab3b.png>)

**Explanation**

To find the equation of the normal line to a polar curve at a point $P$, we follow four steps:

**Step 1**: Calculate the derivative

$$
\dfrac{\textrm{d}y}{\textrm{d}x}
$$

In this case, we're given that

$$
y'(\theta) = \cos\theta-2\cos(2\theta), \qquad x'(\theta) = 2\sin(2\theta) - \sin\theta
$$

Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{dy}{dx} &= (y^{′}(θ))/(x^{′}(θ)) \\
&= (\cos θ - 2\cos (2θ))/(2\sin (2θ) - \sin θ)
\end{aligned}
$$

**Step 2**: Find the slope of the tangent at $P$.

The slope of the tangent line is

$$
\begin{aligned}
m &= \frac{dy}{dx} \mid _{θ = 3π/2} \\
&= ((\cos (\frac{3π}{2}) - 2\cos (2 \cdot \frac{3π}{2})))/((2\sin (2 \cdot \frac{3π}{2}) - \sin (\frac{3π}{2}))) \\
&= ((\cos (\frac{3π}{2}) - 2\cos (3π)))/((2\sin (3π) - \sin (\frac{3π}{2}))) \\
&= (0 - 2(-1))/(0 - (-1)) \\
&= \frac{0 + 2}{0 + 1} \\
&= 2
\end{aligned}
$$

**Step 3**: Find the Cartesian coordinates of $P$.

We compute the $xy$-coordinates of $P$ as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_{1} &= r\cos θ \\
&= (1 - 2\cos θ)\cos θ \\
&= (1 - 2\cos (\frac{3π}{2}))\cos (\frac{3π}{2}) \\
&= (1 - 2 \cdot 0) \cdot 0 \\
&= 0 \\
y_{1} &= r\sin θ \\
&= (1 - 2\cos θ)\sin θ \\
&= (1 - 2\cos (\frac{3π}{2}))\sin (\frac{3π}{2}) \\
&= (1 - 2 \cdot 0) \cdot (-1) \\
&= 1 \cdot (-1) \\
&=-1
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the coordinates of $P$ are

$$
\left(0, -1 \right)
$$

**Step 4**: Using point-slope form, find the equation of the normal.

We find the equation of the normal as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
y - y_{1} &= -\frac{1}{m}(x - x_{1}) \\
y - (-1) &= -\frac{1}{2} \cdot (x - 0) \\
y + 1 &= -\frac{1}{2}x \\
y &= -\frac{1}{2}x - 1
\end{aligned}
$$
