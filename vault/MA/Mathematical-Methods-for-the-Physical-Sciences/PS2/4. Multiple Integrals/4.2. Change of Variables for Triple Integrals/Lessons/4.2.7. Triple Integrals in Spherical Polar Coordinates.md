## Introduction

When evaluating triple integrals, it's sometimes easier to change the variables from Cartesian coordinates to spherical polar coordinates.

For example, suppose we want to evaluate the triple integral

$$
\displaystyle \iiint\limits_R \left(x^2 + y^2 + z^2\right)^{3/2} \: \textrm{d}V
$$

where $R$ is the region enclosed by the sphere of radius $1$ centered at $O$, as shown below.

![](<../Source/4.2.7. Triple Integrals in Spherical Polar Coordinates/Images/55debcebc9ab1cd0d106fcf468163d50.png>)

This integral seems too challenging when written in Cartesian coordinates. However, we should recognize that the problem lends itself to spherical polar coordinates because

- the domain $R$ has a simple spherical polar representation, and
- the integrand $f(x,y,z)=\left(x^2+y^2+z^2\right)^{3/2}$ can also be expressed simply in spherical polar coordinates.

So, let's transform the entire problem to spherical polar coordinates using the transformation $\mathbf{T}$, defined by

$$
x = \rho\cos\theta\sin\phi, \qquad y = \rho\sin\theta\sin\phi, \qquad z = \rho\cos\phi
$$

The preimage of $R$ under the action of $\mathbf{T}$ is the rectangular region $\Delta$, given by

$$
\Delta = \left\{(\rho, \theta, \phi) \,: \, 0 \leq \rho \leq 1, \: 0 \leq \theta \lt 2\pi, \: 0 \leq \phi \leq \pi \right\}
$$

as shown below.

![](<../Source/4.2.7. Triple Integrals in Spherical Polar Coordinates/Images/c142dcc2228d76f1e1f29692f40013e8.png>)

To express our integral in terms of spherical polar coordinates, we will use the change of variables formula

$$
\iiint\limits_{R} f(x,y,z) \, \mathrm{d}V = \iiint\limits_{\Delta} \, f(\rho\cos\theta\sin\phi, \rho\sin\theta\sin\phi, \rho\cos\phi) \: \left| \dfrac{\partial (x,y,z)}{\partial (\rho, \theta, \phi)} \right| \: \mathrm{d}\rho \, \mathrm{d}\theta \, \mathrm{d}\phi
$$

It can be shown that the absolute value of the Jacobian determinant for a spherical change of variables is

$$
\left| \dfrac{\partial (x,y,z)}{\partial (\rho, \theta, \phi)} \right| = \rho^2\sin\phi
$$

Therefore, the change of variables formula is

$$
\iiint\limits_{R} f(x,y,z) \, \mathrm{d}V = \iiint\limits_{\Delta} \, f(\rho\cos\theta\sin\phi, \rho\sin\theta\sin\phi, \rho\cos\phi) \: \rho^2\sin\phi \: \mathrm{d}\rho \, \mathrm{d}\theta \, \mathrm{d}\phi
$$

Now, let's write our function

$$
f(x,y,z) = \left(x^2 + y^2 + z^2\right)^{3/2}
$$

in spherical polar coordinates:

$$
\begin{aligned}
f(x, y, z) &= f(ρ\cos θ\sin ϕ, ρ\sin θ\sin ϕ, ρ\cos ϕ) \\
&= (ρ^{2})^{3/2} \\
&= ρ^{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, using the change of variables formula, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
∭_(R)(x^{2} + y^{2} + z^{2})^{3/2}dV &= ∭_(Δ)ρ^{3} \cdot ρ^{2}\sin ϕdρdθdϕ \\
&= ∫_{0}^{π}∫_{0}^{2π}∫_{0}^{1}ρ^{5}\sin ϕdρdθdϕ \\
&= ∫_{0}^{π}\sin ϕdϕ \cdot ∫_{0}^{2π}dθ \cdot ∫_{0}^{1}ρ^{5}dρ \\
&= [- \cos ϕ]_{ϕ = 0}^{ϕ = π} \cdot [θ]_{θ = 0}^{θ = 2π} \cdot [\frac{1}{6}ρ^{6}]_{ρ = 0}^{ρ = 1}dρ \\
&= - (-1 - 1) \cdot (2π - 0) \cdot \frac{1}{6}(1 - 0) \\
&= 2 \cdot 2π \cdot \frac{1}{6} \\
&= \frac{2π}{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Rewriting a Triple Integral Defined Over a Sphere

**Example:** The region $R$ is enclosed inside the sphere $x^2+y^2+z^2=4$.
Express the following triple integral in spherical polar coordinates.
$\displaystyle \iiint\limits_R (y-z)\sqrt{x^2+y^2+z^2} \:\textrm{d}V$

**Explanation**

We will use the change of variables formula in the form

$$
\iiint\limits_{R} f(x,y,z) \, \mathrm{d}V = \iiint\limits_{\Delta} \, f(\rho\cos\theta\sin\phi, \rho\sin\theta\sin\phi, \rho\cos\phi) \: \rho^2\sin\phi \: \mathrm{d}\rho \, \mathrm{d}\theta \, \mathrm{d}\phi
$$

If $(\rho, \theta, \phi)$ are the spherical coordinates of a point, then its Cartesian coordinates $(x,y,z)$ can be found by using the following formulas:

$$
x = \rho\cos\theta\sin\phi, \qquad y = \rho\sin\theta\sin\phi, \qquad z = \rho\cos\phi
$$

Notice that the region $R$ represents the sphere of radius $2$ centered at the origin.
As a result, in spherical polar coordinates, our region can be expressed as

$$
\Delta = \left\{(\rho, \theta, \phi) \,: \, 0 \leq \rho \leq 2, \: 0 \leq \theta \lt 2\pi, \: 0 \leq \phi \leq \pi \right\}
$$

Next, we substitute the spherical polar coordinates into the function

$$
f(x,y,z)=(y-z)\sqrt{x^2+y^2+z^2}{:}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
f(x, y, z) &= f(ρ\cos θ\sin ϕ, ρ\sin θ\sin ϕ, ρ\cos ϕ) \\
&= (ρ\sin θ\sin ϕ - ρ\cos ϕ) \cdot ρ \\
&= ρ^{2}(\sin θ\sin ϕ - \cos ϕ)
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, using the change of variables formula, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
∭_(R)(y - z)\sqrt{x^{2} + y^{2} + z^{2}}dV &= ∭_(Δ)ρ^{2}(\sin θ\sin ϕ - \cos ϕ) \cdot ρ^{2}\sin ϕdρdθdϕ \\
&= ∫_{0}^{π}∫_{0}^{2π}∫_{0}^{2}ρ^{4}\sin ϕ(\sin θ\sin ϕ - \cos ϕ)dρdθdϕ
\end{aligned}
$$

## Rewriting a Triple Integral Defined Over a Part-Sphere

**Example:** The region $R$ is enclosed inside the sphere $x^2+y^2+z^2=4$ for $y \leq 0$.
Express the following triple integral in spherical polar coordinates.
$\displaystyle \iiint\limits_R \dfrac{1}{x^2+y^2+z^2} \:\textrm{d}V$

**Explanation**

We will use the change of variables formula in the form

$$
\iiint\limits_{R} f(x,y,z) \, \mathrm{d}V = \iiint\limits_{\Delta} \, f(\rho\cos\theta\sin\phi, \rho\sin\theta\sin\phi, \rho\cos\phi) \: \rho^2\sin\phi \: \mathrm{d}\rho \, \mathrm{d}\theta \, \mathrm{d}\phi
$$

If $(\rho, \theta, \phi)$ are the spherical coordinates of a point, then its Cartesian coordinates $(x,y,z)$ can be found by using the following formulas:

$$
x = \rho\cos\theta\sin\phi, \qquad y = \rho\sin\theta\sin\phi, \qquad z = \rho\cos\phi
$$

Notice that the region $R$ represents one-half of the sphere of radius $2$ centered at the origin.

![](<../Source/4.2.7. Triple Integrals in Spherical Polar Coordinates/Images/459440a2a6348b0fdf43a6c47e2b341d.png>)

As a result, we obtain that in spherical polar coordinates, our region can be expressed as

$$
\Delta = \left\{(\rho, \theta, \phi) \,: \, 0 \leq \rho \leq 2, \: \pi \leq \theta \leq 2\pi, \: 0 \leq \phi \leq \pi \right\}
$$

Next, we substitute the spherical polar coordinates into the function

$$
f(x,y,z)=\dfrac{1}{x^2+y^2+z^2}{:}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
f(x, y, z) &= f(ρ\cos θ\sin ϕ, ρ\sin θ\sin ϕ, ρ\cos ϕ) \\
&= \frac{1}{ρ^{2}}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, using the change of variables formula, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
∭_(R)\frac{1}{x^{2} + y^{2} + z^{2}}dV &= ∭_(Δ)\frac{1}{ρ^{2}} \cdot ρ^{2}\sin ϕdρdθdϕ \\
&= ∫_{0}^{π}∫_{π}^{2π}∫_{0}^{2}\sin ϕdρdθdϕ
\end{aligned}
$$

## Rewriting a Triple Integral Defined Over a Shifted Sphere

**Example:** The region $R$ is enclosed inside the sphere $x^2+y^2+z^2=10x$ for $y \leq 0$ and $z \leq 0$, shown above.
Express the following triple integral in spherical polar coordinates.
$\iiint\limits_R \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x^2+y^2+z^2}} \:\textrm{d}V$

![](<../Source/4.2.7. Triple Integrals in Spherical Polar Coordinates/Images/71b8e03df28ef3f5bacac178dccd33d5.png>)

**Explanation**

We will use the change of variables formula in the form

$$
\iiint\limits_{R} f(x,y,z) \, \mathrm{d}V = \iiint\limits_{\Delta} \, f(\rho\cos\theta\sin\phi, \rho\sin\theta\sin\phi, \rho\cos\phi) \: \rho^2\sin\phi \: \mathrm{d}\rho \, \mathrm{d}\theta \, \mathrm{d}\phi
$$

If $(\rho, \theta, \phi)$ are the spherical coordinates of a point, then its Cartesian coordinates $(x,y,z)$ can be found by using the following formulas:

$$
x = \rho\cos\theta\sin\phi, \qquad y = \rho\sin\theta\sin\phi, \qquad z = \rho\cos\phi
$$

Notice that the region $R$ represents one-quarter of the sphere of radius $5$ centered at $(5,0,0)$.

With that in mind, let's now write the

$$
x^2+y^2+z^2=10x
$$

in spherical polar coordinates:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^{2} + y^{2} + z^{2} &= 10x \\
ρ^{2} &= 10ρ\cos θ\sin ϕ \\
ρ(ρ - 10\cos θ\sin ϕ) &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

Since $\rho$ cannot be identical to zero on the entire sphere, we must have

$$
\rho =10\cos\theta\sin\phi
$$

![](<../Source/4.2.7. Triple Integrals in Spherical Polar Coordinates/Images/03e571f6d114f3a79274ef0a17c3cb95.png>)

As a result, we obtain that in spherical polar coordinates, our region can be expressed as

$$
\Delta = \left\{(\rho, \theta, \phi) \,: \, 0 \leq \rho \leq 10\cos\theta\sin\phi, \:\dfrac{3\pi}{2} \leq \theta \leq 2\pi, \: \dfrac{\pi}{2} \leq \phi \leq \pi \right\}
$$

Next, we substitute the spherical polar coordinates into the function

$$
f(x,y,z)= \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x^2+y^2+z^2}}{:}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
f(x, y, z) &= f(ρ\cos θ\sin ϕ, ρ\sin θ\sin ϕ, ρ\cos ϕ) \\
&= \frac{1}{ρ}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, using the change of variables formula, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
∭_(R)\frac{1}{\sqrt{x^{2} + y^{2} + z^{2}}}dV &= ∭_(Δ)\frac{1}{ρ} \cdot ρ^{2}\sin ϕdρdθdϕ \\
&= ∫_{π/2}^{π}∫_{3π/2}^{2π}∫_{0}^{10\cos θ\sin ϕ}ρ\sin ϕdρdθdϕ
\end{aligned}
$$

## Evaluating a Triple Integral by Converting to Spherical Polar Coordinates

**Example:** Evaluate the triple integral
$\displaystyle \iiint\limits_R 2z\sqrt{x^2 + y^2 + z^2} \:\textrm{d}V$
over the region $R$ enclosed inside the sphere $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = 9$ for $y \leq 0$, shown above.

![](<../Source/4.2.7. Triple Integrals in Spherical Polar Coordinates/Images/e70456f7d50c38da4c68f5cbf70dd16f.png>)

**Explanation**

We will use the change of variables formula in the form

$$
\iiint\limits_{R} f(x,y,z) \, \mathrm{d}V = \iiint\limits_{\Delta} \, f(\rho\cos\theta\sin\phi, \rho\sin\theta\sin\phi, \rho\cos\phi) \: \rho^2\sin\phi \: \mathrm{d}\rho \, \mathrm{d}\theta \, \mathrm{d}\phi
$$

If $(\rho, \theta, \phi)$ are the spherical coordinates of a point, then its Cartesian coordinates $(x,y,z)$ can be found by using the following formulas:

$$
x = \rho\cos\theta\sin\phi, \qquad y = \rho\sin\theta\sin\phi, \qquad z = \rho\cos\phi
$$

Notice that the region $R$ represents one-half of the sphere of radius $3$ centered at the origin.

![](<../Source/4.2.7. Triple Integrals in Spherical Polar Coordinates/Images/8d6fe1f4e6380a8f7914b4f06382f31a.png>)

As a result, we obtain that in spherical polar coordinates, our region can be expressed as

$$
\Delta = \left\{(\rho, \theta, \phi) \,: \, 0 \leq \rho \leq 3, \: \pi \leq \theta \leq 2\pi, \: 0 \leq \phi \leq \pi \right\}
$$

Next, we substitute the spherical polar coordinates into the function

$$
f(x,y,z) = 2z\sqrt{x^2 + y^2 + z^2}{:}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
f(x, y, z) &= f(ρ\cos θ\sin ϕ, ρ\sin θ\sin ϕ, ρ\cos ϕ) \\
&= 2ρ\cos ϕ \cdot ρ \\
&= 2ρ^{2}\cos ϕ
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, using the change of variables formula, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
∭_(R)2z\sqrt{x^{2} + y^{2} + z^{2}}dV &= ∭_(Δ)2ρ^{2}\cos ϕ \cdot ρ^{2}\sin ϕdρdθdϕ \\
&= ∫_{0}^{π}∫_{π}^{2π}∫_{0}^{3}ρ^{4}\sin 2ϕdρdθdϕ \\
&= ∫_{0}^{π}\sin 2ϕdϕ \cdot ∫_{π}^{2π}dθ \cdot ∫_{0}^{3}ρ^{4}dρ \\
&= [- \frac{1}{2}\cos 2ϕ]_{ϕ = 0}^{ϕ = π} \cdot [θ]_{θ = π}^{θ = 2π} \cdot [\frac{1}{5}ρ^{5}]_{ρ = 0}^{ρ = 3} \\
&=-\frac{1}{2}(1 - 1) \cdot (2π - π) \cdot \frac{1}{5}(243 - 0) \\
&= 0 \cdot π \cdot \frac{243}{5} \\
&= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

## Deriving the Jacobian for Spherical Polar Coordinates

Throughout this lesson, we've used the fact that

$$
\left| \dfrac{\partial (x, y, z)}{\partial (\rho, \theta, \phi)} \right| = \rho^2 \sin{\phi}
$$

Let's now prove this result.

Given the spherical coordinate transformations

$$
x = \rho\cos\theta\sin\phi, \qquad y = \rho\sin\theta\sin\phi, \qquad z = \rho\cos\phi
$$

we first calculate the required partial derivatives:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\begin{bmatrix}\frac{∂x}{∂ρ} &= \cos θ\sin ϕ & \frac{∂x}{∂θ}|= - ρ\sin θ\sin ϕ & \frac{∂x}{∂ϕ}|= ρ\cos θ\cos ϕ \\ \frac{∂y}{∂ρ}|= \sin θ\sin ϕ & \frac{∂y}{∂θ}|= ρ\cos θ\sin ϕ & \frac{∂y}{∂ϕ}|= ρ\sin θ\cos ϕ \\ \frac{∂z}{∂ρ}|= \cos ϕ & \frac{∂z}{∂θ}|= 0 & \frac{∂z}{∂ϕ}|= - ρ\sin ϕ\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the corresponding Jacobian determinant is

$$
\begin{aligned}
(∂(x, y, z))/(∂(ρ, θ, ϕ)) &= \begin{vmatrix}\cos θ\sin ϕ & - ρ\sin θ\sin ϕ & ρ\cos θ\cos ϕ \\ \sin θ\sin ϕ & ρ\cos θ\sin ϕ & ρ\sin θ\cos ϕ \\ \cos ϕ & 0 & - ρ\sin ϕ\end{vmatrix} \\
&= \cos ϕ\begin{vmatrix}- ρ\sin θ\sin ϕ & ρ\cos θ\cos ϕ \\ ρ\cos θ\sin ϕ & ρ\sin θ\cos ϕ\end{vmatrix}- ρ\sin ϕ\begin{vmatrix}\cos θ\sin ϕ & - ρ\sin θ\sin ϕ \\ \sin θ\sin ϕ & ρ\cos θ\sin ϕ\end{vmatrix} \\
&= \cos ϕ \cdot (- ρ^{2}\sin ϕ\cos ϕ(\sin^{2} θ + \cos^{2} θ)) - ρ\sin ϕ \cdot ρ\sin^{2} ϕ \cdot (\cos^{2} θ + \sin^{2} θ) \\
&= - ρ^{2}\sin ϕ\cos^{2} ϕ - ρ^{2}\sin ϕ\sin^{2} ϕ \\
&= - ρ^{2}\sin ϕ(\cos^{2} ϕ + \sin^{2} ϕ) \\
&= - ρ^{2}\sin ϕ
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we conclude that

$$
\left| \dfrac{\partial (x, y, z)}{\partial (\rho, \theta, \phi)} \right| = \rho^2 \sin{\phi}
$$

## Deriving the Change of Variables Formula Using Geometry

We can also derive the change of variables formula for spherical polar coordinates using geometry.

Consider a bounded spherical box $R$, given by

$$
R = \big\{(\rho,\theta,\phi) \: \mid \: a \leq \rho \leq b, \:\: \alpha \leq \theta \leq \beta, \:\: \gamma \leq \phi \leq \psi \big\}
$$

We can divide each interval into $l,m$, and $n$ subdivisions such that

$$
\Delta\rho = \dfrac{b-a}{l},\qquad \Delta\theta = \dfrac{\beta-\alpha}{m},\qquad \Delta\phi = \dfrac{\psi-\gamma}{n}
$$

This splits the region into "spherical wedges," as shown in the following diagram.

![](<../Source/4.2.7. Triple Integrals in Spherical Polar Coordinates/Images/662afef89ee6f9b4cbfc34878fe0e5bd.png>)

Zooming in on one of these wedges, we obtain a shape resembling a rectangular solid.

![](<../Source/4.2.7. Triple Integrals in Spherical Polar Coordinates/Images/04c85ee2d415955a334da0638bf3726c.png>)

Let's note the volume of this solid as $\Delta V$. To approximate the value of $\Delta V$, we use the formula for the volume of a rectangular solid:

$$
\Delta V \approx lwh
$$

Clearly, the height of the rectangular solid is

$$
h = \Delta \rho
$$

We can find the remaining dimensions using simple geometry.

First, we need to find the length of the radius $r$ in the $xy$-plane. Using the following triangle and a side-on view of the wedge, we conclude that

$$
r=\rho\sin\phi
$$

![](<../Source/4.2.7. Triple Integrals in Spherical Polar Coordinates/Images/0e69226c2122ff54d6e2e01e8cc718d2.png>)

Let's add this to our diagram.

![](<../Source/4.2.7. Triple Integrals in Spherical Polar Coordinates/Images/88ec7c6fa23502acede2a1782545fedc.png>)

Finally, we can use the arc length formula

$$
s = r \cdot \theta
$$

to find the length $l$ and width $w$.

![](<../Source/4.2.7. Triple Integrals in Spherical Polar Coordinates/Images/421389b839ca2e1abf6a7ccf8a3e2f49.png>)

The length of our wedge is

$$
l = \rho \cdot \Delta\phi = \rho\Delta\phi
$$

and the width is

$$
w = \rho\sin\phi \cdot \Delta\theta = \rho\sin\phi\Delta\theta
$$

Calculating the volume as a rectangular solid, we obtain

$$
ΔV \mid \approx lwh; = (ρΔϕ) \cdot (ρ\sin ϕΔθ) \cdot (Δρ); = ρ^{2}\sin ϕΔϕΔθΔρ
$$

As a result, we define the triple integral in spherical coordinates as the limit of a triple Riemann sum, where $(\rho_{ijk},\theta_{ijk},\phi_{ijk})$ being any sample point in the spherical subregion $B{ijk}{:}$

$$
\lim\limits_{l,m,n \to \infty} \sum\limits_{i=1}^{l} \sum\limits_{j=1}^{m} \sum\limits_{k=1}^{n} f(\rho_{ijk},\theta_{ijk},\phi_{ijk}) (\rho_{ijk})^2 \sin \phi_{ijk} \:\Delta\rho\:\Delta\theta\:\Delta\phi
$$

As we increase the number of subregions, the size of $\Delta\rho$, $\Delta\theta$, and $\Delta\phi$ all goes to zero, and the volume of $R$ is more accurately approximated, giving

$$
\textrm{d}V = \rho^2 \sin\phi \:\textrm{d}\rho\:\textrm{d}\theta\:\textrm{d}\phi
$$

Therefore, we conclude that if $f(\rho,\theta,\phi)$ is continuous on a spherical box

$$
R = [a,b] \times [\alpha,\beta] \times [\gamma, \psi]
$$

then

$$
\iiint\limits_{R} f(\rho,\theta,\phi) \:\textrm{d}V = \iiint\limits_{R} f(\rho,\theta,\phi) \rho^2 \sin\phi \:\textrm{d}\rho\:\textrm{d}\theta\:\textrm{d}\phi = \int_{\gamma}^{\psi}\int_{\alpha}^{\beta}\int_{a}^{b} f(\rho,\theta,\phi) \rho^2 \sin\phi \:\textrm{d}\rho\:\textrm{d}\theta\:\textrm{d}\phi
$$
