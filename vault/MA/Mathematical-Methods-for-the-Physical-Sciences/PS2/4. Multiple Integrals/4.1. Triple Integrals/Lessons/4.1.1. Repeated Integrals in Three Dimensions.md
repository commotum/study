## Introduction

Repeated integrals can feature three or more variables. In this lesson, we'll learn how to evaluate repeated integrals containing three variables.

For example, let's consider the repeated integral below:

$$
\int_{1}^2 \int_0^2 \int_0^1 x(y+z)^2 \:\textrm{d}z\:\textrm{d}y\:\textrm{d}x
$$

To evaluate this integral, we first integrate with respect to the inner variable $(z)$, then with respect to the middle variable $(y)$, and finally with respect to the outer variable $(x)$.

We can write the first step more explicitly as

$$
%\int_{1}^2 \int_0^2 \int_0^1 x(y+z)^2 \:\textrm{d}z\:\textrm{d}y\:\textrm{d}x= \int_{1}^2 \int_0^2 \left[ \int_0^1 x(y+z)^2 \:\textrm{d}z\right] \textrm{d}y\:\textrm{d}x. %= \int_{1}^2 \left[ \int_0^2 \left[ \int_0^1 x(y+z)^2 \:\textrm{d}z \right] \textrm{d}y \right] \textrm{d}x
$$

We now carry out the integration.
First, we integrate the inner integral by integrating with respect to $z$, *treating $x$ and $y$ as constants:*

$$
\begin{aligned}
∫_{1}^{2}∫_{0}^{2}[∫_{0}^{1}x(y + z)^{2}dz]dydx &= ∫_{1}^{2}∫_{0}^{2}x[((y + z)^{3})/(3)]_{z = 0}^{z = 1}dydx \\
&= ∫_{1}^{2}∫_{0}^{2}x(((y + 1)^{3})/(3) - \frac{y^{3}}{3})dydx \\
&= ∫_{1}^{2}∫_{0}^{2}\frac{1}{3}x((y + 1)^{3} - y^{3})dydx \\
&= \frac{1}{3}∫_{1}^{2}∫_{0}^{2}x((y + 1)^{3} - y^{3})dydx
\end{aligned}
$$

The next step is to integrate with respect to $y$, which we can write more explicitly as

$$
\dfrac13 \int_{1}^2 \left[ \int_0^2x \left((y+1)^3 - y^3 \right) \textrm{d}y \right] \textrm{d}x
$$

Evaluating the inner integral by integrating with respect to $y$, *treating $x$ as a constant,* we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{1}{3}∫_{1}^{2}[∫_{0}^{2}x((y + 1)^{3} - y^{3})dy]dx &= \frac{1}{3}∫_{1}^{2}x[(((y + 1)^{4})/(4) - \frac{y^{4}}{4})]_{y = 0}^{y = 2}dx \\
&= \frac{1}{3}∫_{1}^{2}x[(\frac{81}{4} - \frac{16}{4}) - (\frac{1}{4} - 0)]dx \\
&= \frac{1}{3}∫_{1}^{2}x \cdot (\frac{64}{4})dx \\
&= \frac{1}{3}∫_{1}^{2}16xdx
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we integrate with respect to $x{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{1}{3}∫_{1}^{2}16xdx &= \frac{1}{3}[8x^{2}]_{x = 1}^{x = 2} \\
&= \frac{1}{3}(32 - 8) \\
&= \frac{1}{3} \cdot 24 \\
&= 8
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, we conclude that

$$
\int_{1}^2 \int_0^2 \int_0^1 x(y+z)^2 \:\textrm{d}z\:\textrm{d}y\:\textrm{d}x = 8
$$

## Evaluating Repeated Integrals

**Example:** Evaluate $\displaystyle \int_{1}^{2} \int_{-1}^{2} \int_{0}^{1} x^2y+xz^2 \: \mathrm{d}z \: \mathrm{d}x \: \mathrm{d}y$.

**Explanation**

First, we evaluate the inner integral by integrating with respect to $z$, treating $x$ and $y$ as constants:

$$
\begin{aligned}
∫_{1}^{2}∫_{-1}^{2}∫_{0}^{1}x^{2}y + xz^{2}dzdxdy &= ∫_{1}^{2}∫_{-1}^{2}[∫_{0}^{1}x^{2}y + xz^{2}dz]dxdy \\
&= ∫_{1}^{2}∫_{-1}^{2}[x^{2}yz + \frac{xz^{3}}{3}]_{z = 0}^{z = 1}dxdy \\
&= ∫_{1}^{2}∫_{-1}^{2}x^{2}y(1 - 0) + x(\frac{1}{3} - 0)dxdy \\
&= ∫_{1}^{2}∫_{-1}^{2}x^{2}y + \frac{1}{3}xdxdy
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we evaluate the inner integral by integrating with respect to $x$, treating $y$ as a constant:

$$
\begin{aligned}
∫_{1}^{2}∫_{-1}^{2}x^{2}y + \frac{1}{3}xdxdy &= ∫_{1}^{2}[∫_{-1}^{2}x^{2}y + \frac{1}{3}xdx]dy \\
&= ∫_{1}^{2}[\frac{1}{3}x^{3}y + \frac{1}{6}x^{2}]_{x =-1}^{x = 2}dy \\
&= ∫_{1}^{2}\frac{1}{3}y(2^{3} - (-1)^{3}) + \frac{1}{6}(2^{2} - (-1)^{2}))dy \\
&= ∫_{1}^{2}3y + \frac{1}{2}dy
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we integrate with respect to $y{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
∫_{1}^{2}3y + \frac{1}{2}dx &= [\frac{3y^{2}}{2} + \frac{y}{2}]_{y = 1}^{y = 2} \\
&= (6 + 1) - (\frac{3}{2} + \frac{1}{2}) \\
&= 5
\end{aligned}
$$

## Evaluating Repeated Integrals Over Non-Rectangular Domains

**Example:** Evaluate $\displaystyle \int_{0}^{2} \int_{0}^{x} \int_{0}^{3-x-y} x \: \mathrm{d}z \: \mathrm{d}y \: \mathrm{d}x$.

**Explanation**

First, we evaluate the inner integral by integrating with respect to $z$, treating $x$ and $y$ as constants:

$$
\begin{aligned}
∫_{0}^{2}∫_{0}^{x}∫_{0}^{3 - x - y}xdzdydx &= ∫_{0}^{2}∫_{0}^{x}[∫_{0}^{3 - x - y}xdz]dydx \\
&= ∫_{0}^{2}∫_{0}^{x}x \cdot [z]_{z = 0}^{z = 3 - x - y}dydx \\
&= ∫_{0}^{2}∫_{0}^{x}x(3 - x - y)dydx \\
&= ∫_{0}^{2}∫_{0}^{x}3x - x^{2} - xydydx
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we evaluate the inner integral by integrating with respect to $y$, treating $x$ as a constant:

$$
\begin{aligned}
∫_{0}^{2}∫_{0}^{x}3x - x^{2} - xydydx &= ∫_{0}^{2}[∫_{0}^{x}3x - x^{2} - xydy]dx \\
&= ∫_{0}^{2}[(3x - x^{2})y - \frac{1}{2}xy^{2}]_{y = 0}^{y = x}dx \\
&= ∫_{0}^{2}(3x - x^{2})(x - 0) - \frac{1}{2}x(x^{2} - 0)dx \\
&= ∫_{0}^{2}3x^{2} - x^{3} - \frac{1}{2}x^{3}dx \\
&= ∫_{0}^{2}3x^{2} - \frac{3}{2}x^{3}dx
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we integrate with respect to $x{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
∫_{0}^{2}3x^{2} - \frac{3}{2}x^{3}dx &= [x^{3} - \frac{3}{8}x^{4}]_{x = 0}^{x = 2} \\
&= 8 - 6 \\
&= 2
\end{aligned}
$$

## Properties of Repeated Integrals

Repeated integrals with multiple variables obey similar rules to those we've seen before.

**The Sum Rule**

Suppose that $f(x,y,z)$ and $g(x,y,z)$ are continuous functions on a domain

$$
R = \big\{(x,y,z) \: \mid \: a \leq x \leq b, \:\: \alpha(x) \leq y \leq \beta(x), \:\: \gamma(x,y) \leq z \leq \psi(x,y) \big\}
$$

Then, we can write the integral
$\displaystyle \int_{a}^{b} \int_{\alpha(x)}^{\beta(x)} \int_{\gamma(x,y)}^{\psi(x,y)} f(x,y,z) + g(x,y,z) \:\textrm{d}z\:\textrm{d}y\:\textrm{d}x$
as
$\int_{a}^{b} \int_{\alpha(x)}^{\beta(x)} \int_{\gamma(x,y)}^{\psi(x,y)} f(x,y,z) \:\textrm{d}z\:\textrm{d}y\:\textrm{d}x + \int_{a}^{b} \int_{\alpha(x)}^{\beta(x)} \int_{\gamma(x,y)}^{\psi(x,y)} g(x,y,z) \:\textrm{d}z\:\textrm{d}y\:\textrm{d}x$.

**The Multiplicative Property**

Suppose that $f(x)$ is continuous on $[a,b]$, $g(y)$ is continuous on $[\alpha,\beta]$, and $h(z)$ is continuous on $[\gamma,\psi]$. Then, we have

$$
\int_{a}^{b} \int_{\alpha}^{\beta} \int_{\gamma}^{\psi} f(x) \cdot g(y) \cdot h(z) \:\textrm{d}z\:\textrm{d}y\:\textrm{d}x = \left(\int_{a}^{b} f(x) \:\textrm{d}x \right) \left(\int_{\alpha}^{\beta} g(y) \:\textrm{d}y \right) \left(\int_{\gamma}^{\psi} h(z) \:\textrm{d}z \right)
$$

<a id="using-the-properties-of-repeated-integrals"></a>
## Using the Properties of Repeated Integrals

Let's evaluate the following repeated integral using the rules we just discussed.

$$
\displaystyle \int_{-1}^{1} \int_{0}^{1} \int_{0}^{2} (x+y+z) \: \textrm{d}x \: \textrm{d}y \: \textrm{d}z
$$

First, we apply the sum rule:
$\int_{-1}^{1} \int_{0}^{1} \int_{0}^{2} x \: \textrm{d}x \: \textrm{d}y \: \textrm{d}z + \int_{-1}^{1} \int_{0}^{1} \int_{0}^{2} y \: \textrm{d}x \: \textrm{d}y \: \textrm{d}z + \int_{-1}^{1} \int_{0}^{1} \int_{0}^{2} z \: \textrm{d}x \: \textrm{d}y \: \textrm{d}z$

Now, notice that the domain of integration

$$
R = [0,2] \times [0,1] \times [-1,1]
$$

is a rectangular box, and each integrand can be expressed as $f(x) \cdot g(y) \cdot h(z)$. So, we can rewrite our sum of repeated integrals as follows:

$$
\int_{0}^{2} x \, \textrm{d}x \int_{0}^{1} \textrm{d}y \int_{-1}^{1} \textrm{d}z + \int_{0}^{2} \textrm{d}x \int_{0}^{1} y \, \textrm{d}y \int_{-1}^{1} \textrm{d}z + \int_{0}^{2} \textrm{d}x \int_{0}^{1} \textrm{d}y \int_{-1}^{1} z \, \textrm{d}z
$$

Let's evaluate each of these separately:

- For the first product, we have
$∫_{0}^{2}xdx∫_{0}^{1}dy∫_{-1}^{1}dz|= [\frac{1}{2}x^{2}]_{0}^{2} \cdot (1 - 0) \cdot (1 - (-1)); = 2 \cdot 1 \cdot 2; = 4$.
- For the second product, we have
$∫_{0}^{2}dx∫_{0}^{1}ydy∫_{-1}^{1}dz|= (2 - 0) \cdot [\frac{1}{2}y^{2}]_{0}^{1} \cdot (1 - (-1)); = 2 \cdot \frac{1}{2} \cdot 2; = 2$.
- For the third product, we have
$∫_{0}^{2}dx∫_{0}^{1}dy∫_{-1}^{1}zdz|= (2 - 0) \cdot (1 - 0) \cdot [\frac{1}{2}z^{2}]_{-1}^{1}; = 2 \cdot 1 \cdot 0; = 0$.

Therefore, we conclude that

$$
∫_{-1}^{1}∫_{0}^{1}∫_{0}^{2}(x + y + z)dxdydz = 4 + 2 + 0 = 6
$$

## Evaluating Repeated Integrals Using the Sum and Product Rules

**Example:** Evaluate $\displaystyle \int_{1}^{2}\int_{0}^{\pi/2} \int_{1}^{4} \dfrac{\sin{y}}{x} \: \textrm{d}z \: \textrm{d}y \: \textrm{d}x$.

**Explanation**

Since the domain of integration

$$
R = [1,2] \times \left[0,\pi/2\right] \times [1,4]
$$

is a rectangular box and the integrand can be expressed as $f(x)\cdot g(y)\cdot h(z)$, we can rewrite our repeated integral as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
∫_{1}^{2}∫_{0}^{π/2}∫_{1}^{4}\frac{\sin y}{x}dzdydx &= ∫_{1}^{2}\frac{dx}{x}∫_{0}^{π/2}\sin ydy∫_{1}^{4}dz
\end{aligned}
$$

Evaluating each integral separately, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
∫_{1}^{2}\frac{1}{x}dx∫_{0}^{π/2}\sin ydy∫_{1}^{4}dz &= [\ln \mid x \mid ]_{1}^{2} \cdot [- \cos y]_{0}^{π/2} \cdot (4 - 1) \\
&= (\ln 2 - \ln 1) \cdot (-\cos (\frac{π}{2}) + \cos (0)) \cdot 3 \\
&= (\ln 2 - 0) \cdot (0 + 1) \cdot 3 \\
&= 3\ln 2
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, we conclude that

$$
\int_{1}^{2}\int_{0}^{\pi/2} \int_{1}^{4} \dfrac{\sin{y}}{x} \: \textrm{d}z \: \textrm{d}y \: \textrm{d}x = 3\ln 2
$$
