## Introduction

Let's consider the following system of linear equations:

$$
{x_{1} + x_{2} =-2; - x_{1} - 4x_{2} = 4; 2x_{1} - x_{2} = 2
$$

It is easy to show that the system is inconsistent (i.e., it has no solution). However, in real-world problems, we might want to find an approximate solution to the system.

Let's formulate the problem a little more rigorously. Firstly, we write down the system in the form

$$
A\mathbf x = \mathbf b
$$

$\begin{bmatrix}1 & 1 \\ -1 & -4 \\ 2 & -1\end{bmatrix}$

We are interested in vectors $\hat{\mathbf{x}}$ such that $A\hat{\mathbf{x}}$ is very close to $\mathbf b$. More precisely, we wish to *minimize the distance* between $A\hat{\mathbf{x}}$ and $\mathbf b$. Recall that the distance between $A\hat{\mathbf{x}}$ and $\mathbf b$ is given by

$$
\Vert A{\hat{\mathbf{x}}} - \mathbf{b} \Vert
$$

The smaller the value of $\Vert A{\hat{\mathbf{x}}} - \mathbf{b} \Vert$, the better our approximation $\hat{\mathbf{x}}$ will be.

In terms of vector subspaces, we wish to find a vector $A{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}$ that lies in $\textrm{Col}(A)$ and is closest to $\mathbf{b}$.
Therefore, $A{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}$ must be the orthogonal projection of $\mathbf{b}$ onto the subspace spanned by the columns of $A$.

![](<../Source/3.1.1. The Least-Squares Solution of a Linear System (Without Collinearity)/Images/9a9d0b2b43fd73bf698e36b97875d410.png>)

It can be shown that ${\hat{\mathbf{x}}}$ is found by solving the corresponding **normal equation**, given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
A^{T}Ax^(^) &= A^{T}b
\end{aligned}
$$

We'll prove this at the end of the lesson. Note that the normal equation is obtained by left-multiplying the equation

$$
A {\hat{\mathbf{x}}} = \mathbf b
$$

by $A^T$.

Since the columns of $A$ are linearly independent, the matrix $A^T \! A$ is invertible. Therefore, left-multiplying the normal equation by $(A^T \! A)^{-1}$, we obtain

$$
{\hat{\mathbf{x}}} = (A^T \! A)^{-1}A^T \mathbf{b}
$$

We now compute the right-hand side as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^(^) &= (A^{T}A)^{-1}A^{T}b \\
&= (\begin{bmatrix}1 & -1 & 2 \\ 1 & -4 & -1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1 & 1 \\ -1 & -4 \\ 2 & -1\end{bmatrix})^{-1}\begin{bmatrix}1 & -1 & 2 \\ 1 & -4 & -1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}-2 \\ 4 \\ 2\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}6 & 3 \\ 3 & 18\end{bmatrix}^{-1}\begin{bmatrix}-2 \\ -20\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \frac{1}{99}\begin{bmatrix}18 & -3 \\ -3 & 6\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}-2 \\ -20\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}\frac{8}{33} \\ -\frac{38}{33}\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

The vector ${\hat{\mathbf{x}}}$ is called the **least-squares solution** of the given system.

**Watch out!** We assumed that the columns of $A$ are linearly independent, which guarantees that $A^T \! A\,$ is invertible. However, if the columns of $A$ are linearly *dependent,* then we have to use a different approach to solve the problem.

## Finding the Least-Squares Solution of a Linear System Given in Matrix Form

**Example:** Given that the columns of $A$ are linearly independent, find the least-squares solution of $A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{b}$, where

$\begin{bmatrix}1 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \\ 2 & -1 \\ 0 & 1\end{bmatrix}$

**Explanation**

First of all, we write the given system in the form

$$
A \mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b}
$$

$\begin{bmatrix}1 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \\ 2 & -1 \\ 0 & 1\end{bmatrix}$

We can find the least-squares solution ${\hat{\mathbf{x}}}$ of the equation above by solving the corresponding normal equation, which we obtain by left-multiplying our original equation by $A^T$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
A^{T}Ax^(^) &= A^{T}b
\end{aligned}
$$

Since the columns of $A$ are linearly independent, the matrix $A^T \! A$ is invertible. Therefore, multiplying by $(A^T \! A)^{-1}$ on the left, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^(^) &= (A^{T}A)^{-1}A^{T}b \\
&= (\begin{bmatrix}1 & 1 & 2 & 0 \\ -1 & 0 & -1 & 1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \\ 2 & -1 \\ 0 & 1\end{bmatrix})^{-1}\begin{bmatrix}1 & 1 & 2 & 0 \\ -1 & 0 & -1 & 1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 0 \\ 1 \\ 0\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}6 & -3 \\ -3 & 3\end{bmatrix}^{-1}\begin{bmatrix}3 \\ -2\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \frac{1}{3}\begin{bmatrix}1 & 1 \\ 1 & 2\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}3 \\ -2\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{3} \\ -\frac{1}{3}\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding the Least-Squares Solution of a Linear System

**Example:** Find the least-squares solution to the system of linear equations given below. You're given that the columns of the corresponding coefficient matrix are linearly independent.

${-2x_{1} - x_{2} = 12; x_{1} - 2x_{2} + x_{3} = 0; - x_{2} - 2x_{3} =-12; x_{1} - x_{3} = 0$

**Explanation**

First of all, we write the given system in the form

$$
A \mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b}
$$

$\begin{bmatrix}-2 & -1 & 0 \\ 1 & -2 & 1 \\ 0 & -1 & -2 \\ 1 & 0 & -1\end{bmatrix}$

We can find the least-squares solution ${\hat{\mathbf{x}}}$ of the equation above by solving the corresponding normal equation, which we obtain by left-multiplying our original equation by $A^T$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
A^{T}Ax^(^) &= A^{T}b
\end{aligned}
$$

Since the columns of $A$ are linearly independent, the matrix $A^T \! A$ is invertible. Therefore, multiplying by $(A^T \! A)^{-1}$ on the left, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^(^) &= (A^{T}A)^{-1}A^{T}b \\
&= (\begin{bmatrix}-2 & 1 & 0 & 1 \\ -1 & -2 & -1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & -2 & -1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}-2 & -1 & 0 \\ 1 & -2 & 1 \\ 0 & -1 & -2 \\ 1 & 0 & -1\end{bmatrix})^{-1}\begin{bmatrix}-2 & 1 & 0 & 1 \\ -1 & -2 & -1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & -2 & -1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}12 \\ 0 \\ -12 \\ 0\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}6 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 6 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 6\end{bmatrix}^{-1}\begin{bmatrix}-24 \\ 0 \\ 24\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \frac{1}{6}\begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}-24 \\ 0 \\ 24\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}-4 \\ 0 \\ 4\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding the Distance Between a Vector to a Subspace of Solutions of a Linear System

**Example:** Consider the system $A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{b}$, where the matrix $A$ and the vector $\mathbf{b}$ are given below. Given that the columns of $A$ are linearly independent, find the minimum value of $\Vert A\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{b} \Vert$.
$\begin{bmatrix}1 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \\ -1 & 0 \\ -1 & -1\end{bmatrix}$

**Explanation**

First of all, we write the given system in the form

$$
A \mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\begin{bmatrix}1 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \\ -1 & 0 \\ -1 & -1\end{bmatrix}^(⏞)^(A)\begin{bmatrix}x_{1} \\ x_{2}\end{bmatrix}^(⏞)^(x) &= \begin{bmatrix}22 \\ 0 \\ 0 \\ 2\end{bmatrix}^(⏞)^(b) \\
Ax &= b
\end{aligned}
$$

We can find the least-squares solution ${\hat{\mathbf{x}}}$ of the equation above by solving the corresponding normal equation, which we obtain by left-multiplying our original equation by $A^T$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
A^{T}Ax^(^) &= A^{T}b
\end{aligned}
$$

Since the columns of $A$ are linearly independent, the matrix $A^T \! A$ is invertible. Therefore, multiplying by $(A^T \! A)^{-1}$ on the left, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^(^) &= (A^{T}A)^{-1}A^{T}b \\
&= (\begin{bmatrix}1 & 1 & -1 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 & 0 & -1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \\ -1 & 0 \\ -1 & -1\end{bmatrix})^{-1}\begin{bmatrix}1 & 1 & -1 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 & 0 & -1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}22 \\ 0 \\ 0 \\ 2\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}4 & 2 \\ 2 & 2\end{bmatrix}^{-1}\begin{bmatrix}20 \\ 20\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \frac{1}{4}\begin{bmatrix}2 & -2 \\ -2 & 4\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}20 \\ 20\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \frac{1}{2}\begin{bmatrix}1 & -1 \\ -1 & 2\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}20 \\ 20\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}0 \\ 10\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

The minimum value of $\Vert A\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{b} \Vert$ is attained when

$$
\mathbf{x}={\hat{\mathbf{x}}}
$$

which is the least-squares solution of the system

$$
A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{b}
$$

In this case, $A{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}$ represents the orthogonal projection of $\mathbf{b}$ onto the column space of $A$, and $\Vert A{\hat{\mathbf{x}}} - \mathbf{b} \Vert$ is the distance between the vector $\mathbf{b}$ and the column space.

Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
Ax^(^) - b &= \begin{bmatrix}1 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \\ -1 & 0 \\ -1 & -1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}0 \\ 10\end{bmatrix} - \begin{bmatrix}22 \\ 0 \\ 0 \\ 2\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}-12 \\ 0 \\ 0 \\ -12\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally,

$$
\Vert A{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}-\mathbf{b}\Vert =\sqrt{(-12)^2+0^2+(-12)^2+0^2}=12\sqrt{2}
$$

## Deriving the Normal Equation

Suppose that $A$ is an $m \times n$ matrix with linearly independent columns. We'll now explain why the least-squares solution ${\hat{\mathbf{x}}}$ to the least-squares problem

$$
A\mathbf x = \mathbf b
$$

can be found by solving the normal equation

$$
\begin{aligned}
A^{T}Ax^(^) &= A^{T}b
\end{aligned}
$$

The vector $A{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}$ must be the orthogonal projection of $\mathbf{b}$ onto the subspace spanned by the columns of $A$.

![](<../Source/3.1.1. The Least-Squares Solution of a Linear System (Without Collinearity)/Images/a5d508e71c2b7ab3ba2ec4e3b3d86e38.png>)

As a result, we have that

$$
(A{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}-\mathbf{b}) \perp \textrm{Col}(A)
$$

which is equivalent to saying that $A{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}-\mathbf{b}$ is orthogonal to each column $\mathbf{a}_i$ of $A$. Thus, the dot product of the columns of $A$ and the vector $A{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}-\mathbf{b}$ must be equal to $0{:}$

$$
{a_{1}^{T}(Ax^(^) - b) = 0; a_{2}^{T}(Ax^(^) - b) = 0; ⋮; a_{n}^{T}(Ax^(^) - b) = 0
$$

This system of equations can be written in terms of matrix products as

$$
A^T (A{\hat{\mathbf{x}}}-\mathbf{b}) = \mathbf{0}
$$

Finally, expanding the left-hand side, we obtain our normal equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
A^{T}(Ax^(^) - b) &= 0 \\
A^{T}Ax^(^) - A^{T}b &= 0 \\
A^{T}Ax^(^) &= A^{T}b
\end{aligned}
$$
