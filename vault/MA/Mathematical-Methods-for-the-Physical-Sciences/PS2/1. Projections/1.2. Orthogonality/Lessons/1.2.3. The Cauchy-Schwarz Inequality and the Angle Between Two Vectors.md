## Introduction

The **Cauchy-Schwarz Inequality** (sometimes called the **Cauchy-Bunyakovsky-Schwarz Inequality**) provides a connection between dot products and norms. It states that for any two vectors $\mathbf{u}$ and $\mathbf{v}$ in $\mathbb{R}^n$, we have the inequality

$$
\vert \mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{v} \vert \leq \Vert \mathbf{u} \Vert \Vert \mathbf{v} \Vert
$$

In other words, the absolute value of the dot product is no more than the product of the norms.

For instance, if $\begin{bmatrix}3 \\ -4\end{bmatrix}$ and $\begin{bmatrix}5 \\ 12\end{bmatrix}$ then

$$
\mid u \cdot v \mid \mid \le ‖u‖ \cdot ‖v‖; \mid 3 \cdot 5 + (-4) \cdot 12 \mid \mid \le \sqrt{3^{2} + (-4)^{2}}\sqrt{5^{2} + 12^{2}}; \mid - 33 \mid \mid \le 5 \cdot 13; 33 \mid \le 65.✓
$$

**Note:** An easy trick to remember the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality is to think of the formula for the dot product:

$$
\mathbf u \cdot \mathbf v = \Vert \mathbf u \Vert \Vert \mathbf v \Vert \cos \theta
$$

Because

$$
\vert \cos \theta \vert \leq 1
$$

we can see that

$$
\vert \mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{v} \vert \leq \Vert \mathbf{u} \Vert \Vert \mathbf{v} \Vert
$$

**Watch out!** The above trick is only a mnemonic trick, *not* an actual proof of the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality. The formula

$$
\mathbf u \cdot \mathbf v = \Vert \mathbf u \Vert \Vert \mathbf v \Vert \cos \theta
$$

is actually a *consequence* of the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, so a proof of the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality cannot use the formula.

<a id="determining-a-minimum-possible-value-using-the-cauchy-schwarz-inequality"></a>
## Determining a Minimum Possible Value Using the Cauchy-Schwarz Inequality

**Example:** Consider the vector $\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ \sqrt{k} \\ -2 \\ 1\end{bmatrix}$ Given that $\mathbf{u}\cdot \mathbf{v}=-12$ and $\Vert \mathbf{v}\Vert =4$, what is the smallest possible value of $k$?

**Explanation**

We will use the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,

$$
\vert \mathbf{u}\cdot \mathbf{v} \vert \leq \Vert \mathbf{u} \Vert \cdot \Vert \mathbf{v} \Vert
$$

We are given all the quantities in the inequality above, except for $\Vert \mathbf{u} \Vert$. So first, we compute $\Vert \mathbf{u} \Vert$ as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
‖u‖ &= \sqrt{1^{2} + (\sqrt{k})^{2} + (-2)^{2} + 1^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{6 + k}
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we have

$$
\mid u \cdot v \mid \mid \le ‖u‖ \cdot ‖v‖; \mid - 12 \mid \mid \le \sqrt{6 + k} \cdot (4); 12\begin{vmatrix}\le 4\sqrt{6 + k} \\ 3\end{vmatrix}\le \sqrt{6 + k}; 3^{2}\begin{vmatrix}\le (\sqrt{6 + k})^{2} \\ 9\end{vmatrix}\le 6 + k; 3 \mid \le k
$$

Therefore, the smallest possible value of $k$ is $3$.

## The Angle Between Two Vectors

Now, using the dot product, we can define the acute angle $\theta$ between two vectors $\mathbf{u}$ and $\mathbf{v}$ in $\mathbb{R}^n$, where

$$
n \ge 2
$$

.

![](<../Source/1.2.3. The Cauchy-Schwarz Inequality and the Angle Between Two Vectors/Images/108f7904606dade0406336d5988db3f2.png>)

To do that, we say that $\cos\theta$ will be equal to the ratio of the two sides of the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, as follows:

$$
\cos\theta = \dfrac{\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v}}{\Vert \mathbf{u} \Vert \cdot \Vert \mathbf{v} \Vert}
$$

Notice that this formula is completely analogous to the formula for the angle between two vectors in two-dimensional or three-dimensional space. It only works for non-zero vectors (otherwise, we will be dividing by zero).

Since the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality guarantees that

$$
\begin{vmatrix}\mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{v} & \leq \| \mathbf{u} \| \cdot \| \mathbf{v} \\end{vmatrix}
$$

, we get

$$
-1 \leq \dfrac{\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v}}{\Vert \mathbf{u} \Vert \cdot \Vert \mathbf{v} \Vert} \leq 1
$$

which means that $\theta$ can be found given any two non-zero $\mathbf{u}$ and $\mathbf{v}$.

<a id="finding-the-acute-angle-between-two-vectors"></a>
## Finding the Acute Angle Between Two Vectors

**Example:** Find the acute angle between the vectors $\begin{bmatrix}-1 \\ 2 \\ -4 \\ -2\end{bmatrix}$ and $\begin{bmatrix}-2 \\ 2 \\ -2 \\ 2\end{bmatrix}$

**Explanation**

Using the formula

$$
\cos\theta = \dfrac{\mathbf{v} \cdot \mathbf{w}}{\Vert\mathbf{v}\Vert \, \Vert \mathbf{w}\Vert}
$$

we have the following:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\cos θ &= \frac{v_{1}w_{1} + v_{2}w_{2} + v_{3}w_{3} + v_{4}w_{4}}{\sqrt{v_{1}^{2} + v_{2}^{2} + v_{3}^{2} + v_{4}^{2}}\sqrt{w_{1}^{2} + w_{2}^{2} + w_{3}^{2} + w_{4}^{2}}} \\
&= ((-1) \cdot (-2) + 2 \cdot 2 + (-4) \cdot (-2) + (-2) \cdot 2)/(\sqrt{(-1)^{2} + 2^{2} + (-4)^{2} + (-2)^{2}}\sqrt{(-2)^{2} + 2^{2} + (-2)^{2} + 2^{2}}) \\
&= \frac{10}{\sqrt{25} \cdot \sqrt{16}} \\
&= \frac{10}{5 \cdot 4} \\
&= \frac{1}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\theta=\arccos \left(\dfrac{1}{2} \right)=\dfrac{\pi}{3}
$$

<a id="calculating-the-cosine-of-an-angle"></a>
## Calculating the Cosine of an Angle

**Example:** Let $A(-2, 4, -2, 2)$ and $B(4, 0, -1, -4)$. Find the cosine of the angle $\angle{AOB}$, where $O(0,0,0,0)$ is the origin.

**Explanation**

First, let

$$
a = OA^(→) = \begin{bmatrix}-2 \\ 4 \\ -2 \\ 2\end{bmatrix}, b = OB^(→) = \begin{bmatrix}4 \\ 0 \\ -1 \\ -4\end{bmatrix}
$$

Also, let

$$
\theta = m\angle{AOB}
$$

be the angle between the vectors $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$. Then, we can find $\cos \theta$ using the formula:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\cos θ &= \frac{a \cdot b}{‖a‖‖b‖} \\
&= \frac{a_{1}b_{1} + a_{2}b_{2} + a_{3}b_{3} + a_{4}b_{4}}{\sqrt{a_{1}^{2} + a_{2}^{2} + a_{3}^{2} + a_{4}^{2}}\sqrt{b_{1}^{2} + b_{2}^{2} + b_{3}^{2} + b_{4}^{2}}} \\
&= ((-2) \cdot 4 + 4 \cdot 0 + (-2) \cdot (-1) + 2 \cdot (-4))/(\sqrt{(-2)^{2} + 4^{2} + (-2)^{2} + 2^{2}}\sqrt{4^{2} + 0^{2} + (-1)^{2} + (-4)^{2}})) \\
&= \frac{-14}{\sqrt{28} \cdot \sqrt{33}} \\
&= \frac{-14}{\sqrt{14} \cdot \sqrt{2} \cdot \sqrt{33}} \\
&=-\frac{\sqrt{14}}{\sqrt{2} \cdot \sqrt{33}} \\
&=-\sqrt{\frac{7}{33}}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Algebraic Proof of the Schwarz Inequality

Now, let's prove the general statement of the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality for vectors $\mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v} \in \mathbb{R}^n$:

$$
\vert \mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v} \vert \leq \Vert \mathbf{u} \Vert \Vert \mathbf{v} \Vert
$$

To start, let $t\in\mathbb{R}$ be any number, and consider the vector $t\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{v}$. Since

$$
\Vert t\mathbf{u} + \mathbf{v} \Vert \geq 0
$$

using the definition and properties of the dot product, we have

$$
\begin{bmatrix}‖tu + v‖^{2} & \ge 0 \\ (tu + v) \cdot (tu + v) & \ge 0 \\ t^{2}(u \cdot u) + t(u \cdot v) + t(v \cdot u) + (v \cdot v) & \ge 0 \\ t^{2}‖u‖^{2} + 2t(u \cdot v) + ‖v‖^{2} & \ge 0\end{bmatrix}
$$

In the last line above, we have a quadratic with the coefficients

$$
a=\Vert \mathbf{u} \Vert^2
$$

$$
b=2\left(\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v} \right)
$$

and

$$
c=\Vert \mathbf{v} \Vert^2
$$

Since the quadratic is greater than or equal to zero, its discriminant must be less than or equal to zero,

$$
\mathcal{D} \leq 0
$$

Therefore, we have

$$
D\begin{vmatrix}\le 0 \\ b^{2} - 4ac\end{vmatrix}\le 0; (2(u \cdot v))^{2} - 4 \cdot ‖u‖^{2} \cdot ‖v‖^{2}\begin{vmatrix}\le 0 \\ 4(u \cdot v)^{2} - 4‖u‖^{2}‖v‖^{2}\end{vmatrix}\le 0; (u \cdot v)^{2}\begin{vmatrix}\le ‖u‖^{2}‖v‖^{2} \\ \sqrt{(u \cdot v)^{2}}\end{vmatrix}\le \sqrt{‖u‖^{2}‖v‖^{2}}; \mid u \cdot v \mid \mid \le ‖u‖‖v‖
$$
