## Introduction

Let $\mathbf{u}$ and $\mathbf{v}$ be vectors in $\mathbb{R}^n$. What do we mean when we say that the vectors $\mathbf{u}$ and $\mathbf{v}$ are orthogonal?

Two vectors $\mathbf{u}$ and $\mathbf{v}$ are **orthogonal** if their dot product equals zero:

$$
\mathbf{u}\cdot \mathbf{v}=0
$$

If two vectors are orthogonal, then we denote this by $\mathbf{u} \!\perp\! \mathbf{v}$. For instance, the vectors

$$
\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 0\end{bmatrix}
$$

in $\mathbb{R^2}$ are orthogonal, since

$$
\begin{aligned}
e_{1} \cdot e_{2} &= \begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 0\end{bmatrix} \cdot \begin{bmatrix}0 \\ 1\end{bmatrix} \\
&= 1 \cdot 0 + 0 \cdot 1 \\
&= 0 + 0 \\
&= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

**Note:** The zero vector $\mathbf{0}$ is orthogonal to any vector $\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{R}^n$ since

$$
\mathbf{0}\cdot\mathbf{u}=0
$$

.

## Identifying Orthogonal Vectors

**Example:** Given the vector $\begin{bmatrix}-5 \\ -3 \\ 2 \\ 0\end{bmatrix}$, which of the following vectors are orthogonal to $\mathbf{u}$?
$\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ -3 \\ -4 \\ 9\end{bmatrix}$

**Explanation**

We compute the dot product between $\mathbf{u}$ and each of the listed vectors:

$$
\begin{aligned}
u \cdot v &= u_{1} \cdot v_{1} + u_{2} \cdot v_{2} + u_{3} \cdot v_{3} + u_{4} \cdot v_{4} \\
&= (-5) \cdot 1 + (-3) \cdot (-3) + 2 \cdot (-4) + 0 \cdot 9 \\
&=-5 + 9 - 8 + 0 \\
&=-4.\cdot
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
u \cdot w &= u_{1} \cdot w_{1} + u_{2} \cdot w_{2} + u_{3} \cdot w_{3} + u_{4} \cdot w_{4} \\
&= (-5) \cdot 2 + (-3) \cdot 2 + 2 \cdot 8 + 0 \cdot 3 \\
&=-10 - 6 + 16 + 0 \\
&= 0.✓
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
u \cdot x &= u_{1} \cdot x_{1} + u_{2} \cdot x_{2} + u_{3} \cdot x_{3} + u_{4} \cdot x_{4} \\
&= (-5) \cdot 2 + (-3) \cdot (-4) + 2 \cdot (-8) + 0 \cdot 6 \\
&=-10 + 12 - 16 + 0 \\
&=-14.\cdot
\end{aligned}
$$

Consequently, the correct answer is $\mathbf{w}$ only.

<a id="determining-components-in-orthogonal-vectors"></a>
## Determining Components in Orthogonal Vectors

**Example:** If $\begin{bmatrix}5 \\ -3 \\ k \\ -2\end{bmatrix}$ and $\begin{bmatrix}-4 \\ 6 \\ -6 \\ 8\end{bmatrix}$ are orthogonal vectors, find the value of $k$.

**Explanation**

Since $\mathbf{u}$ and $\mathbf{v}$ are orthogonal vectors, we have that their dot product is equal to zero.
First, let's calculate the dot product in terms of $k$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
u \cdot v &= u_{1} \cdot v_{1} + u_{2} \cdot v_{2} + u_{3} \cdot v_{3} + u_{4} \cdot v_{4} \\
&= 5 \cdot (-4) + (-3) \cdot 6 + k \cdot (-6) + (-2) \cdot 8 \\
&=-20 - 18 - 6k - 16 \\
&=-6k - 54
\end{aligned}
$$

Equating the last line to zero, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
-6k - 54 &= 0 \\
- 6k &= 54 \\
k &= -9
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="calculating-dot-products-using-orthogonal-vectors"></a>
## Calculating Dot Products Using Orthogonal Vectors

**Example:** Let $\begin{bmatrix}-1 \\ -2 \\ 5\end{bmatrix}$ and $\begin{bmatrix}-1 \\ 3 \\ 1\end{bmatrix}$ be vectors such that $\mathbf{u} \!\perp\! \mathbf{w}$. Find $(\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{v})\cdot \mathbf{w} + \mathbf{u}\cdot(\mathbf{v}+ \mathbf{w})$.

**Explanation**

Since $\mathbf{u}$ and $\mathbf{w}$ are orthogonal vectors, we have that

$$
\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{w}=0
$$

. Therefore, using linearity of the dot product to expand the parentheses, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
(u + v) \cdot w + u \cdot (v + w) &= u \cdot w + v \cdot w + u \cdot v + u \cdot w \\
&= 0 + v \cdot w + u \cdot v + 0 \\
&= v \cdot w + u \cdot v \\
&= (2 \cdot (-1) + (-3) \cdot 3 + 4 \cdot 1) \\
&= + ((-1) \cdot 2 + (-2) \cdot (-3) + 5 \cdot 4) \\
&= (-2 - 9 + 4) + (-2 + 6 + 20) \\
&=-7 + 24 \\
&= 17
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="determining-components-of-a-vector-orthogonal-to-two-given-vectors"></a>
## Determining Components of a Vector Orthogonal to Two Given Vectors

**Example:** Let $\begin{bmatrix}-8 \\ a \\ b \\ -10\end{bmatrix}$ and $\begin{bmatrix}4 \\ 2 \\ -5 \\ -3\end{bmatrix}$ be vectors such that $\mathbf{u}$ is orthogonal to $\mathbf{v}$ and $\mathbf{w}$. What is the value of $a+b$?

**Explanation**

Given that $\mathbf{u}$ is orthogonal to $\mathbf{v}$ and $\mathbf{w}$, we have

$$
{u \cdot v = 0; u \cdot w = 0
$$

Let's consider the first equation

$$
\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v}=0
$$

. Calculating the dot product in terms of $a$ and $b$, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
u \cdot v &= 0 \\
- 8 \cdot 6 + a \cdot (-15) + b \cdot 9 + (-10) \cdot (-12) &= 0 \\
- 48 - 15a + 9b + 120 &= 0 \\
- 15a + 9b &= -72 \\
- 5a + 3b &= -24
\end{aligned}
$$

Similarly, for the second equation

$$
\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{w}=0
$$

, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
u \cdot w &= 0 \\
- 8 \cdot 4 + a \cdot 2 + b \cdot (-5) + (-10) \cdot (-3) &= 0 \\
- 32 + 2a - 5b + 30 &= 0 \\
2a - 5b &= 2
\end{aligned}
$$

So, to find the values of $a$ and $b$, we need to solve the system

$$
{-5a + 3b =-24; 2a - 5b = 2
$$

Multiplying the first equation by $2$ and the second equation by $5$, we get

$$
{-10a + 6b =-48; 10a - 25b = 10
$$

Now, adding the above equations, we obtain

$$
-19b = -38 \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad b = 2
$$

Substituting $b = 2$ into the second equation, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
2a - 5(2) &= 2 \\
2a - 10 &= 2 \\
2a &= 12 \\
a &= 6
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we have that

$$
a+b = 6 + 2 = 8
$$
