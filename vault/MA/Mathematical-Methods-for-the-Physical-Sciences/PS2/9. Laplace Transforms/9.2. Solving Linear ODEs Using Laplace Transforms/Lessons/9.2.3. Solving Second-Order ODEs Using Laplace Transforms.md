## Introduction

In this lesson, we will learn how to use Laplace transforms to solve second-order ODEs. As before, let's practice finding the Laplace transform of a second-order differential equation first.

For example, let's find the Laplace transform of $y(t)$ if

$$
y'' = t, \qquad y(0)=2, \qquad y'(0)=3
$$

First, we take the Laplace transform of both sides of the equation:

$$
\mathcal{L}\left\{y'' \right\} = \mathcal{L}\left\{t \right\}
$$

Next, we simplify each side.

- For the left-hand side, we use the formula for the Laplace transform of a second derivative. Letting $\mathcal{L}\left\{y \right\}=Y(s)$ and using the initial conditions $y(0)=2, \, y'(0)=3$, we have
$L{y^{″}}|= s^{2}L{y} - sy(0) - y^{′}(0); = s^{2}Y(s) - sy(0) - y^{′}(0); = s^{2}Y(s) - 2s - 3$.
- For the right-hand side, we use the following result from the table of Laplace transforms:
$\mathcal{L} \{t \} = \dfrac{1}{s^2}$.

Finally, we can solve for $Y(s){:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
L{y^{″}} &= L{t} \\
s^{2}Y(s) - 2s - 3 &= \frac{1}{s^{2}} \\
s^{2}Y(s) &= \frac{1}{s^{2}} + 3 + 2s \\
Y(s) &= \frac{1}{s^{4}} + \frac{3}{s^{2}} + \frac{2}{s}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the Laplace transform of $y(t)$ is

$$
Y(s) = \dfrac{1}{s^4} + \dfrac{3}{s^2} + \dfrac{2}{s}
$$

Let's see more examples.

## Finding the Laplace Transform of a Second-Order ODE

**Example:** Find the Laplace transform $Y(s) = \mathcal{L}\left\{y(t)\right\}$ for $s\geq\sqrt5$ if
$y'' - 5y = 4e^{-3t}, \quad y(0)=2, \quad y'(0)=-1$.

**Explanation**

First, we take the Laplace transform of both sides and use the addition and scalar multiplication properties of the Laplace transform:

$$
\begin{aligned}
L{y^{″} - 5y} &= L{4e^{-3t}} \\
L{y^{″}} - 5L{y} &= 4L{e^{-3t}}
\end{aligned}
$$

To calculate

$$
\mathcal{L}\left\{y''\right\}
$$

we use the formula for the Laplace transform of a second derivative:

$$
\mathcal{L}\left\{y''\right\}=s^2\mathcal{L}\left\{y\right\}-sy(0)-y'(0)=s^2Y(s)-sy(0)-y'(0)
$$

Taking into account the given initial conditions, we get

$$
\mathcal{L}\left\{y''\right\}=s^2Y(s)-2s+1
$$

From the table of Laplace transforms, we have the following result:

$$
\mathcal{L}\left\{e^{-3t}\right\}=\dfrac{1}{s+3}
$$

Therefore, we can solve for $Y(s)$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
L{y^{″}} - 5L{y} &= 4L{e^{-3t}} \\
(s^{2}Y(s) - 2s + 1) - 5Y(s) &= \frac{4}{s + 3} \\
(s^{2} - 5)Y(s) &= \frac{4}{s + 3} + 2s - 1 \\
(s^{2} - 5)Y(s) &= (4 + (2s - 1)(s + 3))/(s + 3) \\
(s^{2} - 5)Y(s) &= \frac{2s^{2} + 5s + 1}{s + 3} \\
Y(s) &= (2s^{2} + 5s + 1)/((s + 3)(s^{2} - 5))
\end{aligned}
$$

## Using Laplace Transforms to Solve Second-Order Differential Equations

Now, let's see how to use Laplace transforms to solve second-order ODEs. There are two steps:

- **Step 1:** Find the Laplace transform of the equation, and solve for $Y(s)$.
- **Step 2:** Find the solution by taking the inverse Laplace transform.

To illustrate, let's continue solving the following differential equation:

$$
y'' = t, \quad y(0)=2, \quad y'(0)=3
$$

Earlier, we found that the Laplace transform of $y(t)$ is

$$
Y(s) = \dfrac{1}{s^4} + \dfrac{3}{s^2} + \dfrac{2}{s}
$$

Now, we need to take the inverse Laplace transform. From the table of Laplace transforms, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
L^{-1}{\frac{6}{s^{4}}} &= t^{3}, \\
L^{-1}{\frac{1}{s^{2}}} &= t, \\
L^{-1}{\frac{1}{s}} &= 1
\end{aligned}
$$

So, taking the inverse Laplace transform, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
L^{-1}{Y(s)} &= L^{-1}{\frac{1}{s^{4}} + \frac{3}{s^{2}} + \frac{2}{s}} \\
y(t) &= L^{-1}{\frac{1}{s^{4}}} + L^{-1}{\frac{3}{s^{2}}} + L^{-1}{\frac{2}{s}} \\
y(t) &= \frac{1}{6}L^{-1}{\frac{6}{s^{4}}} + 3L^{-1}{\frac{1}{s^{2}}} + 2L^{-1}{\frac{1}{s}} \\
y(t) &= \frac{1}{6}t^{3} + 3t + 2
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the solution to the ODE is

$$
y(t) = \dfrac{1}{6} t^3 + 3 t + 2
$$

## Solving a Second-Order Initial Value Problem Using Laplace Transforms

**Example:** Find $y(t)$ if the Laplace transform of the initial value problem
$y'' + 6y = 2\sin {2t}, \quad y(0) = 0, \quad y'(0) = 2$

is given by

$s^2Y(s) - 2 +6 Y(s) = 2\left(\dfrac{2}{s^2 + 4}\right)$.

**Explanation**

Using the transformed equation, we can solve for $Y(s)$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
s^{2}Y(s) - 2 + 6Y(s) &= 2(\frac{2}{s^{2} + 4}), \\
(s^{2} + 6)Y(s) &= 2 + \frac{4}{s^{2} + 4} \\
(s^{2} + 6)Y(s) &= \frac{2s^{2} + 12}{s^{2} + 4} \\
(s^{2} + 6)Y(s) &= (2(s^{2} + 6))/(s^{2} + 4) \\
Y(s) &= \frac{2}{s^{2} + 4}
\end{aligned}
$$

From the table of Laplace transforms, we have

$$
L{\sin 2t} = \frac{2}{s^{2} + 4}
$$

Now, taking the inverse Laplace transform, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
L^{-1}{Y(s)} &= L^{-1}{\frac{2}{s^{2} + 4}} \\
y(t) &= \sin 2t
\end{aligned}
$$

So,

$$
y(t) = \sin {2t}
$$

is the solution of the original differential equation.
