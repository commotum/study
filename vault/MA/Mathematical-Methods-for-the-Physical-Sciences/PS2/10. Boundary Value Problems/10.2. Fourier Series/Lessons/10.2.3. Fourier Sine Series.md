## Introduction

In the earlier topics, we saw that *symmetry* can force many Fourier coefficients to be zero automatically.
In particular, if a $2\pi$-periodic function is *odd*, then its Fourier series has *sine terms only*.

A **Fourier sine series** uses this idea when a function is initially given only on the half-interval, say, $[MATH: [0,\pi),]$ but we want a full Fourier series on $[MATH: [-\pi,\pi)]$ (and then a $2\pi$-periodic extension to all real $x$).

To do this, we start with a function $F(x)$ on $[MATH: [0,\pi)]$ and define its **odd extension** $f(x)$ to $(-\pi,\pi)$ by
$[MATH: {F(x), \mid x ∈ [0, π),; - F(-x), \mid x ∈ (- π, 0).]$

For example, a function $F(x)$, defined on $[MATH: x \in [0,1),]$ and its odd extension $f(x)$ to $x \in (-1,1)$ are shown below.

![](<../Source/10.2.3. Fourier Sine Series/Images/ea7327f1733f126b31b7c13f8a7f7a13.png>)

By construction, $f$ is an *odd* function, meaning

$$
f(-x)=-f(x)
$$

. Because $f$ is odd, its Fourier series on $[MATH: [-\pi,\pi)]$ has *sine terms only*:

$$
f(x)\sim \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} b_n\sin(nx)
$$

Since $f(x)$ is odd and $\sin(nx)$ is odd for any

$$
n \geq 1
$$

then $f(x)\sin(nx)$ must be even.
So, the sine coefficients can be computed using the *half-range formula*:

$$
\begin{aligned}
b_{n} &= \frac{1}{π}\int_{- π}^{π}f(x)\sin (nx)dx \\
&= \frac{1}{π} \cdot 2\int_{0}^{π}f(x)\sin (nx)dx \\
&= \frac{2}{π}\int_{0}^{π}f(x)\sin (nx)dx
\end{aligned}
$$

So in practice, the workflow is the following:

1. Start with $F$ on $[MATH: [0,\pi)]$ and form the odd extension $f$ on $(-\pi,\pi)$.
2. Compute $b_n$ from the half-range integral on $[MATH: [0,\pi).]$
3. Write the Fourier sine series of $f$.

Let's see some concrete examples.

## Understanding the Odd Extension of a Function

**Example:** Let $F(x)=x^2+10x$ for $[MATH: x\in[0,6),]$ and let $f(x)$ be the **odd** extension of $F(x)$ to $x \in (-6,6)$. What is the value of $f(-1)$?

**Explanation**

If $F(x)$ is defined on $[MATH: x\in[0,6),]$ the odd extension of $F$ is the function $f$ such that
$[MATH: {F(x), \mid x ∈ [0, 6),; - F(-x), \mid x ∈ (-6, 0).]$

In our case, for $x\in(-6,0)$, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
f(x) &= -F(-x) \\
&= - ((-x)^{2} + 10(-x)) \\
&= - (x^{2} - 10x) \\
&=-x^{2} + 10x
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
f(-1) &= - (-1)^{2} + 10(-1) =-11
\end{aligned}
$$

## Computing the Sine Coefficients From the Half-Range Formula

**Example:** Let $F(x)=4x$ for $[MATH: x\in[0,\pi).]$ Find the missing parts in the Fourier sine series of the **odd** $2\pi$-periodic extension $f(x)$ of the function $F(x)$ below.

$AAAAA_{A}^{A}$

**Explanation**

For our Fourier sine series, the coefficients are given by

$$
b_n=\frac{2}{\pi}\int_0^\pi f(x)\sin(nx)\,\text{d}x
$$

In our case,

$$
f(x)=4x
$$

for $x \in (0,\pi)$. So, substituting into the formula, we get

$$
b_n = \dfrac{8}{\pi}\int_0^\pi x \sin(nx)\,\text{d}x
$$

We use the integration by parts:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}u = x & ⇒u^{′} = 1 \\ v^{′} = \sin (nx) & ⇒v =-\frac{1}{n}\cos (nx)\end{bmatrix}
$$

Thus, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
b_{n} &= \left.\frac{8}{π}(uv\right|_{0}^{π} - \int_{0}^{π}vu^{′}dx) \\
&= \frac{8}{π}(-\frac{1}{n}[x\cos (nx)]_{0}^{π} + \frac{1}{n}\int_{0}^{π}\cos (nx)dx) \\
&=-\frac{8}{nπ}((π \cdot \cos (nπ) - 0 \cdot \cos (0)) - \frac{1}{n}[\sin (nx)]_{0}^{π}) \\
&=-\frac{8}{nπ}(π\cos (nπ) - \frac{1}{n}(\sin (nπ) - \sin (0))) \\
&= - (8\cos (nπ))/(n)
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, since

$$
\cos(n\pi)=(-1)^n
$$

we have

$$
b_n = -\dfrac{8(-1)^n}{n}=\dfrac{8(-1)^{n+1}}{n}
$$

Finally, the Fourier sine series is

$$
f(x) \sim \sum_{n=1}^\infty \dfrac{8(-1)^{n+1}}{n} \cdot \sin(nx)
$$
