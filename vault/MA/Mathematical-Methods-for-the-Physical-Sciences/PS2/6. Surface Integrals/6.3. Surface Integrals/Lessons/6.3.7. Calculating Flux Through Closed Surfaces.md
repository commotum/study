## Introduction

Remember that when we measure the flux of a vector field through a *closed* surface, we always calculate with respect to an outward-pointing unit normal vector.

For example, suppose we have the vector field $\mathbf F$, given by

$$
\mathbf{F} = \langle x, \,0, \, -y^2 \rangle
$$

and the closed surface $S$ that represents the boundary of the conical solid shown below.

![](<../Source/6.3.7. Calculating Flux Through Closed Surfaces/Images/7ac700cc8ecdc67020a560a0ed79ba3c.png>)

Let's calculate the flux of $\mathbf F$ through $S$.

First, since $S$ is a closed surface, we have that

- the upper surface $S_1: z = g(x,y) = 2 - \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$ is oriented *upward*, and
- the lower surface $S_2: z = h(x,y) = 0$ is oriented *downward*.

Now, we write down the flux across each of the surfaces separately.

- First, we consider the upper cone $S_1$. We compute the partial derivatives of $z=g(x,y){:}$
$\frac{∂g}{∂x}|= \frac{∂}{∂x}(2 - \sqrt{x^{2} + y^{2}}) =-\frac{x}{\sqrt{x^{2} + y^{2}}}; \frac{∂g}{∂y}|= \frac{∂}{∂y}(2 - \sqrt{x^{2} + y^{2}}) =-\frac{y}{\sqrt{x^{2} + y^{2}}}$
If we denote $\mathbf{F}=\langle P, \, Q, \, R \rangle$, then
$P = x, \qquad Q = 0, \qquad R = -y^2$.
As a result, since the positive orientation is upward, we have
$∬_(S_{1})F \cdot dS|= ∬_(D)(-P\frac{∂g}{∂x} - Q\frac{∂g}{∂y} + R)dA; = ∬_(D) - x \cdot (-\frac{x}{\sqrt{x^{2} + y^{2}}}) - 0 \cdot (-\frac{y}{\sqrt{x^{2} + y^{2}}}) + (-y^{2})dA; = ∬_(D)\frac{x^{2}}{\sqrt{x^{2} + y^{2}}} - y^{2}dA$,
where $D$ is the projection of $S$ onto the $xy$-plane.
- Next, we consider the lower plane $S_2$. Since the positive orientation is downward, the unit normal vector to the plane is $\mathbf{n} = \langle 0,0,-1 \rangle$. As a result, we have
$∬_(S_{2})F \cdot dS|= ∬_(S_{2})F \cdot ndS; = ∬_(D)⟨x, 0,-y^{2}⟩ \cdot ⟨0, 0,-1⟩dA; = ∬_(D)y^{2}dA$.
Therefore, the flux of $\mathbf{F}$ across the entire surface is given by
$∬_(S)F \cdot dS|= ∬_(S_{1})F \cdot dS + ∬_(S_{2})F \cdot dS; = ∬_(D)\frac{x^{2}}{\sqrt{x^{2} + y^{2}}} - y^{2}dA + ∬_(D)y^{2}dA; = ∬_(D)\frac{x^{2}}{\sqrt{x^{2} + y^{2}}}dA$.

## Expressing Flux as a Double Integral Given a Diagram

**Example:** Consider the vector field $\mathbf{F} = \langle 0, \, x, \, -z^2 \rangle$ and the closed surface $S$ that represents the boundary of the solid shown above. Let $D$ be the projection of the solid onto the $xy$-plane. The flux of $\mathbf{F}$ across $S$ can be expressed in terms of a double integral as
$\displaystyle \iint\limits_S \mathbf{F} \cdot \mathrm{d}\mathbf{S} = \iint\limits_D \,\bbox[3pt, border: 1pt solid black]{\phantom{AAAAAAA}} \: \textrm{d}A$.
Determine the missing expression.

![](<../Source/6.3.7. Calculating Flux Through Closed Surfaces/Images/020906e1438cc7bd21000dc9fa4ca331.png>)

**Explanation**

Since we have a closed surface, the positive orientation is outward-pointing by convention. So,

- the upper surface $S_1: z = g(x,y) = 0$ is oriented upward, and
- the lower surface $S_2: z = h(x,y) = -\sqrt{4 - x^2 - y^2}$ is oriented downward.

Now, we write down the flux across each of the surfaces separately.

- Consider the upper plane $S_1$. Since the positive orientation is upward, the unit normal vector to the plane is $\mathbf{n} = \langle 0,0,1 \rangle$. As a result, we have
$∬_(S_{1})F \cdot dS|= ∬_(S_{1})F \cdot ndS; = ∬_(D)⟨0, x,-z^{2}⟩ \cdot ⟨0, 0, 1⟩dA; = ∬_(D) - z^{2}dA; = ∬_(D)0dA; = 0$.
- Consider the lower hemisphere $S_2$.We compute the partial derivatives of $z = h(x,y){:}$
$\frac{∂h}{∂x}|= \frac{∂}{∂x}(-\sqrt{4 - x^{2} - y^{2}}) = \frac{x}{\sqrt{4 - x^{2} - y^{2}}}; \frac{∂h}{∂y}|= \frac{∂}{∂y}(-\sqrt{4 - x^{2} - y^{2}}) = \frac{y}{\sqrt{4 - x^{2} - y^{2}}}$
If we denote $\mathbf{F}=\langle P, \, Q, \, R \rangle$, then
$P = 0, \qquad Q = x, \qquad R = -z^2 = x^2 + y^2 - 4$.
As a result, since the positive orientation is downward, we have
$∬_(S_{2})F \cdot dS|= - ∬_(D)(-P\frac{∂h}{∂x} - Q\frac{∂h}{∂y} + R)dA; = - ∬_(D)(-0 \cdot \frac{x}{\sqrt{4 - x^{2} - y^{2}}} - x \cdot \frac{y}{\sqrt{4 - x^{2} - y^{2}}} + x^{2} + y^{2} - 4)dA; = ∬_(D)\frac{xy}{\sqrt{4 - x^{2} - y^{2}}} - x^{2} - y^{2} + 4dA$.

Therefore, the flux across the entire surface is

$$
\begin{aligned}
∬_(S)F \cdot dS &= ∬_(S_{1})F \cdot dS + ∬_(S_{2})F \cdot dS \\
&= ∬_(D)x y 4 - x 2 - y 2 - x 2 - y 2 + 4dA
\end{aligned}
$$

## Expressing Flux as a Double Integral

**Example:** Consider the vector field $\mathbf{F} = \langle x, \, y, \, 0 \rangle$ and the closed surface $S$ that encloses the finite region between the parabolic cylinder $z=1-x^2$ and the paraboloid $z=x^2+y^2$. Let $D$ be the projection of $S$ onto the $xy$-plane. The flux of $\mathbf{F}$ across $S$ can be expressed in terms of a double integral as
$\displaystyle \iint\limits_S \mathbf{F} \cdot \mathrm{d}\mathbf{S} = \iint\limits_D \,\bbox[3pt, border: 1pt solid black]{\phantom{AAAAAAA}} \: \textrm{d}A$.
Which of the following could be the missing expression?

**Explanation**

Since we have a closed surface, the positive orientation is outward-pointing by convention. So,

- the upper surface $S_1: z=g(x,y)=1-x^2$ is oriented upward, and
- the lower surface $S_2: z=h(x,y)=x^2+y^2$ is oriented downward.

Now, we write down the flux across each of the surfaces separately.

- Consider the upper parabolic cylinder $S_1$.We compute the partial derivatives of $z=g(x,y){:}$
$\frac{∂g}{∂x}|= \frac{∂}{∂x}(1 - x^{2}) =-2x; \frac{∂g}{∂y}|= \frac{∂}{∂y}(1 - x^{2}) = 0$

> If we denote $\mathbf{F}=\langle P, \, Q, \, R \rangle$, then
> $P = x, \qquad Q = y, \qquad R = 0$.
> As a result, since the positive orientation is upward, we have
> $∬_(S_{1})F \cdot dS|= ∬_(D)(-P\frac{∂g}{∂x} - Q\frac{∂g}{∂y} + R)dA; = ∬_(D)(-x \cdot (-2x) - y \cdot (0) + 0)dA; = ∬_(D)2x^{2}dA$.

- Consider the lower paraboloid $S_2$. We compute the partial derivatives of $z=h(x,y){:}$
$\frac{∂h}{∂x}|= \frac{∂}{∂x}(x^{2} + y^{2}) = 2x; \frac{∂h}{∂y}|= \frac{∂}{∂y}(x^{2} + y^{2}) = 2y$
If we denote $\mathbf{F}=\langle P, \, Q, \, R \rangle$, then
$P = x, \qquad Q = y, \qquad R = 0$.
As a result, since the positive orientation is downward, we have
$∬_(S_{2})F \cdot dS|= - ∬_(D)(-P\frac{∂h}{∂x} - Q\frac{∂h}{∂y} + R)dA; = - ∬_(D)(-x \cdot (2x) - y \cdot (2y) + 0)dA; = - ∬_(D) - 2x^{2} - 2y^{2}dA; = ∬_(D)2x^{2} + 2y^{2}dA$.

Therefore, the flux across the entire surface is

$$
\begin{aligned}
∬_(S)F \cdot dS &= ∬_(S_{1})F \cdot dS + ∬_(S_{2})F \cdot dS \\
&= ∬_(D)2x^{2}dA + ∬_(D)2x^{2} + 2y^{2}dA \\
&= ∬_(D)4 x 2 + 2 y 2dA
\end{aligned}
$$
