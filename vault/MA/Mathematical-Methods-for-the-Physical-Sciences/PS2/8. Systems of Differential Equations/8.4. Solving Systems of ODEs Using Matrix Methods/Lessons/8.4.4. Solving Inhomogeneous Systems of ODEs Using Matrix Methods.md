## Introduction

Matrix exponentials can be used for solving *inhomogeneous systems* of ODEs as well. Consider the system

$$
\mathbf{x}'(t)=A\mathbf{x}(t)+\mathbf{g}(t)
$$

where $A$ is a constant square matrix, and suppose we are given an initial condition $\mathbf{x}(t_0)$.

Our goal is to derive the formula for the solution $\mathbf{x}(t)$ of the system.

First, we eliminate the *inhomogeneous term* by a change of variables. We define

$$
\mathbf{y}(t)=e^{-A(t-t_0)}\mathbf{x}(t)
$$

and differentiate it using the product rule:

$$
\begin{aligned}
y^{′}(t) &= (e^{-A(t - t_{0})})^{′}x(t) + e^{-A(t - t_{0})}x^{′}(t) \\
&= (-A)e^{-A(t - t_{0})}x(t) + e^{-A(t - t_{0})}(Ax(t) + g(t)) \\
&=-Ae^{-A(t - t_{0})}x(t) + Ae^{-A(t - t_{0})}x(t) + e^{-A(t - t_{0})}g(t) \\
&= e^{-A(t - t_{0})}g(t)
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we integrate both sides from $t_0$ to $t$ and get

$$
\mathbf{y}(t)-\mathbf{y}(t_0)=\int_{t_0}^{t} e^{-A(s-t_0)}\mathbf{g}(s)\,\text{d}s
$$

Next, since

$$
\mathbf{y}(t_0)=e^{-A(t_0-t_0)}\mathbf{x}(t_0)=\mathbf{x}(t_0)
$$

we have

$$
\mathbf{y}(t)=\mathbf{x}(t_0)+\int_{t_0}^{t} e^{-A(s-t_0)}\mathbf{g}(s)\,\text{d}s
$$

Finally, we convert back to the initial variable $\mathbf{x}(t)$. To do that we multiply both sides by $e^{A(t-t_0)}{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
e^{A(t - t_{0})}y(t) &= e^{A(t - t_{0})}x(t_{0}) + e^{A(t - t_{0})}∫_{t_{0}}^{t}e^{-A(s - t_{0})}g(s)ds \\
x(t) &= e^{A(t - t_{0})}x(t_{0}) + ∫_{t_{0}}^{t}e^{A(t - t_{0})}e^{-A(s - t_{0})}g(s)ds \\
&= e^{A(t - t_{0})}x(t_{0}) + ∫_{t_{0}}^{t}e^{A(t - t_{0}) - A(s - t_{0})}g(s)ds \\
&= e^{A(t - t_{0})}x(t_{0}) + ∫_{t_{0}}^{t}e^{A(t - s)}g(s)ds
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the solution to our IVP is

$$
\mathbf{x}(t) = \underbrace{e^{A(t-t_0)} \, \mathbf{x}(t_0)}_{\text{homogeneous part}} + \underbrace{\int_{t_0}^{t} e^{A(t-s)} \mathbf{g}(s) \, \text{d}s}_{\text{particular solution}}
$$

where the first term represents the solution of the corresponding *homogeneous system*, and the second term gives a particular solution of our *inhomogeneous system*.

Let's see some concrete examples.

## Identifying the Matrix Exponential Formula for IVP Solution of an Inhomogeneous System

**Example:** $\begin{bmatrix}4 & 1 \\ 0 & 4\end{bmatrix}$
Fill in the blanks in the expression that gives the solution of the initial value problem above.

*Hint: $\begin{bmatrix}e^{4t} & te^{4t} \\ 0 & e^{4t}\end{bmatrix}$ where $\begin{bmatrix}4 & 1 \\ 0 & 4\end{bmatrix}$*

$x(t)|= \begin{bmatrix}X X X X \\ 3e^{4(t - 1)}\end{bmatrix} + ∫_{1}^{t}\begin{bmatrix}e^{4t} \\ X X\end{bmatrix}ds$.

**Explanation**

Given the system

$$
\mathbf{x}'(t) = A \mathbf{x}(t)+\mathbf{g}(t)
$$

where $A$ is a constant square matrix, the solution of the initial value problem can be written as the sum of the homogeneous part and a particular solution:

$$
\mathbf{x}(t) = \underbrace{e^{A(t-t_0)} \, \mathbf{x}(t_0)}_{\text{homogeneous part}} + \underbrace{\int_{t_0}^{t} e^{A(t-s)} \mathbf{g}(s) \, \text{d}s}_{\text{particular solution}}
$$

where $\mathbf x(t_0)$ is the initial condition vector.

Let's evaluate each part separately:

- First, we find the homogeneous part. We are given that $\begin{bmatrix}e^{4t} & te^{4t} \\ 0 & e^{4t}\end{bmatrix}$ So,
$e^{A(t - 1)}x(1)|= \begin{bmatrix}e^{4(t - 1)} & (t - 1)e^{4(t - 1)} \\ 0 & e^{4(t - 1)}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}0 \\ 3\end{bmatrix}; = \begin{bmatrix}3(t - 1)e^{4(t - 1)} \\ 3e^{4(t - 1)}\end{bmatrix}$.
- Next, we find the particular solution. Notice that
$\begin{bmatrix}e^{4(t - s)} & (t - s)e^{4(t - s)} \\ 0 & e^{4(t - s)}\end{bmatrix}$
So, we have
$e^{A(t - s)}g(s)|= \begin{bmatrix}e^{4(t - s)} & (t - s)e^{4(t - s)} \\ 0 & e^{4(t - s)}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}e^{4s} \\ 0\end{bmatrix}; = \begin{bmatrix}e^{4t} \\ 0\end{bmatrix}$.

Therefore, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
x(t) &= \begin{bmatrix}3 (t - 1) e 4 (t - 1) \\ 3e^{4(t - 1)}\end{bmatrix} + ∫_{1}^{t}\begin{bmatrix}e^{4t} \\ 0\end{bmatrix}ds
\end{aligned}
$$

## Solving an Inhomogeneous System Using a Given Matrix Exponential

**Example:** ${x^{′}(t) = 3x(t) + y(t) + 1; y^{′}(t) = 3y(t)$,
Find the solution to the initial value problem above.

*Hint: $\begin{bmatrix}e^{3t} & te^{3t} \\ 0 & e^{3t}\end{bmatrix}$ where $\begin{bmatrix}3 & 1 \\ 0 & 3\end{bmatrix}$*

**Explanation**

Writing our system in matrix form, we get
$\begin{bmatrix}3 & 1 \\ 0 & 3\end{bmatrix}$
where $\begin{bmatrix}x(t) \\ y(t)\end{bmatrix}$ and $\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 0\end{bmatrix}$ So, the matrix of the system is $\begin{bmatrix}3 & 1 \\ 0 & 3\end{bmatrix}$

Given the system

$$
\mathbf{x}'(t) = A \mathbf{x}(t)+\mathbf{g}(t)
$$

where $A$ is a constant square matrix, the solution of the initial value problem can be written as the sum of the homogeneous part and a particular solution:

$$
\mathbf{x}(t) = \underbrace{e^{A(t-t_0)} \, \mathbf{x}(t_0)}_{\text{homogeneous part}} + \underbrace{\int_{t_0}^{t} e^{A(t-s)} \mathbf{g}(s) \, \text{d}s}_{\text{particular solution}}
$$

where $\mathbf x(t_0)$ is the initial condition vector.

Let's evaluate each part separately:

- First, we find the homogeneous part. We are given that
$\begin{bmatrix}e^{3t} & te^{3t} \\ 0 & e^{3t}\end{bmatrix}$
Since the initial conditions are given at $t = t_0 = 0$, we have
$e^{A(t - t_{0})}x(0)|= e^{At}x(0); = \begin{bmatrix}e^{3t} & te^{3t} \\ 0 & e^{3t}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}0 \\ 0\end{bmatrix}; = \begin{bmatrix}0 \\ 0\end{bmatrix}$.
- Next, we find the particular solution. Notice that
$\begin{bmatrix}e^{3(t - s)} & (t - s)e^{3(t - s)} \\ 0 & e^{3(t - s)}\end{bmatrix}$
So, we have
$e^{A(t - s)}g(s)|= \begin{bmatrix}e^{3(t - s)} & (t - s)e^{3(t - s)} \\ 0 & e^{3(t - s)}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 0\end{bmatrix}; = \begin{bmatrix}e^{3(t - s)} \\ 0\end{bmatrix}; = \begin{bmatrix}e^{-3s + 3t} \\ 0\end{bmatrix}$.
Thus, we obtain
$∫_{0}^{t}e^{A(t - s)}g(s)ds|= ∫_{0}^{t}\begin{bmatrix}e^{-3s + 3t} \\ 0\end{bmatrix}ds; = \begin{bmatrix}-\frac{1}{3}e^{-3s + 3t} \\ 0\end{bmatrix}_{s = 0}^{s = t}; = \begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{3}e^{3t} - \frac{1}{3} \\ 0\end{bmatrix}$.

Finally, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
x(t) &= \begin{bmatrix}0 \\ 0\end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{3}e^{3t} - \frac{1}{3} \\ 0\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{3}e^{3t} - \frac{1}{3} \\ 0\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \frac{1}{3}\begin{bmatrix}e^{3t} - 1 \\ 0\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the solution to the initial value problem is
$\begin{bmatrix}x(t) \\ y(t)\end{bmatrix}$

## Proving the Matrix Exponential Formula for IVP Solution of an Inhomogeneous System

**Example:** Consider the inhomogeneous system $\mathbf{x}'(t)=A\mathbf{x}(t)+\mathbf{g}(t)$, where $A$ is a constant square matrix. A derivation of the formula for the solution $\mathbf{x}(t)$ passing through $\mathbf{x}(t_0)$ is presented below.

> $\textrm{L1}{:}\;$ Let $\mathbf{y}(t)=e^{-A(t-t_0)}\mathbf{x}(t)$
> $\textrm{L2}{:}\;$ $\mathbf{y}'(t) = -Ae^{-A(t-t_0)}\mathbf{x}(t) + e^{-A(t-t_0)}\mathbf{x}'(t)$
> $\textrm{L3}{:}\;$ $\mathbf{y}'(t)=e^{-A(t-t_0)}\mathbf{g}(t)$
> $\textrm{L4}{:}\;$ $\displaystyle\mathbf{y}(t)-\mathbf{y}(t_0) = \int_{t_0}^{t} e^{-A(s-t_0)}\mathbf{g}(s) \, \text{d}s$
> $\textrm{L5}{:}\;$ $\mathbf{y}(t_0)=\mathbf{x}(t_0)$
> $\textrm{L6}{:}\;$ $\displaystyle\mathbf{y}(t)=\mathbf{x}(t_0)+\int_{t_0}^{t} e^{-A(s-t_0)}\mathbf{g}(s) \, \text{d}s$
> $\textrm{L7}{:}\;$ $\displaystyle e^{A(t-t_0)}\mathbf{y}(t)=e^{A(t-t_0)}\mathbf{x}(t_0)+\int_{t_0}^{t} e^{A(t-s)}\mathbf{g}(s) \, \text{d}s$
> $\textrm{L8}{:}\;$ $e^{A(t-t_0)}\mathbf{y}(t)=\mathbf{x}(t)$
> $\textrm{L9}{:}\;$ $\displaystyle\mathbf{x}(t)=e^{A(t-t_0)}\mathbf{x}(t_0)+\int_{t_0}^{t} e^{A(t-s)}\mathbf{g}(s) \, \text{d}s$

Fill in the blanks with the correct reasons to justify each step of the reasoning.

$\quad$ Line $\textrm{L3}$ follows from $\textrm{L2}$ by $AAAAAAAAAAAAAAA$.

$\quad$ Line $\textrm{L5}$ follows from line $\textrm{L1}$ by $AAAAAAAAAAAAAAA$.

$\quad$ Line $\textrm{L8}$ follows from line $\textrm{L1}$ by $AAAAAAAAAAAAAAA$.

**Explanation**

Let's justify each of the selected steps.

- We first consider lines $\textrm{L2}$ and $\textrm{L3}$. Starting from
$\mathbf{y}'(t) = -Ae^{-A(t-t_0)}\mathbf{x}(t) + e^{-A(t-t_0)}\mathbf{x}'(t)$
in $\textrm{L2}$ and substituting $\mathbf{x}'(t)=A\mathbf{x}(t)+\mathbf{g}(t)$ from the original system gives
$y^{′}(t)|=-Ae^{-A(t - t_{0})}x(t) + e^{-A(t - t_{0})}(Ax(t) + g(t)); =-Ae^{-A(t - t_{0})}x(t) + Ae^{-A(t - t_{0})}x(t) + e^{-A(t - t_{0})}g(t); = e^{-A(t - t_{0})}g(t)$.
Thus, $\textrm{L3}$ follows from $\textrm{L2}$ by $x^{′}(t) = Ax(t) + g(t)$
- Next, we consider lines $\textrm{L1}$ and $\textrm{L5}$. Evaluating $\mathbf{y}(t)=e^{-A(t-t_0)}\mathbf{x}(t)$ at $t=t_0$ gives
$\mathbf{y}(t_0)=e^{-A(t_0-t_0)}\mathbf{x}(t_0)=I\mathbf{x}(t_0)=\mathbf{x}(t_0)$.
Therefore, $\textrm{L5}$ follows from line $\textrm{L1}$ by $t = t_{0}$
- Finally, we consider lines $\textrm{L1}$ and $\textrm{L8}$. Multiplying both sides of
$\mathbf{y}(t)=e^{-A(t-t_0)}\mathbf{x}(t)$
from $\textrm{L1}$ by $e^{A(t-t_0)}$ gives
$e^{A(t - t_{0})}y(t)|= e^{A(t - t_{0})}e^{-A(t - t_{0})}x(t); = x(t)$.
Therefore, $\textrm{L8}$ follows from $\textrm{L1}$ by $e^{A(t - t_{0})}$
