## Introduction

Consider a plane lamina that occupies the region $D \subseteq \mathbb{R}^2$ of mass $M$ where the center of mass is at

$$
\left(\bar{x},\bar{y}\right)
$$

![](<../Source/7.3.9. The Parallel Axis Theorem/Images/e6d0f787d87ad36b4fe86adb9374f8c5.png>)

We know that the lamina has some moment of inertia $I_M$ about the line $l_M$ that passes through the center of mass and is perpendicular to the lamina.

![](<../Source/7.3.9. The Parallel Axis Theorem/Images/4932ea929465c809ec3e54668d196905.png>)

Now, if $l$ is a line parallel to $l_M$, then the lamina also has moment of inertia about $l$, say $I_l$.

![](<../Source/7.3.9. The Parallel Axis Theorem/Images/9894693079df419a00fa75b3a7ce48da.png>)

Given the value of $I_M$, we can find $I_l$ using the **parallel axis theorem**, which states that

$$
I_l = I_M + d^2M
$$

where $d$ is the distance between the lines.

The term $d^2M$ is the moment of inertia about $l$ of the lamina when its entire mass is concentrated at the center of mass.

![](<../Source/7.3.9. The Parallel Axis Theorem/Images/586d448bbcaa36ad577144dd8f7b6a53.png>)

So, our interpretation of the parallel axis theorem is the following:

$$
\underbrace{\text{moment of inertia of}D \ \text{about a parallel line}l}_{I_l} \:=\: \underbrace{\text{moment of inertia of}D \ \text{about the center of mass}}_{I_M} \:+\: \underbrace{\text{moment of inertia of point mass}M \ \text{about the parallel line} l}_{d^2M}
$$

Finally, since $d^2M$ is always positive, we have

$$
I_M \leq I_l
$$

for any line $l$ perpendicular to the lamina. In other words, the moment of inertia about the line through its center of mass is the *smallest* moment of inertia about any line in that direction.

<a id="calculating-a-moment-of-inertia-about-a-perpendicular-axis"></a>
## Calculating a Moment of Inertia About a Perpendicular Axis

**Example:** Consider a plane lamina of mass $2\,\textrm{kg}$ and center of mass $(0,1)$, where $x$ and $y$ are measured in meters. If the moment of inertia of the lamina about an axis perpendicular to the lamina passing through its center of mass is $2.5\,\textrm{kg}\cdot\textrm{m}^2$, what is the moment of inertia of the lamina about an axis parallel to the first passing through the point $(2,1)$?

**Explanation**

For a plane lamina of mass $M$ and a line $l_M$ perpendicular to the lamina passing through its center of mass, the parallel axis theorem states that the moment of inertia of the lamina about a line $l$ parallel to $l_M$ is given by

$$
I_l = I_M + d^2M
$$

where $d$ is the distance between the lines, and $I_M$ and $I_l$ are the moments of inertia about the lines $l_M$ and $l$, respectively.

The distance between the line through the center of mass $(0,1)$ and the line through the point $(2,1)$ is

$$
\begin{aligned}
d &= \sqrt{(2 - 0)^{2} + (1 - 1)^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{4 + 0} \\
&= \sqrt{4} \\
&= 2
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, applying the parallel axis theorem, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
I_{l} &= 2.5 + 2^{2} \cdot 2 \\
&= 2.5 + 8 \\
&= 10.5
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the moment of inertia is $10.5\,\mathrm{kg}\cdot\mathrm{m^2}$.

<a id="a-formula-for-the-moment-of-inertia-about-the-line-through-the-center-of-mass"></a>
## A Formula for the Moment of Inertia About the Line Through the Center of Mass

In a previous lesson, we saw that the moment of inertia about an axis $l$ of a plane lamina occupying the region $D$ with mass density function $\lambda(x,y)$ is given by

$$
I_l = \iint\limits_D [r(x,y)]^2\,\lambda(x,y) \:\textrm{d}A
$$

Therefore, the moment of inertia of $D$ about the line through the center of mass

$$
\left(\bar{x}, \bar{y}\right)
$$

and perpendicular to the lamina is given by

$$
I_M = \iint\limits_D \left((x-\bar{x})^2+(y-\bar{y})^2\right) \lambda(x,y) \:\textrm{d}A
$$

<a id="calculating-a-moment-of-inertia-of-a-rectangular-plate-about-a-perpendicular-axis"></a>
## Calculating a Moment of Inertia of a Rectangular Plate About a Perpendicular Axis

**Example:** A rectangular plate of mass $3\,\textrm{kg}$ and uniform mass density occupies the region
$D = \big\{(x,y) \:: \: 0 \le x \le 2, \: 0 \le y \le 4 \big\}$,
where $x$ and $y$ are measured in meters.
Find the moment of inertia of the plate about a line perpendicular to $D$ at a distance of $1\,\textrm{m}$ from a parallel line passing through its center of mass.

**Explanation**

For a plane lamina of mass $M$ and a line $l_M$ perpendicular to the lamina passing through its center of mass, the parallel axis theorem states that the moment of inertia of the lamina about a line $l$ parallel to $l_M$ is given by

$$
I_l = I_M + d^2M
$$

where $d$ is the distance between the lines, and $I_M$ and $I_l$ are the moments of inertia about the lines $l_M$ and $l$, respectively.

The area of the rectangular plate $D$ is

$$
\mathcal{A} = 2 \cdot 4 = 8\,\textrm{m}^2
$$

Since the plate has a uniform density, the mass density function is given by

$$
\lambda(x,y) = \dfrac{M}{\mathcal{A}} = \dfrac{3}8\,\textrm{kg}/\textrm{m}^2
$$

and its center of mass is its centroid:

$$
\left(\bar{x},\bar{y}\right) = \left(\dfrac{0+2}2, \dfrac{0+4}2 \right) = (1,2)
$$

Then, the moment of inertia about the perpendicular line through the center of mass is

$$
\begin{aligned}
I_{M} &= ∬_(D)((x - x^(¯))^{2} + (y - y^(¯))^{2})λ(x, y)dA \\
&= ∫_{0}^{2}∫_{0}^{4}\frac{3}{8}((x - 1)^{2} + (y - 2)^{2})dydx \\
&= \frac{1}{8}∫_{0}^{2}∫_{0}^{4}3(x - 1)^{2} + 3(y - 2)^{2}dydx \\
&= \frac{1}{8}∫_{0}^{2}[3(x - 1)^{2}y + (y - 2)^{3}]_{y = 0}^{y = 4}dx \\
&= \frac{1}{8}∫_{0}^{2}12(x - 1)^{2} + 16dx \\
&= \frac{1}{8}[4(x - 1)^{3} + 16x]_{0}^{2} \\
&= \frac{1}{8}(4 + 32 - (-4 + 0)) \\
&= 5kg \cdot m^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, applying the parallel axis theorem, the moment of inertia about a line $1\,\textrm{m}$ from the center of mass is

$$
\begin{aligned}
I &= I_{M} + d^{2}M \\
&= 5 + 1^{2} \cdot 3 \\
&= 5 + 3 \\
&= 8kg \cdot m^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="calculating-a-moment-of-inertia-about-a-perpendicular-axis-using-polar-coordinates"></a>
## Calculating a Moment of Inertia About a Perpendicular Axis Using Polar Coordinates

**Example:** Calculate the moment of inertia of a circular plate that occupies the region of a disk $D$ of radius $1\,\textrm{m}$ with mass density $\lambda(x, y) =2(x^2+y^2)$, measured in $\textrm{kg}/\textrm{m}^2$, about a line perpendicular to the disk passing through a point on the circumference.

**Explanation**

For a plane lamina of mass $M$ and a line $l_M$ perpendicular to the lamina passing through its center of mass, the parallel axis theorem states that the moment of inertia of the lamina about a line $l$ parallel to $l_M$ is given by

$$
I_l = I_M + d^2M
$$

where $d$ is the distance between the lines, and $I_M$ and $I_l$ are the moments of inertia about the lines $l_M$ and $l$, respectively.

First, notice that the region $D$ can be expressed in polar coordinates as

$$
D = \big\{(r,\theta) \:: \: 0 \leq r \leq 1, \:\: 0 \leq \theta \lt 2\pi \big\}
$$

Since the mass density is symmetric in $x$ and $y$, $D$'s center of mass is at $(0,0)$. The mass density function, in polar coordinates, is given by

$$
\lambda(r,\theta) = 2r^2
$$

Next, we find the mass of the plate as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
M &= ∬_(D)λ(r, θ) \cdot rdrdθ \\
&= ∫_{0}^{2π}∫_{0}^{1}2r^{2} \cdot rdrdθ \\
&= ∫_{0}^{1}2r^{3}dr \cdot ∫_{0}^{2π}dθ \\
&= \frac{1}{2}r^{4} \mid _{0}^{1} \cdot θ \mid _{0}^{2π} \\
&= \frac{1}{2} \cdot 2π \\
&= πkg
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, the moment of inertia about the perpendicular line through the center of mass is

$$
\begin{aligned}
I_{M} &= ∬_(D)λ(r, θ) \cdot r^{3}drdθ \\
&= ∫_{0}^{2π}∫_{0}^{1}2r^{2} \cdot r^{3}drdθ \\
&= ∫_{0}^{1}2r^{5}dr \cdot ∫_{0}^{2π}dθ \\
&= \frac{1}{3}r^{6} \mid _{0}^{1} \cdot θ \mid _{0}^{2π} \\
&= \frac{1}{3} \cdot 2π \\
&= \frac{2π}{3}kg \cdot m^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, applying the parallel axis theorem, the moment of inertia about a line passing through a point on the circumference is

$$
\begin{aligned}
I &= I_{M} + d^{2}M \\
&= \frac{2π}{3} + 1^{2} \cdot π \\
&= \frac{2π}{3} + π \\
&= \frac{5π}{3}kg \cdot m^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="finding-the-distance-between-two-parallel-axes"></a>
## Finding the Distance Between Two Parallel Axes

**Example:** A circular plate of mass $2 \, \textrm{kg}$ with uniform mass density occupies the region of a disk $D$ of radius $4 \, \textrm{m}$ and is centered at the origin.
Given that the moment of inertia of the plate about a line $l$ perpendicular to the plate is $24 \, \textrm{kg} \cdot \textrm{m}^2$, find the distance of the line $l$ from the center of mass of $D$.

**Explanation**

For a plane lamina of mass $M$ and a line $l_M$ perpendicular to the lamina passing through its center of mass, the parallel axis theorem states that the moment of inertia of the lamina about a line $l$ parallel to $l_M$ is given by

$$
I_l = I_M + d^2M
$$

where $d$ is the distance between the lines, and $I_M$ and $I_l$ are the moments of inertia about the lines $l_M$ and $l$, respectively.

First, notice that the region $D$ can be expressed in polar coordinates as

$$
D = \big\{(r,\theta) \:: \: 0 \leq r \leq 4, \: 0 \leq \theta \lt 2\pi \big\}
$$

The area of the circular plate $D$ is

$$
\mathcal{A} = \pi \cdot 4^2 = 16\pi\,\textrm{m}^2
$$

Since the plate has a uniform density, the mass density function is given by

$$
\lambda(r,\theta) = \dfrac{M}{\mathcal{A}} = \dfrac{2}{16\pi} = \dfrac{1}{8\pi}\,\textrm{kg}/\textrm{m}^2
$$

and its center of mass is its centroid $(0,0)$.

Then, the moment of inertia about the perpendicular line through the center of mass is

$$
\begin{aligned}
I_{M} &= ∬_(D)λ(r, θ) \cdot r^{3}drdθ \\
&= \frac{1}{8π}∫_{0}^{4}r^{3}dr∫_{0}^{2π}dθ \\
&= \frac{1}{8π} \cdot \frac{1}{4}r^{4} \mid _{0}^{4} \cdot θ \mid _{0}^{2π} \\
&= \frac{1}{8π} \cdot 64 \cdot 2π \\
&= 16kg \cdot m^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, applying the parallel axis theorem and solving for $d$, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
I &= I_{M} + d^{2}M \\
24 &= 16 + 2d^{2} \\
2d^{2} &= 8 \\
d^{2} &= 4 \\
d &= 2m
\end{aligned}
$$
