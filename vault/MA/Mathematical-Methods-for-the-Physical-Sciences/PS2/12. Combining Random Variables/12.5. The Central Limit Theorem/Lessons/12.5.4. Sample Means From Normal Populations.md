## Introduction

Let $X_1, X_2, \ldots, X_{n}$ be a random sample of size $n$ drawn from a normal population $N(\mu,\sigma^2)$ with mean $\mu$ and variance $\sigma^2$. In other words,

$$
X_1, X_2, \ldots, X_{n} \sim N(\mu,\sigma^2)
$$

In this case, we can easily determine the sampling distribution of the sample mean

$$
\overline{X} = \dfrac{1}{n} \sum\limits_{i=1}^n X_i
$$

Recall the following:

- The sum of $n$ independent and identically distributed normal random variables is normally distributed. Thus,
$Y = \displaystyle \sum\limits_{i=1}^n X_i$
is normally distributed.
- A normally distributed random variable scaled by a constant factor is normally distributed. Thus,
$\overline{X} = \dfrac1n\cdot Y$
is normally distributed.

Also, we previously saw that the expected value of the sample mean equals the population mean, i.e.,

$$
\textrm{E}[\overline{X}] = \mu
$$

and the variance of the sample mean is

$$
\textrm{Var}[\overline{X}] = \dfrac{\sigma^2}{n}
$$

Putting all of this together, we obtain that

$$
\overline{X} \sim N\left(\mu, \dfrac{\sigma^2}{n}\right)
$$

In other words, the sample mean $\overline{X}$ is normally distributed with mean $\mu$ and variance

$$
\dfrac{\sigma^2}{n}
$$

## Stating the Distribution of the Sample Mean

**Example:** Given that $X_1, X_2, \ldots, X_{28} \sim N(8, 14^2)$ is a random sample of size $n = 28$ from a normal population, what is the distribution of the sample mean $\overline{X}$?

**Explanation**

If $X_1, X_2, \ldots, X_n$ is a random sample of size $n$ from a normal population, where

$$
X_i \sim N(\mu, \sigma^2), \qquad 1\leq i \leq n
$$

then the sample mean $\overline{X}$ has the distribution

$$
\overline{X} \sim N\left(\mu, \dfrac{\sigma^2}{n}\right)
$$

In our case, we have

$$
n = 28, \qquad \mu = 8, \qquad \sigma = 14
$$

Therefore, the distribution of the sample mean $\overline{X}$ is

$$
X^(―) \mid ∼N(8, \frac{14^{2}}{28}); ∼N(8, 7)
$$

## Calculating a Probability Involving a Sample Mean

**Example:** Given that $X_1, X_2, \ldots,X_{100} \sim N(-4, 20^2)$ is a random sample of size $100$ from a normal population, calculate $P(-4.3 \lt \overline{X} \lt -3.9)$.

*Hint: A value table of $\Phi(z)$ for different values of $z$ is given below, where $\Phi(z)$ is the cumulative distribution function for the standard normal distribution.*

| $z$ | $\, 0.04 \,$ | $\, 0.05 \,$ | $\, 0.06 \,$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $\, -0.1 \,$ | $\, 0.4443 \,$ | $\, 0.4404 \,$ | $\, 0.4364 \,$ |
| $\, 0.0 \,$ | $\, 0.5160 \,$ | $\, 0.5199 \,$ | $\, 0.5239 \,$ |
| $\, 0.1 \,$ | $\, 0.5557 \,$ | $\, 0.5596 \,$ | $\, 0.5636 \,$ |

**Explanation**

If $X_1, X_2, \ldots, X_n$ is a random sample of size $n$ from a normal population, where

$$
X_i \sim N(\mu, \sigma^2), \qquad 1\leq i\leq n
$$

then the sample mean $\overline{X}$ has the distribution

$$
\overline{X} \sim N\left(\mu, \dfrac{\sigma^2}{n}\right)
$$

Therefore, the distribution of the sample mean $\overline{X}$ is

$$
X^(―) \mid ∼N(-4, \frac{20^{2}}{100}); ∼N(-4, 4)
$$

We're required to find $P(-4.3 \lt \overline{X} \lt -3.9)$.

First, we transform $X$ into a standard normal random variable $Z$ by $z$-scoring:

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(-4.3 < X^(―) < - 3.9) &= P((-4.3 - (-4))/(\sqrt{4}) < Z < (-3.9 - (-4))/(\sqrt{4})) \\
&= P(-0.15 < Z < 0.05) \\
&= Φ(0.05) - Φ(-0.15)
\end{aligned}
$$

From the table, we have

$$
Φ(0.05) = 0.5199,; Φ(-0.15) = 0.4404
$$

Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(-0.15 < Z < 0.05) &= Φ(0.05) - Φ(-0.15) \\
&= 0.5199 - 0.4404 \\
&= 0.0795
\end{aligned}
$$

## Calculating a Probability Involving a Sample Mean in Context

**Example:** The diastolic blood pressure of adult men in a given population is normally distributed with a mean of $75\,\text{mm}\,\text{Hg}$ and a standard deviation of $10\,\text{mm}\,\text{Hg}$. Suppose $10$ men are selected at random. What is the probability that the mean diastolic blood pressure for this sample will be less than $70\,\text{mm}\,\text{Hg}$?

*Hint: A table of values of $\Phi(z)$ for different values of $z$ is given below, where $\Phi(z)$ is the cumulative distribution function for the standard normal distribution.*

| $z$ | $\, 0.05 \,$ | $\, 0.06 \,$ | $\, 0.07 \,$ | $\, 0.08 \,$ | $\, 0.09 \,$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $\, -1.5 \,$ | $\, 0.0606 \,$ | $\, 0.0594 \,$ | $\, 0.0582 \,$ | $\, 0.0571 \,$ | $\, 0.0559 \,$ |
| $\, -1.4 \,$ | $\, 0.0735 \,$ | $\, 0.0721 \,$ | $\, 0.0708 \,$ | $\, 0.0694 \,$ | $\, 0.0681 \,$ |
| $\, -1.3 \,$ | $\, 0.0885 \,$ | $\, 0.0869 \,$ | $\, 0.0853 \,$ | $\, 0.0838 \,$ | $\, 0.0823 \,$ |

**Explanation**

If $X_1,X_2,\ldots,X_n$ is a random sample of size $n$ from a normal population, where

$$
X_i\sim N(\mu, \sigma^2), \qquad 1\leq i\leq n
$$

then the sample mean $\overline{X}$ has the distribution

$$
\overline{X}\sim N\left(\mu, \dfrac{\sigma^2}{n}\right)
$$

We're told that the population mean $\mu=75$, the population standard deviation

$$
\sigma = 10
$$

and that the sample size $n=10$. Therefore, the distribution of the sample mean $\overline{X}$ is

$$
X^(―) \mid ∼N(75, \frac{10^{2}}{10}); ∼N(75, 10)
$$

We're required to find $P(\overline{X} \lt 70)$.

First, we transform $X$ into a standard normal random variable $Z$ by $z$-scoring:

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(X^(―) < 70) &= P(Z < \frac{70 - 75}{\sqrt{10}}) \\
&\approx P(Z < - 1.58) \\
&= Φ(-1.58)
\end{aligned}
$$

From the table, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
Φ(-1.58) &= 0.0571
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(X^(―) < 70) &= Φ(-1.58) \\
&= 0.0571
\end{aligned}
$$
