## Introduction

The **joint cumulative distribution function** (or joint CDF) of two continuous random variables $X$ and $Y$ is defined as

$$
F(x,y)=P(X \leq x,Y \leq y)
$$

The joint CDF gives the probability that the pair $(X,Y)$ will lie inside the infinite rectangular region $\Delta$ that has its top-right corner at $(x,y)$ and whose sides are parallel to the coordinate axes.

![](<../Source/12.1.5. The Joint CDF of Two Continuous Random Variables/Images/9057261d5db239ab829abf39644026ca.png>)

The joint CDF satisfies the following properties:

- $0\leq F(x,y)\leq 1$
- The **marginal CDF** of $X$ is given by $F_X(x)=F(x,\infty)$.
- The **marginal CDF** of $Y$ is given by $F_Y(y)=F(\infty,y)$.
- $F(\infty, \infty)=1$
- $F(-\infty,y)=F(x,-\infty)=0$
- If $X$ and $Y$ are independent random variables, then
$F(x,y)=F_X(x)\cdot F_Y(y)$.

If $X$ and $Y$ are continuous random variables with joint probability density function $f(x, y)$, we have the following relationship between the joint PDF and joint CDF:

$$
F(x,y)=\int^y_{-\infty}\int^x_{-\infty} f(u,v)\; \textrm{d}u\textrm{d}v
$$

Consequently, if $X$ and $Y$ are continuous random variables that are nonzero only over the rectangle $[a, b] \times [c, d]$, then the joint CDF is

$$
F(x,y)=\int^d_{c}\int^b_{a} f(u,v)\; \textrm{d}u\textrm{d}v
$$

## Computing the Value of the Joint CDF at a Point

**Example:** Let $X$ and $Y$ be continuous random variables with the joint probability density function
${\frac{3}{5}\sqrt{x}y,\begin{vmatrix}(x, y) ∈ D, \\ 0,\end{vmatrix}otherwise$.

![](<../Source/12.1.5. The Joint CDF of Two Continuous Random Variables/Images/aeefb8448758b5edfae41a81ae3dd48a.png>)

If $F(x,y)$ is the joint CDF of $X$ and $Y$, find $F\left(2, \dfrac{5}{2}\right)$.

**Explanation**

If $X$ and $Y$ are continuous random variables with the joint probability density function $f(x,y)$, then the corresponding CDF is given by

$$
F(x,y) = P(X \leq x, Y \leq y) = \int_{-\infty}^y \int_{-\infty}^x f(u,v)\; \textrm{d}u \, \textrm{d}v
$$

Let's draw the point

$$
\left(2, \dfrac{5}{2}\right)
$$

on our diagram. Let's also consider the region $\Delta$ that lies below and to the left of this point.

![](<../Source/12.1.5. The Joint CDF of Two Continuous Random Variables/Images/21dba4db0892a770ef4062c9ae8d334d.png>)

Using the definition of the joint CDF, we have

$$
F\left(2, \dfrac{5}{2}\right) = P\left(X< 2, Y< \dfrac{5}{2}\right) = \int_{-\infty}^{5/2} \int_{-\infty}^{2} f(u,v)\; \textrm{d}u \, \textrm{d}v
$$

For the purposes of computation, we're only interested in the region inside $\Delta$ where $f(x,y)$ is nonzero. This is given by

$$
D \cap \Delta =\left\{(x,y) \:: \: 0 \leq x \leq 1, \: 2 \leq y \leq \dfrac{5}{2} \right\}
$$

Therefore, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
∫_{- ∞}^{5/2}∫_{- ∞}^{2}f(u, v)dudv &= ∫_{2}^{5/2}∫_{0}^{1}\frac{3}{5}\sqrt{u}vdudv \\
&= ∫_{2}^{5/2}∫_{0}^{1}\frac{3}{5}u^{1/2}vdudv \\
&= ∫_{2}^{5/2}[\frac{3}{5} \cdot \frac{2}{3}u^{3/2}v]_{u = 0}^{u = 1}dv \\
&= ∫_{2}^{5/2}[\frac{2}{5}u^{3/2}v]_{u = 0}^{u = 1}dv \\
&= ∫_{2}^{5/2}\frac{2}{5}vdv \\
&= [\frac{v^{2}}{5}]_{2}^{5/2} \\
&= \frac{5}{4} - \frac{4}{5} \\
&= \frac{9}{20}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding a Probability Geometrically Using a CDF

**Example:** Let the joint CDF of $X$ and $Y$ be $F(x, y)$, where
$F(5,5) = 0.9, \quad F(5,2) = 0.3, \quad F(0,5) = 0.45, \quad F(0,2) = 0.15$.
Find $P(0 \leq X \leq 5, 2 \leq Y \leq 5)$.

**Explanation**

Recall that

$$
F(x,y) = P(X \leq x, Y \leq y)
$$

is the probability that the pair $(X,Y)$ will lie inside the infinite rectangular region that has its top-right corner at $(x,y)$ and whose sides are parallel to the coordinate axes.

Let's sketch the region corresponding to the probability we wish to find.

![](<../Source/12.1.5. The Joint CDF of Two Continuous Random Variables/Images/4236e8be079ab8b8262d34d90fede47a.png>)

Geometrically, this region can be obtained as follows:

1. Take the infinite rectangle that has its top-right corner at $(5,5)$.
2. Subtract the infinite rectangles that have their top-right corners at $\color{red}(5,2)$ and ${\color{red}{(0,5)}}$.
3. Finally, we need to *add back* the infinite rectangle that has its top-right corner at ${\color{blue}{(0,2)}}$. This is because we subtracted this region twice in step 2.

Therefore, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(0 \le X \le 5, 2 \le Y \le 5) &= P(X \le 5, Y \le 5)^(⏞)^(top-right corner at [math](5,5)[/math]) \\
&=-P(X \le 5, Y \le 2)^(⏞)^(top-right corner at [math](5,2)[/math]) - P(X \le 0, Y \le 5)^(⏞)^(top-right corner at [math](0,5)[/math]) \\
&= + P(X \le 0, Y \le 2)^(⏞)^(top-right corner at [math](0,2)[/math]) \\
&= F(5, 5) - F(5, 2) - F(0, 5) + F(0, 2) \\
&= 0.9 - 0.3 - 0.45 + 0.15 \\
&= 0.3
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding Part of a Joint CDF from a Joint PDF: Simple Cases

**Example:** Let $X$ and $Y$ be continuous random variables with the joint probability density function
${\frac{1}{4}xy,\begin{vmatrix}0 \le x \le 1, 0 \le y \le 4, \\ 0,\end{vmatrix}otherwise$.
Find the expression for the corresponding joint CDF when $x > 1,\; y > 4$.

**Explanation**

If $X$ and $Y$ are continuous random variables with the joint probability density function $f(x,y)$, then the corresponding joint CDF is given by

$$
F(x,y) = P(X \leq x, Y \leq y) = \int_{-\infty}^y \int_{-\infty}^x f(u,v)\; \textrm{d}u \, \textrm{d}v
$$

Let's draw the region $D$ in the $uv$-plane where the function $f(u,v)$ is nonzero. Let's also consider a point $(x,y)$ where $x > 1,\; y >4$, and a region $\Delta$ that lies below and to the left of this point.

![](<../Source/12.1.5. The Joint CDF of Two Continuous Random Variables/Images/b1e32b1786fec6ac4886c2c00a891771.png>)

For the purposes of computation, we're only interested in the region inside $\Delta$ where $f(x,y)$ is nonzero. This is given by

$$
D \cap \Delta = D
$$

So our region $\Delta$ covers the entire region where $f(x,y)$ is nonzero. Therefore, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
∫_{- ∞}^{y}∫_{- ∞}^{x}f(u, v)dudv &= ∬_{D}f(u, v)dudv = 1
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding Part of a Joint CDF from a Joint PDF

**Example:** Let $X$ and $Y$ be continuous random variables with the joint probability density function
${\frac{1}{3}(x + y),\begin{vmatrix}0 \le x \le 2, 0 \le y \le 1 \\ 0,\end{vmatrix}otherwise$
Find the expression for the corresponding joint CDF when $0 \leq x \leq 2$ and $y\gt 1$.

**Explanation**

If $X$ and $Y$ are continuous random variables with the joint probability density function $f(x,y)$, then the corresponding joint CDF is given by

$$
F(x,y) = P(X \leq x, Y \leq y) = \int_{-\infty}^y \int_{-\infty}^x f(u,v)\; \textrm{d}u \, \textrm{d}v
$$

Let's draw the region $D$ in the $uv$-plane where the function $f(u,v)$ is nonzero. Let's also consider a point $(x,y)$ where

$$
0 \leq x \leq 2,\; y \gt 1
$$

and a region $\Delta$ that lies below and to the left of this point.

![](<../Source/12.1.5. The Joint CDF of Two Continuous Random Variables/Images/0ab35b5d1033c751729be7e5f4662d5c.png>)

For the purposes of computation, we're only interested in the region inside $\Delta$ where $f(x,y)$ is nonzero. This is given by

$$
D \cap \Delta =\left\{(u,v) \:: \: 0 \leq u \leq x, \: 0 \leq v \leq 1 \right\}
$$

Therefore, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
∫_{- ∞}^{y}∫_{- ∞}^{x}f(u, v)dudv &= ∫_{0}^{1}∫_{0}^{x}\frac{1}{3}(u + v)dudv \\
&= \frac{1}{3}∫_{0}^{1}[\frac{u^{2}}{2} + uv]_{u = 0}^{u = x}dv \\
&= \frac{1}{3}∫_{0}^{1}[\frac{x^{2}}{2} + xv]dv \\
&= \frac{1}{3}[\frac{x^{2}v}{2} + \frac{xv^{2}}{2}]_{v = 0}^{v = 1} \\
&= \frac{1}{3}[\frac{x^{2}}{2} + \frac{x}{2} - 0] \\
&= \frac{1}{6}(x^{2} + x)
\end{aligned}
$$
