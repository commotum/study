## Introduction

Suppose we want to find the *maximum* value of the quadratic form

$$
Q(\mathbf{x})=2x_1^2 + 4x_2^2
$$

subject to the constraint

$$
\| \mathbf{x} \| = 1
$$

This type of problem is known as a **constrained optimization problem**.

Since our quadratic form $Q(\mathbf{x})$ has no mixed term $x_1x_2$, we can find its maximum value for

$$
\| \mathbf{x} \mid \mid = 1
$$

using algebraic manipulation, as follows.

First, notice that

$$
x_1^2 \geq 0
$$

So, we have

$$
2x_1^2 \leq 4x_1^2
$$

As a result,

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q(x) &= 2x_{1}^{2} + 4x_{2}^{2} \\
&\le 4x_{1}^{2} + 4x_{2}^{2} \\
&= 4(x_{1}^{2} + x_{2}^{2})
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, recall that

$$
\| \mathbf{x} \| = 1
$$

is equivalent to

$$
x_1^2 + x_2^2 =1
$$

So,

$$
4(x_{1}^{2} + x_{2}^{2}) = 4(1) = 4
$$

which means that

$$
Q(\mathbf{x}) \leq 4
$$

So, the value of $Q(\mathbf{x})$ can be no greater than $4$. But before we conclude that $4$ is the maximum, we need to make sure that $Q(\mathbf{x})$ can actually attain the value $4$. Can we find a vector $\mathbf x$, with

$$
\| \mathbf{x} \mid \mid = 1
$$

such that

$$
Q(\mathbf{x}) = 4
$$

Indeed, we can. Setting $\begin{bmatrix}0 & 1\end{bmatrix}$ we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q(x) &= 2x_{1}^{2} + 4x_{2}^{2} \\
&= 2(0)^{2} + 4(1)^{2} \\
&= 4
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, if

$$
\| \mathbf{x} \| = 1
$$

the maximum value of $Q(\mathbf{x})$ equals $4$.


Proceeding similarly, we can show that the minimum of $Q(\mathbf{x})$ is equal to $2$.

Graphically, the situation can be visualized if we intersect the surface

$$
z= {\color{black}2x_1^2+4x_2^2}
$$

and the cylinder

$$
\color{black}x_1^2+x_2^2=1
$$

as shown below:

![](<../Source/2.2.5. Constrained Optimization of Quadratic Forms/Images/3fc8c6c881cf31d31f1762860be30ec9.png>)

## Finding the Maximum Value Given a Canonical Form on the Unit Sphere

**Example:** Find the maximum value of $Q(\mathbf{x})=2x_1^2 - 5x_2^2 +3x_3^2$ given that $\| \mathbf{x} \| =1$.

**Explanation**

First, notice that

$$
x_1^2 \geq 0
$$

and

$$
x_2^2 \geq 0
$$

So, we have that

$$
2x_1^2 \leq 3x_1^2, \qquad -5x_2^2 \leq 3x_2^2
$$

As a result,

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q(x) &= 2x_{1}^{2} - 5x_{2}^{2} + 3x_{3}^{2} \\
&\le 3x_{1}^{2} + 3x_{2}^{2} + 3x_{3}^{2} \\
&= 3(x_{1}^{2} + x_{2}^{2} + x_{3}^{2})
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, recall that

$$
\| \mathbf{x} \| = 1
$$

is equivalent to

$$
x_1^2 + x_2^2 + x_3^2 =1
$$

So,

$$
3(x_{1}^{2} + x_{2}^{2} + x_{3}^{2}) = 3(1) = 3
$$

and, in turn,

$$
Q(\mathbf{x}) \leq 3
$$

So, the value of $Q(\mathbf{x})$ can be no greater than $3$. But before we conclude that $3$ is the maximum, we need to find a vector such that

$$
Q(\mathbf{x}) = 3
$$

Notice that when $\begin{bmatrix}0 & 0 & 1\end{bmatrix}$ we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q(x) &= 2x_{1}^{2} - 5x_{2}^{2} + 3x_{3}^{2} \\
&= 2(0)^{2} - 5(0)^{2} + 3(1)^{2} \\
&= 3
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, given that

$$
\| \mathbf{x} \| =1
$$

the maximum value of $Q(\mathbf{x})$ is equal to $3$.

## Finding the Minimum Value Given a Canonical Form on the Unit Sphere

**Example:** Find the minimum value of $Q(\mathbf{x})=5x_1^2 - 4x_2^2 - 6x_3^2$ given that $\| \mathbf{x} \| =1$.

**Explanation**

First, notice that

$$
x_1^2 \geq 0
$$

and

$$
x_2^2 \geq 0
$$

So, we have that

$$
5x_1^2 \geq -6x_1^2, \qquad -4x_2^2 \geq -6x_2^2
$$

As a result,

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q(x) &= 5x_{1}^{2} - 4x_{2}^{2} - 6x_{3}^{2} \\
&\ge - 6x_{1}^{2} - 6x_{2}^{2} - 6x_{3}^{2} \\
&=-6(x_{1}^{2} + x_{2}^{2} + x_{3}^{2})
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, recall that

$$
\| \mathbf{x} \| = 1
$$

is equivalent to

$$
x_1^2 + x_2^2 + x_3^2 =1
$$

So,

$$
-6(x_{1}^{2} + x_{2}^{2} + x_{3}^{2}) =-6(1) =-6
$$

and, in turn,

$$
Q(\mathbf{x}) \geq -6
$$

So, the value of $Q(\mathbf{x})$ can be no smaller than $-6$. But before we conclude that $-6$ is the minimum, we need to find a vector such that

$$
Q(\mathbf{x}) = -6
$$

Notice that when $\begin{bmatrix}0 & 0 & 1\end{bmatrix}$ we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q(x) &= 5x_{1}^{2} - 4x_{2}^{2} - 6x_{3}^{2} \\
&= 5(0)^{2} - 4(0)^{2} - 6(1)^{2} \\
&=-6
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, given that

$$
\| \mathbf{x} \| =1
$$

the minimum value of $Q(\mathbf{x})$ is equal to $-6$.

## Constrained Optimization of Quadratic Forms

We can find a quadratic form's maximum and minimum values even when it contains cross-product terms. For this, we have the following theorem:

> *Let $A$ be the matrix corresponding to our quadratic form $Q(\mathbf x)$:*
> - *The maximum value of $Q(\mathbf{x})$, when $\| \mathbf{x} \|=1$, is equal to the largest eigenvalue of $A{:}$* $\max \{Q(\mathbf{x}) \: \mid \: \| \mathbf{x} \| = 1 \} = \lambda_{\textrm{max}}$*The maximum is attained on the corresponding unit eigenvector $\mathbf{u}_{\textrm{max}}$.*
> - *The minimum value of $Q(\mathbf{x})$, when $\| \mathbf{x} \|=1$, is equal to the smallest eigenvalue of $A{:}$* $\min \{Q(\mathbf{x}) \: \mid \: \| \mathbf{x} \| = 1 \} = \lambda_{\textrm{min}}$*The minimum is attained on the corresponding unit eigenvector $\mathbf{u}_{\textrm{min}}$.*

Suppose we are given the quadratic form

$$
Q(\mathbf{x})=4x_1^2-2x_1x_2+4x_2^2
$$

How can we find a unit vector $\mathbf{u}_{\textrm{max}}$ at which the maximum of $Q(\mathbf{x})$ is attained?

First, we write down the matrix of our quadratic form:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}4 & -1 \\ -1 & 4\end{bmatrix}
$$

Now, we need to calculate the eigenvalues of the matrix $A$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mid A - λI \mid &= 0 \\
\begin{vmatrix}4 - λ & -1 \\ -1 & 4 - λ\end{vmatrix} &= 0 \\
(4 - λ)(4 - λ) - 1 &= 0 \\
λ^{2} - 8λ + 15 &= 0 \\
(λ - 5)(λ - 3) &= 0 \\
λ &= 5, 3
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
\max \{Q(\mathbf{x}) \: \mid \: \| \mathbf{x} \ &= 1 \} = \lambda_{\textrm{max}} = 5
\end{aligned}
$$

To find $\mathbf{u}_{\textrm{max}}$, we need a unit eigenvector that corresponds to the eigenvalue

$$
\lambda_{\textrm{max}}=5
$$

We start by computing $(A-5I)$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
A - 5I &= \begin{bmatrix}4 & -1 \\ -1 & 4\end{bmatrix} - 5\begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}-1 & -1 \\ -1 & -1\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Seeking a non-zero solution of

$$
(A-5I)\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{0}
$$

gives the eigenvector $\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ -1\end{bmatrix}$

Finally, dividing $\mathbf{v}$ by its norm

$$
\| \mathbf{v} \| = \sqrt{2}
$$

we get

$$
\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ -1\end{bmatrix}
$$

## Finding the Maximum and Minimum Values of a Quadratic Form on the Unit Sphere

**Example:** Find the minimum value of $Q(\mathbf{x})=x_1^2-2x_2^2-2x_3^2+2x_2x_3$ given that $\| \mathbf{x} \| = 1$.

**Explanation**

Let $A$ be the matrix corresponding to our quadratic form. Recall that:

- The maximum value of $Q(\mathbf{x})$, when $\| \mathbf{x} \|=1$, is equal to the largest eigenvalue of $A{:}$
$\max \{Q(\mathbf{x}) \: \mid \: \| \mathbf{x} \| = 1 \} = \lambda_{\textrm{max}}$
- The minimum value of $Q(\mathbf{x})$, when $\| \mathbf{x} \|=1$, is equal to the smallest eigenvalue of $A{:}$
$\min \{Q(\mathbf{x}) \: \mid \: \| \mathbf{x} \| = 1 \} = \lambda_{\textrm{min}}$

First, we write down the matrix of our quadratic form:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & -2 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & -2\end{bmatrix}
$$

Now, we need to calculate the eigenvalues of the matrix $A$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mid A - λI \mid &= 0 \\
\begin{vmatrix}1 - λ & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & -2 - λ & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & -2 - λ\end{vmatrix} &= 0 \\
(1 - λ)\begin{vmatrix}-2 - λ & 1 \\ 1 & -2 - λ\end{vmatrix} &= 0 \\
(1 - λ)((-2 - λ)(-2 - λ) - 1) &= 0 \\
(1 - λ)(λ^{2} + 4λ + 3) &= 0 \\
(1 - λ)(λ + 3)(λ + 1) &= 0 \\
λ &= 1,-1,-3
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, we have

$$
\min \{Q(\mathbf{x}) \: \mid \: \| \mathbf{x} \| = 1 \} = -3
$$
