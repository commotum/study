## Introduction

**Simple harmonic motion (SHM)** is a special type of periodic motion in which an object oscillates about an equilibrium position. In SHM, the object's acceleration is proportional to the distance from the equilibrium position, and it acts in the opposite direction to the displacement.

If $y(t)$ represents the position of an object experiencing SHM, then it obeys the differential equation

$$
y'' + \omega^2 y = 0
$$

where $\omega > 0$ is the **angular frequency** and the **period** $T$ is given by

$$
T = \dfrac{2\pi}{\omega}
$$

The period $T$ represents the number of seconds it takes for the object to complete one full oscillation. Similarly, the **frequency** $f$ (measured in $s^{-1}$) represents the number of times the object oscillates per second and is given by

$$
f = \dfrac{1}{T} = \dfrac{\omega}{2\pi}
$$

The general solution to the SHM equation can be written in three different forms:

1. $y = R\cos{(\omega t + \phi_1)}$
2. $y = R\sin{(\omega t + \phi_2)}$
3. $y =A\cos{\omega t} + B\sin{\omega t}$

In forms 1 and 2, the constant $R$ is called the **amplitude**, and it represents the object's maximum distance from the equilibrium position.

**Note:** To see where the SHM equation comes from, remember that the object's acceleration $y''$ is proportional to the distance $y$ from the equilibrium and acts in the opposite direction as the displacement. If the constant of proportionality is $\omega^2$, then we have

$$
y'' =-\omega^2 y \quad \Longrightarrow \quad y'' + \omega^2 y = 0
$$

<a id="identifying-equations-that-represent-simple-harmonic-motion"></a>
## Identifying Equations that Represent Simple Harmonic Motion

**Example:** The function $y(t)$ gives the position of a particle at time $t$. Which of the following equations represents a situation in which the particle is undergoing simple harmonic motion?

1. $y'' + 81y = 0$
2. $y'' + 9y'+81y = 0$
3. $y'' - 81y = 0$

**Explanation**

The simple harmonic motion (SHM) equation is

$$
y'' + \omega^2 y = 0
$$

With that in mind, let's inspect each equation.

- Equation I takes the form
$y'' + \omega^2 y = 0$
with
$\omega^2 = 81 \quad \Rightarrow \quad \omega = 9$.
Therefore, equation I represents SHM.
- Equation II does not take the form
$y'' + \omega^2 y = 0$
because it has a $y'$ term.
- Equation III does not take the form
$y'' + \omega^2 y = 0$.
It would require $\omega^2 = -81$, which is not possible for any real number $\omega$ since $\omega^2 \geq 0$.

Therefore, the correct answer is "I only".

## Calculating the Period of a Simple Harmonic Oscillation

**Example:** The position $y(t)$ of a particle oscillating with simple harmonic motion is governed by the equation

$y'' + 100y = 0$,

where $t > 0$ is the time, in seconds. What is the period of the oscillation?

**Explanation**

The simple harmonic motion (SHM) equation is

$$
y'' + \omega^2 y = 0
$$

where the period $T$ is given by

$$
T = \dfrac{2\pi}{\omega}
$$

In our case, we have

$$
\omega^2 = 100\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \omega = 10
$$

so the period (in seconds) is

$$
T = \dfrac{2\pi}{10} = \dfrac{\pi}{5}\,\textrm{s}
$$

## Identifying Solutions to Simple Harmonic Motion Equations

**Example:** A particle is moving with simple harmonic motion. The position of the particle $y(t)$ at time $t$ is governed by the equation

$y'' + 9 y = 0$.

Which of the following could be used to represent the general solution for $y(t)$?

1. $y = R\cos(3t+\phi_1)$
2. $y = R\sin(9t+\phi_2)$
3. $y = A\cos{3t} + B\sin{3t}$

Note that $R,\phi_1,\phi_2, A$ and $B$ are constants.

**Explanation**

The SHM equation is

$$
y'' + \omega^2y = 0
$$

The general solution to the SHM equation can be written in three different forms:

1. $y = R\cos{(\omega t + \phi_1)}$
2. $y = R\sin{(\omega t + \phi_2)}$
3. $y =A\cos{\omega t} + B\sin{\omega t}$

In our case, we have

$$
\omega^2 = 9\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \omega = 3
$$

Substituting $\omega=3$ into the three different forms of the general solution gives the following:

1. $y = R\cos{(3t + \phi_1)}$
2. $y = R\sin{(3t + \phi_2)}$
3. $y =A\cos{3t} + B\sin{3 t}$

Therefore, only functions I and III could be used to represent $y(t)$.

<a id="calculating-the-amplitude-of-an-simple-harmonic-oscillation"></a>
## Calculating the Amplitude of an Simple Harmonic Oscillation

**Example:** The position $y(t)$, measured in meters, of a particle oscillating with simple harmonic motion, is governed by the equation

$y'' + 36y = 0$,

where $t > 0$ is the time, in seconds. Given that the particle is initially at the origin with a velocity of $3\,\textrm{m/s}$, find the amplitude of the oscillation.

**Explanation**

The simple harmonic motion (SHM) equation is

$$
y'' + \omega^2 y = 0
$$

and the general solution can be written as

$$
y = R\sin{(\omega t + \phi)}
$$

where $R$ is the amplitude and $\phi$ is the phase.

In our case, we have

$$
\omega^2 = 36\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \omega = 6
$$

so the general solution can be written as

$$
y = R\sin{(6t+\phi)}
$$

We're given the initial condition $y(0) = 0$. Substituting this into the equation above gives

$$
R\sin\phi = 0\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \phi = 0
$$

Since $\phi=0$, our general solution reduces to

$$
y = R\sin{6 t}
$$

We're told that the velocity is $3\,\textrm{m/s}$ when $t=0$, which means that $y'(0) = 3$. Differentiating the general solution with respect to $t$ gives

$$
y' = 6R\cos{6t}
$$

and substituting $y'(0) = 3$ into the above gives

$$
3 = 6R\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad R=\dfrac{1}{2}
$$

Therefore, the amplitude is

$$
\dfrac{1}{2}\,\textrm{m}
$$

<a id="the-equation-of-motion-for-a-mass-spring-system"></a>
## The Equation of Motion for a Mass-Spring System

A common demonstration of simple harmonic motion (SHM) involves attaching a mass to a spring, pulling the mass some distance away from the equilibrium position, and then letting go so that the mass oscillates back and forth.

In this setting, we can write the SHM equation in terms of the mass $m$ and the spring constant $k$. The force on any general object is $my''$, and in the case of the spring, it must be equal to $-ky$. So we have

$$
m y'' = -k y
$$

and rearranging the above equation gives

$$
y'' +\dfrac{k}{m} y = 0
$$

Comparing this to the SHM equation

$$
y'' + \omega^2 y = 0
$$

we see that

$$
\omega^2 = \dfrac{k}{m}
$$

<a id="identifying-properties-of-a-mass-spring-system"></a>
## Identifying Properties of a Mass-Spring System

**Example:** A particle of mass $36\,\textrm{kg}$ is attached to a massless spring. The spring constant associated with the spring is $k = 9\, \textrm{kg/s}^2$. The position of the particle is $y(t)$ (measured in meters), and $t>0$ is the time in seconds.

If the particle moves with simple harmonic motion, then which of the following statements are true?

1. The governing differential equation is $y'' + \dfrac{1}{2}y = 0$
2. The frequency $f$ of the oscillation is $f = \dfrac{1}{4\pi}\,\textrm{s}^{-1}$
3. The general solution can be expressed as $y = R\sin{\left(\dfrac{1}{2}t+\phi\right)}$, where $R$ is the amplitude and $\phi$ is the phase.

**Explanation**

Let's look at each statement.

- Statement I is false. The equation of motion for a particle of mass $m=36$ moving with simple harmonic motion (SHM) under the action of a spring with spring constant $k=9$ has
$\omega^2 = \dfrac{k}{m} = \dfrac{9}{36} = \dfrac{1}{4}$,
so its equation of motion is
$y'' + \dfrac{1}{4}y = 0$,
which is different from the equation in statement I.
- Statement II is true. The frequency $f$ (measured in $s^{-1}$) is given by
$f = \dfrac{\omega}{2\pi} = \dfrac{\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)}{2\pi} = \dfrac{1}{4\pi} \,\textrm{s}^{-1}. \quad \color{green}\checkmark$
- Statement III is true. The general solution for $y(t)$ can take one of three forms:
$A\cos ωt + B\sin ωt, R\cos (ωt + ϕ_{1}), R\sin (ωt + ϕ_{2})$.
Writing $\omega = \dfrac{1}{2}$ and $\phi_1 = \phi$ in the form $R\sin{(\omega t+\phi_2)}$ gives
$y(t) = R\sin{\left(\dfrac{1}{2}t+\phi\right)}. \quad \color{green}\checkmark$

Therefore, the correct answer is "II and III only".
