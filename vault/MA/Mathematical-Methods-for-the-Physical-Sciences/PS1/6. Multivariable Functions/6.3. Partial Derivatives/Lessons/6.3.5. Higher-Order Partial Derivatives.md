## Introduction

Just like we can compute second derivatives of single-variable functions, we can also compute second derivatives of multivariable functions.

For example, consider the two-variable function

$$
f (x, y)= x y^2 + e^{xy}
$$

Its two first-order partial derivatives are

$$
\begin{aligned}
f_{x}(x, y) &= y^{2} + ye^{xy}, \\
f_{y}(x, y) &= 2xy + xe^{xy}
\end{aligned}
$$

Since $f_x(x,y)$ and $f_y(x,y)$ are themselves functions that are differentiable with respect to both $x$ and $y$, we can compute the **second-order partial derivatives** as well.

- The partial derivatives of $f_x(x,y)$ with respect to $x$ and $y$, denoted $f_{xx}$ and $f_{xy}$ respectively, are
$f_{xx}(x, y)|= \frac{∂}{∂x}(f_{x}(x, y)) = y^{2}e^{xy},; f_{xy}(x, y)|= \frac{∂}{∂y}(f_{x}(x, y)) = 2y + xye^{xy} + e^{xy}$.
- The partial derivatives of $f_y(x,y)$ with respect to $x$ and $y$, denoted $f_{yx}$ and $f_{yy}$ respectively, are
$f_{yx}(x, y)|= \frac{∂}{∂x}(f_{y}(x, y)) = 2y + xye^{xy} + e^{xy},; f_{yy}(x, y)|= \frac{∂}{∂y}(f_{y}(x, y)) = 2x + x^{2}e^{xy}$.

The partial derivatives $f_{xy}$ and $f_{yx}$ are called **mixed partial derivatives** because they involve taking the partial derivatives with respect to different variables, $x$ and $y$.

The following alternative notations are also widely used to represent second-order partial derivatives:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{∂^{2}}{∂x^{2}}(f) &= \frac{∂^{2}f}{∂x^{2}} = f_{xx}\frac{∂^{2}}{∂y^{2}}(f) = \frac{∂^{2}f}{∂y^{2}} = f_{yy} \\
\frac{∂^{2}}{∂y∂x}(f) &= \frac{∂^{2}f}{∂y∂x} = f_{xy}\frac{∂^{2}}{∂x∂y}(f) = \frac{∂^{2}f}{∂x∂y} = f_{yx}
\end{aligned}
$$

**Note:** In our example, the two mixed partial derivatives were equal:

$$
f_{xy}(x,y) = 2y + xye^{xy} + e^{xy} = f_{yx}(x,y)
$$

Later, we will see that under rather mild conditions on $f$, we almost always have that

$$
f_{xy}=f_{yx}
$$

## Finding Mixed Second-Order Partial Derivatives

**Example:** Find $\dfrac {\partial^2 f}{\partial x\partial y}$ given that $\dfrac {\partial f}{\partial y} = \dfrac {2y} {x + y^2}$.

**Explanation**

To find

$$
\dfrac {\partial^2 f}{\partial x\partial y}
$$

we differentiate the given first-order partial derivative

$$
\dfrac {\partial f}{\partial y}
$$

with respect to $x$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{∂^{2}f}{∂x∂y} &= \frac{∂}{∂x}(\frac{∂f}{∂y}) \\
&= \frac{∂}{∂x}(\frac{2y}{x + y^{2}}) \\
&= 2y \cdot \frac{∂}{∂x}((x + y^{2})^{-1}) \\
&=-2y(x + y^{2})^{-2} \cdot 1 \\
&= - (2y)/((x + y^{2})^{2})
\end{aligned}
$$

## Computing Expressions Involving Second-Order Partial Derivatives

**Example:** Find $f_{xy}(x,y)+f_{yx}(x,y)$ given that $f_x(x,y)=y\cos(xy)$ and $f_y(x,y)=x\cos(xy)$.

**Explanation**

To find $f_{xy}(x,y)$, we differentiate the given first-order partial derivative $f_{x}(x,y)$ with respect to $y$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
f_{xy}(x, y) &= \frac{∂}{∂y}(f_{x}(x, y)) \\
&= \frac{∂}{∂y}(y\cos (xy)) \\
&= 1 \cdot \cos (xy) + y(-\sin (xy) \cdot x) \\
&= \cos (xy) - xy\sin (xy)
\end{aligned}
$$

Similarly, to find $f_{yx}(x,y)$, we differentiate the given first-order partial derivative $f_{y}(x,y)$ with respect to $x$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
f_{yx}(x, y) &= \frac{∂}{∂x}(f_{y}(x, y)) \\
&= \frac{∂}{∂x}(x\cos (xy)) \\
&= 1 \cdot \cos (xy) + x(-\sin (xy) \cdot y) \\
&= \cos (xy) - xy\sin (xy)
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
f_{xy}(x, y) + f_{yx}(x, y) &= \cos (xy) - xy\sin (xy) + \cos (xy) - xy\sin (xy) \\
&= 2(\cos (xy) - xy\sin (xy))
\end{aligned}
$$

## Higher-Order Partial Derivatives for Functions of  More Than Two Variables

We can also define other **higher order partial derivatives** such as $f_{xxy}, \, f_{xxyy}, \, f_{xyxy}$, etc.

For instance, the function

$$
f(x,y,z)= z\cos(xy)
$$

has the first-order partial derivative

$$
f_y(x,y,z) = -xz \sin(xy)
$$

and the second-order partial derivative

$$
f_{yx}(x,y,z)= -x yz \cos{\left(x y \right)} - z\sin{\left(x y \right)}
$$

If we differentiate again, with respect to $x$, then we get the **third-order partial derivative** $f_{yxx}$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
f_{yxx}(x, y, z) &= \frac{∂}{∂x}(f_{yx}(x, y)) \\
&= \frac{∂}{∂x}(-xyz\cos (xy) - z\sin (xy)) \\
&=-yz\cos (xy) - xyz(-\sin (xy) \cdot y) - z\cos (xy) \cdot y \\
&= xy^{2}z\sin (xy) - 2yz\cos (xy)
\end{aligned}
$$

In general, to compute a $n$th-order partial derivative, we need to compute a sequence of $n$ individual derivatives.

<a id="finding-higher-order-derivatives"></a>
## Finding Higher-Order Derivatives

**Example:** Evaluate $g_{xyz}(x,y,z)$ at $(1,2,3)$ if $g_x(x,y,z) = 2x y z + y^3$.

**Explanation**

First, we find $g_{xy}(x,y,z)$ by differentiating $g_{x}(x,y,z)$ with respect to $y$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
g_{xy}(x, y, z) &= \frac{∂}{∂y}(2xyz + y^{3}) \\
&= 2xz + 3y^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we find $f_{xyz}(x,y,z)$ by differentiating $f_{xy}(x,y,z)$ with respect to $z$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
g_{xyz}(x, y, z) &= \frac{∂}{∂z}(2xz + 3y^{2}) \\
&= 2x
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
g_{xyz}(1, 2, 3) &= 2(1) \\
&= 2
\end{aligned}
$$
