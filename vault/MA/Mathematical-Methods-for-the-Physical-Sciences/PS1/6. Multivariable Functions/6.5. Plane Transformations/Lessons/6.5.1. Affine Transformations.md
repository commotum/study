## Introduction

An **affine transformation** $\mathbf T:\mathbb R^2\rightarrow\mathbb R^2$ is a function that can be expressed in the form

$$
\mathbf x = A \mathbf u + \mathbf b
$$

where $\mathbf x, \mathbf u$, and $\mathbf b$ are column vectors in $\mathbb R^2$, and $A$ is a $2\times 2$ matrix.

Under this notation,

- $\begin{bmatrix}u \\ v\end{bmatrix}$ represents vectors belonging to the input space, and
- $\begin{bmatrix}x \\ y\end{bmatrix}$ represents vectors belonging to the output space.

For example,

$$
\begin{bmatrix}u \\ v\end{bmatrix}
$$

is an affine transformation because it can be expressed in the form

$$
\mathbf x = A\mathbf u + \mathbf b
$$

as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\begin{bmatrix}x \\ y\end{bmatrix} &= \begin{bmatrix}u - v + 1 \\ 2u + v - 3\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}u - v \\ 2u + v\end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix}1 \\ -3\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}1 & -1 \\ 2 & 1\end{bmatrix}_(⏟)_(A) \cdot \begin{bmatrix}u \\ v\end{bmatrix}_(⏟)_(u) + \begin{bmatrix}1 \\ -3\end{bmatrix}_(⏟)_(b)
\end{aligned}
$$

However, the transformation

$$
\begin{bmatrix}u \\ v\end{bmatrix}
$$

is not affine since it contains the quadratic term $uv$.

Finally, note the following relationships between affine and linear transformations:

- A linear transformation $\mathbf x = A \mathbf u$ is a special case of an affine transformation with $\mathbf b = \mathbf 0$.
- For an affine transformation $\mathbf T$ given by $\mathbf x = A\mathbf u + \mathbf b$, we say that $A$ is *the matrix representation of the linear transformation associated with* $\mathbf T$.

## Identifying Affine Transformations

**Example:** Which of the following are affine transformations?

1. $\begin{bmatrix}u \\ v\end{bmatrix}$
2. $\begin{bmatrix}u \\ v\end{bmatrix}$
3. $\begin{bmatrix}u \\ v\end{bmatrix}$

**Explanation**

An affine transformation $\mathbf T:\mathbb R^2\rightarrow\mathbb R^2$ is a function of the form

$$
\mathbf x = A \mathbf u + \mathbf b
$$

where $\mathbf x, \mathbf u$, and $\mathbf b$ are column vectors in $\mathbb R^2$, and $A$ is a $2\times 2$ matrix.

With this in mind, let's look at each transformation in turn.

- Transformation $\mathbf{T}_1$ is affine. Notice that $\mathbf{T}_1$ can be expressed as
$\begin{bmatrix}x \\ y\end{bmatrix}|= \begin{bmatrix}u + v \\ v - 2u + 1\end{bmatrix}; = \begin{bmatrix}u + v \\ -2u + v\end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix}0 \\ 1\end{bmatrix}; = \begin{bmatrix}1 & 1 \\ -2 & 1\end{bmatrix}_(⏟)_(A) \cdot \begin{bmatrix}u \\ v\end{bmatrix}_(⏟)_(u) + \begin{bmatrix}0 \\ 1\end{bmatrix}_(⏟)_(b)$.
- Transformation $\mathbf{T}_2$ is affine. Notice that $\mathbf{T}_2$ can be expressed as
$\begin{bmatrix}x \\ y\end{bmatrix}|= \begin{bmatrix}u - v \\ v - u\end{bmatrix}; = \begin{bmatrix}u - v \\ -u + v\end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix}0 \\ 0\end{bmatrix}; = \begin{bmatrix}1 & -1 \\ -1 & 1\end{bmatrix}_(⏟)_(A) \cdot \begin{bmatrix}u \\ v\end{bmatrix}_(⏟)_(u) + \begin{bmatrix}0 \\ 0\end{bmatrix}_(⏟)_(b)$.
In fact, the transformation $\mathbf{T}_2$ is a linear transformation.
- Transformation $\mathbf{T}_3$ is not affine since it contains the quadratic term $v^2$.

Therefore, the correct answer is "$\mathbf{T}_1$ and $\mathbf{T}_2$ only."

<a id="finding-the-parametric-representation-of-an-affine-transformation-given-its-matrix-form"></a>
## Finding the Parametric Representation of an Affine Transformation Given its Matrix Form

**Example:** The matrix form of the affine transformation
$x = 3u-v + \bbox[Gainsboro,2pt]{a}, \qquad y = u + \bbox[Gainsboro,2pt]{b}v + \bbox[Gainsboro,2pt]{c}$
is shown below. What is the value of $a+b+c$?
$\begin{bmatrix}x \\ y\end{bmatrix}$

**Explanation**

An affine transformation $\mathbf T:\mathbb R^2\rightarrow\mathbb R^2$ is a function of the form

$$
\mathbf x = A \mathbf u + \mathbf b
$$

where $\mathbf x, \mathbf u$, and $\mathbf b$ are column vectors in $\mathbb R^2$, and $A$ is a $2\times 2$ matrix.

We can carry out the matrix operations using the matrix form, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\begin{bmatrix}x \\ y\end{bmatrix} &= \begin{bmatrix}3 & -1 \\ 1 & -5\end{bmatrix}^(⏞)^(A) \cdot \begin{bmatrix}u \\ v\end{bmatrix}^(⏞)^(u) + \begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 0\end{bmatrix}^(⏞)^(b) \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}3u - v \\ u - 5v\end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 0\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}3u - v + 1 \\ u - 5v\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Equating the corresponding elements, we obtain

$$
x = 3u - v + \bbox[Gainsboro,2pt]{1}, \qquad y = u + (\bbox[Gainsboro,2pt]{-5})v + \bbox[Gainsboro,2pt]{0} \,
$$

Therefore, we have

$$
a+b+c = 1+(-5)+0 = -4
$$

## Finding the Matrix Representation of an Affine Transformation Given its Parametric Form

**Example:** The matrix form of the affine transformation
$x = 5u + v - 6, \qquad y = 2u + 4v$
is shown below. What is the value of $a+b+c$?
$\begin{bmatrix}x \\ y\end{bmatrix}$

**Explanation**

An affine transformation $\mathbf T:\mathbb R^2\rightarrow\mathbb R^2$ is a function of the form

$$
\mathbf x = A \mathbf u + \mathbf b
$$

where $\mathbf x, \mathbf u$, and $\mathbf b$ are column vectors in $\mathbb R^2$, and $A$ is a $2\times 2$ matrix.

First, notice that we can express the given transformation as
$\begin{bmatrix}x \\ y\end{bmatrix}$

Separating the constants and expressing the linear part as a product, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
\begin{bmatrix}x \\ y\end{bmatrix} &= \begin{bmatrix}5u + v - 6 \\ 2u + 4v\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}5u + v \\ 2u + 4v\end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix}-6 \\ 0\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}5 & 1 \\ 2 & 4\end{bmatrix}_(⏟)_(A) \cdot \begin{bmatrix}u \\ v\end{bmatrix}_(⏟)_(u) + \begin{bmatrix}-6 \\ 0\end{bmatrix}_(⏟)_(b)
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, we have

$$
a+b+c = 1+2+0 = 3
$$
