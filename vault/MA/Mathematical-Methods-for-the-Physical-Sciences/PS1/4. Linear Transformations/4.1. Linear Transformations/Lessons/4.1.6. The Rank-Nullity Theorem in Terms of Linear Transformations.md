## Introduction

The **rank-nullity theorem in terms of linear transformations** states the following:

> *If $\mathbf{T}:\mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}^n$ is a linear transformation, then*
> $\textrm{dim(Im}(\mathbf{T})) + \textrm{dim(Ker}(\mathbf{T})) = \textrm{dim}(\mathbb{R}^n)$,
> *or equivalently,*
> $\textrm{rank}(\mathbf{T}) + \textrm{nullity}(\mathbf{T}) = n$.

For example, suppose that $\mathbf{T}:\mathbb{R}^3\to\mathbb{R}^3$ is a linear transformation such that

$$
\textrm{Im}(\mathbf{T})=\{[ x, y, z ]^T \,: \, y+2z=0\}
$$

Let's use the rank-nullity theorem to compute $\textrm{rank}(\mathbf{T})$ and $\text{nullity}(\mathbf{T})$.

First, solving

$$
y+2z=0
$$

for $y$, we get $y = -2z$. Therefore, any vector from $\textrm{Im}(\mathbf{T})$ can be written as

$$
\begin{bmatrix}x \\ -2z \\ z\end{bmatrix} = x\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 0 \\ 0\end{bmatrix}_(⏟)_(v_{1}) + z\begin{bmatrix}0 \\ -2 \\ 1\end{bmatrix}_(⏟)_(v_{2})
$$

This means that

$$
\textrm{Im}(\mathbf{T})=\textrm{Span}\{\mathbf{v}_1,\mathbf{v}_2\}
$$

And since $\mathbf v_1$ and $\mathbf v_2$ are linearly independent, we have

$$
\textrm{rank}(\mathbf{T})=2
$$

Finally, using the rank-nullity theorem, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
nullity(T) &= 3 - rank(T) \\
&= 3 - 2 \\
&= 1
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding the Rank or Nullity of a Linear Transformation Using the Rank-Nullity Theorem

**Example:** Consider a linear transformation $\mathbf{T}:\mathbb{R}^3 \to \mathbb{R}^3$. If $\textrm{nullity}(\mathbf{T})=1$, then what is $\textrm{rank}(\mathbf{T})$?

**Explanation**

According to the rank-nullity theorem, if $\mathbf{T}:\mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}^n$, then

$$
\textrm{rank}(\mathbf{T}) + \textrm{nullity}(\mathbf{T}) = n
$$

For the given transformation, we have $n=3$. Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
rank(T) + nullity(T) &= n \\
rank(T) + 1 &= 3 \\
rank(T) &= 3 - 1 \\
&= 2
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding the Rank or Nullity of a Linear Transformation Given a Description of the Image or Kernel

**Example:** Find $\text{nullity}(\mathbf{T})$ given that $\mathbf{T}:\mathbb{R}^3\to\mathbb{R}^3$ and $\textrm{Im}(\mathbf{T})=\{[x, y, z]^T \,: \, x=y=z\}$.

**Explanation**

Any vector from $\textrm{Im}(\mathbf{T})$ can be written as

$$
\begin{bmatrix}x \\ x \\ x\end{bmatrix} = x\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 1 \\ 1\end{bmatrix}_(⏟)_(v_{1})
$$

This means that

$$
\textrm{Im}(\mathbf{T})=\textrm{Span}\{\mathbf{v}_1\}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\textrm{rank}(\mathbf{T})=1
$$

Finally, according the rank-nullity theorem, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
nullity(T) &= 3 - rank(T) \\
&= 3 - 1 \\
&= 2
\end{aligned}
$$

## Identifying True Statements Using the Rank-Nullity Theorem

**Example:** Let $\mathbf{T}: \mathbb{R}^4 \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^4$ be a linear transformation whose standard matrix is $T$, and let $\{\mathbf{v}_1, \mathbf{v}_2, \mathbf{v}_3 \}$ be a set of linearly independent vectors in $\mathbb{R}^4$. Consider the following statement:

> *If $\textrm{Im}(\mathbf{T})=\textrm{Span}\{\mathbf{v}_1, \mathbf{v}_2, \mathbf{v}_3\}$, then $\textrm{__________}$.*

Which of the following can be placed into the blank space to make a true statement?

1. $\textrm{rank}(T)=3$
2. $\textrm{nullity}(\mathbf{T})=3$
3. $\mathbf{T}$ is a bijection

**Explanation**

Since $\mathbf{T}$ is a linear transformation from $\mathbb{R}^4$ into $\mathbb{R}^4$, the invertible matrix theorem states the following:

> $\mathbf{T}$ is a bijection (an invertible transformation) $Im(T) = R^{4}; rank(T) = 4; Ker(T) = {0}; nullity(T) = 0$

Also, according to the rank-nullity theorem,

$$
\textrm{rank}(\mathbf{T}) + \textrm{nullity}(\mathbf{T}) = 4
$$

With that in mind, let's examine each statement in turn.

- Statement I is true. Since $\textrm{Im}(\mathbf{T})=\textrm{Span}\{\mathbf{v}_1, \mathbf{v}_2, \mathbf{v}_3\}$ and the set $\{\mathbf{v}_1, \mathbf{v}_2, \mathbf{v}_3 \}$ is linearly independent, we get that $\textrm{rank}(T)=\textrm{rank}(\mathbf{T})=3$.
- Statement II is false. According to the rank-nullity theorem, we obtain
$nullity(T)|= 4 - rank(T); = 4 - 3; = 1$.
- Statement III is false. Since $\textrm{rank}(\mathbf{T}) \neq 4$ (or since $\textrm{nullity}(\mathbf{T}) \neq 0$), the linear transformation $\mathbf{T}$ cannot be a bijection according to the invertible matrix theorem.

Therefore, the correct option is "I only."
