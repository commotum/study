## Introduction

Suppose we are given a system of linear equations and its respective augmented matrix:

$$
x_{1} + 3x_{2} - x_{3} = 0; 2x_{1} - x_{2} + 4x_{3} =-2; 5x_{1} + x_{2} + x_{3} = 1
$$

We can apply some operations to the augmented matrix that do not change the set of solutions for the corresponding system. These operations are called **elementary row operations**.

After applying an **elementary row operation** to the augmented matrix, the new corresponding system will have the same solutions.

There are three types of elementary row operations:

- switching any two rows:
$R_i \leftrightarrow R_j$
- multiplying (each element of) a row by a non-zero number:
$R_i:= k R_i,\qquad k \neq 0$
- adding a multiple of one row to another row:
$R_j:= R_j+k R_i,\qquad j \neq i$

In the notations above, the "$:=$" symbol is called the **assignment operator.** We use this to assign new values to every element of a given row.

For example, the row operation $R_1:= 2 R_1$ means, "create a new row $1$ by taking the old row $1$ and multiplying it by $2$." When we do this, we get the following:

$$
M \mid ∼\begin{bmatrix}1 & 3 & -1 & 0 \\ 2 & -1 & 4 & -2 \\ 5 & 1 & 1 & 1\end{bmatrix}R_{1}:=2R_{1}; ∼\begin{bmatrix}2 \cdot 1 & 2 \cdot 3 & 2 \cdot (-1) & 2 \cdot 0 \\ 2 & -1 & 4 & -2 \\ 5 & 1 & 1 & 1\end{bmatrix}; ∼\begin{bmatrix}2 & 6 & -2 & 0 \\ 2 & -1 & 4 & -2 \\ 5 & 1 & 1 & 1\end{bmatrix}
$$

A few final points on notation.

- We use the similarity symbol ($\sim$) to indicate when one matrix is transformed into another matrix using elementary row operations.
- The assignment operator $:=$ is sometimes called the "walrus operator" because it looks like a walrus on its side!

## Applying Row Operations To a Coefficients Matrix

**Example:** Consider the matrix $A$, given by
$\begin{bmatrix}1 & 2 & 3 \\ 4 & 2 & -1 \\ 7 & 12 & 13\end{bmatrix}$
What matrix results from carrying out the row operation $R_2:= R_2 +(-4) \cdot R_1$ on $A$?

**Explanation**

The operation

$$
R_2:=R_2 + (-4) R_1
$$

means that we need to add $-4$ times the first row to the second row.

Carrying out this row operation, we get the following:

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= \begin{bmatrix}1 & 2 & 3 \\ 4 & 2 & -1 \\ 7 & 12 & 13\end{bmatrix}R_{2}:=R_{2} + (-4) \cdot R_{1} \\
&= ∼\begin{bmatrix}1 & 2 & 3 \\ 4 + (-4)(1) & 2 + (-4)(2) & -1 + (-4)(3) \\ 7 & 12 & 13\end{bmatrix} \\
&= ∼\begin{bmatrix}1 & 2 & 3 \\ 0 & -6 & -13 \\ 7 & 12 & 13\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Applying Row Operations To an Augmented Matrix

**Example:** Consider the augmented matrix $A$, given by
$\begin{bmatrix}-1 & 5 & 4 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 & 5 & 2 \\ 3 & 7 & 1 & 9\end{bmatrix}$

What matrix results from carrying out the row operation $R_1 \leftrightarrow R_2$ on $A$?

**Explanation**

The operation

$$
R_1 \leftrightarrow R_2
$$

means that we need to swap the first and second rows.

Applying this row operation, we obtain the following:

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= \begin{bmatrix}-1 & 5 & 4 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 & 5 & 2 \\ 3 & 7 & 1 & 9\end{bmatrix}R_{1}↔R_{2} \\
&= ∼\begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 & 5 & 2 \\ -1 & 5 & 4 & 1 \\ 3 & 7 & 1 & 9\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="identifying-the-appropriate-row-operation-to-transform-one-matrix-into-another"></a>
## Identifying the Appropriate Row Operation To Transform One Matrix Into Another

**Example:** Which elementary row operation could transform the matrix $A$ into the matrix $B$, where
$\begin{bmatrix}-4 & -2 & 4 \\ 4 & 1 & 2 \\ 1 & 2 & 3\end{bmatrix}$

**Explanation**

First, notice that the $1$st and the $3$rd rows are the same in both matrices. The only row that has been changed is the $2$nd row.

Now, applying the operation

$$
R_2:=R_2+1 \cdot R_1
$$

we get the following:

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= \begin{bmatrix}-4 & -2 & 4 \\ 4 & 1 & 2 \\ 1 & 2 & 3\end{bmatrix}R_{2}:=R_{2} + 1 \cdot R_{1} \\
&= ∼\begin{bmatrix}-4 & -2 & 4 \\ 4 + 1 \cdot (-4) & 1 + 1 \cdot (-2) & 2 + 1 \cdot 4 \\ 1 & 2 & 3\end{bmatrix} \\
&= ∼\begin{bmatrix}-4 & -2 & 4 \\ 0 & -1 & 6 \\ 1 & 2 & 3\end{bmatrix} \\
&= B
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the transformation could be done by the operation

$$
R_2:=R_2+1\cdot R_1
$$

or simply

$$
R_2:=R_2+R_1
$$

## Identifying the Appropriate Row Operation To Transform a Given Entry to a Desired Value

**Example:** Given that
$\begin{bmatrix}3 & 1 & -2 \\ -1 & 5 & 6 \\ 0 & 2 & 4\end{bmatrix}$ which elementary row operation could make $a_{22}=0$?

**Explanation**

In the given matrix, we have $a_{22}=5$ and we want to perform an elementary row operation that makes $a_{22}=0$.

- We *cannot* make $a_{22}=0$ by switching two rows, because there are no rows with $0$ as the second entry.
- We *cannot* multiply the second row by $0$, because we are only allowed to multiply rows by *nonzero* constants.
- However, we *can* add a multiple of another row to the $2$nd row to make $a_{22}=0$.

Looking at the first row, we see that $a_{12}=1$. If we add $-5$ times this entry to $a_{22}$, we will get $a_{22}=0$ as desired. So, the elementary row operation that we need to use is

$$
R_2:= R_2 + (-5) \cdot R_1
$$

Applying this operation, we obtain the following:

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= \begin{bmatrix}3 & 1 & -2 \\ -1 & 5 & 6 \\ 0 & 2 & 4\end{bmatrix}R_{2}:=R_{2} + (-5) \cdot R_{1} \\
&= ∼\begin{bmatrix}3 & 1 & -2 \\ -1 + (-5) \cdot 3 & 5 + (-5) \cdot 1 & 6 + (-5) \cdot (-2) \\ 0 & 2 & 4\end{bmatrix} \\
&= ∼\begin{bmatrix}3 & 1 & -2 \\ -16 & 0 & 16 \\ 0 & 2 & 4\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the desired elemetary row operation is

$$
R_2:= R_2 + (-5) \cdot R_1
$$

or simply

$$
R_2:= R_2 -5 R_1
$$
