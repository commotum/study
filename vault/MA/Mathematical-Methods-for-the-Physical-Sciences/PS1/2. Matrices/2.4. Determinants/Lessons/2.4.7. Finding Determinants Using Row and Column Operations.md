## Introduction

One way to simplify a determinant calculation is to create a row (or column) containing all zeros except for one entry. Then, we can use a Laplace expansion across that row (or column) to compute the determinant.

Let's demonstrate this method on the matrix $A$ below.

$$
\begin{bmatrix}2 & 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 8 & 14 & 17\end{bmatrix}
$$

Notice that we can make entries to the right of $a_{21}=1$ equal to zero using elementary *column* operations.

Since adding a multiple of one column to another column in a matrix does not change the value of its determinant, we obtain

$$
=\begin{vmatrix}2 & 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 8 & 14 & 17\end{vmatrix} \mid C_{2}:=C_{2} + (-2)C_{1}; C_{3}:=C_{3} + (-3)C_{1}; =\begin{vmatrix}2 & -3 & -5 \\ 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 8 & -2 & -7\end{vmatrix}
$$

Expanding the result along the $2$nd row, we get

$$
=\begin{vmatrix}2 & -3 & -5 \\ 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 8 & -2 & -7\end{vmatrix}; = 1 \cdot (-1)^{2 + 1}\begin{vmatrix}-3 & -5 \\ -2 & -7\end{vmatrix}; = - (21 - 10); =-11
$$

## Reducing a 4x4 Determinant to a 3x3 Determinant Using Row Operations

**Example:** $\begin{vmatrix}1 & 2 & 3 & 1 \\ 2 & -1 & 5 & -7 \\ 4 & -2 & 4 & -5 \\ -3 & 3 & -3 & -4\end{vmatrix}$

Consider the above $4\times 4$ determinant. Find a $3\times 3$ determinant that has the same value.

*Hint: Use elementary row operations to make the entries below $a_{11}$ equal to zero.*

**Explanation**

Let's make entries below $a_{11}=1$ equal to zero using elementary row operations.

Since adding a multiple of one row (or column) to another row (or column) in a matrix does not change the value of its determinant, we obtain

$$
=\begin{vmatrix}1 & 2 & 3 & 1 \\ 2 & -1 & 5 & -7 \\ 4 & -2 & 4 & -5 \\ -3 & 3 & -3 & -4\end{vmatrix} \mid R_{2}:=R_{2} + (-2)R_{1}; R_{3}:=R_{3} + (-4)R_{1}; R_{4}:=R_{4} + 3R_{1}; =\begin{vmatrix}1 & 2 & 3 & 1 \\ 0 & -5 & -1 & -9 \\ 0 & -10 & -8 & -9 \\ 0 & 9 & 6 & -1\end{vmatrix}
$$

Expanding the result along the $1$st column, we get

$$
=\begin{vmatrix}1 & 2 & 3 & 1 \\ 0 & -5 & -1 & -9 \\ 0 & -10 & -8 & -9 \\ 0 & 9 & 6 & -1\end{vmatrix}; = 1 \cdot (-1)^{1 + 1}\begin{vmatrix}-5 & -1 & -9 \\ -10 & -8 & -9 \\ 9 & 6 & -1\end{vmatrix}; =\begin{vmatrix}-5 & -1 & -9 \\ -10 & -8 & -9 \\ 9 & 6 & -1\end{vmatrix}
$$

## Reducing a 4x4 Determinant to a 3x3 Determinant Using Column Operations

**Example:** $\begin{vmatrix}2 & 3 & 2 & 2 \\ -1 & 2 & 1 & -3 \\ 2 & -2 & -2 & 4 \\ -4 & -4 & 3 & 2\end{vmatrix}$

Consider the above $4\times 4$ determinant. Find a $3\times 3$ determinant that has the same value.

*Hint: Use elementary column operations to make the entries to the left and to the right of $a_{23}$ equal to zero.*

**Explanation**

Let's make entries to the left and to the right of $a_{23}=1$ equal to zero using elementary column operations.

Since adding a multiple of one row (or column) to another row (or column) in a matrix does not change the value of its determinant, we obtain

$$
=\begin{vmatrix}2 & 3 & 2 & 2 \\ -1 & 2 & 1 & -3 \\ 2 & -2 & -2 & 4 \\ -4 & -4 & 3 & 2\end{vmatrix} \mid C_{1}:=C_{1} + C_{3}; C_{2}:=C_{2} + (-2)C_{3}; C_{4}:=C_{4} + 3C_{3}; =\begin{vmatrix}4 & -1 & 2 & 8 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 & -2 & -2 \\ -1 & -10 & 3 & 11\end{vmatrix}
$$

Expanding the result along the $2$nd row, we get

$$
=\begin{vmatrix}4 & -1 & 2 & 8 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 & -2 & -2 \\ -1 & -10 & 3 & 11\end{vmatrix}; = 1 \cdot (-1)^{2 + 3}\begin{vmatrix}4 & -1 & 8 \\ 0 & 2 & -2 \\ -1 & -10 & 11\end{vmatrix}; = -\begin{vmatrix}4 & -1 & 8 \\ 0 & 2 & -2 \\ -1 & -10 & 11\end{vmatrix}
$$

## Computing 4x4 Determinants Using Row and Column Operations

**Example:** Calculate the value of
$\begin{vmatrix}1 & -1 & 3 & 2 \\ 2 & -4 & -2 & 6 \\ 1 & -2 & -3 & 3 \\ -3 & 3 & 4 & 2\end{vmatrix}$

*Hint: Use elementary column operations to make the entries to the right of $a_{31}$ equal to zero. You should be able to reduce the given $4 \times 4$ determinant to a $3 \times 3$ determinant.*

**Explanation**

Using elementary column operations, let's make entries to the right of $a_{31}=1$ equal to zero.

Since adding a multiple of one row (or column) to another row (or column) in a matrix does not change the value of its determinant, we obtain

$$
=\begin{vmatrix}1 & -1 & 3 & 2 \\ 2 & -4 & -2 & 6 \\ 1 & -2 & -3 & 3 \\ -3 & 3 & 4 & 2\end{vmatrix} \mid C_{2}:=C_{2} + 2C_{1}; C_{3}:=C_{3} + 3C_{1}; C_{4}:=C_{4} + (-3)C_{1}; =\begin{vmatrix}1 & 1 & 6 & -1 \\ 2 & 0 & 4 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ -3 & -3 & -5 & 11\end{vmatrix}
$$

Expanding the result along the $3$rd row, we get

$$
=\begin{vmatrix}1 & 1 & 6 & -1 \\ 2 & 0 & 4 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ -3 & -3 & -5 & 11\end{vmatrix}; = 1 \cdot (-1)^{3 + 1}\begin{vmatrix}1 & 6 & -1 \\ 0 & 4 & 0 \\ -3 & -5 & 11\end{vmatrix}; =\begin{vmatrix}1 & 6 & -1 \\ 0 & 4 & 0 \\ -3 & -5 & 11\end{vmatrix}; = 4 \cdot (-1)^{2 + 2}\begin{vmatrix}1 & -1 \\ -3 & 11\end{vmatrix}; = 4(11 - 3); = 32
$$
