## Introduction

Suppose we want to find the inverse of the matrix

$$
\begin{bmatrix}1 & 2 \\ 3 & 7\end{bmatrix}
$$

We can compute the inverse of $A$ using elementary row operations. There are two key advantages to using row operations to compute the inverse of a matrix:

1. The method works for any invertible matrix of any size, not just $2\times 2$ matrices.
2. It requires significantly less computation than some alternative methods. This is especially true for large matrices.

Here, we'll focus on the $2\times 2$ case. We use the following fact:

> *The row operations that transform $A$ into the identity matrix $I_2$ also transform $I_2$ into $A^{-1}$*.

So, to find the inverse of $A$, we proceed as follows:

**Step 1:** Write down $\color{blue}A$ and the identity matrix $I_2$ side-by-side, as shown below:
$\begin{bmatrix}1 & 2 & 1 & 0 \\ 3 & 7 & 0 & 1\end{bmatrix}$

**Step 2:** Apply the standard Gauss-Jordan method to transform the matrix $A$ into the identity matrix. We perform the same row operations on both matrices:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\begin{bmatrix}A & I_{2}\end{bmatrix} &= \begin{bmatrix}1 & 2 & 1 & 0 \\ 3 & 7 & 0 & 1\end{bmatrix}\begin{vmatrix}R_{2} &:=R_{2} + (-3)R_{1} \\ ∼\begin{bmatrix}1 & 2 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & -3 & 1\end{bmatrix} & R_{1}\end{vmatrix}:=R_{1} + (-2)R_{2} \\
&= ∼\begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 & 7 & -2 \\ 0 & 1 & -3 & 1\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}I & A^{-1}\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

The matrix $A^{-1}$ is obtained on the right-hand side of our big matrix after the transformation. Therefore,

$$
\begin{bmatrix}7 & -2 \\ -3 & 1\end{bmatrix}
$$

We can easily check our result by computing $AA^{-1}$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
AA^{-1} &= \begin{bmatrix}1 & 2 \\ 3 & 7\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}7 & -2 \\ -3 & 1\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1\end{bmatrix}✓
\end{aligned}
$$

## Transforming a 2x2 Matrix Into the Identity Matrix Using Row Operations

**Example:** Which of the following matrices are transformed into the $2\times 2$ identity matrix after applying the row operations $R_1 \leftrightarrow R_2 \,$ and $\, R_2:= R_2+(-1)R_1$?

$\begin{bmatrix}-2 & -1 \\ 1 & 0\end{bmatrix}$

**Explanation**

Let's apply the operations to each of the matrices:

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= \begin{bmatrix}-2 & -1 \\ 1 & 0\end{bmatrix}\begin{vmatrix}R_{1} & ↔R_{2} \\ ∼\begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 \\ -2 & -1\end{bmatrix} & R_{2}\end{vmatrix}:=R_{2} + (-1)R_{1} \\
&= ∼\begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 \\ -3 & -1\end{bmatrix} \\
&\ne I\cdot \\
B &= \begin{bmatrix}1 & 1 \\ 1 & 0\end{bmatrix}\begin{vmatrix}R_{1} & ↔R_{2} \\ ∼\begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 \\ 1 & 1\end{bmatrix} & R_{2}\end{vmatrix}:=R_{2} + (-1)R_{1} \\
&= ∼\begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1\end{bmatrix} \\
&= I✓
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the correct answer is "$B$ only".

## Identifying the Inverse of a 2x2 Matrix Calculated Using Row Operations

**Example:** The inverse of the $2\times 2$ matrix $A$ is found using row operations, as shown below. Complete the process, and determine $A^{-1}$.

$\begin{bmatrix}A & I\end{bmatrix}|= \begin{bmatrix}1 & 1 & 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 2 & 0 & 1\end{bmatrix}\begin{vmatrix}R_{2} &:=R_{2} + (-1)R_{1} \\ ∼\begin{bmatrix}1 & 1 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & -1 & 1\end{bmatrix} & R_{1}\end{vmatrix}:=___________; ∼\begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 & a & a \\ 0 & 1 & a & a\end{bmatrix}$

**Explanation**

We write down $A$ and the identity matrix $I$ side-by-side (as shown below) and apply the standard Gauss-Jordan method to transform the matrix $A$ into the identity matrix. We perform the same row operations on both matrices:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\begin{bmatrix}A & I\end{bmatrix} &= \begin{bmatrix}1 & 1 & 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 2 & 0 & 1\end{bmatrix}\begin{vmatrix}R_{2} &:=R_{2} + (-1)R_{1} \\ ∼\begin{bmatrix}1 & 1 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & -1 & 1\end{bmatrix} & R_{1}\end{vmatrix}:=R_{1} + (-1)R_{2} \\
&= ∼\begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 & 2 & -1 \\ 0 & 1 & -1 & 1\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

The matrix $A^{-1}$ is given by the right-hand side of our big matrix after all of the transformations have been completed. Therefore,
$\begin{bmatrix}2 & -1 \\ -1 & 1\end{bmatrix}$

## Determining the Row Operations Required To Find the Inverse of a 2x2 Matrix

**Example:** The inverse of the matrix $A$ is found using row operations, as shown below. What are the row operations corresponding to each step?

$\begin{bmatrix}A & I\end{bmatrix}|= \begin{bmatrix}2 & 6 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 & 1\end{bmatrix}\begin{vmatrix}___________ \\ ∼\begin{bmatrix}1 & 3 & \frac{1}{2} & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 & 1\end{bmatrix}\end{vmatrix}___________; ∼\begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 & \frac{1}{2} & -3 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 & 1\end{bmatrix}$

**Explanation**

We write down $A$ and the identity matrix $I$ side-by-side (as shown below) and apply the standard Gauss-Jordan method to transform the matrix $A$ into the identity matrix. We perform the same row operations on both matrices:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\begin{bmatrix}A & I\end{bmatrix} &= \begin{bmatrix}2 & 6 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 & 1\end{bmatrix}\begin{vmatrix}R_{1} &:=\frac{1}{2}R_{1} \\ ∼\begin{bmatrix}1 & 3 & \frac{1}{2} & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 & 1\end{bmatrix} & R_{1}\end{vmatrix}:=R_{1} + (-3)R_{2} \\
&= ∼\begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 & \frac{1}{2} & -3 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 & 1\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

The corresponding row operations are

$$
R_1:= \dfrac{1}{2}R_1, \qquad R_1:= R_1+(-3)R_2
$$

The matrix $A^{-1}$ is given by the right-hand side of our big matrix after all of the transformations have been completed. Therefore,

$$
\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{2} & -3 \\ 0 & 1\end{bmatrix}
$$
