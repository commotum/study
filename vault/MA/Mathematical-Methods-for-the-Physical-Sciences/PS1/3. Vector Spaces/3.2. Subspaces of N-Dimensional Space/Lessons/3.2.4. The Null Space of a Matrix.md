## Introduction

The **null space** of a matrix $A$ is the set of all the solutions of the equation

$$
A\mathbf{x}= \mathbf{0}
$$

and is denoted by $\textrm{Null}(A)$.

To illustrate, let's consider the following matrix:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}2 & 6 \\ 1 & 3\end{bmatrix}
$$

The null space consists of all the solutions of

$$
A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{0}
$$

For example, the vectors $\begin{bmatrix}-3 \\ 1\end{bmatrix}$ $\begin{bmatrix}6 \\ -2\end{bmatrix}$ and $\begin{bmatrix}-9 \\ 3\end{bmatrix}$ all satisfy the equation

$$
A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{0}
$$

so they are all elements of the null space:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}-3 \\ 1\end{bmatrix}
$$

## Identifying Whether a Vector Lies in the Null Space of a Given Matrix

**Example:** The matrix $A$ and vectors $\mathbf{b}_1$, $\mathbf{b}_2$, $\mathbf{b}_3$ are given below. Which of the vectors lie in $\textrm{Null}(A)$?

$\begin{bmatrix}3 & -6 \\ -5 & 10 \\ -8 & 16\end{bmatrix}$

**Explanation**

A vector $\mathbf{b}$ lies in $\textrm{Null}(A)$ if

$$
A\mathbf{b}=\mathbf{0}
$$

So, we need to compute the product of $A$ and each of the given vectors and check whether it is equal to $\mathbf{0}$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
Ab_{1} &= \begin{bmatrix}3 & -6 \\ -5 & 10 \\ -8 & 16\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}3 \\ 6\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}-27 \\ 45 \\ 72\end{bmatrix} \ne 0\cdot \\
Ab_{2} &= \begin{bmatrix}3 & -6 \\ -5 & 10 \\ -8 & 16\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}6 \\ 3\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}0 \\ 0 \\ 0\end{bmatrix} = 0✓ \\
Ab_{3} &= \begin{bmatrix}3 & -6 \\ -5 & 10 \\ -8 & 16\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 3\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}-15 \\ 25 \\ 40\end{bmatrix} \ne 0\cdot
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, only $\mathbf{b}_2$ lies in $\textrm{Null}(A)$.

## Solve a Variable in a Vector Lying in the Null Space of a Given Matrix

**Example:** Consider the matrix $\begin{bmatrix}3 & 2 & -4 \\ -3 & 3 & -1 \\ 6 & -6 & 2\end{bmatrix}$ and the vector $\begin{bmatrix}2 \\ k \\ 3\end{bmatrix}$ Find the value of $k$ such that $\mathbf{b}\in\textrm{Null}(A)$.

**Explanation**

If $\mathbf{b} \in \textrm{Null}(A)$, then we must have

$$
A\mathbf{b} = \mathbf{0}
$$

Substituting the given information, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
Ab &= 0 \\
\begin{bmatrix}3 & 2 & -4 \\ -3 & 3 & -1 \\ 6 & -6 & 2\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}2 \\ k \\ 3\end{bmatrix} &= \begin{bmatrix}0 \\ 0 \\ 0\end{bmatrix} \\
\begin{bmatrix}6 + 2k - 12 \\ -6 + 3k - 3 \\ 12 - 6k + 6\end{bmatrix} &= \begin{bmatrix}0 \\ 0 \\ 0\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

This corresponds to the system

$$
{2k - 6 = 0; 3k - 9 = 0; - 6k + 18 = 0
$$

and for each of these equations, we obtain $k=3$.

<a id="writing-the-null-space-using-set-builder-notation"></a>
## Writing the Null Space using Set-Builder Notation

Let's again consider the following matrix:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}2 & 6 \\ 1 & 3\end{bmatrix}
$$

To find an expression for the null space using set-builder notation, we need to find all the solutions of

$$
A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{0}
$$

Let's start by considering the augmented matrix $\begin{bmatrix}A\, & \, \mathbf{0}\end{bmatrix}$ and finding its reduced row echelon form as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\begin{bmatrix}A & 0\end{bmatrix} &= \begin{bmatrix}2 & 6 & 0 \\ 1 & 3 & 0\end{bmatrix}\begin{vmatrix}R_{1} &:=\frac{1}{2}R_{1} \\ ∼\begin{bmatrix}1 & 3 & 0 \\ 1 & 3 & 0\end{bmatrix} & R_{2}\end{vmatrix}:=R_{2} + (-1)R_{1} \\
&= ∼\begin{bmatrix}1 & 3 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Note that the reduced matrix above has one pivot column (the $1$st column). The second column is not a pivot column.

Now, we convert the reduced matrix back into a system, as follows:

$$
{x_{1} + 3x_{2} = 0; 0 = 0
$$

Here, $\color{blue}x_1$ is a basic variable (it corresponds to the pivot column), while $\color{red}x_2$ is a free variable (it corresponds to the non-pivot column). Because there is a free variable, the system

$$
A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{0}
$$

has infinitely many solutions.

From the first equation we get

$$
{\color{blue}x_1}=-3{\color{red}x_2}
$$

so the set of all the solutions of

$$
A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{0}
$$

is
$\begin{bmatrix}-3x_{2} \\ x_{2}\end{bmatrix}$

**Note:** It is not necessary to consider the full augmented matrix $\begin{bmatrix}A \, & \, \mathbf{0}\end{bmatrix}$ since the last column won't be changed during the process of reduction. Really, we just need to find the reduced row echelon form of the matrix $A$ and then set up the corresponding system.

<a id="finding-the-null-space-of-a-given-matrix"></a>
## Finding the Null Space of a Given Matrix

**Example:** Consider the matrix $\begin{bmatrix}4 & -12 \\ 3 & -8\end{bmatrix}$ Find $\textrm{Null}(A)$.

**Explanation**

We need to solve the equation

$$
A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{0}
$$

First, we find the row echelon form of $A$ as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= \begin{bmatrix}4 & -12 \\ 3 & -8\end{bmatrix}\begin{vmatrix}R_{1} &:=\frac{1}{4}R_{1} \\ ∼\begin{bmatrix}1 & -3 \\ 3 & -8\end{bmatrix} & R_{2}\end{vmatrix}:=R_{2} + (-3)R_{1} \\
&= ∼\begin{bmatrix}1 & -3 \\ 0 & 1\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

This corresponds to the following system:

$$
{x_{1} - 3x_{2} = 0; x_{2} = 0
$$

The second equation tells us that $x_2=0$. Substituting this into the first equation, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_{1} - 3x_{2} &= 0 \\
x_{1} - 3(0) &= 0 \\
x_{1} &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

So, we conclude that $\begin{bmatrix}0 \\ 0\end{bmatrix}$ is the only solution to this system.

Therefore,

$$
\textrm{Null}(A) = \left\{\mathbf{0} \right\}
$$
