## Matrices Over the Complex Numbers

It's possible to define matrices with complex entries, such as
$\begin{bmatrix}-1 + \textrm{i} & 4 - \textrm{i} \\ 3 + 2\textrm{i} & 1\end{bmatrix}$

In general, $\mathbb{C}^{m \times n}$ is the space of complex matrices with dimension $m \times n$.

Complex matrices work mostly the same as real matrices (we can add them, multiply them, etc). However, complex matrices have a few extra properties:

- We take the complex conjugate of a matrix by taking the complex conjugate of each component:
$\begin{bmatrix}-1 + \textrm{i} & 4 - \textrm{i} \\ 3 + 2\textrm{i} & 1\end{bmatrix}$
- Complex matrices also have a real part and an imaginary part. That means we can write any matrix in the form $A={\color{blue}B}+\textrm{i}\,{\color{red}C}$, where ${\color{blue}B} = {\color{blue}\textrm{Re}(A)}$ and ${\color{red}C} = {\color{red}\textrm{Im}(A)}$.The real part of the matrix contains the real part of each entry, and the imaginary part of the matrix contains the imaginary part of each entry:
$\begin{bmatrix}-1 + \textrm{i} & 4 - \textrm{i} \\ 3 + 2\textrm{i} & 1\end{bmatrix}$

<a id="calculating-the-complex-conjugate-of-a-complex-matrix"></a>
## Calculating the Complex Conjugate of a Complex Matrix

**Example:** Find $B=\overline{A}$, where $\begin{bmatrix}4\textrm{i} & 3 - \textrm{i} & 1 + \textrm{i} \\ 2 - 2\textrm{i} & 2 - \textrm{i} & 1 + 2\textrm{i} \\ \textrm{i} & 3\textrm{i} & 2\textrm{i}\end{bmatrix}$ What is the value of $b_{11} + b_{23}$?

**Explanation**

Taking the complex conjugate of each entry, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
A^(―) &= \begin{bmatrix}4\textrm{i} & 3 - \textrm{i} & 1 + \textrm{i} \\ 2 - 2\textrm{i} & 2 - \textrm{i} & 1 + 2\textrm{i} \\ \textrm{i} & 3\textrm{i} & 2\textrm{i}\end{bmatrix}^(―) \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}4\textrm{i}^(―) & 3 - \textrm{i}^(―) & 1 + \textrm{i}^(―) \\ 2 - 2\textrm{i}^(―) & 2 - \textrm{i}^(―) & 1 + 2\textrm{i}^(―) \\ \textrm{i}^(―) & 3\textrm{i}^(―) & 2\textrm{i}^(―)\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}-4\textrm{i} & 3 + \textrm{i} & 1 - \textrm{i} \\ 2 + 2\textrm{i} & 2 + \textrm{i} & 1 - 2\textrm{i} \\ -\textrm{i} & -3\textrm{i} & -2\textrm{i}\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
b_{11} + b_{23} = (-4\textrm{i}) + (1-2\textrm{i}) = 1-6\textrm{i}
$$

## Computing Components of the Real and Imaginary Parts of a Complex Matrix

**Example:** If $\begin{bmatrix}a & b \\ c & d\end{bmatrix}$ and $\begin{bmatrix}e & f \\ g & h\end{bmatrix}$ are the real and imaginary parts of the matrix $\begin{bmatrix}2 - 3\textrm{i} & -5 - 7\textrm{i} \\ 1 - 6\textrm{i} & 5 - 9\textrm{i}\end{bmatrix}$ then what is the value of $ad+eh$?

**Explanation**

For the given matrix $A$, we have that

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= \begin{bmatrix}2 - 3\textrm{i} & -5 - 7\textrm{i} \\ 1 - 6\textrm{i} & 5 - 9\textrm{i}\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}2 & -5 \\ 1 & 5\end{bmatrix}_(⏟)_(Re(A)) + \textrm{i}\begin{bmatrix}-3 & -7 \\ -6 & -9\end{bmatrix}_(⏟)_(Im(A))
\end{aligned}
$$

So we have

$$
a=2,d=5,e=-3
$$

and $h=-9$. Therefore,

$$
ad+eh=2 \cdot 5 + (-3) \cdot (-9) = 37
$$

<a id="multiplying-complex-matrices"></a>
## Multiplying Complex Matrices

**Example:** If $\begin{bmatrix}-\textrm{i} & \textrm{i} \\ 1 & 2\textrm{i}\end{bmatrix}$ and $\begin{bmatrix}1 + 2\textrm{i} & 1 - \textrm{i} \\ \textrm{i} & 2\end{bmatrix}$, then what is $AB$?

**Explanation**

Multiplying the matrices using the usual method, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
AB &= \begin{bmatrix}-\textrm{i} & \textrm{i} \\ 1 & 2\textrm{i}\end{bmatrix} \cdot \begin{bmatrix}1 + 2\textrm{i} & 1 - \textrm{i} \\ \textrm{i} & 2\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}(-\textrm{i}) \cdot (1 + 2\textrm{i}) + \textrm{i} \cdot \textrm{i} & (-\textrm{i}) \cdot (1 - \textrm{i}) + \textrm{i} \cdot 2 \\ 1 \cdot (1 + 2\textrm{i}) + 2\textrm{i} \cdot \textrm{i} & 1 \cdot (1 - \textrm{i}) + 2\textrm{i} \cdot 2\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}(2 - \textrm{i}) + (-1) & (-1 - \textrm{i}) + 2\textrm{i} \\ (1 + 2\textrm{i}) + (-2) & (1 - \textrm{i}) + 4\textrm{i}\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}1 - \textrm{i} & -1 + \textrm{i} \\ -1 + 2\textrm{i} & 1 + 3\textrm{i}\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Applying Row Operations to a Complex Matrix

**Example:** Given the matrix $\begin{bmatrix}\textrm{i} & -\textrm{i} \\ 2 + 2\textrm{i} & 1\end{bmatrix}$ find the result of applying to $A$ the following elementary row operations:
$R_1:= \dfrac{1}{\textrm{i}}R_1, \qquad R_2:= R_2 - 2\textrm{i}R_1$

**Explanation**

Let's apply the row operations to $A$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= \begin{bmatrix}\textrm{i} & -\textrm{i} \\ 2 + 2\textrm{i} & 1\end{bmatrix}\begin{vmatrix}R_{1}:=\frac{1}{\textrm{i}}R_{1} \\ ∼\begin{bmatrix}1 & -1 \\ 2 + 2\textrm{i} & 1\end{bmatrix}\end{vmatrix}R_{2}:=R_{2} - 2\textrm{i}R_{1} \\
&= ∼\begin{bmatrix}1 & -1 \\ 2 & 1 + 2\textrm{i}\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the result is
$\begin{bmatrix}1 & -1 \\ 2 & 1 + 2\textrm{i}\end{bmatrix}$
