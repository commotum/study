## Introduction

The **normal distribution** has the probability density function

$$
f(x) = \dfrac{1}{\sigma \sqrt{2\pi}} e^{-\frac{1}{2} \left(\frac{x-\mu}{\sigma} \right)^2}
$$

where the $\mu$ represents the mean and $\sigma$ represents the standard deviation. To state that $X$ follows a normal distribution, we write $X \sim N(\mu,\sigma^2)$.

The graph of this probability density function forms a bell curve centered at $x=\mu$.

![](<MA/Mathematical-Methods-for-the-Physical-Sciences/PS1/10. Continuous Probability Distributions/10.1. The Normal Distribution/Source/10.1.4. The Normal Distribution/Images/ed91decbcec8ea6f361ae701047a47f0.png>)

In general, the greater the standard deviation $\sigma$, the flatter the bell curve. Intuitively, this makes sense because the standard deviation represents how "spread out" the distribution is.

## Transforming a Normal Random Variable into a Standard Normal Random Variable

To compute a probability involving a normally distributed variable $X \sim N(\mu, \sigma^2)$, it is convenient to transform $X$ into a standard normal random variable $Z$ by $z$-scoring:

$$
Z = \dfrac{X - \mu}{\sigma}
$$

So, we have

$$
P(X < x) = P \left(Z < \dfrac{x-\mu}{\sigma} \right)
$$

where $Z \sim N(0,1)$ is a standard normal random variable.

For example, suppose we have a random variable $X \sim N(1,2^2)$ and we want to compute

$$
P(X \leq 1.4)
$$

using the following $z$-table:

| $z$ | $\, 0 \,$ | $\, 0.01 \,$ | $\, 0.02 \,$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $\, 0 \,$ | $\, 0.5 \,$ | $\, 0.504 \,$ | $\, 0.508 \,$ |
| $\, 0.1 \,$ | $\, 0.5398 \,$ | $\, 0.5438 \,$ | $\, 0.5478 \,$ |
| $\, 0.2 \,$ | $\, 0.5793 \,$ | $\, 0.5832 \,$ | $\, 0.5871 \,$ |

The $z$-table above shows the cumulative probabilities for a normal random variable $Z \sim N(0,1)$. In order to use the table to compute

$$
P(X \leq 1.4)
$$

we must first transform $X$ into a standard normal random variable $Z$ by $z$-scoring:

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(X \le 1.4) &= P(Z \le \frac{1.4 - 1}{2}) \\
&= P(Z \le 0.2)
\end{aligned}
$$

According to the table, we have

$$
P(Z \leq 0.2) = 0.5793
$$

Therefore,

$$
P(X \leq 1.4) = 0.5793
$$

## Computing the Probability of a Normal Random Variable on an Unbounded Interval

**Example:** The data below is taken from the table of values of the cumulative distribution function $\Phi(z)$ for the standard normal distribution. Given that $X \sim N(2,2^2)$, compute $P(X < 1.24)$.

| $z$ | $\, 0.07 \,$ | $\, 0.08 \,$ | $\, 0.09 \,$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $\, -0.4 \,$ | $\, 0.3192 \,$ | $\, 0.3156 \,$ | $\, 0.3121 \,$ |
| $\, -0.3 \,$ | $\, 0.3557 \,$ | $\, 0.3520 \,$ | $\, 0.3483 \,$ |
| $\, -0.2 \,$ | $\, 0.3936 \,$ | $\, 0.3897 \,$ | $\, 0.3859 \,$ |

**Explanation**

Given a normal random variable $X \sim N(\mu, \sigma^2)$, we have

$$
P(X < x) = P \left(Z < \dfrac{x-\mu}{\sigma} \right)
$$

where $Z \sim N(0,1)$ is a standard normal random variable. In our case, we have $\mu = 2$ and $\sigma = 2$.

First, we transform $X$ into a standard normal random variable $Z$ by $z$-scoring:

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(X < 1.24) &= P(Z < \frac{1.24 - 2}{2}) \\
&= P(Z < - 0.38) \\
&= P(Z \le - 0.38) \\
&= Φ(-0.38)
\end{aligned}
$$

The required probability is represented by the area shown below.

![](<MA/Mathematical-Methods-for-the-Physical-Sciences/PS1/10. Continuous Probability Distributions/10.1. The Normal Distribution/Source/10.1.4. The Normal Distribution/Images/c5a42be64d06c7297fa9cb3c052995a7.png>)

According to the table, we have

$$
\Phi(-0.38) = 0.3520
$$

Therefore, we conclude that

$$
P(X < 1.24) = 0.3520
$$

<a id="computing-the-probability-of-a-normal-random-variable-on-an-unbounded-interval-using-the-complement"></a>
## Computing the Probability of a Normal Random Variable on an Unbounded Interval Using the Complement

**Example:** The data below is taken from the table of values of the cumulative distribution function $\Phi(z)$ for the standard normal distribution. Given that $Y \sim N(5,4^2)$, compute $P(Y \geq 2.92)$.

| $z$ | $\, 0.00 \,$ | $\, 0.01 \,$ | $\, 0.02 \,$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $\, -0.7 \,$ | $\, 0.2420 \,$ | $\, 0.2389 \,$ | $\, 0.2358 \,$ |
| $\, -0.6 \,$ | $\, 0.2743 \,$ | $\, 0.2709 \,$ | $\, 0.2676 \,$ |
| $\, -0.5 \,$ | $\, 0.3085 \,$ | $\, 0.3050 \,$ | $\, 0.3015 \,$ |

**Explanation**

Given a normal random variable $Y \sim N(\mu, \sigma^2)$, we have

$$
P(Y \geq y) = P \left(Z \geq \dfrac{y-\mu}{\sigma} \right)
$$

where $Z \sim N(0,1)$ is a standard normal random variable. In our case, we have $\mu = 5$ and $\sigma = 4$.

First, we transform $Y$ into a standard normal random variable $Z$ by $z$-scoring:

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(Y \ge 2.92) &= P(Z \ge \frac{2.92 - 5}{4}) \\
&= P(Z \ge - 0.52)
\end{aligned}
$$

The required probability is represented by the area shown below.

![](<MA/Mathematical-Methods-for-the-Physical-Sciences/PS1/10. Continuous Probability Distributions/10.1. The Normal Distribution/Source/10.1.4. The Normal Distribution/Images/e696c5f6c514a84f59483d545326ddec.png>)

Now, we express the required probability in terms of $\Phi(z)$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(Z \ge - 0.52) &= 1 - P(Z < - 0.52) \\
&= 1 - P(Z \le - 0.52) \\
&= 1 - Φ(-0.52)
\end{aligned}
$$

From the table, we know that

$$
\Phi(-0.52) =0.3015
$$

Therefore, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(Z \ge - 0.52) &= 1 - Φ(-0.52) \\
&= 1 - 0.3015 \\
&= 0.6985
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we conclude that

$$
P(Y \geq 2.92) = 0.6985
$$

## Computing the Probability of a Normal Random Variable on a Bounded Interval

**Example:** The data below is taken from the table of values of the cumulative distribution function $\Phi(z)$ for the standard normal distribution. Given that $X \sim N(1,9)$, compute $P(-1.13 < X < 3.25)$.

| $z$$\, 0.00 \,$$\, 0.01 \,$$\, 0.02 \,$$\, -0.9 \,$ $\, 0.1841 \,$ $\, 0.1814 \,$ $\, 0.1788 \,$ $\, -0.8 \,$ $\, 0.2119 \,$ $\, 0.2090 \,$ $\, 0.2061 \,$ $\, -0.7 \,$ $\, 0.2420 \,$ $\, 0.2389 \,$ $\, 0.2358 \,$ |  | $z$$\, 0.03 \,$$\, 0.04 \,$$\, 0.05 \,$$\, 0.5 \,$ $\, 0.7019 \,$ $\, 0.7054 \,$ $\, 0.7088 \,$ $\, 0.6 \,$ $\, 0.7357 \,$ $\, 0.7389 \,$ $\, 0.7422 \,$ $\, 0.7 \,$ $\, 0.7673 \,$ $\, 0.7704 \,$ $\, 0.7734 \,$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: |

**Explanation**

Given a normal random variable $X \sim N(\mu, \sigma^2)$, we have

$$
P(a < X < b) = P \left(\dfrac{a-\mu}{\sigma} < Z < \dfrac{b-\mu}{\sigma} \right)
$$

where $Z \sim N(0,1)$ is a standard normal random variable. In our case, we have $\mu = 1$ and

$$
\sigma = \sqrt 9 = 3
$$

First, we transform $X$ into a standard normal random variable $Z$ by $z$-scoring:

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(-1.13 < X < 3.25) &= P(\frac{-1.13 - 1}{3} < Z < \frac{3.25 - 1}{3}) \\
&= P(-0.71 < Z < 0.75)
\end{aligned}
$$

The required probability is represented by the area shown below.

![](<MA/Mathematical-Methods-for-the-Physical-Sciences/PS1/10. Continuous Probability Distributions/10.1. The Normal Distribution/Source/10.1.4. The Normal Distribution/Images/9082689cdb56dc5b0cdb463ce1690c15.png>)

Now, we express the required probability in terms of $\Phi(z)$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(-0.71 < Z < 0.75) &= P(Z < 0.75) - P(Z \le - 0.71) \\
&= P(Z \le 0.75) - P(Z \le - 0.71) \\
&= Φ(0.75) - Φ(-0.71)
\end{aligned}
$$

From the table, we have the following:

$$
\begin{aligned}
Φ(-0.71) &= 0.2389 \\
Φ(0.75) &= 0.7734
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(-0.71 < Z < 0.75) &= Φ(0.75) - Φ(-0.71) \\
&= 0.7734 - 0.2389 \\
&= 0.5345
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we conclude that

$$
P(-1.13< X < 3.25) = 0.5345
$$
