## Introduction

Suppose that the base of a solid is the region enclosed by the line

$$
y=-2x+2
$$

and the $x$-axis for

$$
0 \leq x \leq 1
$$

as shown below. If cross-sections perpendicular to the $x$-axis are semicircles, how do we calculate the volume of the solid?

![](<../Source/10.2.4. Volumes of Solids with Circular Cross Sections/Images/036b037bf71f038c34597060433516cc.png>)

A sketch with a few cross-sections is shown in the picture below.

![](<../Source/10.2.4. Volumes of Solids with Circular Cross Sections/Images/46bd3268b31bdb0d428f3cb5cf115040.png>)

Consider a cross-section at some point $x$, where $x \in (0,1)$, and its projection onto the $xy$-plane:

![](<../Source/10.2.4. Volumes of Solids with Circular Cross Sections/Images/a0d0d5d3ed9d43177bf3c6c7d1709f5b.png>)

If we project the rectangular cross-section onto the $xy$-plane, we get the picture below:

![](<../Source/10.2.4. Volumes of Solids with Circular Cross Sections/Images/a9eac2941dbed8b9dc13141037b78ad8.png>)

Then, the base of the cross-section is

$$
\begin{aligned}
d &= \begin{vmatrix}y|= & -2x + 2\end{vmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Given the diameter $d$ of a semicircle, we can find the corresponding area using the formula

$$
A = \overbrace{\dfrac{1}{2} \cdot \underbrace{\dfrac{1}{4}\pi d^2}_{\textrm{Full circle}}}^{\textrm{Semicircle}} = \dfrac{1}{8}\pi d^2
$$

So, the expression for the area of the cross-section is

$$
\begin{aligned}
A(x) &= \frac{1}{8}πd^{2} \\
&= \frac{π}{8} \mid - 2x + 2 \mid ^{2} \\
&= \frac{π}{8}(4x^{2} - 8x + 4) \\
&= \frac{π}{2}(1 - 2x + x^{2})
\end{aligned}
$$

We can now calculate the volume of the solid, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
V &= ∫_{a}^{b}A(x)dx \\
&= ∫_{0}^{1}\frac{π}{2}(1 - 2x + x^{2})dx \\
&= \frac{π}{2}∫_{0}^{1}(1 - 2x + x^{2})dx \\
&= \frac{π}{2}(x - x^{2} + \frac{x^{3}}{3}) \mid _{0}^{1} \\
&= \frac{π}{2}[(1 - 1 + \frac{1}{3}) - 0] \\
&= \frac{π}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{3} \\
&= \frac{π}{6}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Computing the Volume When the Base Is Bounded by a Curve and the X-Axis

**Example:** The base of a solid is the region enclosed by the curve $y=2-x^2$ and the $x$-axis for $-\sqrt{2} \leq x \leq \sqrt{2}$, as shown below. If cross-sections perpendicular to the $x$-axis are semicircles, find an integral that gives the volume of the solid.

![](<../Source/10.2.4. Volumes of Solids with Circular Cross Sections/Images/2b2f25c5ad33affa989e3fc2134a2098.png>)

**Explanation**

Consider a cross-section at some point $x$, where $x \in \big(-\sqrt{2},\sqrt{2}\big)$, and its projection onto the $xy$-plane:

![](<../Source/10.2.4. Volumes of Solids with Circular Cross Sections/Images/fc064fe4b89201501f886ae5eb9eb94c.png>)

Then, the base of the cross-section is

$$
\begin{aligned}
d &= \begin{vmatrix}y|= & 2 - x^{2}\end{vmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Given the diameter $d$ of a semicircle, the corresponding area can be found using the formula

$$
A = \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot \dfrac{1}{4}\pi d^2 = \dfrac{1}{8}\pi d^2
$$

So, the expression for the area of the cross-section is

$$
\begin{aligned}
A(x) &= \frac{1}{8}πd^{2} \\
&= \frac{π}{8} \mid 2 - x^{2} \mid ^{2} \\
&= \frac{π}{8}(4 - 4x^{2} + x^{4})
\end{aligned}
$$

We can now write the volume of the solid, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
V &= ∫_{a}^{b}A(x)dx \\
&= \frac{π}{8}∫_{-\sqrt{2}}^{\sqrt{2}}(4 - 4x^{2} + x^{4})dx
\end{aligned}
$$

## Computing the Volume When the Base Is Bounded by Two Curves

**Example:** The base of a solid is the region enclosed by the curve $y=2-x^2$ and the line $y=x$, as shown in the picture. If cross-sections perpendicular to the $x$-axis are semicircles, find an integral that gives the volume of the solid.

![](<../Source/10.2.4. Volumes of Solids with Circular Cross Sections/Images/a41c2ec8dd8a21ea9953da1ed647c264.png>)

**Explanation**

Let

$$
f(x)=2-x^2
$$

and $g(x)=x$.

First, we determine the limits of integration. To do that, we find the intersections of the two curves by setting the functions equal to one another and solving for $x$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
f(x) &= g(x) \\
2 - x^{2} &= x \\
x^{2} + x - 2 &= 0 \\
(x - 1)(x + 2) &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

Hence, the solutions are $x=-2$ and $x=1$.

Now, consider a cross-section at some point $x$, where $x \in (-2,1)$, and its projection onto the $xy$-plane:

![](<../Source/10.2.4. Volumes of Solids with Circular Cross Sections/Images/85620a347c6cddc88070aa8855c2ce87.png>)

Then, the base of the cross-section is

$$
\begin{aligned}
d &= \mid f(x) - g(x) \mid \\
&= \mid (2 - x^{2}) - (x) \mid \\
&= \mid - x^{2} - x + 2 \mid
\end{aligned}
$$

Given the diameter $d$ of a semicircle, the corresponding area can be found using the formula

$$
A = \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot \dfrac{1}{4}\pi d^2 = \dfrac{1}{8}\pi d^2
$$

So, the expression for the area of the cross-section is

$$
\begin{aligned}
A(x) &= \frac{1}{8}πd^{2} \\
&= \frac{π}{8}(-x^{2} - x + 2)^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

We can now write the volume of the solid, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
V &= ∫_{a}^{b}A(x)dx \\
&= ∫_{-2}^{1}\frac{π}{8}(-x^{2} - x + 2)^{2}dx \\
&= \frac{π}{8}∫_{-2}^{1}(-x^{2} - x + 2)^{2}dx
\end{aligned}
$$

## Cross-Sections Perpendicular to the Y-Axis

Now, let's suppose that the base of a solid is the region below enclosed by the line

$$
x=y+2
$$

and the $y$-axis for

$$
-2 \leq y \leq 0
$$

Given that cross-sections perpendicular to the $y$-axis are semicircles, how do we calculate the volume of the solid?

![](<../Source/10.2.4. Volumes of Solids with Circular Cross Sections/Images/1a162b343fb0b5d88f298eea189ad69e.png>)

A sketch with a few cross-sections is shown in the picture below:

![](<../Source/10.2.4. Volumes of Solids with Circular Cross Sections/Images/c4de65e03352ed6d7deba328d5e0580a.png>)

Now, consider the picture with only one cross-section that passes through some point $y \in (-2,0)$:

![](<../Source/10.2.4. Volumes of Solids with Circular Cross Sections/Images/e1309d6727079050161f38e0c663b341.png>)

The projection of the previous picture on the $xy$-plane is given below:

![](<../Source/10.2.4. Volumes of Solids with Circular Cross Sections/Images/5d86770eab2428f845ca10c99f4ed720.png>)

Then, the base of the cross-section is

$$
\begin{aligned}
d &= \begin{vmatrix}y|= & y + 2\end{vmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Given the diameter $d$ of a semicircle, we can find the corresponding area using the formula

$$
A = \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot \dfrac{1}{4}\pi d^2 = \dfrac{1}{8}\pi d^2
$$

So, the expression for the area of the cross-section is

$$
\begin{aligned}
A(y) &= \frac{1}{8}πd^{2} \\
&= \frac{π}{8} \mid y + 2 \mid ^{2} \\
&= \frac{π}{8}(y^{2} + 4y + 4)
\end{aligned}
$$

We can now calculate the volume of the solid, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
V &= ∫_{c}^{d}A(y)dy \\
&= ∫_{-2}^{0}\frac{π}{8}(y^{2} + 4y + 4)dy \\
&= \frac{π}{8}∫_{-2}^{0}(y^{2} + 4y + 4)dy \\
&= \frac{π}{8}(\frac{y^{3}}{3} + 2y^{2} + 4y) \mid _{-2}^{0} \\
&= \frac{π}{8}[0 - (-\frac{8}{3} + 8 - 8)] \\
&= \frac{π}{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Computing the Volume  When the Cross Sections Are Perpendicular to the y-Axis

**Example:** The base of a solid is the region enclosed by the line $x=-3y$ and the $y$-axis for $0 \leq y \leq 1$, as shown in the picture. Given that cross-sections perpendicular to the $y$-axis are semicircles, calculate the volume of the solid.

![](<../Source/10.2.4. Volumes of Solids with Circular Cross Sections/Images/cbaea9d7e0c7a69f0955a3613031df07.png>)

**Explanation**

Consider a cross-section at some point $y$, where $y \in (0,1)$, and its projection on the $xy$-plane:

![](<../Source/10.2.4. Volumes of Solids with Circular Cross Sections/Images/bc0b0e9632c93ea5516dbdd0f893ad79.png>)

Then the base of the cross-section is

$$
\begin{aligned}
d &= \mid x \mid \\
&= \mid - 3y \mid \\
&= 3y
\end{aligned}
$$

Given the diameter $d$ of a semicircle, the corresponding area can be found using the formula

$$
A = \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot \dfrac{1}{4}\pi d^2 = \dfrac{1}{8}\pi d^2
$$

So, the expression for the area of the cross-section is

$$
\begin{aligned}
A(y) &= \frac{π}{8}d^{2} \\
&= \frac{π}{8}(3y)^{2} \\
&= \frac{9π}{8}y^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

We can now calculate the volume of the solid, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
V &= ∫_{c}^{d}A(y)dy \\
&= \frac{9π}{8}∫_{0}^{1}y^{2}dy \\
&= \frac{3π}{8} \cdot (y^{3}) \mid _{0}^{1} \\
&= \frac{3π}{8}(1 - 0) \\
&= \frac{3π}{8}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Computing the Volume When the Base Is Bounded by Two Curves

**Example:** The base of a solid is the region enclosed by the curves $x=-y^2$ and $x=y^2-2$, as shown in the picture. Given that cross-sections perpendicular to the $y$-axis are quarter-circles, calculate the volume of the solid.

![](<../Source/10.2.4. Volumes of Solids with Circular Cross Sections/Images/a7a2c6fd589866e38bf97ae07875b577.png>)

**Explanation**

Let

$$
f(y)=-y^2
$$

and $g(y)=y^2-2$.

First, we determine the limits of integration. To do that, we find the intersections of the two curves by setting the functions equal and solving for $y$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
f(y) &= g(y) \\
- y^{2} &= y^{2} - 2 \\
2y^{2} &= 2 \\
y &= ± 1
\end{aligned}
$$

Hence, the solutions are $y= \pm 1$.

Now, consider a cross-section at some point $y$, where $y \in (-1,1)$, and its projection on the $xy$-plane:

![](<../Source/10.2.4. Volumes of Solids with Circular Cross Sections/Images/ff80c5ebb11c217239379342a143e6c1.png>)

Then, the base of the cross-section is

$$
\begin{aligned}
r &= \mid f - g \mid \\
&= \mid (-y^{2}) - (y^{2} - 2) \mid \\
&= \mid - 2y^{2} + 2 \mid
\end{aligned}
$$

Given the radius $r$ of a quarter-circle, we can find the corresponding area using the formula

$$
A = \dfrac{1}{4}\pi r^2
$$

So, the expression for the area of the cross-section is

$$
\begin{aligned}
A(y) &= \frac{1}{4}πr^{2} \\
&= \frac{π}{4} \cdot (-2y^{2} + 2)^{2} \\
&= \frac{π}{4}(4y^{4} - 8y^{2} + 4) \\
&= π(y^{4} - 2y^{2} + 1)
\end{aligned}
$$

We can now calculate the volume of the solid, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
V &= ∫_{c}^{d}A(y)dy \\
&= ∫_{-1}^{1}π(y^{4} - 2y^{2} + 1)dy \\
&= π∫_{-1}^{1}(y^{4} - 2y^{2} + 1)dy \\
&= 2π∫_{0}^{1}(y^{4} - 2y^{2} + 1)dy \\
&= 2π(\frac{y^{5}}{5} - \frac{2}{3}y^{3} + y) \mid _{0}^{1} \\
&= 2π[(\frac{1}{5} - \frac{2}{3} + 1) - 0] \\
&= 2π \cdot \frac{8}{15} \\
&= \frac{16π}{15}
\end{aligned}
$$
