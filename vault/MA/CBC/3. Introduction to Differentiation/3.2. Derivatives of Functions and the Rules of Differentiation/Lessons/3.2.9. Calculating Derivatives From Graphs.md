## Introduction

Consider the graph of the function $y = f(x)$ shown below.

![](<../Source/3.2.9. Calculating Derivatives From Graphs/Images/a79d541e880f48f10719a568b02836d4.png>)

Let's use our knowledge of derivatives to find $f'(0)$, the derivative of $f$ at $x=0$.

Note the following:

- $f'(0)$ represents the slope of the tangent to $y=f(x)$ at the point $x=0$.
- The function $y = f(x)$ is made up of line segments.
- So, to find $f'(0)$, we simply need to find the slope of $y = f(x)$ over an interval that contains $x=0$, and where $f$ is represented by a single line segment.

With that in mind, we consider the graph of $y=f(x)$ over $x\in [-2,1]$.

![](<../Source/3.2.9. Calculating Derivatives From Graphs/Images/c0580267ce2c244f407adc0127ce17d9.png>)

This interval contains $x=0$, and $f$ is represented by a single line segment over this interval.

The slope of $y=f(x)$ over this interval is equal to

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{Δf}{Δx} &= (2 - 1)/(1 - (-2)) = \frac{1}{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
f'(0)=\dfrac{1}{3}
$$

## Calculating the Derivative of a Function From Its Graph

**Example:** The graph of the function $y = f(x)$ is given above. Find $f'\left(\dfrac32\right)$.

![](<../Source/3.2.9. Calculating Derivatives From Graphs/Images/abe66489d05fd250027ac965d13113c8.png>)

**Explanation**

To compute

$$
f'\left(\dfrac32\right)
$$

we need to find the slope of the tangent to $f(x)$ at

$$
x=\dfrac32
$$

Let's consider the graph of $y=f(x)$ on $x\in [1,2]$. Notice that

$$
x=\dfrac32
$$

lies in this interval.

![](<../Source/3.2.9. Calculating Derivatives From Graphs/Images/bddea9186b1d9ddf86f3bf2672bbcd36.png>)

The slope of $y=f(x)$ on $x\in(1,2)$ is constant and equals

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{Δf}{Δx} &= \frac{0 - 2}{2 - 1} = \frac{-2}{1} =-2
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
f'\left(\dfrac32\right)=-2
$$

## Computing the Derivative of a Linear Sum of Functions

**Example:** The graphs of the functions $y = f(x)$ and $y=g(x)$ are given above. Find $h'(-1)$ where $h(x)=f(x)+g(x)$.

![](<../Source/3.2.9. Calculating Derivatives From Graphs/Images/36fb139722771111b1f567de83a5ef7e.png>)

**Explanation**

The addition rule for derivatives gives

$$
h'(x) = f'(x) + g'(x)
$$

As a result, we have that

$$
h'(-1) =f'(-1) + g'(-1)
$$

Also, notice that $x=-1$ corresponds to

- the interval $x\in(-2,1)$ for the function $f$, and
- the interval $x\in(-2,0)$ for the function $g$,

as shown below.

![](<../Source/3.2.9. Calculating Derivatives From Graphs/Images/9a5f87b5ba64614056ff71db4da844c7.png>)

- The slope of $y=f(x)$ is constant on $x\in(-2,1)$ and equals
$\dfrac{\Delta f}{\Delta x} = \dfrac{1-(-1)}{1-(-2)} = \dfrac{2}{3}$.
Therefore, $f'(-1)=\dfrac{2}{3}$.
- The slope of $y=g(x)$ is constant on $x\in(-2,0)$ and equals
$\dfrac{\Delta g}{\Delta x} = \dfrac{-1-2}{0-(-2)} = -\dfrac{3}{2}$.
Therefore, $g'(-1)=-\dfrac{3}{2}$.

Finally, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
h^{′}(-1) &= f^{′}(-1) + g^{′}(-1) \\
&= \frac{2}{3} - \frac{3}{2} \\
&=-\frac{5}{6}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Computing the Derivative of a Product of Functions

**Example:** The graphs of the functions $y=f(x)$ and $y=g(x)$ are given above. Find $h'(0)$ where $h(x)=f(x)g(x)$.

![](<../Source/3.2.9. Calculating Derivatives From Graphs/Images/2b67c1252910191313060f3adfeb1904.png>)

**Explanation**

The product rule for derivatives gives

$$
h'(x) = f'(x)\cdot g(x)+f(x)\cdot g'(x)
$$

As a result, we have that

$$
h'(0)=f'(0)\cdot g(0)+f(0)\cdot g'(0)
$$

From the graphs above, we obtain that

$$
f(0)=1
$$

and $g(0)=-1$.

Additionally,

- The slope of $y=f(x)$ on $x\in(-1,1)$ equals $-1$. So, $f'(0)=-1$.
- The slope of $y=g(x)$ on $x\in(-0.5,1)$ equals $2$. So, $g'(0)=2$.

Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
h^{′}(0) &= f^{′}(0) \cdot g(0) + f(0) \cdot g^{′}(0) \\
&= (-1) \cdot (-1) + 1 \cdot 2 \\
&= 3
\end{aligned}
$$

## Computing the Derivative of a Quotient of Functions

**Example:** Given the plots of $y=f(x)$ and $y=g(x)$ above, find $h'(2)$ where $h(x)=\dfrac{f(x)}{g(x)}$.

![](<../Source/3.2.9. Calculating Derivatives From Graphs/Images/f542de811e465d54bf83b21ffde6a218.png>)

**Explanation**

The quotient rule for derivatives gives

$$
h'(x) = \dfrac{f'(x)\cdot g(x)-f(x)\cdot g'(x)}{(g(x))^2}
$$

As a result, we have that

$$
h'(2) = \dfrac{f'(2)\cdot g(2)-f(2)\cdot g'(2)}{(g(2))^2}
$$

From the graph above, we obtain that

$$
f(2)=1
$$

and $g(2)=0.5$.

Additionally:

- The slope of $y=f(x)$ on $x\in(1,3)$ equals $-1$. So, $f'(2)=-1$.
- The slope of $y=g(x)$ on $x\in(1,3)$ equals $-0.5$. So, $g'(2)=-0.5$.

Finally, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
h^{′}(2) &= (f^{′}(2) \cdot g(2) - f(2) \cdot g^{′}(2))/((g(2))^{2}) \\
&= ((-1) \cdot (0.5) - (1) \cdot (-0.5))/((0.5)^{2}) \\
&= 0
\end{aligned}
$$
