## Introduction

Below are the graphs of the two most important trigonometric functions, $\sin(x)$ and $\cos(x)$.

![](<../Source/1.3.6. Limits of Trigonometric Functions/Images/d962d51b5796abe211d70e85f72308e2.png>)

For any *finite* real number $a$ we have

$$
\lim_\limits{x\to a}\sin x=\sin (a) \qquad\text{and}\qquad\lim_\limits{x\to a}\cos x=\cos (a)
$$

For example,

$$
\begin{aligned}
lim_(x → π/2)\sin x &= \sin (\frac{π}{2}) = 1 \\
lim_(x → 2π/3)\cos x &= \cos (\frac{2π}{3}) =-0.5
\end{aligned}
$$

However, the limits at infinity do not exist. As $x$ approaches $\infty$ or $-\infty$, the values of $\sin(x)$ and $\cos(x)$ oscillate between $1$ and $-1$ forever. So, we have

$$
\lim_\limits{x\to \pm \infty} \sin x=\text{DNE}\qquad\text{and}\qquad \lim_\limits{x\to \pm \infty} \cos x=\text{DNE}
$$

<a id="computing-the-limit-of-sine-or-cosine"></a>
## Computing the Limit of Sine or Cosine

**Example:** Calculate $\lim_\limits{x\to \pi/8} \left(1 + 2\sin \left(2x\right) \right)^2$.

**Explanation**

Substituting

$$
x=\dfrac{\pi}{8}
$$

directly into the limit, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
lim_(x → π/8)(1 + 2\sin (2x))^{2} &= (1 + 2\sin (2 \cdot \frac{π}{8}))^{2} \\
&= (1 + 2\sin (\frac{π}{4}))^{2} \\
&= (1 + 2 \cdot \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2})^{2} \\
&= (1 + \sqrt{2})^{2} \\
&= 1^{2} + 2 \cdot 1 \cdot \sqrt{2} + (\sqrt{2})^{2} \\
&= 1 + 2\sqrt{2} + 2 \\
&= 3 + 2\sqrt{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Computing the Limit at Infinity of a Sine or Cosine Function

**Example:** Calculate $\lim_\limits{x \rightarrow \infty} \sin\left(2x\right)$.

**Explanation**

Let's sketch the graph of

$$
y = \sin\left(2x\right)
$$

![](<../Source/1.3.6. Limits of Trigonometric Functions/Images/426582953ef13630ef33c1642309abdb.png>)

As $x$ increases, the graph oscillates between the values $y=-1$ and $y=1$ indefinitely. Therefore,

$$
\lim_{x\to \infty} \sin\left(2x\right)= \mathrm{DNE.}
$$

## Limits of Tangent

Below is the graph of the tangent function $y=\tan(x)$.

![](<../Source/1.3.6. Limits of Trigonometric Functions/Images/3418e4b86e53f70090a9f448c5c151fe.png>)

Note that $\tan x$ is not defined at the vertical asymptotes

$$
x= \dfrac{\pi}{2} + n\pi
$$

where $n$ is an integer. However, everywhere else, the limit of the tangent function is the value of the tangent function:

$$
\lim_\limits{x \to a} \tan (x)=\tan\left(a\right),\qquad a\neq \dfrac{\pi}{2} + n\pi
$$

For example,

$$
lim_(x → π/6)\tan (x) = \tan (\frac{π}{6}) = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{3}
$$

However, the limits at infinity do not exist. As $x$ approaches $\infty$ or $-\infty$, the values of $\tan(x)$ keep oscillating, and we have

$$
\lim_\limits{x\to \pm \infty} \tan x=\text{DNE}
$$

---

<a id="computing-the-limit-of-tangent"></a>
## Computing the Limit of Tangent

**Example:** Find $\lim_\limits{x \to \sqrt{\pi}/2}\tan\left(2x^2-\dfrac{\pi}{4}\right)$.

**Explanation**

Substituting

$$
x=\dfrac{\sqrt \pi}{2}
$$

directly into the limit, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
lim_(x → \sqrt{π}/2)\tan (2x^{2} - \frac{π}{4}) &= \tan (2(\frac{\sqrt{π}}{2})^{2} - \frac{π}{4}) \\
&= \tan (2(\frac{π}{4}) - \frac{π}{4}) \\
&= \tan (\frac{π}{2} - \frac{π}{4}) \\
&= \tan (\frac{π}{4}) \\
&= 1
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="limits-at-the-vertical-asymptotes-of-tangent"></a>
## Limits at the Vertical Asymptotes of Tangent

Let's take another look at the graph of the tangent function $y=\tan(x)$.

![](<../Source/1.3.6. Limits of Trigonometric Functions/Images/1b54f3968f256b56fc40096461b0bdc2.png>)

As we can see from the graph,

$$
x = \pm \dfrac{\pi}{2}, \pm \dfrac{3\pi}{2}, \ldots
$$

are the points where $y=\tan (x)$ has vertical asymptotes. Both the left and right-sided limits at those points are infinite. In particular,

$$
\lim_\limits{x\to \pi/2^{-}}\tan(x)=\infty,\qquad \lim_\limits{x\to \pi/2^{+}}\tan(x)=-\infty
$$

Since the left and right-sided limits are not equal, we have

$$
\lim_\limits{x\to \pi/2}\tan(x)=\textrm{DNE}
$$

In general, at any vertical asymptote

$$
x= \dfrac{\pi}{2} + n\pi
$$

where $n$ is an integer, the limit is undefined:

$$
\lim_\limits{x\to \pi/2+n\pi}\tan(x)=\textrm{DNE}
$$

<a id="computing-a-limit-at-a-vertical-asymptote-of-tangent"></a>
## Computing a Limit at a Vertical Asymptote of Tangent

**Example:** Evaluate $\lim_\limits {x \to (-\pi/6)^{-}} \tan\left(x-\dfrac\pi 3 \right)$.

**Explanation**

First, let's try direct substitution:

$$
\begin{aligned}
lim_(x → (- π/6)^{-})\tan (x - \frac{π}{3}) &= lim_(x → (- π/6)^{-})(\sin (x - \frac{π}{3}))/(\cos (x - \frac{π}{3})) \\
&= (\sin (-\frac{π}{6} - \frac{π}{3}))/(\cos (-\frac{π}{6} - \frac{π}{3})) \\
&= (\sin (-\frac{π}{2}))/(\cos (-\frac{π}{2})) \\
&= \frac{-1}{0}
\end{aligned}
$$

which is not defined.

Therefore, to find the limit, let's sketch the graph of

$$
y = \tan\left(x-\dfrac\pi 3 \right)
$$

![](<../Source/1.3.6. Limits of Trigonometric Functions/Images/07a53f0e950a0a619d79cd44c38397de.png>)

We see that as $x$ approaches

$$
-\dfrac\pi 6
$$

from the left, the values of

$$
y=\tan\left(x-\dfrac\pi 3 \right)
$$

are positive and increase without bound. Therefore, we conclude that

$$
\lim_\limits {x \to (-\pi/6)^{-}} \tan\left(x-\dfrac\pi 3 \right)= \infty
$$
