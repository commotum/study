## Introduction

Suppose we want to compute $\lim\limits_{x \to \, 1} f(x)$ for the piecewise function $f(x)$ given below.

$$
{x^{3}\begin{vmatrix}forx < 1 \\ 2\end{vmatrix}forx = 1; - (x - 1)^{3} + 3 \mid forx > 1
$$

We begin by sketching a graph of the function:

![](<../Source/1.3.9. Limits of Piecewise Functions/Images/ccc761bee442132a07fcdcca2dd26418.png>)

To compute $\lim\limits_{x \to \, 1} f(x)$, we must compute the left and right-sided limits separately.

- Approaching from the left, we have $x<1$, so we use the expression $f(x) = x^3$. We get
$lim_(x → 1^{-})f(x)|= lim_(x → 1^{-})x^{3}; = 1^{3}; = 1$.
- Approaching from the right, we have $x>1$, so we use the expression $f(x)=-(x-1)^3+3$. We get
$lim_(x → 1^{+})f(x)|= lim_(x → 1^{+}) - (x - 1)^{3} + 3; = - (1 - 1)^{3} + 3; = 3$.

Because the left and right-sided limits are not equal, the overall limit does not exist:

$$
\lim\limits_{x \to \, 1} f(x) = \text{DNE}
$$

## Computing the One-Sided Limits of a Piecewise Function when the Limits Coincide

**Example:** Find the left- and right-sided limits at $x=1$ of the piecewise function $f(x)$ defined by
${6e^{x - 1}\begin{vmatrix}forx < 1 \\ x^{2} + 2x + 3\end{vmatrix}forx > 1$.

**Explanation**

Approaching from the left, we have $x<1$, so we use the expression

$$
f(x) = 6e^{x-1}
$$

We get

$$
\begin{aligned}
lim_(x → 1^{-})f(x) &= lim_(x → 1^{-})6e^{x - 1} \\
&= 6e^{1 - 1} \\
&= 6e^{0} \\
&= 6
\end{aligned}
$$

Approaching from the right, we have $x>1$, so we use the expression

$$
f(x) = x^2+2x+3
$$

We get

$$
\begin{aligned}
lim_(x → 1^{+})f(x) &= lim_(x → 1^{+})x^{2} + 2x + 3 \\
&= 1^{2} + 2(1) + 3 \\
&= 6
\end{aligned}
$$

## Computing the One-Sided Limits of a Piecewise Function when the Limits Do Not Coincide

**Example:** Find the left-sided and right-sided limits at $x=0$ for the function
$f(x) = {-x^{2} + 3\begin{vmatrix}forx < 0 \\ \sqrt{x}\end{vmatrix}forx \ge 0$.

**Explanation**

Approaching from the left, we have $x < 0$, so we use the expression

$$
f(x)=-x^2+3
$$

We get

$$
\begin{aligned}
lim_(x → 0^{-})f(x) &= lim_(x → 0^{-})(-x^{2} + 3) \\
&= - (0)^{2} + 3 \\
&= 3
\end{aligned}
$$

Approaching from the right, we have $x>0$, so we use the expression

$$
f(x)=\sqrt{x}
$$

We get

$$
\begin{aligned}
lim_(x → 0^{+})f(x) &= lim_(x → 0^{+})\sqrt{x} \\
&= \sqrt{0} \\
&= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

## Limits of Piecewise Functions

Suppose we want to find $\lim_\limits{x\rightarrow 0}f(x)$ for the piecewise function given by

$$
f(x) = {3^{x + 1}\begin{vmatrix}forx < 0 \\ \sqrt{x^{2} + 9}\end{vmatrix}forx \ge 0
$$

To get an idea of the behavior of the function around $x=0$, we can plot the function.

![](<../Source/1.3.9. Limits of Piecewise Functions/Images/4028b4e29449a9b6737cb607570ae5ab.png>)

To compute $\lim\limits_{x \to \, 0} f(x)$, we must compute the left and right-sided limits separately.

- Approaching from the left, we have $x < 0$, so we use the expression $f(x)=3^{x+1}$. We get
$lim_(x → 0^{-})f(x)|= lim_(x → 0^{-})3^{x + 1}; = 3^{0 + 1}; = 3$.
- Approaching from the right, we have $x>0$, so we use the expression $f(x)=\sqrt {x^2+9}$. We get
$lim_(x → 0^{+})f(x)|= lim_(x → 0^{+})\sqrt{x^{2} + 9}; = \sqrt{0^{2} + 9}; = 3$.

The left and right-sided limits both exist and are equal. So, the overall limit is

$$
\lim\limits_{x \to \, 0} f(x) =3
$$

## Computing Limits of Piecewise Functions with a Gap in the Domain

**Example:** Find $\lim_\limits{x\rightarrow 1}g(x)$ for the function given by
${\frac{\sqrt{4x^{2} + 5}}{3x - 7}\begin{vmatrix}forx \le 1 \\ (x - 2)^{2} - 1\end{vmatrix}forx \ge 2$.

**Explanation**

To compute $\lim\limits_{x \to \, 1} g(x)$, we must compute the left and right-sided limits separately.

However, the right-sided limit $\lim_\limits{x\rightarrow 1^+}g(x)$ does not exist since $g(x)$ is not defined in the interval $x\in(1,2)$.

Consequently, the overall limit does not exist either:

$$
\lim_\limits{x\rightarrow 1}g(x) = \text{DNE}
$$

<a id="solving-for-a-constant-such-that-the-limit-of-a-piecewise-function-exists"></a>
## Solving for a Constant Such that the Limit of a Piecewise Function Exists

**Example:** For the piecewise function
$f(x) = {4x^{3} - 2\begin{vmatrix}forx < 1 \\ 3x + b\end{vmatrix}forx > 1$,
it is known that $\lim_\limits{x\rightarrow 1}f(x)$ exists. What is the value of the constant $b$?

**Explanation**

In order for $\lim_\limits{x\rightarrow 1}f(x)$ to exist, the left and right-sided limits must both exist and be equal.

- Approaching from the left, we have $x < 1$, so we use the expression $f(x)=4x^3-2$. We get
$lim_(x → 1^{-})f(x)|= lim_(x → 1^{-})4x^{3} - 2; = 4(1)^{3} - 2; = 2$.
- Approaching from the right, we have $x>1$, so we use the expression $f(x)= 3x+b$. We get
$lim_(x → 1^{+})f(x)|= lim_(x → 1^{+})3x + b; = 3(1) + b; = 3 + b$.

Finally, since the left and right-sided limits must be equal, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
lim_(x → 1^{-})f(x) &= lim_(x → 1^{+})f(x) \\
2 &= 3 + b \\
b &= -1
\end{aligned}
$$
