## Introduction

Suppose we want to find all of the solutions to the equation

$$
0^{∘} \le x < 360^{∘}
$$

We can immediately calculate a solution, called the **principal value**, as follows:

$$
x = \arctan\left(\sqrt{3}\right) = 60^\circ
$$

Since $60^\circ$ lies inside the required domain, our first solution is

$$
x_1=60^\circ
$$

The tangent function has a period of $180^\circ$. So, to generate more solutions, we add (or subtract) integer multiples of $180^\circ$ to the principal value.

Therefore, our second solution $x_2$ inside the range

$$
0^\circ \leq x < 360^\circ
$$

can be calculated as

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_{2} &= x_{1} + 180^{∘} \\
&= 60^{∘} + 180^{∘} \\
&= 240^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

We can stop generating solutions here because adding or subtracting further integer multiples of $180^\circ$ to the principal value will give us solutions that lie outside the domain

$$
0^\circ \leq x < 360^\circ
$$

So, the solutions are

$$
x=60^\circ, 240^\circ
$$

## Solving a Trigonometric Equation Involving Tangent with Special Angles

**Example:** Find the solutions to the equation $3\tan x + 3\sqrt{3}=0$ for $0^\circ \leq x < 360^\circ$.

**Explanation**

First, we rearrange the equation and isolate $\tan x$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
3\tan x + 3\sqrt{3} &= 0 \\
3\tan x &= -3\sqrt{3} \\
\tan x &= -\frac{3\sqrt{3}}{3} \\
\tan x &= -\sqrt{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we find the principal value:

$$
x = \arctan (-\sqrt{3}) =-60^{∘}
$$

Since $-60^\circ$ lies outside the given domain, it is not a solution. However, to generate more solutions, we add integer multiples of $180^\circ$ as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_{1} &= x + 180^{∘} =-60^{∘} + 180^{∘} = 120^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_{2} &= x + 360^{∘} =-60^{∘} + 360^{∘} = 300^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the solutions are

$$
x=120^\circ, 300^\circ
$$

## Solving a Trigonometric Equation Involving Tangent with Non-Special Angles

**Example:** Calculate, in degrees to the nearest integer, the solutions to the equation $\tan x=4$ for $0 \leq x < 360^\circ$.

**Explanation**

First, we find the principal value:

$$
x = \arctan (4) = 75.96375...^{∘} \approx 75.964^{∘}
$$

Since $75.964^\circ$ lies inside the given domain, our first solution is

$$
x_1 = 75.964^\circ
$$

To generate more solutions, we add integer multiples of $180^\circ$ as follows:

$$
x_2 = 180^\circ + 75.964^\circ \approx 255.964^\circ
$$

So, the solutions (rounded to the nearest integer) are

$$
x=76^\circ, 256^\circ
$$

## Solving a Trigonometric Equation Involving Tangent Under a Modified Domain

Suppose we want to find all of the solutions to the equation

$$
-180^{∘} < x \le 180^{∘}
$$

Notice that this time, we are looking for solutions in the domain

$$
-180^\circ < x \leq 180^\circ
$$

First, we find the principal value:

$$
x = \arctan (1) = 45^{∘}
$$

Since $45^\circ$ lies inside the given domain, our first solution is

$$
x_1 =45^\circ
$$

To generate more solutions, we subtract integer multiples of $180^\circ$, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_{2} &= x_{1} - 180^{∘} \\
&= 45^{∘} - 180^{∘} \\
&=-135^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the solutions are

$$
x=-135^\circ, 45^\circ
$$

<a id="solving-a-trigonometric-equation-involving-tangent-under-a-modified-domain"></a>
## Solving a Trigonometric Equation Involving Tangent Under a Modified Domain

**Example:** Solve the equation $3 \tan x - \sqrt{3} = 0$ for $-180^\circ < x \leq 180^\circ$.

**Explanation**

First, we rearrange the equation, isolating $\tan x$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
3\tan x - \sqrt{3} &= 0 \\
3\tan x &= \sqrt{3} \\
\tan x &= \frac{\sqrt{3}}{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we find the principal value:

$$
x = \arctan (\frac{\sqrt{3}}{3}) = 30^{∘}
$$

Since $30^\circ$ lies inside the given domain, our first solution is

$$
x_1 =30^\circ
$$

To generate more solutions, we subtract integer multiples of $180^\circ$ as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_{2} &= x_{1} - 180^{∘} \\
&= 30^{∘} - 180^{∘} \\
&=-150^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the solutions are

$$
x=-150^\circ, 30^\circ
$$

## Finding the Points of Intersection of a Horizontal Line and a Tangent Curve

**Example:** The graph below shows a section of the curve $y=\tan{x}$ on the interval $-\dfrac \pi 2 < x < \dfrac {\pi} 2$ and the line $y=1$. The line and the curve intersect at the point $A$, as shown. What is the $x$-coordinate of the point $A$?

![](<../Source/5.2.3. Elementary Trigonometric Equations Containing Tangent/Images/75d3912698b1187b3fc86e2e9db62892.png>)

**Explanation**

The $y$-values of both functions are the same at the intersection point. Equating the two functions gives

$$
\tan x=1, \qquad -\dfrac \pi 2 < x < \dfrac {\pi} 2
$$

First, we find the principal value:

$$
x = \arctan (1) = \frac{π}{4}
$$

Since

$$
\dfrac{\pi}{4}
$$

lies inside the required domain, and there is only one point of intersection, we conclude that the $x$-coordinate of the intersection point $A$ is

$$
x= \dfrac{\pi}{4}
$$
