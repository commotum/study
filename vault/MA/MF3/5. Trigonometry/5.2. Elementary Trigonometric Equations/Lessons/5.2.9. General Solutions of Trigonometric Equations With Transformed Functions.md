## Introduction

How can we find the general solution to the following trigonometric equation?

$$
\sin(2x-30^\circ) = \dfrac{1}{2}
$$

To find the general solution, we perform the following two steps:

- First, we find an implicit general solution by solving the equation, treating the expression inside the parentheses as a single variable.
- Then, we find an explicit general solution by solving the expressions obtained in the first step for the unknown variable.

So for this example, we let

$$
X = 2x-30^\circ
$$

Then, we have the equation

$$
\sin X = \dfrac{1}{2}
$$

This equation has the general solution

$$
\begin{aligned}
X &= 30^{∘} + n \cdot 360^{∘}, \mid X|= 150^{∘} + n \cdot 360^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

where $n$ is any integer.

Writing this general solution in terms of the original variable $x$, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
2x - 30^{∘} &= 30^{∘} + n \cdot 360^{∘}, \mid 2x - 30^{∘}|= 150^{∘} + n \cdot 360^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

Solving the two equations above for $x$, we get the following:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\begin{bmatrix}2x &= 60^{∘} + n \cdot 360^{∘}, & 2x|= 180^{∘} + n \cdot 360^{∘} \\ x|= 30^{∘} + n \cdot 180^{∘}, & x|= 90^{∘} + n \cdot 180^{∘}\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the general solution to our original equation is

$$
\begin{aligned}
x &= 30^{∘} + n \cdot 180^{∘}, \mid x|= 90^{∘} + n \cdot 180^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

Although we introduced an intermediate variable $X$ in this example, it's possible to skip this step. Let's see an example where we do this.

## Finding the General Solution to an Equation Containing a Transformed Sine Function

**Example:** Find the general solution of the following trigonometric equation:

$\sin{\left(4x-90^\circ\right)} = -\dfrac{1}{2}$

**Explanation**

The general solution to our equation is

$$
\begin{aligned}
4x - 90^{∘} &= -30^{∘} + 360^{∘}n, \mid 4x - 90^{∘}|=-150^{∘} + 360^{∘}n
\end{aligned}
$$

Solving the two equations above for $x$, we get the following:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\begin{bmatrix}4x &= 90^{∘} - 30^{∘} + 360^{∘}n & 4x|= 90^{∘} - 150^{∘} + 360^{∘}n \\ 4x|= 60^{∘} + 360^{∘}n & 4x|=-60^{∘} + 360^{∘}n \\ x|= 15^{∘} + 90^{∘}n & x|=-15^{∘} + 90^{∘}n\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding the General Solution to an Equation Containing a Transformed Cosine Function

**Example:** Find the general solution of the following trigonometric equation:
$\cos \left(6x + 75^\circ \right) = -\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$

**Explanation**

The general solution to our equation is

$$
\begin{aligned}
6x + 75^{∘} &= 135^{∘} + n \cdot 360^{∘}, \mid 6x + 75^{∘}|= 225^{∘} + n \cdot 360^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

Solving the two equations above for $x$, we get the following:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\begin{bmatrix}6x &= 60^{∘} + n \cdot 360^{∘} & 6x|= 150^{∘} + n \cdot 360^{∘} \\ x|= 10^{∘} + n \cdot 60^{∘} & x|= 25^{∘} + n \cdot 60^{∘}\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding the General Solution to an Equation Containing a Transformed Tangent or Cotangent Function

**Example:** Find the general solution of the following trigonometric equation:
$\tan\left(2x - \dfrac{\pi}{4} \right) = -1$

**Explanation**

The general solution to our equation is

$$
\begin{aligned}
2x - \frac{π}{4} &= -\frac{π}{4} + nπ
\end{aligned}
$$

Solving the above equation for $x$, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
2x &= nπ \\
x &= \frac{nπ}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding the General Solution to an Equation Containing a Transformed Reciprocal Function

**Example:** Find the general solution of the following trigonometric equation:
$\sec\left(2x + \dfrac{\pi}{2}\right) = \sqrt{2}$

**Explanation**

First, note that the given equation is equivalent to

$$
\cos\left(2x + \dfrac{\pi}{2}\right) = \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}
$$

Therefore, the general solution to our equation is

$$
2x + \dfrac{\pi}{2} = \dfrac{\pi}{4} + 2\pi n, \qquad\qquad 2x + \dfrac{\pi}{2} = \dfrac{7\pi}{4} + 2\pi n
$$

Solving the two equations above for $x$, we get the following:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}2x =-\frac{π}{2} + \frac{π}{4} + 2πn, & 2x =-\frac{π}{2} + \frac{7π}{4} + 2πn \\ 2x =-\frac{π}{4} + 2πn, & 2x = \frac{5π}{4} + 2πn \\ x =-\frac{π}{8} + πn, & x = \frac{5π}{8} + πn\end{bmatrix}
$$
