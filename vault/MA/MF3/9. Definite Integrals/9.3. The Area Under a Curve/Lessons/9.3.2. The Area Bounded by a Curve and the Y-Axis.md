## Introduction

The area $A$ bounded by a curve $x=x(y)$, the $y$-axis, and the horizontal lines $y=c$ and $y=d$ (see the picture below) can be computed as follows:

$$
A = \int_{c}^{d} x(y) \:\textrm{d}y
$$

![](<../Source/9.3.2. The Area Bounded by a Curve and the Y-Axis/Images/3617da118a3d606be5ec67e564aff238.png>)

When using integration to find areas, we must note the following:

- definite integrals that correspond to areas to the right of the $y$-axis are positive, while
- definite integrals that correspond to areas to the left of the $y$-axis are negative.

![](<../Source/9.3.2. The Area Bounded by a Curve and the Y-Axis/Images/c580bb998cd680087318f2b3fd76c403.png>)

## Finding the Area Bounded by a Curve, the y-Axis, and Two Horizontal Lines

**Example:** Find the area bounded between the curve $x=y^2-2y+2$, the $y$-axis, and the lines $y=1$ and $y=2$, as shown below.

![](<../Source/9.3.2. The Area Bounded by a Curve and the Y-Axis/Images/ba7fd1150e24e1bce909556ef88947a8.png>)

**Explanation**

To find the area, we compute the corresponding definite integral, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= ∫_{c}^{d}x(y)dy \\
&= ∫_{1}^{2}(y^{2} - 2y + 2)dy \\
&= ∫_{1}^{2}y^{2}dy - 2∫_{1}^{2}ydy + ∫_{1}^{2}2dy \\
&= \frac{y^{3}}{3}\begin{vmatrix}_{1}^{2} - 2 \cdot \frac{y^{2}}{2} & _{1}^{2} + 2y\end{vmatrix}_{1}^{2} \\
&= (\frac{8}{3} - \frac{1}{3}) - (4 - 1) + (4 - 2) \\
&= \frac{7}{3} - 3 + 2 \\
&= \frac{4}{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding the Finite Area Bounded Between a Curve and the Y-Axis

**Example:** Find the area of the closed region between the curve $x=y^2-4$ and the $y$-axis.

**Explanation**

First, we find the $y$-intercepts of the curve:

$$
\begin{aligned}
y^{2} - 4 &= 0 \\
y^{2} &= 4 \\
y &= ± 2
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we draw a sketch of the region:

![](<../Source/9.3.2. The Area Bounded by a Curve and the Y-Axis/Images/0a73f14099d8a6940e2ee8a6785c25e2.png>)

We want the area between the curve $x=y^2-4$ and the $y$-axis from $y=-2$ to $y=2$. To find the area, we compute the corresponding definite integral, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= ∫_{c}^{d}x(y)dy \\
&= ∫_{-2}^{2}(y^{2} - 4)dy \\
&= 2∫_{0}^{2}(y^{2} - 4)dy \\
&= 2∫_{0}^{2}y^{2}dy - 8∫_{0}^{2}dy \\
&= 2 \cdot \frac{y^{3}}{3} \mid _{0}^{2} - 8y \mid _{0}^{2} \\
&= 2(\frac{8}{3} - 0) - 8(2 - 0) \\
&= \frac{16}{3} - 16 \\
&=-\frac{32}{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

We obtained a negative number because the region lies to the left of the $y$-axis. So the area that we want is

$$
\left|-\dfrac{32}{3}\right| = \dfrac{32}{3}
$$

## Finding the Area Bounded Between a Curve and the Y-Axis by First Rewriting the Function

**Example:** Find the area between the curve $y=\sqrt{x+1}$, the $y$-axis, and the lines $y=0$ and $y=1$, as shown above.

![](<../Source/9.3.2. The Area Bounded by a Curve and the Y-Axis/Images/8eb4608fe3ade4f8a475b24df1952eb2.png>)

**Explanation**

First, we make $x$ the subject:

$$
\begin{aligned}
y &= \sqrt{x + 1} \\
y^{2} &= x + 1 \\
x &= y^{2} - 1
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, to find the area, we compute the corresponding definite integral, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= ∫_{0}^{1}(y^{2} - 1)dy \\
&= ∫_{0}^{1}y^{2}dy - ∫_{0}^{1}dy \\
&= \frac{y^{3}}{3} \mid _{0}^{1} - y \mid _{0}^{1} \\
&= (\frac{1}{3} - 0) - (1 - 0) \\
&= \frac{1}{3} - 1 \\
&=-\frac{2}{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

We obtained a negative number because the region lies to the left of the $y$-axis. So the area that we want is

$$
\left|-\dfrac{2}{3}\right| = \dfrac{2}{3}
$$
