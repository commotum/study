## Introduction

The additive properties of integrals are beneficial when we have to integrate a piecewise function, such as

$$
{x,\begin{vmatrix}x < 0 \\ 5,\end{vmatrix}x \ge 0
$$

For example, let's calculate the integral
$\displaystyle \int_{-5}^4 f(x) \,\textrm{d}x$.

We can split the integral into two integrals using the additive property, one integral for each "piece" of the piecewise function.

- The first integral will be over the integral $[-5,0]$, and
- the second integral will be over the integral $[0,4]$.

Doing this, we get the following:

$$
\begin{aligned}
∫_{-5}^{4}f(x)dx &= ∫_{-5}^{0}f(x)dx + ∫_{0}^{4}f(x)dx \\
&= ∫_{-5}^{0}xdx + ∫_{0}^{4}5dx \\
&= \frac{1}{2}x^{2} \mid _{-5}^{0} + 5x \mid _{0}^{4} \\
&= \frac{1}{2}[0^{2} - (-5)^{2}] + 5[4 - 0] \\
&=-\frac{25}{2} + 20 \\
&= \frac{15}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Calculating the Definite Integral of a Piecewise Continuous Function Given the Function Expression

**Example:** The function $f(x)$ is defined below. What is the value of $\displaystyle \int_{-2}^5 f(x) \,\textrm{d}x\,$?
${3,\begin{vmatrix}x < 0 \\ 2x + 3,\end{vmatrix}x \ge 0$

**Explanation**

By the additive property of the definite integral, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
∫_{-2}^{5}f(x)dx &= ∫_{-2}^{0}3dx + ∫_{0}^{5}(2x + 3)dx \\
&= 3x \mid _{-2}^{0} + (x^{2} + 3x) \mid _{0}^{5} \\
&= [3(0) - 3(-2)] + [(5^{2} + 3(5)) - (0^{2} + 3(0))] \\
&= 6 + 25 + 15 \\
&= 46
\end{aligned}
$$

## Calculating the Definite Integral of a Piecewise Linear Function Given Its Graph

**Example:** The graph of $y=f(x)$, shown above, consists of two line segments. Find the value of $\displaystyle \int_{0}^{4} f(x)\: \textrm{d}x$.

![](<../Source/9.3.6. Definite Integrals of Piecewise Functions/Images/e969825468755643c99584897eed6f04.png>)

**Explanation**

The graph consists of two parts: the line segment for $[MATH: x \in [0,2)]$ and the line segment for $x \in [2,4]$. Therefore, we can write the required integral as follows:

$$
\int_{0}^{4} f(x)\: \textrm{d}x = \underbrace{\int_{0}^{2} f(x)\: \textrm{d}x}_{A_1} + \underbrace{\int_{2}^{4} f(x)\: \textrm{d}x}_{-A_2}
$$

- The $1$st integral represents the signed area between the curve and the $x$-axis, where $[MATH: x \in [0,2).]$ The corresponding region is a rectangle, so we find the area using the formula
$A_{1}|= bh; = 2 \cdot 2; = 4$.
- The $2$nd integral represents the signed area between the curve and the $x$-axis, where $x \in [2,4]$. The corresponding region is a right triangle, so we find the area using the formula
$A_{2}|= \frac{1}{2}bh; = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 2 \cdot 2; = 2$.
Since the region lies below the $x$-axis, we have
$\int_{2}^{4} f(x)\: \textrm{d}x =-A_2 = -2$.

Finally, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
∫_{1}^{4}f(x)dx &= A_{1} - A_{2} \\
&= 4 - 2 \\
&= 2
\end{aligned}
$$

## Calculating the Definite Integral of a Piecewise Curved Function Given Its Graph

**Example:** Given the graph of $y=f(x)$ above, find the value of $\displaystyle \int_{-4}^4 f(x) \,\textrm{d}x$.

![](<../Source/9.3.6. Definite Integrals of Piecewise Functions/Images/eb1ab74ae544a1b33f0f6c89141309e6.png>)

**Explanation**

The graph consists of four parts: the arc of a semi-circle for $x\in [-4,0]$, a line segment for $x\in (0,2)$, a line segment for $[MATH: x\in (2,4],]$ and the point at $x=2$. So, we can use the additive property of integrals to get

$$
\begin{aligned}
∫_{-4}^{4}f(x)dx &= ∫_{-4}^{0}f(x)dx + ∫_{0}^{2}f(x)dx + ∫_{2}^{2}f(x)dx + ∫_{2}^{4}f(x)dx \\
&= ∫_{-4}^{0}f(x)dx_(⏟)_(A_{1}) + ∫_{0}^{2}f(x)dx_(⏟)_(-A_{2}) + 0 + ∫_{2}^{4}f(x)dx_(⏟)_(A_{3})
\end{aligned}
$$

- The $1$st integral represents the signed area between the curve and the $x$-axis, where $x \in [-4,0]$. The corresponding region is a semi-circle with a radius of $2$, so we find the area using the formula
$A_{1}|= \frac{1}{2}πr^{2}; = \frac{1}{2} \cdot π \cdot 2^{2}; = 2π$.
- The $2$nd integral represents the signed area between the curve and the $x$-axis, where $x \in (0,2)$. The corresponding region is a right triangle, so we find the area using the formula
$A_{2}|= \frac{1}{2}bh; = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 2 \cdot 2; = 2$.
Since the region lies below the $x$-axis, we have
$\int_{0}^{2} f(x)\: \textrm{d}x = -A_2 = -2$.
- The $3$rd integral represents the signed area between the curve and the $x$-axis, where $[MATH: x \in (2,4].]$ The corresponding region is a right triangle, so we find the area using the formula
$A_{3}|= \frac{1}{2}bh; = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 2 \cdot 2; = 2$.

Finally,

$$
\begin{aligned}
∫_{-4}^{4}f(x)dx &= A_{1} - A_{2} + A_{3} \\
&= 2π - 2 + 2 \\
&= 2π
\end{aligned}
$$
