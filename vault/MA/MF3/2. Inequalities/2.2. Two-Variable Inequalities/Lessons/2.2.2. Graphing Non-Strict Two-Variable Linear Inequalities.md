<a id="introduction"></a>
## Introduction

Let's think about how to graph the solutions to the following two-variable inequality:

$$
y \geq x
$$

Since this is a two-variable inequality, we must represent its solutions in the $xy$-plane.

We start by replacing the inequality symbol

$$
(\geq)
$$

with an equals sign $(=)$ and plot the resulting line. In this case, we get the line $y=x$.

![](<../Source/2.2.2. Graphing Non-Strict Two-Variable Linear Inequalities/Images/edeab9880d053c6283f3d0ac1dfd77d0.png>)

Notice that we have a "greater than *or equal to*" inequality

$$
(\geq)
$$

Therefore, we draw a *solid* line (as opposed to a dotted or dashed line). The solid line indicates that points on the line are solutions to our inequality.

Now, since we're solving

$$
y \geq x
$$

we need to shade all points whose $y$-value is *greater than or equal to* its $x$-value. This is given by all of the points that lie *above* our solid line or *on* the line. So, let's shade these in.

![](<../Source/2.2.2. Graphing Non-Strict Two-Variable Linear Inequalities/Images/dcaa6fbba2e6ad6178ac23ae1a1b9d9f.png>)

And we're done! All of the points in the shaded region satisfy the inequality

$$
y \geq x
$$

<a id="testing-the-solution"></a>
## Testing the Solution

When solving two-variable inequalities, it's often helpful to test and ensure that we've shaded the correct region.

To demonstrate, let's go back to the following inequality:

$$
y \geq x
$$

The solution to this inequality is indicated by the shaded region below.

![](<../Source/2.2.2. Graphing Non-Strict Two-Variable Linear Inequalities/Images/5b1dc1060f82220600c5f545109f4c23.png>)

To check that we've shaded the correct region, we pick a point inside the shaded region and verify that it satisfies the original inequality. Let's pick the point $(0,1)$.

![](<../Source/2.2.2. Graphing Non-Strict Two-Variable Linear Inequalities/Images/b37af7fee5e13d6f695c2fd2db40c3c0.png>)

When we substitute the coordinates $(0,1)$ into our inequality, we get a true statement:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}y & \ge x \\ 1 & \ge 0✓\end{bmatrix}
$$

This indicates that we have shaded the correct region.

<a id="sketching-the-solution-to-a-greater-than-or-equal-to-inequality"></a>
## Sketching the Solution to a “Greater Than or Equal To” Inequality

**Example:** Sketch the solution to the inequality $y \geq -x+3$.

**Explanation**

First, we draw the corresponding straight line

$$
y=-x+3
$$

Since the inequality involves a 'greater than *or equal to*' sign, the line

$$
y=-x+3
$$

is part of the solution. Therefore, we draw a **solid line**.

![](<../Source/2.2.2. Graphing Non-Strict Two-Variable Linear Inequalities/Images/51dddff15475dda8eeccf93453083b43.png>)

Then, we must decide whether to shade above or below the line. The inequality

$$
y \geq -x+3
$$

refers to the $y$-values that are *greater than* or equal to those on the line

$$
y=-x+3
$$

so we shade *above* the line.

![](<../Source/2.2.2. Graphing Non-Strict Two-Variable Linear Inequalities/Images/e780f565506229f22de233b148492119.png>)

Let's check that we've shaded the correct part. First, we consider a point in the shaded region, say $(0,4)$:

![](<../Source/2.2.2. Graphing Non-Strict Two-Variable Linear Inequalities/Images/4aee9c73c98d4519ccb475a3321d5995.png>)

When we substitute the coordinates of this point into our inequality, we get a true statement:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}y & \ge - x + 3 \\ (4) & \ge - (0) + 3 \\ 4 & \ge 3✓\end{bmatrix}
$$

This indicates that we have shaded the correct region.

<a id="solving-less-than-or-equal-to-inequalities"></a>
## Solving "Less Than or Equal To" Inequalities

Let's now think about how to graph the solutions to the inequality

$$
y \leq x
$$

As before, we start by replacing the inequality symbol

$$
(\leq)
$$

with an equals sign $(=)$ and plot the resulting line $y=x$.

![](<../Source/2.2.2. Graphing Non-Strict Two-Variable Linear Inequalities/Images/e6f999d06687fa5238fe803804fd8e0e.png>)

Notice that we have a "less than *or equal to*" inequality

$$
(\leq)
$$

Therefore, we draw a *solid* line.

Now, since we're solving

$$
y \leq x
$$

we need to shade all points whose $y$-value is *smaller than or equal to* its $x$-value. This is given by all of the points that lie *below* our solid line or *on* the line.

![](<../Source/2.2.2. Graphing Non-Strict Two-Variable Linear Inequalities/Images/6ce3c87fadfb755de9c44cac527d8470.png>)

And we're done! All of the points in the shaded region satisfy the inequality

$$
y \leq x
$$

To check that we've shaded the correct region, we pick a point inside the shaded region and check that it satisfies the original inequality. Let's pick the point $(1,0)$.

![](<../Source/2.2.2. Graphing Non-Strict Two-Variable Linear Inequalities/Images/9fa05d8d09fb86eb3237b88aff290a64.png>)

When we substitute the coordinates $(1,0)$ into our inequality, we get a true statement:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}y & \le x \\ 0 & \le 1✓\end{bmatrix}
$$

This indicates that we have shaded the correct region.

## Sketching the Solution to a “Less Than or Equal To” Inequality

**Example:** Sketch the solution to the inequality $y \leq x-2$.

**Explanation**

First, we draw the corresponding straight line $y=x-2$. Since the inequality involves a 'less than *or equal to*' sign, the line $y=x-2$ itself is part of the solution. Therefore, we draw a **solid line**.

![](<../Source/2.2.2. Graphing Non-Strict Two-Variable Linear Inequalities/Images/44a99636a80e094acec4ef8e63078162.png>)

Then, we must decide whether to shade above or below the line. The inequality

$$
y \leq x-2
$$

refers to the $y$-values that are *less than* or equal to those on the line $y=x-2$, so we shade *below* the line.

![](<../Source/2.2.2. Graphing Non-Strict Two-Variable Linear Inequalities/Images/6bcc44629dfc228168470eca7b967915.png>)

Let's check that we've shaded the correct part. First, we consider a point in the shaded region, say $(0,-3)$:

![](<../Source/2.2.2. Graphing Non-Strict Two-Variable Linear Inequalities/Images/c3c81278485021e362e06cbd816ea6bd.png>)

When we substitute the coordinates of this point into our inequality, we get a true statement:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}y & \le x - 2 \\ (-3) & \le (0) - 2 \\ -3 & \le - 2✓\end{bmatrix}
$$

This indicates that we have shaded the correct region.

<a id="finding-an-inequality-corresponding-to-a-given-graph"></a>
## Finding an Inequality Corresponding to a Given Graph

**Example:** What is the inequality that describes the shaded region shown below?

![](<../Source/2.2.2. Graphing Non-Strict Two-Variable Linear Inequalities/Images/4b82f776a8b436696f3a9f76308c4176.png>)

**Explanation**

First, we find the equation of the corresponding line in the slope-intercept form

$$
y=mx+b
$$

- The line intersects the $y$-axis at $(0,-1)$, so the $y$-intercept is $b=-1$.
- The line passes through the points $(0,-1)$ and $(-1,2)$, so its slope is
$m = \frac{y_{2} - y_{1}}{x_{2} - x_{1}} = (2 - (-1))/(-1 - 0) = \frac{3}{-1} =-3$.

Therefore, the equation of the line is $y=-3x-1$.

Finally, we need to decide which inequality symbol to use. Since the line is solid, we will use an "or equal to" inequality. Since the shaded region is *above* the line, the inequality refers to the $y$-values that are *greater than* or equal to those on the line.

So, the corresponding inequality is

$$
y \geq -3x-1
$$
