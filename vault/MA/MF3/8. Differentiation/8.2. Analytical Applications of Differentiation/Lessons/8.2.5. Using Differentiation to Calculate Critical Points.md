## Introduction

Recall that a critical point of $f(x)$ is a point $x=c$ in the domain of $f$ such that

- either $f'(c)=0$
- or $f'(c)$ does not exist.

For example, let's find the critical points of the function

$$
f(x) = x^3- 12x
$$

Differentiating $f(x)$, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
f^{′}(x) &= \frac{d}{dx}(x^{3} - 12x) \\
&= 3x^{2} - 12
\end{aligned}
$$

We now proceed to find the critical points:

- First, we compute the points where $f'(c) = 0$. We set the derivative equal to zero, and solve for $x$:
$3x^{2} - 12|= 0; 3x^{2}|= 12; x^{2}|= 4; x|= ± 2$
Therefore, $f(x)$ has stationary points at $x = \pm 2$.
- Second, we note that $f'(x)$ exists for all values of $x$ in the domain of $f(x)$. So $f(x)$ has no non-stationary critical points.

Therefore, we conclude that the critical points of $f(x)$ is $x= \pm 2$.

## Finding Critical Points of Polynomials

**Example:** Find the critical points of the function $f(x)=2x^3-3x^2+5$.

**Explanation**

A critical point of $f(x)$ is a point $x=c$ in the domain of $f$ such that either $f'(c)=0$ or $f'(c)$ does not exist.

Differentiating $f(x)$, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
f^{′}(x) &= \frac{d}{dx}(2x^{3} - 3x^{2} + 5) \\
&= 6x^{2} - 6x
\end{aligned}
$$

We now proceed to find the critical points:

- First, we compute the points where $f'(c) = 0$. We set the derivative equal to zero, and solve for $x$:
$6x^{2} - 6x|= 0; 6x(x - 1)|= 0; x = 0, 1$
Therefore, $f(x)$ has stationary points at $x=0$ and $x=1$.
- Second, we note that $f'(x)$ exists for all values of $x$ in the domain of $f(x)$. So $f(x)$ has no non-stationary critical points.

Therefore, we conclude that the critical points of $f(x)$ are $x=0$ and $x=1$.

## Finding Critical Points of Exponential Functions

**Example:** Find the critical points of the function $f(x) = e^{x^3-x}$.

**Explanation**

A critical point of $f(x)$ is a point $x=c$ in the domain of $f$ such that either $f'(c)=0$ or $f'(c)$ does not exist.

Differentiating $f(x)$, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
f^{′}(x) &= \frac{d}{dx}(e^{x^{3} - x}) \\
&= (x^{3} - x)^{′}e^{x^{3} - x} \\
&= (3x^{2} - 1)e^{x^{3} - x}
\end{aligned}
$$

We now proceed to find the critical points:

- First, we compute the points where $f'(c) = 0$. We set the derivative equal to zero, and solve for $x$:
$(3x^{2} - 1)e^{x^{3} - x}|= 0$
Since $e^{x^3-x} \neq 0$ for all $x$, we can divide this equation by $e^{x^3-x}$ and solve the resulting equation.
$(3x^{2} - 1)e^{x^{3} - x}|= 0; 3x^{2} - 1|= 0; x|= ± \frac{\sqrt{3}}{3}$
Therefore, $f(x)$ has stationary points at $x=\dfrac{\sqrt 3}{3}$ and $x=-\dfrac{\sqrt 3}{3}$.
- Second, we note that $f'(x)$ exists for all values of $x$ in the domain of $f(x)$. So $f(x)$ has no non-stationary critical points.

Therefore, we conclude that the critical points of $f(x)$ are

$$
x=\dfrac{\sqrt 3}{3}
$$

and

$$
x=-\dfrac{\sqrt 3}{3}
$$

<a id="finding-critical-points-of-rational-functions"></a>
## Finding Critical Points of Rational Functions

**Example:** What are the critical points of the function $f(x) = \dfrac {x-3}{x^2 -6}$?

**Explanation**

A critical point of $f(x)$ is a point $x=c$ in the domain of $f$ such that either $f'(c)=0$ or $f'(c)$ does not exist.

Differentiating $f(x)$, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
f^{′}(x) &= \frac{d}{dx}(\frac{x - 3}{x^{2} - 6}) \\
&= ((x - 3)^{′} \cdot (x^{2} - 6) - (x - 3) \cdot (x^{2} - 6)^{′})/((x^{2} - 6)^{2}) \\
&= ((x^{2} - 6) - (x - 3) \cdot 2x)/((x^{2} - 6)^{2}) \\
&= (-6 + 6x - x^{2})/((x^{2} - 6)^{2}) \\
&= - (x^{2} - 6x + 6)/((x^{2} - 6)^{2})
\end{aligned}
$$

We now proceed to find the critical points:

- First, we compute the points where $f'(c) = 0$. We set the derivative equal to zero, and solve for $x$ by completing the square:
$- (x^{2} - 6x + 6)/((x^{2} - 6)^{2})|= 0; x^{2} - 6x + 6|= 0; x^{2} - 2 \cdot 3x + 3^{2} - 3^{2} + 6|= 0; (x - 3)^{2} - 9 + 6|= 0; (x - 3)^{2} - 3|= 0; (x - 3)^{2}|= 3; x - 3|= ± \sqrt{3}; x|= 3 ± \sqrt{3}$.
Therefore, $f(x)$ has stationary points at $x=3\pm \sqrt 3$.
- Second, we note that $f'(x)$ does not exist at $x=\pm \sqrt 6$. But these points do not lie in the domain of $f(x)$. So $f(x)$ has no non-stationary critical points.

Therefore, we conclude that the critical points of $f(x)$ are

$$
x=3\pm \sqrt 3
$$

<a id="calculating-critical-points-of-trigonometric-functions"></a>
## Calculating Critical Points of Trigonometric Functions

**Example:** Consider the function $f(x) = \sqrt 3\sin{2x} - \cos{2x}$. Find critical points of $f(x)$ for $x \in \left(-\dfrac{\pi}{2}, \dfrac{\pi}{2}\right)$.

**Explanation**

A critical point of $f(x)$ is a point $x=c$ in the domain of $f$ such that either $f'(c) = 0$ or $f'(c)$ does not exist.

Differentiating $f(x)$, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
f^{′}(x) &= \frac{d}{dx}(\sqrt{3}\sin 2x - \cos 2x) \\
&= \sqrt{3} \cdot \frac{d}{dx}(\sin 2x) - \frac{d}{dx}(\cos 2x) \\
&= 2\sqrt{3}\cos 2x + 2\sin 2x
\end{aligned}
$$

We now proceed to find the critical points:

- First, we compute the points where $f'(c) = 0$. We set the derivative equal to zero, and solve for $x$:
$2\sqrt{3}\cos 2x + 2\sin 2x|= 0; 2\sin 2x|=-2\sqrt{3}\cos 2x; \frac{\sin 2x}{\cos 2x}|=-\frac{2\sqrt{3}}{2} \cdot \frac{\cos 2x}{\cos 2x}; \tan 2x|=-\sqrt{3}$
The general solution to our equation is
$2x|= \arctan (-\sqrt{3}) + nπ; 2x|=-\frac{π}{3} + nπ$.
Solving for $x$, we get
$x|=-\frac{π}{6} + \frac{nπ}{2}$.
We set $n=0, \pm 1, \pm 2,\ldots$, until we have generated all solutions in the domain $-\dfrac{\pi}{2} \lt x \lt \dfrac{\pi}{2}$:setting $n=0$ gives $x = -\dfrac{\pi}{6}$setting $n=1$ gives $x = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$All other values of $n$ generate solutions outside the domain $-\dfrac{\pi}{2} \lt x \lt \dfrac{\pi}{2}$. So, the solutions to this equation for $x \in \left(-\dfrac{\pi}{2}, \dfrac{\pi}{2}\right)$ are
$x =-\dfrac{\pi}{6}, \qquad x = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$.
Therefore, $f(x)$ has stationary points at $x =-\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ and $x = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$.
- Second, we note that $f'(x)$ exists for all values of $x\in \left(-\dfrac\pi2, \dfrac\pi2\right)$. So $f(x)$ has no non-stationary critical points.

Therefore, we conclude that the critical points of $f(x)$ for

$$
x\in \left(-\dfrac\pi2, \dfrac\pi2\right)
$$

are

$$
x_1 =-\dfrac{\pi}{6}, \qquad x_2 = \dfrac{\pi}{3}
$$
