## Introduction

Recall that the $n$th roots of unity are the solutions to the equation

$$
z^n = 1
$$

In general, the $n$th roots of unity can be expressed as

$$
z_k = e^{2\pi k\textrm i /n}, \qquad k=0,1,2,\ldots,n-1
$$

Also, recall that the root corresponding to $k=1$ is the *principal $n$th root of unity.*

We have the following theorem:

> *Any $n$th root of unity is an integer power of the principal $n$th root.*

Let's see an example.

---

<a id="expressing-a-root-of-unity-using-its-principal-root"></a>
## Expressing a Root of Unity Using Its Principal Root

**Example:** The $12$th root of unity $e^{2\pi \text{i}/3}$ can be written as
$e^{2\pi \text{i}/3} = z^p$,
where $z$ is the principal $12$th root of unity. What is the value of $p$?

**Explanation**

Recall that the $12$th roots of unity can be written in the form

$$
e^{2 k\pi \text{i} / 12}
$$

where

$$
k=0,1,\ldots,11
$$

We obtain the *principal* $12$th root of unity by substituting $k=1{:}$

$$
z= e^{2 \cdot 1 \cdot \pi \text{i} / 12} = {\color{blue}e^{\pi \text{i} / 6}}
$$

We can write the given root of unity as a power of the principal root as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
e^{2π\textrm{i}/3} &= e^{(2 \cdot 2π)\textrm{i}/(2 \cdot 3)} \\
&= e^{4π\textrm{i}/6} \\
&= e^{4 \cdot π\textrm{i}/6} \\
&= (e^{π\textrm{i}/6})^{4}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
p={\color{red}4}
$$

<a id="product-and-quotient-properties"></a>
## Product and Quotient Properties

The $n$th roots of unity have other interesting properties, which we list below, along with a proof of each result.

When proving the results, assume that $z$ and $w$ are $n$th roots of unity, and therefore

$$
z^n = w^n=1
$$

- *A product of two $n$th roots of unity is a $n$th root of unity.*To prove this, note that
$\big(zw\big)^n= z^n\cdot w^n = 1 \cdot 1 = 1$,
which means that $zw$ is also an $n$th root of unity.
- *The reciprocal of an $n$th root of unity is a $n$th root of unity.*To prove this, note that
$\left(\dfrac{1}{z}\right)^n= \dfrac{1}{z^n} = \dfrac 1 1 = 1$,
which means that $\dfrac 1 z$ is an $n$th root of unity too.
- *The quotient of two $n$th roots of unity is a $n$th root of unity.*To prove this, note that
$\left(\dfrac{z}{w}\right)^n= \dfrac{z^n}{w^n} = \dfrac 1 1 = 1$,
which means that $\dfrac z w$ is an $n$th root of unity.
- *Any integer power of an $n$th root of unity is a $n$th root of unity.*To prove this, note that
$\big(z^k\big)^n= \big(z^n\big)^k = 1^k = 1$,
which means that $z^k$ is an $n$th root of unity.

<a id="identifying-true-statements-regarding-product-and-quotient-properties"></a>
## Identifying True Statements Regarding Product and Quotient Properties

**Example:** Let $z$ be a $7$th root of unity. Which of the following statements are true?

1. $z^7 = 1$
2. $(z+z)^7 = 1$
3. $z+z=2z$ is a $7$th root of unity

**Explanation**

Let's examine our statements in turn.

- Statement I is true. If $z$ is a $7$th root of unity, then (by definition), we have that
$z^7=1$.
- Statement II is false. We have
$(z + z)^{7}|= (2z)^{7}; = 2^{7} \cdot z^{7}; = 128 \cdot 1; = 128; \ne 1$.
- Statement III is false. From statement II, we have that $(z+z)^7 \neq 1$, which means that $z+z=2z$ is *not* a $7$th root of unity. In general, the sum of two $n$th roots of unity is *not* an $n$th root of unity.

Therefore, the correct answer is "I only."

<a id="sums-of-roots-of-unity"></a>
## Sums of Roots of Unity

The sum of all $n$th roots of unity equals zero.

To see why, recall that

- the principal $n$th root of unity is $z=e^{2\pi\text{i}/n}\neq1$, and
- any $n$th root of unity is of the form $z^k$ for $k=0,1,...,n-1$.

So, the sum of all $n$ roots can be calculated as the sum of the following geometric series:

$$
\begin{aligned}
1 + z + z^{2} + ⋯ + z^{n - 1} &= \frac{1 - z^{n}}{1 - z} \\
&= \frac{1 - 1}{1 - z} \\
&= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="products-of-roots-of-unity"></a>
## Products of Roots of Unity

The product of all $n$th roots of unity equals $1$ if $n$ is odd and equals $-1$ if $n$ is even.

As an example, the principal fourth root of unity is

$$
z=e^{2\pi\text{i}/4}=e^{\pi\text{i}/2}
$$

Any fourth root of unity is of the form $z^k$ for $k=0,1,2,3$. Also, we have $z^4=1$.

Therefore, the product of all four roots of unity is

$$
\begin{aligned}
z^{0} \cdot z^{1} \cdot z^{2} \cdot z^{3} &= z^{0 + 1 + 2 + 3} \\
&= z^{6} \\
&= z^{4} \cdot z^{2} \\
&= 1 \cdot (e^{π\textrm{i}/2})^{2} \\
&= e^{π\textrm{i}} \\
&=-1
\end{aligned}
$$

Let's see an example of this multiplicative property for odd powers.

<a id="identifying-true-statements-regarding-sums-and-products"></a>
## Identifying True Statements Regarding Sums and Products

**Example:** Find the product of all three cube roots of unity.

**Explanation**

Recall that the principal cube root of unity is

$$
z=e^{2\pi\text{i}/3}
$$

and that any cube root of unity is of the form $z^k$ for $k=0,1,2$. Also, we have $z^3=1$.

Therefore, the product of all three roots is

$$
\begin{aligned}
z^{0} \cdot z^{1} \cdot z^{2} &= z^{0 + 1 + 2} \\
&= z^{3} \\
&= 1
\end{aligned}
$$
