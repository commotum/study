## Introduction

Consider the function $f(x)$ below:

$$
{3x^{2} + kx,\begin{vmatrix}x < - 3, \\ x^{3},\end{vmatrix}x \ge - 3
$$

The function $f(x)$ seems to have a jump discontinuity at $x=-3$. However, by selecting a particular value for $k$, we can remove the jump discontinuity and make the function continuous.

We can find this value of $k$ by forcing the left and right-sided limits of the function to be equal at $x=-3$:

$$
\lim\limits_{x\to (-3)^-} f(x) = \lim\limits_{x\to (-3)^+} f(x)
$$

For the left limit, we have,

$$
\begin{aligned}
lim_(x → (-3)^{-})f(x) &= lim_(x → (-3)^{-})(3x^{2} + kx) \\
&= 3(-3)^{2} + k(-3) \\
&= 27 - 3k
\end{aligned}
$$

and for the right limit, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
lim_(x → (-3)^{+})f(x) &= lim_(x → (-3)^{+})x^{3} \\
&= (-3)^{3} \\
&=-27
\end{aligned}
$$

Setting the left and right limits as equal, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
27 - 3k &= -27 \\
3k &= 54 \\
k &= 18
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, to make $f(x)$ continuous at $x=-3$, we need to choose $k=18$.

## Removing a Jump Discontinuity in a Piecewise Polynomial Function

**Example:** Find the value(s) of the constant $a$ that make the function $f(x)$ below continuous at $x=2$.
${(2x + a)^{2},\begin{vmatrix}x \le 2 \\ x^{2} - 3,\end{vmatrix}x > 2$

**Explanation**

At $x=2$, the left-sided limit is

$$
\begin{aligned}
lim_(x → 2^{-})f(x) &= lim_(x → 2^{-})(2x + a)^{2} \\
&= (2(2) + a)^{2} \\
&= (4 + a)^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

and the right-sided limit is

$$
\begin{aligned}
lim_(x → 2^{+})f(x) &= lim_(x → 2^{+})(x^{2} - 3) \\
&= (2)^{2} - 3 \\
&= 4 - 3 \\
&= 1
\end{aligned}
$$

In order for $f(x)$ to be continuous at $x=2$, we require that

$$
\begin{aligned}
lim_(x → 2^{-})f(x) &= lim_(x → 2^{+})f(x) \\
(4 + a)^{2} &= 1 \\
\sqrt{(a + 4)^{2}} &= \sqrt{1} \\
\mid a + 4 \mid &= 1 \\
a + 4 &= ± 1 \\
a &= -4 ± 1 \\
a &= -5,-3
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, $f(x)$ is continuous at $x=2$ for $a=-5$ or $a=-3$.

## Removing a Jump Discontinuity in a Piecewise Radical Function

**Example:** Given that the function
${\sqrt{2b - 3x},\begin{vmatrix}x \le 0 \\ -x + 2,\end{vmatrix}x > 0$
is continuous at $x=0$, find the value of the constant $b$.

**Explanation**

At $x=0$, the left-sided limit is

$$
\begin{aligned}
lim_(x → 0^{-})f(x) &= lim_(x → 0^{-})\sqrt{2b - 3x} \\
&= \sqrt{2b - 3(0)} \\
&= \sqrt{2b}
\end{aligned}
$$

and the right-sided limit is

$$
\begin{aligned}
lim_(x → 0^{+})f(x) &= lim_(x → 0^{+})(-x + 2) \\
&= - (0) + 2 \\
&= 2
\end{aligned}
$$

In order for $f(x)$ to be continuous at $x=0$, we require that

$$
\begin{aligned}
lim_(x → 0^{-})f(x) &= lim_(x → 0^{+})f(x) \\
2 &= \sqrt{2b} \\
4 &= 2b \\
b &= 2
\end{aligned}
$$

## Removing a Jump Discontinuity in a Piecewise Exponential Function

**Example:** Find the value of the constant $c$ that makes the function $h(x)$ below continuous at $x=4$.
${4^{cx + 1},\begin{vmatrix}x \le 4 \\ 4x,\end{vmatrix}x > 4$

**Explanation**

At $x=4$, the left-sided limit is

$$
\begin{aligned}
lim_(x → 4^{-})h(x) &= h(4) \\
&= 4^{c(4) + 1} \\
&= 4^{4c + 1}
\end{aligned}
$$

and the right-sided limit is

$$
\begin{aligned}
lim_(x → 4^{+})h(x) &= lim_(x → 4^{+})(4x) \\
&= 4(4) \\
&= 16
\end{aligned}
$$

In order for $h(x)$ to be continuous at $x=4$, we require that

$$
\begin{aligned}
4^{4c + 1} &= 16 \\
4^{4c + 1} &= 4^{2} \\
4c + 1 &= 2 \\
4c &= 1 \\
c &= \frac{1}{4}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, $h(x)$ is continuous at $x=4$ for

$$
c = \dfrac{1}{4}
$$

## Removing a Jump Discontinuity in a Piecewise Logarithmic Function

**Example:** Given that the function
${\log_{2} (ax + 1),\begin{vmatrix}0 \le x \le 1 \\ \log_{3} (2x + 1),\end{vmatrix}x > 1$
is continuous at $x=1$, find the value of $a$.

**Explanation**

At $x=1$, the left-sided limit is

$$
\begin{aligned}
lim_(x → 1^{-})g(x) &= lim_(x → 1^{-})\log_{2} (ax + 1) \\
&= \log_{2} (a(1) + 1) \\
&= \log_{2} (a + 1)
\end{aligned}
$$

and the right-sided limit is

$$
\begin{aligned}
lim_(x → 1^{+})g(x) &= lim_(x → 1^{+})(\log_{3} (2x + 1)) \\
&= \log_{3} (2(1) + 1) \\
&= \log_{3} (3) \\
&= 1
\end{aligned}
$$

In order for $g(x)$ to be continuous at $x=1$, we require that

$$
\begin{aligned}
lim_(x → 1^{-})g(x) &= lim_(x → 1^{+})g(x) \\
\log_{2} (a + 1) &= 1 \\
a + 1 &= 2^{1} \\
a &= 1
\end{aligned}
$$
