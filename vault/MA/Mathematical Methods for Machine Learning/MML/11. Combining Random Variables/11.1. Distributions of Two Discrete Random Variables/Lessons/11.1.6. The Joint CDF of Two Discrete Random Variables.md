## Introduction

The **joint cumulative distribution function** (or joint CDF) of two discrete random variables $X$, and $Y$ is defined as

$$
F(x,y) = P(X \leq x, Y \leq y) = \sum\limits_{x_i \leq x} \sum\limits_{y_i \leq y} f(x_i, y_i)
$$

The sets $S_X$ and $S_Y$, on which the variables $X$ and $Y$ are defined, are called the **supports** of $X$ and $Y$, respectively.

We can see from this definition that the joint CDF is analogous to the cumulative distribution function of a single random variable.

Consider the joint PMF for the discrete random variables $X$ and $Y$, shown below.

| $f$ | $Y=0$ | $Y=1$ | $Y=2$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $X=0$ | $0.1$ | $0.2$ | $0.1$ |
| $X=1$ | $0.3$ | $0.2$ | $0.1$ |

Here, the supports of $X$ and $Y$ are

$$
S_X = \{0, 1 \}
$$

and

$$
S_Y = \{0, 1, 2 \}
$$

respectively.

As an example, let's compute $F(1,1)$. By definition, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
F(1, 1) &= P(X \le 1, Y \le 1)
\end{aligned}
$$

We start by highlighting all cells in the table that correspond to

$$
X \leq 1, Y \leq 1
$$

| $f$ | $Y=0$ | $Y=1$ | $Y=2$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $X=0$ | $0.1$ | $0.2$ | $0.1$ |
| $X=1$ | $0.3$ | $0.2$ | $0.1$ |

Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
F(1, 1) &= f(0, 0) + f(0, 1) + f(1, 0) + f(1, 1) \\
&= 0.1 + 0.2 + 0.3 + 0.2 \\
&= 0.8
\end{aligned}
$$

## Evaluating a Joint CDF

**Example:** Let $X$ and $Y$ be discrete random variables with the joint probability mass function $f(x,y)$ shown below.

| $f$ | $Y=-2$ | $Y=-1$ | $Y=0$ | $Y=2$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $X=1$ | $0.1$ | $0.3$ | $0.05$ | $0.1$ |
| $X=3$ | $0.1$ | $0.05$ | $0.1$ | $0.2$ |

If $F(x,y)$ is the joint CDF of $X$ and $Y$, find $F\left(2,1\right)$.

**Explanation**

If $X$ and $Y$ are discrete random variables with the joint probability mass function $f(x,y)$, then the corresponding joint CDF is given by

$$
F(x,y) = P(X \leq x, Y \leq y) = \sum\limits_{x_i \leq x} \sum\limits_{y_i \leq y} f(x_i, y_i)
$$

By definition, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
F(2, 1) &= P(X \le 2, Y \le 1)
\end{aligned}
$$

We start by highlighting all cells in the table that correspond to

$$
X \leq 2, Y \leq 1
$$

| $f$ | $Y=-2$ | $Y=-1$ | $Y=0$ | $Y=2$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $X=1$ | $0.1$ | $0.3$ | $0.05$ | $0.1$ |
| $X=3$ | $0.1$ | $0.05$ | $0.1$ | $0.2$ |

Therefore, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
F(2, 1) &= ∑_(x \le 2)∑_(y \le 1)f(x, y) \\
&= f(1,-2) + f(1,-1) + f(1, 0) \\
&= 0.1 + 0.3 + 0.05 \\
&= 0.45
\end{aligned}
$$

## Geometric Interpretation of the Joint CDF

Consider the joint PMF for the discrete random variables $X$ and $Y$, shown below.

| $f$ | $Y=0$ | $Y=1$ | $Y=2$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $X=1$ | $0.1$ | $0.2$ | $0.1$ |
| $X=2$ | $0.3$ | $0.2$ | $0.1$ |

We can think of each point where

$$
f(x,y)\neq 0
$$

as a point mass in the plane, where the "probability mass" at each point equals the probability associated with that point.

![](<../Source/11.1.6. The Joint CDF of Two Discrete Random Variables/Images/d5477810c9ec4aedfc458cc26a4382aa.png>)

Now, the joint CDF is given by

$$
F(x,y) = \sum\limits_{x_i \leq x} \sum\limits_{y_i \leq y} f(x_i, y_i)
$$

Geometrically, then, the joint CDF gives the total "probability mass" that lies inside the infinite rectangular region that has its top-right corner at $(x,y)$ and whose sides are parallel to the coordinate axes.

For example, consider the point $(2,1.5)$ and the infinite rectangular region that has its top-right corner at this point, shown below.

![](<../Source/11.1.6. The Joint CDF of Two Discrete Random Variables/Images/a1fcf88dd083c25725c6b4745e1bca29.png>)

The value of $F(2,1.5)$ equals the total "probability mass" contained within this rectangle. That is,

$$
F(2,1.5) = f(1,0) + f(2,0) + f(1,1) + f(2,1)
$$

It's worth highlighting a few important cases:

- If our rectangular region doesn't include any points where the probability is nonzero, then we must have $F(x,y) = 0$.![](<../Source/11.1.6. The Joint CDF of Two Discrete Random Variables/Images/e76f164a7eb1ac14d5f88d41dc91bf02.png>)
- If our rectangular region includes *all* points where the probability is nonzero, then we must have $F(x,y) = 1$.![](<../Source/11.1.6. The Joint CDF of Two Discrete Random Variables/Images/1e98949c4c37bb8afc0845c4ee5aa383.png>)

Finally, we note the following properties of the joint CDF:

- $0 \leq F(x,y) \leq 1$
- The marginal CDF of $X$ is given by $F_X(x) = F(x, \infty)$.
- The marginal CDF of $Y$ is given by $F_Y(y) = F(\infty, y)$.
- $F(\infty, \infty) = 1$
- $F(-\infty,y) = F(x, -\infty) = 0$
- If $X$ and $Y$ are independent random variables, then $F(x,y) = F_X(x) \cdot F_Y(y)$.

<a id="interpreting-the-joint-cdf-geometrically"></a>
## Interpreting the Joint CDF Geometrically

**Example:** Suppose $X$ and $Y$ are discrete random variables. Let the joint CDF of $X$ and $Y$ be $F(x,y)$, where the supports of $X$ and $Y$, respectively, are

$S_X = \{1, 2, 3, 4 \}, \quad S_Y = \{2, 3, 4 \}$.

Find an expression for the corresponding joint CDF when $x > 4, y < 2$.

**Explanation**

If $X$ and $Y$ are discrete random variables with the joint probability mass function $f(x,y)$, then the corresponding joint CDF is given by

$$
F(x,y) = P(X \leq x, Y \leq y) = \sum\limits_{x_i \leq x} \sum\limits_{y_i \leq y} f(x_i, y_i)
$$

Note the following:

- We can think of each point where $f(x,y)\neq 0$ as a point mass in the plane, where the "probability mass" at each point equals the probability associated with that point.
- The joint CDF $F(x,y) = P(X \leq x, Y \leq y)$ is the total "probability mass" that lies in the infinite rectangular region that has its top-right corner at $(x,y)$ and whose sides are parallel to the coordinate axis.

Given the supports of $X$ and $Y$, the joint support $S$ is

$$
S = S_X \times S_Y = \{1, 2, 3, 4 \} \times \{2, 3, 4 \}
$$

Let's sketch the points in the joint support. Let's also consider a point $(x,y)$ where $x > 4, y < 2$, and a region $\Delta$ that lies below and to the left of this point.

![](<../Source/11.1.6. The Joint CDF of Two Discrete Random Variables/Images/c797ca08912a72dcbbdc3dd92955f257.png>)

For the purposes of computation, we're only interested in the points that lie inside $\Delta$ where $f(x,y)$ is nonzero. This is given by

$$
S \cap \Delta = \emptyset
$$

So there are no points in our region $\Delta$ where $f(x,y)$ is nonzero. Therefore, we obtain

$$
\sum\limits_{x_i \leq x} \sum\limits_{y_i \leq y} f(x_i, y_i) = \sum\limits_{(x,y) \in \emptyset} f(x, y) = 0
$$

## Finding Cumulative Probabilities Given Values From the Joint CDF

**Example:** Suppose $X$ and $Y$ are discrete random variables. Let the joint CDF of $X$ and $Y$ be $F(x,y)$, where

$F(5,4) = 0.9, \quad F(5,2) = 0.2, \quad F(3,4) = 0.5, \quad F(3,2) = 0.1$,

and the supports of $X$ and $Y$, respectively, are

$S_X = \{1, 3, 5,7\}, \quad S_Y = \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5 \}$.

Find $P(3 < X \leq 5, 2 < Y \leq 4)$.

**Explanation**

Note the following:

- For a given joint PMF $f(x,y)$, we can think of each point where $f(x,y)\neq 0$ as a point mass in the plane, where the "probability mass" at each point equals the probability associated with that point.
- The joint CDF $F(x,y) = P(X \leq x, Y \leq y)$ is the total "probability mass" that lies in the infinite rectangular region that has its top-right corner at $(x,y)$ and whose sides are parallel to the coordinate axis.

Given the supports of $X$ and $Y$, the joint support $S$ is

$$
S = S_X \times S_Y = \{1, 3, 5,7 \} \times \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5 \}
$$

Let's sketch the points in the joint support.

![](<../Source/11.1.6. The Joint CDF of Two Discrete Random Variables/Images/68799167af91529cde6ac02d8d5b1958.png>)

Let's sketch the region corresponding to the probability we wish to find.

![](<../Source/11.1.6. The Joint CDF of Two Discrete Random Variables/Images/fbff892a94632cad4d6b4e855b534725.png>)

Geometrically, this region can be obtained as follows:

1. Take the infinite rectangle that has its top-right corner at $(5,4)$.
2. Subtract the infinite rectangles that have their top-right corners at $\color{red}(5,2)$ and ${\color{red}(3,4)}$.
3. Finally, we need to add back the infinite rectangle that has its top-right corner at ${\color{blue}(3,2)}$. This is because we subtracted this region twice in step 2.

Therefore, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(3 < X \le 5, 2 < Y \le 4) &= P(X \le 5, Y \le 4)^(⏞)^(top-right corner at [math](5,4)[/math]) \\
&=-P(X \le 5, Y \le 2)^(⏞)^(top-right corner at [math](5,2)[/math]) - P(X \le 3, Y \le 4)^(⏞)^(top-right corner at [math](3,4)[/math]) \\
&= + P(X \le 3, Y \le 2)^(⏞)^(top-right corner at [math](3,2)[/math]) \\
&= F(5, 4) - F(5, 2) - F(3, 4) + F(3, 2) \\
&= 0.9 - 0.2 - 0.5 + 0.1 \\
&= 0.3
\end{aligned}
$$

## Further Finding Cumulative Probabilities Given Values From the Joint CDF

**Example:** Suppose $X$ and $Y$ are discrete random variables. Let the joint CDF of $X$ and $Y$ be $F(x,y)$, where

$F(8,3) = 0.9, \qquad F(6,3) = 0.7, \qquad F(4,3) = 0.4$,

and the supports of $X$ and $Y$, respectively, are

$S_X = \{2, 4, 6, 8\}, \qquad S_Y = \{1, 3, 5 \}$.

Find $P(X \gt 6, Y \leq 3)$.

**Explanation**

Note the following:

- For a given joint PMF $f(x,y)$, we can think of each point where $f(x,y)\neq 0$ as a point mass in the plane, where the "probability mass" at each point equals the probability associated with that point.
- The joint CDF $F(x,y) = P(X \leq x, Y \leq y)$ is the total "probability mass" that lies in the infinite rectangular region that has its top-right corner at $(x,y)$ and whose sides are parallel to the coordinate axis.

Given the supports of $X$ and $Y$, the joint support $S$ is

$$
S = S_X \times S_Y = \{2, 4, 6, 8 \} \times \{1, 3, 5 \}
$$

Let's plot the points in the joint support and sketch the region corresponding to the probability we wish to find.

![](<../Source/11.1.6. The Joint CDF of Two Discrete Random Variables/Images/37f021ef82910d617b2c75f929bd652e.png>)

Notice that this region encompasses the same points in the joint support as the infinite rectangle with its top-right corner at $(8,3)$ and top-left corner at $(6,3)$, where the top and right boundaries are included, and the left boundary is excluded.

![](<../Source/11.1.6. The Joint CDF of Two Discrete Random Variables/Images/43a0282fba6376c652148d2e4702ed8a.png>)

Geometrically, this region can be obtained by taking all points in the infinite rectangle that has its top-right corner at $(8,3)$ (the blue region below) and *subtracting* all points in the infinite rectangle that has its top-right corner at $(6,3)$ (the red region below).

![](<../Source/11.1.6. The Joint CDF of Two Discrete Random Variables/Images/602e5e1a5ed4b5f4ccdd957b76a0f540.png>)

Therefore, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(X > 6, Y \le 3) &= P(X \le 8, Y \le 3)^(⏞)^(top-right corner at [math](8,3)[/math]) - P(X \le 6, Y \le 3)^(⏞)^(top-right corner at [math](6,3)[/math]) \\
&= F(8, 3) - F(6, 3) \\
&= 0.9 - 0.7 \\
&= 0.2
\end{aligned}
$$
