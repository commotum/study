## Introduction

How do we represent a double integral over a type II region as a repeated integral?

Specifically, let's consider

$$
\displaystyle \iint\limits_D (x+2y) \, \textrm d A, %= \int_a^b \int_c^d (x+2y) \,\textrm d y\, \textrm d x
$$

where $D$ is the finite region bounded by the lines $x=0$, $y=0, y=1$ and the curve $x=e^y$, as shown below.

![](<../Source/9.7.6. Double Integrals Over Type II Regions/Images/2704ffddd266856252355e1e6d6ae534.png>)

First, notice that $D$ is indeed a type II region.
To determine the right and left functions, we draw a horizontal arrow through the region, as usual.

![](<../Source/9.7.6. Double Integrals Over Type II Regions/Images/5d9acf03837efc3db6ff987d302fb90c.png>)

Hence, the region $D$ can be defined as

$$
D = \left\{(x,y) \:: \: {\color{red}0} \leq y \leq {\color{blue}1}, \quad {\color{red}0} \leq x \leq {\color{blue}e^y} \right\}
$$

Now, we can express the integral as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
∬_(D)(x + 2y)dA &= ∫_((lower limit))^((upper limit))[∫_((left function))^((right function))(x + 2y)dx]dy \\
&= ∫_{0}^{1}[∫_{0}^{e^{y}}(x + 2y)dx]dy
\end{aligned}
$$

And that's our answer! Notice that when we have a type II region,

- the limits for $x$ are functions of $y$, and
- the limits for $y$ are constants.

## Representing a Double Integral as a Repeated Integral When the Limits are Given

**Example:** If $D$ is the finite region enclosed by the curves $y=\sqrt{x-2}$ and $y=1-x$ from $y=0$ to $y=1$, as shown below, then what is $\displaystyle \iint\limits_D (x^2y+xy^2) \, \mathrm{d}A$ expressed as a repeated integral?

![](<../Source/9.7.6. Double Integrals Over Type II Regions/Images/77c8f86821d718dbfe450f51148a59e3.png>)

**Explanation**

First, let's represent $D$ as a type II region. Writing

$$
y=\sqrt{x-2}
$$

and $y=1-x$ in terms of $y$, we obtain

$$
\begin{bmatrix}y = \sqrt{x - 2} & ⟹x = y^{2} + 2 \\ y = 1 - x & ⟹x = 1 - y\end{bmatrix}
$$

To determine the left and right functions, we draw a horizontal arrow from left to right through the region, as usual.

![](<../Source/9.7.6. Double Integrals Over Type II Regions/Images/6bcc7bf82e91ec4c15377d04f25681af.png>)

As a result, we get

$$
D = \bigg\{(x,y) \:: \: 0 \leq y \leq 1, \quad 1-y \leq x \leq y^2+2 \bigg\}
$$

Therefore, we can express the integral as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
∬_(D)(x^{2}y + xy^{2})dA &= ∫_{0}^{1}∫_{1 - y}^{y^{2} + 2}(x^{2}y + xy^{2})dxdy
\end{aligned}
$$

## Representing a Double Integral as a Repeated Integral When the Limits are Not Given

**Example:** Consider the finite region $D$ in the $xy$-plane enclosed between the curves $y=x^2$ and $y=3-2x$ below the line $y=3$. What is $\displaystyle \iint\limits_{D} f\left({x,y} \right) \, \mathrm{d}A\:$ expressed as a repeated integral?

**Explanation**

First, let's make a sketch of our region.

![](<../Source/9.7.6. Double Integrals Over Type II Regions/Images/1c78772e8e145e3e09dbc2d763e7f1cb.png>)

We need to represent $D$ as a type II region. Writing $y=x^2$ and $y=3-2x$ in terms of $y$, we obtain

$$
\begin{bmatrix}y = x^{2} & ⟹x = \sqrt{y} \\ y = 3 - 2x & ⟹x = \frac{3 - y}{2}\end{bmatrix}
$$

Now, let's determine the limits of integration.

- To determine the $y$-limits, we need to find the $y$-coordinates of the points at which the curves intersect. So, we set the following equation and solve it for $y$:
$\sqrt{y}|= \frac{3 - y}{2}; 4y|= (3 - y)^{2}; 4y|= 9 - 6y + y^{2}; y^{2} - 10y + 9|= 0; (y - 1)(y - 9)|= 0$So, since our region lies below the line $y=3$, the $y$-limits are
$1 \le y \le 3$.
- To determine the left and right functions, we draw a horizontal arrow from left to right through the region, as usual.

![](<../Source/9.7.6. Double Integrals Over Type II Regions/Images/d3c6291cf3fb20c734e14cf5a74027de.png>)

As a result, we get

$$
D = \big\{(x,y) \:: \: 1 \leq y \leq 3, \quad \dfrac{3-y}{2} \leq x \leq \sqrt{y} \big\}
$$

Therefore, we can express the integral as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
∬_(D)f(x, y)dA &= ∫_{1}^{3}∫_{(3 - y)/2}^{\sqrt{y}}f(x, y)dxdy
\end{aligned}
$$

## Evaluating Repeated Integrals With Variable Limits

Suppose that we want to evaluate the repeated integral

$$
\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{e^y} (x+2) \ \mathrm{d} x \, \mathrm{d} y
$$

Notice that the domain of integration is a type II region.

To compute this integral, we must bear the following important points in mind.

- We start with the inner integral, as usual.
- When integrating with respect to $x$, we treat $y$ as though it were a constant.
- The limits of integration for the inner integral are functions of $y$. Once again, we treat $y$ as though it were a constant and substitute the integration limits as usual. After this step, there should be no more $x$'s in our integral.
- Finally, we compute the outer integral.

Let's apply these steps to solve the given problem.

First, we evaluate the inner integral by integrating with respect to $x$, treating $y$ as a constant:

$$
\begin{aligned}
∫_{0}^{1}[∫_{0}^{e^{y}}(x + 2)dx]dy &= ∫_{0}^{1}[\frac{x^{2}}{2} + 2x]_{0}^{e^{y}}dy \\
&= ∫_{0}^{1}(\frac{e^{2y}}{2} + 2e^{y})dy
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we integrate with respect to $y$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
∫_{0}^{1}(\frac{e^{2y}}{2} + 2e^{y})dy &= [\frac{e^{2y}}{4} + 2e^{y}]_{0}^{1} \\
&= \frac{e^{2}}{4} + 2e - (\frac{e^{0}}{4} + 2e^{0}) \\
&= \frac{1}{4}(e^{2} + 8e - 9)
\end{aligned}
$$

## Evaluating a Repeated Integral Defined Over a Type II Region

**Example:** Calculate $\displaystyle \int_{\sqrt{\pi}}^{\sqrt{2\pi}} \int_0^{y^2} \frac{\sin y^2}{y} \, \mathrm{d}x \,\mathrm{d}y$.

**Explanation**

The integral is defined over a type II region.

First, we evaluate the inner integral by integrating with respect to $x$, treating $y$ as a constant:

$$
\begin{aligned}
∫_{\sqrt{π}}^{\sqrt{2π}}∫_{0}^{y^{2}}\frac{\sin y^{2}}{y}dxdy &= ∫_{\sqrt{π}}^{\sqrt{2π}}\frac{\sin y^{2}}{y}[∫_{0}^{y^{2}}dx]dy \\
&= ∫_{\sqrt{π}}^{\sqrt{2π}}\frac{\sin y^{2}}{y}[x]_{0}^{y^{2}}dy \\
&= ∫_{\sqrt{π}}^{\sqrt{2π}}\frac{\sin y^{2}}{y}(y^{2} - 0)dy \\
&= ∫_{\sqrt{π}}^{\sqrt{2π}}y\sin y^{2}dy
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we integrate with respect to $y$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
∫_{\sqrt{π}}^{\sqrt{2π}}y\sin y^{2}dy &= [- \frac{1}{2}\cos y^{2}]_{\sqrt{π}}^{\sqrt{2π}} \\
&=-\frac{1}{2}(\cos 2π - \cos π) \\
&=-1
\end{aligned}
$$

## Calculating a Double Integral Defined Over a Type II Region

**Example:** Evaluate the double integral $\displaystyle \iint \limits_{D} xy^2\,\mathrm{d}A,\:$
where $D$ is the region enclosed between the curves $x^2+y^2=4$ and $x=0$, and above the line $y=-1$, as shown in the picture.

![](<../Source/9.7.6. Double Integrals Over Type II Regions/Images/d3efc009081220a488deb11b6d536fce.png>)

**Explanation**

Notice that $D$ is a type II region. Since

$$
x \ge 0
$$

we obtain the following equations in terms of $y$:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}x^{2} + y^{2} = 4 & ⟹x = \sqrt{4 - y^{2}} \\ x = 0 & ⟹x = 0\end{bmatrix}
$$

Now, let's determine the limits of integration.

- To determine the $y$-limits, we need to find the $y$-coordinates of the points at which the curves intersect. So, we set the following equation and solve it for $y$:
$\sqrt{4 - y^{2}}|= 0; 4 - y^{2}|= 0; y^{2}|= 4; y|= ± 2$.
So, since our region lies above the line $y=-1$, the $y$-limits are
$-1 \le y \le 2$.
- To determine the left and right functions, we draw a horizontal arrow from left to right through the region, as usual.

![](<../Source/9.7.6. Double Integrals Over Type II Regions/Images/8b3b3ffed1e4aeb6dd4c9e503c34687d.png>)

As a result, we get

$$
D = \left\{(x,y) \:: \: -1 \leq y \leq 2, \quad 0 \leq x \leq \sqrt{4-y^2} \right\}
$$

So, we can express the integral as follows:

$$
\iint \limits_{D} xy^2\,\mathrm{d}A = \int_{-1}^2 \int_0^{\sqrt{4-y^2}}\, xy^2\, \mathrm dx\, \mathrm{d} y
$$

First, we evaluate the inner integral by integrating with respect to $x$, treating $y$ as a constant:

$$
\begin{aligned}
∫_{-1}^{2}∫_{0}^{\sqrt{4 - y^{2}}}xy^{2}dxdy &= ∫_{-1}^{2}y^{2}[∫_{0}^{\sqrt{4 - y^{2}}}xdx]dy \\
&= ∫_{-1}^{2}y^{2}[\frac{x^{2}}{2}]_{0}^{\sqrt{4 - y^{2}}}dy \\
&= ∫_{-1}^{2}\frac{y^{2}}{2}(4 - y^{2} - 0)dy \\
&= ∫_{-1}^{2}(2y^{2} - \frac{y^{4}}{2})dy
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we integrate with respect to $y$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
∫_{-1}^{2}(2y^{2} - \frac{y^{4}}{2})dy &= [\frac{2}{3}y^{3} - \frac{y^{5}}{10}]_{-1}^{2} \\
&= (\frac{16}{3} - \frac{32}{10}) - (-\frac{2}{3} + \frac{1}{10}) \\
&= \frac{27}{10}
\end{aligned}
$$
