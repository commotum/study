## Introduction

Recall that the principal components of a standardized observation matrix $X$ are the unit eigenvectors of the covariance matrix $C$. If

$$
\lambda_1 \gt \lambda_2 \gt \cdots\gt \lambda_n
$$

are the eigenvalues of $C$ written in descending order, then:

- the $1$st principal component is the unit eigenvector $\mathbf u_1$ corresponding to the $1$st eigenvalue $\lambda_1$,
- the $2$nd principal component is the unit eigenvector $\mathbf u_2$ corresponding to the $2$nd eigenvalue $\lambda_2$,
- $\cdots$

and so on.

For example, suppose that the covariance matrix $C$ of some particular observations is

$$
\begin{bmatrix}9 & 1 \\ 1 & 9\end{bmatrix}
$$

It's easy to show that the eigenvalues of the covariance matrix $C$, in descending order, are

$$
\lambda_1=10, \qquad \lambda_2=8
$$

To find the first principal component of the data, we need the unit eigenvector corresponding to the eigenvalue

$$
\lambda_1=10
$$

Applying our usual algorithm for finding eigenvectors, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
C - 10I &= \begin{bmatrix}9 & 1 \\ 1 & 9\end{bmatrix} - 10\begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}-1 & 1 \\ 1 & -1\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, seeking a non-zero solution of

$$
(C-10I)\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{0}
$$

gives the eigenvector

$$
\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 1\end{bmatrix}
$$

Finally, dividing $\mathbf{v}_1$ by its norm

$$
\| \mathbf{v}_1 \| = \sqrt{2}
$$

we get

$$
\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 1\end{bmatrix}
$$

Therefore, the $1$st principal component of the data is $\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 1\end{bmatrix}$

**Note:** The opposite vector $\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 1\end{bmatrix}$ also works in the example above.

## Finding a Principal Component Given a 2x2 Covariance Matrix

**Example:** The covariance matrix $C$ of some particular observations is shown below.
$\begin{bmatrix}7 & 3 \\ 3 & 15\end{bmatrix}$
Find the second principal component of the data, given that the eigenvalues of $C$ are $16$ and $6$.

**Explanation**

Recall that the unit eigenvectors of the covariance matrix $C$ are called the principal components of the data. If

$$
\lambda_1 \gt \lambda_2 \gt \cdots\gt \lambda_n
$$

are the eigenvalues of $C$ written in descending order, then:

- the $1$st principal component is the unit eigenvector $\mathbf u_1$ corresponding to the $1$st eigenvalue $\lambda_1$,
- the $2$nd principal component is the unit eigenvector $\mathbf u_2$ corresponding to the $2$nd eigenvalue $\lambda_2$,
- $\cdots$

and so on.

In our case, the eigenvalues of the covariance matrix $C$, in descending order, are

$$
\lambda_1=16
$$

and

$$
\lambda_2=6
$$

So, we need the unit eigenvector that corresponds to the eigenvalue

$$
\lambda_2=6{:}
$$

Applying our usual algorithm for finding eigenvectors, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
C - 6I &= \begin{bmatrix}7 & 3 \\ 3 & 15\end{bmatrix} - 6\begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}1 & 3 \\ 3 & 9\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Seeking a non-zero solution of

$$
(C-6I)\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{0}
$$

gives the eigenvector $\begin{bmatrix}-3 \\ 1\end{bmatrix}$

Finally, dividing $\mathbf{v}_2$ by its norm

$$
\| \mathbf{v}_2 \| = \sqrt{10}
$$

we get

$$
\begin{bmatrix}-3 \\ 1\end{bmatrix}
$$

Therefore, the second principal component of the data is $\begin{bmatrix}-3 \\ 1\end{bmatrix}$

## Finding a Principal Component Given a 3x3 Covariance Matrix

**Example:** The covariance matrix $C$ of some particular observations is shown below.
$\begin{bmatrix}5 & 2 & 2 \\ 2 & 6 & 0 \\ 2 & 0 & 4\end{bmatrix}$
Find the first principal component of the data, given that the eigenvalues of $S$ are $8$, $5$, and $2$.

**Explanation**

Recall that the unit eigenvectors of the covariance matrix $C$ are called the principal components of the data. If

$$
\lambda_1 \gt \lambda_2 \gt \cdots\gt \lambda_n
$$

are the eigenvalues of $C$ written in descending order, then:

- the $1$st principal component is the unit eigenvector $u_1$ corresponding to the $1$st eigenvalue $\lambda_1$,
- the $2$nd principal component is the unit eigenvector $u_2$ corresponding to the $2$nd eigenvalue $\lambda_2$,
- $\cdots$

and so on.

In our case, the eigenvalues of the covariance matrix $C$, in descending order, are

$$
\lambda_1=8,\qquad \lambda_2=5,\qquad \lambda_3=2
$$

So, we need the unit eigenvector that corresponds to the eigenvalue

$$
\lambda_1=8{:}
$$

Applying our usual algorithm for finding eigenvectors, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
C - 8I &= \begin{bmatrix}5 & 2 & 2 \\ 2 & 6 & 0 \\ 2 & 0 & 4\end{bmatrix} - 8\begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}-3 & 2 & 2 \\ 2 & -2 & 0 \\ 2 & 0 & -4\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Seeking a non-zero solution of

$$
(C-8I)\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{0}
$$

gives the eigenvector $\begin{bmatrix}2 \\ 2 \\ 1\end{bmatrix}$

Finally, dividing $\mathbf{v}_1$ by its norm

$$
\| \mathbf{v}_1 \| =3
$$

we get

$$
\begin{bmatrix}2 \\ 2 \\ 1\end{bmatrix}
$$

Therefore, the first principal component of the data is $\begin{bmatrix}2 \\ 2 \\ 1\end{bmatrix}$
