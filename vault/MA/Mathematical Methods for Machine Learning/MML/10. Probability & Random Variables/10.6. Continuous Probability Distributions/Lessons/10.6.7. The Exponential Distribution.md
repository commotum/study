## Introduction

A continuous random variable $X$ follows an **exponential distribution** if it has the following probability density function:

$$
{λe^{- λx},\begin{vmatrix}x \ge 0 \\ 0,\end{vmatrix}otherwise
$$

The **rate parameter** $\lambda$ is a positive real number, and

$$
e\approx 2.71828
$$

is Euler's number.

If a random variable $X$ follows an exponential distribution, we write

$$
X\sim \textrm{Exp}(\lambda)
$$

The exponential distribution models the amount of continuous space or time between two events. In a future lesson, we will learn how to model situations using the exponential distribution. But for now, let's get some practice working with the PDF to compute probabilities.

## Computing a "Less Than" Probability With the Exponential Distribution

**Example:** Given that the random variable $X\sim \textrm{Exp}\left(1\right)$, calculate $P\left(X < 2\right)$. Round your final answer to four decimal places.

**Explanation**

An exponential random variable $X$ with rate $\lambda$ has the following probability density function:

$$
{λe^{- λx},\begin{vmatrix}x \ge 0 \\ 0,\end{vmatrix}otherwise
$$

We're given that

$$
X\sim \textrm{Exp} \left(1 \right)
$$

So, $X$ has the following probability density function:

$$
{e^{-x},\begin{vmatrix}x \ge 0 \\ 0,\end{vmatrix}otherwise
$$

Therefore, we can calculate

$$
P\left(X < 2\right)
$$

as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(X < 2) &= ∫_{0}^{2}f(x)dx \\
&= ∫_{0}^{2}e^{-x}dx \\
&=-e^{-x} \mid _{0}^{2} \\
&=-e^{-2} + e^{0} \\
&\approx 0.8647
\end{aligned}
$$

## Computing a "Greater Than" Probability With the Exponential Distribution

**Example:** Given that the random variable $X\sim \textrm{Exp}\left(\dfrac 23\right)$, calculate $P\left(X \geq 3\right)$. Round your final answer to four decimal places.

**Explanation**

An exponential random variable $X$ with rate $\lambda$ has the following probability density function:

$$
{λe^{- λx},\begin{vmatrix}x \ge 0 \\ 0,\end{vmatrix}otherwise
$$

We're given that

$$
X\sim \textrm{Exp} \left(\dfrac 23 \right)
$$

So, $X$ has the following probability density function:

$$
{\frac{2}{3}e^{-2x/3},\begin{vmatrix}x \ge 0 \\ 0,\end{vmatrix}otherwise
$$

Therefore, we can calculate

$$
P\left(X \geq 3\right)
$$

as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(X \ge 3) &= ∫_{3}^{∞}f(x)dx \\
&= ∫_{3}^{∞}\frac{2}{3}e^{-2x/3}dx \\
&=-e^{-2x/3} \mid _{3}^{∞} \\
&= e^{-2} \\
&\approx 0.1353
\end{aligned}
$$

## Computing a Probability on a Bounded Interval Using the Exponential Distribution

**Example:** Given that the random variable $X\sim \textrm{Exp}\left(2\right)$, calculate $P\left(\dfrac 12 < X < 2\right)$. Round your final answer to four decimal places.

**Explanation**

An exponential random variable $X$ with rate $\lambda$ has the following probability density function:

$$
{λe^{- λx},\begin{vmatrix}x \ge 0 \\ 0,\end{vmatrix}otherwise
$$

We're given that

$$
X\sim \textrm{Exp} \left(2 \right)
$$

So, $X$ has the following probability density function:

$$
{2e^{-2x},\begin{vmatrix}x \ge 0 \\ 0,\end{vmatrix}otherwise
$$

Therefore, we can calculate

$$
P\left(\dfrac 12 < X < 2\right)
$$

as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(\frac{1}{2} < X < 2) &= ∫_{1/2}^{2}f(x)dx \\
&= ∫_{1/2}^{2}2e^{-2x}dx \\
&=-e^{-2x} \mid _{1/2}^{2} \\
&=-e^{-4} + e^{-1} \\
&\approx 0.3496
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="the-memoryless-property"></a>
## The Memoryless Property

Consider a random variable $X \sim \mathrm{Exp}(\lambda)$, and two positive real numbers $s, t$.

Suppose we're given that $X>s$, and we want to determine the probability that $X>s+t$. In other words, we wish to compute the conditional probability
$P(X>s+t \,\big \mid \, X>s)$.

By the definition of conditional probability, we have that

$$
P(X>s+t \, \big \mid \, X>s) = \dfrac{P\big((X> s+t) \cap (X > s)\big)}{P(X>s)}
$$

Notice that $X>s+t$ implies that $X>s$, therefore

$$
P\big((X> s+t) \cap (X > s)\big) = P(X > s+t)
$$

So, the above conditional probability simplifies to

$$
P(X>s+t \,\big \mid \, X>s) = \dfrac{P(X> s+t)}{P(X>s)}
$$

Next, let's recall how to calculate $P(X > a)$ for some $a>0{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(X > a) &= 1 - P(X < a) \\
&= 1 - ∫_{0}^{a}λe^{- λx}dx \\
&= 1 + e^{- λx} \mid _{0}^{a} \\
&= 1 + e^{- λa} - e^{0} \\
&= e^{- λa}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, we can use this fact to calculate $P(X> s+t)$ and $P(X>s)$

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(X > s + t \mid X > s) &= (P(X > s + t))/(P(X > s)) \\
&= (e^{- λ(s + t)})/(e^{- λs}) \\
&= \frac{e^{- λs} \cdot e^{- λt}}{e^{- λs}} \\
&= \frac{e^{- λs} \cdot e^{- λt}}{e^{- λs}} \\
&= e^{- λt}
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, notice that

$$
e^{-\lambda t} = P(X > t)
$$

So, we have proved the following property:

$$
P(X>s+t \big \mid X>s) = P(X > t)
$$

This property is called **memoryless property.** It can be proved that the exponential distribution is the only continuous distribution with this property (note that the geometric distribution is the only *discrete* distribution with the memoryless property).

Intuitively, the memoryless property states that the probability of an event occurring after a certain amount of time has already elapsed is independent of how much time has passed. This means that the distribution "forgets" what has happened before time $s$.

We'll discuss how the memoryless property is used for mathematical modeling in future lessons.

<a id="a-worked-example"></a>
## A Worked Example

Given that the random variable $X\sim \textrm{Exp}(2)$, let's calculate the following probability:

$$
P\left(X > \dfrac32 \,\bigg \mid \, X > \dfrac12\right)
$$

Given a random variable $X \sim \mathrm{Exp}(\lambda)$ and two positive real numbers $s, t$, the memoryless property of the exponential distribution states that

$$
P(X>s+t \, \big \mid \, X>s) = P(X > t) = e^{-\lambda t}
$$

In our case, we have

$$
\lambda = 2, \qquad s=\dfrac 12,\qquad s+t=\dfrac32
$$

From this, we can find $t$ as follows:

$$
t = (t+s) - s = \dfrac32-\dfrac12 = 1
$$

Substituting these values into the above formula, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(X > \frac{3}{2} \mid X > \frac{1}{2}) &= e^{- λt} \\
&= e^{- (2) \cdot (1)} \\
&= e^{-2} \\
&= \frac{1}{e^{2}} \\
&\approx 0.135
\end{aligned}
$$

## Calculating Probabilities Using the Memoryless Property

**Example:** Given that the random variable $X\sim \textrm{Exp}\left(4\right)$, calculate $P\left(X \leq 7 \, \bigg \mid \, X > 5\right)$.

**Explanation**

Given a random variable $X \sim \mathrm{Exp}(\lambda)$ and two positive real numbers $s, t$, the memoryless property of the exponential distribution states that

$$
P(X>s+t \, \big \mid \, X>s) = P(X > t) = e^{-\lambda t}
$$

We can compute the complementary conditional probability as follows:

$$
P(X\leq s+t \, \big \mid \, X>s) = 1-P(X>s+t \, \big \mid \, X>s) = 1-e^{-\lambda t}
$$

In our case, we have

$$
\lambda = 4
$$

$s=5$, and

$$
s+t=7
$$

From this, we can find $t$ as follows:

$$
t = (t+s) - s =7 - 5 = 2
$$

Substituting these values into the above formula, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(X \le 7 \mid X > 5) &= 1 - e^{- λt} \\
&= 1 - e^{- (4) \cdot (2)} \\
&= 1 - e^{-8} \\
&\approx 0.999664
\end{aligned}
$$
