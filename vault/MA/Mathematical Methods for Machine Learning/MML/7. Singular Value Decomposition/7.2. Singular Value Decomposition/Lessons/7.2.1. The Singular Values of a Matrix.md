## Introduction

Let's suppose we are given the matrix

$$
\begin{bmatrix}1 & 3 \\ -3 & -1\end{bmatrix}
$$

Then, the corresponding linear transformation

$$
\mathbf x \mapsto A \mathbf x
$$

maps the unit circle

$$
\begin{aligned}
\big\{\mathbf x \, \mid \, \|\mathbf x\ &= 1 \big\}
\end{aligned}
$$

in $\mathbb R^2$ onto an ellipse

$$
\big\{A\mathbf{x} \, \mid \, \| \mathbf{x} \| =1 \big\}
$$

in $\mathbb{R}^2$, as shown below.

![](<../Source/7.2.1. The Singular Values of a Matrix/Images/a92c85eba00bbff7d00ffc66ec42158a.png>)

How can we find the maximum possible value of $\|A \mathbf x\|$? Geometrically, this is equivalent to finding the semi-major axis of our ellipse.

![](<../Source/7.2.1. The Singular Values of a Matrix/Images/dc3a25c210a9e4278b7817367f34a183.png>)

First, we note the following:

- The value of $\mathbf x$ that maximizes $\mid \mid A\mathbf x \mid \mid$ also maximizes $\mid \mid A\mathbf x \mid \mid ^2$.
- If $\mathbf v$ is a column vector, then $\mid \mid \mathbf v \mid \mid ^2 = \mathbf v^T \mathbf v$:

Therefore, we can re-write the squared distance from the origin to the point $A\mathbf{x}$ on our ellipse as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
‖Ax‖^{2} &= (Ax)^{T}(Ax) \\
&= (x^{T}A^{T})(Ax) \\
&= x^{T}(A^{T}A)x
\end{aligned}
$$

Note that $\mathbf{x}^T (A^T A) \mathbf{x}$ is a quadratic form with the matrix $A^T A$. Therefore, the maximum value of this quadratic form for

$$
\| \mathbf{x} \| =1
$$

equals the largest eigenvalue of the matrix $A^T A$.

In our case, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
A^{T}A &= \begin{bmatrix}1 & 3 \\ -3 & -1\end{bmatrix}^{T}\begin{bmatrix}1 & 3 \\ -3 & -1\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}1 & -3 \\ 3 & -1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1 & 3 \\ -3 & -1\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}10 & 6 \\ 6 & 10\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we calculate the eigenvalues of the matrix $A^TA$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mid A^{T}A - λI \mid &= 0 \\
\begin{vmatrix}10 - λ & 6 \\ 6 & 10 - λ\end{vmatrix} &= 0 \\
(10 - λ)^{2} - 36 &= 0 \\
(10 - λ)^{2} &= 36 \\
10 - λ &= ± 6 \\
λ &= 4, 16
\end{aligned}
$$

Since the largest eigenvalue of $A^T A$ is $16$, we obtain

$$
\max\limits_{\| \mathbf{x} \| = 1} \big\{\| A\mathbf{x} \|^2 \big\} = 16 \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \max\limits_{\| \mathbf{x} \| = 1} \big\{\| A\mathbf{x} \| \big\} = \sqrt{16} = 4
$$

Therefore, the length of the semi-major axis of our ellipse equals $4$.

## Finding the Semi-Major Axis of the Image of the Unit Sphere

**Example:** A $2 \times 3$ matrix $A$ maps the unit sphere $\big\{\mathbf{x} \, \mid \, \| \mathbf{x} \| =1 \big\}$ from $\mathbb{R}^3$ onto an ellipse in $\mathbb{R}^2$. Given that the eigenvalues of $A^T A$ are $7, 2$, and $0$, find the length of the ellipse's semi-major axis.

**Explanation**

The matrix $A$ maps the unit sphere

$$
\big\{\mathbf{x} \, \mid \, \| \mathbf{x} \| =1 \big\}
$$

from $\mathbb{R}^3$ onto the ellipse

$$
\big\{A\mathbf{x} \, \mid \, \| \mathbf{x} \| =1 \big\}
$$

in $\mathbb{R}^2$.

First, let's re-write the squared distance from the origin to the point $A\mathbf{x}$ on our ellipse, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
‖Ax‖^{2} &= (Ax)^{T}(Ax) \\
&= (x^{T}A^{T})(Ax) \\
&= x^{T}(A^{T}A)x
\end{aligned}
$$

where $\mathbf{x}^T (A^T A) \mathbf{x}$ is the quadratic form with the matrix $A^T A$.

The maximum value of this quadratic form, when

$$
\| \mathbf{x} \| =1
$$

is equal to the largest eigenvalue of the matrix $A^T A$.

Since the largest eigenvalue of $A^T A$ is $7$, we obtain

$$
\max\limits_{\| \mathbf{x} \| = 1} \big\{\| A\mathbf{x} \|^2 \big\} = 7 \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \max\limits_{\| \mathbf{x} \| = 1} \big\{\| A\mathbf{x} \| \big\} = \sqrt{7}
$$

Therefore, the semi-major axis of our ellipse is $\sqrt{7}$.

## The Singular Values of a Matrix

Let $A$ be an $m\times n$ matrix. Then, $A^T A$ is always a symmetric, positive semi-definite matrix. In particular, $A^TA$ can be orthogonally diagonalized, and its eigenvalues are non-negative real numbers.

The **singular values** of $A$ are defined as the square roots of the eigenvalues of the matrix $A^T A$. The $i$th singular value of $A$ is denoted $\sigma_i$:

- $\sigma_1 = \sqrt{\lambda_1}$
- $\sigma_2 = \sqrt{\lambda_2}$
- $\sigma_3 = \sqrt{\lambda_3}$$\:\vdots$
- $\sigma_n = \sqrt{\lambda_n}$

We usually list the singular values in *decreasing* order.

Now, we know that $A^TA$ is orthogonally diagonalizable. Therefore, if

$$
\left\{\mathbf v_1, \mathbf v_2\,\ldots, \mathbf v_n\right\}
$$

is an *orthonormal* basis of $\mathbb R^n$ consisting of the eigenvectors of ${A^T}{A}$ with the corresponding eigenvalues

$$
\lambda_1\geq \lambda_2\geq \ldots \geq \lambda_n
$$

then it can be shown that

- $\sigma_1 = \sqrt{\lambda_1}$ is the length of $A\mathbf v_1$,
- $\sigma_2 = \sqrt{\lambda_2}$ is the length of $A\mathbf v_2$,$\:\vdots$
- $\sigma_n = \sqrt{\lambda_n}$ is the length of $A\mathbf v_n$.

Furthermore:

- $\sigma_1$ is the maximum value of $\mid \mid A\mathbf x \mid \mid$ over all *unit* vectors $\mathbf x$, and is attained on the unit eigenvector $\mathbf v_1$
- $\sigma_2$ is the maximum value of $\mid \mid A\mathbf x \mid \mid$ over all *unit* vectors $\mathbf x$ that are orthogonal to $\mathbf v_1$, and is attained on the unit eigenvector $\mathbf v_2$
- $\sigma_3$ is the maximum value of $\mid \mid A\mathbf x \mid \mid$ over all *unit* vectors $\mathbf x$ that are orthogonal to $\mathbf v_1$ *and* $\mathbf v_2$, and is attained on the unit eigenvector $\mathbf v_3$$\:\vdots$
- $\sigma_n$ is the maximum value of $\mid \mid A\mathbf x \mid \mid$ over all *unit* vectors $\mathbf x$ that are orthogonal to $\mathbf v_1,\mathbf v_2,\ldots,\mathbf v_{n-1}$, and is attained on the unit eigenvector $\mathbf v_n$

Let's take a look at a concrete example.

## Associating Singular Values With an Ellipse's Principal Axes

We've seen that for the matrix $A$, given by

$$
\begin{bmatrix}1 & 3 \\ -3 & -1\end{bmatrix}
$$

the eigenvalues of $A^TA$ are

$$
\lambda_1=16
$$

and

$$
\lambda_2=4
$$

It's easy to show that the corresponding unit eigenvectors of $A^T A$ are

$$
\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 1\end{bmatrix}
$$

The singular values of $A$ are

$$
\begin{aligned}
σ_{1} &= \sqrt{λ_{1}} = \sqrt{16} = 4, \\
σ_{2} &= \sqrt{λ_{2}} = \sqrt{4} = 2
\end{aligned}
$$

Putting all of this information together gives us the following diagram.

![](<../Source/7.2.1. The Singular Values of a Matrix/Images/588c3ea89534e1300ac6affdbd35f525.png>)

Note that our ellipse's semi-major and semi-minor axes are

$$
\sigma_1 = 4
$$

and

$$
\sigma_2 =2
$$

respectively.

## Finding the Semi-Major Axis of the Image of the Unit Sphere Given the Corresponding Singular Values

**Example:** A $2 \times 3$ matrix $A$ maps the unit sphere $\big\{\mathbf{x} \, \mid \, \| \mathbf{x} \| =1 \big\}$ from $\mathbb{R}^3$ onto an ellipse in $\mathbb{R}^2$. Given that the singular values of $A$ are $\sigma_1=4$, $\sigma_2=2$, and $\sigma_3 = 0$, find the length of the semi-major axis of the ellipse.

**Explanation**

The matrix $A$ maps the unit sphere

$$
\big\{\mathbf{x} \, \mid \, \| \mathbf{x} \| =1 \big\}
$$

from $\mathbb{R}^3$ onto the ellipse

$$
\big\{A\mathbf{x} \, \mid \, \| \mathbf{x} \| =1 \big\}
$$

in $\mathbb{R}^2$.

First, let's re-write the squared distance from the origin to the point $A\mathbf{x}$ on our ellipse, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
‖Ax‖^{2} &= (Ax)^{T}(Ax) \\
&= (x^{T}A^{T})(Ax) \\
&= x^{T}(A^{T}A)x
\end{aligned}
$$

where $\mathbf{x}^T (A^T A) \mathbf{x}$ is the quadratic form with the symmetric matrix $A^T A$.

The maximum value of this quadratic form, when

$$
\| \mathbf{x} \| =1
$$

is equal to the largest eigenvalue of the matrix $A^T A$.

The square roots of the eigenvalues of $A^T A$ are called the *singular values* of $A$. Since the largest singular value of $A$ is $\sigma_1=4$, the largest eigenvalue of $A^T A$ must be

$$
\lambda_1 = \sigma_1^2=4^2
$$

So, we obtain that

$$
\max\limits_{\| \mathbf{x} \| = 1} \big\{\| A\mathbf{x} \|^2 \big\} = 4^2 \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \max\limits_{\| \mathbf{x} \| = 1} \big\{\| A\mathbf{x} \| \big\} = 4
$$

Therefore, the semi-major axis of our ellipse is $\sigma_1=4$.

## Solving Problems Regarding the Image of the Unit Eigenvectors of a Quadratic Form

**Example:** A $2 \times 2$ matrix $A$ maps the unit circle $\big\{\mathbf{x} \, \mid \, \| \mathbf{x} \| =1 \big\}$ from $\mathbb{R}^2$ onto an ellipse in $\mathbb{R}^2$. The corresponding ellipse and its principal axes are depicted in the diagram above. Given that $\mathbf v_1$ and $\mathbf v_2$ are unit eigenvectors of $A^TA$, find an eigenvector corresponding to the eigenvalue $36$ of $A^T A$?

![](<../Source/7.2.1. The Singular Values of a Matrix/Images/ba65b415e5958ff6ec13493e6641c014.png>)

**Explanation**

Suppose $A$ is an $m\times n$ matrix. Let

$$
\left\{\mathbf v_1, \mathbf v_2\,\ldots, \mathbf v_n\right\}
$$

be an *orthonormal* basis of $\mathbb R^n$ consisting of the eigenvectors of $A^T A$ with corresponding eigenvalues

$$
\lambda_1\geq \lambda_2\geq \ldots \geq \lambda_n
$$

Then,

- $\sigma_1 = \sqrt{\lambda_1}$ is the length of $A\mathbf v_1$,
- $\sigma_2 = \sqrt{\lambda_2}$ is the length of $A\mathbf v_2$,
- $\cdots$

In our example, we see that

- $\mid \mid A\mathbf v_1 \mid \mid = 8 = \sigma_1$, which means that $\lambda_1 = 8^2 = 64$, and
- $\mid \mid A\mathbf v_2 \mid \mid = 6 = \sigma_2$, which means that $\lambda_2 = 6^2 = 36$.

Therefore, an eigenvector corresponding to the eigenvalue

$$
\lambda_2 = 36
$$

of $A^T A$ is $\mathbf{v}_2$.
