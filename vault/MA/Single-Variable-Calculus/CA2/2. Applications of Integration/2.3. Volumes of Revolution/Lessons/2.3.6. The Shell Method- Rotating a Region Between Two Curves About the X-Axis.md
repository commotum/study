## Introduction

Consider the region enclosed by the curves $x=f(y)$ on the right, $x=g(y)$ on the left, and the horizontal lines $y=c$ and $y=d$, as shown below. We assume that

$$
f(y) \geq g(y)
$$

for all $y \in [c,d]$.

![](<../Source/2.3.6. The Shell Method- Rotating a Region Between Two Curves About the X-Axis/Images/fd14916a98a5a550c11714d87644fa4a.png>)

To find the volume of the solid generated when this region is rotated $2\pi$ radians about the $x$-axis, we use the shell method as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
V &= 2π∫_{c}^{d}y([right] - [left])dy \\
&= 2π∫_{c}^{d}y([f(y)] - [g(y)])dy
\end{aligned}
$$

## Constructing an Integral Representing the Volume of a Solid

**Example:** Consider the finite region bounded by the curves $x=2-y^2$, $x=y$, and the $x$-axis, as shown below. Using the shell method, find an integral expression for the volume generated when this region is rotated $2\pi$ radians about the $x$-axis.

![](<../Source/2.3.6. The Shell Method- Rotating a Region Between Two Curves About the X-Axis/Images/e54f23ad906170b0f9f26e8b54e8616f.png>)

**Explanation**

First, let

$$
f(y)=2-y^2
$$

and $g(y)=y$.

We start by determining the points where the curves intersect by solving the following equation for $y$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
f(y) &= g(y) \\
2 - y^{2} &= y \\
y^{2} + y - 2 &= 0 \\
(y + 2)(y - 1) &= 0 \\
y &= -2, 1
\end{aligned}
$$

The solution corresponding to our region is $y=1$. So, we need to integrate from $y=0$ to $y=1$.

Now, let's draw our usual horizontal arrow through the region to determine the left and right functions.

![](<../Source/2.3.6. The Shell Method- Rotating a Region Between Two Curves About the X-Axis/Images/9b4c50c1b6151ddd49e2ca36321f5f3e.png>)

The horizontal arrow

- *enters* the region through the left function $x=g(y)=y$, and
- *leaves* through the right function $x=f(y)=2-y^2$.

Therefore, using the shell method, the volume of the solid can be expressed as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
V &= 2π∫_{c}^{d}y([right] - [left])dy \\
&= 2π∫_{0}^{1}y([2 - y^{2}] - [y])dy \\
&= 2π∫_{0}^{1}y(2 - y - y^{2})dy
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding the Volume of a Solid Using the Shell Method

**Example:** Consider the finite region bounded by the curves $x=3y-y^2$ and $x=y$, as shown below. Calculate the volume of the solid generated when this region is rotated $2\pi$ radians about the $x$-axis.

![](<../Source/2.3.6. The Shell Method- Rotating a Region Between Two Curves About the X-Axis/Images/b1ac331db408077238d2f3f5bbe2dda8.png>)

**Explanation**

First, let

$$
f(y)=3y-y^2
$$

and $g(y)=y$.

We start by determining the points where the curves intersect by solving the following equation for $y$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
f(y) &= g(y) \\
3y - y^{2} &= y \\
2y - y^{2} &= 0 \\
y(2 - y) &= 0 \\
y &= 0, 2
\end{aligned}
$$

The solutions corresponding to our region are $y=0$ and $y=2$. So, we need to integrate from $y=0$ to $y=2$.

Now, let's draw our usual horizontal arrow through the region to determine the left and right functions.

![](<../Source/2.3.6. The Shell Method- Rotating a Region Between Two Curves About the X-Axis/Images/8cd13b86a725c6a40528ab50ad03f3d9.png>)

The horizontal arrow

- *enters* the region through the left function $x=g(y)=y$, and
- *leaves* through the right function $x=f(y)=3y-y^2$.

Therefore, using the shell method, the volume of the solid can be found as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
V &= 2π∫_{c}^{d}y([right] - [left])dy \\
&= 2π∫_{0}^{2}y([3y - y^{2}] - [y])dy \\
&= 2π∫_{0}^{2}y(2y - y^{2})dy \\
&= 2π∫_{0}^{2}(2y^{2} - y^{3})dy \\
&= 2π(2∫_{0}^{2}y^{2}dy - ∫_{0}^{2}y^{3}dy) \\
&= 2π(2 \cdot \frac{y^{3}}{3} \mid _{0}^{2} - \frac{y^{4}}{4} \mid _{0}^{2}) \\
&= 2π(2(\frac{8}{3} - 0) - (\frac{16}{4} - 0)) \\
&= 2π(\frac{16}{3} - 4) \\
&= \frac{8π}{3}
\end{aligned}
$$
