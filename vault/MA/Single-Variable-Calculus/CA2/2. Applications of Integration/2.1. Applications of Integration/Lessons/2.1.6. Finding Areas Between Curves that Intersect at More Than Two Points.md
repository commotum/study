## Introduction

Let's find the total area of the finite region enclosed between curves the curves

$$
f(x)=x^3-2x
$$

and $g(x)=2x$, as shown below.

![](<../Source/2.1.6. Finding Areas Between Curves that Intersect at More Than Two Points/Images/3c7a51fa48c473bc731150ff5b9b8cad.png>)

First, we find the points where the curves intersect:

$$
\begin{aligned}
f(x) &= g(x) \\
x^{3} - 2x &= 2x \\
x^{3} - 4x &= 0 \\
x(x^{2} - 4) &= 0 \\
x(x - 2)(x + 2) &= 0 \\
x &= 0, 2,-2
\end{aligned}
$$

The area we want consists of two symmetric regions: $A_1$ and $A_2$.

![](<../Source/2.1.6. Finding Areas Between Curves that Intersect at More Than Two Points/Images/e872ed977270eb137c70fb22b19d3516.png>)

For the area $A_1$, we have $x \in [-2,0]$, and the upper function is $f(x)$ while the lower function is $g(x)$. So, the area is given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
A_{1} &= ∫_{-2}^{0}(f(x) - g(x))dx \\
&= ∫_{-2}^{0}((x^{3} - 2x) - 2x)dx \\
&= ∫_{-2}^{0}(x^{3} - 4x)dx \\
&= ∫_{-2}^{0}x^{3}dx - 4∫_{-2}^{0}xdx \\
&= \frac{x^{4}}{4} \mid _{-2}^{0} - 4 \cdot \frac{x^{2}}{2} \mid _{-2}^{0} \\
&= (0 - 4) - 4(0 - 2) \\
&=-4 + 8 \\
&= 4
\end{aligned}
$$

Since the regions are symmetric, we have that $A_2=A_1=4$.

Finally, the required area is given by

$$
A = A_1 + A_2 = 4 + 4 = 8
$$

## Constructing an Integral Expression That Represents the Area Between Two Curves

**Example:** Which expression represents the total finite area enclosed between the curves $y=-x^5$ and $y=-x$, as shown below?

![](<../Source/2.1.6. Finding Areas Between Curves that Intersect at More Than Two Points/Images/b3f40177a26c641f2e6e49e2f120cfea.png>)

**Explanation**

Let

$$
f(x)=-x^5
$$

and $g(x)=-x$. First, we find the points where the two curves intersect:

$$
\begin{aligned}
f(x) &= g(x) \\
- x^{5} &= -x \\
x^{5} - x &= 0 \\
x(x^{4} - 1) &= 0 \\
x(x^{2} - 1)(x^{2} + 1) &= 0 \\
x(x - 1)(x + 1)(x^{2} + 1) &= 0 \\
x &= -1, 0, 1
\end{aligned}
$$

The area we want consists of two regions: $A_1$ and $A_2$.

![](<../Source/2.1.6. Finding Areas Between Curves that Intersect at More Than Two Points/Images/48dc4b25b85c02df816a9b9f2ebc21e8.png>)

- For the area $A_1$, we have $x \in [-1,0]$, and the upper function is $g(x)$ while the lower function is $f(x)$. So, the area is given by
$A_{1}|= ∫_{-1}^{0}[g(x) - f(x)]dx; = ∫_{-1}^{0}[(-x) - (-x^{5})]dx; = ∫_{-1}^{0}(x^{5} - x)dx$.
- For the area $A_2$, we have $x \in [0,1]$, and the upper function is $f(x)$ while the lower function is $g(x)$. So, the area is given by
$A_{2}|= ∫_{0}^{1}[f(x) - g(x)]dx; = ∫_{0}^{1}[(-x^{5}) - (-x)]dx; = ∫_{0}^{1}(x - x^{5})dx$.

Therefore, the required area is given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= A_{1} + A_{2} \\
&= ∫_{-1}^{0}(x^{5} - x)dx + ∫_{0}^{1}(x - x^{5})dx
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding the Area Between Two Curves Expressed as Functions of X That Intersect at Three Points

**Example:** Find the total finite area enclosed between the curves $y=f(x)=x^3+x^2+1$ and $y=g(x)=x^2+x+1$, as shown below.

![](<../Source/2.1.6. Finding Areas Between Curves that Intersect at More Than Two Points/Images/aa6eb324b450646b4af374bc46b044a0.png>)

*(Note: Figure not drawn to scale)*

**Explanation**

First, we find the intersections of the two curves:

$$
\begin{aligned}
f(x) &= g(x) \\
x^{3} + x^{2} + 1 &= x^{2} + x + 1 \\
x^{3} - x &= 0 \\
x(x^{2} - 1) &= 0 \\
x &= 0, ± 1
\end{aligned}
$$

The area we want consists of two regions: $A_1$ and $A_2$.

![](<../Source/2.1.6. Finding Areas Between Curves that Intersect at More Than Two Points/Images/105c571b28c60f315fc036cad7c14b35.png>)

- For the area $A_1$, we have $x \in [-1,0]$, and the upper function is $f(x)$ while the lower function is $g(x)$. So, the area is given by
$A_{1}|= ∫_{-1}^{0}(f(x) - g(x))dx; = ∫_{-1}^{0}((x^{3} + x^{2} + 1) - (x^{2} + x + 1))dx; = ∫_{-1}^{0}(x^{3} - x)dx; = (\frac{x^{4}}{4} - \frac{x^{2}}{2}) \mid _{-1}^{0}; = 0 - (\frac{1}{4} - \frac{1}{2}); = 0 - (-\frac{1}{4}); = \frac{1}{4}$.

- For the area $A_2$, we have $x \in [0,1]$, and the upper function is $g(x)$ while the lower function is $f(x)$. So, the area is given by
$A_{2}|= ∫_{0}^{1}(g(x) - f(x))dx; = ∫_{-1}^{0}((x^{2} + x + 1) - (x^{3} + x^{2} + 1))dx; = - ∫_{0}^{1}(x^{3} - x)dx; = - (\frac{x^{4}}{4} - \frac{x^{2}}{2}) \mid _{0}^{1}; = - (\frac{1}{4} - \frac{1}{2}) + 0; = \frac{1}{4}$.

Therefore, the required area is

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= A_{1} + A_{2} = \frac{1}{4} + \frac{1}{4} = \frac{1}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding the Area Between Two Curves Expressed as Functions of Y That Intersect at Three Points

**Example:** What is the total finite area enclosed between the curves $x=y$ and $x=y^{1/7}$, as shown below?

![](<../Source/2.1.6. Finding Areas Between Curves that Intersect at More Than Two Points/Images/da26159f74ded7df536e7b093d6e9adb.png>)

**Explanation**

Let

$$
\color{black}f(y)=y\,
$$

and

$$
\, \color{black}g(y)= y^{1/7}
$$

First, we find the intersections of the two curves:

$$
\begin{aligned}
f(y) &= g(y) \\
y &= y^{1/7} \\
y^{7} &= y \\
y(y^{6} - 1) &= 0 \\
y(y^{3} - 1)(y^{3} + 1) &= 0 \\
y &= 0, ± 1
\end{aligned}
$$

The area we want consists of two regions: $A_1$ and $A_2$.

![](<../Source/2.1.6. Finding Areas Between Curves that Intersect at More Than Two Points/Images/4b95530788397c616f286787022825e7.png>)

- For the area $A_1$, we have $y \in [-1,0]$, and the right function is $f(y)$ while the left function is $g(y)$. So, the area is given by
$A_{1}|= ∫_{-1}^{0}(f(y) - g(y))dy; = ∫_{-1}^{0}(y - y^{1/7})dy; = (\frac{1}{2}y^{2} - \frac{7}{8}y^{8/7}) \mid _{-1}^{0}; = 0 - (\frac{1}{2} - \frac{7}{8}); = \frac{3}{8}$.
- For the area $A_2$, we have $y \in [0,1]$, and the right function is $g(y)$ while the left function is $f(y)$. So, the area is given by
$A_{2}|= ∫_{0}^{1}(g(y) - f(y))dy; = ∫_{0}^{1}(y^{1/7} - y)dy; = (\frac{7}{8}y^{8/7} - \frac{1}{2}y^{2}) \mid _{0}^{1}; = (\frac{7}{8} - \frac{1}{2}) - 0; = \frac{3}{8}$.

Therefore, the required area is

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= A_{1} + A_{2} = \frac{3}{8} + \frac{3}{8} = \frac{3}{4}
\end{aligned}
$$
