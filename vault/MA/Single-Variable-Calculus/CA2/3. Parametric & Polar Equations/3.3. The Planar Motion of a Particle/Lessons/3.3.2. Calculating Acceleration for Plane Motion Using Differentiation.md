## Introduction

When a particle moves in two dimensions, we find the acceleration vector $\mathbf{a}$ by differentiating the velocity vector

$$
\mathbf{v} = \left< u,\, v \right>
$$

with respect to $t$:

$$
a = \frac{dv}{dt} = ⟨\frac{du}{dt}, \frac{dv}{dt}⟩
$$

In other words, we need to differentiate both components of the velocity to get the acceleration.

---

<a id="finding-the-acceleration-vector-of-a-particle-given-its-velocity-vector"></a>
## Finding the Acceleration Vector of a Particle Given Its Velocity Vector

**Example:** The velocity vector $\mathbf{v} = \langle u, v \rangle$ of a particle $P$ at time $t$ is given by
$\mathbf{v} = \langle 12t+t^2, t^3+1\rangle$.
Calculate the acceleration vector $\mathbf{a}$.

**Explanation**

To compute the acceleration vector, we differentiate the velocity vector:

$$
\begin{aligned}
a &= \frac{dv}{dt} \\
&= ⟨\frac{du}{dt}, \frac{dv}{dt}⟩ \\
&= ⟨\frac{d}{dt}(12t + t^{2}), \frac{d}{dt}(t^{3} + 1)⟩ \\
&= ⟨12 + 2t, 3t^{2}⟩
\end{aligned}
$$

## Computing the Acceleration of a Particle at a Given Moment Given Its Velocity Vector

**Example:** The velocity vector $\mathbf{v} = \langle u, v \rangle$, measured in $\textrm{m}/\textrm s$, of a particle $P$ at time $t$ seconds, is given by
$\mathbf{v} = \langle t^4-t^2, 2t^3\rangle$.
Find the acceleration vector and the magnitude of the acceleration of $P$ at time $t=1$.

**Explanation**

To compute the acceleration vector, we differentiate the velocity vector:

$$
\begin{aligned}
a &= \frac{dv}{dt} \\
&= ⟨\frac{du}{dt}, \frac{dv}{dt}⟩ \\
&= ⟨\frac{d}{dt}(t^{4} - t^{2}), \frac{d}{dt}(2t^{3})⟩ \\
&= ⟨4t^{3} - 2t, 6t^{2}⟩
\end{aligned}
$$

To find the acceleration at time $t=1$, we substitute $t=1$ into the above:

$$
\begin{aligned}
a(1) &= ⟨4(1)^{3} - 2(1), 6(1)^{2}⟩ \\
&= ⟨2, 6⟩
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, to find the magnitude of the acceleration, we compute $\mid \mathbf a(1) \mid$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\begin{vmatrix}a(1) & &= & ⟨2, 6⟩\end{vmatrix} \\
&= \sqrt{2^{2} + 6^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{40} \\
&= 2\sqrt{10}m/s^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding the Acceleration Vector of a Particle Given Its Position Vector

**Example:** The position vector $\mathbf{r}$ of a particle $P$ (relative to a fixed origin $O$) at time $t$ is given by
$\mathbf{r} = \langle 4t^2+1, 3t^2-t\rangle$.
Calculate the acceleration vector $\mathbf{a}$.

**Explanation**

To compute the acceleration vector, we need to differentiate the velocity vector. So first, we need to compute the velocity vector. We can do this by differentiating the position vector:

$$
\begin{aligned}
v &= \frac{dr}{dt} \\
&= ⟨\frac{dx}{dt}, \frac{dy}{dt}⟩ \\
&= ⟨\frac{d}{dt}(4t^{2} + 1), \frac{d}{dt}(3t^{2} - t)⟩ \\
&= ⟨8t, 6t - 1⟩
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, to compute the acceleration vector, we differentiate the velocity vector:

$$
\begin{aligned}
a &= \frac{dv}{dt} \\
&= ⟨\frac{du}{dt}, \frac{dv}{dt}⟩ \\
&= ⟨\frac{d}{dt}(8t), \frac{d}{dt}(6t - 1)⟩ \\
&= ⟨8, 6⟩
\end{aligned}
$$

## Computing the Acceleration of a Particle at a Given Moment Given Its Position Vector

**Example:** The position vector $\mathbf{r}$, in meters, of a particle $P$ relative to a fixed origin $O$ at time $t$ seconds is given by
$\mathbf{r} = \left\langle t^{5}-t^2,\, t^2+6t-3 \right\rangle$.
Calculate the acceleration vector and the magnitude of the acceleration of $P$ at time $t=0$.

**Explanation**

To compute the acceleration vector, we need to differentiate the velocity vector. So first, we need to compute the velocity vector, which we can do this by differentiating the position vector:

$$
\begin{aligned}
v &= \frac{dr}{dt} \\
&= ⟨\frac{dx}{dt}, \frac{dy}{dt}⟩ \\
&= ⟨\frac{d}{dt}(t^{5} - t^{2}), \frac{d}{dt}(t^{2} + 6t - 3)⟩ \\
&= ⟨5t^{4} - 2t, 2t + 6⟩
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, to compute the acceleration vector, we differentiate the velocity vector:

$$
\begin{aligned}
a &= \frac{dv}{dt} \\
&= ⟨\frac{du}{dt}, \frac{dv}{dt}⟩ \\
&= ⟨\frac{d}{dt}(5t^{4} - 2t), \frac{d}{dt}(2t + 6)⟩ \\
&= ⟨20t^{3} - 2, 2⟩
\end{aligned}
$$

To find the acceleration at time $t=0$, we substitute $t=0$ into the above:

$$
\begin{aligned}
a(0) &= ⟨20(0)^{3} - 2, 2⟩ \\
&= ⟨ - 2, 2⟩
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, to find the magnitude of the acceleration, we compute $\mid \mathbf a(0) \mid$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\begin{vmatrix}a(0) & &= & ⟨ - 2, 2⟩\end{vmatrix} \\
&= \sqrt{(-2)^{2} + 2^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{8} \\
&= 2\sqrt{2}m/s^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$
