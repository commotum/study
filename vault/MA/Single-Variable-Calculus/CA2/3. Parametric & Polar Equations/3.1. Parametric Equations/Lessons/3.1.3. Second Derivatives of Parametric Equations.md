## Introduction

For a parametrically defined function

$$
x = x(t), y = y(t)
$$

we know that the derivative is given by

$$
\frac{dy}{dx} = (y^{′}(t))/(x^{′}(t))
$$

To calculate the second derivative

$$
\dfrac{\textrm d^2 y}{\textrm d x^2}
$$

we differentiate

$$
\dfrac{\textrm d y}{\textrm d x}
$$

with respect to $x$ and use the chain rule, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{d^{2}y}{dx^{2}} &= \frac{d}{dx}(\frac{dy}{dx}) \\
&= \frac{d}{dt}(\frac{dy}{dx}) \cdot \frac{dt}{dx} \\
&= (\frac{d}{dt}(\frac{dy}{dx}))/((\frac{dx}{dt}))
\end{aligned}
$$

For example, let's calculate

$$
\dfrac{\textrm d^2 y}{\textrm d x^2}
$$

for the curve defined by the parametric equations

$$
x= t^2, \qquad y= 2 t^3, \qquad t\in(-\infty,\infty)
$$

To calculate

$$
\dfrac{\textrm{d}y}{\textrm{d}x}
$$

we first need to compute the derivatives of $x$ and $y$. We get

$$
x'(t) = 2t, \qquad y'(t) = 6t^2
$$

So, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{dy}{dx} &= (y^{′}(t))/(x^{′}(t)) = \frac{6t^{2}}{2t} = 3t
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, computing the second derivative, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{d^{2}y}{dx^{2}} &= (\frac{d}{dt}(\frac{dy}{dx}))/((\frac{dx}{dt})) \\
&= (\frac{d}{dt}(3t))/(2t) \\
&= \frac{3}{2t}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding the Second Derivative of a Parametric Curve Given the First Derivative

**Example:** A curve is defined by the parametric equations $x =e^{t}, \, y= e^{2t}+1$. Given that $\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}= 2e^t$, calculate $\dfrac{\textrm d^2 y}{\textrm d x^2}$.

**Explanation**

To compute the second derivative, we can use the formula

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{d^{2}y}{dx^{2}} &= (\frac{d}{dt}(\frac{dy}{dx}))/((\frac{dx}{dt}))
\end{aligned}
$$

We're given that

$$
\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}= 2e^t
$$

and we can calculate

$$
\dfrac{\mathrm dx}{\mathrm dt}
$$

as follows:

$$
\dfrac{\mathrm dx}{\mathrm dt} = \dfrac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dt} (e^t) = e^t
$$

Substituting into the formula, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{d^{2}y}{dx^{2}} &= (\frac{d}{dt}(2e^{t}))/(e^{t}) \\
&= \frac{2e^{t}}{e^{t}} \\
&= 2
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding the Second Derivative of a Parametric Curve

**Example:** A curve is defined by the parametric equations $x=e^t, y=2e^{2t}$. Calculate $\dfrac{\textrm d^2 y}{\textrm d x^2}$.

**Explanation**

To find the first derivative, we will use the formula

$$
\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x} = \dfrac{y'(t)}{x'(t)}
$$

Differentiating $x$ and $y$, we get

$$
x'(t) = e^t, \qquad y'(t)=4e^{2t}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x} = \dfrac{4e^{2t}}{e^t} = 4e^t
$$

To compute the second derivative, we can use the formula

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{d^{2}y}{dx^{2}} &= (\frac{d}{dt}(\frac{dy}{dx}))/((\frac{dx}{dt}))
\end{aligned}
$$

Substituting into the formula, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{d^{2}y}{dx^{2}} &= (\frac{d}{dt}(4e^{t}))/(e^{t}) \\
&= \frac{4e^{t}}{e^{t}} \\
&= 4
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="calculating-the-derivative-of-a-parametric-curve-at-a-given-point"></a>
## Calculating the Derivative of a Parametric Curve at a Given Point

**Example:** A curve is defined by the parametric equations

$x = \sin(2t), \qquad y= 2\cos (2t) \qquad 0 \leq t \lt \pi$.

Given that $\dfrac{\textrm d y}{\textrm d x} = -2 \tan(2t)$, calculate $\dfrac{\textrm d^2 y}{\textrm d x^2}$ at the point where $t=\dfrac\pi 8$.

**Explanation**

To compute

$$
\dfrac{\textrm d^2 y}{\textrm d x^2}
$$

we can use the formula

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{d^{2}y}{dx^{2}} &= (\frac{d}{dt}(\frac{dy}{dx}))/((\frac{dx}{dt}))
\end{aligned}
$$

We're given that

$$
\dfrac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}x}= -2 \tan(2t)
$$

and we can calculate

$$
\dfrac{\mathrm dx}{\mathrm dt}
$$

as follows:

$$
\dfrac{\mathrm dx}{\mathrm dt} = \dfrac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dt} (\sin 2t) = 2 \cos(2t)
$$

Substituting into the formula, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{d^{2}y}{dx^{2}} &= (\frac{d}{dt}(-2\tan 2t))/(2\cos (2t)) \\
&= (-4\sec^{2} (2t))/(2\cos (2t)) \\
&=-4\sec^{2} (2t) \cdot \frac{1}{2}\sec (2t) \\
&=-2\sec^{3} (2t)
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we evaluate at

$$
t=\dfrac{\pi}{8}
$$

and we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{d^{2}y}{dx^{2}} \mid _{t = π/8} &= -2\sec^{3} (2 \cdot \frac{π}{8}) \\
&=-2\sec^{3} (\frac{π}{4}) \\
&=-2(\sqrt{2})^{3} \\
&=-4\sqrt{2}
\end{aligned}
$$
