## Introduction

Consider the function $f(x)$ below:

$$
{\frac{x^{2} - 1}{x^{2} + x - 2},\begin{vmatrix}x \ne 1 \\ k,\end{vmatrix}x = 1
$$

The function $f(x)$ seems to have a point discontinuity at $x=1$. However, by selecting a particular value for $k$, we can remove the point discontinuity and make the function continuous.

We can find this value of $k$ by forcing the limit of the function to be equal to the value of the function at $x=1$:

$$
\lim\limits_{x\to 1} f(x) = f(1)
$$

Remember that $\lim\limits_{x\to 1} f(x)$ represents the value that $f(x)$ approaches as $x$ gets closer and closer, but not equal, to $1$. So, when evaluating $\lim\limits_{x\to 1} f(x)$, we need to use the function definition for

$$
x \neq 1
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
lim_(x → 1)f(x) &= lim_(x → 1)\frac{x^{2} - 1}{x^{2} + x - 2} \\
&= lim_(x → 1)((x - 1)(x + 1))/((x - 1)(x + 2)) \\
&= lim_(x → 1)((x - 1)(x + 1))/((x - 1)(x + 2)) \\
&= lim_(x → 1)\frac{x + 1}{x + 2} \\
&= \frac{1 + 1}{1 + 2} \\
&= \frac{2}{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

At $x=1$, the function is defined as

$$
f(x)=k
$$

So,

$$
f(1)=k
$$

, and for continuity we must have

$$
\begin{aligned}
lim_(x → 1)f(x) &= f(1) \\
\frac{2}{3} &= k
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, to make $f(x)$ continuous at $x=1$, we need to choose

$$
k=\dfrac{2}{3}
$$

## Removing Point Discontinuities From Rational Functions

**Example:** Let $g(x)$ be the function defined below. For what value(s) of $k$ is $g(x)$ continuous at $x=-1$?
${\frac{x^{3} + 1}{kx + k},\begin{vmatrix}x \ne - 1 \\ \frac{k}{3},\end{vmatrix}x =-1$

**Explanation**

First, we factor the numerator and denominator and cancel any common factors:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{x^{3} + 1}{kx + k} &= ((x + 1)(x^{2} - x + 1))/(k(x + 1)) \\
&= ((x + 1)(x^{2} - x + 1))/(k(x + 1)) \\
&= \frac{x^{2} - x + 1}{k}
\end{aligned}
$$

Now we can compute the limit at $x=-1$, and get

$$
\lim_\limits{x\to -1} g(x) = \lim_\limits{x\to -1} \left(\dfrac{x^2-x+1}{k}\right) = \dfrac{3}{k}
$$

For $g(x)$ to be continuous at $x=-1$, we must have

$$
\begin{aligned}
lim_(x → - 1)g(x) &= g(-1) \\
\frac{3}{k} &= \frac{k}{3} \\
9 &= k^{2} \\
± 3 &= k
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, for the function to be continuous at $x=-1$, the value of $k$ must be either $-3$ or $3$.

## Removing Jump Discontinuities From Rational Functions

**Example:** Let $f(x)$ be the function defined below. Find the values of $k$ for which $f(x)$ is continuous at $x=2$.
${\frac{x^{2} + 4x - 12}{x^{2} - 4},\begin{vmatrix}x < 2 \\ 2^{kx},\end{vmatrix}x \ge 2$.

**Explanation**

From the definition of $f(x)$, the left-sided limit of $f(x)$ at $x=2$ is

$$
\begin{aligned}
lim_(x → 2^{-})f(x) &= lim_(x → 2^{-})\frac{x^{2} + 4x - 12}{x^{2} - 4} \\
&= lim_(x → 2^{-})((x - 2)(x + 6))/((x - 2)(x + 2)) \\
&= lim_(x → 2^{-})((x - 2)(x + 6))/((x - 2)(x + 2)) \\
&= lim_(x → 2^{-})\frac{x + 6}{x + 2} \\
&= \frac{2 + 6}{2 + 2} \\
&= 2
\end{aligned}
$$

On the other hand, the right-sided limit is

$$
\begin{aligned}
lim_(x → 2^{+})f(x) &= lim_(x → 2^{+})2^{kx} \\
&= 2^{2k}
\end{aligned}
$$

We also note that

$$
f(2) = 2^{2k}
$$

So, for $f(x)$ to be continuous at $x=2$, we must have

$$
\begin{aligned}
lim_(x → 2^{+})f(x) &= lim_(x → 2^{-})f(x) \\
2^{2k} &= 2 \\
2^{2k} &= 2^{1} \\
2k &= 1 \\
k &= \frac{1}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
k=\dfrac12
$$

removes the discontinuity at $x=2$.
