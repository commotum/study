## Introduction

Consider the graph of the function $y = f(x)$ shown below.

![](<../Source/4.1.6. Calculating Derivatives From Graphs Using the Chain Rule/Images/847860614194c2c6c21a464a9368cc41.png>)

Suppose we define a new function $h(x)$ as

$$
h(x)=\left({f(x)}\right)^5
$$

Let's use our knowledge of derivatives to find $h'(2)$, the derivative of $h$ at $x=2$.

To find an expression for $h'(x)$ in terms of $f(x)$, we differentiate $h(x)$ using the chain rule. This gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
h^{′}(x) &= \frac{d}{dx}(f(x))^{5} \\
&= 5(f(x))^{4} \cdot f^{′}(x)
\end{aligned}
$$

Substituting $x=2$ into the above expression, we have

$$
h'(2) =5\left({f(2)}\right)^4\cdot f'(2)
$$

To evaluate this expression, we need to collect data from the graph of $y=f(x){:}$

- The graph passes through the point $(2,1)$. Therefore, $f(2) = 1$.
- Next, we need to find the slope of the tangent to $f$ at $x=2{:}$![](<../Source/4.1.6. Calculating Derivatives From Graphs Using the Chain Rule/Images/f49b0c68ff9e3ff49320bbb9bc76d691.png>)
The slope of $f(x)$ is constant on $x\in \left(1,3\right)$ and is given by
$\dfrac{\Delta f}{\Delta x} = \dfrac{3 - (-1)}{3-1} = \dfrac42 = 2$.
Therefore, $f'(2)=2$.

Substituting this data into our expression for $h'(2)$, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
h^{′}(2) &= 5(f(2))^{4} \cdot f^{′}(2) \\
&= 5 \cdot 1^{4} \cdot 2 \\
&= 10
\end{aligned}
$$

So, we conclude that $h'(2) = 10$.

## Calculating a Derivative Using the Power and Chain Rules

**Example:** The graph of $y=f(x)$ is shown above. If $h(x)=\dfrac{1}{\left(f(x)\right)^5}$, what is $h'(2)$?

![](<../Source/4.1.6. Calculating Derivatives From Graphs Using the Chain Rule/Images/7e955903e248765f7a0bfd582bee6166.png>)

**Explanation**

The chain rule gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
h^{′}(x) &= \frac{d}{dx}(f(x))^{-5} \\
&=-5(f(x))^{-6} \cdot f^{′}(x) \\
&= - (5)/((f(x))^{6}) \cdot f^{′}(x) \\
&= - (5f^{′}(x))/((f(x))^{6})
\end{aligned}
$$

and therefore

$$
h'(2)= -\dfrac{5f'(2)}{\left(f(2)\right)^6}
$$

Now, note the following:

- From the graph, we obtain $f(2)=2$.
- Additionally, the slope of $f(x)$ on $\left[0,3\right]$ equals $-1$, so $f'(2)=-1$.

Therefore, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
h^{′}(2) &= - (5f^{′}(2))/((f(2))^{6}) \\
&= - (5 \cdot (-1))/(2^{6}) \\
&= \frac{5}{64}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Differentiating Composite Special Functions

**Example:** The graph of $y=f(x)$ is shown above. If $h(x)=e^{{f(x)}}$, what is $h'\left(-\dfrac12\right)$?

![](<../Source/4.1.6. Calculating Derivatives From Graphs Using the Chain Rule/Images/e420256a8a5b99cd2372307e63ec547b.png>)

**Explanation**

The chain rule gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
h^{′}(x) &= \frac{d}{dx}(e^{f(x)}) \\
&= e^{f(x)} \cdot f^{′}(x)
\end{aligned}
$$

and therefore

$$
h'\left(-\dfrac12\right) = e^{f\left(-1/2\right)}\cdot f'\left(-\dfrac12\right)
$$

Now, note the following:

- From the graph, we obtain $f\left(-\dfrac12\right) = 3$.
- Additionally, the slope of $f(x)$ on $\left[-2.5,0\right]$ equals $1$, so $f'\left(-\dfrac12\right)=1$.

Therefore, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
h^{′}(-\frac{1}{2}) &= e^{f(-1/2)} \cdot f^{′}(-\frac{1}{2}) \\
&= e^{3} \cdot 1 \\
&= e^{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Differentiating a Sum of Composite Functions

**Example:** The graph of $y=f(x)$ is shown above. If $h(x)=\sin\left({f(x)}\right)+\cos\left(f(x)\right)$, what is $h'(-1)$?

![](<../Source/4.1.6. Calculating Derivatives From Graphs Using the Chain Rule/Images/6e2dd4b72e857b48b46956e55c93f05b.png>)

**Explanation**

The chain rule gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
h^{′}(x) &= \frac{d}{dx}(\sin (f(x)) + \cos (f(x))) \\
&= \frac{d}{dx}(\sin (f(x))) + \frac{d}{dx}(\cos (f(x))) \\
&= \cos (f(x)) \cdot f^{′}(x) - \sin (f(x)) \cdot f^{′}(x)
\end{aligned}
$$

and therefore

$$
h'(-1) = \cos\left(f(-1)\right) \cdot f'(-1) -\sin(f(-1))\cdot f'(-1)
$$

Now, note the following:

- From the graph, we obtain $f(-1)=0$.
- Additionally, the slope of $f(x)$ on $\left[-2,0\right]$ equals $-2$, so $f'(-1)=-2$.

Therefore, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
h^{′}(-1) &= \cos (f(-1)) \cdot f^{′}(-1) - \sin (f(-1)) \cdot f^{′}(-1) \\
&= \cos (0) \cdot (-2) - \sin (0) \cdot (-2) \\
&= 1 \cdot (-2) - 0 \\
&=-2
\end{aligned}
$$

## Differentiating a Composite Function Given Two Graphs

**Example:** Functions $f$ and $g$ are represented by the graphs above. If $h(x)=g(f(x))$, what is $h'\left(2\right)$?

![](<../Source/4.1.6. Calculating Derivatives From Graphs Using the Chain Rule/Images/5d31597727fc9fc4176dc2eb9b6a9444.png>)

**Explanation**

The chain rule gives

$$
h'(x) = g'(f(x)) \cdot f'(x)
$$

and therefore

$$
h'(2) = g'(f(2)) \cdot f'(2)
$$

From the graph we obtain

$$
f(2)=\dfrac{5}{2}
$$

Additionally:

- The slope of $f(x)$ on $[1,3]$ equals $-\dfrac{1}{2}$. So, $f'(2)=-\dfrac{1}{2}$.
- The slope of $g(x)$ on $\left[1,3\right]$ equals $-1$. So, $g'\left(\dfrac{5}{2}\right)=-1$.

Therefore, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
h^{′}(2) &= g^{′}(f(2)) \cdot f^{′}(2) \\
&= g^{′}(\frac{5}{2}) \cdot (-\frac{1}{2}) \\
&= (-1) \cdot (-\frac{1}{2}) \\
&= \frac{1}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$
