## Introduction

In this lesson, we'll learn how to compute derivatives of composite functions when given function data at specific points.

For example, suppose we know the values of two functions, $f(x), g(x)$, and their derivatives at the points $x=1$ and $x=2$. This data is given in the table below.

| $x$ | $f(x)$ | $f'(x)$ | $g(x)$ | $g'(x)$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $1$ | $3$ | $-1$ | $\color{blue}2$ | $\color{violet}-4$ |
| $2$ | $1$ | $\color{red}5$ | $2$ | $-3$ |

Now, let's define a new function $h(x)$ as

$$
h(x)=f(g(x))
$$

By the chain rule, we have that

$$
h'(x) = f'(g(x)) \cdot g'(x)
$$

To compute, for example, $h'(1)$, we substitute $x=1$ into the expression for $h'(x)$ above.

$$
h'(1) = f'(g(1)) \cdot g'(1)
$$

We can now evaluate this derivative by substituting some values from the table above:

$$
\begin{aligned}
h^{′}(1) &= f^{′}(g(1)) \cdot g^{′}(1) \\
&= f^{′}(2) \cdot g^{′}(1) \\
&= 5 \cdot (-4) \\
&=-20
\end{aligned}
$$

So, we conclude that

$$
h'(1) = -20
$$

## Applying the Chain Rule Given Data For Two Functions

**Example:** The table below shows the values of functions $f$, $g$, $f'$, and $g'$ at $x=1$ and $x=2$. Find $h'(2)$, if $h(x)=g(f(x))$.

| $x$ | $f(x)$ | $f'(x)$ | $g(x)$ | $g'(x)$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $1$ | $0$ | $1$ | $-1$ | $4$ |
| $2$ | $1$ | $2$ | $3$ | $-2$ |

**Explanation**

The chain rule gives

$$
h'(x) = g'(f(x)) \cdot f'(x)
$$

Evaluating at $x=2$ and using the values given in the table, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
h^{′}(2) &= g^{′}(f(2)) \cdot f^{′}(2) \\
&= g^{′}(1) \cdot f^{′}(2) \\
&= 4 \cdot 2 \\
&= 8
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="using-the-chain-rule-with-the-power-rule"></a>
## Using the Chain Rule With the Power Rule

Let's now suppose we have the following data regarding the function $f(x)$ and its derivative $f'(x){:}$

$$
f(-1)={\color{blue}2}, \qquad\qquad f'(-1)={\color{red}-8}
$$

Let's use this data to evaluate the following derivative:

$$
\dfrac{\textrm{d}}{\textrm{d}x}\left.\left(\dfrac{1}{f(x)}\right)\right|_{x=-1}
$$

First, we find an expression for the derivative using the chain rule:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{d}{dx}((1)/(f(x))) &= \frac{d}{dx}(f(x))^{-1} \\
&= - (f(x))^{-2} \cdot f^{′}(x) \\
&= - (1)/((f(x))^{2}) \cdot f^{′}(x) \\
&= - (f^{′}(x))/((f(x))^{2})
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, evaluating this expression at $x = -1$, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{d}{dx}((1)/(f(x))) \mid _{x =-1} &= - (f^{′}(-1))/((f(-1))^{2}) \\
&= - ((-8))/(2^{2}) \\
&= \frac{8}{4} \\
&= 2
\end{aligned}
$$

## Applying the Chain Rule With the Power Rule

**Example:** The values of the function $f(x)$ and its derivative at $x=-1$ are given in the table below.

| $x$ | $f(x)$ | $f'(x)$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| $-1$ | $8$ | $-2$ |

Find $g'\left(-1\right)$, if $g(x)=\sqrt[3]{f(x)}$.

**Explanation**

Using the chain rule, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
g^{′}(x) &= \frac{d}{dx}(\sqrt[3]{f(x)}) \\
&= \frac{d}{dx}(f(x))^{1/3} \\
&= \frac{1}{3}(f(x))^{-2/3} \cdot f^{′}(x) \\
&= (1)/(3(\sqrt[3]{f(x)})^{2}) \cdot f^{′}(x)
\end{aligned}
$$

Evaluating at $x = -1$ and using the values given in the table, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
g^{′}(-1) &= (1)/(3(\sqrt[3]{f(-1)})^{2}) \cdot f^{′}(-1) \\
&= (1)/(3(\sqrt[3]{8})^{2}) \cdot (-2) \\
&= \frac{1}{3 \cdot 4} \cdot (-2) \\
&=-\frac{1}{6}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Applying the Chain Rule With Trigonometric Functions

**Example:** Given that $f(1)=\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ and $f'(1)=-\dfrac{3\pi}{4}$, compute $\dfrac{\textrm{d}}{\textrm{d}x}\big(\cos{\left(f(x)\right)}\big)$ at $x=1$.

**Explanation**

Using the chain rule, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{d}{dx}(\cos (f(x))) &= -\sin (f(x)) \cdot f^{′}(x)
\end{aligned}
$$

Evaluating at $x=1$ and using the values given in the table, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{d}{dx}(\cos (f(x))) \mid _{x = 1} &= -\sin (f(1)) \cdot f^{′}(1) \\
&=-\sin (\frac{π}{2}) \cdot (-\frac{3π}{4}) \\
&=-1 \cdot (-\frac{3π}{4}) \\
&= \frac{3π}{4}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Applying the Chain Rule With Exponential and Logarithmic Functions

**Example:** The values of the function $f(x)$ and its derivative at $x = -3$ are given in the table below.

| $x$ | $f(x)$ | $f'(x)$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| $-3$ | $-2$ | $e^{4}$ |

Find $w'(-3)$, if $w(x) = e^{f(x)}$.

**Explanation**

Using the chain rule, we get

$$
w'(x) = e^{f(x)} \cdot f'(x)
$$

Evaluating at $x = -3$ and using the values given in the table, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
w^{′}(-3) &= e^{f(-3)} \cdot f^{′}(-3) \\
&= e^{-2} \cdot e^{4} \\
&= e^{-2 + 4} \\
&= e^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$
