## Introduction

Sometimes, we need to combine multiple rules of differentiation to differentiate a given function. As a reminder, the product rule, quotient rule, and chain rule are stated below.

- Product rule: $\hspace{.5cm} \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x} \left[u(x) \cdot v(x) \right] = u'(x) \cdot v(x) + u(x) \cdot v'(x)$
- Quotient rule: $\hspace{.5cm} \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x} \left[\dfrac{u(x)}{v(x)} \right] = \dfrac{u'(x)\cdot v(x) - u(x)\cdot v'(x)}{[v(x)]^2}$
- Chain rule: $\hspace{.5cm} \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x} \left[u(v(x)) \right] = u'(v(x))\cdot v'(x)$

## Calculating a Derivative Using Multiple Instances of the Chain Rule

**Example:** Differentiate $y = \cos^4{2x}$.

**Explanation**

First, we express $y$ as a composite function

$$
y = u^4, \qquad u = \cos{v}, \qquad v = 2x
$$

Differentiating gives

$$
\dfrac{\textrm d y}{\textrm d u} = 4u^3,\qquad \dfrac{\textrm{d}u}{\textrm d v} = -\sin{v},\qquad \dfrac{\textrm{d}v}{\textrm d x} = 2
$$

Applying now the chain rule twice, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{dy}{dx} &= \frac{dy}{du} \cdot \frac{du}{dx} \\
&= \frac{dy}{du} \cdot \frac{du}{dv} \cdot \frac{dv}{dx} \\
&= 4u^{3} \cdot (-\sin v) \cdot (2) \\
&= 4(\cos 2x)^{3} \cdot (-\sin 2x) \cdot (2) \\
&=-8\cos^{3} 2x\sin 2x
\end{aligned}
$$

## Calculating a Derivative Using the Chain and Product Rules

**Example:** If $f(x) = e^{-2x}\tan({x^2})$, then what is $f'(x)$?

**Explanation**

This question requires the product rule. Let

$$
u(x) = e^{-2x}
$$

and

$$
v(x)=\tan{(x^2)}
$$

Then

$$
u'(x) = -2e^{-2x}, \qquad v'(x) = 2x\sec^2(x^2)
$$

Note that we used the chain rule to calculate both $u'(x)$ and $v'(x)$.

Now, applying the product rule and simplifying, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
f^{′}(x) &= u^{′}(x) \cdot v(x) + u(x) \cdot v^{′}(x) \\
&= (-2e^{-2x}) \cdot \tan (x^{2}) + e^{-2x} \cdot 2x\sec^{2} (x^{2}) \\
&= 2e^{-2x}(-\tan (x^{2}) + x\sec^{2} (x^{2})) \\
&= 2e^{-2x}(x\sec^{2} (x^{2}) - \tan (x^{2}))
\end{aligned}
$$

## Calculating a Derivative Using the Chain and Quotient Rules

**Example:** Given that $y =\dfrac{e^{3x}}{2^{x}+1}$, find $\dfrac{\textrm{d}y}{\textrm{d}x}$.

**Explanation**

We use the quotient rule with the numerator $u(x)=e^{3x}$ and the denominator

$$
v(x)=2^x +1
$$

Differentiating $u(x)$ and $v(x)$, we have

$$
u'(x) = 3e^{3x}, \qquad v'(x) = 2^x \ln 2
$$

Note that we used the chain rule to differentiate $u$.

Now, applying the quotient rule and simplifying, we have

$$
\begin{aligned} \dfrac{\textrm{d}y}{\textrm{d}x} &= \dfrac{u'(x)\cdot v(x) - u(x)\cdot v'(x)}{[v(x)]^2} \\ &=\dfrac{3e^{3x} \cdot \left(2^{x}+1\right) - e^{3x} \cdot \left(2^x \cdot \ln2\right)}{\left(2^{x}+1 \right)^2} \\ &=\dfrac{3e^{3x}2^{x} + 3e^{3x} - e^{3x}2^{x}\ln 2}{\left(2^{x}+1 \right)^2} \\ &=\dfrac{e^{3x} \left(3(2^{x}) - 2^{x} \ln 2 +3\right)}{\left(2^{x}+1 \right)^2} \end{aligned}
$$
