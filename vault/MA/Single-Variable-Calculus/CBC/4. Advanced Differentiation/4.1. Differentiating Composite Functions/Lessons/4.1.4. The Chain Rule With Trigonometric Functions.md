## Introduction

The chain rule can be used to differentiate trigonometric functions like

$$
y = \sin (2x)
$$

First, notice that this function can be written as the composite function

$$
y = \sin u, \qquad u = 2x
$$

We can use the chain rule to compute the derivative of this composite function:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{dy}{dx} &= \frac{dy}{du} \cdot \frac{du}{dx} \\
&= \frac{d}{du}(\sin u) \cdot \frac{d}{dx}(2x) \\
&= \cos u \cdot 2 \\
&= 2\cos u
\end{aligned}
$$

Substituting $u=2x$, we obtain the final result:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{dy}{dx} &= 2\cos (2x)
\end{aligned}
$$

## Differentiating a Trigonometric Function Using the Chain Rule

**Example:** Calculate $f'(x)$ if $f(x) = 2\sin (3x - 1)$.

**Explanation**

First, we express $f(x)$ as a composite function

$$
f = 2\sin u, \qquad u=3x-1
$$

Differentiating gives

$$
\dfrac{\textrm d f}{\textrm d u} = 2\cos u, \qquad\dfrac{\textrm d u}{\textrm d x} = 3
$$

We now apply the chain rule, and get

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{df}{dx} &= \frac{df}{du} \cdot \frac{du}{dx} \\
&= 2\cos u \cdot 3 \\
&= 6\cos u \\
&= 6\cos (3x - 1)
\end{aligned}
$$

## Differentiating a Reciprocal Trigonometric Function Using the Chain Rule

**Example:** Calculate $\dfrac{\textrm d y}{\textrm d x}$ if $y = 4\csc (5x + 3)$.

**Explanation**

First, we express $y$ as the composite function

$$
y = 4 \csc u, \qquad u = 5x+3
$$

Differentiating gives

$$
\dfrac{\textrm d y}{\textrm d u} = -4\csc u\cot u, \qquad\dfrac{\textrm d u}{\textrm d x} = 5
$$

We now apply the chain rule, and get

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{dy}{dx} &= \frac{dy}{du} \cdot \frac{du}{dx} \\
&=-4\csc u\cot u \cdot 5 \\
&=-20\csc u\cot u \\
&=-20\csc (5x + 3)\cot (5x + 3)
\end{aligned}
$$

## Differentiating a Power of a Trigonometric Function Using the Chain Rule

**Example:** Given that $f(x) = \sin^3{x}$, calculate $f'(x)$.

**Explanation**

First, we write

$$
f(x) = (\sin{x})^3
$$

Now, we see that $f(x)$ can be expressed as the composite function

$$
f = u^3, \qquad u=\sin x
$$

Differentiating, we get

$$
\dfrac{\textrm d f}{\textrm d u} = 3u^2, \qquad \dfrac{\textrm d u}{\textrm d x} = \cos{x}
$$

We now apply the chain rule, and get

$$
\begin{aligned}
f^{′}(x) &= \frac{df}{du} \cdot \frac{du}{dx} \\
&= 3u^{2} \cdot (\cos x) \\
&= 3(\sin x)^{2}\cos x \\
&= 3\sin^{2} x\cos x
\end{aligned}
$$

## Differentiating a Composition of Trigonometric Functions Using the Chain Rule

**Example:** Find the derivative of $y = \tan{\left(\sin(x)\right)}$.

**Explanation**

First, we express $y$ as the composite function

$$
y = \tan u, \qquad u = \sin x
$$

Differentiating gives

$$
\dfrac{\textrm d y}{\textrm d u} = \sec^2 u,\qquad \dfrac{\textrm{d}u}{\textrm d x} = \cos x
$$

We now apply the chain rule, and get

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{dy}{dx} &= \frac{dy}{du} \cdot \frac{du}{dx} \\
&= \sec^{2} u \cdot \cos x \\
&= \sec^{2} (\sin x)\cos x
\end{aligned}
$$
