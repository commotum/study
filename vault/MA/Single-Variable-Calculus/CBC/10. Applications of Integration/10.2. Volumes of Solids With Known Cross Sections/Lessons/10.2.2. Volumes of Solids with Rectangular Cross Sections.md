## Introduction

Suppose that the base of a solid is the semicircle enclosed by the curve

$$
y=-\sqrt{4-x^2}
$$

and the $x$-axis, as shown in the picture below. Cross-sections perpendicular to the $x$-axis are rectangles. If the height of each rectangle is half of the length of its base, then what is the volume of the solid?

![](<../Source/10.2.2. Volumes of Solids with Rectangular Cross Sections/Images/7b6911e29edbdb3b4b9cdabfcc0137ee.png>)

A sketch with a few cross-sections is shown in the picture below.

![](<../Source/10.2.2. Volumes of Solids with Rectangular Cross Sections/Images/af7583263e661be9ad7ddd190269fda1.png>)

If we are given the expression $A(x)$ for the area of each cross-section, then we find the volume as follows:

$$
V = \int_{a}^{b} A(x)\:\textrm{d}x
$$

Consider now a cross-section at some point $x$, where $x \in (-2,2)$:

![](<../Source/10.2.2. Volumes of Solids with Rectangular Cross Sections/Images/6d5ff69973e7dd3effda625729e3146e.png>)

If we project the rectangular cross-section onto the $xy$-plane, we get the following picture:

![](<../Source/10.2.2. Volumes of Solids with Rectangular Cross Sections/Images/d5511aed284db7cfa67448282ca86140.png>)

Then the base of the cross-section is

$$
\begin{aligned}
b &= \mid y \mid \\
&= \mid - \sqrt{4 - x^{2}} \mid \\
&= \sqrt{4 - x^{2}}
\end{aligned}
$$

Since the height ($h$) of each rectangle is

$$
\dfrac{1}{2}
$$

times the base ($b$), the area of the rectangle is

$$
A = bh = b \cdot \left(\dfrac{1}{2} b \right) = \dfrac{1}{2}b^2
$$

So, the expression for the area of the cross-section is

$$
\begin{aligned}
A(x) &= \frac{1}{2}b^{2} \\
&= \frac{1}{2}(\sqrt{4 - x^{2}})^{2} \\
&= \frac{1}{2}(4 - x^{2})
\end{aligned}
$$

We can now calculate the volume of the solid using the following integral:

$$
\begin{aligned}
V &= ∫_{a}^{b}A(x)dx \\
&= \frac{1}{2}∫_{-2}^{2}(4 - x^{2})dx
\end{aligned}
$$

## Computing the Volume When the Base Is Bounded by a Curve

**Example:** The base of a solid is the triangular region enclosed by the line $y=x-1$ and the $x$-axis from $x=0$ to $x=1$. Cross-sections perpendicular to the $x$-axis are rectangles. Given that the height of each rectangle is $2$ times the length of its base, calculate the volume of the solid.

![](<../Source/10.2.2. Volumes of Solids with Rectangular Cross Sections/Images/dc20fe74de60f4edf89e81eae772365a.png>)

**Explanation**

Consider a cross-section at some point $x$, where $x \in (0,1)$, and its projection on the $xy$-plane:

![](<../Source/10.2.2. Volumes of Solids with Rectangular Cross Sections/Images/ec1a779f0e028c37276cb05c0950ce33.png>)

The base of the rectangle (cross-section) is

$$
\begin{aligned}
b &= \begin{vmatrix}y|= & x - 1\end{vmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

The area of a rectangle is
$A = bh$.
So, since we have $h=2b$, the expression for the area of the cross-section is

$$
\begin{aligned}
A(x) &= bh \\
&= 2b^{2} \\
&= 2 \mid x - 1 \mid ^{2} \\
&= 2(x - 1)^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

We can now calculate the volume of the solid, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
V &= ∫_{a}^{b}A(x)dx \\
&= ∫_{0}^{1}2(x - 1)^{2}dx \\
&= 2∫_{0}^{1}(x - 1)^{2}dx \\
&= 2∫_{0}^{1}x^{2} - 2x + 1dx \\
&= 2[\frac{x^{3}}{3} - x^{2} + x]_{0}^{1} \\
&= 2([((1)^{3})/(3) - (1)^{2} + (1)] - [((0)^{3})/(3) - (0)^{2} + (0)]) \\
&= 2(\frac{1}{3} - 0) \\
&= \frac{2}{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Computing the Volume When the Base Is Bounded by Two Curves

**Example:** The base of a solid is the region enclosed by the curves $y=\dfrac{4}{x}$ and the lines $x=1$, $y=1$, as shown in the picture. Cross-sections perpendicular to the $x$-axis are rectangles. If the height of each rectangle is $3$ times the length of its base, which integral gives the volume of the solid?

![](<../Source/10.2.2. Volumes of Solids with Rectangular Cross Sections/Images/1abe9dcb78bc8ebff40f832a61463fe8.png>)

**Explanation**

First, we let

$$
f(x)=\dfrac4x
$$

and $g(x)=1$.

We need to determine the limits of integration. To do that, we find the intersections of the two curves by setting the functions equal to each other and solving for $x$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
f(x) &= g(x) \\
\frac{4}{x} &= 1 \\
x &= 4
\end{aligned}
$$

Hence, the limits of integration are $x=1$ and $x=4$.

Now, consider a cross-section at some point $x$, where $x \in (1,4)$, and its projection on the $xy$-plane:

![](<../Source/10.2.2. Volumes of Solids with Rectangular Cross Sections/Images/0c3e3736a69e804c404343d8d19fc96e.png>)

The base of the cross-section is

$$
\begin{aligned}
b &= \mid f(x) - g(x) \mid \\
&= \mid \frac{4}{x} - 1 \mid
\end{aligned}
$$

The area of a rectangle is
$A = bh$.
So, since we have $h=3b$, the expression for the area of the cross-section is

$$
\begin{aligned}
A(x) &= bh \\
&= 3b^{2} \\
&= 3(\frac{4}{x} - 1)^{2} \\
&= 3(\frac{16}{x^{2}} - \frac{8}{x} + 1)
\end{aligned}
$$

We can now calculate the volume of the solid using the following integral.

$$
\begin{aligned}
V &= ∫_{a}^{b}A(x)dx \\
&= 3∫_{1}^{4}(\frac{16}{x^{2}} - \frac{8}{x} + 1)dx
\end{aligned}
$$

## Cross-Sections Perpendicular to the y-Axis

Let's now suppose that the base of a solid is the right triangle enclosed by the line

$$
x=2y+2
$$

and the $y$-axis between $y=-1$ and $y=0$, as shown in the picture below.

![](<../Source/10.2.2. Volumes of Solids with Rectangular Cross Sections/Images/f52759bc9a4ad3737b89626b6c00958b.png>)

Suppose further that cross-sections perpendicular to the $\mathbf y$**-axis** are rectangles and that the height of each rectangle is

$$
\dfrac{3}{4}
$$

times the length of its base. How do we find the volume of that solid?

A sketch with a few cross-sections is shown in the picture below.

![](<../Source/10.2.2. Volumes of Solids with Rectangular Cross Sections/Images/c8bef77a03956846153ef6e51a5eb5e5.png>)

If we are given the expression $A(y)$ for the area of each cross-section, then we find the volume as follows:

$$
V = \int_{c}^{d} A(y)\:\textrm{d}y
$$

Consider now a cross-section at some point $y$, where $y \in (-1,0)$:

![](<../Source/10.2.2. Volumes of Solids with Rectangular Cross Sections/Images/5739af45de0127200a339467c704dd03.png>)

By projecting the rectangular cross-section onto the $xy$-plane, we get the following picture:

![](<../Source/10.2.2. Volumes of Solids with Rectangular Cross Sections/Images/7f444015d69d66a00ce72ae1d5b73da8.png>)

Then, the base of the cross-section is

$$
\begin{aligned}
b &= \begin{vmatrix}x|= & 2y + 2\end{vmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Since the height $h$ is

$$
\dfrac{3}{4}
$$

times the base $b$, the area of the rectangle is

$$
\begin{aligned}
A(x) &= bh \\
&= b \cdot (\frac{3}{4}b) \\
&= \frac{3}{4}b^{2} \\
&= \frac{3}{4}(2y + 2)^{2} \\
&= 3(y + 1)^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

We can now calculate the volume of the solid using the following integral:

$$
\begin{aligned}
V &= ∫_{c}^{d}A(y)dy \\
&= 3∫_{-1}^{0}(y + 1)^{2}dy
\end{aligned}
$$

## Computing the Volume  When the Cross Sections Are Perpendicular to the y-Axis

**Example:** The base of a solid is the region enclosed by the curve $x=\sqrt{3+y}$ and the $y$-axis from $y=-2$ to $y=0$, as shown in the picture. Cross-sections perpendicular to the $y$-axis are rectangles. Given that the height of each rectangle is $2$ times the length of its base, calculate the volume of the solid.

![](<../Source/10.2.2. Volumes of Solids with Rectangular Cross Sections/Images/7c97636c7551f83cb9bdf1170c48f68c.png>)

**Explanation**

Let's consider a cross-section at some point $y$, where $y \in (-2,0)$, and its projection on the $xy$-plane:

![](<../Source/10.2.2. Volumes of Solids with Rectangular Cross Sections/Images/503c6d6374cb7b8b269e44883cde78aa.png>)

Then, the base of the rectangle (cross-section) is

$$
\begin{aligned}
b &= \mid x|= \sqrt{3 + y}
\end{aligned}
$$

The area of the rectangle is
$A = bh$.
So, since we have $h=2b$, the expression for the area of the cross-section is

$$
\begin{aligned}
A(y) &= bh \\
&= 2b^{2} \\
&= 2(\sqrt{3 + y})^{2} \\
&= 2(3 + y)
\end{aligned}
$$

We can now calculate the volume of the solid, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
V &= ∫_{c}^{d}A(y)dy \\
&= ∫_{-2}^{0}2(3 + y)dy \\
&= ∫_{-2}^{0}(6 + 2y)dy \\
&= ∫_{-2}^{0}6dy + ∫_{-2}^{0}2ydy \\
&= 6y \mid _{-2}^{0} + y^{2} \mid _{-2}^{0} \\
&= 6(0 - (-2)) + (0 - 4) \\
&= 12 - 4 \\
&= 8
\end{aligned}
$$

## Computing the Volume When the Base Is Bounded by Two Curves

**Example:** The base of a solid is the region enclosed by the curves $x=-y^4$ and the line $x=-1$, as shown in the picture. Cross-sections perpendicular to the $y$-axis are rectangles. If the height of each rectangle is $\dfrac{1}{2}$ times the length of its base, which integral gives the volume of the solid?

![](<../Source/10.2.2. Volumes of Solids with Rectangular Cross Sections/Images/93d665c2a59cc35f38f7b784f523ba8d.png>)

**Explanation**

First, we let

$$
f(y)=-y^4
$$

and $g(y)=-1$.

We need to determine the limits of integration. To do that, we find the intersections of the two curves by setting the functions equal to each other and solving for $y$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
f(y) &= g(y) \\
- y^{4} &= -1 \\
y^{4} - 1 &= 0 \\
(y^{2} + 1)(y^{2} - 1) &= 0 \\
(y^{2} - 1) &= 0 \\
y &= ± 1
\end{aligned}
$$

Hence, the limits of integration are $y=-1$ and $y=1$.

Now, consider a cross-section at some point $y$, where $y \in (-1,1)$, and its projection on the $xy$-plane:

![](<../Source/10.2.2. Volumes of Solids with Rectangular Cross Sections/Images/fb579899458840b69639b4f42fc19d6c.png>)

Then, the base of the cross-section is

$$
\begin{aligned}
b &= \mid f(y) - g(y) \mid \\
&= \mid - y^{4} - (-1) \mid \\
&= \mid 1 - y^{4} \mid
\end{aligned}
$$

The area of a rectangle is
$A =bh$.
So, since we have

$$
h=\dfrac12b
$$

the expression for the area of the cross-section is

$$
\begin{aligned}
A(y) &= bh \\
&= \frac{1}{2}b^{2} \\
&= \frac{1}{2}(1 - y^{4})^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

We can now calculate the volume of the solid using the following integral:

$$
\begin{aligned}
V &= ∫_{c}^{d}A(y)dy \\
&= \frac{1}{2}∫_{-1}^{1}(1 - y^{4})^{2}dy
\end{aligned}
$$
