## Introduction

Consider the function $f(x)$ below.

$$
{3x + a,\begin{vmatrix}x \ne 4, \\ 13,\end{vmatrix}x = 4
$$

The function $f(x)$ seems to have a point discontinuity at $x=4$. However, by selecting a particular value for $a$, we can remove the point discontinuity and make the function continuous.

We can find this value of $a$ by forcing the limit of the function to be equal to the value of the function at $x=4$:

$$
\lim\limits_{x\to 4} f(x) = f(4)
$$

Remember that $\lim\limits_{x\to 4} f(x)$ represents the value that $f(x)$ approaches as $x$ gets closer and closer, but not equal, to $4$. So, when evaluating $\lim\limits_{x\to 4} f(x)$, we need to use the function definition for

$$
x \neq 4
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
lim_(x → 4)f(x) &= lim_(x → 4)(3x + a) \\
&= 3(4) + a \\
&= 12 + a
\end{aligned}
$$

At $x=4$, the function is defined as

$$
f(x)=13
$$

So

$$
f(4)=13
$$

and we can solve the equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
lim_(x → 4)f(x) &= f(4) \\
12 + a &= 13 \\
a &= 1
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, to make $f(x)$ continuous at $x=4$, we need to choose $a=1$.

## Removing a Point Discontinuity in a Piecewise Polynomial Function

**Example:** The function $f(x)$ is defined for every real number $x$ and is given by
${x^{2} + 2kx + 1,\begin{vmatrix}x \ne 1, \\ 4,\end{vmatrix}x = 1$.
If $f(x)$ is continuous at $x=1$, what is the value of $k$?

**Explanation**

From the definition of $f(x)$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
lim_(x → 1)f(x) &= lim_(x → 1)(x^{2} + 2kx + 1) \\
&= 1^{2} + 2k(1) + 1 \\
&= 2 + 2k
\end{aligned}
$$

In order for $f(x)$ to be continuous at $x=1$, we must have

$$
\begin{aligned}
lim_(x → 1)f(x) &= f(1) \\
2 + 2k &= 4 \\
2k &= 2 \\
k &= 1
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, setting $k=1$ removes the point discontinuity.

## Removing a Point Discontinuity in a Piecewise Exponential or Logarithmic Function

**Example:** The function $h(x)$ is given by
${\log_{3} (x + c),\begin{vmatrix}x \ne 0, \\ 2,\end{vmatrix}x = 0$.

Given that $h(x)$ is continuous at $x=0$, what is the value of $c$?

**Explanation**

From the definition of $h(x)$, we have that

$$
\begin{aligned}
lim_(x → 0)h(x) &= lim_(x → 0)\log_{3} (x + c) \\
&= \log_{3} (0 + c) \\
&= \log_{3} (c)
\end{aligned}
$$

In order for $h(x)$ to be continuous at $x=0$, we must have

$$
\begin{aligned}
lim_(x → 0)h(x) &= h(0) \\
\log_{3} (c) &= 2 \\
c &= 3^{2} \\
c &= 9
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, setting $c=9$ removes the point discontinuity.

## Removing a Point Discontinuity in a Piecewise Radical Function

**Example:** The function $g(x)$ is given by
${\sqrt{bx + 3},\begin{vmatrix}x \ne 2, \\ 5,\end{vmatrix}x = 2$.
If $g(x)$ is continuous at $x=2$, what is the value $b$?

**Explanation**

From the definition of $g(x)$,

$$
lim_(x → 2)g(x) = lim_(x → 2)(\sqrt{bx + 3}) = \sqrt{2b + 3}
$$

In order for $g(x)$ to be continuous at $x=2$, we must have

$$
\begin{aligned}
lim_(x → 2)g(x) &= g(2) \\
\sqrt{2b + 3} &= 5 \\
2b + 3 &= 25 \\
2b &= 22 \\
b &= 11
\end{aligned}
$$
