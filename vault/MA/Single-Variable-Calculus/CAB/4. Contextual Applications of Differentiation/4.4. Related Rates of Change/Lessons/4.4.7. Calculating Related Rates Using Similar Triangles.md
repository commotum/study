## Introduction

Suppose that Bob, who is $1.8 \,\text{m}$ tall, stands in front of a $3\,\text{m}$ street light. How fast does the length of Bob's shadow change if he walks *away* from the street light with a speed of $0.5\,\text{m/s}$?

![](<../Source/4.4.7. Calculating Related Rates Using Similar Triangles/Images/01a2ecbc54d95850aff331fe59cc1a72.png>)

We can solve problems like this using three simple steps.

**Step 1:** Draw a diagram and identify the variables that change with time.

![](<../Source/4.4.7. Calculating Related Rates Using Similar Triangles/Images/42fbb3697f4995760429d9cc5833b750.png>)

In the above diagram, we have denoted the following:

- $x$ is the distance between Bob and the street light
- $y$ is the length of Bob's shadow

Both $x$ and $y$ change with time as Bob walks away from the light. In particular, we're told that Bob's velocity is given by

$$
\dfrac {\textrm d x}{\textrm d t}= 0.5\,\text{m/s}
$$

Notice that

$$
\dfrac{\textrm d x}{\textrm d t}
$$

is positive. This is because the distance between Bob and the street light increases as Bob walks away from the light.

Our goal is to calculate

$$
\dfrac {\textrm d y}{\textrm d t}
$$

**Step 2:** Write down an equation that relates $x$ and $y$.

From our simplified diagram, we see that we have two right triangles that share a common angle. Therefore, they are similar by the AA (angle-angle) criterion for similar triangles.

Since the two triangles are similar, the ratios between the corresponding sides are equal. So, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{x + y}{3} &= \frac{y}{1.8} \\
1.8x + 1.8y &= 3y \\
1.8x &= 1.2y \\
18x &= 12y \\
y &= 1.5x
\end{aligned}
$$

**Step 3:** Differentiate with respect to time and plug in the known values.

We use the chain rule to differentiate the above equation with respect to time. This gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{d}{dt}(y) &= \frac{d}{dt}(1.5x) \\
\frac{dy}{dt} &= 1.5 \cdot \frac{dx}{dt}
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, substituting the known value of

$$
\dfrac{\textrm d x}{\textrm d t}
$$

gives

$$
\dfrac {\textrm d y}{\textrm d t} =1.5\cdot (0.5) = 0.75 \, \text {m/s}
$$

Therefore, the length of Bob's shadow increases at a rate of $0.75 \, \text {m/s}$.

## Calculating the Rate of Change of a Shadow's Length Using Similar Triangles

**Example:** An $8\,\text{m}$ tall lamppost is casting a shadow of a $2\,\text{m}$ man onto the ground. The man walks away from the lamppost with a speed of $2.4\,\text{m/s}$. How fast is the length of the man's shadow changing?

**Explanation**

Let's draw a figure according to the given data.

![](<../Source/4.4.7. Calculating Related Rates Using Similar Triangles/Images/0a33ecba519baee470f24dfdce7e6725.png>)

In the figure, $x$ is the man's distance from the lamp, while $y$ is the length of his shadow. Both $x$ and $y$ change with time. In particular, we know that the man's velocity is

$$
\dfrac{\textrm{d}x}{\textrm{d}t} = 2.4\,\textrm{m/s}
$$

Notice that the man's velocity is *positive* because he is walking away from the lamp, and therefore, the distance between him and the lamp *increases* with time.

Since we want to calculate

$$
\dfrac{\textrm{d}y}{\textrm{d}t}
$$

we need to find a relation between $x$ and $y$. Since the two triangles are similar, the ratios between the corresponding sides are equal, and we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{x + y}{8} &= \frac{y}{2} \\
2x + 2y &= 8y \\
2x &= 6y \\
y &= \frac{1}{3}x
\end{aligned}
$$

We differentiate both sides of the above equation with respect to $t$ using the chain rule, and we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{d}{dt}(y) &= \frac{d}{dt}(\frac{1}{3}x) \\
&= \frac{1}{3} \cdot \frac{dx}{dt}
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, substituting

$$
\dfrac{\textrm{d}x}{\textrm{d}t} =2.4\,\textrm{m/s}
$$

into the above gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{dy}{dt} &= \frac{1}{3} \cdot (2.4) \\
&= \frac{2.4}{3} \\
&= 0.8m/s
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the length of the man's shadow increases at a rate of $0.8\,\textrm{m/s}$.

## Calculating the Rate of Change of a Shadow's Length Using Similar Triangles and Negative Rates

**Example:** A $6\,\text{m}$ tall street lamp is casting a shadow of a $1.5\,\text{m}$ woman onto the ground. The woman is walking towards the lamppost with a speed of $1.5\,\text{m/s}$. How fast is the length of the woman's shadow decreasing?

**Explanation**

Let's draw a figure according to the given data.

![](<../Source/4.4.7. Calculating Related Rates Using Similar Triangles/Images/56c32388bc44ca2a7e64486de5fcf113.png>)

In the figure, $x$ is the woman's distance from the lamp, while $y$ is the length of her shadow. Both $x$ and $y$ change with time. In particular, we know that the woman's velocity is

$$
\dfrac{\textrm{d}x}{\textrm{d}t} = -1.5\,\textrm{m/s}
$$

Notice that the woman's velocity is *negative* because she is walking *toward* the lamp, and therefore the distance between her and the lamp *decreases* with time.

Since we want to calculate

$$
\dfrac{\textrm{d}y}{\textrm{d}t}
$$

we need to find a relation between $x$ and $y$. Since the two triangles are similar, the ratios between the corresponding sides are equal, and we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{x + y}{6} &= \frac{y}{1.5} \\
1.5x + 1.5y &= 6y \\
1.5x &= 4.5y \\
y &= \frac{1}{3}x
\end{aligned}
$$

We differentiate both sides of the above equation with respect to $t$ using the chain rule, and we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{d}{dt}(y) &= \frac{d}{dt}(\frac{1}{3}x) \\
\frac{dy}{dt} &= \frac{1}{3} \cdot \frac{dx}{dt}
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, substituting

$$
\dfrac{\textrm{d}x}{\textrm{d}t} = -1.5\,\textrm{m/s}
$$

into the above gives

$$
\dfrac{\textrm{d}y}{\textrm{d}t} = \dfrac13 \cdot (-1.5) = -0.5\,\textrm{m/s}
$$

Therefore, the length of the woman's shadow is decreasing at $0.5\,\textrm{m/s}$.
