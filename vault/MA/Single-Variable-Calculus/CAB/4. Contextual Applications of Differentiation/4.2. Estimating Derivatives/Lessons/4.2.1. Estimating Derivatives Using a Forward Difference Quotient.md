## Introduction

For a function $f(x)$, its derivative at a point $x$ is defined by the limit

$$
f'(x) = \lim\limits_{h\to 0} \frac{f(x+h) - f(x)}{h}
$$

But often, computing this limit exactly is impossible or impractical. One example might be when we don't have an explicit expression for $f(x)$. In such cases, instead of finding an exact value for $f'(x)$, we can *approximate* the derivative using the formula

$$
f'(x)\approx \frac{f(x+h) - f(x)}{h}
$$

for small values of the "step size" $h$, with $h>0$.

This expression is called the **forward difference approximation** because if $h >0$ then $x+h$ is a step forward (i.e., to the right) from $x$.

![](<../Source/4.2.1. Estimating Derivatives Using a Forward Difference Quotient/Images/58c0fc28ed1e2bb0698d1789623d448e.png>)

Generally, this and other similar methods are known as **finite-difference** approximations. The smaller the value of $h$, the better the approximation.

## Estimating a Derivative From a Table Using a Forward Difference Approximation

**Example:** A function $f(x)$ has values according to the table below:

| $x$ | $2$ | $3$ | $4$ | $5$ | $6$ | $7$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $f(x)$ | $-1.0$ | $-0.7$ | $-0.3$ | $0.7$ | $1.5$ | $2.1$ |

Approximate at the point $x=3$ using a forward difference approximation.

**Explanation**

We use the forward difference approximation formula

$$
f'(x) \approx \frac{f(x+h) - f(x)}{h}
$$

at the point $x=3$. We choose the step size $h$ to be as small as possible, in this case $h=1$. This gives

$$
f^{′}(3) \mid \approx (f(3 + 1) - f(3))/(1); = (f(4) - f(3))/(1); = ((-0.3) - (-0.7))/(1); = \frac{0.4}{1}; = 0.4
$$

## Estimating a Derivative From a Graph Using a Forward Difference Approximation

**Example:** The graph of the function $y=g(x)$ is shown below.
Approximate $g'(x)$ at the point $x=4$ using a forward difference approximation with step size $h=2$.

![](<../Source/4.2.1. Estimating Derivatives Using a Forward Difference Quotient/Images/f7601d421583e4e520cba5b7a0094276.png>)

**Explanation**

We use the forward difference approximation

$$
g'(x) \approx \frac{g(x+h) - g(x)}{h}
$$

at the point $x=4$. Applying the above formula with $x=4$ and $h=2$ gives

$$
g^{′}(4) \mid \approx (g(4 + 2) - g(4))/(2); = (g(6) - g(4))/(2); = \frac{2 - 6}{2}; = \frac{-4}{2}; =-2
$$

## Estimating a Derivative From a Table Using a Forward Difference Approximation: Word Problem

**Example:** The position of a race car along a straight track, measured in feet from the starting line, is given by the function $s(t)$, where $t$ is the time in seconds. The values of $s(t)$ for different values of $t$ are given in the table below:

| $t$ | $0$ | $0.1$ | $0.2$ | $0.3$ | $0.4$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $s(t)$ | $0$ | $3$ | $7$ | $12$ | $19$ |

Approximate the velocity $s'(t)$ at the time $t=0.1$ using a forward difference approximation.

**Explanation**

We use the forward difference approximation

$$
s'(t) \approx \frac{s(t+h) - s(t)}{h}
$$

at the time $t=0.1$. We choose the step size $h$ to be as small as possible, in this case $h=0.1$. This gives

$$
s^{′}(0.1) \mid \approx (s(0.1 + 0.1) - s(0.1))/(0.1); = (s(0.2) - s(0.1))/(0.1); = \frac{7 - 3}{0.1}; = \frac{4}{0.1}; = 40
$$

So at $t=0.1$ the velocity is approximately $40\, \textrm{ft}/\textrm{s}$.

## Estimating a Derivative From a Graph Using a Forward Difference Approximation: Word Problem

**Example:** An oil tank has leaked $q(t)$ liters of oil after $t$ hours. The graph of $q(t)$ is shown below. Approximate the rate at which the oil is leaking from the tank when $t=1$ using a forward difference approximation with step size $h=1$.

![](<../Source/4.2.1. Estimating Derivatives Using a Forward Difference Quotient/Images/b42fd1d22af4ee74a06a5344ab52a8c8.png>)

**Explanation**

We use the forward difference approximation

$$
q'(t) \approx \frac{q(t+h) - q(t)}{h}
$$

at the time $t=1$ with step size $h=1$. This gives

$$
q^{′}(1) \mid \approx (q(1 + 1) - q(1))/(1); = (q(2) - q(1))/(1); = 3 - 2; = 1
$$

So, after $1$ hour, the tank is leaking oil at a rate of approximately $1\,\textrm{liter}/ \textrm{hour}$.
