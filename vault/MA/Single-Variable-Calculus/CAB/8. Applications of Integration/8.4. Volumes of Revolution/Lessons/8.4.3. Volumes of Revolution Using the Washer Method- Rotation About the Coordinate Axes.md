## Introduction

Consider the plane region enclosed by the curve

$$
y=\sqrt{x}
$$

the curve

$$
y=\dfrac{1}{2}x
$$

and the vertical line $x=2$, as shown below.

![](<../Source/8.4.3. Volumes of Revolution Using the Washer Method- Rotation About the Coordinate Axes/Images/412992e16c05a6566bedba04f7c272dd.png>)

We rotate this region $360^\circ$ about the $x$-axis to get a solid of revolution, as shown below. How can we find the volume of this solid?

![](<../Source/8.4.3. Volumes of Revolution Using the Washer Method- Rotation About the Coordinate Axes/Images/e85a3df0718cab6ff8f9965ef9ac69da.png>)

To find the volume of the solid generated when the region bounded by the outer curve $y=f(x)$, the inner curve $y=g(x)$, and the vertical lines $x=a$ and $x=b$ is rotated $360^\circ$ about the $x$-axis, we use the following formula:

$$
V = \pi \int_{a}^{b} \Big(\big[ \underbrace{f(x)}_\text{outer} \big]^2 - \big[ \underbrace{g(x)}_\text{inner} \big]^2 \Big) \:\textrm{d}x
$$

This method of computing the volume is called the **washer method** because it involves breaking up the region into many thin washers (one such washer is shown below).

![](<../Source/8.4.3. Volumes of Revolution Using the Washer Method- Rotation About the Coordinate Axes/Images/fed3119bf984f02fc6d683e13451bf3f.png>)

The cross-sectional area of each washer corresponds to the region between two discs of radius $f(x)$ and $g(x)$, so the area of each washer cross-section is

$$
\pi [f(x)]^2 - \pi [g(x)]^2
$$

and this is what we integrate.

In our case, the outer curve is

$$
f(x)=\sqrt{x}
$$

and the inner curve is

$$
g(x) = \dfrac{1}{2}x
$$

so the volume is given by

$$
V = π∫_{0}^{2}(\sqrt{x})^{2} - (\frac{1}{2}x)^{2}dx
$$

which simplifies to

$$
V = π∫_{0}^{2}(x - \frac{x^{2}}{4})dx
$$

Evaluating this integral gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
V &= π(∫_{0}^{2}xdx - \frac{1}{4}∫_{0}^{2}x^{2}dx) \\
&= π(\frac{x^{2}}{2} \mid _{0}^{2} - \frac{1}{4} \cdot \frac{x^{3}}{3} \mid _{0}^{2}) \\
&= π((\frac{4}{2} - 0) - \frac{1}{4}(\frac{8}{3} - 0)) \\
&= π(2 - \frac{2}{3}) \\
&= \frac{4π}{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

So the volume of the solid is

$$
\dfrac{4\pi}{3}
$$

cubic units.

## Constructing an Integral Representing the Volume of a Solid Generated by Rotation About the X-Axis

**Example:** Consider the region bounded by $y=\sqrt{x}$ and $y=x^3$, as shown above. Find an integral expression for the volume generated when this region is rotated $2\pi$ radians about the $x$-axis.

![](<../Source/8.4.3. Volumes of Revolution Using the Washer Method- Rotation About the Coordinate Axes/Images/717f67720ed2bd6cb21b89fe23684b40.png>)

**Explanation**

Let

$$
f(x)=\sqrt{x}
$$

and $g(x)=x^3$.

First, we determine the limits of integration. To do that, we find the intersections of the curves by solving the following equation for $x$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
f(x) &= g(x) \\
\sqrt{x} &= x^{3} \\
x &= x^{6} \\
x^{6} - x &= 0 \\
x(x^{5} - 1) &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

Hence, the limits of integration are from $x=0$ to $x=1$.

Let's draw our vertical arrow through the region to determine the inner function and the outer function. The arrow should be pointing *away* from the axis of rotation.

![](<../Source/8.4.3. Volumes of Revolution Using the Washer Method- Rotation About the Coordinate Axes/Images/377c31406bbd2d49601f4710ce71c3f9.png>)

The vertical arrow

- *enters* the region through the inner function $y=g(x)=x^3$, and
- *leaves* through the outer function $y=f(x)=\sqrt{x}$.

Therefore, the integral that gives our volume is

$$
\begin{aligned}
V &= π∫_{a}^{b}([outer]^{2} - [inner]^{2})dx \\
&= π∫_{0}^{1}([\sqrt{x}]^{2} - [x^{3}]^{2})dx \\
&= π∫_{0}^{1}(x - x^{6})dx
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="computing-the-volume-of-a-solid-generated-by-rotation-about-the-x-axis"></a>
## Computing the Volume of a Solid Generated by Rotation About the X-Axis

**Example:** Consider the finite region bounded by $y=x^2$ and $y=x^5$, as shown above. Calculate the volume of the solid generated when this region is rotated $2\pi$ radians about the $x$-axis.

![](<../Source/8.4.3. Volumes of Revolution Using the Washer Method- Rotation About the Coordinate Axes/Images/052083476ff31e9fbde6acb59b6a9ea2.png>)

**Explanation**

Let

$$
f(x)=x^2
$$

and $g(x)=x^5$.

First, we determine the limits of integration. To do that, we find the intersections of the curves by solving the following equation for $x$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
f(x) &= g(x) \\
x^{2} &= x^{5} \\
x^{5} - x^{2} &= 0 \\
x^{2}(x^{3} - 1) &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

Hence, the limits of integration are from $x=0$ to $x=1$.

Let's draw our vertical arrow through the region to determine the inner function and the outer function. The arrow should be pointing *away* from the axis of rotation.

![](<../Source/8.4.3. Volumes of Revolution Using the Washer Method- Rotation About the Coordinate Axes/Images/a5f37789045f72a603b4307d34919b56.png>)

The vertical arrow

- *enters* the region through the inner function $y=g(x)=x^5$, and
- *leaves* through the outer function $y=f(x)=x^2$.

Therefore, the volume is given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
V &= π∫_{a}^{b}([outer]^{2} - [inner]^{2})dx \\
&= π∫_{0}^{1}([x^{2}]^{2} - [x^{5}]^{2})dx \\
&= π∫_{0}^{1}(x^{4} - x^{10})dx \\
&= π(\frac{x^{5}}{5} - \frac{x^{11}}{11}) \mid _{0}^{1} \\
&= π[(\frac{1}{5} - \frac{1}{11}) - 0] \\
&= \frac{6π}{55}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Rotation About the Y-Axis

Consider the plane region bounded by the curve

$$
x=\sqrt{y}
$$

the curve $x=y^2$, and the horizontal line

$$
y=\dfrac{1}{2}
$$

as shown below.

![](<../Source/8.4.3. Volumes of Revolution Using the Washer Method- Rotation About the Coordinate Axes/Images/cbc35840d91dfd6b0c06214c1c2f181c.png>)

We rotate this region $360^\circ$ about the $y$-axis to get a solid of revolution, as shown below. How can we find the volume of this solid?

![](<../Source/8.4.3. Volumes of Revolution Using the Washer Method- Rotation About the Coordinate Axes/Images/c5daedd33c9681519ec20ca7666d0377.png>)

To find the volume of this solid, we can use the same washer method approach that we used earlier for the $x$-axis. The only difference is that we need to swap $x$ and $y$ in our formula.

So, to find the volume of the solid generated when the region bounded by the outer curve $x=f(y)$, the inner curve $x=g(y)$, and the horizontal lines $y=c$ and $y=d$ is rotated $2\pi$ radians about the $y$-axis, we use the following formula:

$$
\begin{aligned}
V &= ∫_{c}^{d}π([f(y)_(⏟)_(outer)]^{2} - [g(y)_(⏟)_(inner)]^{2})dy
\end{aligned}
$$

This is the same washer method as before, the only difference is that we need to swap $x$ and $y$. It involves breaking up the region into many thin washers (one such washer is shown below).

![](<../Source/8.4.3. Volumes of Revolution Using the Washer Method- Rotation About the Coordinate Axes/Images/2ad9bd30054d48d190b035dd0f7472b3.png>)

The cross-sectional area of each washer corresponds to the region between two discs of radius $f(y)$ and $g(y)$, so the area of each washer cross-section is

$$
\pi [f(y)]^2 - \pi [g(y)]^2
$$

and this is what we integrate.

In our example, the outer curve is

$$
x=\sqrt{y}
$$

and the inner curve is $x=y^2$, so the volume is given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
V &= ∫_{0}^{1/2}π([\sqrt{y}]^{2} - [y^{2}]^{2})dy
\end{aligned}
$$

which simplifies to

$$
\begin{aligned}
V &= π∫_{0}^{1/2}(y - y^{4})dy
\end{aligned}
$$

Evaluating this integral gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
V &= π(∫_{0}^{1/2}ydy - ∫_{0}^{1/2}y^{4}dy) \\
&= π(\frac{y^{2}}{2} \mid _{0}^{1/2} - \frac{y^{5}}{5} \mid _{0}^{1/2}) \\
&= π((\frac{1}{8} - 0) - (\frac{1}{160} - 0)) \\
&= \frac{19π}{160}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Constructing an Integral Representing the Volume of a Solid Generated by Rotation About the Y-Axis

**Example:** Consider the finite region bounded by $y=x^3$ and $x=y$, where $y \leq 0$, as shown above. Find an integral expression for the volume generated when this region is rotated $2\pi$ radians about the $y$-axis.

![](<../Source/8.4.3. Volumes of Revolution Using the Washer Method- Rotation About the Coordinate Axes/Images/774fdf8f85e93bebd2f2e50488687ba6.png>)

**Explanation**

Writing our first function in terms of $y$, we get

$$
y = x^{3}⟹x = \sqrt[3]{y}
$$

Let

$$
f(y)=\sqrt[3]{y}
$$

and $g(y)=y$.

Now, we determine the limits of integration. To do that, we find the intersections of the curves by solving the following equation for $y$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
f(y) &= g(y) \\
\sqrt[3]{y} &= y \\
y &= y^{3} \\
y^{3} - y &= 0 \\
y(y^{2} - 1) &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

Hence $y=0$ and $y=\pm 1$. But, since

$$
y \leq 0
$$

the limits of integration are from $y=0$ to $y=-1$.

Let's draw our horizontal arrow through the region to determine the inner function and the outer function. The arrow should be pointing *away* from the axis of rotation.

![](<../Source/8.4.3. Volumes of Revolution Using the Washer Method- Rotation About the Coordinate Axes/Images/0a57cd58d6ef9de4e8fcb1bd7ddb2470.png>)

The horizontal arrow

- *enters* the region through the inner function $x=g(y)=y$, and
- *leaves* through the outer function $x=f(y)=\sqrt[3]{y}$.

Therefore, the integral that gives our volume is

$$
\begin{aligned}
V &= π∫_{a}^{b}([outer]^{2} - [inner]^{2})dy \\
&= π∫_{-1}^{0}([\sqrt[3]{y}]^{2} - [y]^{2})dy \\
&= π∫_{-1}^{0}(\sqrt[3]{y^{2}} - y^{2})dy
\end{aligned}
$$

## Computing the Volume of a Solid Generated by Rotation About the Y-Axis

**Example:** Consider the region bounded by $x=\sqrt{4-y^2}$, $x=1$, and the horizontal lines $y=-1$ and $y=1$, as shown above. Calculate the volume of the solid generated when this region is rotated $2\pi$ radians about the $y$-axis.

![](<../Source/8.4.3. Volumes of Revolution Using the Washer Method- Rotation About the Coordinate Axes/Images/6966391c13faad4668ada923fcf2c17b.png>)

**Explanation**

Let

$$
f(y)=\sqrt{4-y^2}\,
$$

and $g(y)=1$.

Let's draw our horizontal arrow through the region to determine the inner function and the outer function. The arrow should be pointing away from the axis of rotation.

![](<../Source/8.4.3. Volumes of Revolution Using the Washer Method- Rotation About the Coordinate Axes/Images/e73cee16d74035d7ce1f67f89adb1a4f.png>)

The horizontal arrow

- *enters* the region through the inner function $x=g(y)=1$, and
- *leaves* through the outer function $x=f(y)=\sqrt{4-y^2}$.

Therefore, the integral that gives our volume is

$$
\begin{aligned}
V &= π∫_{c}^{d}([outer]^{2} - [inner]^{2})dy \\
&= π∫_{-1}^{1}([\sqrt{4 - y^{2}}]^{2} - 1^{2})dy \\
&= π∫_{-1}^{1}(3 - y^{2})dy \\
&= 2π∫_{0}^{1}(3 - y^{2})dy \\
&= 2π(3y - \frac{y^{3}}{3}) \mid _{0}^{1} \\
&= 2π(3 - \frac{1}{3} - 0) \\
&= \frac{16π}{3}
\end{aligned}
$$
