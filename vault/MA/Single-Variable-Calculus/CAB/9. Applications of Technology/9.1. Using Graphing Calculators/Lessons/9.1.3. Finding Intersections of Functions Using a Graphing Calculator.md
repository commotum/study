## Introduction

We can apply our graphing calculator's root-finding capabilities to accurately approximate a point of intersection of two curves.

For example, let's consider the following functions:

$$
f(x)=x^4-1, \qquad g(x)=\sqrt x
$$

A sketch of the functions and their common intersection point is shown below.

![](<../Source/9.1.3. Finding Intersections of Functions Using a Graphing Calculator/Images/1afcca314dca4f437a233b556c68cd88.png>)

While it may be tempting to approximate the intersection point by plotting the two functions on a graphing calculator, it's often easier and more accurate to reduce the problem to one that involves finding roots.

To find the $x$-values of the intersection point, we need to solve the equation

$$
f(x) = g(x)
$$

This equation can be written as

$$
f(x)-g(x) = 0
$$

Therefore, the problem can be reduced to finding the roots of the function $h(x)$, given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
h(x) &= f(x) - g(x) \\
&= (x^{4} - 1) - (\sqrt{x}) \\
&= x^{4} - 1 - \sqrt{x}
\end{aligned}
$$

The function $h(x)$ and its root are shown below.

![](<../Source/9.1.3. Finding Intersections of Functions Using a Graphing Calculator/Images/0d7f4079a834fdc154bb21bf1d3150a0.png>)

We can now apply the usual root-finding techniques to find a good approximation of the root of $h(x)$. The $x$-coordinate of the root corresponds to the $x$-coordinate of the intersection point of $f(x)$ and $g(x)$.

## Finding the Number of Solutions to an Equation on an Interval

**Example:** Find the number of solutions of the equation $e^{-x^2} = \sin 3x$ on the interval $x \in \left[0,3\right]$.

**Explanation**

The solutions of the equation correspond to the $x$-coordinates of the intersection points between the curves

$$
f(x)=e^{-x^2}, \qquad g(x)=\sin 3x
$$

The $x$-values of the intersection points are given by the equation

$$
f(x) = g(x)
$$

which can be written as

$$
f(x)-g(x) = 0
$$

As a result, the problem can be reduced to finding the roots of the function

$$
\begin{aligned}
h(x) &= f(x) - g(x) \\
&= e^{-x^{2}} - \sin 3x
\end{aligned}
$$

First, we plot $y=h(x)$ using a graphing calculator. Since $h(x)$ contains trigonometric functions, you need to make sure your calculator is in $\bbox[2px, border: 2px solid gray]{\color{gray}\,\text{RAD}\,}$ (radians) mode.

To plot the function, we press

$$
\bbox[2px, border: 2px solid gray]{\color{gray}\,y=\,}
$$

and enter the function definition in the first available space. We then press $\bbox[2px, border: 2px solid gray]{\color{gray}\,\text{graph}\,}$ to graph the function.

We can adjust the window settings using the $\bbox[2px, border: 2px solid gray]{\color{gray}\,\text{window}\,}$ button (or equivalent).

In our case, the appropriate window ranges could be the following:

- for the horizontal axis, $x \in [0, 3]$
- for the vertical axis, $y \in [-2,2]$

This gives us the following graph:

![](<../Source/9.1.3. Finding Intersections of Functions Using a Graphing Calculator/Images/5698745e0df1cf8348ca19db97ee1ef4.png>)

From the graph, we see that $y=h(x)$ has $3$ roots in the interval $x\in [0,3]$.

Therefore, the equation

$$
e^{-x^2} = \sin 3x
$$

has $3$ solutions on the interval

$$
x \in \left[0,3\right]
$$

## Approximating a Point of Intersection of Two Functions

**Example:** Find an approximation for the $x$-coordinate of the intersection point between the curves
$f(x) = x^3 + 2x + 3, \qquad g(x) = \dfrac{1}{x}$
that lies to the right of the $y$-axis.

**Explanation**

![](<../Source/9.1.3. Finding Intersections of Functions Using a Graphing Calculator/Images/ca3786a998d4909e563fc89613464b0d.png>)

To find the $x$-values of the intersection points, we need to solve the equation

$$
f(x) = g(x)
$$

which can be written as

$$
f(x) - g(x) = 0
$$

As a result, the problem can be reduced to finding the roots of the function

$$
\begin{aligned}
h(x) &= f(x) - g(x) \\
&= x^{3} + 2x + 3 - \frac{1}{x}
\end{aligned}
$$

First, we plot $y = h(x)$ using a graphing calculator to find an interval that contains the root.

To plot the function, we press

$$
\bbox[2px, border: 2px solid gray]{\color{gray}\,y=\,}
$$

and enter the function definition in the first available space. We then press $\bbox[2px, border: 2px solid gray]{\color{gray}\,\text{graph}\,}$ to graph the function.

To get a better view, we might need to either

- zoom in or out, or
- adjust the window ranges.

We can adjust the window settings using the $\bbox[2px, border: 2px solid gray]{\color{gray}\,\text{window}\,}$ button (or equivalent).

In our case, the appropriate window ranges could be the following:

- for the horizontal axis, $x \in [-2, 2]$
- for the vertical axis, $y \in [-2,4]$

This gives us the following graph:

![](<../Source/9.1.3. Finding Intersections of Functions Using a Graphing Calculator/Images/961a120e3aeca6e32417c2f797273b79.png>)

The graph shows that the root lies inside the interval $x \in [0,1]$.

To get a good approximation for the root, we use the $\bbox[2px, border: 2px solid gray]{\color{gray}\,\text{zero}\,}$ command (or equivalent). This is usually accessed by pressing the $\bbox[2px, border: 2px solid gray]{\color{gray}\,\text{calc}\,}$ button (or equivalent). Note that we sometimes need to press the $\bbox[2px, border: 2px solid gray]{\color{gray}\,\text{2nd}\,}$ or $\bbox[2px, border: 2px solid gray]{\color{gray}\,\text{shift}\,}$ button first.

We enter the following when prompted:

- The "left bound" should be a value close to the root on its left-hand side. Let's pick $0$.
- The "right bound" should be a value close to the root on its right-hand side. Let's pick $1$.
- The "guess" could be any number from $(0,1)$. Let's pick $0.3$.

![](<../Source/9.1.3. Finding Intersections of Functions Using a Graphing Calculator/Images/7629a52aed55d19906c633b3135f20ea.png>)

The calculator returns the zero

$\qquad$

$$
x \approx 0.279\,299
$$

Rounding to $3$ decimal places, we obtain the following:

$\qquad$

$$
x \approx 0.279
$$

## Approximating a Point of Intersection of Two Functions Outside the Standard Range

**Example:** Find an approximation for the $x$-coordinate of the intersection point between the curves
$f(x)= 5 - \sqrt x, \qquad g(x)= \sin \left(\dfrac{x}{5}\right)$
that lies to the right of the $y$-axis.

**Explanation**

To find the $x$-values of the intersection points, we need to solve the equation

$$
f(x) = g(x)
$$

which can be written as

$$
f(x)-g(x) = 0
$$

As a result, the problem can be reduced to finding the roots of the function

$$
\begin{aligned}
h(x) &= f(x) - g(x) \\
&= (5 - \sqrt{x}) - \sin (\frac{x}{5}) \\
&= 5 - \sqrt{x} - \sin (\frac{x}{5})
\end{aligned}
$$

First, we plot $y=h(x)$ using a graphing calculator to find an interval that contains the root. Since $h(x)$ contains trigonometric functions, you need to make sure your calculator is in $\bbox[2px, border: 2px solid gray]{\color{gray}\,\text{RAD}\,}$ (radians) mode.

To plot the function, we press

$$
\bbox[2px, border: 2px solid gray]{\color{gray}\,y=\,}
$$

and enter the function definition in the first available space. We then press $\bbox[2px, border: 2px solid gray]{\color{gray}\,\text{graph}\,}$ to graph the function.

The default view on most graphing calculators is $x \in [-10,10], y \in [-10,10]$. However, in this instance, the root falls outside the default view.

To get a better view, we might need to either

- zoom in or out, or
- adjust the window ranges.

We can adjust the window settings using the $\bbox[2px, border: 2px solid gray]{\color{gray}\,\text{window}\,}$ button (or equivalent).

In our case, the appropriate window ranges could be the following:

- for the horizontal axis, $x \in [15,35]$
- for the vertical axis, $y \in [-2,2]$

This gives us the following graph:

![](<../Source/9.1.3. Finding Intersections of Functions Using a Graphing Calculator/Images/c6e565cbb6f882a0ae14a0d4cd4e64b8.png>)

The graph shows that the root lies inside the interval $x\in [25,30]$.

To get a good approximation for the root, we use the $\bbox[2px, border: 2px solid gray]{\color{gray}\,\text{zero}\,}$ command (or equivalent). This is usually accessed by pressing the $\bbox[2px, border: 2px solid gray]{\color{gray}\,\text{calc}\,}$ button (or equivalent). Note that we sometimes need to press the $\bbox[2px, border: 2px solid gray]{\color{gray}\,\text{2nd}\,}$ or $\bbox[2px, border: 2px solid gray]{\color{gray}\,\text{shift}\,}$ button first.

We enter the following when prompted:

- The "left bound" should be a value close to the root on its left-hand side. Let's pick $25$.
- The "right bound" should be a value close to the root on its right-hand side. Let's pick $30$.
- The "guess" could be any number from $[25,30]$. Let's pick $28$.

![](<../Source/9.1.3. Finding Intersections of Functions Using a Graphing Calculator/Images/983e33f35b725751a39853d77a2a135c.png>)

The calculator returns the zero

$\qquad$

$$
x \approx 29.291\,697
$$

Rounding to $3$ decimal places, we obtain the following:

$\qquad$

$$
x \approx 29.292
$$
