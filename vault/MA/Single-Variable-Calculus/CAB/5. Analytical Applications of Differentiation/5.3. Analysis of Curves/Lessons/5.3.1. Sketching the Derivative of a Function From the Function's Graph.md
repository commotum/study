## Introduction

Suppose we are given the graph of the function $f(x)$, as shown below. The function has critical points at $x=-1$, $x=2$, and an inflection point at $x=0.5$.
How can we sketch the graph of the derivative $f'(x)$?

![](<../Source/5.3.1. Sketching the Derivative of a Function From the Function's Graph/Images/4a9e6eb093e0376051e68b6f296d2a7e.png>)

Notice the following:

- At the stationary points, where the tangent line to $y=f(x)$ is parallel to the $x$-axis, we have $f'(x)=0$.
- When $f(x)$ is increasing, we have $f'(x) > 0$.
- When $f(x)$ is decreasing, we have $f'(x) < 0$.

We can now summarize the information from the graph in the table below:

| $x$ | $(-\infty,-1)$ | $-1$ | $(-1,2)$ | $2$ | $(2,\infty)$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $f$ | $\nearrow$ | $\textrm{max}$ | $\searrow$ | $\textrm{min}$ | $\nearrow$ |
| $f'$ | $+$ | $0$ | $-$ | $0$ | $+$ |

Using this information, we can identify the regions where $f'(x)$ should cross the $x$-axis. According to the table, these are points $(-1,0)$ and $(2,0)$:

![](<../Source/5.3.1. Sketching the Derivative of a Function From the Function's Graph/Images/154ff05101f46cba7912a03db8327859.png>)

Additionally, we are told that the function has an inflection point at $x = 0.5$. So $f''(0.5)=0$ and the tangent to the graph of $y=f'(x)$ must be parallel to the $x$-axis.

We don't know the exact value of $f'(0.5)$ but from the graph of $y=f(x)$, we can conclude that at $x = 0.5$ the slope is negative. Therefore, we get a point below the $x$-axis:

![](<../Source/5.3.1. Sketching the Derivative of a Function From the Function's Graph/Images/d3ecf66501215f33974f353e53ea9e3f.png>)

Finally, connecting up the parts of the graph above, we get an approximate sketch of $f'(x)$:

![](<../Source/5.3.1. Sketching the Derivative of a Function From the Function's Graph/Images/f77c9a43854e8291ccfdda33b7ba7d86.png>)

## Sketching the Derivative of a Quadratic Function

**Example:** Consider the graph of $y=f(x)$ below. Sketch the graph of $y=f'(x)$, the derivative of $f(x)$.

![](<../Source/5.3.1. Sketching the Derivative of a Function From the Function's Graph/Images/41a0b94e2319193b9337a7433f93b1fb.png>)

**Explanation**

Notice the following:

- At the stationary points, where the tangent line to $y=f(x)$ is parallel to the $x$-axis, we have $f'(x)=0$.
- When $f(x)$ is increasing, we have $f'(x)>0$.
- When $f(x)$ is decreasing, we have $f'(x) < 0$.

We can now summarize the information from the graph in the table below. Note that the stationary point was identified (approximately) from the graph of $y=f(x)$:

| $x$ | $(-\infty,2)$ | $2$ | $(2,\infty)$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $f$ | $\searrow$ | $\textrm{min}$ | $\nearrow$ |
| $f'$ | $-$ | $0$ | $+$ |

Additionally, from the graph of $y=f(x)$, we can conclude that there are no inflection points. The graph of $y=f(x)$ is concave up in $(-\infty,\infty)$. So, $f''(x) > 0$ and $y=f'(x)$ is increasing.

Therefore, the graph of $y=f'(x)$ may look as shown below.

- At the stationary points, where the tangent line to $y=f(x)$ is parallel to the $x$-axis, we have $f'(x)=0$.

## Sketching the Derivative of a Piecewise-Differentiable Function

**Example:** Consider the graph of $y=f(x)$ below. Sketch the graph of $y=f'(x)$, the derivative of $f(x)$.

![](<../Source/5.3.1. Sketching the Derivative of a Function From the Function's Graph/Images/be1fe796582a7a59732750acc7020a06.png>)

**Explanation**

Notice the following:

- When $f(x)$ is increasing, we have $f'(x)>0$.
- When $f(x)$ is decreasing, we get $f'(x) < 0$.

We can now summarize the information from the graph in the table below.

| $x$ | $(-\infty,-1)$ | $-1$ | $(-1,\infty)$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $f$ | $\nearrow$ | $\textrm{max}$ | $\searrow$ |
| $f'$ | $+$ | $\textrm{undef.}$ | $-$ |

From the graph of $y=f(x)$, we can conclude that there are no inflection points. Also:

- For $x \in (-\infty,-1)$, the graph of $y=f(x)$ is an increasing straight line. So, $y=f'(x)$ will be a positive constant on this interval.
- For $x \in (-1,\infty)$, the graph of $y=f(x)$ is concave down. So, $f''(x) < 0$ and $y=f'(x)$ is decreasing on this interval.

Additionally, we have a rapid change of the slope of the tangent line when we pass through $x=-1$ from left to right. This means that the derivative is not defined at $x=-1$, and we obtain a jump discontinuity at this point for the graph of $y=f'(x)$.

Therefore, the graph of $y=f'(x)$ may look as shown below.

![](<../Source/5.3.1. Sketching the Derivative of a Function From the Function's Graph/Images/a48c5808b05ddaeb92d9bdfaa585aa8c.png>)

<a id="sketching-the-second-derivative-of-a-function"></a>
## Sketching the Second Derivative of a Function

**Example:** The graph of the function $y=f'(x)$, the derivative of $y=f(x)$, is shown below. Sketch the graph of $y=f''(x)$.

![](<../Source/5.3.1. Sketching the Derivative of a Function From the Function's Graph/Images/68a8de9560a29a33ee90b6095f9f1dc2.png>)

**Explanation**

First, if we denote

$$
g(x) = f'(x)
$$

then

$$
f''(x)=g'(x)
$$

So, we need to sketch the graph of $y=g'(x)$ given the graph of $y=g(x)$, as usual.

Notice the following:

- At the critical points, where the tangent line to $y=f(x)$ is parallel to the $x$-axis, we have $g'(x)=0$.
- When $f(x)$ is increasing, we have $g'(x)>0$.
- When $f(x)$ is decreasing, we have $g'(x) < 0$.

We can now summarize the information from the graph in the table below. Note that the critical points were identified (approximately) from the graph of $y=g(x)$:

| $x$ | $(-\infty,1)$ | $1$ | $(1,3)$ | $3$ | $(3,\infty)$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $g$ | $\searrow$ | $\textrm{not extr.}$ | $\searrow$ | $\textrm{min}$ | $\nearrow$ |
| $g'$ | $-$ | $0$ | $-$ | $0$ | $+$ |

Additionally, the function has an inflection point at

$$
x \approx 1
$$

and

$$
x\approx 2.25
$$

At the inflection points, we have $g''(x)=0$, so the tangent to the graph of $y=g'(x)$ must be parallel to the $x$-axis at

$$
x\approx 1
$$

and

$$
x\approx 2.25
$$

We don't know the exact value of $g'(1)$ and $g'(2.25)$, but from the graph of $y=g(x)$, we can conclude that at

$$
x \approx 1
$$

and

$$
x\approx 2.25
$$

the slope is zero and negative, respectively.

Therefore, the graph of

$$
y=g'(x)=f''(x)
$$

may look as shown below.

![](<../Source/5.3.1. Sketching the Derivative of a Function From the Function's Graph/Images/36d7d93b29a310e98e8077f0af4dbc21.png>)
