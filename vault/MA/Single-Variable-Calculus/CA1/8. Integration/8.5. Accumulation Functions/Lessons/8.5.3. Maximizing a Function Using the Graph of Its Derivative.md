## Introduction

In this lesson, we will learn how to use the graph of a function's derivative to maximize the function. Let's understand how this process works by studying a specific example.

The graph of $y = f'(x)$, the *derivative* of $f(x)$, is shown in the picture below.

![](<../Source/8.5.3. Maximizing a Function Using the Graph of Its Derivative/Images/a4e1de2f6e126576204b3dccd251c42f.png>)

The areas of the shaded regions are indicated on the diagram. Additionally, it is known that

$$
f(-2)=3
$$

Our goal is find the *maximum* value of $f(x)$ on the interval $x\in [-2,2]$.

To find the maximum value of $f(x)$, we use the candidates test.

According to the candidates test, the maximum value of $f(x)$ on $[-2,2]$ must occur at a critical point or an endpoint of the interval. Therefore, we proceed as follows.

**Step 1**: Find the critical points:

We see that $f'(x)$ is defined everywhere on $x\in (-2,2)$, so there are no non-stationary critical points. Additionally, we see that $f'(x)=0$ at $x=-1$. Therefore, the candidates are

$$
x=-2,\quad x=-1,\quad x=2
$$

**Step 2**: Compute the value of $f$ for each candidate using the fundamental theorem of calculus:

- The value of $f(x)$ at the left endpoint is given:
$f(-2) = 3$
- To compute $f(-1)$, we apply the fundamental theorem of calculus. Since the area of the left triangle in our picture equals ${\color{red}{1}}$, we have
$f(-1)|= f(-2) + ∫_{-2}^{-1}f^{′}(x)dx; = 3 + 1; = 4$.

- To compute $f(2)$, we apply the fundamental theorem of calculus once more. Using the fact that the area of the left triangle equals ${\color{red}{1}}$ and the area of the right triangle equals ${\color{blue}{\dfrac32}}$, we have
$f(2)|= f(-2) + ∫_{-2}^{2}f^{′}(x)dx; = 3 + 1 - \frac{3}{2}; = \frac{5}{2}$.
Notice that we *subtracted* the area of the right triangle since it lies *under* the $x$-axis.

**Step 3**: Select the largest value of $f$ from our candidates.

Comparing the values of $f$ found in step $2$, we have

$$
f_{\text{max}} = \max\left\lbrace 3, 4, \dfrac{5}{2} \right\rbrace = 4
$$

Therefore, the maximum value of $f$ is

$$
f_{\text{max}}=4
$$

and the value of $x$ that maximizes $f(x)$ is

$$
x_\text{max}=-1
$$

## Finding a Maximum Value Given a Derivative Graph With One Area

**Example:** The function $f(x)$ is defined and differentiable on $[-2,3]$. The graph of $f'(x)$, the derivative of $f(x)$, is shown above, where the area of the region between the graph and the $x$-axis is $10$. Find the maximum value of $f(x)$ on the interval $[-2,3]$ given that $f(-2)=3$.

![](<../Source/8.5.3. Maximizing a Function Using the Graph of Its Derivative/Images/e1a2bac1d32dab4a851fbc23ce04bd6d.png>)

**Explanation**

According to the candidates test, the maximum value of $f(x)$ on $[-2,3]$ must occur at a critical point or an endpoint of the interval.

**Step 1**: Find the critical points:

We see that $f'(x)$ is defined everywhere on $x\in (-2,3)$, and

$$
f'(x)\neq 0
$$

on this interval. Therefore, the candidates are $x=-2,\,3$.

**Step 2**: Compute the value of $f$ for each candidate using the fundamental theorem of calculus:

Using the given information and the fundamental theorem of calculus, we have the following:

$$
\begin{aligned}
f(-2) &= 3 \\
f(3) &= f(-2) + ∫_{-2}^{3}f^{′}(t)dt \\
&= 3 + (-10) \\
&=-7
\end{aligned}
$$

**Step 3**: Select the largest value of $f$ from our candidates.

Comparing the values of $f$ found in step $2$, we have

$$
f_{\text{max}} = \max\{3,-7\} = 3
$$

Therefore, the maximum value of $f$ is

$$
f_{\text{max}}=3
$$

and the value of $x$ that maximizes $f(x)$ is

$$
x_\text{max}=-2
$$

## Finding a Maximum Value Given a Derivative Graph With More Than One Area

**Example:** The function $f$ is defined and differentiable on $[0,6]$. The graph of $f'(x)$, the derivative of $f(x)$, is shown below, where the areas of the regions between the graph and the $x$-axis are $6$ and $2$ respectively (from left to right). Find the maximum value of $f(x)$ on the interval $[0,6]$ given that $f(0)=1$.

![](<../Source/8.5.3. Maximizing a Function Using the Graph of Its Derivative/Images/ef99d02c813c289d71f7c2d22403f7d3.png>)

**Explanation**

According to the candidates test, the maximum value of $f(x)$ on $[0,6]$ must occur at a critical point or an endpoint of the interval.

**Step 1**: Find the critical points:

We see that $f'(x)$ is defined everywhere on $x\in (0,6)$, and $f'(x)=0$ at $x=4$. Therefore, the candidates are $x=0,4,6$.

**Step 2**: Compute the value of $f$ for each candidate using the fundamental theorem of calculus:

Using the given information and the fundamental theorem of calculus, we have the following:

$$
\begin{aligned}
f(0) &= 1 \\
f(4) &= f(0) + ∫_{0}^{4}f^{′}(x)dx \\
&= 1 + 6 \\
&= 7 \\
f(6) &= f(0) + ∫_{0}^{6}f^{′}(x)dx \\
&= 1 + 6 - 2 \\
&= 5
\end{aligned}
$$

**Step 3**: Select the largest value of $f$ from our candidates.

Comparing the values of $f$ found in step $2$, we have

$$
f_{\text{max}} = \max\{1, 7, 5\} = 7
$$

Therefore, the maximum value of $f$ is

$$
f_{\text{max}}=7
$$

and the value of $x$ that maximizes $f(x)$ is

$$
x_\text{max}=4
$$

## Finding a Maximum Value by Computing Areas Under Curves

**Example:** The function $f(x)$ is defined and differentiable on $1 \leq x \leq 5$. The graph of $f'(x)$, the derivative of $f(x)$, is shown below. Find a value of $x$ that maximizes $f(x)$ on the interval $[1,5]$ given that $f(1)=-2$.

![](<../Source/8.5.3. Maximizing a Function Using the Graph of Its Derivative/Images/6f0ac7a7f28d9c61c9a4902868080355.png>)

**Explanation**

According to the candidates test, the maximum value of $f(x)$ on $[1,5]$ must occur at a critical point or an endpoint of the interval.

**Step 1**: Find the critical points:

We see that $f'(x)$ is defined everywhere on $x\in (1,5)$, and $f'(x)=0$ at $x=2$ and $x=4$. Therefore, the candidates are $x=1,2,4,5$.

**Step 2**: Compute the value of $f$ for each candidate using the fundamental theorem of calculus:

The function is made up of $3$ triangles. Let's add the areas bounded by these shapes to our diagram:

![](<../Source/8.5.3. Maximizing a Function Using the Graph of Its Derivative/Images/3bd071c099f6254869f5d21799bcdd55.png>)

Using the given information and the fundamental theorem of calculus, we have the following:

$$
\begin{aligned}
f(1) &= -2 \\
f(2) &= f(1) + ∫_{1}^{2}f^{′}(x)dx \\
&=-2 + \frac{3}{2} \\
&=-\frac{1}{2} \\
f(4) &= f(1) + ∫_{1}^{4}f^{′}(x)dx \\
&=-2 + \frac{3}{2} - 1 \\
&=-\frac{3}{2} \\
f(5) &= f(1) + ∫_{1}^{5}f^{′}(x)dx \\
&=-2 + \frac{3}{2} - 1 + \frac{1}{2} \\
&=-1
\end{aligned}
$$

**Step 3**: Select the largest value of $f$ from our candidates.

Comparing the values of $f$ found in step $2$, we have

$$
f_{\text{max}} = \max\left\lbrace -2,-\dfrac{1}{2},-\dfrac{3}{2},-1 \right\rbrace = -\dfrac{1}{2}
$$

Therefore, the maximum value of $f$ is

$$
f_{\text{max}}=-\dfrac{1}{2}
$$

and the value of $x$ that maximizes $f(x)$ is

$$
x_\text{max}=2
$$
