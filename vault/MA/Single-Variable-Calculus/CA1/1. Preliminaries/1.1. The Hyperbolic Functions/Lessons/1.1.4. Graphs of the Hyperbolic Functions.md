## Introduction

$$
\newcommand{\sech}{\mathop{\rm sech}\nolimits} \newcommand{\csch}{\mathop{\rm csch}\nolimits} \newcommand{\coth}{\mathop{\rm coth}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arsinh}{\mathop{\rm arsinh}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arcosh}{\mathop{\rm arcosh}\nolimits} \newcommand{\artanh}{\mathop{\rm artanh}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arsech}{\mathop{\rm arsech}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arcsch}{\mathop{\rm arcsch}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arcoth}{\mathop{\rm arcoth}\nolimits} \newcommand{\cosh}{\mathop{\rm cosh}\nolimits} \newcommand{\sinh}{\mathop{\rm sinh}\nolimits} \newcommand{\csch}{\mathop{\rm csch}\nolimits} \newcommand{\coth}{\mathop{\rm coth}\nolimits}
$$

Suppose we want to plot the graph of the function $y= \sinh x$. To do this, we start with the definition:

$$
\sinh x = \dfrac{e^x-e^{-x}}{2}
$$

We create a table of values by substituting some $x$-values into the definition of $y = \sinh x$ and finding the corresponding (approximate) $y$-values:

| **$x$** | $-2$ | $-1.5$ | $-1$ | $-0.5$ | $0$ | $0.5$ | $1$ | $1.5$ | $2$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $\sinh x$ | $-3.63$ | $-2.13$ | $-1.18$ | $-0.52$ | $0$ | $0.52$ | $1.18$ | $2.13$ | $3.63$ |

Plotting these points, we get the following curve:

![](<../Source/1.1.4. Graphs of the Hyperbolic Functions/Images/cef48e135449bc8038ee97bd41f1e178.png>)

The key features of the graph are as follows:

- The domain and range are $(-\infty,\infty)$.
- It is an odd function.
- It has a zero at $x=0$.
- Its graph is increasing over the entire real line. Moreover,$\qquad$ $\sinh{x} \to -\infty$ as $x \to -\infty$, $\qquad$ $\sinh{x} \to \infty$ as $x \to \infty$.
- It is the mean (average) of the functions $y=e^x$ and $y=-e^{-x}$.

![](<../Source/1.1.4. Graphs of the Hyperbolic Functions/Images/c9f5c07f0845e89ae945dd6e446d8e8f.png>)

## Identifying the Graph and Properties of the Hyperbolic Sine

**Example:** Which of the following statements are true regarding the function
$\newcommand{\sech}{\mathop{\rm sech}\nolimits} \newcommand{\csch}{\mathop{\rm csch}\nolimits} \newcommand{\coth}{\mathop{\rm coth}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arsinh}{\mathop{\rm arsinh}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arcosh}{\mathop{\rm arcosh}\nolimits} \newcommand{\artanh}{\mathop{\rm artanh}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arsech}{\mathop{\rm arsech}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arcsch}{\mathop{\rm arcsch}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arcoth}{\mathop{\rm arcoth}\nolimits} \newcommand{\cosh}{\mathop{\rm cosh}\nolimits} \newcommand{\sinh}{\mathop{\rm sinh}\nolimits} \newcommand{\csch}{\mathop{\rm csch}\nolimits} \newcommand{\coth}{\mathop{\rm coth}\nolimits} y=\sinh{x}$?

1. The function is the average of $y=e^x$ and $y=e^{-x}$.
2. The function is decreasing over $(-\infty,0)$ and increasing over $(0, \infty)$.
3. The function is odd.

**Explanation**

Let's recall the graph of

$$
\newcommand{\sech}{\mathop{\rm sech}\nolimits} \newcommand{\csch}{\mathop{\rm csch}\nolimits} \newcommand{\coth}{\mathop{\rm coth}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arsinh}{\mathop{\rm arsinh}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arcosh}{\mathop{\rm arcosh}\nolimits} \newcommand{\artanh}{\mathop{\rm artanh}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arsech}{\mathop{\rm arsech}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arcsch}{\mathop{\rm arcsch}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arcoth}{\mathop{\rm arcoth}\nolimits} \newcommand{\cosh}{\mathop{\rm cosh}\nolimits} \newcommand{\sinh}{\mathop{\rm sinh}\nolimits} \newcommand{\csch}{\mathop{\rm csch}\nolimits} \newcommand{\coth}{\mathop{\rm coth}\nolimits} y=\sinh{x}
$$

![](<../Source/1.1.4. Graphs of the Hyperbolic Functions/Images/0c5bca5565e0718b00c647c86fdee077.png>)

Let's now examine our statements in turn.

- Statement I is false. The function $y=\sinh{x}$ is the average of $y=e^x$ and $y=-e^{-x}{:}$
$\sinh{x} = \dfrac{e^x+\left(-e^{-x}\right)}{2}= \dfrac{e^x-e^{-x}}{2}$
- Statement II is false. The function $y=\sinh{x}$ is increasing over the entire real line.
- Statement III is true. Indeed, the function $y=\sinh{x}$ is odd, i.e., $\sinh(-x)=-\sinh(x)$, and the graph has rotational symmetry of order $2$ about the origin.

Therefore, the correct answer is "III only."

## The Graph of the Hyperbolic Cosine

Let's now plot the graph of the hyperbolic cosine. As before, we start with the definition:

$$
\newcommand{\sech}{\mathop{\rm sech}\nolimits} \newcommand{\csch}{\mathop{\rm csch}\nolimits} \newcommand{\coth}{\mathop{\rm coth}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arsinh}{\mathop{\rm arsinh}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arcosh}{\mathop{\rm arcosh}\nolimits} \newcommand{\artanh}{\mathop{\rm artanh}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arsech}{\mathop{\rm arsech}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arcsch}{\mathop{\rm arcsch}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arcoth}{\mathop{\rm arcoth}\nolimits} \newcommand{\cosh}{\mathop{\rm cosh}\nolimits} \newcommand{\sinh}{\mathop{\rm sinh}\nolimits} \newcommand{\csch}{\mathop{\rm csch}\nolimits} \newcommand{\coth}{\mathop{\rm coth}\nolimits} y= \cosh x = \dfrac{e^x+e^{-x}}{2}
$$

Creating a table of values (rounded to two decimal places where appropriate), we get the following:

| **$x$** | $-2$ | $-1.5$ | $-1$ | $-0.5$ | $0$ | $0.5$ | $1$ | $1.5$ | $2$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $\cosh x$ | $3.76$ | $2.35$ | $1.54$ | $1.13$ | $1$ | $1.13$ | $1.54$ | $2.35$ | $3.76$ |

Plotting these points, we get the curve shown below:

![](<../Source/1.1.4. Graphs of the Hyperbolic Functions/Images/6c0b821a1d7ef7a487232c7b1cffedcd.png>)

The key features of the graph are as follows:

- The domain is $(-\infty,\infty)$ and the range is $[MATH: [1,\infty).]$
- It is an even function (the graph is symmetric about the $y$-axis).
- It has no zeros.
- Its minimum value occurs at $x=0$.
- Its graph is decreasing over $(-\infty,0)$ and is increasing over $(0,\infty)$. Moreover,$\qquad$ $\cosh{x} \to \infty$ as $x \to \pm\infty$.
- It is the mean (average) of the functions $y=e^x$ and $y=e^{-x}$.

![](<../Source/1.1.4. Graphs of the Hyperbolic Functions/Images/3f77657321c09dfc8245f6f5a457c4fb.png>)

## Identifying the Graph and Properties of the Hyperbolic Cosine

**Example:** Which of the following statements are true regarding the function
$\newcommand{\sech}{\mathop{\rm sech}\nolimits} \newcommand{\csch}{\mathop{\rm csch}\nolimits} \newcommand{\coth}{\mathop{\rm coth}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arsinh}{\mathop{\rm arsinh}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arcosh}{\mathop{\rm arcosh}\nolimits} \newcommand{\artanh}{\mathop{\rm artanh}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arsech}{\mathop{\rm arsech}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arcsch}{\mathop{\rm arcsch}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arcoth}{\mathop{\rm arcoth}\nolimits} \newcommand{\cosh}{\mathop{\rm cosh}\nolimits} \newcommand{\sinh}{\mathop{\rm sinh}\nolimits} \newcommand{\csch}{\mathop{\rm csch}\nolimits} \newcommand{\coth}{\mathop{\rm coth}\nolimits} y=\cosh{x}$?

1. The domain of the function is $[MATH: [0, \infty).]$
2. The graph of the function is symmetric with respect to the $y$-axis.
3. The minimum of the function occurs at $x=0$.

**Explanation**

Let's recall the graph of

$$
\newcommand{\sech}{\mathop{\rm sech}\nolimits} \newcommand{\csch}{\mathop{\rm csch}\nolimits} \newcommand{\coth}{\mathop{\rm coth}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arsinh}{\mathop{\rm arsinh}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arcosh}{\mathop{\rm arcosh}\nolimits} \newcommand{\artanh}{\mathop{\rm artanh}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arsech}{\mathop{\rm arsech}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arcsch}{\mathop{\rm arcsch}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arcoth}{\mathop{\rm arcoth}\nolimits} \newcommand{\cosh}{\mathop{\rm cosh}\nolimits} \newcommand{\sinh}{\mathop{\rm sinh}\nolimits} \newcommand{\csch}{\mathop{\rm csch}\nolimits} \newcommand{\coth}{\mathop{\rm coth}\nolimits} y=\cosh{x}
$$

![](<../Source/1.1.4. Graphs of the Hyperbolic Functions/Images/1ca8513ff00e5a9bb5aba2ee16b7e14d.png>)

Let's now examine our statements in turn.

- Statement I is false. The domain of the function $y=\cosh{x}$ is $(-\infty,\infty)$ and the range is $[MATH: [1,\infty).]$
- Statement II is true. Indeed, the function $y=\cosh{x}$ is even, i.e. $\cosh(-x)=\cosh(x)$, and the graph is symmetric about the $y$-axis.

- Statement III is true. Indeed, the minimum value of the function $y=\cosh{x}$ is $y=1$ and occurs at $x=0$.

Therefore, the correct answer is "II and III only."

## The Graph of the Hyperbolic Tangent

Finally, let's plot the graph of the function

$$
\newcommand{\sech}{\mathop{\rm sech}\nolimits} \newcommand{\csch}{\mathop{\rm csch}\nolimits} \newcommand{\coth}{\mathop{\rm coth}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arsinh}{\mathop{\rm arsinh}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arcosh}{\mathop{\rm arcosh}\nolimits} \newcommand{\artanh}{\mathop{\rm artanh}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arsech}{\mathop{\rm arsech}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arcsch}{\mathop{\rm arcsch}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arcoth}{\mathop{\rm arcoth}\nolimits} \newcommand{\cosh}{\mathop{\rm cosh}\nolimits} \newcommand{\sinh}{\mathop{\rm sinh}\nolimits} \newcommand{\csch}{\mathop{\rm csch}\nolimits} \newcommand{\coth}{\mathop{\rm coth}\nolimits} y= \tanh x
$$

We start with the definition:

$$
\tanh x = \dfrac{\sinh x}{\cosh x} = \dfrac{e^x-e^{-x}}{e^x+e^{-x}}
$$

Our table of values (rounded to two decimal places where appropriate) is given below:

| **$x$** | $-2$ | $-1.5$ | $-1$ | $-0.5$ | $0$ | $0.5$ | $1$ | $1.5$ | $2$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $\tanh x$ | $-0.96$ | $-0.91$ | $-0.76$ | $-0.46$ | $0$ | $0.46$ | $0.76$ | $0.91$ | $0.96$ |

Plotting these points, we get the following curve:

![](<../Source/1.1.4. Graphs of the Hyperbolic Functions/Images/c1cbed142fa89ffc27ea7991ad8496d9.png>)

The key features of the graph are as follows:

- The domain is $(-\infty,\infty)$ and the range is $(-1,1)$.
- It is an odd function (the graph has rotational symmetry of order $2$ about the origin).
- It has a zero at $x=0$.
- The graph is increasing over the entire real line. Moreover,$\qquad$ $\tanh{x} \to -1$ as $x \to -\infty$, $\qquad$ $\tanh{x} \to 1$ as $x \to \infty$.

![](<../Source/1.1.4. Graphs of the Hyperbolic Functions/Images/54e12c06f53b0e0211616771e00ff387.png>)

## Identifying the Graph and Properties of the Hyperbolic Tangent

**Example:** Which of the following statements are true regarding the function
$\newcommand{\sech}{\mathop{\rm sech}\nolimits} \newcommand{\csch}{\mathop{\rm csch}\nolimits} \newcommand{\coth}{\mathop{\rm coth}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arsinh}{\mathop{\rm arsinh}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arcosh}{\mathop{\rm arcosh}\nolimits} \newcommand{\artanh}{\mathop{\rm artanh}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arsech}{\mathop{\rm arsech}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arcsch}{\mathop{\rm arcsch}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arcoth}{\mathop{\rm arcoth}\nolimits} \newcommand{\cosh}{\mathop{\rm cosh}\nolimits} \newcommand{\sinh}{\mathop{\rm sinh}\nolimits} \newcommand{\csch}{\mathop{\rm csch}\nolimits} \newcommand{\coth}{\mathop{\rm coth}\nolimits} y=\tanh{x}$?

1. $\tanh{x} \to 1$ as $x \to \infty$.
2. The range of the function is $(-\infty,\infty)$.
3. The function has no zeros.

**Explanation**

Let's recall the graph of

$$
\newcommand{\sech}{\mathop{\rm sech}\nolimits} \newcommand{\csch}{\mathop{\rm csch}\nolimits} \newcommand{\coth}{\mathop{\rm coth}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arsinh}{\mathop{\rm arsinh}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arcosh}{\mathop{\rm arcosh}\nolimits} \newcommand{\artanh}{\mathop{\rm artanh}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arsech}{\mathop{\rm arsech}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arcsch}{\mathop{\rm arcsch}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arcoth}{\mathop{\rm arcoth}\nolimits} \newcommand{\cosh}{\mathop{\rm cosh}\nolimits} \newcommand{\sinh}{\mathop{\rm sinh}\nolimits} \newcommand{\csch}{\mathop{\rm csch}\nolimits} \newcommand{\coth}{\mathop{\rm coth}\nolimits} y=\tanh{x}
$$

![](<../Source/1.1.4. Graphs of the Hyperbolic Functions/Images/0caccbdf01e7bf8b920e1f0217b1c902.png>)

Let's now examine our statements in turn.

- Statement I is true. Indeed, we have that $\tanh{x} \to 1$ as $x \to \infty$.
- Statement II is false. The range of $\tanh{x}$ is $(-1,1)$.
- Statement III is false. The function $y=\tanh{x}$ has a zero at $x=0$.

Therefore, the correct answer is "I only."
