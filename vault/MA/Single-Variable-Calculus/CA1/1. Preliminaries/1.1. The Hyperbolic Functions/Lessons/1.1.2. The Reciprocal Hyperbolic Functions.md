## Introduction

$$
\newcommand{\sech}{\mathop{\rm sech}\nolimits} \newcommand{\csch}{\mathop{\rm csch}\nolimits} \newcommand{\coth}{\mathop{\rm coth}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arsinh}{\mathop{\rm arsinh}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arcosh}{\mathop{\rm arcosh}\nolimits} \newcommand{\artanh}{\mathop{\rm artanh}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arsech}{\mathop{\rm arsech}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arcsch}{\mathop{\rm arcsch}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arcoth}{\mathop{\rm arcoth}\nolimits}
$$

We can define the **reciprocal hyperbolic functions** similarly to how we define reciprocal trigonometric functions.

The **hyperbolic cosecant function** (pronounced "cosetch") is defined as

$$
\csch{x} = \dfrac{1}{\sinh{x}} = \dfrac{2}{e^x-e^{-x}}\,\,\,
$$

The graph of $y=\csch x$ is shown below.

![](<../Source/1.1.2. The Reciprocal Hyperbolic Functions/Images/a544b6842b8023fe6dc2a7243ab4aa8d.png>)

Note the following properties of $y=\csch x$:

- Its domain is $x \in (-\infty, 0)\cup (0,\infty)$.
- Its range is $y \in (-\infty, 0)\cup (0,\infty)$.
- It is an odd function.

## Evaluating Hyperbolic Cosecant

**Example:** $\newcommand{\arsinh}{\mathop{\rm arsinh}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arcosh}{\mathop{\rm arcosh}\nolimits} \newcommand{\artanh}{\mathop{\rm artanh}\nolimits} \newcommand{\sech}{\mathop{\rm sech}\nolimits} \newcommand{\csch}{\mathop{\rm csch}\nolimits} \newcommand{\coth}{\mathop{\rm coth}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arsech}{\mathop{\rm arsech}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arcsch}{\mathop{\rm arcsch}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arcoth}{\mathop{\rm arcoth}\nolimits}$

Evaluate $\csch (-9)$.

**Explanation**

$$
\newcommand{\arsinh}{\mathop{\rm arsinh}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arcosh}{\mathop{\rm arcosh}\nolimits} \newcommand{\artanh}{\mathop{\rm artanh}\nolimits} \newcommand{\sech}{\mathop{\rm sech}\nolimits} \newcommand{\csch}{\mathop{\rm csch}\nolimits} \newcommand{\coth}{\mathop{\rm coth}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arsech}{\mathop{\rm arsech}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arcsch}{\mathop{\rm arcsch}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arcoth}{\mathop{\rm arcoth}\nolimits}
$$

The definition of $\csch x$ is

$$
\csch x = \dfrac{1}{\sinh x} = \dfrac {2}{e^{x} - e^{-x}}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\csch (-9) =\dfrac{2}{e^{-9}-e^{-(-9)}}=\dfrac{2}{e^{-9}-e^{9}}
$$

## Hyperbolic Secant

$$
\newcommand{\sech}{\mathop{\rm sech}\nolimits} \newcommand{\csch}{\mathop{\rm csch}\nolimits} \newcommand{\coth}{\mathop{\rm coth}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arsinh}{\mathop{\rm arsinh}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arcosh}{\mathop{\rm arcosh}\nolimits} \newcommand{\artanh}{\mathop{\rm artanh}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arsech}{\mathop{\rm arsech}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arcsch}{\mathop{\rm arcsch}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arcoth}{\mathop{\rm arcoth}\nolimits}
$$

The **hyperbolic secant function** (pronounced "sesh") is defined as

$$
\sech{x} = \dfrac{1}{\cosh{x}} = \dfrac{2}{e^x+e^{-x}}\,\,\,
$$

The graph of $y=\sech x$ is shown below.

![](<../Source/1.1.2. The Reciprocal Hyperbolic Functions/Images/2297f59763224e98dc496bf5e3625dd1.png>)

Note the following properties of $y=\sech x$:

- Its domain is $x \in (-\infty, \infty)$.
- Its range is $[MATH: y \in (0, 1].]$
- It has the horizontal asymptote $y=0$.
- It is an even function.

<a id="evaluating-hyperbolic-secant"></a>
## Evaluating Hyperbolic Secant

**Example:** $\newcommand{\arsinh}{\mathop{\rm arsinh}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arcosh}{\mathop{\rm arcosh}\nolimits} \newcommand{\artanh}{\mathop{\rm artanh}\nolimits} \newcommand{\sech}{\mathop{\rm sech}\nolimits} \newcommand{\csch}{\mathop{\rm csch}\nolimits} \newcommand{\coth}{\mathop{\rm coth}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arsech}{\mathop{\rm arsech}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arcsch}{\mathop{\rm arcsch}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arcoth}{\mathop{\rm arcoth}\nolimits}$

Evaluate $\sech \left(-t \right)$.

**Explanation**

$$
\newcommand{\arsinh}{\mathop{\rm arsinh}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arcosh}{\mathop{\rm arcosh}\nolimits} \newcommand{\artanh}{\mathop{\rm artanh}\nolimits} \newcommand{\sech}{\mathop{\rm sech}\nolimits} \newcommand{\csch}{\mathop{\rm csch}\nolimits} \newcommand{\coth}{\mathop{\rm coth}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arsech}{\mathop{\rm arsech}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arcsch}{\mathop{\rm arcsch}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arcoth}{\mathop{\rm arcoth}\nolimits}
$$

The definition of $\sech{x}$ is

$$
\sech{x} = \dfrac{1}{\cosh x} = \dfrac{2}{e^{x}+e^{-x}}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
sech (-t) &= (2)/(e^{-t} + e^{- (-t)}) \\
&= \frac{2}{e^{-t} + e^{t}}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Hyperbolic Cotangent

The **hyperbolic cotangent function** (pronounced "coth") is defined as

$$
\begin{aligned}
\coth x = \frac{1}{\tanh x} &= \frac{e^{x} + e^{-x}}{e^{x} - e^{-x}}
\end{aligned}
$$

The graph of $y=\coth x$ is shown below.

![](<../Source/1.1.2. The Reciprocal Hyperbolic Functions/Images/1610da442abb62245def1203f4441579.png>)

Note the following properties of $y=\coth x$:

- Its domain is $x\in (-\infty, 0)\cup (0,\infty)$.
- Its range is $y \in (-\infty, -1)\cup (1,\infty)$.
- It has horizontal asymptotes $y=\pm 1$.
- It is an odd function.

Using the alternative definitions of $\tanh x$, we can write down two more definitions for $\coth x$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\coth x &= \frac{1}{\tanh x} \\
&= \frac{e^{2x} + 1}{e^{2x} - 1} \\
&= \frac{1 + e^{-2x}}{1 - e^{-2x}}
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="evaluating-hyperbolic-cotangent"></a>
## Evaluating Hyperbolic Cotangent

**Example:** $\newcommand{\arsinh}{\mathop{\rm arsinh}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arcosh}{\mathop{\rm arcosh}\nolimits} \newcommand{\artanh}{\mathop{\rm artanh}\nolimits} \newcommand{\sech}{\mathop{\rm sech}\nolimits} \newcommand{\csch}{\mathop{\rm csch}\nolimits} \newcommand{\coth}{\mathop{\rm coth}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arsech}{\mathop{\rm arsech}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arcsch}{\mathop{\rm arcsch}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arcoth}{\mathop{\rm arcoth}\nolimits}$

Evaluate $\coth (-\ln 3)$.

**Explanation**

$$
\newcommand{\arsinh}{\mathop{\rm arsinh}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arcosh}{\mathop{\rm arcosh}\nolimits} \newcommand{\artanh}{\mathop{\rm artanh}\nolimits} \newcommand{\sech}{\mathop{\rm sech}\nolimits} \newcommand{\csch}{\mathop{\rm csch}\nolimits} \newcommand{\coth}{\mathop{\rm coth}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arsech}{\mathop{\rm arsech}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arcsch}{\mathop{\rm arcsch}\nolimits} \newcommand{\arcoth}{\mathop{\rm arcoth}\nolimits}
$$

The definition of $\coth x$ is

$$
\begin{aligned}
\coth x &= \frac{1}{\tanh x} \\
&= \frac{e^{x} + e^{-x}}{e^{x} - e^{-x}} \\
&= \frac{e^{2x} + 1}{e^{2x} - 1} \\
&= \frac{1 + e^{-2x}}{1 - e^{-2x}}
\end{aligned}
$$

Using the fourth definition, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
\coth (-\ln 3) &= (1 + e^{-2(-\ln 3)})/(1 - e^{-2(-\ln 3)}) \\
&= \frac{1 + e^{2\ln 3}}{1 - e^{2\ln 3}} \\
&= \frac{1 + e^{\ln 3^{2}}}{1 - e^{\ln 3^{2}}} \\
&= \frac{1 + 3^{2}}{1 - 3^{2}} \\
&= \frac{1 + 9}{1 - 9} \\
&= (10)/((-8)) \\
&=-\frac{5}{4}
\end{aligned}
$$
