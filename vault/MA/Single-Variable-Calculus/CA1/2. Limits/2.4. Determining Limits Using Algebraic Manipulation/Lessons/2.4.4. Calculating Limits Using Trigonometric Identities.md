## Introduction

Suppose that we want to calculate the following limit:

$$
\lim_\limits{x\to \pi/2}\dfrac{\sin{2x}}{2\cos{x}}
$$

If we attempt to evaluate this limit by directly substituting

$$
x=\dfrac{\pi}{2}
$$

we get an indeterminate form:

$$
\begin{aligned}
lim_(x → π/2)\frac{\sin 2x}{2\cos x} &= (\sin (2 \cdot \frac{π}{2}))/(2\cos (\frac{π}{2})) \\
&= (\sin (π))/(2\cos (\frac{π}{2})) \\
&= \frac{0}{0}
\end{aligned}
$$

However, if we simplify the fraction before attempting to evaluate the limit, we obtain a more clear result.

To simplify the fraction in this particular case, the double angle formula for sine will be useful:

$$
\sin{2x}=2\sin{x}\cos{x}
$$

Using the double angle formula above, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
lim_(x → π/2)\frac{\sin 2x}{2\cos x} &= lim_(x → π/2)\frac{2\sin x\cos x}{2\cos x} \\
&= lim_(x → π/2)\frac{2\sin x\cos x}{2\cos x} \\
&= lim_(x → π/2)\sin x \\
&= \sin (\frac{π}{2}) \\
&= 1
\end{aligned}
$$

In other cases, we may need to use a different trigonometric identity.

## Calculating a Limit Using a Double Angle Formula

**Example:** $\lim_\limits{x\to \pi/4}\dfrac{\sin^2{x}-\cos^2{x}}{2\sin{x}\cos{2x}}=$

**Explanation**

If we attempt to evaluate this limit directly, we get an indeterminate form:

$$
\begin{aligned}
lim_(x → π/4)\frac{\sin^{2} x - \cos^{2} x}{2\sin x\cos 2x} &= (\sin^{2} (\frac{π}{4}) - \cos^{2} (\frac{π}{4}))/(2\sin (\frac{π}{4})\cos (2 \cdot \frac{π}{4})) \\
&= (1 - 1)/(\sqrt{2}\cos (\frac{π}{2})) \\
&= \frac{0}{0}
\end{aligned}
$$

So we need to simplify the expression. The double angle formula for cosine will be useful in this case:

$$
\cos{2x}=\cos^2{x}-\sin^2{x}
$$

Using the double angle formula above, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
lim_(x → π/4)\frac{\sin^{2} x - \cos^{2} x}{2\sin x\cos 2x} &= lim_(x → π/4)(\sin^{2} x - \cos^{2} x)/(2\sin x(\cos^{2} x - \sin^{2} x)) \\
&= lim_(x → π/4)(- (\cos^{2} x - \sin^{2} x))/(2\sin x(\cos^{2} x - \sin^{2} x)) \\
&= lim_(x → π/4)(-\frac{1}{2\sin x}) \\
&=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \\
&=-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="calculating-a-limit-using-a-variation-of-a-double-angle-formula"></a>
## Calculating a Limit Using a Variation of a Double Angle Formula

**Example:** Evaluate $\lim_\limits{x\to 0}\dfrac{1-\cos x}{3\sin^2\left(\dfrac{x}{2}\right)}$.

**Explanation**

If we attempt to evaluate this limit directly, we get an indeterminate form:

$$
\begin{aligned}
lim_(x → 0)(1 - \cos x)/(3\sin^{2} (\frac{x}{2})) &= (1 - \cos (0))/(3\sin^{2} (0)) \\
&= \frac{1 - 1}{3 \cdot 0} \\
&= \frac{0}{0}
\end{aligned}
$$

So we need to simplify the expression. Again we will use the double angle formula for cosine, but in a different form:

$$
\cos{x}=1-2\sin^2\left(\dfrac{x}{2}\right)
$$

Substituting the above formula, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
lim_(x → 0)(1 - \cos x)/(3\sin^{2} (\frac{x}{2})) &= lim_(x → 0)(1 - (1 - 2\sin^{2} (\frac{x}{2})))/(3\sin^{2} (\frac{x}{2})) \\
&= lim_(x → 0)(2\sin^{2} (\frac{x}{2}))/(3\sin^{2} (\frac{x}{2})) \\
&= lim_(x → 0)\frac{2}{3} \\
&= \frac{2}{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="calculating-a-limit-using-the-pythagorean-identity"></a>
## Calculating a Limit Using the Pythagorean Identity

**Example:** $\lim_\limits{x\to (-\pi/2)}\dfrac{\cos^2x}{1+\sin x}=$

**Explanation**

If we attempt to evaluate this limit directly, we get an indeterminate form:

$$
\begin{aligned}
lim_(x → (- π/2))\frac{\cos^{2} x}{1 + \sin x} &= (\cos^{2} (-\frac{π}{2}))/((1 + \sin (-\frac{π}{2}))) \\
&= \frac{0}{1 - 1} \\
&= \frac{0}{0}
\end{aligned}
$$

So we need to simplify the expression. In particular, we will use the Pythagorean identity:

$$
\cos^2{x}=1-\sin^2{x}
$$

Substituting the above formula, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
lim_(x → (- π/2))\frac{\cos^{2} x}{1 + \sin x} &= lim_(x → (- π/2))\frac{\cos^{2} x}{1 + \sin x} \\
&= lim_(x → (- π/2))\frac{1 - \sin^{2} x}{1 + \sin x} \\
&= lim_(x → (- π/2))((1 + \sin x)(1 - \sin x))/(1 + \sin x) \\
&= lim_(x → (- π/2))(1 - \sin x) \\
&= 1 - \sin (-\frac{π}{2}) \\
&= 1 - (-1) \\
&= 2
\end{aligned}
$$

## Calculating a Limit Using Another Form of the Pythagorean Identity

**Example:** Evaluate $\lim_\limits{x\to 0}\dfrac{\sec x-1}{7\tan^2x}$.

**Explanation**

If we attempt to evaluate this limit directly, we get an indeterminate form:

$$
\begin{aligned}
lim_(x → 0)\frac{\sec x - 1}{7\tan^{2} x} &= (\sec (0) - 1)/(7\tan^{2} (0)) \\
&= \frac{1 - 1}{7 \cdot 0} \\
&= \frac{0}{0}
\end{aligned}
$$

So we need to simplify the expression. In particular, we will use the Pythagorean identity for tangent and secant:

$$
\tan^2{x}=\sec^2{x}-1
$$

Substituting the above formula, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
lim_(x → 0)\frac{\sec x - 1}{7\tan^{2} x} &= lim_(x → 0)(\sec x - 1)/(7(\sec^{2} x - 1)) \\
&= lim_(x → 0)(\sec x - 1)/(7(\sec x - 1)(\sec x + 1)) \\
&= lim_(x → 0)(1)/(7(\sec x + 1)) \\
&= (1)/(7(\sec (0) + 1)) \\
&= (1)/(7(1 + 1)) \\
&= \frac{1}{14}
\end{aligned}
$$
