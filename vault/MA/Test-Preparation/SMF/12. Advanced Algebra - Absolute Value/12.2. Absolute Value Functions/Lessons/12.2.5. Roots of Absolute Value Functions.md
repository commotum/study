## Introduction

Recall that to find the **roots** of a function $f(x)$, we must solve the equation

$$
f(x) = 0
$$

For instance, let's calculate the roots of

$$
f(x) = \mid x-3 \mid -6
$$

To find the roots of $f(x)$, we need to solve the following absolute value equation:

$$
\mid x-3 \mid -6 = 0
$$

First, we isolate the absolute value on the left side of the equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mid x - 3 \mid &= 6
\end{aligned}
$$

Either the positive or negative of the expression inside the absolute value must be equal to the right-hand side of the equation. So, we set up the two corresponding equations and solve them independently:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x - 3 &= 6\begin{vmatrix}- (x - 3)|= 6 \\ x|= 9\end{vmatrix}- x + 3|= 6 \\
- x &= 3 \\
x &= -3
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the roots are $x=-3$ and $x=9$.

## Finding the Roots of an Absolute Value Function

**Example:** Calculate the roots of $f(x) = 4 \mid x-1 \mid -16$.

**Explanation**

To find the roots of the function, we must solve the equation

$$
f(x) = 0
$$

In this case, our equation is

$$
4 \mid x-1 \mid -16 = 0
$$

First, we isolate the absolute value on the left side of the equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
4 \mid x - 1 \mid &= 16 \\
\mid x - 1 \mid &= 4
\end{aligned}
$$

Either the positive or negative of the expression inside the absolute value must be equal to the right-hand side of the equation. So, we set up the two corresponding equations and solve them independently:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x - 1 &= 4\begin{vmatrix}- (x - 1)|= 4 \\ x|= 5\end{vmatrix}- x + 1|= 4 \\
- x &= 3 \\
x &= -3
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the roots are $x=-3$ and $x=5$.

## Finding the Roots of a Reflected Function

**Example:** Calculate the roots of $f(x) = -3 \mid x+2 \mid +9$.

**Explanation**

To find the roots of the function, we must solve the equation

$$
f(x) = 0
$$

In this case, our equation is

$$
-3 \mid x+2 \mid +9 = 0
$$

First, we isolate the absolute value on the left side of the equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
-3 \mid x + 2 \mid &= -9 \\
\mid x + 2 \mid &= 3
\end{aligned}
$$

Either the positive or negative of the expression inside the absolute value must be equal to the right-hand side of the equation. So, we set up the two corresponding equations and solve them independently:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x + 2 &= 3\begin{vmatrix}- (x + 2)|= 3 \\ x|= 1\end{vmatrix}- x - 2|= 3 \\
- x &= 5 \\
x &= -5
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the roots are $x=-5$ and $x=1$.

## Identifying Absolute Value Functions That Have Roots

**Example:** Which of the following functions has at least one root?

1. $f(x) =-\mid x-3 \mid$
2. $g(x) =-\mid x-3 \mid + 3$
3. $h(x) =-\mid x-3 \mid - 2$

**Explanation**

Let's start by sketching the graph of $y = f(x)$.

![](<../Source/12.2.5. Roots of Absolute Value Functions/Images/b6261d2c263a3fa3e2ad5da7dff51e7a.png>)

Let's now examine our three functions:

- The function $f(x)$ has one root at $x=3$.
- The function $g(x)$ has two roots. The graph $y=g(x)$ is obtained from $y = f(x)$ by shifting it vertically *up* by $3$ units. As a result, $y=g(x)$ lies partly above and partly below the $x$-axis.![](<../Source/12.2.5. Roots of Absolute Value Functions/Images/80ed3549d8cb87bae7293a6310edb768.png>)
- The function $h(x)$ has no roots. The graph $y=h(x)$ is obtained from $y = f(x)$ by shifting it vertically *down* by $2$ units. As a result, $y=h(x)$ lies entirely below the $x$-axis.![](<../Source/12.2.5. Roots of Absolute Value Functions/Images/683ee58c8365c373ae5b49b610d076c8.png>)

Therefore, the correct answer is "I and II only."
