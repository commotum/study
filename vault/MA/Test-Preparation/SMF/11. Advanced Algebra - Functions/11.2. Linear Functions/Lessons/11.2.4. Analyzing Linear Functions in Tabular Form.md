## Introduction

In a previous lesson, we learned how to write down a linear function using values in a table. In this lesson, we will focus on analyzing linear functions in tabular form by finding specific values, identifying points on the line, and locating intercepts directly from the table.

Let's see an example.

| $x$ | $f(x)$ |
| --- | ---: |
| $0$ | $9$ |
| $3$ | $3$ |
| $6$ | $-3$ |

Some values of the linear function $f$ are shown in the table above. If $x$ can be any real number, let's find the value of $f(8)$.

Since $f$ is a linear function, we know it must be of the form

$$
f(x) = mx + c
$$

where $m$ is the slope, and $c$ is the $y$-intercept.

First, we find the slope using the two given points

$$
(x_1, y_1)=(0, 9)
$$

and

$$
(x_2, y_2)=(3, 3){:}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
m &= \frac{y_{2} - y_{1}}{x_{2} - x_{1}} \\
&= \frac{3 - 9}{3 - 0} \\
&= \frac{-6}{3} \\
&=-2
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the slope is $-2$.
Then, we find the $y$-intercept using the point $(0, 9){:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
9 &= -2(0) + c \\
9 &= 0 + c \\
c &= 9
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the function is

$$
f(x) = -2x + 9
$$

Finally, we find $f(8){:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
f(8) &= -2(8) + 9 \\
&=-16 + 9 \\
&=-7
\end{aligned}
$$

In this example, we were given the $y$-intercept $(0,9)$, which meant we could deduce $c=9$ immediately. Let's take a look at an example where the $y$-intercept is NOT given.

## Evaluating a Linear Function Given in Tabular Form

**Example:** Some values of the linear function $f$ are shown in the table above. If $x$ can be any real number, what is the value of $f(12)$?

| $x$ | $f(x)$ |
| --- | ---: |
| $2$ | $8$ |
| $6$ | $-4$ |
| $10$ | $-16$ |

**Explanation**

Since $f$ is a linear function, we know it must be of the form

$$
f(x) = mx + c
$$

where $m$ is the slope, and $c$ is the $y$-intercept.

First, we find the slope using the two given points

$$
(x_1, y_1)=(2, 8)
$$

and

$$
(x_2, y_2)=(6, -4){:}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
m &= \frac{y_{2} - y_{1}}{x_{2} - x_{1}} \\
&= \frac{-4 - 8}{6 - 2} \\
&= \frac{-12}{4} \\
&=-3
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the slope is $-3$.
Then, we find the $y$-intercept using the point $(2, 8){:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
8 &= -3(2) + c \\
8 &= -6 + c \\
c &= 14
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the function is

$$
f(x) = -3x + 14
$$

Finally, we find $f(12){:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
f(12) &= -3(12) + 14 \\
&=-36 + 14 \\
&=-22
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding a Point on a Line Represented in Tabular Form

**Example:** Some values of the linear function $f$ are shown in the table above. In the $xy$-plane, the line $y=f(x)$ passes through the point where $x=\dfrac12$. Find the $y$-coordinate of this point.

| $x$ | $f(x)$ |
| --- | ---: |
| $-4$ | $-5$ |
| $2$ | $7$ |

**Explanation**

Since $f$ is a linear function, we know it must be of the form

$$
f(x) = mx + c
$$

where $m$ is the slope, and $c$ is the $y$-intercept.

First, we find the slope using the two given points

$$
(x_1, y_1)=(-4, -5)
$$

and

$$
(x_2, y_2)=(2, 7){:}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
m &= \frac{y_{2} - y_{1}}{x_{2} - x_{1}} \\
&= (7 - (-5))/(2 - (-4)) \\
&= \frac{12}{6} \\
&= 2
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the slope is $2$.
Next, we find the $y$-intercept using one point. For $x=2$, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
7 &= 2(2) + c \\
7 &= 4 + c \\
c &= 3
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the function is

$$
f(x) = 2x + 3
$$

Finally, we evaluate

$$
x = \dfrac{1}{2}
$$

to find the corresponding value of $y{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
y &= f(\frac{1}{2}) \\
&= 2(\frac{1}{2}) + 3 \\
&= 1 + 3 \\
&= 4
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding Intercepts

Before we move on, let's remind ourselves how to compute the intercepts of linear functions:

- To find the $y$-intercept of a linear relationship $y = f(x)$, we set $x=0$ and solve for $y$.
- To find the $x$-intercept of a linear relationship $y = f(x)$, we set $y=0$ and solve for $x$.

Let's see an example.

---

<a id="finding-intercepts-of-lines-given-in-tabular-form"></a>
## Finding Intercepts of Lines Given in Tabular Form

**Example:** The table above shows some values of $x$ and their corresponding values of $y$. There is a linear relationship between $x$ and $y$. What is the $x$-intercept of the line represented by this relationship in the $xy$-plane?

| $x$ | $y$ |
| --- | ---: |
| $2$ | $-6$ |
| $4$ | $-3$ |
| $10$ | $6$ |

**Explanation**

Since the relationship is linear, we know it must be of the form

$$
y=mx+c
$$

where $m$ is the slope, and $c$ is the $y$-intercept.

First, we find the slope $m$ using the points

$$
(x_1, y_1)=(2, -6)
$$

and

$$
(x_2, y_2)=(10, 6){:}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
m &= \frac{y_{2} - y_{1}}{x_{2} - x_{1}} \\
&= (6 - (-6))/(10 - 2) \\
&= \frac{12}{8} \\
&= \frac{3}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we use point-slope form with the point $(10, 6){:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
y - 6 &= m(x - 10) \\
y - 6 &= \frac{3}{2}(x - 10)
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we set $y = 0$ to find the value of the $x$-intercept:

$$
\begin{aligned}
0 - 6 &= \frac{3}{2}(x - 10) \\
- 6 &= \frac{3}{2}x - 15 \\
\frac{3}{2}x &= 9 \\
x &= 6
\end{aligned}
$$
