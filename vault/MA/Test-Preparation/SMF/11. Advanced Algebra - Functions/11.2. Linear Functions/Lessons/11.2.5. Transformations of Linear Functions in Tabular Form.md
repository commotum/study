## Introduction

In this lesson, we will learn how to transform linear functions given in tabular form.

| $x$ | $f(x)$ |
| --- | ---: |
| $-2$ | $10$ |
| $2$ | $2$ |
| $10$ | $-14$ |

For the linear function $f$, the table shows three values of $x$ and their corresponding values of $f(x)$. If the function $g$ is defined as

$$
g(x) = f(x) - 7
$$

let's use the given information to find $g$.

Since $f(x)$ is linear, it can be written as

$$
f(x) = mx + b
$$

First, we find the slope $m$ using the points

$$
(x_1, y_1)=(-2,10)
$$

and

$$
(x_2, y_2)=(2,2){:}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
m &= \frac{y_{2} - y_{1}}{x_{2} - x_{1}} \\
&= (2 - 10)/(2 - (-2)) \\
&= \frac{-8}{4} \\
&=-2
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we find the $y$-intercept $b$ using the point $(-2, 10){:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
f(x) &= -2 \cdot x + b \\
10 &= -2(-2) + b \\
b &= 6
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus, we have

$$
f(x) = -2x + 6
$$

from which it follows that

$$
\begin{aligned}
g(x) &= f(x) - 7 \\
&= (-2x + 6) - 7 \\
&=-2x - 1
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
g(x) = -2x - 1
$$

## Translating Linear Functions Given in Tabular Form

**Example:** The table above shows three $x$-values and their corresponding $y$-values that lie on the line $u$ in the $xy$-plane. Line $v$ is the result of translating line $u$ up $8$ units in the $xy$-plane. Which of the following equations represents the line $v$?

| $x$ | $y$ |
| --- | ---: |
| $-4$ | $11$ |
| $0$ | $3$ |
| $4$ | $-5$ |

**Explanation**

Since the relationship is linear, it can be written as

$$
y = mx + c
$$

First, we find the slope $m$ using the points

$$
(x_1, y_1)=(-4,11)
$$

and

$$
(x_2, y_2)=(0,3){:}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
m &= \frac{y_{2} - y_{1}}{x_{2} - x_{1}} \\
&= (3 - 11)/(0 - (-4)) \\
&= \frac{-8}{4} \\
&=-2
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we find the $y$-intercept $c$ using the point $(0, 3){:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
3 &= (-2) \cdot 0 + c \\
c &= 3
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus, the equation of the line $u$ is

$$
y = -2x + 3
$$

Since line $v$ is the result of translating line $u$ by $8$ units up, its equation is

$$
y = (-2x+3) + 8 = -2x+11
$$

## Identifying a Linear Function Given a Corresponding Nonlinear Function in Tabular Form

**Example:** The table above shows three $x$-values and their corresponding values of $g(x)$, where $g(x)=\dfrac{f(x)}{x+1}$ and $f$ is a linear function. What is the equation of the function $f(x)$?

| $x$ | $g(x)$ |
| --- | ---: |
| $2$ | $1$ |
| $4$ | $2$ |
| $8$ | $\dfrac{8}{3}$ |

**Explanation**

First, since

$$
g(x)=\dfrac{f(x)}{x+1}
$$

we can write $f$ in terms of $g$ by multiplying both sides by $(x+1){:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
g(x) &= (f(x))/(x + 1) \\
(x + 1) \cdot g(x) &= (x + 1) \cdot (f(x))/(x + 1) \\
(x + 1) \cdot g(x) &= (x + 1) \cdot (f(x))/(x + 1) \\
(x + 1)g(x) &= f(x)
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, we have

$$
f(x)=(x+1)g(x)
$$

Substituting in each given value of $x$, we find coordinates on the line $y=f(x){:}$

- For $x=2$, we have
$f(2)=(2+1)\cdot g(2)=(3)\cdot 1=3$.
So, the point $(2,3)$ is on the line.
- For $x=4$, we have
$f(4)=(4+1)\cdot g(4)=(5)\cdot 2=10$.
So, the point $(4,10)$ is on the line.
- For $x=8$, we have
$f(8)=(8+1)\cdot g(8)=(9)\cdot \dfrac{8}{3}=24$.
So, the point $(8,24)$ is on the line.

So, we now have a table of values for $y = f(x)$.

| $x$ | $f(x)$ |
| --- | ---: |
| $2$ | $3$ |
| $4$ | $10$ |
| $8$ | $24$ |

Since $f$ is a linear function, it can be written as

$$
f(x)=mx+c
$$

Now, we find the slope $m$ using the points

$$
(x_1, y_1)=(2,3)
$$

and

$$
(x_2, y_2)=(4,10){:}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
m &= \frac{y_{2} - y_{1}}{x_{2} - x_{1}} \\
&= \frac{10 - 3}{4 - 2} \\
&= \frac{7}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we find the $y$-intercept $c$ using the point $(2,3){:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
3 &= \frac{7}{2}(2) + c \\
3 &= 7 + c \\
c &= -4
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the function is

$$
f(x) = \dfrac{7}{2}x - 4
$$

## Finding Intercepts

Let's again remind ourselves how to compute the intercepts of linear functions:

- To find the $y$-intercept of a linear relationship $y = f(x)$, we set $x=0$ and solve for $y$.
- To find the $x$-intercept of a linear relationship $y = f(x)$, we set $y=0$ and solve for $x$.

We can apply the same ideas to transformed functions. We just need to remember to find the equation of the transformed function first. Let's see an example.

---

<a id="finding-intercepts-of-transformed-functions"></a>
## Finding Intercepts of Transformed Functions

**Example:** The table above shows three $x$-values and their corresponding $y$-values that lie on the line $k$ in the $xy$-plane. Line $l$ is the result of translating line $k$ down $2$ units in the $xy$-plane. What is the $x$-intercept of the line $l$?

| $x$ | $y$ |
| --- | ---: |
| $-2$ | $-4$ |
| $0$ | $-1$ |
| $4$ | $5$ |

**Explanation**

Since the relationship is linear, it can be written as

$$
y = mx+c
$$

First, we find the slope $m$ using the points

$$
(x_1, y_1)=(-2, -4)
$$

and

$$
(x_2, y_2)=(0, -1){:}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
m &= \frac{y_{2} - y_{1}}{x_{2} - x_{1}} \\
&= (-1 - (-4))/(0 - (-2)) \\
&= \frac{3}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we find the $y$-intercept $c$ using the point $(-2, -4){:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
-4 &= \frac{3}{2} \cdot (-2) + c \\
- 4 &= -3 + c \\
c &= -1
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus, the equation of line $k$ is

$$
y = \dfrac{3}{2}x - 1
$$

Since line $l$ is the result of translating line $k$ by $2$ units down, its equation is

$$
y = \dfrac{3}{2}x - 3
$$

Finally, to find the $x$-intercept of line $l$, we set $y = 0$ in the above equation and solve for $x{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
0 &= \frac{3}{2}x - 3 \\
\frac{3}{2}x &= 3 \\
x &= 2
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the value of $x$-intercept is $2$.
