## Introduction

Remember that to solve a rational equation, such as the one below, we can use cross-multiplication:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{2}{x} &= \frac{3}{5} \\
5 \cdot 2 &= 3 \cdot x \\
10 &= 3x \\
\frac{10}{3} &= x
\end{aligned}
$$

However, there is also another method that we can use, known as the **flip method,** which involves taking the reciprocal of both sides of an equation. So, when an equation involves two fractions, we can "flip" them as follows:

$$
\dfrac{a}{b} = \dfrac{c}{d}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \dfrac ba =\dfrac dc
$$

The above technique works whenever $a$ and $c$ are both different from zero.

The flip method is useful because it allows us to remove a variable from a denominator. For example, applying the flip method, we have

$$
\dfrac{\color{red}2}{\color{blue}x}=\dfrac{\color{blue}3}{\color{red}5} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \dfrac{\color{blue}x}{\color{red}2} = \dfrac{\color{red}5}{\color{blue}3}
$$

Now, we can solve the resulting equation using the multiplication principle:

$$
\begin{aligned}
2 \cdot \frac{x}{2} &= 2 \cdot \frac{5}{3} \\
2 \cdot \frac{x}{2} &= 2 \cdot \frac{5}{3} \\
x &= \frac{10}{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Solving Rational Equations with a Variable in One Denominator

**Example:** Solve the equation $\dfrac 23 = \dfrac 2x$.

**Explanation**

First, we note that $x=0$ cannot be a solution since this would make the denominator on the right-hand side zero.

We can flip an equation involving two fractions as follows:

$$
\dfrac{a}{b} = \dfrac{c}{d}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \dfrac ba =\dfrac dc
$$

The above technique works whenever $a$ and $c$ are both different from zero.

Therefore, we can flip the given equation as follows:

$$
\dfrac 23 = \dfrac 2x \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \dfrac 32 = \dfrac x2
$$

We can now solve this equation using the multiplication principle:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{3}{2} &= \frac{x}{2} \\
2 \cdot \frac{3}{2} &= 2 \cdot \frac{x}{2} \\
2 \cdot \frac{3}{2} &= 2 \cdot \frac{x}{2} \\
x &= 3
\end{aligned}
$$

## Solving Rational Equations with a Binomial in One Denominator

**Example:** Solve the equation $\dfrac 1{x+1} = 2$.

**Explanation**

First, we note that $x=-1$ cannot be a solution since this would make the denominator on the left-hand side zero.

We can flip an equation involving two fractions as follows:

$$
\dfrac{a}{b} = \dfrac{c}{d}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \dfrac ba =\dfrac dc
$$

The above technique works whenever $a$ and $c$ are both different from zero.

Therefore, we can flip the given equation as follows:

$$
\dfrac 1{x+1} = 2 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \dfrac {x+1}{1} = \dfrac 12
$$

The above equation simplifies to

$$
x+1 = \dfrac 12
$$

We can now solve this equation using the addition principle:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x + 1 &= \frac{1}{2} \\
x + 1 - 1 &= \frac{1}{2} - 1 \\
x &= -\frac{1}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Solving Rational Equations with a Binomial and a Monomial in Denominators

**Example:** Solve the equation $\dfrac 1{x-2} = \dfrac 3{5x}$.

**Explanation**

First, we note that

- $x=2$ cannot be a solution since this would make the denominator on the left-hand side zero, and
- $x=0$ cannot be a solution since this would make the denominator on the right-hand side zero.

We can flip an equation involving two fractions as follows:

$$
\dfrac{a}{b} = \dfrac{c}{d}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \dfrac ba =\dfrac dc
$$

The above technique works whenever $a$ and $c$ are both different from zero.

Therefore, we can flip the given equation as follows:

$$
\dfrac 1{x-2} = \dfrac 3{5x} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \dfrac {x-2}{1} = \dfrac{5x}{3}
$$

The above equation simplifies to

$$
x-2 = \dfrac{5x}{3}
$$

We can now solve this equation with the addition and multiplication principles:

$$
\begin{aligned}
3 \cdot (x - 2) &= 3 \cdot \frac{5x}{3} \\
3x - 6 &= 5x \\
3x - 5x &= 6 \\
- 2x &= 6 \\
x &= -3
\end{aligned}
$$

## Solving Rational Equations with Binomials in Both Denominators

**Example:** Solve the equation $\dfrac 1{2x-1} = \dfrac{1}{x+1}$.

**Explanation**

First, we note that

- $x=\dfrac 12$ cannot be a solution since this would make the denominator on the left-hand side zero, and
- $x=-1$ cannot be a solution since this would make the denominator on the right-hand side zero.

We can flip an equation involving two fractions as follows:

$$
\dfrac{a}{b} = \dfrac{c}{d}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \dfrac ba =\dfrac dc
$$

The above technique works whenever $a$ and $c$ are both different from zero.

Therefore, we can flip the given equation as follows:

$$
\dfrac 1{2x-1} = \dfrac{1}{x+1} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \dfrac{2x-1}{1} = \dfrac{x+1}1
$$

The above equation simplifies to

$$
2x-1 = x+1
$$

We can now solve this equation with the addition principle:

$$
\begin{aligned}
2x - 1 &= x + 1 \\
2x - x &= 1 + 1 \\
x &= 2
\end{aligned}
$$
