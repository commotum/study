## Introduction

In algebra, equations often involve fractions (rational expressions) with more than one variable. The key strategy is to eliminate the denominator and then carefully isolate the variable we want to solve for.

For example, suppose we are given the relation between three distinct positive numbers $p$, $q$, and $h{:}$

$$
p = -\dfrac{q}{7h}
$$

We want to express $q$ in terms of $p$ and $h$.

To eliminate the denominator, we multiply *both sides* of the equation by $7h$ to remove the fractions.

$$
\begin{aligned}
p &= -\frac{q}{7h} \\
p \cdot 7h &= -\frac{q}{7h} \cdot 7h \\
p \cdot 7h &= -\frac{q}{7h} \cdot 7h \\
7hp &= -q
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we multiply *both sides* by $-1$ to isolate $q$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
7hp \cdot (-1) &= -q \cdot (-1) \\
- 7hp &= q
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the final answer is
$q = -7hp$.

## Solving for a Variable in the Numerator of a Single-Fraction Equation

**Example:** The following equation relates the distinct positive numbers $m$, $n$, and $p{:}$
$m = \dfrac{n}{\,4+5p\,}$
Express $n$ in terms of $m$ and $p$.

**Explanation**

To eliminate the denominator, we multiply *both sides* of the equation by $(4+5p)$ to remove the fractions.

$$
\begin{aligned}
m &= \frac{n}{4 + 5p} \\
m \cdot (4 + 5p) &= \frac{n}{4 + 5p} \cdot (4 + 5p) \\
m \cdot (4 + 5p) &= \frac{n}{4 + 5p} \cdot (4 + 5p) \\
m(4 + 5p) &= n
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
n = m(4+5p)
$$

## Solving for a Variable in the Denominator of a Single-Fraction Equation

**Example:** The variables $x$ and $y$, such that $x \neq 11$ and $y \neq 8$, are related by the equation
$x-11 = \dfrac{55}{y-8}$.
Express $y$ in terms of $x$.

**Explanation**

First, we multiply both sides of the given equation by $(y-8)$ to remove the fractions:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x - 11 &= \frac{55}{y - 8} \\
(x - 11) \cdot (y - 8) &= \frac{55}{y - 8} \cdot (y - 8) \\
(x - 11) \cdot (y - 8) &= \frac{55}{y - 8} \cdot (y - 8) \\
(x - 11)(y - 8) &= 55
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we divide both sides by $(x-11)$ to isolate $(y-8){:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
((x - 11)(y - 8))/(x - 11) &= \frac{55}{x - 11} \\
y - 8 &= \frac{55}{x - 11}
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we add $8$ to both sides and simplify:

$$
\begin{aligned}
y - 8 + 8 &= \frac{55}{x - 11} + 8 \\
y &= \frac{55}{x - 11} + (8(x - 11))/(x - 11) \\
y &= (55 + 8(x - 11))/(x - 11) \\
y &= \frac{55 + 8x - 88}{x - 11} \\
y &= \frac{8x - 33}{x - 11}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
y = \dfrac{8x-33}{x-11}
$$

<a id="solving-using-cross-multiplication"></a>
## Solving Using Cross-Multiplication

When both sides of the equation are fractions, it is usually faster to apply *cross multiplication*. This avoids the extra step of multiplying through by each denominator separately.

Let's consider an example. Suppose the following equation relates the positive numbers $m$, $n$, and $p{:}$

$$
\dfrac{1}{4m} = \dfrac{17n}{6p}
$$

Let's express $p$ in terms of $m$ and $n$.

To eliminate the denominators, we first *cross-multiply*:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{1}{4m} &= \frac{17n}{6p} \\
1 \cdot (6p) &= 17n \cdot 4m \\
6p &= 68mn
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we divide both sides by $6$ to isolate $p{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{6p}{6} &= \frac{68mn}{6} \\
p &= \frac{68mn}{6} \\
p &= \frac{34mn}{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the correct answer is

$$
p = \dfrac{34mn}{3}
$$

<a id="solving-for-a-variable-using-cross-multiplication"></a>
## Solving for a Variable Using Cross-Multiplication

**Example:** The following equation relates the positive numbers $r$, $s$, and $t{:}$
$\dfrac{1}{6r} = \dfrac{7s}{t}$
Express $s$ in terms of $t$ and $r$.

**Explanation**

To eliminate the denominators, we use *cross multiplication*:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{1}{6r} &= \frac{7s}{t} \\
1 \cdot t &= 7s \cdot 6r \\
t &= 42rs
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we divide both sides by $42r$ to isolate $s{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{t}{42r} &= \frac{42rs}{42r} \\
\frac{t}{42r} &= s
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
s = \dfrac{t}{42r}
$$

## Solving for Variables Present in Both Numerator and Denominator

**Example:** The variables $r$ and $s$ are related by the equation
$r = \dfrac{3s + 16}{s}$.
Express $s$ in terms of $r$.

**Explanation**

To get rid of fractions, we multiply both sides of the equation by $s{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
r &= \frac{3s + 16}{s} \\
r \cdot s &= \frac{3s + 16}{s} \cdot s \\
r \cdot s &= \frac{3s + 16}{s} \cdot s \\
rs &= 3s + 16
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we move all terms with $s$ to one side:

$$
\begin{aligned}
rs &= 3s + 16 \\
rs - 3s &= 3s + 16 - 3s \\
rs - 3s &= 16
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we factor $s$ out:

$$
\begin{aligned}
s(r - 3) &= 16
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we solve for $s{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
s(r - 3) &= 16 \\
(s(r - 3))/(r - 3) &= \frac{16}{r - 3} \\
(s(r - 3))/(r - 3) &= \frac{16}{r - 3} \\
s &= \frac{16}{r - 3}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
s = \dfrac{16}{r - 3}
$$
