## Introduction

The square root of a squared quantity can be simplified using absolute value.

$$
\sqrt{x^2} = \mid x \mid
$$

However, if the domain of the variable is restricted, some expressions might allow further simplification.

For example, suppose we know that $a>0$. Let's use this information to fully simplify $\sqrt{36a^2}$.

We can start by simplifying the expression as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{36a^{2}} &=  \\
\sqrt{(6a)^{2}} &=  \\
\mid 6a \mid &=  \\
&= \begin{vmatrix}6 & \cdot & a\end{vmatrix} \\
&= 6 \mid a \mid
\end{aligned}
$$

We are told that $a>0$, and we know that the absolute value of any positive number is just itself, so we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mid a &= a
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, our final answer is

$$
6 \mid a \mid = 6a
$$

## Simplifying an Expression Given That Some Variables Are Non-Negative

**Example:** Simplify $\sqrt{\dfrac{r^2}{s^2}}$ given that $s>0$.

**Explanation**

The square root of a squared quantity can be simplified using absolute value:

$$
\sqrt{x^2} = \mid x \mid
$$

In our case, we can simplify the given expression as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned} \sqrt{\dfrac{r^2}{s^2}} \\[5pt] &= \sqrt{\dfrac{r}{s} \cdot \dfrac{r}{s}} \\[5pt] &= \sqrt{\left(\dfrac{r}{s}\right)^2} \\[5pt] &= \left\begin{vmatrix}\dfrac{r}{s}\right & \\[5pt] &= \dfrac{\begin{vmatrix}r &}{\end{vmatrix}s\end{vmatrix}} & \end{aligned}
$$

We are told that $s \gt 0$, and we know that the absolute value of any positive number is just itself, so we have

$$
\mid s \mid = s
$$

Therefore, our final answer is

$$
\dfrac{\begin{vmatrix}r &}{\begin{vmatrix}s &} = \dfrac{\end{vmatrix}r\end{vmatrix}}{s}
$$

## The Square Root of a Perfect Square With Negative Variables

Let's see what changes if we consider square roots of perfect squares when some variables are negative.

For example, suppose that $t \lt 0$. Let's use this to simplify the expression $\sqrt{t^2}$.

Simplifying this expression in the usual way, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned} \sqrt{t^2} \\[5pt] &= \sqrt{(t)^2} \\[5pt] &= \mid t \mid.& \end{aligned}
$$

We're given that $t \lt 0$. To take the absolute value of a negative number, we need to flip the sign. This gives

$$
\mid t \mid = -t
$$

Therefore, our final answer is

$$
{\color{blue}\sqrt{t^2}} = {\color{red}-t}
$$

You can convince yourself that this is correct by plugging a few values of $t$ into the above equation. Provided that $t$ is negative, this expression will always give a true statement.

For example, suppose that $t = -2$. Substituting into the left-hand side of the equation, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{t^{2}} &=  \\
\sqrt{(-2)^{2}} &=  \\
\sqrt{4} &=  \\
&= 2 \mid
\end{aligned}
$$

Substituting $t=-2$ into the right-hand side of the equation, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
-t &=  \\
- (-2) &=  \\
&= 2 \mid
\end{aligned}
$$

Since both sides give the same result, the statement is true.

## Simplifying an Expression Given That Some Variables Are Negative

**Example:** Simplify $\sqrt{y^4 z^{6}}$ given that $z \lt 0$.

**Explanation**

The square root of a squared quantity can be simplified using absolute value.

$$
\sqrt{x^2} = \mid x \mid
$$

In our case, we can simplify the given expression as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned} \sqrt{y^4 z^{6}} \\[5pt] &= \sqrt{y^2 z^3 \cdot y^2 z^3} \\[5pt] &= \sqrt{\left(y^2 z^3\right)^2} \\[5pt] &= \begin{vmatrix}y^2 z^3 & \\[5pt] &= & y^2 & \cdot & z^3 & \\[5pt] &= & y^2 & \cdot & z\end{vmatrix}^3 \end{aligned}
$$

A squared number is always positive or zero, and the absolute value of any positive (or zero) number is itself, so we have

$$
\mid y^2 \mid = y^2
$$

On the other hand, we are told that $z \lt 0$, and to take the absolute value of any negative number we need to flip the sign, so we have

$$
\mid z \mid = -z
$$

Therefore, our final answer is

$$
\begin{vmatrix}y^{2} & \cdot & z\end{vmatrix}^{3} = y^{2} \cdot (-z)^{3} =-y^{2}z^{3}
$$
