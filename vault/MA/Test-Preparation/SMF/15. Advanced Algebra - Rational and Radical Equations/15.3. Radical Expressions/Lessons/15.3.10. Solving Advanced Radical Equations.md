## Introduction

When a radical equation contains two square roots, like

$$
\sqrt{2-x} - \sqrt{x} = 0
$$

we manipulate the equation so that we have one square root on each side:

$$
\sqrt{2-x} = \sqrt{x}
$$

Then, we raise both sides to the power of $2$, and we solve the equation as usual:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(\sqrt{2 - x})^{2} &= (\sqrt{x})^{2} \\
2 - x &= x \\
2x &= 2 \\
x &= 1
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we check whether the obtained value $x=1$ is actually a solution of the original equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{2 - (1)} &= ^(?)\sqrt{(1)} \\
\sqrt{1} &= ^(?)\sqrt{1} \\
1 &= 1✓
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the only solution of

$$
\sqrt{2-x} - \sqrt{x} = 0
$$

is $x=1$.

## Solving Equations With Radical Expressions on Both Sides

**Example:** Solve $\sqrt{2x-5} = \sqrt{4 - x}$.

**Explanation**

To solve the equation

$$
\sqrt{2x-5} = \sqrt{4 - x}
$$

we raise both sides to the power of $2$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{2x - 5} &= \sqrt{4 - x} \\
(\sqrt{2x - 5})^{2} &= (\sqrt{4 - x})^{2} \\
2x - 5 &= 4 - x \\
3x &= 9 \\
x &= 3
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we check that the obtained value $x=3$ is actually a solution of the original equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{2(3) - 5} &= ^(?)\sqrt{4 - 3} \\
\sqrt{1} &= ^(?)\sqrt{1} \\
1 &= 1✓
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the only solution of

$$
\sqrt{2x-5} = \sqrt{4 - x}
$$

is $x=3$.

## Solving Equations With Radical Expressions on Both Sides and an Extra Term

**Example:** Solve $\sqrt {x - 2} = \sqrt {3x} - 2$.

**Explanation**

Even though we have an extra term on the right-hand side, we proceed in the same way and raise both sides of the equation to the power of $2$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{x - 2} &= \sqrt{3x} - 2 \\
(\sqrt{x - 2})^{2} &= (\sqrt{3x} - 2)^{2} \\
x - 2 &= (\sqrt{3x})^{2} - 2\sqrt{3x} \cdot 2 + 2^{2} \\
x - 2 &= 3x - 4\sqrt{3x} + 4 \\
4\sqrt{3x} &= 3x - x + 4 + 2 \\
4\sqrt{3x} &= 2x + 6 \\
2\sqrt{3x} &= x + 3
\end{aligned}
$$

Now we have an equation with one square root on one side and a linear expression on the other side. To solve this type of equation, we raise both sides to the power of $2$ one more time:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(2\sqrt{3x})^{2} &= (x + 3)^{2} \\
2^{2} \cdot 3x &= (x)^{2} + 2(x) \cdot 3 + 3^{2} \\
12x &= x^{2} + 6x + 9 \\
0 &= x^{2} + 6x + 9 - 12x \\
0 &= x^{2} - 6x + 9 \\
0 &= (x - 3)^{2} \\
x &= 3
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we check that the obtained value $x=3$ is actually a solution to the original equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{3 - 2} &= ^(?)\sqrt{3(3)} - 2 \\
\sqrt{1} &= ^(?)3 - 2 \\
1 &= 1✓
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the equation

$$
\sqrt {x - 2} = \sqrt {3x} - 2
$$

has a single solution $x=3$.

## Solving Radical Equations With Radical Expressions of Binomials on Both Sides and an Extra Term

**Example:** Solve for $x$ where $\sqrt {2x +5} -\sqrt {x+6} = 1$.

**Explanation**

First, we move the radicals to separate sides of the equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{2x + 5} - \sqrt{x + 6} &= 1 \\
\sqrt{2x + 5} &= \sqrt{x + 6} + 1
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we raise both sides to the power of $2$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{2x + 5} &= \sqrt{x + 6} + 1 \\
(\sqrt{2x + 5})^{2} &= (\sqrt{x + 6} + 1)^{2} \\
2x + 5 &= (\sqrt{x + 6})^{2} + 2\sqrt{x + 6} + 1^{2} \\
2x + 5 &= x + 7 + 2\sqrt{x + 6} \\
2\sqrt{x + 6} &= 2x + 5 - x - 7 \\
2\sqrt{x + 6} &= x - 2
\end{aligned}
$$

Now we have an equation with one square root on one side and a linear expression on the other side. To solve this type of equation, we raise both sides to the power of $2$ one more time:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(2\sqrt{x + 6})^{2} &= (x - 2)^{2} \\
4(x + 6) &= x^{2} - 4x + 4 \\
4x + 24 &= x^{2} - 4x + 4 \\
x^{2} - 8x - 20 &= 0 \\
x^{2} - 10x + 2x - 20 &= 0 \\
(x - 10)(x + 2) &= 0 \\
&= x = 10,-2
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we check the obtained values by substituting them into the original equation.

- Checking $x=10$, we get
$\sqrt{2(10) + 5}|= ^(?)\sqrt{(10) + 6} + 1; \sqrt{25}|= ^(?)\sqrt{16} + 1; 5|= ^(?)4 + 1; 5|= 5.✓$
So, the value $x=10$ is indeed a solution of the original equation
- Checking $x=-2$, we get
$\sqrt{2(-2) + 5}|= ^(?)\sqrt{(-2) + 6} + 1; \sqrt{1} = 1 \mid \ne 2 + 1 = 3.\cdot$
The value $x=-2$ does *not* satisfy the original equation, so we discard it.

Therefore, the equation

$$
\sqrt {2x +5} -\sqrt {x+6} = 1
$$

has a single solution $x=10$.
