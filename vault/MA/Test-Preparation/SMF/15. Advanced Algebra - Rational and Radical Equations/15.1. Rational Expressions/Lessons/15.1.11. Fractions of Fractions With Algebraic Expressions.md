## Introduction

In this lesson, we'll learn how to simplify fractions of fractions with algebraic expressions. The core idea is much like simplifying numerical fractions.

For example, let's simplify the following expression:

$$
\dfrac{\left(\dfrac{30}{x + 4}\right)}{15x + 60}
$$

First, we write our fraction as a division:

$$
\dfrac{30}{x + 4} \div (15x + 60)
$$

Now, we write the divisor as a fraction over $1{:}$

$$
\dfrac{30}{x + 4} \div \dfrac{15x + 60}{1}
$$

Next, recall that division is equivalent to multiplication by the reciprocal:

$$
\dfrac{30}{x + 4} \cdot \dfrac{1}{15x + 60}
$$

Notice that the denominator of the second fraction can be written as

$$
15x + 60 = 15(x + 4)
$$

With that in mind, we multiply the rational expressions:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{30}{x + 4} \cdot \frac{1}{15x + 60} &= \frac{30}{x + 4} \cdot (1)/(15(x + 4)) \\
&= (30 \cdot 1)/((x + 4) \cdot 15(x + 4)) \\
&= (30)/(15(x + 4)^{2})
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we simplify by canceling out common factors in the numerator and denominator:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(30)/(15(x + 4)^{2}) &= (2 \cdot 15)/(15(x + 4)^{2}) \\
&= (2)/((x + 4)^{2})
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, we conclude that

$$
\dfrac{\left(\dfrac{30}{x + 4}\right)}{15x + 60} = \dfrac{2}{(x + 4)^2}
$$

valid for all

$$
x\neq -4
$$

<a id="simplifying-rational-expressions"></a>
## Simplifying Rational Expressions

**Example:** Simplify $\dfrac{\left(\dfrac{64}{x + 3}\right)}{\left(\dfrac{80}{x - 10}\right)}$, where $x \neq -3$ and $x \neq 10$.

**Explanation**

First, we write the fraction using division:

$$
\dfrac{\left(\dfrac{64}{x + 3}\right)}{\left(\dfrac{80}{x - 10}\right)} = \dfrac{64}{x + 3} \div \dfrac{80}{x - 10}
$$

Next, recall that division is equivalent to multiplication by the reciprocal:

$$
\dfrac{64}{x + 3} \div \dfrac{80}{x - 10} = \dfrac{64}{x + 3} \cdot \dfrac{x - 10}{80}
$$

Now we multiply the rational expressions:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{64}{x + 3} \cdot \frac{x - 10}{80} &= (64 \cdot (x - 10))/((x + 3) \cdot 80) \\
&= (64(x - 10))/(80(x + 3))
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we simplify:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(64(x - 10))/(80(x + 3)) &= (16 \cdot 4(x - 10))/(16 \cdot 5(x + 3)) \\
&= (4(x - 10))/(5(x + 3))
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="further-simplifying-rational-expressions"></a>
## Further Simplifying Rational Expressions

**Example:** Simplify $\dfrac{\left(\dfrac{4x - 8}{9}\right)}{\left(\dfrac{x - 2}{7}\right)}$, where $x \neq 2$.

**Explanation**

First, we write the fraction using division:

$$
\dfrac{\left(\dfrac{4x - 8}{9}\right)}{\left(\dfrac{x - 2}{7}\right)} = \dfrac{4x - 8}{9} \div \dfrac{x - 2}{7}
$$

Next, recall that dividing by a fraction is equivalent to multiplying by its reciprocal:

$$
\dfrac{4x - 8}{9} \div \dfrac{x - 2}{7} = \dfrac{4x - 8}{9} \cdot \dfrac{7}{x - 2}
$$

Notice that

$$
4x - 8 = 4(x - 2)
$$

With that in mind, we multiply the rational expressions:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{4x - 8}{9} \cdot \frac{7}{x - 2} &= (4(x - 2))/(9) \cdot \frac{7}{x - 2} \\
&= (4(x - 2) \cdot 7)/(9(x - 2)) \\
&= (28(x - 2))/(9(x - 2))
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we simplify:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(28(x - 2))/(9(x - 2)) &= (28(x - 2))/(9(x - 2)) \\
&= \frac{28}{9}
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="the-reciprocal-of-a-sum-of-rational-expressions"></a>
## The Reciprocal of a Sum of Rational Expressions

Now, suppose we wish to simplify the following expression:

$$
\dfrac{1}{\dfrac{6}{x - 7} + x + 5}
$$

To simplify this expression using the techniques we've discussed so far, we need to express the numerator as a rational expression.

First, we consider the denominator only:

$$
\dfrac{6}{x - 7} + x + 5
$$

We can express the polynomial part as a rational expression with a denominator of $1{:}$

$$
\dfrac{6}{x - 7} + \dfrac{x + 5}{1}
$$

Now, we form a common denominator by multiplying the numerator and denominator of the second term by $(x-7){:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{6}{x - 7} + \frac{x + 5}{1} &= \frac{6}{x - 7} + \frac{x - 7}{x - 7} \cdot \frac{x + 5}{1} \\
&= \frac{6}{x - 7} + ((x - 7)(x + 5))/(x - 7)
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we add our expressions as usual.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{6}{x - 7} + ((x - 7)(x + 5))/(x - 7) &= (6 + (x - 7)(x + 5))/(x - 7) \\
&= \frac{6 + x^{2} - 2x - 35}{x - 7} \\
&= \frac{x^{2} - 2x - 29}{x - 7}
\end{aligned}
$$

Ntext, we rewrite our original expression as a fraction of fractions:

$$
\dfrac{1}{\dfrac{6}{x - 7} + x + 5} = \dfrac{1}{\left(\dfrac{x^2 - 2x - 29}{x - 7}\right)}
$$

Writing the fraction as a division, we get

$$
\dfrac{1}{\left(\dfrac{x^2 - 2x - 29}{x - 7}\right)} = 1 \div \dfrac{x^2 - 2x - 29}{x - 7}
$$

Finally, recall that division is equivalent to multiplication by the reciprocal:

$$
\begin{aligned}
1 \div \frac{x^{2} - 2x - 29}{x - 7} &= 1 \cdot \frac{x - 7}{x^{2} - 2x - 29} \\
&= \frac{x - 7}{x^{2} - 2x - 29}
\end{aligned}
$$

Let's consider some more examples.

## Finding the Reciprocal of a Sum of Rational Expressions

**Example:** Simplify
$\dfrac{1}{\dfrac{7}{x - 6} - \dfrac{4}{x + 1}}$.

**Explanation**

We begin by rewriting the denominator as a single fraction:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{7}{x - 6} - \frac{4}{x + 1} &= \frac{7}{x - 6} \cdot \frac{x + 1}{x + 1} - \frac{4}{x + 1} \cdot \frac{x - 6}{x - 6} \\
&= (7(x + 1))/((x - 6)(x + 1)) - (4(x - 6))/((x - 6)(x + 1)) \\
&= (7(x + 1) - 4(x - 6))/((x - 6)(x + 1)) \\
&= (7x + 7 - 4x + 24)/((x - 6)(x + 1)) \\
&= (3x + 31)/((x - 6)(x + 1))
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we can rewrite our expression as follows:

$$
\dfrac{1}{\dfrac{7}{x - 6} - \dfrac{4}{x + 1}} = \dfrac{1}{\left(\dfrac{3x + 31}{(x - 6)(x + 1)}\right)}
$$

Next, we write the fraction using division:

$$
\dfrac{1}{\left(\dfrac{3x + 31}{(x - 6)(x + 1)}\right)} = 1 \div \dfrac{3x + 31}{(x - 6)(x + 1)}
$$

Finally, recall that division is equivalent to multiplication by the reciprocal:

$$
\begin{aligned}
1 \div (3x + 31)/((x - 6)(x + 1)) &= 1 \cdot ((x - 6)(x + 1))/(3x + 31) \\
&= ((x - 6)(x + 1))/(3x + 31)
\end{aligned}
$$
