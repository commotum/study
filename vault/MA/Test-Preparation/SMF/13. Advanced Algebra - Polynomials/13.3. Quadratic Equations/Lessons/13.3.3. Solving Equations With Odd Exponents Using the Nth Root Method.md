## Introduction

How do we solve an equation with a variable raised to an odd exponent, such as

$$
x^3 = 125
$$

We can solve this equation by taking the $3$rd root of each side and solving for $x$.

Since $n=3$ is odd, there will be only one real solution:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^{3} &= 125 \\
\sqrt[3]{x^{3}} &= \sqrt[3]{125} \\
x &= \sqrt[3]{5^{3}} \\
x &= 5
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the solution is $x=5$.

In general, we have the following rule of thumb:

> *When $n$ is odd, and we take the $n$th root of both sides of $x^n = a$, we get only one solution:* $x = \sqrt[n]{a}$

## Solving Equations With Odd Exponents

**Example:** Find the real solution to the equation $96x^5=3$.

**Explanation**

We can solve this equation by isolating the $x^5$ term and then taking the $5$th root of each side.

First, let's isolate the $x^5$ term on the left-hand side:

$$
\begin{aligned}
96x^{5} &= 3 \\
x^{5} &= \frac{1}{32}
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we take the $5$th root of each side and solve for $x$. Since $n=5$ is odd, there is only one real solution to this equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^{5} &= \frac{1}{32} \\
\sqrt[5]{x^{5}} &= \sqrt[5]{\frac{1}{32}} \\
x &= \sqrt[5]{(\frac{1}{2})^{5}} \\
x &= \frac{1}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the solution is

$$
x=\dfrac12
$$

## Solving Equations Containing an Expression Raised to an Odd Power

**Example:** Find the real solution to the equation $5(x-6)^7 = 640$.

**Explanation**

We can solve this equation by isolating the $(x-6)^7$ term and then taking the $7$th root of each side.

First, let's isolate the $(x-6)^7$ term on the left-hand side:

$$
\begin{aligned}
5(x - 6)^{7} &= 640 \\
(x - 6)^{7} &= 128
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we take the $7$th root of each side and solve for $x$. Since $n=7$ is odd, there is only one real solution to this equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x - 6)^{7} &= 128 \\
\sqrt[7]{(x - 6)^{7}} &= \sqrt[7]{128} \\
x - 6 &= \sqrt[7]{2^{7}} \\
x - 6 &= 2 \\
x &= 8
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the solution is $x=8$.
