## Introduction

In this topic, we'll learn how to determine which values of a polynomial $p(x)$ are possible over a specified $x$-interval without needing to compute exact outputs or plot the full graph.

For example, in the $xy$-plane, the graph of the equation

$$
y=2(x+1)(x-1)(x-3)
$$

contains the point $(a,b)$, where

$$
-1 \leq a \leq 1
$$

What can we say about the possible values of $b$ on this interval?

First, we sketch the graph of the cubic. From the equation, we conclude the following:

- The graph has $x$-intercepts at $x=-1,1,3$.
- Since the leading coefficient is positive, we have:
$\begin{bmatrix}y → ∞ & as & x → ∞ \\ y → - ∞ & as & x → - ∞\end{bmatrix}$
- The $y$-intercept is
$y|= 2(0 + 1)(0 - 1)(0 - 3); = 2 \cdot (1) \cdot (-1) \cdot (-3); = 6$.

The corresponding graph (not drawn to scale) is shown below.

![](<../Source/13.9.7. Identifying Valid Outputs of Factored Polynomials/Images/a97eff28f0f613f15a9a722b0a7a29b0.png>)

We are interested in the points $(a,b)$ whose $x$-coordinate $a$ satisfies

$$
-1 \leq a \leq 1
$$

This interval is highlighted on the graph.

Notice that on this interval, the graph of the cubic lies on or above the $x$-axis (it meets the axis at $x=-1$ and $x=1$ and is positive in between). So, we must have

$$
b \ge 0
$$

Therefore, $b$ can't be negative.

## Identifying Valid Outputs for Cubic Curves With Three Distinct Real Roots

**Example:** $y=\dfrac{1}{8}(2-x)(x+1)(x+5)$
In the $xy$-plane, the graph of the equation above contains the point $(a,b)$. If $-5 \leq a \leq -1$, which of the following is **NOT** a possible value of $b$?

1. $-3$
2. $0$
3. $1$

**Explanation**

First, notice that we can take out a factor of $-1$ from the factor $(2-x)$, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
y &= \frac{1}{8}(2 - x)(x + 1)(x + 5) \\
&=-\frac{1}{8}(x - 2)(x + 1)(x + 5)
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we sketch the graph of the cubic. From the equation, we conclude the following:

- The graph has $x$-intercepts at $x=-5,-1,2$.
- $y \to -\infty$ as $x \to \infty$ and $y \to \infty$ as $x \to -\infty$.
- The $y$-intercept is
$y|=-\frac{1}{8}(0 - 2)(0 + 1)(0 + 5); =-\frac{1}{8} \cdot (-2) \cdot (1) \cdot (5); = \frac{5}{4}$.
The corresponding graph (not drawn to scale) is shown below.

![](<../Source/13.9.7. Identifying Valid Outputs of Factored Polynomials/Images/ebbe7a76223aff86349b56bf72a7b30e.png>)

We are interested in the points $(a,b)$ whose $x$-coordinate $a$ satisfies

$$
-5 \leq a \leq -1
$$

The corresponding interval on the $x$-axis is highlighted on the graph. Notice that in this interval, the graph of the cubic lies below the $x$-axis. So, we must have

$$
b \leq 0
$$

Among the given options, only option III does **NOT** satisfy this condition.

## Identifying Valid Outputs for Cubic Curves With a Double Root

**Example:** $y= -5(x+1)(x-4)^2$
In the $xy$-plane, the graph of the equation above contains the point $(a,b)$. If $-1 \leq a \leq 4$, which of the following is **NOT** a possible value of $b$?

1. $-1$
2. $0$
3. $1$

**Explanation**

First, we sketch the graph of the cubic. From the equation, we conclude the following:

- The graph has $x$-intercepts at $x=-1,4$, where $x=4$ is a "double root."
- $y \to -\infty$ as $x \to \infty$ and $y \to \infty$ as $x \to -\infty$.
- The $y$-intercept is
$y|=-5(0 + 1)(0 - 4)^{2}; =-5 \cdot (1) \cdot (-4)^{2}; =-80$.

The corresponding graph (not drawn to scale) is shown below.

![](<../Source/13.9.7. Identifying Valid Outputs of Factored Polynomials/Images/274c3bf6d6871a5d5e566ce90deefa88.png>)

We are interested in the points $(a,b)$ whose $x$-coordinate $a$ satisfies

$$
-1 \leq a \leq 4
$$

The corresponding interval on the $x$-axis is highlighted on the graph. Notice that in this interval, the graph of the cubic lies below the $x$-axis. So, we must have

$$
b \leq 0
$$

Among the given options, only $x=1$ (option III) does **NOT** satisfy this condition.

## Identifying Valid Outputs for Cubic Curves With One Real Root

**Example:** $y=(x-2)(x^2+2x+5)$
In the $xy$-plane, the graph of the equation above contains the point $(a,b)$. If $0 \leq a \leq 2$, which of the following is **NOT** a possible value of $b$?

1. $-3$
2. $0$
3. $1$

**Explanation**

First, notice that the discriminant of the factor $x^2+2x+5$ is

$$
\mathcal D = 2^2-4(1)(5) = -16 < 0
$$

so our cubic cannot be factored any further over the real numbers.

Now, we sketch the graph of the cubic. From the equation, we conclude the following:

- The graph has an $x$-intercept at $x=2$.
- $y \to \infty$ as $x \to \infty$ and $y \to -\infty$ as $x \to -\infty$.
- The $y$-intercept is
$y|= (0 - 2)(0^{2} + 2(0) + 5); = (-2) \cdot (5); =-10$.

The corresponding partial graph is shown below.

![](<../Source/13.9.7. Identifying Valid Outputs of Factored Polynomials/Images/8b3d48afb3c2881b99ee8581cd142196.png>)

We are interested in the points $(a,b)$ whose $x$-coordinate $a$ satisfies

$$
0 \leq a \leq 2
$$

The corresponding interval on the $x$-axis is highlighted on the graph.
Notice that in this interval, the graph of the cubic lies below the $x$-axis. So, we must have

$$
b \leq 0
$$

Among the given options, only option III does **NOT** satisfy this condition.
