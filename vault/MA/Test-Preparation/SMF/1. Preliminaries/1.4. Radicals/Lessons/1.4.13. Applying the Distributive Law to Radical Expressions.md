## Introduction

In a previous lesson, we saw how to apply the distributive law to exponential expressions containing one or more variables. In this lesson, we'll see how the same techniques can be used for expanding radical expressions.

For example, suppose we wish to expand the parentheses in the following expression.

$$
\sqrt{y}\left(\sqrt{y} + \sqrt{yz}\right)
$$

In this case, we can rewrite the radicals in our expression using fractional exponents and then apply the distributive law and the laws of exponents.

First, we write radicals as fractional exponents:

$$
y^{1/2}(y^{1/2} + (yz)^{1/2})
$$

Now, we can use the power of product rule to distribute the second exponent inside the parentheses, as follows:

$$
y^{1/2}(y^{1/2} + y^{1/2}z^{1/2})
$$

Next, we apply the distributive law. This gives

$$
y^{1/2}\cdot y^{1/2} + y^{1/2}\cdot y^{1/2}z^{1/2}
$$

Then, we apply the product rule for exponents to combine the powers of $y$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
y^{1/2} \cdot 3y^{1/2} + y^{1/2} \cdot y^{1/2}z^{1/2} &= (y^{1/2} \cdot y^{1/2}) + (y^{1/2} \cdot y^{1/2})z^{1/2} \\
&= y^{1} + y^{1}z^{1/2} \\
&= y + yz^{1/2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Since the original expression contained radicals, we write our final expression using radicals, too. Therefore, our final answer is

$$
y + yz^{1/2} = y + y\sqrt{z}
$$

We can apply similar techniques to expressions containing fractions. Let's see an example.

## Using the Distributive Law With Square Root Expressions

**Example:** Simplify $\left(\sqrt{x^5y^6} - \sqrt{y^7}\right)\sqrt{x^3}$.

**Explanation**

First, we write radicals as fractional exponents:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(\sqrt{x^{5}y^{6}} - \sqrt{y^{7}})\sqrt{x^{3}} &= ((x^{5}y^{6})^{1/2} - (y^{7})^{1/2})(x^{3})^{1/2} \\
&= (x^{5/2}y^{3} - y^{7/2})x^{3/2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we use the distributive law, as shown below:

$$
\left(x^{5/2} y^3 - y^{7/2} \right)x^{3/2} = x^{5/2} y^3 \cdot x^{3/2} - y^{7/2} \cdot x^{3/2}
$$

Next, we rewrite the terms by combining the rules of exponents with algebraic expressions:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^{5/2}y^{3} \cdot x^{3/2} - y^{7/2} \cdot x^{3/2} &= (x^{5/2} \cdot x^{3/2})y^{3} - x^{3/2}y^{7/2} \\
&= x^{5/2 + 3/2}y^{3} - x^{3/2}y^{7/2} \\
&= x^{8/2}y^{3} - x^{3/2}y^{7/2} \\
&= x^{4}y^{3} - x^{3/2}y^{7/2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we write fractional exponents as radicals:

$$
x^4 y^3 - x^{3/2} y^{7/2} = x^4 y^3 - \sqrt{x^3 y^7}
$$

## Simplifying Fractional Expressions Containing Square Roots

**Example:** Simplify $\dfrac{\sqrt{x^6}+\sqrt{x^3y^2}}{\sqrt{x^6}}$.

**Explanation**

First, we write radicals as fractional exponents:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\sqrt{x^{6}} + \sqrt{x^{3}y^{2}}}{\sqrt{x^{6}}} &= (x^{6/2} + (x^{3}y^{2})^{1/2})/(x^{6/2}) \\
&= \frac{x^{3} + x^{3 \cdot 1/2}y^{2 \cdot 1/2}}{x^{3}} \\
&= \frac{x^{3} + x^{3/2}y^{1}}{x^{3}}
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we rewrite the denominator using negative exponents:

$$
\dfrac{x^3+x^{3/2}y}{x^3}=\left(x^3+x^{3/2}y\right)x^{-3}
$$

Now, we use the distributive law, as shown below:

$$
\left(x^3+x^{3/2}y\right)x^{-3}=x^3\cdot x^{-3}+x^{3/2}y\cdot x^{-3}
$$

Next, we rewrite the terms by combining the rules of exponents with algebraic expressions:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^{3} \cdot x^{-3} + x^{3/2}y \cdot x^{-3} &= (x^{3} \cdot x^{-3}) + (x^{3/2} \cdot x^{-3})y \\
&= x^{3 - 3} + x^{3/2 - 3}y \\
&= x^{0} + x^{-3/2}y \\
&= 1 + \frac{y}{x^{3/2}}
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we write fractional exponents as radicals:

$$
\begin{aligned}
1 + \frac{y}{x^{3/2}} &= 1 + \frac{y}{x^{3/2}} \\
&= 1 + \frac{y}{\sqrt{x^{3}}}
\end{aligned}
$$
