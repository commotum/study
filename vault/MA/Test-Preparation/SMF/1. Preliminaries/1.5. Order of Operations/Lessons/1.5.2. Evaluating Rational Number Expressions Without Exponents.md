## Introduction

As we already know, to evaluate numerical expressions with a mix of different operations, we must follow the order of operations known as PEMDAS.

The same rules apply to numerical expressions with negative numbers. For example, let's compute the value of

$$
\left(-5 + 7 \right) \div (-2) \times 6
$$

First, we perform the operation inside the parentheses:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(-5 + 7) \div (-2) \cdot 6 &=  \\
&= 2 \div (-2) \cdot 6
\end{aligned}
$$

Now recall that if both multiplication and division appear in an expression, we complete each operation from left to right (similarly for addition and subtraction).
So, we first divide:

$$
\begin{aligned}
2 \div (-2) \cdot 6 &=  \\
&= - 1 \cdot 6
\end{aligned}
$$

Then finally, we multiply:

$$
\begin{aligned}
-1 \cdot 6 &= -6
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="evaluating-expressions-with-addition-and-subtraction"></a>
## Evaluating Expressions With Addition and Subtraction

**Example:** Evaluate $-7 + 3.1 - 5$.

**Explanation**

To evaluate this expression, we use PEMDAS.

We add and subtract, from left to right:

$$
\begin{aligned}
-7 + 3.1 - 5 &=  \\
(-7 + 3.1) - 5 &=  \\
- 3.9 - 5 &=  \\
&= - 8.9
\end{aligned}
$$

## Evaluating Expressions With Multiplication and Division

**Example:** What is the value of $2 \div 6 \times (-5)$?

**Explanation**

To evaluate this expression, we use PEMDAS.

We divide and multiply, from left to right:

$$
\begin{aligned}
2 \div 6 \cdot (-5) &=  \\
(2 \div 6) \cdot (-5) &=  \\
\frac{2}{6} \cdot (-5) &=  \\
\frac{1}{3} \cdot (-5) &=  \\
\frac{1}{3} \cdot ((-5))/(1) &=  \\
(1 \cdot (-5))/(3 \cdot 1) &=  \\
&= - \frac{5}{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Evaluating Numerical Expressions Containing Multiple Operations

**Example:** Evaluate $-2 + 6 \div (-10)$.

**Explanation**

To evaluate this expression, we use PEMDAS.

First, we divide:

$$
\begin{aligned}
-2 + 6 \div (-10) &=  \\
- 2 + (6 \div (-10)) &=  \\
&= - 2 + (-0.6)
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we add:

$$
\begin{aligned}
-2 + (-0.6) &= -2.6
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="evaluating-expressions-containing-parentheses"></a>
## Evaluating Expressions Containing Parentheses

**Example:** What is the value of $\left(-3 + 6 \right) \div \dfrac{4}{3} - \dfrac{1}{4}$?

**Explanation**

To evaluate this expression, we use PEMDAS.

First, we perform the operation inside the parentheses:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(-3 + 6) \div \frac{4}{3} - \frac{1}{4} &=  \\
&= 3 \div \frac{4}{3} - \frac{1}{4}
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we divide:

$$
\begin{aligned}
3 \div \frac{4}{3} - \frac{1}{4} &=  \\
(3 \div \frac{4}{3}) - \frac{1}{4} &=  \\
(\frac{3}{1} \div \frac{4}{3}) - \frac{1}{4} &=  \\
(\frac{3}{1} \cdot \frac{3}{4}) - \frac{1}{4} &=  \\
\frac{3 \cdot 3}{1 \cdot 4} - \frac{1}{4} &=  \\
&= \frac{9}{4} - \frac{1}{4}
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we subtract:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{9}{4} - \frac{1}{4} &=  \\
\frac{9 - 1}{4} &=  \\
\frac{8}{4} &=  \\
8 \div 4 &=  \\
&= 2
\end{aligned}
$$
