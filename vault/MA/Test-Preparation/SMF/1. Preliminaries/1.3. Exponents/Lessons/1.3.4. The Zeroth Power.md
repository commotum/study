## Introduction

Any number raised to the power of zero is equal to $1$. For example,

$$
2^0 = 1 \quad\textrm{and}\quad (-55)^0=1
$$

To understand why, let's try to convince ourselves that $2^0 = 1$. First, we write down a table containing all of the powers of $2$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
2^{4} &= 16 \\
2^{3} &= 8 \\
2^{2} &= 4 \\
2^{1} &= 2 \\
2^{0} &= ◻
\end{aligned}
$$

As we go down the list, the number in blue is divided by $2$ each time. Following this pattern, we must have

$$
2^{\color{red}{0}} = {\color{blue}{2}}\div 2 = 1
$$

It doesn't matter whether the number is positive or negative, a fraction or a decimal, big or small. Raising any non-zero number to the power of zero always gives $1$.
