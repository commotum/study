## Introduction

When summarizing a data set, we often want to produce a *single* number that best represents *all* numbers in the data set. One way to do this is to find the **mean** of the data set. The mean can be considered the "center" of the data set.

To compute the mean, we add all the data points and divide the sum by the number of data points.

To demonstrate, let's compute the mean of the data set below:

$$
1, \quad 7, \quad 3, \quad 9
$$

First, we add up all the data points:

$$
1 + 7 + 3 + 9 = 20
$$

Then, we divide by the number of data points. Since there are $4$ data points, we have

$$
\dfrac{20}{4} = 5
$$

Therefore, the mean of the data set is $5$.

<a id="computing-the-mean-of-a-data-set"></a>
## Computing the Mean of a Data Set

**Example:** What is the mean of the following data set?
$9, \: 10, \: 8, \: 11, \: 12$

**Explanation**

First, notice that our data set contains $5$ numbers.

Now, let's add all the numbers together:

$$
9 + 10 + 8 + 11 + 12 = 50
$$

Finally, we divide the sum by the number of data points:

$$
\dfrac{50}{5} = 10
$$

Therefore, the mean of the data set is $10$.

## Computing the Mean of a Data Set: Word Problems

**Example:** A student obtained the following grades on her last $5$ chemistry quizzes:

$7, \: 8, \: 9, \: 7, \: 6$
What was the student's mean score?

**Explanation**

First, notice that our data set contains $5$ numbers.

Now, let's add all the numbers together:

$$
7 + 8 + 9 + 7 + 6 = 37
$$

Finally, we divide the sum by the number of data points:

$$
\dfrac{37}{5} = 7.4
$$

Therefore, the student's mean score is $7.4$.

## Determining a Sum of Data Points From the Mean

We calculate the mean of a data set by dividing the sum of the values in the data set by the total number of data points.

$$
\text{mean} = \dfrac{\text{sum of data points}}{\text{number of data points}}
$$

This means that if we know the mean and the number of data points, we can find the sum of the data points by multiplying these two values together:

$$
\text{sum of data points} = \text{mean} \times \text{number of data points}
$$

In other words, the sum of the values in a dataset is the product of the mean and the number of data points.

Let's see an example.

---

<a id="recovering-a-sum-from-the-mean"></a>
## Recovering a Sum From the Mean

**Example:** At a cooking competition, the mean score received by each of the $40$ contestants was $82$. What was the total of all the scores given in the competition?

**Explanation**

The total of all the scores is the sum of all the scores received by the contestants.

The sum of the values in a dataset is the product of the mean and the number of data points:

$$
\text{sum} = \text{mean} \times \text{number of data points}
$$

In our case, the mean is $82$ (points) and there are $40$ data points (contestants). Hence, the sum is

$$
\text{sum} = 82 \cdot 40 = 3,280
$$

Therefore, the total of all the scores given in the competition is $3,280$.
