## Introduction

Let's calculate the surface area of the rectangular prism shown below:

![](<../Source/8.4.4. Surface Areas of Rectangular Solids/Images/b95baffb9dd6566a118efe55e32b90b8.png>)

The surface area is made up of 6 faces, as follows:

- the top and bottom faces each have area $4\,\textrm{cm}\times 2\,\textrm{cm} = 8\,\textrm{cm}^2$
- the front and back faces each have area $4\,\textrm{cm}\times 3\,\textrm{cm} = 12\,\textrm{cm}^2$
- the left and right faces each have area $2\,\textrm{cm}\times 3\,\textrm{cm} = 6\,\textrm{cm}^2$

Therefore, the total surface area $S$ of the prism is

$$
\begin{aligned}
S &= 2(8cm^{2}) + 2(12cm^{2}) + 2(6cm^{2}) \\
&= 16cm^{2} + 24cm^{2} + 12cm^{2} \\
&= 52cm^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

We conclude that the surface area of the rectangular prism is $52\,\textrm{cm}^2$.

## Deriving a General Formula for the Surface Area of a Rectangular Prism

A general rectangular prism has length $l$, width $w$, and height $h$. Let's now derive a general formula for the surface area $S$ of a rectangular prism.

![](<../Source/8.4.4. Surface Areas of Rectangular Solids/Images/4dbe5747d5f1023dc7752a0b1ad84b01.png>)

Similarly to the previous example, the surface area is made up of six faces:

- the top and bottom faces both have area $lw$
- the front and back faces both have area $lh$
- the left and right faces both have area $wh$

Adding the areas of the faces together, we get

$$
S = 2lw + 2lh + 2wh
$$

which simplifies to

$$
S = 2(lw + lh + wh)
$$

From now on, we will use this formula to compute the surface area of a rectangular prism.

---

<a id="calculating-the-surface-area-of-a-rectangular-solid-given-its-dimensions"></a>
## Calculating the Surface Area of a Rectangular Solid Given Its Dimensions

**Example:** Find the surface area of the rectangular prism shown below.

![](<../Source/8.4.4. Surface Areas of Rectangular Solids/Images/b60e05135402486d1acde978a1e5b959.png>)

**Explanation**

We can find the surface area $S$ of a rectangular prism using the formula

$$
S = 2(lw + lh + wh)
$$

where $l$ is the length, $w$ is the width, and $h$ is the height of the solid.

Substituting

$$
l=4 \, \textrm{in}, \qquad w=5 \, \textrm{in}, \qquad h=1 \, \textrm{in}
$$

into the formula, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
S &= 2(4 \cdot 5 + 4 \cdot 1 + 5 \cdot 1) \\
&= 2(20 + 4 + 5) \\
&= 2 \cdot 29 \\
&= 58in^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding a Dimension of a Rectangular Solid Given Its Surface Area and Other Dimensions

**Example:** The width and height of a rectangular prism are $5 \, \textrm {cm}$ and $4 \, \textrm{cm}$, respectively, and its surface area is $148 \, \textrm{cm}^2$. What is the length of the prism?

**Explanation**

We can find the surface area $S$ of a rectangular prism using the formula

$$
S = 2(lw + lh + wh)
$$

where $l$ is the length, $w$ is the width, and $h$ is the height of the solid.

Substituting

$$
S = 148, \qquad w=5, \qquad h=4
$$

into the formula, we obtain the following:

$$
\begin{aligned}
S &= 2(lw + lh + wh) \\
148 &= 2(5l + 4l + 5 \cdot 4) \\
148 &= 2(9l + 20) \\
74 &= 9l + 20 \\
54 &= 9l \\
l &= 6
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the length of the prism is $6 \, \textrm{cm}$.

## Lateral Surface Areas of Rectangular Solids

The **lateral surface area** of a rectangular solid is the surface area of the side faces alone. In other words, it's the surface area that does not include the area of the top or bottom faces.

![](<../Source/8.4.4. Surface Areas of Rectangular Solids/Images/042d95c783863ef6b944380073f75ec4.png>)

The lateral surface area $S_L$ of a rectangular solid can be found using the formula

$$
S_L = 2(lh + wh)
$$

where $l$, $w$, and $h$ are the solid's length, width, and height, respectively.

Notice that the $lw$ term is missing from the expression for $S_L$. This is because $lw$ denotes the area of the top and bottom faces.

---

<a id="calculating-the-lateral-surface-area-of-a-rectangular-solid-given-its-dimensions"></a>
## Calculating the Lateral Surface Area of a Rectangular Solid Given Its Dimensions

**Example:** Determine the lateral surface area for the rectangular solid below.

![](<../Source/8.4.4. Surface Areas of Rectangular Solids/Images/76588aaa920da1d4e86d48ec8bba301a.png>)

**Explanation**

We can find the lateral surface area $S_L$ of a rectangular solid using the formula

$$
S_L = 2(lh + wh)
$$

where $l$ is the length, $w$ is the width, and $h$ is the height of the solid.

Substituting

$$
l = 4\: \textrm{mm}, \qquad w=5\: \textrm{mm}, \qquad h=2\: \textrm{mm}
$$

into the formula, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
S_{L} &= 2(4 \cdot 2 + 5 \cdot 2) \\
&= 2(8 + 10) \\
&= 2(18) \\
&= 36mm^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$
