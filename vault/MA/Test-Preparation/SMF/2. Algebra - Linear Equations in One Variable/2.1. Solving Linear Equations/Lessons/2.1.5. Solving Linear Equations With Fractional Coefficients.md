## Introduction

Suppose that we want to solve the equation

$$
\dfrac{2}{3}x = 8
$$

This equation has a **fractional coefficient** of

$$
\dfrac{2}{3}
$$

The term

$$
\dfrac{2}{3}x
$$

means that $x$ is being multiplied by $2$ and divided by $3$.

We can solve this problem by applying the multiplication principle, twice. First, we multiply both sides by ${\color{blue}{3}}$ to get rid of the fraction:

$$
\begin{aligned} \dfrac{2}{3}x &= 8 \\[5pt] {\color{blue}{3}}\cdot \dfrac{2}{3}x &={\color{blue}{3}}\cdot 8 \\[5pt] \cancel{\color{blue}{3}} \cdot \dfrac{2}{\cancel{3}}x &={\color{blue}{3}}\cdot 8 \\ 2x &=24 \end{aligned}
$$

Then, we divide both sides by ${\color{red}{2}}$ to isolate $x$:

$$
\begin{aligned} 2x &=24 \\[5pt] \dfrac{2x}{{\color{red}{2}}} &=\dfrac{24}{{\color{red}{2}}} \\[5pt] \dfrac{\cancel{2}x}{\cancel{\color{red}{2}}} &=\dfrac{24}{{\color{red}{2}}} \\ x &=12 \end{aligned}
$$

<a id="solving-one-step-linear-equations-without-the-reciprocal"></a>
## Solving One-Step Linear Equations Without the Reciprocal

**Example:** Solve for $x$, if $-\dfrac{3}{4}x = 12$.

**Explanation**

First, we multiply both sides by $\color{blue}4$ to get rid of the fraction:

$$
\begin{aligned}
-\frac{3}{4}x &= 12 \\
4 \cdot (-\frac{3}{4}x) &= 4 \cdot 12 \\
4 \cdot (-\frac{3}{4}x) &= 48 \\
- 3x &= 48
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we divide both sides of the equation by ${\color{red}-3}$ to isolate $x$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
-3x &= 48 \\
\frac{-3x}{-3} &= \frac{48}{-3} \\
\frac{-3x}{-3} &= -16 \\
x &= -16
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="using-the-reciprocal"></a>
## Using the Reciprocal

When solving equations with fractional coefficients, there is a shortcut! We can complete division and multiplication in a single step by multiplying both sides of the equation by the reciprocal of the fractional coefficient.

For example, to solve the equation

$$
\dfrac{2x}{5}= 6
$$

we first express the fraction as a coefficient:

$$
\require{cancel} \begin{aligned} \dfrac{2}{5}x &= 6 \end{aligned}
$$

The fractional coefficient is

$$
\dfrac{2}{5}
$$

, and its reciprocal is

$$
\left(\dfrac 2 5\right)^{-1} = {\color{blue}{\dfrac 5 2}}
$$

Because the product of a fraction and its reciprocal is equal to $1$, we can multiply both sides of the equation by the reciprocal to isolate $x$:

$$
\require{cancel} \begin{aligned} \dfrac{2}{5}x &= 6 \\[5pt] \underbrace{{\color{blue}{\dfrac 5 2}} \cdot \dfrac{2}{5}}_{\large =\, 1} x &= {\color{blue}{\dfrac 5 2}} \cdot 6 \\[5pt] 1 x &= \dfrac{5\cdot 6}{2} \\[5pt] x &= \dfrac{30}{2} \\[5pt] x &= 15 \end{aligned}
$$

<a id="solving-one-step-linear-equations-with-the-reciprocal"></a>
## Solving One-Step Linear Equations With the Reciprocal

**Example:** If $-\dfrac{3}{2}t = 24$, then what is the value of $t$?

**Explanation**

We multiply both sides of the equation by the reciprocal of

$$
-\dfrac{3}{2}
$$

which is

$$
\left(-\dfrac{3}{2} \right)^{-1} = -\dfrac{2}{3}
$$

This gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
-\frac{3}{2}t &= 24 \\
- \frac{2}{3} \cdot (-\frac{3}{2}t) &= -\frac{2}{3} \cdot 24 \\
1t &= -\frac{2}{3} \cdot 3 \cdot 8 \\
t &= -16
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="solving-two-step-linear-equations-with-the-reciprocal"></a>
## Solving Two-Step Linear Equations With the Reciprocal

**Example:** Solve the equation $\dfrac{3x}{4}-5 = -8$.

**Explanation**

First, we isolate the fractional term

$$
\dfrac{3x}{4}
$$

by adding $5$ to both sides:

$$
\begin{aligned} \dfrac{3x}{4}-5 &=-8 \\[5pt] \dfrac{3x}{4}-5+5 &=-8+5 \\[5pt] \dfrac{3x}{4}+0 &=-3 \\[5pt] \dfrac{3x}{4} &=-3 \end{aligned}
$$

The fractional coefficient is

$$
\dfrac{3}{4}
$$

so we multiply by its reciprocal

$$
\left(\dfrac{3}{4} \right)^{-1} = \dfrac{4}{3}
$$

This gives

$$
\begin{aligned} \dfrac{3}{4}x &=-3 \\[5pt] \dfrac{4}{3} \cdot \dfrac{3}{4}x &= \dfrac{4}{3} \cdot (-3) \\[5pt] 1 x &=-\dfrac{4}{\cancel{3}} \cdot \cancel{3} \\[5pt] x &= -4. \end{aligned}
$$
