## Introduction

Whenever we have a fraction in an equation, we apply the multiplication principle to get rid of it. For example, consider the equation below:

$$
\dfrac{2x+12}{3} =6
$$

Since the opposite of dividing by $3$ is multiplying by $3$, we apply the multiplication principle and multiply both sides of the equation by $3$:

$$
\require{cancel} \begin{aligned} \dfrac{2x+12}{3} &=6 \\ 3 \cdot \left(\dfrac{2x+12}{3}\right) &= 3 \cdot 6\ \cancel{3} \cdot \left(\dfrac{2x+12}{\cancel{3}}\right) &= 18 \\ 2x+12 &= 18 \end{aligned}
$$

The process of removing the fraction is called **clearing the rational expression**. We now have an equation that we can solve using the usual methods:

$$
\begin{aligned} 2x+12 &= 18 \\ 2x+12-12 &=18-12 \\ 2x &=6 \ \dfrac{2x}{2} &=\dfrac{6}{2} \\ x &=3 \end{aligned}
$$

<a id="clearing-a-rational-expression-with-a-variable-on-one-side"></a>
## Clearing a Rational Expression With a Variable on One Side

**Example:** Solve the equation $13 = \dfrac{6y+8}{2}$.

**Explanation**

First, we clear the rational expression by multiplying both sides by $2$:

$$
\begin{aligned} 13 &= \dfrac{6y+8}{2} \\ 2 \cdot 13 &= 2 \cdot \left(\dfrac{6y+8}{2}\right) \\ 2 \cdot 13 &= \cancel{2} \cdot \left(\dfrac{6y+8}{\cancel{2}}\right) \\ 26 &= 6y+8 \end{aligned}
$$

Next, we solve the remaining equation using the addition and multiplication principles:

$$
\begin{aligned} 26 &= 6y+8 \\ 26-8 &= 6y+8-8 \\ 18 &= 6y \ \dfrac{18}{6} &= \dfrac{6y}{6} \\ 3 &= y \end{aligned}
$$

Thus, $y=3$.

## Clearing a Rational Expression With a Variable on Both Sides

**Example:** Solve the equation $\dfrac{a+2}{3} =a$.

**Explanation**

First, we clear the rational expression by multiplying both sides by $3$:

$$
\require{cancel} \begin{aligned} \dfrac{a+2}{3} &=a \\ 3 \cdot \left(\dfrac{a+2}{3} \right) &= 3 \cdot a \ \cancel{3} \cdot \left(\dfrac{a+2}{\cancel{3}} \right) &= 3a \\ a+2 &= 3a \end{aligned}
$$

Next, we solve the remaining equation using the addition and multiplication principles:

$$
\require{cancel} \begin{aligned} a+2 &= 3a \\ a + 2 - a &= 3a - a \\ 2 &= 2a \ \dfrac{2}{2} &= \dfrac{2a}{2} \\ 1 &= a \end{aligned}
$$

Thus, $a=1$.

## Clearing a Rational Expression With Binomials on Both Sides

**Example:** Solve the equation $\dfrac{2n+6}{3} = 4n+1$.

**Explanation**

First, we clear the rational expression by multiplying both sides by $3$. We must make sure that *all* of the terms on the right-hand side are multiplied by $3$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{2n + 6}{3} &= 4n + 1 \\
3 \cdot (\frac{2n + 6}{3}) &= 3 \cdot (4n + 1) \\
3 \cdot (\frac{2n + 6}{3}) &= 3 \cdot 4n + 3 \cdot 1 \\
2n + 6 &= 12n + 3
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we solve the remaining equation using the addition and multiplication principles:

$$
\begin{aligned}
2n + 6 &= 12n + 3 \\
2n + 6 - 2n &= 12n + 3 - 2n \\
6 &= 10n + 3 \\
6 - 3 &= 10n + 3 - 3 \\
3 &= 10n \\
\frac{3}{10} &= \frac{10n}{10} \\
\frac{3}{10} &= n
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus,

$$
n = \dfrac{3}{10}
$$

## Clearing a Rational Expression That Includes a Factored Expression

**Example:** Solve $\dfrac{5p+10}{6} =2(p-5)$.

**Explanation**

First, we clear the rational expression by multiplying both sides by $6$:

$$
\begin{aligned} \dfrac{5p+10}{6} &=2(p-5) \\ 6 \cdot \left(\dfrac{5p+10}{6}\right) &=6\cdot 2 (p-5) \ \cancel{6} \cdot \left(\dfrac{5p+10}{\cancel{6}}\right) &=12(p-5) \\ 5p+10 &=12p-60 \end{aligned}
$$

Then, we solve the remaining equation using the addition and multiplication principles:

$$
\begin{aligned} 5p+10 &=12p-60 \\ 5p+10-5p &=12p-60-5p \\ 10 &=7p-60 \\ 10+60 &=7p-60+60 \\ 70 &=7p \ \dfrac{70}{7} &=\dfrac{7p}{7} \\ 10 &= p \end{aligned}
$$

Thus, $p=10$.
