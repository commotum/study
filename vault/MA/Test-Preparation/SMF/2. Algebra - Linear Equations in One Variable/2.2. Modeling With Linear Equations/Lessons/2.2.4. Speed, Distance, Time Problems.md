## Introduction

If an object moves at a constant speed for some length of time, we can calculate how far it travels using the formula

$$
\text{distance} = \text{speed} \times \text{time}
$$

We can use this formula to determine how long it takes for two objects to meet.

For example, suppose a car leaves London for Glasgow, driving at $70$ miles per hour. At the same time, another car departs Glasgow for London, driving at $60$ miles per hour. If the distance between Glasgow and London is $416$ miles, how long until the cars drive past each other?

Let $T$ be the number of hours from when the cars left to when they meet.

We fill in a speed-time-distance table for each vehicle:

| Car | Speed (mi/hr) | Time (hr) | Distance (mi) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| **London to Glasgow** | $70$ | $T$ | $70 T$ |
| **Glasgow to London** | $60$ | $T$ | $60T$ |

When the cars cross paths, they will have driven a combined distance of $416$ miles. Therefore, we can set the sum of the distances equal to $416$ and solve for time, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
70T + 60T &= 416 \\
130T &= 416 \\
\frac{130T}{130} &= \frac{416}{130} \\
&= T \mid \approx 3.2
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the cars drive past each other after about $3.2$ hours.

## Modeling Two Objects Traveling in Opposite Directions

**Example:** John and Mary leave their office simultaneously, heading directly away from each other. If John is walking at $7\,\textrm{km/hr}$ and Mary walks at $5\,\textrm{km/hr}$, how long will it take for them to be $3\,\textrm{km}$ apart?

**Explanation**

Let $T$ be the time it takes for John and Mary to be $3\,\textrm{km}$ apart.

We fill in a speed-time-distance table:

| Person | Speed (km/hr) | Time (hr) | Distance (km) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| **Mary** | $5$ | $T$ | $5T$ |
| **John** | $7$ | $T$ | $7T$ |

After $T$ hours, they will have traveled a combined distance of $3\,\textrm{km}$. Therefore, we can set the sum of the distances equal to $3$ and solve for time:

$$
\begin{aligned}
5T + 7T &= 3 \\
12T &= 3 \\
T &= \frac{3}{12} \\
T &= \frac{1}{4}
\end{aligned}
$$

It will take

$$
\dfrac{1}{4}
$$

hours for John and Mary to be $3\,\textrm{km}$ apart. If we convert this time to minutes, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{1}{4}hr &= \frac{1}{4}hr \cdot \frac{60min}{hr} \\
&= \frac{1}{4}hr \cdot \frac{60min}{hr} \\
&= \frac{1}{4} \cdot 60min \\
&= 15min
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, it will take $15$ minutes for them to be $3\,\textrm{km}$ apart.

## Modeling Two Objects Traveling in Opposite Directions and Different Start Times

**Example:** A train traveling $180$ kilometers per hour leaves Chicago for New York at $1$ pm. A second train traveling $150$ kilometers per hour leaves New York for Chicago $3$ hours later. If the distance between Chicago and New York is $1\,200$ kilometers, at what time will the trains pass each other?

**Explanation**

Let's call $N$ the first train (heading toward New York) and $C$ the second train (heading toward Chicago).

Let $T$ be the number of hours from when $N$ leaves Chicago to when it meets $C$. Then, $C$ will have been traveling for $T-3$ hours because it left the station three hours after the train $N$.

We fill in a speed-time-distance table:

| Train | Speed (km/hr) | Time (hr) | Distance (km) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| N | $180$ | $T$ | $180T$ |
| C | $150$ | $T-3$ | $150(T-3)$ |

After $T$ hours, the trains have traveled a combined distance of $1\,200$ kilometers. Therefore, we can set the sum of the distances equal to $1\,200$ and solve for time:

$$
\begin{aligned}
180T + 150(T - 3) &= 1200 \\
180T + 150T - 450 &= 1200 \\
330T - 450 &= 1200 \\
330T &= 1650 \\
T &= 5
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the trains will pass each other $5$ hours after $N$ leaves Chicago. Since $N$ leaves Chicago at $1$ pm, trains will pass each other at $6$ pm.
