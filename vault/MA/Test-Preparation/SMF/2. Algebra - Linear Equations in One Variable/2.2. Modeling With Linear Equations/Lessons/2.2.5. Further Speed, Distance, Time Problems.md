## Introduction

If an object moves at a constant speed for some length of time, we can calculate how far it travels using the formula

$$
\text{distance} = \text{speed} \times \text{time}
$$

We can use this formula to determine the time it takes for one object to catch up with another when both objects travel at a constant speed.

Let's see an example.

---

<a id="modeling-two-objects-traveling-in-the-same-direction"></a>
## Modeling Two Objects Traveling in the Same Direction

**Example:** Martha and Lucy jog on an Olympic track, which is $0.4 \, \textrm{km}$ long. If Martha and Lucy started running simultaneously from the same point at speeds of $7 \, \textrm{km/h}$ and $5 \, \textrm{km/h}$, respectively, how long did it take Martha to lap Lucy for the first time?

**Explanation**

Let $T$ be the time it takes Martha to lap Lucy for the first time.

We start by filling in a speed-time-distance table:

| Person | Speed (km/h) | Time (h) | Distance (km) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| **Martha** | $7$ | $T$ | $7T$ |
| **Lucy** | $5$ | $T$ | $5T$ |

When Martha first laps Lucy, Martha will have run exactly one more lap around the track than Lucy has. Since the track is $0.4 \, \textrm{km}$ around, we must have

$$
\begin{aligned}
7T &= 5T + 0.4 \\
2T &= 0.4 \\
T &= 0.2
\end{aligned}
$$

So, Martha takes $0.2$ hours to catch up with Lucy. If we convert this time to minutes, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
0.2h &= 0.2h \cdot \frac{60min}{h} \\
&= 0.2h \cdot \frac{60min}{h} \\
&= 0.2 \cdot 60min \\
&= 12min
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, it takes Martha $12$ minutes to lap Lucy.

<a id="modeling-two-objects-traveling-in-the-same-direction-with-different-start-times"></a>
## Modeling Two Objects Traveling in the Same Direction With Different Start Times

**Example:** On a highway, a taxi passes a gas station at a speed of $50 \, \textrm{km/h}$. After $2.4$ hours, another taxi passes the same gas station at $80 \, \textrm{km/h}$, moving in the same direction. How long will it take for the second taxi to catch up with the first?

**Explanation**

Let $T$ be the time the second taxi takes to catch up with the first. Then, the first taxi has been traveling for $T+2.4$ hours before the second taxi catches up with it.

We start by filling in a speed-time-distance table for both taxis:

| Taxi | Speed (km/h) | Time (h) | Distance (km) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| **First** | $50$ | $T + 2.4$ | $50(T + 2.4)$ |
| **Second** | $80$ | $T$ | $80T$ |

When the second taxi catches up with the first, both have traveled the same distance from the gas station. Therefore, we can set the distances equal and solve for time:

$$
\begin{aligned}
50(T + 2.4) &= 80T \\
50T + 120 &= 80T \\
120 &= 30T \\
\frac{120}{30} &= T \\
4 &= T \\
T &= 4
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, it takes the second taxi $4$ hours to catch up with the first.

## Modeling Two Objects Traveling in the Same Direction With Different Start Times and Unit Conversions

**Example:** Bus $A$ leaves a station at $9{:}00$ am traveling $40\,\textrm{mi/h}$. $30$ minutes later, bus $B$ leaves the same station, traveling in the same direction at $70\,\textrm{mi/h}$. At what time does bus $B$ catch up with bus $A$?

**Explanation**

Let $T$ be the number of hours it takes bus $B$ to catch up with bus $A$. Then bus $A$ will have been traveling for

$$
T+\dfrac{1}{2}
$$

hours because bus $A$ leaves the station $30$ minutes (or

$$
\dfrac{1}{2}
$$

an hour) earlier than bus $B$.

We start by filling in a speed-time-distance table:

| Bus | Speed (mi/h) | Time (h) | Distance (mi) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| **A** | $40$ | $T+\dfrac{1}{2}$ | $40\left(T+\dfrac{1}{2}\right)$ |
| **B** | $70$ | $T$ | $70T$ |

Both will have traveled the same distance from the station when they meet. Therefore, we can set the distances equal and solve for time:

$$
\begin{aligned}
40(T + \frac{1}{2}) &= 70T \\
40T + 20 &= 70T \\
20 &= 30T \\
T &= \frac{2}{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

So, bus $B$ reaches bus $A$ in

$$
\dfrac{2}{3}
$$

hours. If we convert this time to minutes, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{2}{3}h &= \frac{2}{3}h \cdot \frac{60min}{h} \\
&= \frac{2}{3}h \cdot \frac{60min}{h} \\
&= \frac{2}{3} \cdot 60min \\
&= 40min
\end{aligned}
$$

So, bus $B$ reaches bus $A$ in $40$ minutes after leaving the station. Since bus $B$ leaves the station at $9{:}30$ am ($30$ minutes after $A$), it catches up with bus $A$ at $10{:}10$ am.
