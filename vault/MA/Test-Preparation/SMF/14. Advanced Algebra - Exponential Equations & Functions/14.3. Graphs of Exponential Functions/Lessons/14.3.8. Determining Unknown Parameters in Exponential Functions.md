## Introduction

In this lesson, we will learn how to determine unknown parameters in exponential functions using information about points on their graphs.

Often, we are given a function with an unknown constant, and by knowing where the graph passes through a particular point, we can solve for that constant.

Let's look at an example to make this concrete.

A function is defined by

$$
f(x) = 7^{x} + a
$$

where $a$ is a constant. The graph of $y = f(x)$ has its $y$-intercept at $(0,4)$. Let's use this information to find the value of $a$.

Since the graph passes through the point $(0,4)$, we have

$$
f(0) = 4
$$

So, we have the following equation, which we solve for $a{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
f(0) &= 4 \\
7^{0} + a &= 4 \\
1 + a &= 4 \\
a &= 3
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, $a = 3$.

Now, let’s look at a few more examples.

## Determining a Vertical Shift Given the Y-Intercept of an Exponential Curve

**Example:** A function is defined by $f(x) = 6 \cdot 3^{x} + n$, where $n$ is a constant. The graph of $y = f(x)$ has its $y$-intercept at $(0,15)$. What is the value of $n$?

**Explanation**

Since the graph passes through the point $(0,15)$, we have

$$
f(0) = 15
$$

So, we have the following equation, which we solve for $n{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
f(0) &= 15 \\
6 \cdot 3^{0} + n &= 15 \\
6 \cdot 1 + n &= 15 \\
6 + n &= 15 \\
n &= 9
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, $n =9$.

## Determining the Base of an Exponential Function Given a Point on the Curve

**Example:** An exponential function is defined by $f(x) = 15 \cdot c^{x}$, where $c$ is a positive constant. What is the value of $c$ if $f(2) = 135$?

**Explanation**

We are told that

$$
f(x) = 15 \cdot c^{x}
$$

and that

$$
f(2) = 135
$$

Substituting $x = 2$ into the function $f$ and solve for $c$, we obtain the following:

$$
\begin{aligned}
f(2) &= 15 \cdot c^{2} \\
135 &= 15c^{2} \\
15c^{2} &= 135 \\
\frac{15c^{2}}{15} &= \frac{135}{15} \\
c^{2} &= 9 \\
c &= ± 3
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, taking into account that $c$ is positive, we get $c = 3$.

## Cases With Multiple Unknown Parameters

We now consider a slightly more involved function:

$$
f(x) = 2 \cdot m^x + n
$$

where $m$ and $n$ are constants. Suppose we are told that the graph of $y = f(x)$ in the $xy$-plane passes through the point

$$
\left(0, 1\right)
$$

and the product of $m$ and $n$ is $-12$.

Let's use this information to find the values of $m$ and $n$. To do this, we examine the given conditions in turn.

- Since the graph passes through the point $\left(0, 1\right)$, we have that $f(0) = 1$. So, we have the following equation, which we solve for $n{:}$
$f(0)|= 1; 2 \cdot m^{0} + n|= 1; 2 \cdot 1 + n|= 1; 2 + n|= 1; 2 + n - 2|= 1 - 2; n|=-1$
- Since $mn=-12$ and using the fact that $n = -1$, we obtain the following equation, which we solve for $m{:}$
$mn|=-12; m(-1)|=-12; - m|=-12; (-m)(-1)|= (-12)(-1); m|= 12$

Therefore, we conclude that $m=12$ and $n=-1$.

## Determining Multiple Unknown Parameters Given a Product and Y-Intercept

**Example:** The function is defined by $f(x) = -a^{x} + d$, where $a$ and $d$ are positive constants. The graph of $y = f(x)$ in the $xy$-plane passes through the point $\left(0, \dfrac{5}{2}\right)$, and the product of $a$ and $d$ is $\dfrac{21}{2}$. What is the value of $a$?

**Explanation**

Let's examine the given conditions in turn.

- Since the graph passes through the point $\left(0, \dfrac{5}{2}\right)$, we have that $f(0) = \dfrac{5}{2}$. So, we have the following equation, which we solve for $d{:}$
$f(0)|= \frac{5}{2}; - a^{0} + d|= \frac{5}{2}; - 1 + d|= \frac{5}{2}; - 1 + d + 1|= \frac{5}{2} + 1; d|= \frac{7}{2}$
- Since $ad=\dfrac{21}{2}$, using the fact that $d = \dfrac{7}{2}$, we obtain the following equation, which we solve for $a{:}$
$ad|= \frac{21}{2}; a(\frac{7}{2})|= \frac{21}{2}; \frac{7a}{2}|= \frac{21}{2}; 2 \cdot \frac{7a}{2}|= 2 \cdot \frac{21}{2}; 7a|= 21; \frac{7a}{7}|= \frac{21}{7}; a|= 3$

Therefore, $a=3$.

## Determining Multiple Unknown Parameters Given the Y-Intercept and Another Point

**Example:** The function $g$ is defined by $g(x) = a^{x} + b$, where $a$ and $b$ are constants and $a > 0$. In the $xy$-plane, the graph of $y = g(x)$ has the $y$-intercept at $(0,-1)$ and passes through the point $(3,25)$. What is the value of $a + b$?

**Explanation**

Let's examine the given conditions in turn.

- Since the graph passes through the point $(0,-1)$, we have $g(0) = -1$. So, we have the following equation, which we solve for $b{:}$
$g(0)|=-1; a^{0} + b|=-1; 1 + b|=-1; b|=-2$
- Since the graph passes through the point $(3,25)$, we have $g(3) = 25$. Then, using the fact that $b=-2$, we can determine the value of $c$, as follows:
$g(3)|= 25; a^{3} + (-2)|= 25; a^{3} - 2|= 25; a^{3}|= 27; a|= \sqrt[3]{27}; a|= 3$

Therefore, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
a + b &= 3 + (-2) = 1
\end{aligned}
$$
