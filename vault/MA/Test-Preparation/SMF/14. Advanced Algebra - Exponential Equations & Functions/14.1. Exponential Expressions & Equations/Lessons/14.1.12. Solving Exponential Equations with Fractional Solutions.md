## Introduction

We can use the rule of exponents for fractional powers to help us solve exponential equations.

For example, suppose that we want to find the solution to the following equation:

$$
3^x = \sqrt{3}
$$

The rules of exponents tell us that $\sqrt{3}$ can be written as follows:

$$
\sqrt{3} = 3^{1/2}
$$

Therefore, we can rewrite the right-hand side of our equation as follows:

$$
3^x = 3^{1/2}
$$

Now that both sides of the equation share a common base $(3)$, we can equate the powers:

$$
3^{x} = 3^{1/2}; x = \frac{1}{2}
$$

So the solution to the equation is

$$
x=\dfrac 1 2
$$

## Solving an Exponential Equation Using Fractional Exponents

**Example:** Solve $2^y = \sqrt[3]{2}$.

**Explanation**

The fractional exponent rule tells us that the radical on the right-hand side can be rewritten using a fractional power, as follows:

$$
\sqrt[3]{2} = 2^{1/3}
$$

So, we can rewrite our equation as

$$
2^y = 2^{1/3}
$$

Now that both sides have a common base of $2$, we can equate the powers:

$$
\begin{aligned}
2^{y} &= 2^{1/3} \\
y &= \frac{1}{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the solution is

$$
y=\dfrac{1}{3}
$$

## Solving an Exponential Equation Using Negative Fractional Exponents

**Example:** Find the solution of the equation $4^x = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt[3]{4}}$.

**Explanation**

First, we rewrite the radical in the denominator using the fractional power rule:

$$
\begin{aligned}
4^{x} &= \frac{1}{4^{1/3}}
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we write the expression on the right-hand side using a negative exponent:

$$
\begin{aligned}
4^{x} &= 4^{-1/3}
\end{aligned}
$$

Now that both sides of the equation have the same base $(4)$, we can equate the powers:

$$
\begin{aligned}
4^{x} &= 4^{-1/3} \\
x &= -\frac{1}{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the solution is

$$
x=-\dfrac{1}{3}
$$

## Solving an Exponential Equation Using Fractional Exponents and the Power Rule

**Example:** If $5^y = \sqrt[3]{25}$, find the value of $y$.

**Explanation**

Since the number under the radical is $25$ and the base of the variable exponent expression is ${\color{blue}5}$, we begin by rewriting $25$ as ${\color{blue}5}^2$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
5^{y} &= \sqrt[3]{25} \\
5^{y} &= \sqrt[3]{5^{2}}
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we rewrite the radical using the fractional exponent rule:

$$
\begin{aligned}
5^{y} &= (5^{2})^{1/3}
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we use the power rule to simplify the right-hand side:

$$
\begin{aligned}
5^{y} &= 5^{2 \cdot 1/3} \\
5^{y} &= 5^{2/3}
\end{aligned}
$$

Now that both sides of the equation share a common base, we can equate the exponents. This gives the solution

$$
y=\dfrac{2}{3}
$$

## Solving an Exponential Equation Using Negative Fractional Exponents and the Power Rule

**Example:** What is the solution to $6^x = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt[4]{36}}$?

**Explanation**

Since the number under the radical is $36$ and the base of the variable exponent expression is ${\color{blue}6}$, we begin by rewriting $36$ as ${\color{blue}6}^2$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
6^{x} &= \frac{1}{\sqrt[4]{6^{2}}}
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we rewrite the radical using the fractional exponent rule:

$$
\begin{aligned}
6^{x} &= (1)/((6^{2})^{1/4})
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we use the power rule to simplify the right-hand side:

$$
\begin{aligned}
6^{x} &= \frac{1}{6^{2 \cdot 1/4}} \\
6^{x} &= \frac{1}{6^{1/2}}
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we rewrite the right-hand side using a negative exponent:

$$
6^x =6^{-1/2}
$$

Now that both sides of the equation share a common base, we can equate the exponents. This gives the solution

$$
x=-\dfrac{1}{2}
$$
