## Introduction

We can use the distributive laws

$$
(a+b) \cdot c = a \cdot c+b \cdot c
$$

and

$$
a\cdot (b+c) = a \cdot b+a \cdot c
$$

to simplify exponential expressions.

For example, suppose we wish to simplify the expression

$$
\left(3d^2 e^{5} f + 6e f^3 \right) f^2
$$

First, we apply the distributive law.

$$
3d^2 e^{5} f \cdot f^2 + 6e f^3 \cdot f^2
$$

Next, we combine exponents with like bases. In this case, both terms have multiple factors containing powers of $f$, so let's group these together using parentheses:

$$
3d^2 e^{5} \left(f \cdot f^2 \right) + 6e \left(f^3 \cdot f^2 \right)
$$

Finally, we simplify using the laws of exponents. In this case, we apply the product rule for exponents:
$3d^{2}e^{5}f^{1 + 2} + 6ef^{3 + 2}$

which simplifies to

$$
3d^2 e^{5} f^3 + 6e f^5
$$

Let's see some more examples.

## Applying the Distributive Law

**Example:** Simplify the expression $b^{3/4}\left(b^{5/4} c + b^{1/4} c^2 \right)$.

**Explanation**

First, we use the distributive law, as shown below:

$$
b^{3/4}\left(b^{5/4} c + b^{1/4} c^2 \right) = b^{3/4} \cdot b^{5/4} c + b^{3/4} \cdot b^{1/4} c^2
$$

Now, we rewrite the terms by combining the rules of exponents with algebraic expressions:

$$
\begin{aligned}
b^{3/4} \cdot b^{5/4}c + b^{3/4} \cdot b^{1/4}c^{2} &= (b^{3/4} \cdot b^{5/4})c + (b^{3/4} \cdot b^{1/4})c^{2} \\
&= b^{(3/4 + 5/4)}c + b^{(3/4 + 1/4)}c^{2} \\
&= b^{8/4}c + b^{4/4}c^{2} \\
&= b^{2}c + bc^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Applying the Distributive Law to Rational Expressions

**Example:** Simplify the expression $\dfrac{p^7 q + p q^3}{p^4}$.

**Explanation**

First, we rewrite the denominator using negative exponents:

$$
\dfrac{p^7 q + p q^3}{p^4} = \left(p^7 q + p q^3 \right) p^{-4}
$$

Now, we use the distributive law, as shown below:

$$
\left(p^7 q + p q^3 \right) p^{-4} = p^7 q \cdot p^{-4} + p q^3 \cdot p^{-4}
$$

Next, we rewrite the terms by combining the rules of exponents with algebraic expressions:

$$
\begin{aligned}
p^{7}q \cdot p^{-4} + pq^{3} \cdot p^{-4} &= (p^{7} \cdot p^{-4})q + (p \cdot p^{-4})q^{3} \\
&= p^{7 - 4}q + p^{1 - 4}q^{3} \\
&= p^{3}q + p^{-3}q^{3} \\
&= p^{3}q + \frac{q^{3}}{p^{3}}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Simplifying an Expression Using Radicals

**Example:** Simplify the expression $\dfrac{v^{15/8} u^2 + v^{5/4} u}{v^{7/8}}$.

**Explanation**

First, we rewrite the denominator using negative exponents:

$$
\dfrac{v^{15/8} u^2 + v^{5/4} u}{v^{7/8}} = \left(v^{15/8} u^2 + v^{5/4} u \right) v^{-7/8}
$$

Now, we use the distributive law, as shown below:

$$
\left(v^{15/8} u^2 + v^{5/4} u \right) v^{-7/8} = v^{15/8} u^2 \cdot v^{-7/8} + v^{5/4} u \cdot v^{-7/8}
$$

Next, we rewrite the terms by combining the rules of exponents with algebraic expressions:

$$
\begin{aligned}
v^{15/8}u^{2} \cdot v^{-7/8} + v^{5/4}u \cdot v^{-7/8} &= (v^{15/8} \cdot v^{-7/8})u^{2} + (v^{5/4} \cdot v^{-7/8})u \\
&= v^{15/8 - 7/8}u^{2} + v^{10/8 - 7/8}u \\
&= v^{8/8}u^{2} + v^{3/8}u \\
&= vu^{2} + v^{3/8}u
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we write fractional exponents as radicals:

$$
v u^2 + v^{3/8} u = v u^2 + u \sqrt[8]{v^3}
$$
