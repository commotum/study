## Introduction

In a previous lesson, we saw how to create and apply a conversion formula to convert quantities with mixed units, such as converting from "kilometers per hour" to "meters per second."

In a separate lesson, we saw how to convert between different units of area, such as converting from "square meters" to "square centimeters" and vice versa.

By the end of this lesson, you'll be able to create and apply conversion formulas for mixed units that contain units of area.

For example, suppose a drone maps an area at a rate of

$$
0.9\,\dfrac{\textrm{km}^2}{\textrm{h}}
$$

What is this rate in square meters per second?

We're given the rate in

$$
\dfrac{\textrm{km}^2}{\textrm{h}}
$$

and want to convert it into

$$
\dfrac{\textrm{m}^2}{\textrm{s}}
$$

This means we'll need two conversion factors: one for the area units (from $\textrm{km}^2$ to $\textrm{m}^2$) and one for the time units (from $\textrm{h}$ to $\textrm{s}$):

$$
\dfrac{\textrm{km}^2}{\textrm{h}} \cdot \dfrac{?}{?} \cdot \dfrac{?}{?} = \dfrac{\textrm{m}^2}{\textrm{s}}
$$

One of the conversion factors should change $\textrm{km}^2$ to $\textrm{m}^2$.

$$
\dfrac{\cancel{\textrm{km}^2}}{\textrm{h}} \cdot \dfrac{\textrm{m}^2}{\cancel{\textrm{km}^2}} \cdot \dfrac{?}{?} = \dfrac{\textrm{m}^2}{\textrm{s}}
$$

The other conversion factor should change $\textrm{h}$ to $\textrm{s}$.

$$
\dfrac{\cancel{\textrm{km}^2}}{\cancel{\textrm{h}}} \cdot \dfrac{\textrm{m}^2}{\cancel{\textrm{km}^2}} \cdot \dfrac{\cancel{\textrm{h}}}{\textrm{s}} = \dfrac{\textrm{m}^2}{\textrm{s}}
$$

So, the complete conversion formula is

$$
\dfrac{\textrm{km}^2}{\textrm{h}} \cdot \dfrac{\textrm{m}^2}{\textrm{km}^2} \cdot \dfrac{\textrm{h}}{\textrm{s}} = \dfrac{\textrm{m}^2}{\textrm{s}}
$$

Now that we have our conversion formula, we need to determine the correct numbers to use and apply them.

- We are given that the drone maps at a rate of $0.9\,\dfrac{\textrm{km}^2}{\textrm{h}}$.
- There are $1,000$ meters in one kilometer. We can write this as follows:
$1,000\,\dfrac{\textrm{m}}{\textrm{km}}$
To determine the number of square meters in one square kilometer, we square this quantity and apply the rules of exponents, as follows:
$(1, 000\frac{m}{km})^{2}|= 1, 000^{2}\frac{m^{2}}{km^{2}}; = 1, 000, 000\frac{m^{2}}{km^{2}}$
- There are $60\cdot60=3,600$ seconds per hour, or $\dfrac{1}{3,600}$ hours per second. We can write this as follows:
$\dfrac{1}{3,600}\,\dfrac{\textrm{h}}{\textrm{s}}$

Now, applying the conversion formula, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
0.9\frac{km^{2}}{h} &= 0.9\frac{km^{2}}{h} \cdot 1, 000, 000\frac{m^{2}}{km^{2}} \cdot \frac{1}{3, 600}\frac{h}{s} \\
&= (0.9 \cdot 1, 000, 000 \cdot \frac{1}{3, 600})\frac{km^{2}}{h} \cdot \frac{m^{2}}{km^{2}} \cdot \frac{h}{s} \\
&= \frac{900, 000}{3, 600}\frac{m^{2}}{s} \\
&= 250\frac{m^{2}}{s}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the drone maps the area at a rate of

$$
250\,\dfrac{\textrm{m}^2}{\textrm{s}}
$$

## Converting Larger Area Units to Smaller Area Units

**Example:** Convert $33\,\dfrac{\textrm{yd}^2}{\textrm{min}}$ into square feet per second.

**Explanation**

We're given the rate in

$$
\dfrac{\textrm{yd}^2}{\textrm{min}}
$$

and want to convert it into

$$
\dfrac{\textrm{ft}^2}{\textrm{s}}
$$

To do this, we must apply two conversion factors: one for the area units (from $\textrm{yd}^2$ to $\textrm{ft}^2$) and one for the time units (from $\textrm{min}$ to $\textrm{s}$):

$$
\dfrac{\textrm{yd}^2}{\textrm{min}} \cdot \dfrac{?}{?} \cdot \dfrac{?}{?} = \dfrac{\textrm{ft}^2}{\textrm{s}}
$$

One of the conversion factors should change $\textrm{yd}^2$ to $\textrm{ft}^2$.

$$
\dfrac{\cancel{\textrm{yd}^2}}{\textrm{min}} \cdot \dfrac{\textrm{ft}^2}{\cancel{\textrm{yd}^2}} \cdot \dfrac{?}{?} = \dfrac{\textrm{ft}^2}{\textrm{s}}
$$

The other conversion factor should change $\textrm{min}$ to $\textrm{s}$.

$$
\dfrac{\cancel{\textrm{yd}^2}}{\cancel{\textrm{min}}} \cdot \dfrac{\textrm{ft}^2}{\cancel{\textrm{yd}^2}} \cdot \dfrac{\cancel{\textrm{min}}}{\textrm{s}} = \dfrac{\textrm{ft}^2}{\textrm{s}}
$$

So, the complete conversion formula is

$$
\dfrac{\textrm{yd}^2}{\textrm{min}} \cdot \dfrac{\textrm{ft}^2}{\textrm{yd}^2} \cdot \dfrac{\textrm{min}}{\textrm{s}} = \dfrac{\textrm{ft}^2}{\textrm{s}}
$$

Now that we have our conversion formula, we need to determine the correct numbers to use and apply them.

- We are given the quantity $33\,\dfrac{\textrm{yd}^2}{\textrm{min}}$.
- There are $3$ feet per yard. We can write this as follows:
$3\,\dfrac{\textrm{ft}}{\textrm{yd}}$
To determine the number of square feet in one square yard, we square this quantity and apply the rules of exponents, as follows:
$(3\frac{ft}{yd})^{2}|= 3^{2}\frac{ft^{2}}{yd^{2}}; = 9\frac{ft^{2}}{yd^{2}}$
- There are $60$ seconds per minute, or $\dfrac{1}{60}$ minutes per second. We can write this as follows:
$\dfrac{1}{60}\,\dfrac{\textrm{min}}{\textrm{s}}$

Now, applying our conversion formula, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
33\frac{yd^{2}}{min} &= 33\frac{yd^{2}}{min} \cdot 9\frac{ft^{2}}{yd^{2}} \cdot \frac{1}{60}\frac{min}{s} \\
&= (33 \cdot 9 \cdot \frac{1}{60})\frac{yd^{2}}{min} \cdot \frac{ft^{2}}{yd^{2}} \cdot \frac{min}{s} \\
&= \frac{297}{60}\frac{ft^{2}}{s} \\
&= 4.95\frac{ft^{2}}{s}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
33\,\dfrac{\textrm{yd}^2}{\textrm{min}}
$$

is equivalent to

$$
4.95\,\dfrac{\textrm{ft}^2}{\textrm{s}}
$$

<a id="converting-smaller-area-units-to-larger-area-units"></a>
## Converting Smaller Area Units to Larger Area Units

**Example:** A park maintenance robot is clearing leaves at a rate of $132{,}000\,\dfrac{\textrm{ft}^2}{\textrm{h}}$. Express this rate in square meters per minute. Round your answer to the nearest integer.

*Hint: $1\,\textrm{m} \approx 3.28\,\textrm{ft}$*

**Explanation**

We're given the rate in

$$
\dfrac{\textrm{ft}^2}{\textrm{h}}
$$

and we want to convert it to

$$
\dfrac{\textrm{m}^2}{\textrm{min}}
$$

We'll need to multiply by two conversion factors to change both the area unit ($\textrm{ft}^2$ to $\textrm{m}^2$) and the time unit ($\textrm{h}$ to $\textrm{min}$):

$$
\dfrac{\textrm{ft}^2}{\textrm{h}}\cdot \,\dfrac{?}{?} \cdot \,\dfrac{?}{?} = \dfrac{\textrm{m}^2}{\textrm{min}}
$$

One of the conversion factors should change $\textrm{ft}^2$ to $\textrm{m}^2$.

$$
\dfrac{\cancel{\textrm{ft}^2}}{\textrm{h}}\cdot \dfrac{\textrm{m}^2}{\cancel{\textrm{ft}^2}} \cdot \,\dfrac{?}{?} = \dfrac{\textrm{m}^2}{\textrm{min}}
$$

The other conversion factor should change $\textrm{h}$ to $\textrm{min}$.

$$
\dfrac{\cancel{\textrm{ft}^2}}{\cancel{\textrm{h}}}\cdot \dfrac{\textrm{m}^2}{\cancel{\textrm{ft}^2}} \cdot \dfrac{\cancel{\textrm{h}}}{\textrm{min}} = \dfrac{\textrm{m}^2}{\textrm{min}}
$$

So, our conversion formula is

$$
\dfrac{\textrm{ft}^2}{\textrm{h}}\cdot \dfrac{\textrm{m}^2}{\textrm{ft}^2} \cdot \dfrac{\textrm{h}}{\textrm{min}} = \dfrac{\textrm{m}^2}{\textrm{min}}
$$

Now that we have our conversion formula, we need to determine the correct numbers to use and apply them.

- The robot's clearing rate is given as $132{,}000 \, \dfrac{\textrm{ft}^2}{\textrm{h}}$.
- We are given that there are $3.28$ feet per meter. Taking the reciprocal, we find that there are $\dfrac{1}{3.28}$ meters per foot. We can write this as follows:
$\dfrac{1}{3.28} \dfrac{\textrm{m}}{\textrm{ft}}$
To determine the number of square meters in one square foot, we square this quantity and apply the rules of exponents, as follows:
$(\frac{1}{3.28}\frac{m}{ft})^{2}|= \frac{1^{2}}{3.28^{2}}\frac{m^{2}}{ft^{2}}; = \frac{1}{10.7584}\frac{m^{2}}{ft^{2}}$
- There are $60$ minutes per hour, or $\dfrac{1}{60}$ hours per minute. We can write this as follows:
$\dfrac{1}{60} \dfrac{\textrm{h}}{\textrm{min}}$

Now, applying our conversion formula, we have

$$
132, 000\frac{ft^{2}}{h} \mid \approx 132, 000\frac{ft^{2}}{h} \cdot \frac{1}{10.7584}\frac{m^{2}}{ft^{2}} \cdot \frac{1}{60}\frac{h}{min}; = (132, 000 \cdot \frac{1}{10.7584} \cdot \frac{1}{60})\frac{ft^{2}}{h} \cdot \frac{m^{2}}{ft^{2}} \cdot \frac{h}{min}; = \frac{132, 000}{10.7584 \cdot 60}\frac{m^{2}}{min}; = \frac{132, 000}{645.504}\frac{m^{2}}{min}; \approx 204\frac{m^{2}}{min}
$$

rounded to the nearest integer.

Therefore, the park maintenance robot is clearing leaves at a rate of approximately

$$
204\,\dfrac{\textrm{m}^2}{\textrm{min}}
$$

## Converting Between Squared Units of Time

**Example:** The acceleration of a train is measured as $5.1$ meters per second squared. What is this rate in miles per minute squared, rounding to the nearest tenth?

*Hint: $1\,\textrm{mi} \approx 1,609\,\textrm{m}$*

**Explanation**

We are given the units

$$
\dfrac{\textrm{m}}{\textrm{s}^2}
$$

and we want to convert this into

$$
\dfrac{\textrm{mi}}{\textrm{min}^2}
$$

We'll need two conversion factors: one to convert the distance unit ($\textrm{m}$ to $\textrm{mi}$), and one to convert the squared time unit ($\textrm{s}^2$ to $\textrm{min}^2$):

$$
\dfrac{\textrm{m}}{\textrm{s}^2} \cdot \dfrac{?}{?} \cdot \dfrac{?}{?} = \dfrac{\textrm{mi}}{\textrm{min}^2}
$$

One of the conversion factors should change $\textrm{m}$ to $\textrm{mi}$.

$$
\dfrac{\cancel{\textrm{m}}}{\textrm{s}^2} \cdot \dfrac{\textrm{mi}}{\cancel{\textrm{m}}} \cdot \dfrac{?}{?} = \dfrac{\textrm{mi}}{\textrm{min}^2}
$$

The other conversion factor should change $\textrm{s}^2$ to $\textrm{min}^2$.

$$
\dfrac{\cancel{\textrm{m}}}{\cancel{\textrm{s}^2}} \cdot \dfrac{\textrm{mi}}{\cancel{\textrm{m}}} \cdot \dfrac{\cancel{\textrm{s}^2}}{\textrm{min}^2} = \dfrac{\textrm{mi}}{\textrm{min}^2}
$$

So, the complete conversion formula is

$$
\dfrac{\textrm{m}}{\textrm{s}^2} \cdot \dfrac{\textrm{mi}}{\textrm{m}} \cdot \dfrac{\textrm{s}^2}{\textrm{min}^2} = \dfrac{\textrm{mi}}{\textrm{min}^2}
$$

Now that we have our conversion formula, we need to determine the correct numbers to use and apply them.

- We are given the quantity $5.1\,\dfrac{\textrm{m}}{\textrm{s}^2}$.
- There are $1,609$ meters in a mile. Taking the reciprocal, we find that there are $\dfrac{1}{1,609}$ miles per meter. We can write this as follows:
$\dfrac{1}{1,609}\,\dfrac{\textrm{mi}}{\textrm{m}}$
- There are $60\,\textrm{s}$ per $\textrm{min}$. We can write this as follows:
$60\,\dfrac{\textrm{s}}{\textrm{min}}$
To determine the number of square seconds in one square minute, we square this quantity and apply the rules of exponents, as follows:
$60(\frac{s}{min})^{2}|= 60^{2}\frac{s^{2}}{min^{2}}; = 3, 600\frac{s^{2}}{min^{2}}$

Now, applying our conversion formula, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
5.1\frac{m}{s^{2}} &= 5.1\frac{m}{s^{2}} \cdot \frac{1}{1, 609}\frac{mi}{m} \cdot 3, 600\frac{s^{2}}{min^{2}} \\
&= (5.1 \cdot \frac{1}{1, 609} \cdot 3, 600)\frac{m}{s^{2}} \cdot \frac{mi}{m} \cdot \frac{s^{2}}{min^{2}} \\
&= \frac{5.1 \cdot 3, 600}{1, 609}\frac{mi}{min^{2}} \\
&\approx 11.4\frac{mi}{min^{2}}
\end{aligned}
$$

rounded to the nearest tenth.

Therefore, the train is accelerating at a rate of approximately

$$
11.4\,\dfrac{\textrm{mi}}{\textrm{min}^2}
$$
