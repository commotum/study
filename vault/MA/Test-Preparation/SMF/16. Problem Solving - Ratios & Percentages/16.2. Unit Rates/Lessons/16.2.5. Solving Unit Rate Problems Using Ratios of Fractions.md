## Introduction

We can often use unit rates to solve problems. For example, suppose that Jen can type $30$ words in

$$
\dfrac{1}{2}
$$

of a minute. How many words can she type in $5$ minutes?

To solve this problem, we first need to find the unit rate of words per minute. Then, we can multiply the unit rate by $5$ minutes.

To find the unit rate of words per minute, we divide the number of words, $30$, by the number of minutes,

$$
\dfrac{1}{2}
$$

as follows:

$$
30 \div \dfrac{1}{2}
$$

To divide by a fraction, we multiply by the fraction's reciprocal:

$$
\begin{aligned}
30 \div \frac{1}{2} &= 30 \cdot \frac{2}{1} \\
&= 30 \cdot 2 \\
&= 60
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the unit rate is $60$ words per minute. Finally, to find the number of words that Jen can type in $5$ minutes, we multiply the unit rate by $5$ minutes and get

$$
60 \times 5 = 300
$$

Therefore, Jen can type $300$ words in $5$ minutes.

## Problem Solving Using the Ratio of an Integer and a Fraction

**Example:** Miriam is collecting sap from trees so that she can turn it into syrup. If she can collect $2$ cups of sap in $\dfrac{3}{4}$ of an hour, then how many cups of sap can she collect in $4$ hours?

**Explanation**

To find the number of cups of sap that Miriam can collect in $4$ hours, we can first find the unit rate of cups per hour, and then multiply the unit rate by $4$ hours.

To find the unit rate of cups per hour, we divide the number of cups, $2$, by the number of hours,

$$
\dfrac{3}{4}
$$

as follows:

$$
2 \div \dfrac{3}{4}
$$

To divide by a fraction, we multiply by the fraction's reciprocal:

$$
\begin{aligned}
2 \div \frac{3}{4} &= 2 \cdot \frac{4}{3} \\
&= \frac{2}{1} \cdot \frac{4}{3} \\
&= \frac{2 \cdot 4}{1 \cdot 3} \\
&= \frac{8}{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the unit rate is

$$
\dfrac{8}{3}
$$

cups per hour. To find the number of cups of sap that Miriam can collect in $4$ hours, we multiply the unit rate by $4$ hours and get

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{8}{3} \cdot 4 &= \frac{8}{3} \cdot \frac{4}{1} \\
&= \frac{8 \cdot 4}{3 \cdot 1} \\
&= \frac{32}{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we write the resulting improper fraction as a mixed number:

$$
\dfrac{32}{3} = 10 \, \textrm{R} 2 = 10 \, \dfrac{2}{3}
$$

Therefore, Miriam can collect

$$
10 \, \dfrac{2}{3}
$$

cups in $4$ hours.

## Problem Solving Using the Ratio of a Fraction and an Integer

**Example:** Alfred runs $5$ laps around the track in $\dfrac{1}{4}$ of a workout. How many workouts does it take him to run $28$ laps?

**Explanation**

To find the number of workouts it takes Alfred to run $28$ laps, we can first find the unit rate of workouts per lap, and then multiply the unit rate by $28$ laps.

To find the unit rate of workouts per lap, we divide the number of workouts,

$$
\dfrac{1}{4}
$$

by the number of laps, $5$, as follows:

$$
\dfrac{1}{4} \div 5
$$

Dividing a fraction by a whole number is equivalent to multiplying the fraction by the reciprocal of that whole number:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{1}{4} \div 5 &= \frac{1}{4} \cdot \frac{1}{5} \\
&= \frac{1 \cdot 1}{4 \cdot 5} \\
&= \frac{1}{20}
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the unit rate is

$$
\dfrac{1}{20}
$$

workouts per lap. To find the number of workouts it takes Alfred to run $28$ laps, we multiply the unit rate by $28$ laps and get

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{1}{20} \cdot 28 &= \frac{1}{20} \cdot \frac{28}{1} \\
&= \frac{1 \cdot 28}{20 \cdot 1} \\
&= \frac{28}{20} \\
&= \frac{28 \div 4}{20 \div 4} \\
&= \frac{7}{5}
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we write the resulting improper fraction as a mixed number:

$$
\dfrac{7}{5} = 1 \, \textrm{R} 2 = 1 \, \dfrac{2}{5}
$$

Therefore, it takes Alfred

$$
1 \, \dfrac{2}{5}
$$

workouts to run $28$ laps.

## Problem Solving Using the Ratio of a Fraction and a Fraction

**Example:** Sylvia is writing a book. She can write $\dfrac{2}{3}$ of a page in $\dfrac{1}{6}$ of an hour. How many hours will it take her to write $10$ pages?

**Explanation**

To find the number of hours it takes Sylvia to write $10$ pages, we can first find the unit rate of hours per page, and then multiply the unit rate by $10$ pages.

To find the unit rate of hours per page, we divide the number of hours,

$$
\dfrac{1}{6}
$$

by the number of pages,

$$
\dfrac{2}{3}
$$

as follows:

$$
\dfrac{1}{6} \div \dfrac{2}{3}
$$

Dividing one fraction by another is equivalent to multiplying the fraction by the reciprocal of the second fraction:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{1}{6} \div \frac{2}{3} &= \frac{1}{6} \cdot \frac{3}{2} \\
&= \frac{1 \cdot 3}{6 \cdot 2} \\
&= \frac{3}{12} \\
&= \frac{3 \div 3}{12 \div 3} \\
&= \frac{1}{4}
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the unit rate is

$$
\dfrac{1}{4}
$$

hours per page. To find the number of hours it will take Sylvia to write $10$ pages, we multiply the unit rate by $10$ pages and get

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{1}{4} \cdot 10 &= \frac{1}{4} \cdot \frac{10}{1} \\
&= \frac{1 \cdot 10}{4 \cdot 1} \\
&= \frac{10}{4} \\
&= \frac{10 \div 2}{4 \div 2} \\
&= \frac{5}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we write the resulting improper fraction as a mixed number:

$$
\dfrac{5}{2} = 2 \, \textrm{R} 1 = 2 \, \dfrac{1}{2}
$$

Therefore, it will take Sylvia

$$
2 \, \dfrac{1}{2}
$$

hours to write $10$ pages.
