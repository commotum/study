## Introduction

When items are sold or purchased, the final bill is not always the same as the original price of the item.

- Sometimes the final bill is *greater than* the original price because some extra amount is added to the original price. Examples include markups, taxes, commissions, gratuities, and fees.
- Other times, the final bill is *less than* the original price because some amount is subtracted from the original price. Examples include markdowns and discounts.

For example, suppose that Jane buys a shirt from the store for $\[math]20$.

- If there is a $10\%$ tax, then an extra $10\%$ of $\[/math]20$ is added to the bill.
- On the other hand, if there is a $25\%$ discount, then $25\%$ of $\[math]20$ is subtracted from the bill.

## Calculating a Tax or Commission Amount

**Example:** Sergey is a car salesman. Every time he sells a car, he gets a commission of $4\%$. If Sergey sells a car for $\[math]15,000$, then how much does he earn in commission?

**Explanation**

Sergey's commission is $4\%$ of the price of the car. In this case, the price of the car is $\[math]15,000$. So, to calculate how much Sergey earns in commission, we need to find $4\%$ of $\[/math]15,000$.

To do this, we first write $4\%$ as a decimal:

$$
4\% = 4 \div 100 = 0.04
$$

Then, we multiply by $15,000$:

$$
0.04 \times 15,000 = 600
$$

Therefore, Sergey earns $\[math]600$ in commission.

## Calculating a Markdown or Discount Amount

**Example:** John's Jeans is having a sale in which a $20\%$ discount is applied to all pairs of jeans. If a pair of jeans cost $\[math]35$ before the discount is applied, how much is the discount?

**Explanation**

There is a $20\%$ discount on the price of the jeans. Here, the price of the jeans is $\[math]35$. So to calculate the amount of the discount, we need to find $20\%$ of $\[/math]35$.

To do this, we first write $20\%$ as a decimal:

$$
20\% = 20 \div 100 = 0.2
$$

Then, we multiply by $35$:

$$
0.2 \times 35 = 7
$$

So, the amount of the discount is $\[math]7$.

## Calculating a Total Bill After a Tax or Commission

**Example:** Larry bought a computer for $\[math]800$ before tax. If the sales tax was $7\%$, then what was Larry's total bill after tax?

**Explanation**

First, we need to find the amount of tax that Larry must pay. Then, we will add that amount to the price of the computer.

The tax rate is $7\%$ of the price of the computer, and the price of the computer is $\[math]800$. So, to calculate how much tax Larry must pay, we need to find $7\%$ of $\[/math]800$.

To do this, we first write $7\%$ as a decimal:

$$
7\% = 7 \div 100 = 0.07
$$

Then, we multiply by $800$:

$$
0.07 \times 800 = 56
$$

So Larry must pay $\[math]56$ in tax. We add this amount to the price of the computer:

$$
800 + 56 = 856
$$

Therefore, Larry's total bill after tax is $\[/math]856$.

## Calculating a Total Bill After a Markdown or Discount

**Example:** Otto's Outdoorswear is having a sale in which a $15\%$ markdown is applied to all items in the store. If Dillon buys a winter jacket for $\[math]120$ before the markdown is applied, what is Dillon's total bill after the markdown?

**Explanation**

First, we need to find the amount of the markdown. Then, we will subtract that amount from the price of the jacket.

The markdown is $15\%$ of the price of the jacket, and the price of the jacket is $\[math]120$. So, to calculate the amount of the markdown, we need to find $15\%$ of $\[/math]120$.

To do this, we first write $15\%$ as a decimal:

$$
15\% = 15 \div 100 = 0.15
$$

Then, we multiply by $120$:

$$
0.15 \times 120 = 18
$$

So the amount of the markdown is $\[math]18$. We subtract this amount from the price of the jacket:

$$
120 - 18 = 102
$$

Therefore, Dillon's total bill after the markdown is $\[/math]102$.
