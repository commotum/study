## Introduction

The **midpoint** of a line segment is the point that splits it into two segments of equal length. For example, $M$ is the midpoint of $\overline{PQ}$ shown below.

![](<../Source/5.1.6. Midpoints/Images/c20777345badffa71ddd0c9dc40d677e.png>)

The midpoint splits the line segment into two congruent segments,

$$
\overline{PM}\cong \overline{QM}
$$

In addition, the segment $\overline{PQ}$ is twice as long as the segments $\overline{PM}$ and $\overline{QM}$:

$$
PQ = 2PM, \qquad PQ = 2QM
$$

## Using Properties of Midpoints to Calculate an Unknown Value From a Diagram

**Example:** Given that $M$ is the midpoint of $\overline{PQ}$, what is the value of $x$?

![](<../Source/5.1.6. Midpoints/Images/3c54cee56d80b2eb5c554f8f9e9042df.png>)

**Explanation**

Since $M$ is the midpoint of $\overline{PQ}$, the segment $\overline{PQ}$ is twice the length of the segment $\overline{PM}$. So, we have:

$$
\begin{aligned}
2PM &= PQ \\
2(4x - 3) &= 5x + 9 \\
8x - 6 &= 5x + 9
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we subtract $5x$ from both sides of the equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
8x - 6 - 5x &= 5x + 9 - 5x \\
3x - 6 &= 9
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we add $6$ to both sides:

$$
\begin{aligned}
3x - 6 + 6 &= 9 + 6 \\
3x &= 15
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we divide both sides by $3{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{3x}{3} &= \frac{15}{3} \\
x &= 5
\end{aligned}
$$

## Using Properties of Midpoints to Calculate an Unknown Value From a Description

**Example:** The point $M$ is the midpoint of the line segment $\overline{LN}$. What is the value of $x$ if $MN=4x+1$ centimeters and $LM=3x+4$ centimeters?

**Explanation**

We have the following diagram.

![](<../Source/5.1.6. Midpoints/Images/ef40ffe6a115c68a7cd3b45870623dce.png>)

Since $M$ is the midpoint of $\overline{LN}$, the two segments $\overline{LM}$ and $\overline{MN}$ have the same length. So, we have:

$$
\begin{aligned}
LM &= MN \\
3x + 4 &= 4x + 1 \\
3x + 4 - 3x &= 4x + 1 - 3x \\
4 &= x + 1 \\
4 - 1 &= x + 1 - 1 \\
3 &= x \\
x &= 3
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
x = 3\,\textrm{cm}
$$

.

## The Midpoint Formula

Let's find the midpoint $M$ of the line segment $\overline{AB}$ on a number line, if $A$ corresponds to $x_1= 3$ and $B$ corresponds to $x_2=7$, as depicted below.

![](<../Source/5.1.6. Midpoints/Images/edd6e2b2e9e1fc1bccf39f2d2c5e8e0a.png>)

The midpoint of a line segment splits it into two segments of equal length.
To find the number $x_m$ corresponding to $M$, we use the following **midpoint formula**:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_{m} &= \frac{x_{1} + x_{2}}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Substituting $x_1=3$ and $x_2=7$, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_{m} &= \frac{3 + 7}{2} \\
&= \frac{10}{2} \\
&= 5
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the midpoint $M$ corresponds to $x_m=5$.

![](<../Source/5.1.6. Midpoints/Images/16735ff8be8087704c40af4e2d716fae.png>)

## Finding a Midpoint Using the Midpoint Formula

**Example:** Which number corresponds to the midpoint of the line segment $\overline{AB}$ shown below?

![](<../Source/5.1.6. Midpoints/Images/fef9c58e124f50b4982b2c4a889f4873.png>)

**Explanation**

The point $A$ corresponds to the number $x_1=-1$ while $B$ corresponds to $x_2=5$.

To find the number $x_m$ corresponding to the midpoint $M$, we use the midpoint formula:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_{m} &= \frac{x_{1} + x_{2}}{2} \\
&= \frac{-1 + 5}{2} \\
&= \frac{4}{2} \\
&= 2
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the midpoint $M$ corresponds to $x_m=2$.

![](<../Source/5.1.6. Midpoints/Images/b2598621895bbcfb93d4189c20efa30e.png>)

<a id="using-the-midpoint-formula-to-calculate-an-endpoint"></a>
## Using the Midpoint Formula to Calculate an Endpoint

**Example:** For the diagram depicted below, which number corresponds to the point $R$ if $M$ is the midpoint of the line segment $\overline{RS}$?

![](<../Source/5.1.6. Midpoints/Images/664a6807e2f78bd6dd90552b90b08403.png>)

**Explanation**

From the picture, we can see that the point $S$ corresponds to the number $x_2=3$ while the midpoint $M$ corresponds to $x_m=1$.

To find the number $x_1$ corresponding to the point $R$, we first set up an equation using the midpoint formula:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_{m} &= \frac{x_{1} + x_{2}}{2} \\
1 &= \frac{x_{1} + 3}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we solve for $x_1$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
1 &= \frac{x_{1} + 3}{2} \\
2 \cdot 1 &= x_{1} + 3 \\
2 &= x_{1} + 3 \\
2 - 3 &= x_{1} \\
- 1 &= x_{1}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the point $R$ corresponds to $x_1=-1$.

![](<../Source/5.1.6. Midpoints/Images/022ba73d779f310db375fcaffec3f305.png>)
