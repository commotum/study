## Determining Areas of Similar Shapes

**Example:** The radius of circle $A$ is three times the radius of circle $B$. If the area of circle $B$ is $15\,\textrm{cm}^2$, what is the area of circle $A$?

**Explanation**

Recall that all circles are similar.

If two shapes are similar with scale factor $k$, then the ratio of the two areas is equal to $k^2$.

Since circle $A$ has a radius that is $3$ times the radius of circle $B$, circle $A$ is larger than circle $B$, and we have $k=3$.

Therefore, the ratio of the areas is

$$
k^2 = 3^2 = 9
$$

and we have

$$
\dfrac{\mathcal{A}_A}{\mathcal{A}_B} = 9
$$

Solving for $\mathcal{A}_A$ and substituting in

$$
\mathcal{A}_B = 15\,\textrm{cm}^2
$$

we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
A_{A} &= 9 \cdot A_{B} \\
&= 9 \cdot 15cm^{2} \\
&= 135cm^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Determining Areas of Similar Shapes With Unknown Parameters

**Example:** Circle $R$ has a radius of $4t$ and circle $S$ has a radius of $28t$, where $t$ is a positive constant. The area of circle $S$ is how many times the area of circle $R$?

**Explanation**

Recall that all circles are similar.

If two shapes are similar with scale factor $k$, then the ratio of the two areas is equal to $k^2$.

Here, circle $S$ is similar to circle $R$ with a scale factor of

$$
k = \dfrac{28t}{4t} = 7
$$

Therefore, the area of $S$ is

$$
k^2 = 7^2 = 49
$$

times larger than the area of $R$.

## Determining the Area of a Scaled Rectangle Given a Percentage Increase

**Example:** A rectangular banner has an area of $280$ square feet. A new version of the banner is built in which both the length and width are increased by $25\%$. What is the area, in square feet, of the new banner?

**Explanation**

When an object is scaled by a factor of $k$ in all dimensions, its area is scaled by a factor of $k^2$.

In our case, the increase is $25\%$, so the scale factor is

$$
k = 1 + \dfrac{25}{100} = 1.25
$$

Thus, the area of the new banner will be

$$
k^2 = \left(1.25 \right)^2 = 1.5625
$$

times the area of the old one.

Therefore, the area of the new banner is

$$
\begin{aligned}
Area &= k^{2} \cdot 280 \\
&= 1.5625 \cdot 280 \\
&= 437.5
\end{aligned}
$$

square feet.

## Working With Scale Models

**Example:** A teacher builds a scale foam model of a classroom. The actual classroom has an area of $1{,}120\,\textrm{ft}^2$. The length of each side of the model classroom is $\dfrac{1}{20}$ times the length of the corresponding side of the actual classroom. What is the area of the model classroom?

**Explanation**

If two shapes are similar with scale factor $k$, then the ratio of the two areas is equal to $k^2$.

The length of each side of the model classroom is

$$
\dfrac{1}{20}
$$

times the length of the corresponding side of the actual classroom.

So, the length of each side of the actual classroom is $20$ times the length of the corresponding side of the model classroom, and we have $k=20$.

Therefore, the ratio of the areas is

$$
k^2 = (20)^2=400
$$

and we have

$$
\dfrac{\mathcal{A}_{\text{actual}}}{\mathcal{A}_{\text{model}}}=400
$$

Solving for $\mathcal{A}_{\text{model}}$ and substituting in

$$
\mathcal{A}_{\text{actual}}=1{,}120\,\textrm{ft}^2
$$

we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
A_{model} &= \frac{A_{actual}}{400} \\
&= \frac{1,120ft^{2}}{400} \\
&= 2.8ft^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Determining Areas Between Similar Shapes

**Example:** The diagram above shows two circles, where point $V$ and the center of each circle lie on $\overline{UW}$. The ratio of $UV$ to $VW$ is $9:1$. If the area of the smaller circle is $81\,\textrm{cm}^2$, what is the area of the shaded region?

![](<../Source/3.8.8. Relating Length and Area in Similar Shapes/Images/54d1982788da8e4a0f7c6267621a8691.png>)

**Explanation**

Recall that all circles are similar.

If two shapes are similar with scale factor $k$, then the ratio of the two areas is equal to $k^2$.

First, since $\overline{UW}$ joins two points on the larger circle and passes through its center, it is a diameter.
Similarly, since $\overline{UV}$ joins two points on the smaller circle and passes through its center, it is a diameter.

Given that

$$
UV: VW = 9: 1
$$

we can let $UV = 9k$ and $VW = 1k$ for some value $k$. Then, the larger circle is similar to the smaller circle with a scale factor of

$$
\begin{aligned}
k &= \frac{UW}{UV} \\
&= \frac{UV + VW}{UV} \\
&= \frac{9k + 1k}{9k} \\
&= \frac{10k}{9k} \\
&= \frac{10}{9}
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus, the ratio of the areas is

$$
k^2 = \left(\dfrac{10}{9}\right)^2 = \dfrac{100}{81}
$$

and we have

$$
\dfrac{\mathcal{A}_{\text{larger}}}{\mathcal{A}_{\text{smaller}}} = \dfrac{100}{81}
$$

Solving for $\mathcal{A}_{\text{larger}}$ and substituting in

$$
\mathcal{A}_{\text{smaller}} = 81\,\textrm{cm}^2
$$

we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
A_{larger} &= \frac{100}{81} \cdot A_{smaller} \\
&= \frac{100}{81} \cdot 81cm^{2} \\
&= 100cm^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the area of the shaded region is

$$
\begin{aligned}
A_{shaded} &= A_{larger} - A_{smaller} \\
&= 100 - 81 \\
&= 19cm^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$
