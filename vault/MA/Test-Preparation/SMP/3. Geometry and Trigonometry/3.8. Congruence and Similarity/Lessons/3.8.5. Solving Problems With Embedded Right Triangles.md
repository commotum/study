## Introduction

The math section of the SAT exam may involve problems about embedded right triangles that test the ability to identify similar triangles hidden within larger figures. These problems blend geometry and algebra, requiring us to set up proportions or use the Pythagorean theorem.

In this topic, we’ll practice spotting right triangles within compound diagrams and using their relationships to find missing sides, angles, and ratios.

## Finding a Hypotenuse Given a Leg From a Similar Triangle and the Similarity Factor

**Example:** In the figure shown, $\overline{RT}$ is parallel to $\overline{PU}$. What is the length of $\overline{QU}$?

*(Note: Figure not drawn to scale)*

![](<../Source/3.8.5. Solving Problems With Embedded Right Triangles/Images/960abd966f911ecbce946a128c8c026a.png>)

**Explanation**

We are given that $\overline{RT} \parallel \overline{PU}$. Hence, $\angle TRQ$ and $\angle P$ are both right angles, and triangles $\triangle QUP$ and $\triangle QTR$ are similar by AA Similarity Criterion.

From the diagram, we have:

- $QR = 6$
- $RT = 3$
- $PU = 12$
- $QU$ is unknown

We can now write a proportion based on corresponding sides:

$$
\dfrac{QU}{QT} = \dfrac{PU}{RT} =\dfrac{12}{3} = 4
$$

So, $QU=4QT$. We use the Pythagorean Theorem to find $QT{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
QT &= \sqrt{6^{2} + 3^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{36 + 9} \\
&= \sqrt{45} \\
&= 3\sqrt{5}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
QU &= 4QT \\
&= 4 \cdot 3\sqrt{5} \\
&= 12\sqrt{5}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding Ratios of Side Lengths in Embedded Similar Right Triangles

**Example:** In right triangle $DEF$, angle $E$ is the right angle and $FE = 180$. Point $H$ on side $\overline{DF}$ is connected to point $K$ on side $\overline{DE}$ such that line segment $\overline{HK}$ is parallel to side $\overline{EF}$ and $EK = 2\,DK$. What is the length of the line segment $\overline{HK}$?

**Explanation**

Since $\triangle DEF$ is a right triangle with the right angle at $E$, and $\overline{HK} \parallel \overline{FE}$ (where point $H$ lies on side $\overline{DF}$ and point $K$ on side $\overline{DE}$), it can be represented as follows:

![](<../Source/3.8.5. Solving Problems With Embedded Right Triangles/Images/0d1a6f9f4c52b203f0a8ac7105a9906c.png>)

In the triangles $\triangle DKH$ and $\triangle DEF$, there are two pairs of congruent angles:

- $\angle HKD \cong \angle FED$ since they are both right angles, and
- $\angle D$ is common to both triangles.

Thus, $\triangle DKH \sim \triangle DEF$ by the AA similarity criterion. As a result, we have

$$
\dfrac{HK}{FE} = \dfrac{DK}{DE}
$$

Since $EK = 2\,DK$, the entire side $DE$ is

$$
\begin{aligned}
DE &= DK + EK \\
&= DK + 2DK \\
&= 3DK
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, since $FE = 180$, the length of $\overline{HK}$ is

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{HK}{FE} &= \frac{DK}{DE} \\
\frac{FG}{180} &= \frac{DK}{3DK} \\
\frac{HK}{180} &= \frac{1}{3} \\
HK &= \frac{1}{3} \cdot 180 \\
&= 60
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding Areas of Embedded Right Triangles

**Example:** In the figure, $SU=5$ units, $ST=4$ units, and $TW=12$ units. What is the area, in square units, of $\triangle{SVW}$?

![](<../Source/3.8.5. Solving Problems With Embedded Right Triangles/Images/8a3b6cc549d162a438993642e5bdbca9.png>)

Note: Figure not drawn to scale.

**Explanation**

Applying the Pythagorean Theorem to $\triangle{STU}$, we can obtain $TU$ as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
SU^{2} &= ST^{2} + TU^{2} \\
5^{2} &= 4^{2} + TU^{2} \\
25 &= 16 + TU^{2} \\
TU^{2} &= 9 \\
TU &= 3
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, note that

$$
\begin{aligned}
SW &= ST + TW \\
&= 4 + 12 \\
&= 16
\end{aligned}
$$

From the diagram, we can see that $\triangle{SWV}$ and $\triangle{STU}$ are right triangles that have the non-right angle $\angle{S}$ as a common angle. Therefore, $\triangle{SWV} \sim \triangle{STU}$ by the AA Similarity Criterion. As a consequence of the similarity, we obtain

$$
\dfrac{VW}{TU} = \dfrac{SW}{ST}
$$

Substituting the values for $TU$, $SW$, and $ST$ into the equality above, and solving for $VW$, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{VW}{TU} &= \frac{SW}{ST} \\
\frac{VW}{3} &= \frac{16}{4} \\
\frac{VW}{3} &= 4 \\
VW &= 12
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, the formula for the area of a triangle is

$$
\mathcal{A} = \dfrac{b \cdot h}{2}
$$

where $b$ is a base and $h$ is the corresponding height.

In the case of $\triangle{SVW}$, its base is

$$
b = SW = 16
$$

and its corresponding height is

$$
h = VW = 12
$$

Therefore, the area of $\triangle{SVW}$ is

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= \frac{b \cdot h}{2} \\
&= \frac{16 \cdot 12}{2} \\
&= 96square units
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding a Ratio of Side Lengths in Embedded Similar Right Triangles

**Example:** In $\triangle PQR$, the measure of $\angle Q$ is $90^{\circ}$ and $\overline{QS}$ is an altitude of the triangle. The length of $\overline{PQ}$ is $12$ and the length of $\overline{PR}$ is $20$ greater than the length of $\overline{PQ}$. What is the value of $\dfrac{QR}{QS}$?

**Explanation**

Given that $\triangle PQR$ is a right triangle with

$$
m\angle Q = 90^\circ
$$

$PQ = 12$, and $\overline{QS}$ is the altitude, it can be represented as follows:

![](<../Source/3.8.5. Solving Problems With Embedded Right Triangles/Images/3aec89c761b8af2f5b50954ff8608115.png>)

Because $\overline{PR}$ is $20$ units longer than $\overline{PQ}$, we compute:

$$
\begin{aligned}
PR &= PQ + 20 \\
&= 12 + 20 \\
&= 32
\end{aligned}
$$

Hence, in the triangles $\triangle PSQ$ and $\triangle PQR$, there are two pairs of congruent angles:

- $\angle QSP \cong \angle RQP$ since they are both right angles, and
- $\angle P$ is common to both triangles.

Therefore, $\triangle PSQ \sim \triangle PQR$ by the AA similarity criterion. As a result, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{QR}{QS} &= \frac{PR}{PQ} \\
&= \frac{32}{12} \\
&= \frac{8}{3}
\end{aligned}
$$
