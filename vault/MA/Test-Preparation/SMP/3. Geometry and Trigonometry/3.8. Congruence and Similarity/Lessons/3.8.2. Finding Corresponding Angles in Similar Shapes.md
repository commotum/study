## Determining Measures of Corresponding Angles in Similar Triangles

**Example:** Triangles $M N P$ and $Q R S$ are congruent, where $M$, $N$, and $P$ correspond to $Q$, $R$, and $S$, respectively. The measure of angle $M$ is $30^{\circ}$ and the measure of angle $N$ is $50^{\circ}$. What is the measure of angle $Q$?

**Explanation**

Since $\triangle MNP \cong \triangle QRS$, with the vertex correspondence

$$
M \leftrightarrow Q
$$

$$
N \leftrightarrow R
$$

and

$$
P \leftrightarrow S
$$

their corresponding angles must also be congruent.

Therefore, the measure of angle $Q$ is

$$
m\angle Q = m\angle M = 30^\circ
$$

## Determining Measures of Corresponding Angles in Similar Triangles Given a Ratio of Sides

**Example:** Quadrilateral $E'F'G'H'$ is similar to quadrilateral $EFGH$, with $E$, $F$, $G$, and $H$ corresponding to $E'$, $F'$, $G'$, and $H'$, respectively. The measure of $\angle{E}$ is $65^{\circ}$, the measure of $\angle{F}$ is $50^{\circ}$, and the measure of $\angle{G}$ is $85^{\circ}$. Each side of $E'F'G'H'$ is $3$ times the length of the corresponding side in $EFGH$. What is the measure of $\angle{E'}$?

**Explanation**

Two quadrilaterals are similar if there is a one-to-one correspondence between their vertices such that their corresponding angles are congruent and their corresponding sides are proportional.

We are given that $E'F'G'H'$ is similar to $EFGH$, where vertex $E'$ corresponds to vertex $E$. Therefore, $\angle{E'}$ is congruent to $\angle{E}$, that is,

$$
m\angle{E'} = m\angle{E}
$$

Since we are given that

$$
m\angle{E} = 65^\circ
$$

we can conclude that

$$
m\angle{E'} = 65^\circ
$$

## Finding an Angle Given Two Other Angles in Similar Right Triangles

**Example:** Right triangles $M N O$ and $P Q R$ are similar, where $M$ corresponds to $P$. If the measure of angle $N$ is $35^{\circ}$, what is the measure of angle $P$?

**Explanation**

We are told that

- the triangles $MNO$ and $PQR$ are right triangles,
- $\triangle MNO \sim \triangle PQR$,
- $M$ corresponds to $P$, which implies $m\angle M = m\angle P$,
- and $m\angle N = 35^\circ$.

First, let's find the value of $m\angle M$.

Since triangle $MNO$ is a right triangle and

$$
m\angle N = 35^\circ
$$

the right angle cannot be at $M$ because it is not among the given choices. Therefore, the right angle must be

$$
m\angle O = 90^\circ
$$

Now that

$$
m\angle O = 90^\circ
$$

and

$$
m\angle N = 35^\circ
$$

we find $m\angle M$ by subtracting from the triangle angle sum:

$$
\begin{aligned}
m∠M &= 180^{∘} - (m∠O + m∠N) \\
&= 180^{∘} - (90^{∘} + 35^{∘}) \\
&= 180^{∘} - 125^{∘} \\
&= 55^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the measure of angle $P$ is

$$
m\angle P = m\angle M = 55^\circ
$$

## Measures of Corresponding Angles of Similar Triangles in the Coordinate Plane

**Example:** Triangles $\triangle UVW$ and $\triangle LMN$ are plotted in the $xy$-plane. Triangle $\triangle UVW$ has vertices $U$, $V$, and $W$ at $(6,2)$, $(6,7)$, and $(10,2)$, respectively. Triangle $\triangle LMN$ has vertices $L$, $M$, and $N$ at $(6,2)$, $(6,2+5k)$, and $(6+4k,2)$, respectively, where $k$ is a positive constant. If $m\angle W = t^\circ - 15^\circ$, what is the measure of $\angle M$?

**Explanation**

First, let's sketch the two triangles described.

![](<../Source/3.8.2. Finding Corresponding Angles in Similar Shapes/Images/9fb75ac4b213e26b2acd9b112d42ac6e.png>)

Notice that

- the segments $\overline{UV}$ and $\overline{LM}$ both lie on the vertical line $x=6$, and
- the segments $\overline{UW}$ and $\overline{LN}$ both lie on the horizontal line $y=2$.

So, the angles $\angle U$ and $\angle L$ in $\triangle UVW$ and $\triangle LMN$, respectively, are both enclosed by these perpendicular lines; hence, they are right angles.

We find the measure of $\angle V$ using the fact that the sum of interior angles in a triangle equals $180^\circ{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
m∠U + m∠V + m∠W &= 180^{∘} \\
90^{∘} + m∠V + (t^{∘} - 15^{∘}) &= 180^{∘} \\
75^{∘} + t^{∘} + m∠V &= 180^{∘} \\
m∠V &= 180^{∘} - 75^{∘} - t^{∘} \\
&= 105^{∘} - t^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, the measures of the legs of the triangle $\triangle UVW$ are

$$
\begin{aligned}
UV &= 7 - 2 = 5, \\
UW &= 10 - 6 = 4
\end{aligned}
$$

and the measures of the legs of the triangle $\triangle LMN$ are

$$
\begin{aligned}
LM &= (2 + 5k) - 2 = 5k, \\
LN &= (6 + 4k) - 6 = 4k
\end{aligned}
$$

Hence, we have a pair of congruent angles,

$$
m\angle U = m\angle L = 90^\circ
$$

and the lengths of the sides enclosing the congruent angles are in proportion:

$$
\dfrac{UV}{LM} = \dfrac{5}{5k} = \dfrac{1}{k} = \dfrac{4}{4k} = \dfrac{UW}{LN}
$$

Consequently, $\triangle UVW \sim \triangle LMN$ by the $SAS$ similarity criterion, with the vertex correspondence

$$
U \leftrightarrow L, \qquad V \leftrightarrow M, \qquad W \leftrightarrow N
$$

Therefore, the measure of angle $M$ is

$$
m\angle M = m\angle V = 105^\circ - t^\circ
$$
