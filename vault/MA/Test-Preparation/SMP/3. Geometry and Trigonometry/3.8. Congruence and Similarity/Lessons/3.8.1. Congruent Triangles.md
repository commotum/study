## Introduction

Questions about congruent triangles often test whether we can match corresponding sides and angles using a particular congruence criterion.

In this topic, we’ll practice spotting congruence, using angle-sum facts to find unknowns, and transferring equal parts to solve for targeted angles and sides.

<a id="finding-angles-in-congruent-triangles"></a>
## Finding Angles in Congruent Triangles

**Example:** Triangles $T U W$ and $D E G$ are congruent, where $T$, $U$, and $W$ correspond to $D$, $E$, and $G$, respectively. The measure of angle $T$ is $39^{\circ}$ and the measure of angle $U$ is $28^{\circ}$. What is the measure of angle $G$?

**Explanation**

Let's draw a sketch of the situation described.

![](<../Source/3.8.1. Congruent Triangles/Images/597ef469467d5b86d6e8f927f5351ab7.png>)

First, we find the measure of $\angle W$ using the fact that the sum of interior angles in a triangle equals $180^\circ{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
m∠T + m∠U + m∠W &= 180^{∘} \\
39^{∘} + 28^{∘} + m∠W &= 180^{∘} \\
67^{∘} + m∠W &= 180^{∘} \\
m∠W &= 180^{∘} - 67^{∘} \\
&= 113^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, since $\triangle TUW \cong \triangle DEG$, with the vertex correspondence

$$
T \leftrightarrow D, \qquad U \leftrightarrow E, \qquad W \leftrightarrow G
$$

their corresponding angles must also be congruent.

Therefore, the measure of angle $G$ is

$$
m \angle G = m \angle W = 113^\circ
$$

## Finding Angles in Congruent Triangles in the Coordinate Plane

**Example:** Triangles $\triangle WXY$ and $\triangle UVZ$ are plotted in the $xy$-plane. Triangle $\triangle WXY$ has vertices $W$, $X$, and $Y$ at $(3,2)$, $(3,8)$, and $(11,2)$, respectively. Triangle $\triangle UVZ$ has vertices $U$, $V$, and $Z$ at $(-2,-1)$, $(-2,-7)$, and $(-10,-1)$, respectively. If $m\angle X = y^\circ+11^\circ$, what is the measure of $\angle V$?

**Explanation**

First, let's sketch the two triangles described.

![](<../Source/3.8.1. Congruent Triangles/Images/735c5b806ebe9b13c0321829c5ca4898.png>)

Notice that

- the segments $\overline{WX}$ and $\overline{UV}$ both lie on vertical lines, and
- the segments $\overline{WY}$ and $\overline{UZ}$ both lie on horizontal lines.

So, the angles $\angle W$ and $\angle U$ in $\triangle WXY$ and $\triangle UVZ$, respectively, are both enclosed by these perpendicular lines; hence, they are right angles.

We find the measure of $\angle Y$ using the fact that the sum of interior angles in a triangle equals $180^\circ{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
m∠W + m∠X + m∠Y &= 180^{∘} \\
90^{∘} + (y^{∘} + 11^{∘}) + m∠Y &= 180^{∘} \\
101^{∘} + y^{∘} + m∠Y &= 180^{∘} \\
m∠Y &= 180^{∘} - 101^{∘} - y^{∘} \\
&= 79^{∘} - y^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, the measures of the legs of the triangle $\triangle WXY$ are

$$
\begin{aligned}
WX &= 8 - 2 = 6, \\
WY &= 11 - 3 = 8
\end{aligned}
$$

and the measures of the legs of the triangle $\triangle UVZ$ are

$$
\begin{aligned}
UV &= -1 - (-7) = 6, \\
UZ &= -2 - (-10) = 8
\end{aligned}
$$

Hence, we have a pair of congruent angles,

$$
m\angle W = m\angle U = 90^\circ
$$

and the corresponding sides enclosing the congruent angles are congruent:

$$
\begin{aligned}
WX &= 6 = UV \\
WY &= 8 = UZ
\end{aligned}
$$

Consequently, $\triangle WXY \cong \triangle UVZ$ by the SAS congruence criterion, with the vertex correspondence

$$
W \leftrightarrow U,\qquad X \leftrightarrow V,\qquad Y \leftrightarrow Z
$$

Therefore, the measure of angle $V$ is

$$
m\angle V = m\angle Y = 79^\circ - y^\circ
$$

## Identifying Information Needed to Prove Congruence of Triangles (Diagram)

**Example:** Triangles $ABC$ and $RST$ each have a side measuring $4$ and a side measuring $9$, as shown above. Which of the following additional pieces of information is sufficient to prove that these triangles are congruent?

![](<../Source/3.8.1. Congruent Triangles/Images/42faefc25991756f6052cf66304ee5d1.png>)

1. The measure of $\angle{T}$ is $20^\circ$.
2. The measures of $\angle B$ and $\angle S$ are equal.
3. The measures of $\angle A$ and $\angle R$ are equal.
4. No additional information is needed.

*(Note: Images not drawn to scale)*

**Explanation**

To prove *congruence* between two triangles, we can use any of the following congruence criteria:

- **SSS**: all three pairs of corresponding sides are equal
- **SAS**: two pairs of corresponding sides and the included angle are equal
- **ASA**: two pairs of corresponding angles and the included side are equal
- **AAS**: two pairs of corresponding angles and a non-included side are equal
- **HL**: In right triangles, the hypotenuse and one leg are equal

In the triangles $\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle RST$, there are two pairs of congruent sides:

- $AB = RS = 4$
- $AC = RT = 9$

Thus, we have two pairs of equal corresponding sides. This alone is not enough to prove congruency.

With that in mind, let’s analyze each piece of additional information in turn.

- Consider option I (The measure of $\angle{T}$ is $20^\circ$). Knowing the measure of a single angle only in one triangle is insufficient to determine congruence between two triangles. ${\color{red}\times}$

![](<../Source/3.8.1. Congruent Triangles/Images/346993968fa334ff5b12447ca42c0803.png>)

- Consider option II (The measures of $\angle B$ and $\angle S$ are equal). Knowing that two sides and a non-included angle of two triangles have equal measures is insufficient to conclude that the triangles are congruent. In other words, there is no Side-Side-Angle criterion for congruent triangles. ${\color{red}\times}$

![](<../Source/3.8.1. Congruent Triangles/Images/efd8259779ade9f3e4bb14ef11de5a44.png>)

- Consider option III (The measures of $\angle A$ and $\angle R$ are equal). These are corresponding angles in the two triangles. If they are equal in measures, the triangles must be congruent by the Side-Angle-Side (SAS) criterion. ${\color{green}\checkmark}$

![](<../Source/3.8.1. Congruent Triangles/Images/023dfbf1c14ae1324658bd92f8b0961f.png>)

Therefore, the correct answer is "III only".

## Identifying Information Needed to Determine Congruence of Triangles

**Example:** In triangles $ABC$ and $GHI$, sides $\overline{BC}$ and $\overline{HI}$ each have length $6$, and angles $\angle{A}$ and $\angle{G}$ each have measure $90^\circ$. Which additional piece of information is sufficient to prove that triangle $ABC$ is congruent to triangle $GHI$?

1. The lengths of $\overline{AB}$ and $\overline{GH}$ are equal.
2. The length of $\overline{AB}$.
3. The measure of $\angle{I}$.
4. No additional information is needed.

**Explanation**

To prove *congruence* between two triangles, we can use any of the following congruence criteria:

- **SSS**: all three pairs of corresponding sides are equal
- **SAS**: two pairs of corresponding sides and the included angle are equal
- **ASA**: two pairs of corresponding angles and the included side are equal
- **AAS**: two pairs of corresponding angles and a non-included side are equal
- **HL**: In right triangles, the hypotenuse and one leg are equal

![](<../Source/3.8.1. Congruent Triangles/Images/6b3a90ba0ac3f887f857957fa38a7a27.png>)

In the triangles $\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle GHI$, there is one pair of congruent angles and one pair of congruent sides:

- $m\angle A = m\angle G = 90^\circ$
- $BC = HI = 25$

Thus, we have a pair of congruent corresponding angles and a pair of congruent corresponding sides. This alone is not enough to prove congruency.

With that in mind, let’s analyze each piece of additional information in turn.

- Consider option I (The lengths of $\overline{AB}$ and $\overline{GH}$ are equal). Then the triangles *must* be congruent by the Hypotenuse-Leg (HL) criterion. ${\color{green}\checkmark}$

![](<../Source/3.8.1. Congruent Triangles/Images/7a7bfafeb74ef2377a645fb6ac9e22de.png>)

- Consider option II (The length of $\overline{AB}$). Knowing the length of a single side only in one triangle is insufficient to determine congruence between two triangles. ${\color{red}\times}$

![](<../Source/3.8.1. Congruent Triangles/Images/e9bced72dde488ee19dd695dc6adee95.png>)

- Consider option III (The measure of $\angle{I}$). Knowing the measure of a single angle only in one triangle is insufficient to determine congruence between two triangles. ${\color{red}\times}$

![](<../Source/3.8.1. Congruent Triangles/Images/0d59905d129ccd0f9da050330f69b98e.png>)

Therefore, the correct answer is "I only".
