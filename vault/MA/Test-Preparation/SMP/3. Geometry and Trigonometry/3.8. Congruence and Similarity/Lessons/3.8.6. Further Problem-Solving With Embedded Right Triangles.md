## Introduction

Embedded right-triangle problems often hide similar triangles created by altitudes, parallel lines, or split segments.

In this topic, we’ll practice spotting those hidden right triangles and setting up the correct ratios to solve problems.

## Finding Heights in Similar Embedded Right Triangles

**Example:** In the figure above, what is the length of $\overline{BD}$?

![](<../Source/3.8.6. Further Problem-Solving With Embedded Right Triangles/Images/b6ca6f16bf659e5cbc2f898d5177607e.png>)

**Explanation**

We are given a right triangle $\triangle{ABC}$ with legs of length $AB = 5$ and $BC = 12$, and an altitude $\overline{BD}$ dropped from $B$ to hypotenuse $\overline{AC}$.

First, we calculate the length of the hypotenuse $\overline{AC}$. According to the Pythagorean Theorem,

$$
AC^2 = AB^2 + BC^2
$$

Therefore, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
AC &= \sqrt{AB^{2} + BC^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{5^{2} + 12^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{25 + 144} \\
&= \sqrt{169} \\
&= 13
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, consider the triangles $\triangle ADB$ and $\triangle ABC$. Both triangles share $\angle A$, and both contain a right angle: $\angle ADB$ and $\angle ABC$, respectively.

![](<../Source/3.8.6. Further Problem-Solving With Embedded Right Triangles/Images/5590e7e1e6e3f1b94279911374b534db.png>)

So, by the AA similarity criterion, the triangles are similar.

Since $\triangle ADB \sim \triangle ABC$, the ratios of corresponding sides are equal. In particular,

$$
\dfrac{DB}{BC} = \dfrac{AB}{AC}
$$

Substituting in the known values, we have

$$
\dfrac{BD}{12} = \dfrac{5}{13}
$$

Finally, solving for $BD$, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
BD &= \frac{5}{13} \cdot 12 \\
&= \frac{60}{13} \\
&\approx 4.62
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the length of $\overline{BD}$ is $4.62$.

## Finding Side Lengths in Similar Embedded Right Triangles

**Example:** In the figure above, $TX=8$ and $RX=15$. What is the length of $\overline{XY}$?

![](<../Source/3.8.6. Further Problem-Solving With Embedded Right Triangles/Images/ef4d724078082de7c5338f0bfe9f188c.png>)

*Note: Figure not drawn to scale.*

**Explanation**

Applying the Pythagorean Theorem to $\triangle{RXT}$, we can obtain $RT$ as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
RT^{2} &= TX^{2} + RX^{2} \\
RT^{2} &= 8^{2} + 15^{2} \\
RT^{2} &= 64 + 225 \\
RT^{2} &= 289 \\
RT &= 17
\end{aligned}
$$

From the diagram, we can see that $\triangle{RYT}$ and $\triangle{RXT}$ are right triangles that have the non-right angle $\angle{R}$ as a common angle. Therefore, $\triangle{RYT} \sim \triangle{RXT}$ by the AA Similarity Criterion. As a consequence of the similarity, we obtain

$$
\dfrac{RY}{RT} = \dfrac{RT}{RX}
$$

Substituting the values for $RT$ and $RX$ into the equality above, and solving for $RY$, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{RY}{17} &= \frac{17}{15} \\
\frac{RY}{17} &= \frac{17}{15} \\
RY &= 17 \cdot \frac{17}{15} \\
RY &= \frac{289}{15}
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, note that

$$
XY = RY - RX
$$

Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
XY &= \frac{289}{15} - 15 \\
&= \frac{289}{15} - \frac{225}{15} \\
&= \frac{289 - 225}{15} \\
&= \frac{64}{15}
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the length of $\overline{XY}$ is

$$
\bbox[4pt,border: 1px solid lightgray]{\dfrac{64}{15}}
$$

## Determining Possible Values of a Side Length in Embedded Right Triangles

**Example:** In the figure above, $\triangle{XYZ}$ has an area between $70$ and $90$, inclusive, and $q$ is an integer. What is the largest possible value of $p$?

![](<../Source/3.8.6. Further Problem-Solving With Embedded Right Triangles/Images/b2b98422ee75d654497164d3aa737338.png>)

Note: Figure not drawn to scale.

**Explanation**

The formula for the area of a triangle is

$$
\mathcal{A} = \dfrac{b \cdot h}{2}
$$

where $b$ is a base and $h$ is the corresponding height.

In the case of $\triangle{XYZ}$, from the diagram we can see that its base is

$$
b = 3 + 17 = 20
$$

and its corresponding height is
$h = q$.

Therefore, the area of $\triangle{XYZ}$ is

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= \frac{b \cdot h}{2} \\
&= \frac{20 \cdot q}{2} \\
&= 10q
\end{aligned}
$$

We are told that the area is between $70$ and $90$, inclusive. Thus, we have the following compound inequality:

$$
70 \leq 10q \leq 90
$$

Dividing by $10$ to isolate $q$, we obtain

$$
7 \leq q \leq 9
$$

Since $q$ is an integer, its possible values are $7$, $8$, and $9$.

Looking at the diagram again, we can see that the two right triangles are similar: each has a right angle, and they share the angle $\angle{X}$. As a consequence of this similarity, we obtain

$$
\dfrac{p}{q} = \dfrac{3}{3 + 17}
$$

Solving for $p$, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{p}{q} &= \frac{3}{3 + 17} \\
\frac{p}{q} &= \frac{3}{20} \\
p &= \frac{3q}{20}
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, from the possible values of $q$, we will find the possible values of $p$.

- For $q = 7$, we obtain
$p = \dfrac{3(7)}{20} = \dfrac{21}{20}$.
- For $q = 8$, we obtain
$p = \dfrac{3(8)}{20} = \dfrac{24}{20} = \dfrac{6}{5}$.
- For $q = 9$, we obtain
$p = \dfrac{3(9)}{20} = \dfrac{27}{20}$.

Therefore, the largest possible value of $p$ is

$$
\bbox[4pt,border: 1px solid lightgray]{\dfrac{27}{20}}
$$
