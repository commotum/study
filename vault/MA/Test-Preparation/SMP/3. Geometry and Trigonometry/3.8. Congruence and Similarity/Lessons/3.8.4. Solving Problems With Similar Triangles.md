## Introduction

Similar-triangle problems often appear in geometry and modeling contexts.

In this topic, we’ll practice applying similarity rules in diagrams and word problems. We’ll need to recognize angle relationships, set up side ratios correctly, and efficiently solve for unknown lengths or angles.

## Finding Angle Measures in Embedded Right Triangles

**Example:** In a triangle $\triangle{GRS}$, the angle $\angle{R}$ is a right angle. Point $T$ lies on $\overline{GS}$, and point $U$ lies on $\overline{RS}$ such that $\overline{TU}$ is parallel to $\overline{GR}$. If $m\angle{GSR} = 26^\circ$, what is the measure, in degrees, of $\angle GTU$?

**Explanation**

We proceed to represent the given information in a diagram.

![](<../Source/3.8.4. Solving Problems With Similar Triangles/Images/c086a64e975c90b39541fd8943bcfb47.png>)

Since $\overline{TU}$ is parallel to $\overline{GR}$ and $\angle{R}$ is a right angle, $\angle{SUT}$ is also a right angle.

We consider the triangle $\triangle{STU}$. In any triangle, the sum of the measures of its interior angles is $180^\circ$. Therefore,

$$
m\angle{UTS} + m\angle{SUT} + m\angle{TSU} = 180^\circ
$$

Taking into account that

$$
m\angle{SUT} = 90^\circ
$$

and

$$
m\angle{TSU} = m\angle{GSR} = 26^\circ
$$

we can determine $m\angle{UTS}$ as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
m∠UTS + m∠SUT + m∠TSU &= 180^{∘} \\
m∠UTS + 90^{∘} + 26^{∘} &= 180^{∘} \\
m∠UTS + 116^{∘} &= 180^{∘} \\
m∠UTS &= 64^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, note that $\angle{GTU}$ and $\angle{UTS}$ form a linear pair, so they are supplementary. This means

$$
m\angle{GTU} + m\angle{UTS} = 180^\circ
$$

Finally, substituting

$$
m\angle{UTS} = 64^\circ
$$

in the equality above and solving for $m\angle{GTU}$, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
m∠GTU + m∠UTS &= 180^{∘} \\
m∠GTU + 64^{∘} &= 180^{∘} \\
m∠GTU &= 116^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Calculating Side Lengths in Similar Triangles

**Example:** Note: Figure not drawn to scale.

![](<../Source/3.8.4. Solving Problems With Similar Triangles/Images/9469dda973a277fdce91325d7f2ce2da.png>)

In the figure shown, $\overline{E Y}$ and $\overline{F W}$ intersect at point $P$. $WP = 40$, $EP = 64$, $EF = 48$, and $FP = 96$. What is the length of $\overline{W Y}$?

**Explanation**

In the triangles $\triangle EFP$ and $\triangle WYP$, there are two pairs of congruent angles:

- $m\angle E = m\angle W = b^\circ$, and
- $\angle EPF$ and $\angle WPY$ are vertical and so have equal measures.

Thus, $\triangle EFP \sim \triangle WYP$ by the AA similarity criterion. As a result, we have

$$
\frac{EF}{WY} = \frac{EP}{WP}
$$

Therefore, since $WP = 40$, $EP = 64$, and $EF = 48$, we substitute these values and solve for $WY{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{48}{WY} &= \frac{64}{40} \\
48 \cdot 40 &= 64 \cdot WY \\
1920 &= 64 \cdot WY \\
WY &= \frac{1920}{64} \\
&= 30
\end{aligned}
$$

## Calculating Side Lengths in Similar Triangles With Parallel Sides

**Example:** Note: Figure not drawn to scale.

![](<../Source/3.8.4. Solving Problems With Similar Triangles/Images/64df8a632849855432f029b8609c87a7.png>)

In the figure above, $\overline{BF}$ intersects $\overline{DE}$ at point $C$, and $\overline{BD} \parallel \overline{EF}$. The lengths of $\overline{DC}$, $\overline{BC}$, and $\overline{CE}$ are $3$, $16$, and $21$, respectively. What is the length of $\overline{BF}$?

**Explanation**

From the diagram, we can see that $\overline{BF}$ is transversal to $\overline{BD}$ and $\overline{EF}$. Since we are given that $\overline{BD}$ and $\overline{EF}$ are parallel, the pair of alternate interior angles $\angle{B}$ and $\angle{F}$ are congruent.

On the other hand, we know that vertical angles are congruent. Thus, $\angle{BCD}$ and $\angle{FCE}$ are congruent.

Therefore, $\triangle{BCD} \sim \triangle{FCE}$ by the AA similarity criterion. As a consequence of the similarity, we obtain

$$
\dfrac{BC}{CF} = \dfrac{DC}{CE}
$$

Substituting the given values for $DC$, $BC$, and $CE$ into the above equality, and then solving for $CF$, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{BC}{CF} &= \frac{DC}{CE} \\
\frac{16}{CF} &= \frac{3}{21} \\
\frac{CF}{16} &= \frac{21}{3} \\
\frac{CF}{16} &= 7 \\
CF &= 112
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, since

$$
BF = BC + CF
$$

we get

$$
BF = 16 + 112 = 128
$$

Therefore, the length of $\overline{BF}$ is $128$.

## Modeling With Similar Triangles

**Example:** A $300$-foot tall radio tower casts a shadow $120$ feet long. At the same time, a nearby antenna casts a shadow $20$ feet long. What is the height, in feet, of the antenna?

**Explanation**

In the figure below, $AC$ represents the tower, which is $300$ feet tall, and $CB$ is the shadow cast by the tower, measuring $120$ feet. The antenna is shown as $DE$, with $BE$ as its shadow, which is $20$ feet long.

![](<../Source/3.8.4. Solving Problems With Similar Triangles/Images/5a286e3ecf523594603001ca1e1b9b8b.png>)

Note that since the measurements are taken at the same time, both objects have the same angle of elevation $\theta$, making the right triangles $\triangle ACB$ and $\triangle DEB$ similar by the AA similarity criterion.

Since the triangles are similar, the ratio of height to shadow length is the same for both, so

$$
\frac{AC}{CB} = \frac{DE}{BE}
$$

Substituting the known values, we get

$$
\frac{300}{120} = \frac{DE}{20}
$$

Solving for $DE$ gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
DE &= 20 \cdot \frac{300}{120} \\
&= \frac{6, 000}{120} \\
&= 50feet
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the height of the antenna is $50$ feet.

## Finding Angle Measures in Embedded Right Triangles Given Areas

**Example:** In a triangle $\triangle{AED}$, the angle $\angle{D}$ is a right angle. The points $B$ and $C$ lie on $\overline{AE}$ and $\overline{ED}$, respectively, such that $\overline{BC}$ is parallel to $\overline{AD}$. If the length of $\overline{AD}$ is $49$ units, the length of $\overline{BC}$ is $14$ units, and the area of $\triangle{AED}$ is $735$ square units, what is the length of $\overline{CD}$, in units?

**Explanation**

We proceed to represent the given information in a diagram.

![](<../Source/3.8.4. Solving Problems With Similar Triangles/Images/9ac3bc34f9ccea6e25d91929b23c2619.png>)

The formula for the area of a triangle is

$$
\mathcal{A} = \dfrac{b \cdot h}{2}
$$

where $b$ is a base and $h$ is the corresponding height.

In the case of $\triangle{AED}$, from the diagram we can see that its base is
$b = 49$,
and its corresponding height is
$h = ED$.

Therefore, the area of $\triangle{AED}$ is

$$
\mathcal{A} = \dfrac{49 \cdot ED}{2} = 24.5\,ED
$$

We are given that this area is equal to $735$ square units. Therefore, we can establish the equality

$$
24.5\,ED = 735
$$

Solving for $ED$, we obtain

$$
ED = \dfrac{735}{24.5} = 30
$$

On the other hand, since $\overline{BC}$ is parallel to $\overline{AD}$ and $\angle{D}$ is a right angle, $\angle{BCE}$ is also a right angle.

Since $\triangle{BCE}$ and $\triangle{ADE}$ each have a right angle and share angle $\angle{E}$, we have $\triangle{BCE} \sim \triangle{ADE}$ by the AA similarity criterion. As a consequence of the similarity, we obtain

$$
\dfrac{BC}{AD}=\dfrac{EC}{ED}
$$

Substituting $BC = 14$, $AD = 49$, and $ED=30$ gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{14}{49} &= \frac{EC}{30} \\
\frac{2}{7} &= \frac{EC}{30} \\
EC &= \frac{60}{7}
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, since

$$
CD = ED - EC
$$

we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
CD &= 30 - \frac{60}{7} \\
&= \frac{210}{7} - \frac{60}{7} \\
&= \frac{150}{7}units
\end{aligned}
$$
