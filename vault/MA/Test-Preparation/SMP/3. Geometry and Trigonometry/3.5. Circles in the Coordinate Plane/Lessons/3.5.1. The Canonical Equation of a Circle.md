## Introduction

Questions about equations of circles often test our ability to recognize a circle’s center and radius from its equation.

In this lesson, we’ll practice identifying and constructing equations of circles, rewriting them into standard form, and interpreting how changes in the equation affect a circle’s position and size.

## Determining the Radius, Diameter, and Center of a Circle Given Its Canonical Equation

**Example:** What is the diameter of the circle in the $xy$-plane with equation $(x - 12)^2 + (y + 4)^2 = 169$?

**Explanation**

The general equation of a circle with radius $r$ centered at the point $(a,b)$ is

$$
(x-a)^2 + (y-b)^2 = r^2
$$

We are given the equation

$$
(x - 12)^2 + (y + 4)^2 = 169
$$

which can be rewritten as

$$
(x - 12)^2 + (y - (-4))^2 = 13^2
$$

Comparing the last equation with the general equation of a circle, we obtain that the radius of our circle is $r = 13$.

Finally, the diameter $d$ of the circle is twice its radius. Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
d &= 2r \\
&= 2(13) \\
&= 26
\end{aligned}
$$

## Determining the Canonical Equation of a Circle Given Its Center and Radius

**Example:** In the $xy$-plane, a circle with radius $13$ has center $(8,-4)$. Find the equation of the circle.

**Explanation**

The general equation of a circle with radius $r$ centered at the point $(a,b)$ is

$$
(x-a)^2 + (y-b)^2 = r^2
$$

We are given that the radius of the circle is $13$ and it's centered at the point $(8,-4)$. So, $r = 13$, $a = 8$, and $b = -4$. Substituting these values into the equation above and simplifying, we obtain

$$
(x - 8)^2 + (y - (-4))^2 = 13^2
$$

or equivalently,

$$
(x - 8)^2 + (y + 4)^2 = 169
$$

## Calculating a Circle's Radius Given Two Points on the Diameter

**Example:** A circle in the $xy$-plane has a diameter with endpoints $(1, -2)$ and $(7, -2)$. An equation of this circle is $(x - 4)^2 + (y + 2)^2 = r^2$, where $r$ is a positive constant. What is the value of $r$?

**Explanation**

In the equation

$$
(x - 4)^2 + (y + 2)^2 = r^2
$$

the positive constant $r$ represents the radius of the circle.

We are given that the points with coordinates $(1, -2)$ and $(7, -2)$ are the endpoints of a diameter. Hence, we can calculate the length $d$ of this diameter using the distance formula:

$$
\begin{aligned}
d &= \sqrt{(x_{2} - x_{1})^{2} + (y_{2} - y_{1})^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{(7 - 1)^{2} + (-2 - (-2))^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{6^{2} + 0^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{36} \\
&= 6
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the radius of the circle is

$$
\begin{aligned}
r &= \frac{d}{2} \\
&= \frac{6}{2} \\
&= 3
\end{aligned}
$$

## Determining the Canonical Equation of a Circle Given Its Center and a Point on the Circumference

**Example:** A circle in the $xy$-plane has its center at $(-8,-5)$ and the point $(-1,-1)$ lies on the circle. Find the equation representing this circle.

**Explanation**

The general equation of a circle with radius $r$ centered at the point $(a,b)$ is

$$
(x-a)^2 + (y-b)^2 = r^2
$$

We are given that the circle has its center at $(-8,-5)$. Hence, $a = -8$ and $b = -5$.

We are also given that the circle passes through the point $(-1,-1)$. Therefore, the radius $r$ of the circle is

$$
\begin{aligned}
r &= \sqrt{(-1 - (-5))^{2} + (-1 - (-8))^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{(-1 + 5)^{2} + (-1 + 8)^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{4^{2} + 7^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{16 + 49} \\
&= \sqrt{65}
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, substituting $a$, $b$, and $r$ into the equation of the circle, we obtain

$$
(x - (-8))^2 + (y - (-5))^2 = 65
$$

or equivalently,

$$
(x + 8)^2 + (y + 5)^2 = 65
$$
