## Finding the Radius of a Scaled Circle

**Example:** The circle $R$ in the $xy$-plane has the equation
$(x - 2)^2 + (y + 3)^2 = 25$.
The circle $S$ has the same center as circle $R$. The radius of circle $S$ is three times the radius of circle $R$. The equation defining circle $S$ in the $xy$-plane is
$(x - 2)^2 + (y + 3)^2 = k$,
where $k$ is a constant. What is the value of $k$?

**Explanation**

The general equation of a circle with radius $r$ centered at the point $(a,b)$ is

$$
(x-a)^2 + (y-b)^2 = r^2
$$

The equation of the circle $R$ can be rewritten as

$$
(x - 2)^2 + (y - (-3))^2 = 5^2
$$

Therefore, its center has coordinates $(2,-3)$ and its radius is $r_{R} = 5$.

We are given that the radius of circle $S$ is three times the radius of circle $R$. So, the radius of circle $S$ is

$$
\begin{aligned}
r_{S} &= 3 \cdot r_{R} \\
&= 3 \cdot 5 \\
&= 15
\end{aligned}
$$

Since $S$ and $R$ have the same center, the general equation of the circle $S$ is given by

$$
(x - 2)^2 + (y + 3)^2 = 15^2
$$

or equivalently,

$$
(x - 2)^2 + (y + 3)^2 = 225
$$

Finally, comparing the last equation with

$$
(x - 2)^2 + (y + 3)^2 = k
$$

we can conclude that $k = 225$.

## Determining the Equation of a Shifted Circle

**Example:** The equation $(x - 3)^2 + (y + 7)^2 = 100$ represents circle $M$. Circle $N$ is obtained by shifting circle $M$ left $4$ units in the $xy$-plane. Find the equation that represents circle $N$.

**Explanation**

The general equation of a circle with radius $r$ centered at the point $(a,b)$ is

$$
(x-a)^2 + (y-b)^2 = r^2
$$

The equation representing circle $M$ can be rewritten as

$$
(x - 3)^2 + (y - (-7))^2 = 10^2
$$

Comparing the last equation with the general equation of a circle, we obtain that the coordinates of the center of the circle $M$ are $(3,-7)$ and its radius is $10$.

Since circle $N$ is obtained by shifting circle $M$ left $4$ units, we obtain

- $N$ has the same radius as circle $M$, and
- the center of $N$ has coordinates $(3 - 4, -7) = (-1,-7)$.

Therefore, the equation of circle $N$ is

$$
(x - (-1))^2 + (y - (-7))^2 = 10^2
$$

or equivalently,

$$
(x + 1)^2 + (y + 7)^2 = 100
$$

## Determining Possible Coordinates on a Circle

**Example:** The equation $(x-3)^2+(y+2)^2=25$ represents circle $\text{A}$. Circle $\text{B}$ is obtained by shifting circle $\text{A}$ down $4$ units in the $xy$-plane. The point $(a,b)$ lies on circle $\text{B}$. Which of the following is a possible value of $b$?

1. $-1$
2. $-12$
3. $0$

**Explanation**

We're given the equation of circle $\text{A}$

$$
(x-3)^2+(y+2)^2=25
$$

and we wish to translate it by $4$ units down.

For each point on circle $\text{A}$, we must subtract $4$ from each $y$-coordinate to get the corresponding point on circle $\text{B}$. So, the new $y$-coordinates of the points on circle $\text{B}$ are given by

$$
y_{\textrm{new}} = y - 4
$$

This equation is equivalent to

$$
y = y_{\textrm{new}} + 4
$$

To get the equation of circle $\text{B}$, we substitute this into the equation of circle $\text{A}$. Doing this, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x - 3)^{2} + (y + 2)^{2} &= 25 \\
(x - 3)^{2} + ((y_{new} + 4) + 2)^{2} &= 25
\end{aligned}
$$

Dropping the $\textrm{new}$ suffix, we get

$$
(x-3)^2+\big((y + 4)+2\big)^2 = 25
$$

Finally, simplifying the expression, we have

$$
(x-3)^2+(y+6)^2 = 25
$$

Therefore, the equation of circle $\text{B}$ is

$$
(x-3)^2+(y+6)^2 = 25
$$

Now, we need to find the range of possible values of $b$, where $(a,b)$ is a point on the circle.

The center of circle $\text{B}$ is $(3,-6)$, and its radius is $r = 5$. Therefore, the range of $y$-values on the circle is

$$
-6 - 5 \leq y \leq -6 + 5
$$

which simplifies as

$$
-11 \leq y \leq -1
$$

We can write this as $y\in [-11,-1]$.

Let's now consider each value in turn.

- $b=-1$ is possible since $-1\in [-11,-1]\quad{\color{green}{\checkmark}}$
- $b=-12$ is NOT possible since $-12\notin [-11,-1]\quad{\color{red}{\times}}$
- $b=0$ is NOT possible since $0\notin [-11,-1]\quad{\color{red}{\times}}$

Therefore, the correct answer is, "I only."

## Determining the Equations of a Shifted and Scaled Circle

**Example:** Circle $M$ (shown) is defined by the equation $(x + 1)^2 + (y + 1)^2 = 4$. Circle $N$ (not shown) is the result of shifting circle $M$ right $8$ units and up $3$ units and increasing the radius so that the radius of circle $N$ is $5$ times the radius of circle $M$. Find the equation defining circle $N$.

![](<../Source/3.5.2. Canonical Equations of Transformed Circles/Images/ca5f95933ed82edf9fd18dc3469ad3a2.png>)

**Explanation**

The general equation of a circle with radius $r$ centered at the point $(a,b)$ is

$$
(x-a)^2 + (y-b)^2 = r^2
$$

The equation representing circle $M$ can be rewritten as

$$
(x - (-1))^2 + (y - (-1))^2 = 2^2
$$

Comparing the last equation with the general equation of a circle, we obtain that the coordinates of the center of the circle $M$ are $(-1,-1)$ and its radius is $2$.

Since the circle $N$ is the result of shifting circle $M$ right $8$ units and up $3$ units and increasing the radius so that its radius is $5$ times the radius of circle $M$, we get

- the center of $N$ has coordinates $(-1+8,\,-1+3) = (7,2)$, and
- the radius of $N$ is $r = 5(2) = 10$.

Therefore, the equation of circle $N$ is

$$
(x - 7)^2 + (y - 2)^2 = 10^2
$$

or equivalently,

$$
(x - 7)^2 + (y - 2)^2 = 100
$$
