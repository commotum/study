## Introduction

Often, questions involving general equations of circles require us to recognize or convert between the standard form and the general form of a circle’s equation.

In this lesson, we’ll practice transforming general equations and identifying centers and radii.

---

<a id="converting-a-circles-equation-from-canonical-to-general-form"></a>
## Converting a Circle's Equation From Canonical to General Form

**Example:** A circle in the $xy$-plane has its center at $(-8,5)$ and has a radius of $6$. An equation of this circle is $x^2 + y^2 + a x + b y + c = 0$, where $a$, $b$, and $c$ are constants. What is the value of $c$?

**Explanation**

The general equation of a circle with radius $r$ centered at the point $(a,b)$ is

$$
(x-a)^2 + (y-b)^2 = r^2
$$

We are given that the radius of the circle is $6$ and it's centered at the point $(-8,5)$. So, $r = 6$, $a = -8$, and $b = 5$. Substituting these values into the equation above, we obtain

$$
(x - (-8))^2 + (y - 5)^2 = 6^2
$$

or equivalently,

$$
(x + 8)^2 + (y - 5)^2 = 36
$$

Expanding and grouping like terms, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x + 8)^{2} + (y - 5)^{2} &= 36 \\
x^{2} + 16x + 64 + y^{2} - 10y + 25 &= 36 \\
x^{2} + y^{2} + 16x - 10y + 53 &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

Comparing the last equation with

$$
x^2 + y^2 + ax + by + c = 0
$$

we get $c =53$.

## Determining the Center and Radius of a Circle Given Its General Equation

**Example:** The graph of $x^2 - 14x + y^2 + 2y = 20$ in the $xy$-plane is a circle. What is the radius of the circle?

**Explanation**

The general equation of a circle with radius $r$ centered at the point $(a,b)$ is

$$
(x-a)^2 + (y-b)^2 = r^2
$$

Therefore, to find the radius of our circle, we must first find its general equation.

Grouping the $x$ and $y$ terms and completing the squares, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^{2} - 14x + y^{2} + 2y &= 20 \\
(x^{2} - 14x) + (y^{2} + 2y) &= 20 \\
(x^{2} - 2 \cdot 7x + 7^{2}) + (y^{2} + 2 \cdot 1y + 1^{2}) &= 20 + 7^{2} + 1^{2} \\
&= 20 + 49 + 1 \\
&= 70
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we can factor the perfect squares and get

$$
(x - 7)^2 + (y + 1)^2 = 70
$$

or equivalently,

$$
(x - 7)^2 + (y + 1)^2 = (\sqrt{70})^2
$$

Comparing the last equation with the general equation of a circle, we obtain that the radius of our circle is

$$
r = \sqrt{70}
$$

## Finding the Radius of a Circle Given Its General Equation With Leading Coefficients

**Example:** In the $xy$-plane, the graph of $7x^{2} + 14x + 7y^{2} - 28y = 0$ is a circle. What is the radius of the circle?

**Explanation**

The general equation of a circle with radius $r$ centered at the point $(a,b)$ is

$$
(x-a)^2 + (y-b)^2 = r^2
$$

Therefore, to find the radius of our circle, we must first find its general equation.

We begin by simplifying the given equation as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
7x^{2} + 14x + 7y^{2} - 28y &= 0 \\
7(x^{2} + 2x + y^{2} - 4y) &= 0 \\
x^{2} + 2x + y^{2} - 4y &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, grouping the $x$ and $y$ terms and completing the squares, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^{2} + 2x + y^{2} - 4y &= 0 \\
(x^{2} + 2x) + (y^{2} - 4y) &= 0 \\
(x^{2} + 2 \cdot 1x + 1^{2}) + (y^{2} - 2 \cdot 2y + 2^{2}) &= 0 + 1^{2} + 2^{2} \\
(x^{2} + 2 \cdot 1x + 1^{2}) + (y^{2} - 2 \cdot 2y + 2^{2}) &= 5
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we can factor the perfect squares and get

$$
(x + 1)^2 + (y - 2)^2 = 5
$$

or equivalently,

$$
(x - (-1))^2 + (y - 2)^2 = \big(\sqrt{5}\big)^2
$$

Comparing the last equation with the general equation of a circle, we obtain that the radius of our circle is

$$
r = \sqrt{5}
$$
