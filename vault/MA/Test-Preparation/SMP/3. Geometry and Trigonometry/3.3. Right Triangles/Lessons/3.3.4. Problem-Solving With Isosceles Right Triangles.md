## Introduction

Problems involving isosceles right triangles often appear in geometry and coordinate setups. These questions test our ability to apply special triangle ratios to find unknown sides, perimeters, and other quantities.

In this lesson, we’ll practice recognizing these triangles and use their side relationships to solve problems.

## Finding the Height of an Isosceles Right Triangle Using ASA Congruence

**Example:** In the triangle $PQR$ shown, what is the length of $\overline{PU}$?

![](<../Source/3.3.4. Problem-Solving With Isosceles Right Triangles/Images/2d09aa097c53a34661d32dc45c4618ea.png>)

(Note: Figure not drawn to scale)

**Explanation**

First, we recall the ASA (Angle–Side–Angle) congruence criterion:

> *Two triangles are congruent if and only if two angles and the included side of one triangle are congruent to two angles and the included side of the other triangle.*

In the figure, we observe the following:

- Both triangles $\triangle PQU$ and $\triangle RQU$ are $30^\circ$-$60^\circ$-$90^\circ$ triangles with a *right angle* at $U$.
- $\angle PQU = \angle RQU = 30^\circ$.
- The segment $\overline{QU}$ is shared by both triangles (common side).

So, both triangles have:

- Two congruent angles ($90^\circ$ and $30^\circ$),
- And the included side $\overline{QU}$.

By the *ASA congruence criterion*, we conclude:
$\triangle PQU \cong \triangle RQU$

Since corresponding parts of congruent triangles are equal, it follows that:

$$
\overline{PU} = \overline{RU}
$$

Now, we compute $RU$. Triangle $\triangle RQU$ is a $30^\circ$–$60^\circ$–$90^\circ$ triangle with hypotenuse $RQ = 16$. In such triangles, the side opposite the $30^\circ$ angle is half the hypotenuse:

$$
RU = PU = \dfrac{16}{2} = 8
$$

Therefore, the length of $\overline{PU}$ is $8$.

## Finding the Perimeter of an Isosceles Right Triangle Given Its Hypotenuse

**Example:** An isosceles right triangle has a hypotenuse of length $50$ meters. What is the perimeter, in meters, of this triangle?

**Explanation**

In an isosceles right triangle, the legs are congruent and the hypotenuse is equal to the leg length multiplied by $\sqrt{2}$.

Let the length of each leg be $x$. Then the hypotenuse is:
$x\sqrt{2}$

We are told that the hypotenuse is $50$, so we equalize and solve for $x{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
x\sqrt{2} &= 50 \\
x &= \frac{50}{\sqrt{2}} \\
&= \frac{50}{\sqrt{2}} \cdot \frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{2}} \\
&= \frac{50\sqrt{2}}{2} \\
&= 25\sqrt{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we compute the perimeter. Since both legs are $25\sqrt{2}$ and the hypotenuse is $50$, the perimeter is

$$
\begin{aligned}
25\sqrt{2} + 25\sqrt{2} + 50 &= 50\sqrt{2} + 50meters
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding the Leg of an Isosceles Right Triangle Given Its Perimeter

**Example:** An isosceles right triangle has a perimeter of $50 + 25\sqrt{2}$ yards. What is the length, in yards, of one leg of this triangle?

**Explanation**

In an isosceles right triangle, the two legs are congruent and the hypotenuse is equal to the leg length multiplied by $\sqrt{2}$.

Let the length of each leg be $x$. Then the perimeter of the triangle is

$$
x + x + x\sqrt{2} = 2x + x\sqrt{2}
$$

We are told that the perimeter is $50 + 25\sqrt{2}$, so we write

$$
2x + x\sqrt{2} = 50 + 25\sqrt{2}
$$

Next, we group terms on the left and solve for $x{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
x(2 + \sqrt{2}) &= 50 + 25\sqrt{2} \\
x &= \frac{50 + 25\sqrt{2}}{2 + \sqrt{2}} \\
&= (25(2 + \sqrt{2}))/(2 + \sqrt{2}) \\
&= 25
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus, the length of one leg is $25$ yards.
