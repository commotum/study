## Stating the Pythagorean Theorem

**Example:** What is the relationship between the side lengths of the triangle shown above?

![](<../Source/3.3.2. The Pythagorean Theorem/Images/17d31858f764cc1c77e7d44ec74f0d3d.png>)

**Explanation**

The Pythagorean theorem states that for a right triangle,

$$
a^2 + b^2 = c^2
$$

where $a$ and $b$ represent the lengths of the legs of the triangle, and $c$ represents the length of its hypotenuse.

In our case, we are given the lengths of the legs $a = 6$ and $b = d$ of a right triangle, and the length of the hypotenuse $c = 10$. Substituting these values into the Pythagorean theorem formula, we conclude that the expression that gives the relationship between the sides of the triangle is

$$
6^2 + d^2 = 10^2
$$

## Finding a Hypotenuse Given Two Legs in a Right Triangle

**Example:** A right triangle has legs with lengths of $10\,\textrm{ft}$ and $20\,\textrm{ft}$. What is the length of the triangle's hypotenuse?

**Explanation**

The Pythagorean theorem states that for a right triangle,

$$
c^2 = a^2 + b^2
$$

where $a$ and $b$ represent the lengths of the legs of the triangle, and $c$ represents the length of its hypotenuse.

In our case, we are given the lengths of the legs

$$
a = 10\,\textrm{ft}
$$

and

$$
b = 20\,\textrm{ft}
$$

of a right triangle. Substituting these values into the Pythagorean theorem formula, we can find the length of the hypotenuse $c$ as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
c^{2} &= 10^{2} + 20^{2} \\
&= 100 + 400 \\
&= 500
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, taking square roots and using the fact that $c$ denotes a length, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
c &= \sqrt{500} \\
&= \sqrt{100 \cdot 5} \\
&= \sqrt{100} \cdot \sqrt{5} \\
&= 10\sqrt{5}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the length of the triangle’s hypotenuse is $10\sqrt{5}\,\textrm{ft}$.

## Finding a Leg Given Another Leg and the Hypotenuse in a Right Triangle

**Example:** One leg of a right triangle has a length of $27.6\,\textrm{in}$. The hypotenuse of the triangle has a length of $74.5\,\textrm{in}$. What is the length of the other leg of the triangle, rounded to $2$ decimal places?

**Explanation**

The Pythagorean theorem states that for a right triangle,

$$
a^2 + b^2 = c^2
$$

where $a$ and $b$ represent the lengths of the legs of the triangle, and $c$ represents the length of its hypotenuse.

We are given that one leg of the right triangle has a length of

$$
b = 27.6\,\textrm{in}
$$

while the hypotenuse has a length of

$$
c = 74.5\,\textrm{in}
$$

Substituting these values into the Pythagorean theorem formula, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
a^{2} + (27.6)^{2} &= (74.5)^{2} \\
a^{2} + 761.76 &= 5, 550.25 \\
a^{2} &= 5, 550.25 - 761.76 \\
a^{2} &= 4, 788.49
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, taking square roots and using the fact that $a$ denotes a length, we obtain

$$
a = \sqrt{4,788.49} \approx 69.20
$$

rounded to two decimal places.

Therefore, the length of the other leg is approximately $69.20\,\textrm{in}$.
