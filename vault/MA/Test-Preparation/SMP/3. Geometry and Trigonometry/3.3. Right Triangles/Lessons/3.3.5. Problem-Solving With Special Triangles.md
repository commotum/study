## Introduction

Problems involving special triangles (equilateral, isosceles right, and $30^\circ$–$60^\circ$–$90^\circ$ triangles) are common in both geometry and coordinate setups.

Here, we’ll practice recognizing these triangle types and apply their relationships to find unknown sides, heights, and areas.

## Identifying Equilateral Triangles

**Example:** For $\triangle{JKL}$, we are given $JK = 0.84\, \mathrm{in}$ and $KL = 0.84\, \mathrm{in}$. Which of the following statements is sufficient to prove that $\triangle{JKL}$ is equilateral?

1. $JL = 0.42 \, \mathrm{in}$
2. $JL = 0.84 \, \mathrm{in}$
3. $JL = 0.42\sqrt{3} \, \mathrm{in}$
4. No additional information is needed

**Explanation**

A triangle is equilateral if all three sides have the same length.

In our case, $\triangle{JKL}$ is an equilateral triangle if

$$
JL = JK = KL
$$

Since we are given that

$$
JK = KL = 0.84\, \mathrm{in}
$$

we can conclude that $\triangle{JKL}$ is an equilateral triangle if

$$
JL = 0.84 \, \mathrm{in}
$$

## Finding the Area of Part of a Composite Shape Given Its Perimeter

**Example:** A tile path in a park features three congruent equilateral tiles arranged in a straight line. One of the tiles is shaded. If the total perimeter of the tile arrangement is $20$ centimeters, what is the area of the shaded region, in square centimeters?

![](<../Source/3.3.5. Problem-Solving With Special Triangles/Images/5a2cd4026eed5ed09f33899393a0780c.png>)

**Explanation**

The perimeter of the row formed by the three congruent equilateral triangles consists of five outer triangle sides. If $s$ is the side length of each small triangle, then:

$$
\begin{aligned}
Perimeter &= 20 \\
5s &= 20 \\
s &= \frac{20}{5} \\
&= 4
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we calculate the area of the shaded equilateral triangle using the formula:

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}s^{2} \\
&= \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4} \cdot 4^{2} \\
&= \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4} \cdot 16 \\
&= 4\sqrt{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the area of the shaded region is $4\sqrt{3}$ square centimeters.

## Finding the Height of an Equilateral Triangle Given Its Perimeter

**Example:** The perimeter of an equilateral triangle is $432$ inches. The height of this triangle is $k\sqrt{3}$ inches, where $k$ is a constant. What is the value of $k$?

**Explanation**

In an equilateral triangle, all three sides are equal. So if the perimeter is $432$, then each side has length

$$
\dfrac{432}{3} = 144
$$

To find the height of an equilateral triangle, we draw an altitude from one vertex to the midpoint of the opposite side. This altitude splits the equilateral triangle into two $30^\circ$–$60^\circ$–$90^\circ$ right triangles.

In a $30^\circ$–$60^\circ$–$90^\circ$ triangle:

- The hypotenuse is the side of the equilateral triangle, which is $144$.
- The shorter leg is half the base: $\dfrac{144}{2} = 72$.
- The height is the longer leg, which is $\sqrt{3}$ times the shorter leg.

So, the height is
$72\sqrt{3}$.

Thus, the value of $k$ is $72$.

## Finding the Hypotenuse of a 30-60-90 Triangle Given Its Perimeter

**Example:** A triangle with angle measures $30^\circ$, $60^\circ$, and $90^\circ$ has a perimeter of $15 + 5\sqrt{3}$. What is the length of the longest side of the triangle?

**Explanation**

For a $30^\circ$–$60^\circ$–$90^\circ$ triangle, we have the following:

- The shorter leg is opposite the $30^\circ$ angle.
- The longer leg is opposite the $60^\circ$ angle.
- The hypotenuse is twice the length of the shorter leg.
- The longer leg is $\sqrt{3}$ times the length of the shorter leg.

Let the shortest side have length $x$. Then the perimeter is

$$
x + x\sqrt{3} + 2x = 3x + x\sqrt{3}
$$

We are told that the perimeter is $15 + 5\sqrt{3}$, so we write

$$
3x + x\sqrt{3} = 15 + 5\sqrt{3}
$$

Next, we group terms on the left and solve for $x{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
x(3 + \sqrt{3}) &= 15 + 5\sqrt{3} \\
x &= \frac{15 + 5\sqrt{3}}{3 + \sqrt{3}} \\
&= (5(3 + \sqrt{3}))/(3 + \sqrt{3}) \\
&= 5
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the shortest side is $5$, and the longest side, the hypotenuse, is

$$
2x = 2(5) = 10
$$
