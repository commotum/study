## Introduction

On the math section of the SAT exam, problems involving right triangles often require finding areas using given side lengths or geometric relationships. In this topic, we'll practice finding areas of triangles.

## Finding the Area of a Triangle Given Its Base and Height

**Example:** A triangle has a base length of $12$ feet and a height of $90$ feet. What is the area, in square feet, of the triangle?

**Explanation**

To find the area of a triangle, we use the formula

$$
A = \frac{1}{2} \cdot b \cdot h
$$

where $b$ is the base length and $h$ is the height.

We substitute the given values into the formula

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= \frac{1}{2} \cdot 12 \cdot 90 \\
&= 6 \cdot 90 \\
&= 540ft^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding the Area of a Right Triangle Given Its Legs

**Example:** Note: Figure not drawn to scale.

![](<../Source/3.3.1. Areas of Right Triangles/Images/16b4bf1688c81544dd84c74a6f4a1e00.png>)

The figure shows the lengths, in meters, of two sides of a right triangle. What is the area of the triangle, in square meters?

**Explanation**

In a right triangle, we can use the two legs as the base and the height because they are perpendicular.

We are given that the legs have lengths $10$ meters and $4$ meters.

We use the area formula for a triangle:

$$
A = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \text{base} \cdot \text{height}
$$

Now, we substitute the values:

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= \frac{1}{2} \cdot 10 \cdot 4 \\
&= \frac{1}{2} \cdot 40 \\
&= 20m^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the area of the triangle is $20 \, \textrm{m}^2$.
