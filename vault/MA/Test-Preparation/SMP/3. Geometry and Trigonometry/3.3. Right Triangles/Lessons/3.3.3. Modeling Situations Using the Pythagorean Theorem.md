## Introduction

The Pythagorean theorem often appears in questions where right triangles are part of larger or composite figures. These problems test the ability to recognize right triangles and apply the theorem to model real-world or geometric situations.

With practice, we can develop the skill to quickly spot these relationships and set up the correct equations.

---

<a id="applying-the-pythagorean-theorem"></a>
## Applying the Pythagorean Theorem

**Example:** A ladder of length $14$ feet leans against a vertical wall, reaching a height of $7$ feet above the ground. How far is the base of the ladder from the wall?

**Explanation**

We can model this scenario as a right triangle:

![](<../Source/3.3.3. Modeling Situations Using the Pythagorean Theorem/Images/4c571c36102f2c3a4b4b0040e418218b.png>)

We want to find the distance $x$ between the base of the ladder and the wall.

Applying the Pythagorean Theorem and solving for $x{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^{2} + 7^{2} &= 14^{2} \\
x^{2} + 49 &= 196 \\
x^{2} &= 196 - 49 \\
x^{2} &= 147 \\
x &= \sqrt{147} \\
x &= 7\sqrt{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the base of the ladder is $7\sqrt{3}$ feet from the wall.

## Finding the Side of a Rectangle Given Another Side and the Diagonal

**Example:** A rectangular yoga mat has a diagonal length of $75$ centimeters. If the shorter side of the mat is $45$ centimeters, what is the length, in centimeters, of the longer side?

**Explanation**

We can represent the yoga mat as follows:

![](<../Source/3.3.3. Modeling Situations Using the Pythagorean Theorem/Images/82a8b8c3b78b342fcb9960943c1b3a63.png>)

Let the longer side be $x$ centimeters. Then:

$$
\begin{aligned}
45^{2} + x^{2} &= 75^{2} \\
2, 025 + x^{2} &= 5, 625 \\
x^{2} &= 5, 625 - 2, 025 \\
x^{2} &= 3, 600 \\
x &= \sqrt{3, 600} \\
&= 60
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the length of the longer side is $\bbox[4pt, border: 1px solid lightgray]{60}$ centimeters.

## Finding the Diameter of a Circle Given the Area of an Inscribed Rectangle

**Example:** A tablet display is shaped like a rectangle and is mounted inside a circular casing, so that each vertex of the rectangle touches the circle. The diagonal of the rectangle is three times as long as its shortest side. The area of the rectangle is $200\sqrt{2}$ square centimeters. What is the length, in centimeters, of the diameter of the circle?

**Explanation**

We can draw this composite shape as:

![](<../Source/3.3.3. Modeling Situations Using the Pythagorean Theorem/Images/9f35c21f8dcef0cb80dac7a40d986686.png>)

So, we observe that the rectangle's diagonal is equal to the diameter of the circle.

Let the shorter side of the rectangle be $x$ centimeters and the longer side be $y$ centimeters. Since the diagonal is three times the shorter side, it follows that the diagonal is $3x$ centimeters.

In $\triangle ABC$, we apply the Pythagorean Theorem and solve for $y{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^{2} + y^{2} &= (3x)^{2} \\
x^{2} + y^{2} &= 9x^{2} \\
y^{2} &= 8x^{2} \\
y &= \sqrt{8}x \\
y &= 2\sqrt{2}x
\end{aligned}
$$

Since the area of the rectangle is $200\sqrt{2}$, we have

$$
x \cdot y = 200\sqrt{2}
$$

Next, we substitute

$$
y = 2\sqrt{2}\,x
$$

and solve for $x{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
x(2\sqrt{2}x) &= 200\sqrt{2} \\
2\sqrt{2}x^{2} &= 200\sqrt{2} \\
x^{2} &= \frac{200\sqrt{2}}{2\sqrt{2}} \\
&= 100 \\
x &= \sqrt{100} \\
&= 10
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the diagonal is

$$
3x = 3\cdot 10 = 30
$$

Therefore, the circle's diameter is $30$ centimeters.
