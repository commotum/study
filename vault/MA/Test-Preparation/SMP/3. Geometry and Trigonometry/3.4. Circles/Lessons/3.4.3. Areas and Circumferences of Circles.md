## Introduction

Questions about circles appear frequently on the math section of the SAT, testing the ability to use the formulas for area and circumference as well as to reason about how these quantities change with the radius or diameter.

By practicing these problems, we’ll strengthen our understanding of how circular measures relate to one another and learn to recognize opportunities to apply proportional reasoning.

## Finding the Area of a Circle Given Its Radius

**Example:** Circle $R$ has a radius of $10$ centimeters $(\mathrm{cm})$. Circle $S$ has an area of $36\pi\,\mathrm{cm}^{2}$. What is the total area, in $\mathrm{cm}^{2}$, of circles $R$ and $S$?

**Explanation**

Let $\mathcal{A}_R$ and $\mathcal{A}_S$ represent the areas of circles $R$ and $S$, respectively. We are given

- Circle $R$ has a radius of $10\,\mathrm{cm}$.
- Circle $S$ has an area of $36\pi\,\mathrm{cm}^2$.

First, we calculate the area of circle $R$ using the formula for the area of a circle:

$$
\mathcal{A} = \pi r^2
$$

Substituting $r = 10$ into the formula, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
A_{R} &= π \cdot 10^{2} \\
&= π \cdot 100 \\
&= 100π
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we add the two areas to find the total:

$$
\begin{aligned}
A_{total} &= A_{R} + A_{S} \\
&= 100π + 36π \\
&= 136π
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the total area of the two circles is $136\pi\,\mathrm{cm}^2$.

## Finding the Diameter of a Circle Given Its Circumference

**Example:** A circle has a circumference of $72\pi$ centimeters. What is the diameter, in centimeters, of the circle?

**Explanation**

To find the diameter of a circle, we use the formula for circumference in terms of diameter:
$C = \pi d$

We are told the circumference is $72\pi$, so we substitute and solve for $d{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
72π &= πd \\
d &= \frac{72π}{π} \\
d &= 72
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the diameter of the circle is $72$ centimeters.

## Finding the Side Length of a Square That Has the Same Area as a Circle of Given Radius

**Example:** What is the length of one side of a square that has the same area as a circle with radius $13$ feet?

**Explanation**

Since the radius of the circle is $13$ feet, using the formula for the area of a circle with radius $r$, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
A_{circle} &= πr^{2} \\
&= π \cdot 13^{2} \\
&= 169π
\end{aligned}
$$

Now suppose a square has side length $s$. The formula for the area of a square is

$$
\mathcal{A}_{\text{square}} = s^2
$$

Since the square has the same area as the circle, we can set the two areas equal and solve for $s{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
A_{square} &= A_{circle} \\
s^{2} &= 169π \\
s &= \sqrt{169π} \\
&= 13\sqrt{π}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the side length of the square must be $13\sqrt{\pi}$ feet.
