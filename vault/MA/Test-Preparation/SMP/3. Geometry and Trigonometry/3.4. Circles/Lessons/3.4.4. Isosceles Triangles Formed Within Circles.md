## Determining Base Angles of Central Triangles

**Example:** A circle has center $O$, and points $A$ and $B$ lie on the circle. The measure of arc $\overset{\frown}{AB}$ is $84^\circ$. What is the measure of $\angle OAB$, in degrees?

**Explanation**

![](<../Source/3.4.4. Isosceles Triangles Formed Within Circles/Images/5854ee78c2b8684cfbc6dd8ae5bfe128.png>)

Since $\overline{OA}$ and $\overline{OB}$ are both radii of the circle, triangle $OAB$ is isosceles with $OA = OB$. Thus, the base angles $\angle OAB$ and $\angle OBA$ are congruent.

In $\triangle OAB$, the measures of the interior angles sum to $180^\circ{:}$

$$
m\angle OAB + m\angle OBA + m\angle AOB = 180^\circ
$$

We are given that

$$
m\overset{\frown}{AB} = 84^\circ
$$

Since $\angle AOB$ is the central angle corresponding to arc $\overset{\frown}{AB}$, we have

$$
m\angle AOB = 84^\circ
$$

Now, since

$$
m\angle OAB = m\angle OBA
$$

we let each equal $x$. Substituting, we solve for $x{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
x + x + 84^{∘} &= 180^{∘} \\
2x + 84^{∘} &= 180^{∘} \\
2x &= 96^{∘} \\
x &= 48^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the measure of $\angle OAB$ is

$$
m\angle OAB = 48^\circ
$$

## Determining Base Lengths of Central Triangles

**Example:** Points $M$ and $N$ lie on a circle with center $O$. The radius of the circle is $9\,\mathrm{cm}$. Triangle $OMN$ has a perimeter of $29 \: \text{cm}$. What is the length, in centimeters, of $MN$?

**Explanation**

![](<../Source/3.4.4. Isosceles Triangles Formed Within Circles/Images/440455c674edcd101d4f50af91de2ef1.png>)

The triangle $OMN$ is formed by connecting the center $O$ to two points on the circle, $M$ and $N$. In this triangle, the segments $\overline{OM}$ and $\overline{ON}$ are both radii of the circle, which means

$$
OM = ON = 9 \: \text{cm}
$$

Now, the perimeter of triangle $OMN$ is given by

$$
p = OM + ON + MN
$$

Therefore, since the perimeter of $\triangle OMN$ is $29$, we can solve for MN the follwoing eqaution:

$$
\begin{aligned}
p &= OM + ON + MN \\
29 &= 9 + 9 + MN \\
29 &= 18 + MN \\
MN &= 29 - 18 \\
&= 11cm
\end{aligned}
$$

## Determining an Arc Length Given an Isosceles Triangle in a Circle

**Example:** Point $O$ is the center of the circle above, and the measure of $\angle OCD$ is $25^{\circ}$. If the length of $\overline{OE}$ is $18$, what is the length of arc $\overset{\frown}{CD}$?

![](<../Source/3.4.4. Isosceles Triangles Formed Within Circles/Images/5f5e789fa87d4813cc722e6d5a810201.png>)

**Explanation**

To find the length of a circular arc with central angle $\theta$ and radius $r$, we use the formula:

$$
\text{Arc Length} = \dfrac{\theta}{360^\circ} \cdot 2\pi r
$$

Since $\overline{OC}, \overline{OD}$, and $\overline{OE}$ are all radii of the circle, triangle $COD$ is isosceles with

$$
\overline{OC} = \overline{OD}
$$

Thus, the base angles are equal:

$$
m\angle ODC = m\angle OCD = 25^\circ
$$

Using the triangle angle sum formula, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
m∠ODC + m∠OCD + m∠COD &= 180^{∘} \\
m∠COD &= 180^{∘} - (m∠OCD + m∠COD) \\
&= 180^{∘} - (25^{∘} + 25^{∘}) \\
&= 180^{∘} - 50^{∘} \\
&= 130^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, since the central angle of arc $\overset{\frown}{CD}$ is

$$
\theta = 130^\circ
$$

and the radius of the circle is $r = 18$, the length of arc $\overset{\frown}{CD}$ is

$$
\begin{aligned}
Length(CD^(⌢)) &= \frac{130^{∘}}{360^{∘}} \cdot 2π \cdot 18 \\
&= \frac{13}{36} \cdot 36π \\
&= 13π
\end{aligned}
$$

## Determining Base Angles of Central Triangles Using the Distance Formula

**Example:** In the $xy$-plane, a circle has center $O$ with coordinates $(a, b)$. Points $P$ and $Q$ lie on the circle. Point $P$ has coordinates $(a+4, b+\sqrt{18})$, and $\angle{POQ}$ is a right angle. What is the length of $\overline{PQ}$?

**Explanation**

![](<../Source/3.4.4. Isosceles Triangles Formed Within Circles/Images/ff8d78d4397a6ac5a2ba0f0cf181df9b.png>)

We are given that $O$ is the center of the circle, and $P$ and $Q$ lie on the circle. Therefore, $\overline{OP}$ and $\overline{OQ}$ are radii of the circle. Hence, $OP = OQ$.

We know that the coordinates of $O$ and $P$ are $(a, b)$ and $(a+4, b+\sqrt{18})$, respectively. Hence, we can calculate $OP$ using the distance formula:

$$
\begin{aligned}
OP &= \sqrt{(x_{2} - x_{1})^{2} + (y_{2} - y_{1})^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{(a + 4 - a)^{2} + (b + \sqrt{18} - b)^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{4^{2} + (\sqrt{18})^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{16 + 18} \\
&= \sqrt{34}
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus,

$$
OQ = OP = \sqrt{34}
$$

Finally, $\triangle{POQ}$ is a right triangle because $\angle{POQ}$ is a right angle. Therefore, applying the Pythagorean theorem, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
PQ^{2} &= OP^{2} + OQ^{2} \\
&= OP^{2} + OP^{2} \\
&= 2OP^{2} \\
&= 2(\sqrt{34})^{2} \\
&= 2(34) \\
&= 68
\end{aligned}
$$

Consequently,

$$
\begin{aligned}
PQ &= \sqrt{68} \\
&= \sqrt{4 \cdot 17} \\
&= \sqrt{4} \cdot \sqrt{17} \\
&= 2\sqrt{17}
\end{aligned}
$$
