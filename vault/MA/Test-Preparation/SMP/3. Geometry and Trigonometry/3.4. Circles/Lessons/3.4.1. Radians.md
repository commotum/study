## Introduction

Radians provide a natural way to measure angles based on the arc length of a circle. In this lesson, we'll practice converting between degrees and radians.

---

<a id="converting-from-degrees-to-radians"></a>
## Converting From Degrees to Radians

**Example:** The number of radians in a $300$-degree angle can be written as $a\pi$, where $a$ is a constant. What is the value of $a$?

**Explanation**

To convert $300^\circ$ to radians, we multiply it by

$$
\dfrac{\pi}{180^\circ}{:}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
300^{∘} \cdot \frac{π}{180^{∘}} &= \frac{300π}{180} \\
&= \frac{5π}{3} \\
&= \frac{5}{3}π
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the value of $a$ is

$$
\dfrac{5}{3}
$$

## Converting From Radians to Degrees

**Example:** An angle has a measure of $\dfrac{16\pi}{15}$ radians. What is the measure of the angle, in degrees?

**Explanation**

To convert radians to degrees, we multiply by

$$
\dfrac{180^\circ}{\pi}{:}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{16π}{15} \cdot \frac{180^{∘}}{π} &= \frac{16 \cdot 180^{∘}}{15} \\
&= \frac{2880^{∘}}{15} \\
&= 192^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding an Angle and Converting It to Degrees

**Example:** The measure of angle $X$ is $\dfrac{4\pi}{3}$ radians. The measure of angle $Y$ is $\dfrac{2\pi}{5}$ radians less than the measure of angle $X$. What is the measure of angle $Y$, in degrees?

**Explanation**

We are told that angle $X$ measures

$$
\dfrac{4\pi}{3}
$$

radians, and that angle $Y$ is

$$
\dfrac{2\pi}{5}
$$

radians less than angle $X$.

First, we compute the measure of angle $Y$ in radians:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{4π}{3} - \frac{2π}{5} &= \frac{20π}{15} - \frac{6π}{15} \\
&= \frac{14π}{15}
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the measure of angle $Y$ is

$$
\dfrac{14\pi}{15}
$$

radians. To convert this to degrees, we multiply it by

$$
\dfrac{180^\circ}{\pi}{:}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{14π}{15} \cdot \frac{180^{∘}}{π} &= \frac{14 \cdot 180^{∘}}{15} \\
&= \frac{2520^{∘}}{15} \\
&= 168^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding the Angle and Converting It to Radians

**Example:** In the $xy$-plane above, points $W$, $X$, $Y$, and $Z$ lie on a circle centered at $O$. The measures of angles $\angle WOX$ and $\angle YOZ$ are $50^\circ$ and $40^\circ$, respectively. What is the radian measure of angle $\angle XOY$?

![](<../Source/3.4.1. Radians/Images/34ac89ffe898aa13db4bbb253882e155.png>)

**Explanation**

We are given that

$$
\angle WOX = 50^\circ
$$

and

$$
\angle YOZ = 40^\circ
$$

and we are asked to find the radian measure of $\angle XOY$.

Because these three angles, $\angle WOX$, $\angle XOY$, and $\angle YOZ$, form a straight central angle from $W$ to $Z$, the sum of the three angles is $180^\circ$.

First, we subtract the measures of $\angle WOX$ and $\angle YOZ$ from $180^\circ$ to find the degree measure of $\angle XOY{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
m∠XOY &= 180^{∘} - 50^{∘} - 40^{∘} \\
&= 90^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

To convert this to radians, we multiply it by

$$
\dfrac{\pi}{180^\circ}{:}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
90^{∘} \cdot \frac{π}{180^{∘}} &= \frac{90π}{180} \\
&= \frac{π}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$
