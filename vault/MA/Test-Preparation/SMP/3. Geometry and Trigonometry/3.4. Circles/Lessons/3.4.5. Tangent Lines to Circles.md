## Introduction

Circle tangent-line problems almost always hinge on two facts: the radius to the point of tangency is perpendicular to the tangent line, and tangent segments from the same external point have equal lengths.

In this lesson, we’ll practice applying these geometric relationships to find slopes, equations, and distances involving tangent lines and circles.

---

<a id="finding-the-slope-of-the-tangent-to-a-circle-at-a-point"></a>
## Finding the Slope of the Tangent to a Circle at a Point

**Example:** A circle in the $xy$-plane has its center at $(2, 3)$. The line $n$ is tangent to this circle at the point $(5, 6)$. What is the slope of line $n$?

**Explanation**

We are given that the circle has its center at $(2,3)$ and that line $n$ is tangent to this circle at the point $(5,6)$. Therefore, the segment connecting the center $(2,3)$ with the point of tangency $(5,6)$ is a circle's radius.

The slope of a line (or line segment) passing through points $(x_1,y_1)$ and $(x_2, y_2)$ is given by

$$
m = \dfrac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}
$$

Then, the slope of the radius with endpoints at $(2,3)$ and $(5,6)$ is

$$
\begin{aligned}
m &= \frac{6 - 3}{5 - 2} \\
&= \frac{3}{3} \\
&= 1
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, recall that a line is tangent to a circle if and only if it's perpendicular to the radius drawn to the point of tangency. Consequently, $n$ is perpendicular to the radius at the point $(5,6)$. Hence, the slope of $n$ is the negative reciprocal of the slope of this radius, that is, the slope of $n$ is $-1$.

## Determining Points That Lie on the Tangent to a Circle

**Example:** A circle in the $xy$-plane has its center at $(1,1)$. The line $k$ is tangent to this circle at the point $(3, -1)$. Which of the following points also lies on line $k$?

1. $(1,-2)$
2. $(5,1)$
3. $(0,-1)$

**Explanation**

We are given that the circle has its center at $(1,1)$ and that line $k$ is tangent to this circle at the point $(3,-1)$. Therefore, the segment connecting the center $(1,1)$ with the point of tangency $(3,-1)$ is a circle's radius.

The slope of a line (or line segment) passing through points $(x_1,y_1)$ and $(x_2, y_2)$ is given by

$$
m = \dfrac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}
$$

Then, the slope of the radius with endpoints at $(1,1)$ and $(3,-1)$ is

$$
\begin{aligned}
m &= \frac{-1 - 1}{3 - 1} \\
&= \frac{-2}{2} \\
&=-1
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, recall that a line is tangent to a circle if and only if it's perpendicular to the radius drawn to the point of tangency. Consequently, $k$ is perpendicular to the radius at the point $(3, -1)$. Hence, the slope of $k$ is the negative reciprocal of the slope of this radius, that is, the slope of $k$ is $1$.

Since the slope of line $k$ is the same between any two points on line $k$, a point lies on line $k$ if the slope of the line segment connecting the point and $(3,-1)$ is $1$. Taking this into account, we proceed to verify which of the given points is on the line $k$.

- The slope of the line segment with endpoints at $(1,-2)$ and $(3,-1)$ is
$m|= (-1 - (-2))/(3 - 1); = \frac{1}{2}; \ne 1$.
Therefore, $(1,-2)$ does not lie on the line $k$.
- The slope of the line segment with endpoints at $(5,1)$ and $(3,-1)$ is
$m|= \frac{-1 - 1}{3 - 5}; = \frac{-2}{-2}; = 1$.
Therefore, $(5,1)$ lies on the line $k$.

- The slope of the line segment with endpoints at $(0,-1)$ and $(3,-1)$ is
$m|= (-1 - (-1))/(3 - 0); = \frac{0}{3}; = 0; \ne 1$.
Therefore, $(0,-1)$ does not lie on the line $k$.

Therefore, the correct answer is "$(5,1)$ only."

## Finding the Distance From a Circle's Center to the Intersection of Two Tangents

**Example:** A circle has center $U$, and points $T$ and $V$ lie on the circle. Line segments $\overline{TW}$ and $\overline{VW}$ are tangent to the circle at points $T$ and $V$, respectively. If the radius of the circle is $36$ kilometers and the perimeter of quadrilateral $UTWV$ is $226$ kilometers, what is the distance, in kilometers, between points $U$ and $W$?

**Explanation**

We are given that the radius of the circle is $36$ kilometers. Since the points $T$ and $V$ lie on the circle, the segments $\overline{TU}$ and $\overline{VU}$ are both radii. Therefore,

$$
TU = VU = 36
$$

![](<../Source/3.4.5. Tangent Lines to Circles/Images/759fb6d875a62db358604ce854f340ef.png>)

We are also given that the perimeter of quadrilateral $UTWV$ is $226$ kilometers. Since the perimeter of a quadrilateral is equal to the sum of the lengths of the sides of the quadrilateral, we can obtain the value of $TW$ (or $VW$) as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
TU + TW + VW + VU &= 226 \\
36 + TW + VW + 36 &= 226 \\
TW + VW &= 154
\end{aligned}
$$

On the other hand, two tangent segments with a common endpoint in a circle's exterior are congruent. Since $\overline{TW}$ and $\overline{VW}$ are tangent to the circle, we have $TW = VW$. Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
TW + VW &= 154 \\
TW + TW &= 154 \\
2TW &= 154 \\
TW &= 77
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, a segment that's tangent to a circle is perpendicular to the radius of the circle at the point of tangency. Hence, $\overline{TW}$ is perpendicular to $\overline{TU}$. Moreover, $\triangle{UTW}$ is a right triangle with $\angle{T}$ a right angle.

![](<../Source/3.4.5. Tangent Lines to Circles/Images/c24038384d935856eedd0e31dafaa31b.png>)

Applying the Pythagorean theorem, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
UW^{2} &= TU^{2} + TW^{2} \\
UW^{2} &= 36^{2} + 77^{2} \\
UW^{2} &= 1, 296 + 5, 929 \\
UW^{2} &= 7, 225 \\
UW &= \sqrt{7, 225} \\
UW &= 85
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the distance between points $U$ and $W$ is $85$ kilometers.
