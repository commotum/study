## Introduction

Arc length problems link geometry and proportional reasoning by comparing parts of a circle to the whole.

Practicing these relationships helps us see circles as proportional systems, making it easier to work efficiently with angles and measurements.

## Finding the Arc Length of a Sector

**Example:** A circle with center $O$ has a total circumference of $50$. In the diagram above, $\angle EOA = 72^\circ$. What is the length of minor arc $\overset{\frown}{EA}$?

![](<../Source/3.4.2. Arc Lengths of Sectors/Images/89792923d14404a9df4954edd8ba1051.png>)

**Explanation**

To find the length of a circular arc with central angle $\theta$ and circumference $C$, we use the formula:

$$
\text{Arc Length} = \frac{\theta}{360^\circ} \cdot C
$$

The given circle has a circumference of $C = 50$, and the central angle is

$$
\angle EOA = 72^\circ
$$

so

$$
\theta = 72^\circ
$$

Therefore, the length of minor arc $\overset{\frown}{EA}$ is

$$
\begin{aligned}
Length(EA^(⌢)) &= \frac{72^{∘}}{360^{∘}} \cdot 50 \\
&= \frac{1}{5} \cdot 50 \\
&= 10
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding a Circumference Given an Arc Length

**Example:** In a circle centered at $G$, points $P$ and $Q$ lie on the circumference such that the measure of arc $\overset{\frown}{PQ}$ is $80^\circ$, and the arc length of $\overset{\frown}{PQ}$ is $8 \textrm{m}$. What is the circumference, in meters, of the circle?

**Explanation**

The length of a circular arc with central angle $\theta$ and circumference $C$ is given by:

$$
\text{Arc Length} = \dfrac{\theta}{360^\circ} \cdot C
$$

We are given:

- Arc length of $\overset{\frown}{PQ}$ is $8\textrm{m}$
- The central angle subtending the arc is $\theta = 80^\circ$

Therefore, using the arc length formula, we find that the circumference $C$ of the circle is

$$
\begin{aligned}
Length(PQ^(⌢)) &= \frac{80^{∘}}{360^{∘}} \cdot C \\
8 &= \frac{2}{9} \cdot C \\
C &= 8 \cdot \frac{9}{2} \\
&= 36m
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding a Ratio of Arc Length to Circumference

**Example:** Points $L$ and $M$ lie on a circle with radius $9$, and arc $\overset{\frown}{LM}$ has length $\dfrac{3\pi}{2}$. What fraction of the circumference of the circle is the length of arc $\overset{\frown}{LM}$?

**Explanation**

We are told:

- The radius of the circle is $r = 9$
- The arc length of $\overset{\frown}{LM}$ is $\dfrac{3\pi}{2}$

First, we compute the circumference of the circle:

$$
C = 2\pi r = 2\pi(9) = 18\pi
$$

Hence, the fraction of the circumference that this arc represents is

$$
\begin{aligned}
Fraction &= ((3π/2))/(18π) \\
&= \frac{3π}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{18π} \\
&= \frac{3π}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{18π} \\
&= \frac{1}{12}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, arc $\overset{\frown}{LM}$ is

$$
\dfrac{1}{12}
$$

of the circle's circumference.

## Finding the Arc Length Given Relations Between Arcs in a Circle

**Example:** Note: Figure not drawn to scale.

![](<../Source/3.4.2. Arc Lengths of Sectors/Images/732683b721a109c60d4885ccbc69a724.png>)

The circle shown has center $O$, circumference $90\pi$, and diameters $\overline{FD}$ and $\overline{GE}$. The length of arc $\overset{\frown}{FE}$ is one fourth the length of arc $\overset{\frown}{FG}$. What is the length of arc $\overset{\frown}{GD}$?

**Explanation**

We are told that

$$
\textrm{Length}(\overset{\frown}{FE}) = \dfrac{1}{4} \cdot \textrm{Length}(\overset{\frown}{FG})
$$

The diameters $\overline{FD}$ and $\overline{GE}$ divide the circle into four arcs: $\overset{\frown}{FG},\, \overset{\frown}{GD},\, \overset{\frown}{FE}$, and $\overset{\frown}{ED}$.

Let

$$
\textrm{Length}(\overset{\frown}{FG}) = x
$$

Since each pair of opposite arcs is congruent, we have:

- $\textrm{Length}(\overset{\frown}{FG}) = \textrm{Length}(\overset{\frown}{ED}) = x$
- $\textrm{Length}(\overset{\frown}{GD}) = \textrm{Length}(\overset{\frown}{FE}) = \dfrac{1}{4}x$

As the total arc length (circumference) is $90\pi$, we write:

$$
\textrm{Length}(\overset{\frown}{FG}) + \textrm{Length}(\overset{\frown}{GD}) + \textrm{Length}(\overset{\frown}{ED}) + \textrm{Length}(\overset{\frown}{FE}) = 90\pi
$$

Next, we substitute each arc length with its expression in terms of $x$ and solve for $x{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
x + \frac{1}{4}x + x + \frac{1}{4}x &= 90π \\
\frac{5}{2}x &= 90π \\
x &= 90π \cdot \frac{2}{5} \\
&= 36π
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the length of arc $\overset{\frown}{GD}$ is

$$
\textrm{Length}(\overset{\frown}{GD}) = \dfrac{1}{4}x = \dfrac{1}{4}(36\pi) = 9\pi
$$
