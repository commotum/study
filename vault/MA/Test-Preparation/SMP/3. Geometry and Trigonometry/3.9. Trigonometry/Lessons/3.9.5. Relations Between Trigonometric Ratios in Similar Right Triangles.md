## Introduction

On the math section of the SAT exam, we may see problems involving similar right triangles, where we must recognize that the trigonometric ratios for corresponding angles remain the same.

In this lesson, we’ll practice applying this principle to connect sine, cosine, and tangent values across similar triangles.

---

## Determining Trigonometric Ratios From Corresponding Ratios in Similar Triangles

**Example:** Triangle $KLM$ is similar to triangle $NOP$, where angle $K$ corresponds to angle $N$ and angles $L$ and $O$ are right angles. If $\sin(K) = \dfrac{20}{29}$, what is the value of $\sin(N)$?

**Explanation**

First, let's sketch a figure (not drawn to scale).

![](<../Source/3.9.5. Relations Between Trigonometric Ratios in Similar Right Triangles/Images/2e83f3b7e2c27ad4cac67aa5747d3c2f.png>)

Since triangle $KLM$ is similar to triangle $NOP$, we know that corresponding angles are congruent. Angle $K$ corresponds to angle $N$, so $\angle K \cong \angle N$.

The sine of an angle in a right triangle is defined as the ratio of the length of the side opposite the angle to the hypotenuse.

Since the triangles are similar and these angles are congruent, their sine values must be equal.

So, we have

$$
\sin(N) = \sin(K) = \dfrac{20}{29}
$$

## Determining Trigonometric Ratios From Corresponding Ratios in Similar Triangles With Simplifications

**Example:** The side lengths of right triangle $WXY$ are given as $WX = 198$, $XY = 336$, and $YW = 390$. Triangle $WXY$ is similar to triangle $GHI$, where $X$ corresponds to $H$ and $Y$ corresponds to $I$. What is the value of $\tan I$?

**Explanation**

We are told that triangle $WXY$ is a right triangle with side lengths $WX = 198$, $XY = 336$, and $YW = 390$. Since the longest side of a triangle is always opposite the largest angle, and $YW = 390$ is the longest side, it must be the hypotenuse. This means the right angle is at vertex $X$, so $\angle W$ and $\angle Y$ are both acute.

Let's sketch a figure (not drawn to scale).

![](<../Source/3.9.5. Relations Between Trigonometric Ratios in Similar Right Triangles/Images/44c9943da3bc7663dba76d4602726f4b.png>)

Since triangle $WXY$ is similar to triangle $GHI$, and $Y$ corresponds to $I$, then $\angle I \cong \angle Y$ and therefore

$$
\tan I = \tan Y
$$

To find $\tan Y$, we examine triangle $WXY$.

The tangent of an acute angle in a right triangle is defined as

$$
\tan(\text{angle}) = \dfrac{\text{opposite side}}{\text{adjacent side}}
$$

In triangle $WXY$, the side opposite angle $Y$ is $WX = 198$, and the side adjacent to angle $Y$ is $XY = 336$. So

$$
\tan Y = \dfrac{WX}{XY} = \dfrac{198}{336}
$$

Now, we simplify the fraction:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{198}{336} &= \frac{33 \cdot 6}{56 \cdot 6} \\
&= \frac{33}{56}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\tan I = \tan Y = \dfrac{33}{56}
$$

## Finding a Tangent Given a Tangent From a Similar Triangle

**Example:** Triangle $EFG$ is similar to triangle $HIJ$, where angle $F$ corresponds to angle $I$ and angle $G$ corresponds to angle $J$. Angles $G$ and $J$ are right angles. If $\tan F = \dfrac{12}{35}$, what is the value of $\tan H$? Do not round your answer.

**Explanation**

First, let's sketch a figure (not drawn to scale).

![](<../Source/3.9.5. Relations Between Trigonometric Ratios in Similar Right Triangles/Images/07fff5b06148974044ed114987d70c8f.png>)

We are asked to find $\tan H$ in triangle $HIJ$. To do this, we will use the definition of tangent along with corresponding side ratios.

We are given that

$$
\tan F = \dfrac{12}{35}
$$

In a right triangle, the tangent of an acute angle is the ratio of the opposite side to the adjacent side, then we have

$$
\tan F = \dfrac{EG}{GF} = \dfrac{12}{35}
$$

Since triangle $EFG$ is similar to triangle $HIJ$, we know that corresponding sides are proportional:

$$
\dfrac{EG}{HJ} = \dfrac{GF}{JI}
$$

Rewriting this, we get

$$
\dfrac{JI}{HJ} = \dfrac{GF}{EG}
$$

So, the tangent of angle $H$ is

$$
\begin{aligned}
\tan H &= \frac{JI}{HJ} \\
&= \frac{GF}{EG} \\
&= \frac{1}{\frac{EG}{GF}} \\
&= \frac{1}{\frac{12}{35}} \\
&= 35 12
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding a Sine or Cosine Given a Tangent From a Similar Triangle

**Example:** Triangle $JKL$ is similar to triangle $OPQ$, where angle $J$ corresponds to angle $O$ and angles $L$ and $Q$ are right angles. The length of $\overline{JK}$ is $2.6$ times the length of $\overline{OP}$. If $\tan J = \dfrac{33}{56}$, what is the value of $\sin O$? Do not round your answer.

**Explanation**

First, let's sketch a figure (not drawn to scale).

![](<../Source/3.9.5. Relations Between Trigonometric Ratios in Similar Right Triangles/Images/e4c2b483aa9f1d5098b4a2a32f0297c6.png>)

Since triangle $JKL$ is similar to triangle $OPQ$, and $J$ corresponds to $O$, we know that $\angle J \cong \angle O$. So,

$$
\sin O = \sin J
$$

We are given

$$
\tan J = \dfrac{33}{56}
$$

The tangent of an acute angle is the ratio of the opposite side to the adjacent side:

$$
\tan J = \dfrac{KL}{JL} = \dfrac{33}{56}
$$

This tells us that the legs of triangle $JKL$ are in a $33:56$ ratio. So we let $KL = 33k$ and $JL = 56k$ for some positive constant $k$. We use the Pythagorean Theorem to find the hypotenuse $JK{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
JK &= \sqrt{(33k)^{2} + (56k)^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{1089k^{2} + 3136k^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{4225k^{2}} \\
&= 65k
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we compute $\sin J$, which is the ratio of the opposite side to the hypotenuse:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sin J &= \frac{KL}{JK} \\
&= \frac{33k}{65k} \\
&= 33 65
\end{aligned}
$$
