## Introduction

Sometimes, we may encounter multi-step geometry problems that require using similar triangles and trigonometric ratios together to find missing sides or distances.

In this topic, we’ll get hands-on practice setting up proportions, applying trigonometric ratios, and solving for unknowns.

## Finding the Hypotenuse Given a Leg and a Tangent From a Similar Triangle

**Example:** Triangle $ABC$ is similar to triangle $DEF$, where $A$ corresponds to $D$ and $C$ corresponds to $F$. Angles $C$ and $F$ are right angles. If $\tan(A) = \dfrac{5}{12}$ and $DF = 36$, what is the length of $\overline{DE}$?

**Explanation**

First, let's sketch a figure (not drawn to scale).

![](<../Source/3.9.6. Problem-Solving Using Trigonometric Ratios in Similar Right Triangles/Images/21d247da3b97ab8f6b029775b6990b46.png>)

To find the length of $\overline{DE}$, we use triangle similarity and the definition of tangent.

Since triangle $ABC$ is similar to triangle $DEF$, and $A$ corresponds to $D$, we know that $\angle A \cong \angle D$. Therefore,

$$
\tan(D) = \tan(A) = \dfrac{5}{12}
$$

In triangle $DEF$, $\angle F$ is the right angle. The tangent of an acute angle is defined as

$$
\tan(\text{angle}) = \dfrac{\text{opposite side}}{\text{adjacent side}}
$$

Here, the side opposite $\angle D$ is $\overline{EF}$, and the side adjacent to $\angle D$ is $\overline{DF}$.
Thus,

$$
\tan(D) = \dfrac{EF}{DF}
$$

Now, we substitute

$$
\tan(D) = \dfrac{5}{12}
$$

and $DF=36$ into the last expression and solve for $EF{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\tan (D) &= \frac{EF}{DF} \\
\frac{5}{12} &= \frac{EF}{36} \\
\frac{5}{12} \cdot 36 &= EF \\
EF &= 15
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we find the hypotenuse $\overline{DE}$ using the Pythagorean Theorem:

$$
\begin{aligned}
DE &= \sqrt{DF^{2} + EF^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{36^{2} + 15^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{1296 + 225} \\
&= \sqrt{1521} \\
&= 39
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding a Leg Given a Tangent and Some Measures From a Similar Triangle

**Example:** In the figure shown, $\tan V = \dfrac{3}{4}$. If $VY = 45$ and $WX = 8$, what is the length of $\overline{WZ}$?

![](<../Source/3.9.6. Problem-Solving Using Trigonometric Ratios in Similar Right Triangles/Images/9292c7c0b17d7206090ba0d742152164.png>)

*(Note: Figure not drawn to scale)*

**Explanation**

To find the length of $\overline{WZ}$, we use triangle similarity and the given trigonometric ratio to determine missing side lengths.

We are given that

$$
\tan V = \dfrac{3}{4}
$$

and triangle $VXY$ is similar to triangle $VWZ$ by construction.

In triangle $VXY$, since $\angle X$ is a right angle, then

$$
\tan V = \dfrac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}} = \dfrac{XY}{VX}
$$

Since

$$
\tan V = \dfrac{3}{4}
$$

the side $XY$ and the side $VX$ are in a $3:4$ ratio. So we let $XY = 3k$ and $VX = 4k$ for some positive constant $k$.

We are told that $VY = 45$. Applying the Pythagorean Theorem, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
VY &= \sqrt{(VX)^{2} + (XY)^{2}} \\
45 &= \sqrt{(4k)^{2} + (3k)^{2}} \\
45 &= \sqrt{16k^{2} + 9k^{2}} \\
45 &= \sqrt{25k^{2}} \\
45 &= 5k \\
k &= 9
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we compute $XY$ and $VX{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
XY &= 3k = 3 \cdot 9 = 27 \\
VX &= 4k = 4 \cdot 9 = 36
\end{aligned}
$$

We are also told that $WX=8$, which implies that

$$
\begin{aligned}
VW &= VX - WX \\
&= 36 - 8 \\
&= 28
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, since triangles $VXY$ and $VWZ$ are similar, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{WZ}{XY} &= \frac{VW}{VX} \\
\frac{WZ}{27} &= \frac{28}{36} \\
WZ &= \frac{27 \cdot 28}{36} \\
WZ &= 21
\end{aligned}
$$
