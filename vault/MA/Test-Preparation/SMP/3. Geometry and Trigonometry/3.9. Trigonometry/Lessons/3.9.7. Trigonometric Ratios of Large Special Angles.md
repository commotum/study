## Introduction

The math section of the SAT exam sometimes asks to evaluate simple trigonometric functions at large angles. These evaluations are made by reducing the argument of the trigonometric function using periodicity.

In this lesson, we’ll practice reducing large angles to their coterminal or reference angles, identifying the relevant quadrants, and determining the correct sign and value of the trigonometric ratio.

## Finding the Sine of a Large Angle

**Example:** Find the value of $\sin\left(\dfrac{127\pi}{6}\right)$.

**Explanation**

First, we find an angle that's coterminal with

$$
\dfrac{127\pi}{6}
$$

and lies in the range $[MATH: [0,2\pi).]$

Recall that coterminal angles differ by a multiple of $2\pi$ radians.

So, we divide $127$ by

$$
2 \cdot 6 = 12
$$

with remainder:

$$
127 \div 12 = 10 \ \text{R} \ 7
$$

Therefore,

$$
127 = 10 \cdot 12 + 7
$$

As a result, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{127π}{6} &= ((10 \cdot 12 + 7)π)/(6) \\
&= 10 \cdot \frac{12π}{6} + \frac{7π}{6} \\
&= 10 \cdot 2π + \frac{7π}{6}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\sin \left(\dfrac{127\pi}{6}\right) = \sin \left(\dfrac{7\pi}{6}\right)
$$

Let's remind ourselves of the special trigonometric ratios, as given by the unit circle.

![](<../Source/3.9.7. Trigonometric Ratios of Large Special Angles/Images/385bb5e32dac34a5f58c04a6b7c648ea.png>)

From the unit circle, we see that the angle

$$
\dfrac{7\pi}{6}
$$

corresponds to the point

$$
\left(-\dfrac{\sqrt 3}{2},-\dfrac{1}{2}\right)
$$

Since the sine of the angle corresponds to the $y$-coordinate, we have

$$
\sin\left(\dfrac{7\pi}{6}\right) = -\dfrac{1}{2}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\sin\left(\dfrac{127\pi}{6}\right) = -\dfrac{1}{2}
$$

## Finding the Cosine of a Large Angle

**Example:** Find the value of $\cos\left(\dfrac{159\pi}{4}\right)$.

**Explanation**

First, we find an angle that's coterminal with

$$
\dfrac{159\pi}{4}
$$

and lies in the range $[MATH: [0,2\pi).]$

Recall that coterminal angles differ by a multiple of $2\pi$ radians.

So, we divide $159$ by

$$
2 \cdot 4 = 8
$$

with remainder:

$$
159 \div 8 = 19 \ \text{R} \ 7
$$

Therefore,

$$
159 = 19 \cdot 8 + 7
$$

As a result, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{159π}{4} &= ((19 \cdot 8 + 7)π)/(4) \\
&= 19 \cdot \frac{8π}{4} + \frac{7π}{4} \\
&= 19 \cdot 2π + \frac{7π}{4}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\cos \left(\dfrac{159\pi}{4}\right) = \cos \left(\dfrac{7\pi}{4}\right)
$$

Let's remind ourselves of the special trigonometric ratios, as given by the unit circle.

![](<../Source/3.9.7. Trigonometric Ratios of Large Special Angles/Images/2854c052a435b9ac3a9da41c17faa652.png>)

From the unit circle, we see that the angle

$$
\dfrac{7\pi}{4}
$$

corresponds to the point

$$
\left(\dfrac{\sqrt 2}{2},-\dfrac{\sqrt 2}{2}\right)
$$

Since the cosine of the angle corresponds to the $x$-coordinate, we have

$$
\cos \left(\dfrac{7\pi}{4}\right) = \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\cos\left(\dfrac{159\pi}{4}\right) = \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}
$$

## Finding the Tangent of a Large Angle

**Example:** Find the value of $\tan\left(\dfrac{215\pi}{4}\right)$.

**Explanation**

First, we find an angle that's coterminal with

$$
\dfrac{215\pi}{4}
$$

and lies in the range $[MATH: [0,2\pi).]$

Recall that coterminal angles differ by a multiple of $2\pi$ radians.

So, we divide $215$ by

$$
2 \cdot 4 = 8
$$

with remainder:

$$
215 \div 8 = 26 \ \text{R} \ 7
$$

Therefore,

$$
215 = 26 \cdot 8 + 7
$$

As a result, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{215π}{4} &= ((26 \cdot 8 + 7)π)/(4) \\
&= 26 \cdot \frac{8π}{4} + \frac{7π}{4} \\
&= 26 \cdot 2π + \frac{7π}{4}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\tan \left(\dfrac{215\pi}{4}\right) = \tan \left(\dfrac{7\pi}{4}\right)
$$

Let's remind ourselves of the special trigonometric ratios, as given by the unit circle.

![](<../Source/3.9.7. Trigonometric Ratios of Large Special Angles/Images/9685e77e05f791de110abebf3d62a868.png>)

From the unit circle, we see that the angle

$$
\dfrac{7\pi}{4}
$$

corresponds to the point

$$
\left(\dfrac{\sqrt 2}{2}, -\dfrac{\sqrt 2}{2}\right)
$$

- Since the cosine of the angle corresponds to the $x$-coordinate, we have $\cos \left(\dfrac{7\pi}{4}\right) = \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$.
- Since the sine of the angle corresponds to the $y$-coordinate, we have $\sin \left(\dfrac{7\pi}{4}\right) = -\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$.

Therefore, using the fact that

$$
\tan\theta = \dfrac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}
$$

we have

$$
\tan \left(\dfrac{7\pi}{4}\right) = \dfrac{\sin \left(\dfrac{7\pi}{4}\right)}{\cos\left(\dfrac{7\pi}{4}\right)} = \dfrac{-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}}{\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}} = -1
$$
