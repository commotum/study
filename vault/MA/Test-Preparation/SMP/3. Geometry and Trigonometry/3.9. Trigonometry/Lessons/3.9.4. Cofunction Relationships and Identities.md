## Introduction

Cofunction questions test our understanding of how sine, cosine, and tangent relate to complementary angles in right triangles.

In this topic, we’ll practice recognizing and applying cofunction relationships to simplify trigonometric expressions and solve for missing values.

## Determining Sine and Cosine Given the Cofunction of the Complementary Angle

**Example:** In a right triangle $\triangle MNO$, the sum of the measures of angles $\angle M$ and $\angle N$ is $90^\circ$. If $\cos(m\angle M) = \dfrac{16}{25}$, what is the value of $\sin(m\angle N)$?

**Explanation**

In any right triangle, the two non-right angles are complementary. Since triangle $\triangle MNO$ is a right triangle, angles $\angle M$ and $\angle N$ must satisfy

$$
m\angle M + m\angle N = 90^\circ
$$

Therefore,

$$
m\angle N = 90^\circ - m\angle M
$$

Hence,

$$
\sin(m\angle N) = \sin(90^\circ - m\angle M)
$$

Using the trigonometric identity

$$
\sin(90^\circ - \theta) = \cos\theta
$$

we find that

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sin (m∠N) &= \sin (90^{∘} - m∠M) \\
&= \cos (m∠M)
\end{aligned}
$$

We are told

$$
\cos(m\angle M) = \dfrac{16}{25}
$$

So, it follows that

$$
\sin(m\angle N) = \dfrac{16}{25}
$$

## Simplifying an Expression With Cofunction Identities

**Example:** Which of the following expressions is equivalent to

$(\sin 12^\circ)(\cos 78^\circ) + (\cos 12^\circ)(\sin 78^\circ)$?

1. $(\sin 12^\circ)^2 + (\cos 12^\circ)^2$
2. $\sin 12^\circ + \cos 12^\circ$
3. $2(\sin 12^\circ)^2$
4. $2(\cos 12^\circ)^2$

**Explanation**

First, notice that all the answer choices only involve trigonometric functions of $12^\circ$. So, we need to rewrite the original expression entirely in terms of $12^\circ$.

Since

$$
78^\circ = 90^\circ - 12^\circ
$$

we can do this with the cofunction identities

$$
\sin(90^\circ - \theta) = \cos\theta, \qquad \cos(90^\circ - \theta) = \sin\theta
$$

Replacing every instance of $78^\circ$ with $90^\circ - 12^\circ$, we have

$$
(\sin 12^\circ)(\cos (90^\circ - 12^\circ)) + (\cos 12^\circ)(\sin (90^\circ - 12^\circ))
$$

Applying the cofunction identities, the expression becomes

$(\sin 12^\circ)(\sin 12^\circ) + (\cos 12^\circ)(\cos 12^\circ)$
which we can write as

$$
(\sin 12^\circ)^2 + (\cos 12^\circ)^2
$$

which further simplifies to $1$ by the Pythagorean identity.

## Finding a Tangent Given the Tangent of the Complementary Angle

**Example:** Right triangle $ABC$ is shown. What is the value of $\tan{C}$?

![](<../Source/3.9.4. Cofunction Relationships and Identities/Images/b8a32da0cd4238dea5f32d4146337b2e.png>)

(Note: Figure not drawn to scale)

**Explanation**

Since triangle $ABC$ is a right triangle and angle $A$ is $60^\circ$, we know angle $C$ is

$$
90^\circ - 60^\circ = 30^\circ
$$

So, we are asked to compute $\tan 30^\circ$. This is a known value:

$$
\tan 30^\circ = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}
$$

Therefore, the value of $\tan C$ is

$$
\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}
$$

## Finding a Cosine Given the Cosine of the Complementary Angle in a Right Triangle

**Example:** In a triangle $\triangle LMN$, angle $\angle L$ is a right angle and $\cos(m\angle N) = \dfrac{28}{53}$. Determine the value of $\cos(m\angle M)$.

**Explanation**

The cosine of an acute angle $\theta$ in a right triangle is given by
$θ$

A sketch of such a triangle $\triangle LMN$ is given below where angle $\angle L$ is a right angle,

$$
\cos(m\angle N) = \dfrac{28}{53}
$$

and $k > 0$.

![](<../Source/3.9.4. Cofunction Relationships and Identities/Images/af9c2802990f1dfdcddc0f73c5bc3c13.png>)

(Note: Figure not drawn to scale)

By the Pythagorean theorem,

$$
\begin{aligned}
LM^{2} &= MN^{2} - LN^{2} \\
&= (53k)^{2} - (28k)^{2} \\
&= 2809k^{2} - 784k^{2} \\
&= 2025k^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, we have
$LM = 45k$.

Now, for angle $\angle M$, the adjacent side is $LM$ and the hypotenuse is $MN$.
Therefore,

$$
\cos(m\angle M) = \dfrac{LM}{MN} = \dfrac{45k}{53k} = \dfrac{45}{53}
$$

Thus,

$$
\cos(m\angle M) = \bbox[4pt,border: 1px solid lightgray]{\dfrac{45}{53}}
$$
