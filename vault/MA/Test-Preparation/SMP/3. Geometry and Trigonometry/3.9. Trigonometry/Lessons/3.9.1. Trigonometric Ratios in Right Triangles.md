## Computing the Sine of an Angle From a Diagram

**Example:** (Note: Figure not drawn to scale)

![](<../Source/3.9.1. Trigonometric Ratios in Right Triangles/Images/ce25751b691197bec3a1453310a6858f.png>)

In the right triangle shown, what is the value of $\sin E$?

**Explanation**

We are given a right triangle with a right angle at point $D$, and we are asked to find $\sin E$.

By definition, the sine of an angle in a right triangle is the ratio of the length of the *opposite side* to the *hypotenuse*. In this triangle:

- Angle $E$ is at vertex $E$.
- The side *opposite* angle $E$ is $\overline{DF}$, which has length $8$.
- The *hypotenuse* is $\overline{EF}$, which has length $17$.

So,

$$
\sin E = \dfrac{8}{17}
$$

## Computing the Cosine of an Angle From a Diagram

**Example:** What is the value of $\cos(R)$ in the triangle shown?

![](<../Source/3.9.1. Trigonometric Ratios in Right Triangles/Images/714745a438f58cd870b20ba17cdaa527.png>)

(Note: Figure not drawn to scale)

**Explanation**

We are given a right triangle with right angle at point $P$, and we are asked to find $\cos(R)$.

By definition, the cosine of an angle in a right triangle is the ratio of the length of the *adjacent side* to the *hypotenuse*. In this triangle:

- The *adjacent side* to angle $R$ is $\overline{RP}$, which has length $20$.
- The *hypotenuse* is $\overline{RQ}$, which has length $29$.

So,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\cos (R) &= \frac{20}{29}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Computing the Tangent of an Angle From a Diagram

**Example:** In the figure above, what is the value of $\tan(K)$?

![](<../Source/3.9.1. Trigonometric Ratios in Right Triangles/Images/f1f7da703bc4cbbab5a11ca59d6d0da8.png>)

*(Note: Figure not drawn to scale)*

**Explanation**

The tangent of an angle is equal to the opposite divided by the adjacent. Relative to the angle $K$, the opposite side is $\overline{JL}$, and the adjacent is $\overline{JK}$. Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\tan K &= \frac{JL}{JK} \\
&= \frac{7}{24}
\end{aligned}
$$
