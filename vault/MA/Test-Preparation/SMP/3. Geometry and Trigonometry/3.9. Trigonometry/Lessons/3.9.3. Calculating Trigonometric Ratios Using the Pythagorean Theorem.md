## Introduction

Trigonometry problems sometimes require us to find a missing side or trigonometric ratio when only two sides of a right triangle are given. These questions test the ability to connect the Pythagorean theorem with sine, cosine, and tangent definitions.

In this lesson, we’ll practice using the Pythagorean theorem to complete missing triangle information and then find trigonometric ratios.

## Finding the Cosine of an Angle Given the Legs of a Right Triangle

**Example:** In the triangle shown, what is the value of $\cos \theta$?

![](<../Source/3.9.3. Calculating Trigonometric Ratios Using the Pythagorean Theorem/Images/7c9c19729abcdda1d9a3274f8139dd3b.png>)

(Note: Figure not drawn to scale)

**Explanation**

In a right triangle, the cosine of an angle is defined as the ratio of the *adjacent side* to the *hypotenuse*.

In the given triangle, the adjacent side to $\theta$ has length $4$.

To find the hypotenuse, $c$, we apply the Pythagorean Theorem:

$$
\begin{aligned}
c^{2} &= 3^{2} + 4^{2} \\
c^{2} &= 9 + 16 \\
c^{2} &= 25 \\
c &= \sqrt{25} \\
c &= 5
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus,

$$
\cos \theta = \dfrac{4}{5}
$$

## Finding the Sine of an Angle Given the Adjacent Leg and Hypotenuse of a Right Triangle

**Example:** In the triangle shown, what is the value of $\sin \theta$?

![](<../Source/3.9.3. Calculating Trigonometric Ratios Using the Pythagorean Theorem/Images/90122659334d1fe9f8d24d7d9881b7e4.png>)

(Note: Figure not drawn to scale)

**Explanation**

In a right triangle, the sine of an angle is defined as the ratio of the *opposite side* to the *hypotenuse*.

In the given triangle, the hypotenuse has length $5$, and the adjacent side to $\theta$ has length $4$.

The side opposite $\theta$ is unknown, so we apply the Pythagorean Theorem to solve for it:

$$
\begin{aligned}
5^{2} &= 4^{2} + x^{2} \\
25 &= 16 + x^{2} \\
x^{2} &= 9 \\
x &= \sqrt{9} \\
x &= 3
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus,

$$
\sin \theta = \dfrac{3}{5}
$$
