## Finding the Length of a Leg in a Right Triangle Using Trigonometry

**Example:** *(Note: Figure not drawn to scale)*

![](<../Source/3.9.2. Calculating Lengths and Areas Using Trigonometry/Images/6f2df125c604c093ea6f57ca4b9d99fa.png>)

Triangle $JKL$ above is a right triangle, and $\sin(L) = \dfrac{4}{5}$. What is the length of side $\overline{JK}$?

**Explanation**

In a right triangle, the sine of an angle is the ratio of the length of the opposite side to the hypotenuse:

$$
\sin(L) = \dfrac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}} = \dfrac{JK}{JL}
$$

Therefore, substituting

$$
\sin(L) = \dfrac{4}{5}
$$

and $JL = 60$, we find that the length of side $\overline{JK}$ is

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{4}{5} &= \frac{JK}{60} \\
JK &= 60 \cdot \frac{4}{5} \\
&= 12 \cdot 4 \\
&= 48
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding the Perimeter of a Right Triangle Using Trigonometry

**Example:** In triangle $PQR$, angle $R$ is a right angle and the length of $\overline{QR}$ is $24$ units. If $\tan Q = \dfrac{5}{12}$, what is the perimeter, in units, of triangle $PQR$?

**Explanation**

First, since $\angle R$ is a right angle, triangle $PQR$ is a right triangle with the right angle at vertex $R$, which can be represented as follows:

![](<../Source/3.9.2. Calculating Lengths and Areas Using Trigonometry/Images/547bb160d03d65054a2ed0b5e19020cb.png>)

We are told

$$
\tan Q = \dfrac{5}{12}
$$

and $QR = 24$. Using the definition of tangent, we have:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\tan Q &= \frac{opposite}{adjacent} \\
\frac{5}{12} &= \frac{PR}{QR} \\
\frac{5}{12} &= \frac{PR}{24} \\
PR &= 24 \cdot \frac{5}{12} \\
&= 10
\end{aligned}
$$

To find the hypotenuse of the triangle, $\overline{PQ}$, we apply the Pythagorean Theorem:

$$
\begin{aligned}
PQ &= \sqrt{(QR)^{2} + (PR)^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{24^{2} + 10^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{576 + 100} \\
&= \sqrt{676} \\
&= 26
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the perimeter of triangle $PQR$ is

$$
\begin{aligned}
Perimeter &= PQ + PR + QR \\
&= 26 + 10 + 24 \\
&= 60
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding the Area of a Right Triangle Using Trigonometry

**Example:** In triangle $ABC$, angle $B$ is a right angle, the measure of angle $C$ is $57^\circ$, and the length of $\overline{BC}$ is $8$ units. If the area, in square units, of triangle $ABC$ can be represented by the expression $k \tan 57^\circ$, where $k$ is a constant, what is the value of $k$?

**Explanation**

We want to find the area of triangle $ABC$ and express it in the form $k\tan 57^\circ$.

The area of a triangle is given by the formula:

$$
\text{Area} = \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot \text{base} \cdot \text{height}
$$

Since triangle $ABC$ is a right triangle with

$$
\angle B = 90^\circ
$$

and

$$
\angle C = 57^\circ
$$

we can represent it as follows:

![](<../Source/3.9.2. Calculating Lengths and Areas Using Trigonometry/Images/0cae02e04ebcc5fdba651f4c75d9d2af.png>)

Now, we see that $\overline{AB}$ is the base and $\overline{BC}$ is the height, so the area of triangle $ABC$ is

$$
\text{Area} = \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot AB \cdot BC
$$

Next, since $BC = 8$ and

$$
\angle C = 57^\circ
$$

we can find the length of $\overline{AB}$ using the following trigonometric identity:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\tan ∠C &= \frac{opposite}{adjacent} \\
\tan 57^{∘} &= \frac{AB}{BC} \\
AB &= BC \cdot \tan 57^{∘} \\
&= 8\tan 57^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus, the area is

$$
\begin{aligned}
Area &= \frac{1}{2} \cdot AB \cdot BC \\
&= \frac{1}{2} \cdot (8\tan 57^{∘}) \cdot 8 \\
&= 32\tan 57^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, by comparing this with $k \tan 57^\circ$, we get
$k = 32$.
