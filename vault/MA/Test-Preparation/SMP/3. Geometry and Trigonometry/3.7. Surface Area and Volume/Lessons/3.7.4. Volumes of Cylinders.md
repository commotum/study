## Introduction

Cylinder problems often test our ability to apply and manipulate the volume formula, or to compare volumes when dimensions change. These questions sometimes mix geometry with proportional reasoning.

In this lesson, we’ll practice using the cylinder volume formula across different problem setups.

## Finding the Volume of a Cylinder

**Example:** A cylindrical shipping tube has a base diameter of $8$ inches and a height of $30$ inches. What is the volume, in cubic inches, of the tube?

**Explanation**

We are given the diameter and height of a right circular cylinder and asked to find its volume.

The formula for the volume of a cylinder is

$$
V = \pi r^2 h
$$

where $r$ is the radius and $h$ is the height.

We are given the diameter of the base, then we first find the radius:

$$
r = \dfrac{8}{2} = 4
$$

Let's substitute $r = 4$ and $h = 30$ into the volume formula:

$$
\begin{aligned}
V &= π(4)^{2}(30) \\
&= π \cdot 16 \cdot 30 \\
&= 480π
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the volume is $480\pi$ cubic inches.

## Finding the Radius of a Cylinder Given Its Volume and Height

**Example:** The volume of a right circular cylinder is $32\pi$ cubic units. If the height of the cylinder is $2$ units, what is the radius?

**Explanation**

We are given the volume and height of a right circular cylinder, and we are asked to find the radius.

The formula for the volume of a right circular cylinder is

$$
V = \pi r^2 h
$$

where $r$ is the radius and $h$ is the height.

Let's substitute the known values $V = 32\pi$ and $h = 2$ into the formula:

$$
32\pi = \pi r^2 (2)
$$

Now, we simplify and solve for $r{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
32π &= 2πr^{2} \\
\frac{32π}{π} &= \frac{2πr^{2}}{π} \\
32 &= 2r^{2} \\
\frac{32}{2} &= \frac{2r^{2}}{2} \\
16 &= r^{2} \\
r &= \sqrt{16}
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the radius is

$$
\sqrt{16} = 4
$$

units.

## Finding the Height of a Cylinder Given Its Volume and Base Area

**Example:** The volume of a right circular cylinder is $400$ cubic inches, and the area of its base is $25$ square inches. What is the height, in inches, of the cylinder?

**Explanation**

We are given the volume and base area of a right circular cylinder, and we are asked to find the height.

The formula for the volume of a cylinder is

$$
V = \pi r^2 h
$$

where $V$ is the volume, $\pi r^2$ is the area of the circular base, and $h$ is the height.

Let's substitute $V = 400$ and

$$
\pi r^2 = 25
$$

into the formula:

$$
\begin{aligned}
400 &= 25h \\
\frac{400}{25} &= \frac{25h}{25} \\
16 &= h \\
h &= 16
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the height of the cylinder is $16$ inches.

## Finding the Volume of a Cylinder Given a Percentage Increase in Context

**Example:** A soda company manufactures cylindrical cans with a height of $45$ millimeters. The radius of the standard can is $9$ millimeters, while the radius of the large can is $33\%$ greater than that of the standard can. What is the volume, in cubic millimeters, of the large can?

**Explanation**

We are given the height and radius of a standard cylindrical can, and we’re told that the large version has a radius that is $33\%$ greater. We're asked to find the volume of the large can.

First, we compute the radius of the large can, which we will denote as $R$. Since the radius of the standard can is $9$, we increase that by $33\%{:}$

$$
R = 9 + 0.33(9) = 9 + 2.97 = 11.97
$$

Now, we use the formula for the volume of a cylinder:

$$
V = \pi r^2 h
$$

Let's substitute $r = 11.97$ and $h = 45$ into the formula:

$$
\begin{aligned}
V &= π(11.97)^{2}(45) \\
&= π \cdot 143.28 \cdot 45 \\
&= 6,448π
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the volume is $6{,}448\pi$ cubic millimeters.
