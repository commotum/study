Cone problems often test our ability to apply and manipulate the volume formula, sometimes in combination with other geometry concepts, such as the Pythagorean theorem.

Here, we’ll practice using the cone volume formula across both direct and multi-step problem types.

## Finding the Volume of a Cone

**Example:** A right circular cone has a height of $27$ centimeters $(\mathrm{cm})$ and a base with a diameter of $8~\mathrm{cm}$. The volume of this cone is $n\pi~\mathrm{cm}^3$. What is the value of $n$?

**Explanation**

We are given the height and the diameter of the cone. The formula for the volume of a cone is

$$
V = \dfrac{1}{3} \pi r^2 h
$$

Since the diameter is $8~\mathrm{cm}$, the radius is $4~\mathrm{cm}$.

We substitute the known values into the formula:

$$
\begin{aligned}
V &= \frac{1}{3}π(4)^{2}(27) \\
&= \frac{1}{3}π \cdot 16 \cdot 27 \\
&= \frac{1}{3}π \cdot 432 \\
&= 144πcm^{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the value of $n$ is $\bbox[4pt,border: 1px solid lightgray]{144}$.

## Finding the Radius of a Cone's Base Given Its Volume and Height

**Example:** A right circular cone has a volume of $\dfrac{5}{4}\pi$ cubic meters and a height of $2$ meters. What is the radius, in meters, of the base of the cone?

**Explanation**

We are given the volume of the cone and its height. The formula for the volume of a cone is

$$
V = \dfrac{1}{3} \pi r^2 h
$$

Now, we substitute

$$
V = \dfrac{5}{4}\pi
$$

and $h = 2$ into the formula and simplify:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{5}{4}π &= \frac{1}{3}πr^{2}(2) \\
\frac{5}{4}π &= \frac{2}{3}πr^{2} \\
\frac{5}{4} &= \frac{2}{3}r^{2} \\
\frac{15}{8} &= r^{2} \\
r &= \sqrt{\frac{15}{8}}
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we rationalize the denominator:

$$
\begin{aligned}
r &= \sqrt{\frac{15}{8}} \\
r &= \frac{\sqrt{15}}{2\sqrt{2}} \cdot \frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{2}} \\
r &= \frac{\sqrt{30}}{4}
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the radius of the base is

$$
\dfrac{\sqrt{30}}{4}
$$

meters.

## Finding the Height of a Cone Given Its Volume and Base Area

**Example:** A right circular cone has a volume of $19{,}200\pi$ cubic inches, and the area of its base is $1{,}200\pi$ square inches. What is the slant height, in inches, of this cone?

**Explanation**

We are given the volume and the base area of the cone and are asked to find the slant height. To do this, we first need to determine the height $h$ and the radius $r$, and then use the Pythagorean Theorem.

The volume formula for a cone is

$$
V = \dfrac{1}{3} \pi r^2 h
$$

Since the area of the base is $1,200\pi$, we know that

$$
\pi r^2 = 1{,}200\pi
$$

We substitute this into the volume formula and simplify:

$$
\begin{aligned}
V &= \frac{1}{3} \cdot πr^{2} \cdot h \\
19, 200π &= \frac{1}{3} \cdot 1,200π \cdot h \\
19, 200 &= 400 \cdot h \\
48 &= h \\
h &= 48
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we solve for the slant height using the Pythagorean Theorem

$$
l = \sqrt{r^2 + h^2}
$$

We already know that

$$
\pi r^2 = 1{,}200\pi
$$

, so

$$
r^2 = 1{,}200
$$

Substituting into the formula:

$$
\begin{aligned}
l &= \sqrt{r^{2} + h^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{1,200 + 48^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{1,200 + 2,304} \\
&= \sqrt{3,504} \\
&\approx 59.19
\end{aligned}
$$

rounded to $2$ decimal places.

So, the slant height is $59.19$ inches.
