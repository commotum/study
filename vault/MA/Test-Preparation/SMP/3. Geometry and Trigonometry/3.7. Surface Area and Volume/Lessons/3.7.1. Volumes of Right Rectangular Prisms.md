## Finding the Volume of a Right Rectangular Prism

**Example:** The figure shows the dimensions, in yards ($\mathrm{yd}$), of a right rectangular prism.

![](<../Source/3.7.1. Volumes of Right Rectangular Prisms/Images/82754752732125d34d10da5d28148979.png>)

The volume $V$ of a right rectangular prism is given by the formula
$V = \ell w h$,

where $\ell$ is the length, $w$ is the width, and $h$ is the height. What is the volume, in cubic yards, of the prism?

**Explanation**

To find the volume of a right rectangular prism, we use the formula

$$
V = \ell w h
$$

where $\ell$ is the length, $w$ is the width, and $h$ is the height.

From the diagram, we are given:

- $\ell = 8\,\text{yd}$
- $w = 4\,\text{yd}$
- $h = 3\,\text{yd}$

Substituting these values into the formula, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
V &= 8 \cdot 4 \cdot 3 \\
&= 96
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the volume of the prism is $96\,\text{yd}^3$.

## Finding the Base Area of a Triangular Prism Given Its Volume and Height

**Example:** What is the area, in square feet $(\mathrm{ft}^2)$, of the base of a triangular prism that has a height of $4$ feet and a volume of $56\,\mathrm{ft}^3$.

**Hint:** *The volume of a triangular prism is given by* $V = B h$, *where* $B$ *is the area of the base and* $h$ *is the height.*

**Explanation**

To find the area of the base $B$, we use the formula for the volume of a triangular prism:

$$
V = B \cdot h
$$

We are given the volume $V = 56$ and the height $h = 4$. Substituting these values into the formula, we get

$$
56 = B \cdot 4
$$

Now, we solve for $B$ by dividing both sides by $4{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
B &= \frac{56}{4} \\
&= 14
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the area of the base is $14$ square feet.

## Finding the Height of a Right Square-Based Prism Given the Volume and Base Edge Length

**Example:** A ventilation column is shaped like a right square prism. The edge of the square base is $6$ feet long, and the volume of the column is $432$ cubic feet. What is the height, in feet, of the column?

**Explanation**

To find the height of a right square prism, we use the volume formula

$$
V = B \cdot h
$$

where $B$ is the area of the square base and $h$ is the height.

The edge length of the square base is $6$ feet, so the area of the base is

$$
B = 6^2 = 36
$$

We are told the volume is $432$ cubic feet. Substituting into the formula, we get

$$
432 = 36 \cdot h
$$

Now, we solve for $h$ by dividing both sides by $36{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
h &= \frac{432}{36} \\
&= 12
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the height of the column is $12$ feet.

## Finding the Base Edge Length of a Right Square-Based Rectangular Prism Given the Volume and Height

**Example:** A stone plinth is shaped like a right square prism. Its height is $20$ units, and its volume is $500$ cubic units. What is the length, in units, of one edge of the base?

**Explanation**

To find the length of an edge of the square base, we use the volume formula for a right square prism:

$$
V = B \cdot h
$$

where $B$ is the area of the square base and $h$ is the height.

Since the base is a square, its area is given by $B = s^2$, where $s$ is the edge length. So, we have

$$
V = s^2 \cdot h
$$

We are given $V = 500$ and $h = 20$. Substituting these values, we get

$$
500 = s^2 \cdot 20
$$

Now, we divide both sides by $20{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
s^{2} &= \frac{500}{20} \\
&= 25
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, by taking the square root of both sides, we obtain

$$
s = \sqrt{25} = 5
$$

Therefore, the length of the edge of the base is $5$ units.
