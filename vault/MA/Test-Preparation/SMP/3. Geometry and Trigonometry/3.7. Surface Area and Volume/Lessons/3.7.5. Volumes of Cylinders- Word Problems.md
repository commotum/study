## Interpreting Functions Representing the Volume of a Cylinder

**Example:** $V = \pi \cdot (2r + 6) \cdot r^2$
A cylindrical can has a height that is $6$ centimeters more than twice its radius $r$ of the base. The function $V$, given above, models the volume of the can, in cubic centimeters. Find the expression representing the height, in centimeters, of the can.

**Explanation**

The volume of a cylinder is given by

$$
V = \pi \cdot \text{height} \cdot \text{radius}^2
$$

Here, we have

$$
V = \pi \cdot (2r+6) \cdot r^2
$$

where $r$ is the radius of the base.

Therefore, comparing the two expressions, we conclude that the height must be
$2r+6$.

## Determining the Difference Between the Volumes of Two Cylinders

**Example:** A cylindrical food container holds soup and is topped off with broth. The container has a base area of $80\,\mathrm{cm}^2$ and a height of $9\,\mathrm{cm}$. If $240\,\mathrm{cm}^3$ of broth is added to fill the container to the top, what is the volume of soup in the container?

**Explanation**

We are given the base area and height of a cylindrical container, and the volume of broth used to top it off. We are asked to find the volume of soup.

First, let's calculate the total volume of the container using the formula:
$V = B h$,
where $B$ is the base area and $h$ is the height.

Substituting $B = 80$ and $h = 9{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
V &= 80 \cdot 9 \\
&= 720
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the total volume of the container is $720\,\mathrm{cm}^3$. Since $240\,\mathrm{cm}^3$ of broth was added, the remaining space must be soup.

Let $V_s$ be this volume. Then we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
V_{s} &= 720 - 240 \\
&= 480
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the volume of soup in the container is approximately $480\,\mathrm{cm}^3$.

## Determining the Volume of a Cylinder Given the Ratio Between Its Height and Radius

**Example:** A company manufactures cylindrical drums whose height is $1$ inch less than five times the radius. Find the expression representing the volume $V$, in cubic inches, of one of these drums in terms of its radius $r$, in inches.

**Explanation**

We are given that the drum is a right circular cylinder. The formula for the volume of a cylinder is

$$
V = \pi r^2 h
$$

We are told that the height $h$ is $1$ inch less than five times the radius. In other words,
$h = 5r - 1$.

Now, we substitute this expression into the volume formula:

$$
\begin{aligned}
V &= πr^{2}h \\
&= πr^{2}(5r - 1)
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the final answer is

$$
V = \pi r^2 (5r - 1)
$$

## Determining the Volume Given a Comparison to Another Cylinder

**Example:** The volume of a right circular cylinder $M$ is $90$ cubic inches. What is the volume, in cubic inches, of a right circular cylinder with five times the radius and one-fifth the height of cylinder $M$?

**Explanation**

We are given that the volume of cylinder $M$ is $90$ cubic inches.

The volume of a cylinder is given by the formula

$$
V = \pi r^2 h
$$

Let's suppose the radius and height of cylinder $M$ are $r$ and $h$, respectively. Then its volume is

$$
V_M = \pi r^2 h = 90
$$

Now, let's consider a new cylinder with radius $5r$ and height

$$
\dfrac{1}{5}h
$$

We compute its volume as

$$
\begin{aligned}
V_{new} &= π(5r)^{2} \cdot \frac{1}{5}h \\
&= π(25r^{2}) \cdot \frac{1}{5}h \\
&= πr^{2}h \cdot 5 \\
&= 90 \cdot 5 \\
&= 450
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the volume of the new cylinder is $450$ cubic inches.

## Comparing the Volumes of Two Cylinders

**Example:** A large pipe is shaped like a right circular cylinder with radius $r$ and height $h$.

![](<../Source/3.7.5. Volumes of Cylinders- Word Problems/Images/ce6fc3960e193b2e16645a63b87aa655.png>)

Another pipe has a volume that is $162$ times greater than the volume of the first pipe. Find the expressions representing the radius $R$, in terms of $r$, and the height $H$, in terms of $h$, of the second pipe.

**Explanation**

We compare the volumes of two cylinders using their formulas, given that the second pipe’s volume is $162$ times greater than the first. Recall that the volume of a cylinder is given by

$$
V = \pi r^2 h
$$

Let the volume of the smaller pipe be $V_1{:}$

$$
V_1 = \pi r^2 h
$$

We are told the second pipe has $162$ times the volume of the first pipe, so its volume, $V_2$, is

$$
V_2 = 162 \cdot \pi r^2 h
$$

Suppose the second pipe has radius $R$ and height $H$. Its volume is

$$
V_2 = \pi R^2 H
$$

Now, we set the two expressions for $V_2$ equal and simplify:

$$
\begin{aligned}
πR^{2}H &= 162 \cdot πr^{2}h \\
R^{2}H &= 162r^{2}h
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we test the choices to find which pair of values for $R$ and $H$ makes this equation true.

- For $R = 36r$ and $H = \dfrac{1}{2}h{:}$
$R^{2}H|= (36r)^{2} \cdot \frac{1}{2}h; = 1296r^{2} \cdot \frac{1}{2}h; = 648r^{2}h\cdot$
This doesn't match the given equation.
- For $R = 6r$ and $H = 4h{:}$
$R^{2}H|= (6r)^{2} \cdot 4h; = 36r^{2} \cdot 4h; = 144r^{2}h\cdot$
This doesn't match the given equation.
- For $R = 12r$ and $H = \dfrac{9}{5}h{:}$
$R^{2}H|= (12r)^{2} \cdot \frac{9}{5}h; = 144r^{2} \cdot \frac{9}{5}h; = 259.2r^{2}h\cdot$
This doesn't match the given equation.
- For $R = 6r$ and $H = 18h{:}$
$R^{2}H|= (6r)^{2} \cdot 18h; = 36r^{2} \cdot 18h; = 648r^{2}h\cdot$
This doesn't match the given equation.

- For $R = 9r$ and $H = 2h{:}$
$R^{2}H|= (9r)^{2} \cdot 2h; = 81r^{2} \cdot 2h; = 162r^{2}h✓$
This matches the given equation.

So, the correct answer is $R = 9r$ and $H = 2h$.
