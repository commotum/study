## Introduction

Cube problems often test our ability to connect volume and surface area formulas, especially when faces are missing or when we need to work backward from a given measure.

Here, we’ll practice using the volume and surface formulas to solve real-world and test-style problems.

## Finding the Volume of a Cube

**Example:** A room in the shape of a cube has an edge length of $13$ feet. What is the volume, in cubic feet, of the room?

**Explanation**

To find the volume of a cube, we use the formula
$V = s^3$,
where $s$ is the edge length of the cube.

Here, the edge length is $13$ feet. Substituting into the formula, we get
$V = 13^3$.

Now, we compute the cube:

$$
\begin{aligned}
13^{3} &= 13 \cdot 13 \cdot 13 \\
&= 169 \cdot 13 \\
&= 2,197
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the volume of the cube is $2{,}197$ cubic feet.

## Finding the Surface Area of a Cube

**Example:** A cube-shaped toy chest has one open face. Each edge of the chest measures $30$ centimeters. What is the exterior surface area, in square centimeters, of this open-front chest?

**Explanation**

The chest is shaped like a cube, meaning all its faces are squares of equal size. Since the front is open, it has only $5$ square faces: $4$ sides and $1$ back.

To find the total exterior surface area $S$, we multiply the area of one square face by $5$. So,

$$
S = 5 \cdot s^2
$$

where $s$ is the edge length of the cube.

Next, we substitute $s = 30$, and simplify:

$$
\begin{aligned}
S &= 5 \cdot s^{2} \\
&= 5 \cdot 30^{2} \\
&= 5 \cdot 900 \\
&= 4,500
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the surface area of the chest is $4{,}500$ square centimeters.

## Finding the Volume of a Cube Given Its Surface Area

**Example:** A cube has a surface area of $294$ square centimeters. What is the volume, in cubic centimeters, of the cube?

**Explanation**

We are given the cube's surface a. The formula for the surface area of a cube is
$A = 6s^2$,
where $s$ is the side length.

We set up the equation and simplify:

$$
\begin{aligned}
6s^{2} &= 294 \\
s^{2} &= 49 \\
s &= 7
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we compute the volume using the formula $V = s^3{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
V &= 7^{3} \\
&= 343
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the volume of the cube is $343$ cubic centimeters.

## Finding the Surface Area of a Cube Given Its Volume

**Example:** A cube has a volume of $97\,336$ cubic centimeters. What is the surface area $A$, in square centimeters, of the cube?

**Explanation**

We are given the volume of the cube. The formula for the volume of a cube is
$V = s^3$,
where $s$ is the side length.

Let's set up the equation and simplify:

$$
\begin{aligned}
s^{3} &= 97336 \\
s &= \sqrt[3]{97336} \\
&= 46
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we compute the surface area using the formula $A = 6s^2{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= 6 \cdot 46^{2} \\
&= 6 \cdot 2116 \\
&= 12 696
\end{aligned}
$$
