## Introduction

On the math section of the SAT exam, we're often asked to create a function for surface area or volume directly from a description.

In this topic, we’ll practice constructing and interpreting functions that represent surface area and volume, turning real-world descriptions into precise mathematical formulas.

---

<a id="interpreting-functions-representing-surface-area-or-volume"></a>
## Interpreting Functions Representing Surface Area or Volume

**Example:** $V = \dfrac{1}{3}\pi \cdot (r + 2) \cdot r^2$
A right cone has a height that is $2$ inches more than its radius $r$ of the base. The function $V$, given above, models the volume of the cone, in cubic inches. Which expression represents the height, in inches, of the cone?

**Explanation**

The volume of a right cone is given by

$$
V = \dfrac{1}{3}\pi \cdot \text{height} \cdot \text{radius}^2
$$

Here, we have

$$
V = \dfrac{1}{3}\pi \cdot (r+2) \cdot r^2
$$

where $r$ is the radius of the base.

Therefore, comparing the two expressions, we conclude that the height must be
$r+2$.

## Constructing Functions Representing Surface Area or Volume of Rectangular Prisms

**Example:** A right rectangular prism has a base width of $x$ meters. The length of the prism's base is $7$ meters, and its height is $2x$ meters less than the length. Which function $S$ gives the surface area of the prism, in square meters, in terms of the width of the prism's base?

**Explanation**

From the description, our right rectangular prism has the following measurements:

- The width is $w = x$ meters.
- The length is $l = 7$ meters.
- The height is $h = 7 - 2x$ meters ($2x$ meters less than the length).

Therefore, the surface area of the prism is given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
S(x) &= 2(lw + lh + wh) \\
&= 2(7 \cdot x + 7 \cdot (7 - 2x) + x \cdot (7 - 2x)) \\
&= 2(7x + 49 - 14x + 7x - 2x^{2}) \\
&= 2(49 - 2x^{2})
\end{aligned}
$$

## Constructing Functions Representing Volume of Non-Polyhedra

**Example:** A right cone has a height of $x$ meters. The radius of the cone's base is $2$ meters more than the height. What is the function $V$ that gives the volume of the cone, in cubic meters, in terms of the height of the cone?

**Explanation**

From the description, our right cone has the following measurements:

- The height is $h = x$ meters.
- The radius of the base is $r = x+2$ meters ($2$ meters more than the height).

Therefore, the volume of the cone is given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
V(x) &= \frac{1}{3}πr^{2}h \\
&= \frac{1}{3}π \cdot (x + 2)^{2} \cdot x \\
&= \frac{1}{3}πx(x + 2)^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Constructing Functions Representing Area or Volume Given Two Cubes

**Example:** Cube $M$ has a side length of $m$ inches. Cube $N$ has a surface area that is $18$ square inches greater than one-half of the surface area of cube $M$. What is the function $V$ that represents the volume of a cube $N$, in cubic inches?

**Explanation**

Let

- $m$ be the side length of cube $M$, and
- $n$ be the side length of cube $N$.

Then,

- the surface area of cube $M$ is $6m^2$, and
- the surface area of cube $N$ is $6n^2$.

We are told that the surface area of cube $N$ is $18$ square inches greater than one half of the surface area of cube $M$. In symbols, this means that

$$
6n^2 = \dfrac{1}{2}\cdot 6m^2 + 18
$$

Now, we solve the obtained equation for $n{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
6n^{2} &= 3m^{2} + 18 \\
\frac{6n^{2}}{6} &= \frac{3m^{2} + 18}{6} \\
n^{2} &= \frac{m^{2}}{2} + 3 \\
n &= \sqrt{\frac{m^{2}}{2} + 3}
\end{aligned}
$$

We disregard the negative solutions since the side length must be positive.

Therefore, the function $V$ that represents the volume of cube $N$ is given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
V(m) &= n^{3} \\
&= (\sqrt{\frac{m^{2}}{2} + 3})^{3} \\
&= ((\frac{m^{2}}{2} + 3)^{1/2})^{3} \\
&= ((\frac{m^{2}}{2} + 3)^{3})^{1/2} \\
&= \sqrt{(\frac{m^{2}}{2} + 3)^{3}}
\end{aligned}
$$
