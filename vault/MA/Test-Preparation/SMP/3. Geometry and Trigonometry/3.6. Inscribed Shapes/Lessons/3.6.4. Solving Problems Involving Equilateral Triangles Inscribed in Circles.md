## Solving for a Triangle's Parameters Given an Equilateral Triangle Inscribed in a Circle

**Example:** An equilateral triangle is inscribed in a circle, as shown above. What is the area of the triangle if the radius of the circle is $6$ meters?

![](<../Source/3.6.4. Solving Problems Involving Equilateral Triangles Inscribed in Circles/Images/67eceeef1f2b8a0e5ee2c13da0e261ae.png>)

**Explanation**

To find the area of the triangle, we draw radii from the center to each of the triangle’s vertices. This forms three identical isosceles triangles, each with a central angle of $120^\circ$, as shown below.

![](<../Source/3.6.4. Solving Problems Involving Equilateral Triangles Inscribed in Circles/Images/f916101bce7e121d91b256306cc3857b.png>)

Now, we take one of these isosceles triangles and draw an altitude from the center to the opposite side. This divides the triangle into two right triangles with angles $30^\circ$, $60^\circ$, and $90^\circ$.

In each $30$-$60$-$90$ triangle:

- Let the longer leg, which is opposite the $60^\circ$ angle, be $x$. Notice that it is half of our equilateral triangle's side length.
- The hypotenuse is a radius of the circle and has a length $r = 6\:\text{m}$.

Next, in the $30$-$60$-$90$ triangle, the longer leg is

$$
\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}
$$

times the hypotenuse:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x &= r \cdot \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \\
&= 6 \cdot \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \\
&= 3\sqrt{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, our equilateral triangle's side length is

$$
s = 2x = 2 \cdot 3 \sqrt{3} = 6 \sqrt{3} \: \text{m}
$$

and the corresponding area of the triangle is

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}s^{2} \\
&= \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}(6\sqrt{3})^{2} \\
&= \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4} \cdot 108 \\
&= 27\sqrt{3}m^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Solving for a Circle's Parameters Given an Equilateral Triangle Inscribed in a Circle

**Example:** The side of an equilateral triangle is $27$ feet. The three vertices of the triangle lie on a circle, as depicted above. What is the radius of the circle?

![](<../Source/3.6.4. Solving Problems Involving Equilateral Triangles Inscribed in Circles/Images/7ad89d2b9e4300705dc02b1345f8d10c.png>)

**Explanation**

To find the radius of the circle, we draw radii from the center to each of the triangle’s vertices. This forms three identical isosceles triangles, each with a central angle of $120^\circ$, as shown below.

![](<../Source/3.6.4. Solving Problems Involving Equilateral Triangles Inscribed in Circles/Images/2a1061e1a06c2e8ff241d0cba1888041.png>)

Now, we take one of these isosceles triangles and draw an altitude from the center to the opposite side. This divides the triangle into two right triangles with angles $30^\circ$, $60^\circ$, and $90^\circ$.

In each $30$-$60$-$90$ triangle:

- The length of the longer leg $x$, which is opposite the $60^\circ$ angle, is half of our equilateral triangle's side length:
$x = \dfrac{AC}{2} = \dfrac{27}{2} = 13.5$.
- The length of the hypotenuse is the radius $r$ of the circle.

Finally, in a $30$-$60$-$90$ triangle, the longer leg is

$$
\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}
$$

times the length of the hypotenuse:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x &= r \cdot \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \\
13.5 &= r \cdot \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \\
r &= \frac{2}{\sqrt{3}} \cdot 13.5 \\
&= \frac{27}{\sqrt{3}} \\
&= \frac{27}{\sqrt{3}} \cdot \frac{\sqrt{3}}{\sqrt{3}} \\
&= \frac{27\sqrt{3}}{3} \\
&= 9\sqrt{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the radius of the circle is $9 \sqrt{3} \: \text{ft}$.
