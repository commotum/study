## Introduction

Inscribed‐circle problems often ask us to switch quickly between a circle’s radius or diameter and a square’s side.

In this lesson, we’ll practice setting up these relationships and solving multi-step geometric problems.

## Solving for a Circle's Parameters Given a Circle Inscribed in a Square

**Example:** Find the area of a circle inscribed in a square with sides of length $18\,\textrm{m}$.

**Explanation**

![](<../Source/3.6.2. Solving Problems Involving Inscribed Circles/Images/741155d84f0c1f9b5b8cc3325d84c346.png>)

The side of the square is $18 \: \textrm{m}$, which means that the diameter $d$ of the circle is

$$
d = 18 \: \textrm{m}
$$

Thus, the radius $r$ of the circle is

$$
r = \dfrac{d}{2} = \dfrac{18}{2} = 9 \: \textrm{m}
$$

Therefore, the area of the circle is

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= πr^{2} \\
&= π(9)^{2} \\
&= 81πm^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Solving for a Square's Parameters Given a Circle Inscribed in a Square

**Example:** The circumference of a circle inscribed in a square is $7\pi$. What is the area of the square?

**Explanation**

![](<../Source/3.6.2. Solving Problems Involving Inscribed Circles/Images/e20bb5520dc2a7d5c47716821b0e2e68.png>)

The formula for a circle's circumference is $C = \pi d$, where $d$ is the diameter. So, we can find the diameter as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
d &= \frac{C}{π} \\
&= \frac{7π}{π} \\
&= 7
\end{aligned}
$$

The diameter of the circle is $7$, which means that the side $s$ of the square is
$s = 7$.

Therefore, the area of the square is

$$
\mathcal{A} = s^2 = (7)^2 = 49
$$

## Problems Involving Diagonals of Squares Given a Circle Inscribed in a Square

**Example:** The area of a circle inscribed in a square is $32\pi \,\textrm{mm}^2$. Find the diagonal of the square.

**Explanation**

![](<../Source/3.6.2. Solving Problems Involving Inscribed Circles/Images/738fdf7853f98af824ef8d3019d1c1bf.png>)

The area of a circle is given by

$$
\mathcal{A} = \pi r^2
$$

Substituting the known area and solving for the radius $r$, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= πr^{2} \\
32π &= πr^{2} \\
32π &= πr^{2} \\
32 &= r^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

and taking the square root gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
r &= \sqrt{32} \\
&= \sqrt{16 \cdot 2} \\
&= \sqrt{16} \cdot \sqrt{2} \\
&= 4\sqrt{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the radius of the circle equals $4\sqrt 2\,\textrm{mm}$.

The length $s$ of the square's side equals the diameter of the inscribed circle, and the diameter is twice the radius. Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
s &= 2r \\
&= 2(4\sqrt{2}) \\
&= 8\sqrt{2}mm
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, the length $d$ of the square's diagonal is

$$
\begin{aligned}
d &= s\sqrt{2} \\
&= 8\sqrt{2} \cdot \sqrt{2} \\
&= 8 \cdot 2 \\
&= 16mm
\end{aligned}
$$
