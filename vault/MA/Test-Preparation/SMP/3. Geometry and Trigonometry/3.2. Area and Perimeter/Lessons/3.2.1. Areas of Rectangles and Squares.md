## Introduction

Questions about areas of rectangles and squares appear frequently in both straightforward calculations and multi-step geometry problems.

Here, we’ll practice the ability to apply area formulas quickly and accurately.

<a id="finding-the-area-of-a-square"></a>
## Finding the Area of a Square

**Example:** A square has side length $32$ meters. What is the area, in $\text{m}^2$, of the square?

**Explanation**

To find the area of a square, we use the formula

$A = s^2$,
where $s$ is the side length of the square.

We substitute the given side length:

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= 32^{2} \\
&= 1, 024
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the area is $1,\!024 \,\text{m}^2$.

## Finding the Area of a Rectangle

**Example:** A rectangle has a length of $64$ centimeters and a width of $27$ centimeters. What is the area, in square centimeters, of the rectangle?

**Explanation**

To find the area of a rectangle, we use the formula

$$
A = \ell \cdot w
$$

where $\ell$ is the length and $w$ is the width.

We substitute the given values into the formula

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= 64 \cdot 27 \\
&= 1, 728cm^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding the Side Length of a Square Given Its Area

**Example:** The area of a square is $1,\!024$ square centimeters. What is the side length, in centimeters, of the square?

**Explanation**

To find the side length of a square from its area, we use the formula
$A = s^2$,
where $s$ is the side length of the square.

We substitute the given area and solve for $s{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
1, 024 &= s^{2} \\
\sqrt{1, 024} &= \sqrt{s^{2}} \\
32 &= s \\
s &= 32
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the side length is $32\,\text{cm}$.

## Finding a Side Length of a Rectangle Given the Area and the Other Side

**Example:** The area of a rectangle is $112$ square meters. The length of the longest side of the rectangle is $16$ meters. What is the length, in meters, of the shortest side of this rectangle?

**Explanation**

To find the unknown side length of a rectangle, we use the formula

$$
A = \ell \cdot w
$$

where $\ell$ is the length and $w$ is the width.

We substitute the known values into the equation and solve for $w{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
112 &= 16 \cdot w \\
\frac{112}{16} &= \frac{16 \cdot w}{16} \\
7 &= w \\
w &= 7m
\end{aligned}
$$

## The Area of a "Cut-Out" Rectangle

**Example:** Rectangle $F$ has an area of $72$ square feet. If a rectangle with an area of $28$ square feet is cut out of rectangle $F$, what is the area, in square feet, of the remaining figure?

**Explanation**

To find the area of the remaining figure, we subtract the area that was removed from the original area:

$$
72 - 28 = 44
$$

So, the area of the remaining figure is $44\,\text{ft}^2$.
