## Introduction

Problems involving perimeters of squares, rectangles, and triangles often appear in both straightforward geometry questions and real-world applications involving shapes and measurements.

In this topic, we’ll practice solving perimeter problems.

## Finding the Perimeter of a Square

**Example:** Each side of a square has a length of $40$ units. What is the perimeter of this square?

**Explanation**

To find the perimeter of a square, we use the formula
$P = 4s$,
where $s$ is the length of one side.

We substitute the given side length into the formula

$$
\begin{aligned}
P &= 4 \cdot 40 \\
&= 160units
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding the Perimeter of a Rectangle

**Example:** What is the perimeter, in feet, of a rectangle with a length of $15$ feet and a width of $6$ feet?

**Explanation**

To find the perimeter of a rectangle, we use the formula

$$
P = 2\ell + 2w
$$

where $\ell$ is the length and $w$ is the width.

We substitute the given values:

$$
\begin{aligned}
P &= 2(15) + 2(6) \\
&= 30 + 12 \\
&= 42
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the perimeter is $42$ feet.

## Finding a Side Length Given the Perimeter and Two Other Sides of a Triangle

**Example:** The perimeter of triangle $JKL$ is $36$ meters. The length of side $JK$ is $11$ meters, and the length of side $JL$ is $10$ meters. What is the length, in meters, of side $KL$?

**Explanation**

To find the missing side of a triangle, we use the perimeter formula, which is the sum of the lengths of its sides. In this case

$$
P = JK + JL + KL
$$

We substitute the known values into the formula and solve for $KL{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
36 &= 11 + 10 + KL \\
36 &= 21 + KL \\
36 - 21 &= KL \\
15 &= KL \\
KL &= 15
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the length of side $KL$ is $15\,\text{m}$.

## Finding the Side Length of a Square Given the Side Length of Another Square

**Example:** The side length of square $P$ is $8$ meters. The perimeter of square $Q$ is $5$ times the perimeter of square $P$. What is the side length, in meters, of square $Q$?

**Explanation**

We are told that square $P$ has a side length of $8\,\text{m}$.

To find the perimeter of square $P$, we use the formula for the perimeter of a square
$P = 4s$.
Substituting $s = 8$, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
P &= 4 \cdot 8 \\
&= 32m
\end{aligned}
$$

Let $P_Q$ be the perimeter of square $Q$. We use the fact that it is $5$ times the perimeter of square $P{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
P_{Q} &= 5 \cdot 32 \\
&= 160m
\end{aligned}
$$

To find the side length of square $Q$, we divide its perimeter by $4{:}$

$$
s = \frac{160}{4} = 40\, \text{m}
$$

So, the side length of square $Q$ is $40\,\text{m}$.
