## Introduction

Questions about vertical angles often appear in geometry problems where two or more lines intersect, testing whether we can quickly recognize which angles are equal and apply that fact to find unknown measures.

With a bit of practice, we can spot these angle relationships almost instantly, saving time and avoiding mistakes.

---

<a id="identifying-vertical-angles"></a>
## Identifying Vertical Angles

**Example:** In the diagram above, two lines intersect at a point. If the measure of $\angle 1$ is $125^\circ$, what is the measure of angle $\angle 2$?

![](<../Source/3.1.3. Vertical Angles/Images/3aebc3dc0bb7f408685c3aafe5f2f379.png>)

**Explanation**

When two lines meet at a point, they create two pairs of opposite angles. The angles in each pair are called vertical angles. Vertical angles are always congruent.

In this case, angle $\angle 1$ and angle $\angle 2$ are vertical angles, so they have the same measure.

Therefore, the measure of $\angle 2$ is $125^\circ$.

## Using Vertical and Supplementary Angles

**Example:** In the diagram above, three lines intersect at the point $R$. If $m = 60$ and $n = 70$, what is the value of $p$?

![](<../Source/3.1.3. Vertical Angles/Images/6d865480b44d237757ab91522015151e.png>)

**Explanation**

Since vertical angles are always congruent, the angle labeled $n^\circ$ and its opposite angle have the same measure.

![](<../Source/3.1.3. Vertical Angles/Images/3d043e67f3a93d7861e6c1ea67d89c3b.png>)

Now, the three angles labeled $m^\circ$, $n^\circ$, and $p^\circ$ are adjacent and together form a straight line at point $R$. The measure of a straight angle is $180^\circ$, so we can write

$$
m^\circ + n^\circ + p^\circ = 180^\circ
$$

Substituting the known values, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
60^{∘} + 70^{∘} + p^{∘} &= 180^{∘} \\
130^{∘} + p^{∘} &= 180^{∘} \\
p^{∘} &= 50^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the value of $p$ is $50$.

## Using Vertical and Consecutive Interior Angles

**Example:** In the diagram above, lines $u$ and $v$ are parallel, and the line $w$ intersects both. If $x = 2k + 14$ and $y = 6k - 10$, what is the value of $z$?

![](<../Source/3.1.3. Vertical Angles/Images/4bfaaa5f173ca58394a18d3500bda247.png>)

**Explanation**

From the diagram, we see that

- The angles labeled $x^\circ$ and $y^\circ$ are vertical angles, so they are congruent:
$x^\circ = y^\circ$
- The angles labeled $x^\circ$ and $z^\circ$ are consecutive interior angles, so they are supplementary:
$x^\circ + z^\circ = 180^\circ$

We are given expressions for $x$ and $y$. Since

$$
x^\circ = y^\circ
$$

we can write

$$
(2k + 14)^\circ = (6k - 10)^\circ
$$

Now, we solve for $k{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
2k + 14 &= 6k - 10 \\
14 + 10 &= 6k - 2k \\
24 &= 4k \\
k &= 6
\end{aligned}
$$

Substituting this value into the expression for $x$, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
x &= 2k + 14 \\
&= 2(6) + 14 \\
&= 12 + 14 \\
&= 26
\end{aligned}
$$

Since $x^\circ$ and $z^\circ$ are supplementary, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
z^{∘} &= 180^{∘} - x^{∘} \\
&= 180^{∘} - 26^{∘} \\
&= 154^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, we conclude that $z = 154$.

## Using Vertical, Supplementary, and Corresponding Angles

**Example:** In the diagram above, parallel lines $m$ and $n$ are intersected by the lines $p$ and $q$. If $x = 40$ and $y = 120$, what is the value of $z$?

![](<../Source/3.1.3. Vertical Angles/Images/d705a8db8be8d17625c20ac2aa821c0b.png>)

**Explanation**

We begin by examining the triangle formed by lines $p$, $q$, and $m$. This triangle has three interior angles:

- One angle is formed at the intersection of lines $p$ and $q$. This angle is vertical to the angle labeled $x^\circ$, hence, they are congruent. So, the measure of this first angle is $40^\circ$.
- A second angle is formed at the intersection of lines $p$ and $m$. This angle lies adjacent to the angle labeled $y^\circ$, forming a linear pair. Since linear pairs are supplementary, the measure of this second angle is
$180^\circ - y^\circ = 180^\circ - 120^\circ = 60^\circ$.
- The third angle is formed at the intersection of lines $q$ and $m$. Since in any triangle the sum of the measures of its interior angles is $180^\circ$, the measure of the third angle is
$180^\circ - 40^\circ - 60^\circ = 80^\circ$.

We can summarize these angles by adding them to the diagram, shown below.

![](<../Source/3.1.3. Vertical Angles/Images/66f2b854cd679aef922be96f64b184f1.png>)

Now observe the interior angle at the point where line $q$ intersects line $n$. Since $m \parallel n$, and the line $q$ acts as a transversal, this angle corresponds to the $80^\circ$ angle at the intersection of $q$ and $m$. Since corresponding angles are congruent, the measure of this angle is also $80^\circ$.

![](<../Source/3.1.3. Vertical Angles/Images/3f1fd3f9eb9952543c2252dcf5b0e2f9.png>)

At the intersection of lines $q$ and $n$, the two angles labeled $z^\circ$ form a straight angle on $q$ with the $80^\circ$ angle we just found. Since a straight angle measures $180^\circ$, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
z^{∘} + z^{∘} + 80^{∘} &= 180^{∘} \\
&= 2z^{∘} = \mid 100^{∘} \\
z^{∘} &= 50^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, we conclude that $z = 50$.
