## Introduction

Problems about exterior angles of triangles often appear in geometry questions that combine several angle relationships at once.

In this lesson, we’ll practice recognizing how exterior and interior angles relate to each other, helping us solve multi-step geometry problems.

## Finding an Exterior Angle in a Triangle Given Two Interior Angles

**Example:** In the figure above, two sides of a triangle are extended. What is the value of $x$?

![](<../Source/3.1.7. Exterior Angles of Triangles/Images/754ad676ff05bb2c7177ccd6e00fcc9a.png>)

Note: Figure not drawn to scale.

**Explanation**

Let the measure of the unknown interior angle of the given triangle be $y{:}$

![](<../Source/3.1.7. Exterior Angles of Triangles/Images/b1ff4dee35954a564d28dcf7011a9a00.png>)

In any triangle, the sum of the measures of the interior angles is always $180^\circ$.
So, in the given triangle, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
45^{∘} + 43^{∘} + y &= 180^{∘} \\
88^{∘} + y &= 180^{∘} \\
y &= 180^{∘} - 88^{∘} \\
y &= 92^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, using the fact that the interior and exterior angles are supplementary, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
x + y &= 180^{∘} \\
x + 92^{∘} &= 180^{∘} \\
x &= 180^{∘} - 92^{∘} \\
x &= 88^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the value of $x$ is $88^\circ$.

## Finding an Interior Angle in an Isosceles Triangle Given the Base Exterior Angle

**Example:** In the figure, $\overline{UW}$ extends to point $X$. If $m\angle UVW = m\angle VWU$, what is the value of $x$?

![](<../Source/3.1.7. Exterior Angles of Triangles/Images/13e9a595360df565223bba47055d6458.png>)

Note: Figure not drawn to scale.

**Explanation**

Since $\angle WUX$ is a straight angle and

$$
m\angle VWX=124^\circ
$$

we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
m∠VWU + m∠VWX &= 180^{∘} \\
m∠VWU + 124^{∘} &= 180^{∘} \\
m∠VWU &= 56^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus, we have

$$
m\angle UVW = m\angle VWU = 56^\circ
$$

In any triangle, the sum of the measures of the interior angles is always $180^\circ$. For $\triangle UVW$, this gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
m∠UVW + m∠VWU + m∠WUV &= 180^{∘} \\
x^{∘} + 56^{∘} + 56^{∘} &= 180^{∘} \\
x^{∘} + 112^{∘} &= 180^{∘} \\
x^{∘} &= 68^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the value of $x$ is $68$.

## Finding an Exterior Angle in a Triangle Given Other Interior and Exterior Angles

**Example:** Intersecting lines $r$, $s$, and $t$ are shown. What is the value of $x$?

![](<../Source/3.1.7. Exterior Angles of Triangles/Images/c1542ad588a1fae26700e96bee119168.png>)

Note: Figure not drawn to scale.

**Explanation**

First, let the measure of the acute angle formed at the intersection of lines $s$ and $t$ be $y^\circ$, and the measure of the acute angle formed at the intersection of lines $s$ and $r$ be $z^\circ$.

![](<../Source/3.1.7. Exterior Angles of Triangles/Images/de67a49e8d26516ba361f13374a58965.png>)

Now, since the straight angle formed on line $s$ has a measure of $180^\circ$, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
113^{∘} + y^{∘} &= 180^{∘} \\
y^{∘} &= 67^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

The sum of the measures of the interior angles of a triangle is $180^\circ$. This gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
y^{∘} + z^{∘} + 36^{∘} &= 180^{∘} \\
67^{∘} + z^{∘} + 36^{∘} &= 180^{∘} \\
103^{∘} + z^{∘} &= 180^{∘} \\
z^{∘} &= 77^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, since the straight angle formed on line $r$ also has a measure of $180^\circ$, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^{∘} + z^{∘} &= 180^{∘} \\
x^{∘} + 77^{∘} &= 180^{∘} \\
x^{∘} &= 103^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the value of $x$ is $103$.
