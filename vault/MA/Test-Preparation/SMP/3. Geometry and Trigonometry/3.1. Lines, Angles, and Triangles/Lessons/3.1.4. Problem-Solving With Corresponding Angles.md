## Introduction

On the math section of the SAT exam, corresponding angles often appear in multi-step geometry problems involving parallel lines, transversals, and algebraic expressions.

Practicing how to connect these relationships, especially when they combine with supplementary or triangle angle rules, will help us solve geometry problems efficiently.

---

<a id="using-corresponding-and-supplementary-angles"></a>
## Using Corresponding and Supplementary Angles

**Example:** In the diagram, line $a$ is parallel to line $b$, and line $t$ intersects both lines. If $y = 4x + 3$, what is the value of $x$?

![](<../Source/3.1.4. Problem-Solving With Corresponding Angles/Images/dea8036fb73d245d9715d2dbb09f1af2.png>)

**Explanation**

Since lines $a$ and $b$ are parallel, and the line $t$ acts as a transversal, the angle labeled $69^\circ$ and the angle adjacent to $y^\circ$ inside the parallel lines are corresponding angles. Hence, they are congruent.

![](<../Source/3.1.4. Problem-Solving With Corresponding Angles/Images/754c31781ca77cc771d022922c6e7e78.png>)

The angle labeled $y^\circ$ and the adjacent angle form a linear pair, creating a straight angle at the point of intersection of $b$ and $t$. The measure of a straight angle is $180^\circ$, so we can write

$$
y^\circ + 69^\circ = 180^\circ
$$

We are also told that

$$
y = 4x + 3
$$

Substituting this and solving for $x$, we conclude that

$$
\begin{aligned}
(4x + 3)^{∘} + 69^{∘} &= 180^{∘} \\
4x + 3 + 69 &= 180 \\
4x + 72 &= 180 \\
4x &= 108 \\
x &= 27
\end{aligned}
$$

## Using Corresponding Angles in Triangles

**Example:** In the diagram above, lines $m$ and $n$ are parallel, $r = 50$, and $s = 60$. What is the value of $x$?

![](<../Source/3.1.4. Problem-Solving With Corresponding Angles/Images/d2c62b89ca066d9840291ed8e2579064.png>)

**Explanation**

Consider the triangle, shown in blue below, formed by the two non-parallel lines and the line $m$. Let $t^\circ$ be the measure of the third angle of this triangle.

![](<../Source/3.1.4. Problem-Solving With Corresponding Angles/Images/dfca879119cfc6e20f8a767ad5ba4f19.png>)

Note that, except for the side that is contained in the line $m$, the sides of the triangle act as transversals to the lines $m$ and $n$. Therefore, the angles whose measures are $s^\circ$ and $t^\circ$ are corresponding.

We are given that $m$ and $n$ are parallel. When a transversal intersects two parallel lines, corresponding angles are congruent. Therefore,

$$
t^\circ = s^\circ
$$

![](<../Source/3.1.4. Problem-Solving With Corresponding Angles/Images/a5b744b6209161453449aa4d7f18f149.png>)

In any triangle, the sum of the measures of its interior angles is $180^\circ$. So, we have

$$
x^\circ + r^\circ + t^\circ = 180^\circ
$$

Substituting in the known values, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^{∘} + 50^{∘} + 60^{∘} &= 180^{∘} \\
x^{∘} + 110^{∘} &= 180^{∘} \\
x^{∘} &= 70^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, we can conclude that $x = 70$.

## Using Corresponding and Supplementary Angles With Inequalities

**Example:** In the diagram above, lines $p$ and $q$ are parallel, and the line $t$ intersects both. If $a > 105$, which of the following must be true?

![](<../Source/3.1.4. Problem-Solving With Corresponding Angles/Images/69ca499a6e4392c3dee330e4ee7c882d.png>)

1. $b < 75$
2. $a + b > 180$
3. $b > 75$

**Explanation**

Since lines $p$ and $q$ are parallel, and the line $t$ acts as a transversal, the angle labeled $a^\circ$ and the angle adjacent to $b^\circ$ outside the parallel lines are corresponding angles. Hence, they are congruent.

![](<../Source/3.1.4. Problem-Solving With Corresponding Angles/Images/39dcbe9c54a7b59369ab33858b5fd89a.png>)

The angle labeled $a^\circ$ and the adjacent angle form a linear pair, creating a straight angle at the point of intersection of $t$ and $q$. The measure of a straight angle is $180^\circ$, so we can write

$$
b + a = 180
$$

So, statement II is false.

Next, we are told that $a > 105$. Substituting this into the equation, we get
$\begin{bmatrix}a & > 105 \\ b + a & > b + 105 \\ 180 & > b + 105 \\ 75 & > b \\ b & < 75\end{bmatrix}$.

So, statement I is true while statement III is false.

Therefore, the correct answer is "I only".

## Finding an Unknown Constant Given Expressions for Supplementary Angles

**Example:** A line intersects two parallel lines, forming four acute angles and four obtuse angles. The measure of one of the obtuse angles is $(11m + 94)^\circ$. The sum of one obtuse angle and three of the acute angles is $(k - 22m)^\circ$. What is the value of $k$?

**Explanation**

When a transversal intersects two parallel lines, it creates eight angles in total: four acute angles and four obtuse angles. All acute angles are congruent, and all obtuse angles are congruent, as shown below.

![](<../Source/3.1.4. Problem-Solving With Corresponding Angles/Images/473f35fb33db4090d0063c6e62ff37c5.png>)

Let $\angle A$ be an acute angle and $\angle O$ be an obtuse angle.

In our case, the measure of one obtuse angle is

$$
m\angle O = (11m + 94)^\circ
$$

Since acute and obtuse angles formed in this configuration are supplementary, their measures sum to $180^\circ{:}$

$$
m\angle A + m\angle O = 180^\circ
$$

Hence, the measure of each acute angle is

$$
\begin{aligned}
m∠A &= 180^{∘} - m∠O \\
&= 180^{∘} - (11m + 94)^{∘} \\
&= 180^{∘} - 11m^{∘} - 94^{∘} \\
&= (86 - 11m)^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

We are told that the sum of one obtuse angle and three acute angles is $(k - 22m)^\circ$. Writing as an equation, we have

$$
m\angle O + 3 \cdot m\angle A = (k - 22m)^\circ
$$

Substituting in our expressions for $m\angle O$ and $m\angle A$, we solve for $k$, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(11m + 94)^{∘} + 3 \cdot (86 - 11m)^{∘} &= (k - 22m)^{∘} \\
11m + 94 + 258 - 33m &= k - 22m \\
352 - 22m &= k - 22m \\
k &= 352
\end{aligned}
$$
