## Introduction

Understanding how corresponding angles behave when a transversal intersects parallel lines is a frequent focus on the math section of the SAT exam.

Mastering these angle relationships and practicing how to spot them quickly will help us solve many geometry problems with confidence and speed.

---

<a id="identifying-corresponding-angles"></a>
## Identifying Corresponding Angles

**Example:** In the diagram above, lines $\ell$ and $k$ are parallel, and the line $p$ intersects both of them. What is the value of $x$?

![](<../Source/3.1.1. Corresponding Angles/Images/b240d873795290ca34dc52940080100b.png>)

**Explanation**

We are given that $\ell$ and $k$ are parallel. Since line $p$ intersects both lines $\ell$ and $k$, it's a transversal.

The angle whose measure is $152^\circ$ and the angle whose measure is $x^\circ$ are corresponding pairs of angles since they are located in the same position as each other relative to the parallel lines and on the same side of the transversal.

When a transversal intersects two parallel lines, corresponding angles are congruent. So,

$$
x^\circ = 152^\circ
$$

Therefore, we can conclude that

$$
x = \bbox[4pt,border: 1px solid gainsboro]{152}
$$

## Identifying Supplementary Angles Formed by a Transversal

**Example:** In the diagram above, lines $j$ and $k$ are parallel, and the line $t$ intersects both. What is the value of $x^\circ + 154^\circ$?

![](<../Source/3.1.1. Corresponding Angles/Images/0ad19807b7fc616334f01f9582a4e153.png>)

**Explanation**

The angles labeled $154^\circ$ and $x^\circ$ are on a straight line at the point where the transversal $t$ intersects the line $j$.

Since angles on a straight line form a linear pair, their measures must sum to $180^\circ$. So, we can write

$$
x^\circ + 154^\circ = 180^\circ
$$

## Using Corresponding and Supplementary Angles

**Example:** In the diagram above, lines $m$ and $n$ are parallel, and the line $t$ intersects both. What is the value of $x$?

![](<../Source/3.1.1. Corresponding Angles/Images/bc747ae2b7b3a705d53491d6d5c38cc8.png>)

**Explanation**

Since lines $m$ and $n$ are parallel, and the line $t$ acts as a transversal, the angle labeled $143^\circ$ and the angle adjacent to $x^\circ$ outside the parallel lines are corresponding angles. Hence, they are congruent.

![](<../Source/3.1.1. Corresponding Angles/Images/1350553ed20a412409ba901a3dff0cbe.png>)

The angle labeled $x^\circ$ and the adjacent angle form a linear pair, creating a straight angle at the point of intersection of $t$ and $n$. The measure of a straight angle is $180^\circ$. Therefore, we have

$$
x^\circ + 143^\circ = 180^\circ
$$

Solving for $x$, we get

$$
x^\circ = 180^\circ - 143^\circ = 37^\circ
$$

Therefore, we conclude that

$$
x = \bbox[4pt,border: 1px solid gainsboro]{37}
$$
