## Introduction

Questions about triangle angle relationships are common on the math section of the SAT exam, often appearing in geometry problems that require quick reasoning about missing or unknown angles.

By practicing working with these relationships, we’ll build speed and accuracy for solving multi-step problems involving triangles and other polygons.

## Finding an Angle in a Triangle Given the Other Two Angles

**Example:** In $\triangle ABC$, the measure of $\angle A$ is $64^{\circ}$ and the measure of $\angle B$ is $81^{\circ}$. What is the measure of $\angle C$?

**Explanation**

The sum of the measures of the interior angles of a triangle is $180^\circ$. For the given $\triangle ABC$, this gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
m∠A + m∠B + m∠C &= 180^{∘} \\
64^{∘} + 81^{∘} + m∠C &= 180^{∘} \\
145^{∘} + m∠C &= 180^{∘} \\
m∠C &= 180^{∘} - 145^{∘} \\
m∠C &= 35^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the measure of $\angle C$ is $35^\circ$.

**Explanation**

In any triangle, the sum of the measures of the interior angles is always $180^\circ$.
So, in the given triangle, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}

31^{∘} + 63^{∘} + 2n^{∘} &= 180^{∘} \\
94^{∘} + 2n^{∘} &= 180^{∘} \\

n^{∘} &= 43^{∘}

## Solving for a Variable in Triangle Angle Measures

**Example:** In $\triangle R S T$, the measure of $\angle R$ is $58^{\circ}$, the measure of $\angle S$ is $90^{\circ}$, and the measure of $\angle T$ is $\left(\dfrac{z}{3}\right)^{\circ}$. What is the value of $z$?

**Explanation**

The sum of the measures of the interior angles of a triangle is $180^\circ$. For the given $\triangle RST$, this gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
m∠R + m∠S + m∠T &= 180^{∘} \\
58^{∘} + 90^{∘} + (\frac{z}{3})^{∘} &= 180^{∘} \\
148^{∘} + (\frac{z}{3})^{∘} &= 180^{∘} \\
(\frac{z}{3})^{∘} &= 32^{∘} \\
\frac{z}{3} &= 32 \\
z &= 96
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the value of $z$ is $96$.

## Identifying an Upper Bound for the Measure of Triangle Angle

**Example:** In $\triangle ABC$, the measure of $\angle A$ is $67^{\circ}$. Which of the following could be the measure, in degrees, of $\angle B$?

1. $121^\circ$
2. $129^\circ$
3. $109^\circ$
4. $125^\circ$

**Explanation**

In $\triangle ABC$, the sum of the measures of the interior angles is always $180^\circ$.

Thus, the sum of the measures of $\angle A$ and $\angle B$ must be *less than* $180^\circ$, as there must be room for $\angle C$. This means

$$
\begin{bmatrix}m∠A + m∠B & < 180^{∘} \\ 67^{∘} + m∠B & < 180^{∘} \\ m∠B & < 113^{∘}\end{bmatrix}
$$

Therefore, among the choices, only $109^\circ$ could be the measure of $\angle B$.
