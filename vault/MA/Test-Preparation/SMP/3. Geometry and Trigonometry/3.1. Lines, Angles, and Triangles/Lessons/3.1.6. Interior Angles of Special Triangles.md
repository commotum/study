## Introduction

Questions about special triangles, especially right and isosceles triangles, appear frequently in geometry and trigonometry problems. By practicing recognizing these triangles and their angle relationships, we can solve these kinds of problems with fluency.

## Finding an Angle in a Right Triangle Given Another Angle

**Example:** In the right triangle shown above, what is the value of $x$?

![](<../Source/3.1.6. Interior Angles of Special Triangles/Images/d9f806e4bb1c2d974ab19a055ac85bfe.png>)

Note: Figure not drawn to scale.

**Explanation**

In any triangle, the sum of the measures of the interior angles is always $180^\circ$.
So, in the given right triangle, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
90^{∘} + 44^{∘} + x^{∘} &= 180^{∘} \\
134^{∘} + x^{∘} &= 180^{∘} \\
x^{∘} &= 180^{∘} - 134^{∘} \\
x^{∘} &= 46^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the value of $x$ is $46$.

## Finding an Angle in an Isosceles Triangle Given the Apex Angle

**Example:** In the given triangle, $RS = ST$ and $\angle SRT$ has a measure of $47^{\circ}$. What is the value of $x$?

![](<../Source/3.1.6. Interior Angles of Special Triangles/Images/c0a82b127b944a6520fd40ced4e005e7.png>)

Note: Figure not drawn to scale.

**Explanation**

Consider $\triangle RST$. Since $RS=ST$, it is an isosceles triangle with the base $\overline{RT}$.

So, the base angles are equal:

$$
m\angle SRT = m\angle RTS = 47^\circ
$$

Since the sum of the measures of the interior angles of a triangle is $180^\circ$, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
m∠SRT + m∠RTS + m∠RST &= 180^{∘} \\
47^{∘} + 47^{∘} + x^{∘} &= 180^{∘} \\
94^{∘} + x^{∘} &= 180^{∘} \\
x^{∘} &= 86^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the value of $x$ is $86$.

## Finding an Angle in an Isosceles Triangle Given the Base Angle

**Example:** In $\triangle A U V$, the measures of $\angle A$ and $\angle V$ are each $80^{\circ}$. What is the measure of $\angle U$, in degrees?

**Explanation**

The sum of the measures of the interior angles of a triangle is $180^\circ$. For the given $\triangle AUV$, this gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
m∠A + m∠U + m∠V &= 180^{∘} \\
80^{∘} + m∠U + 80^{∘} &= 180^{∘} \\
160^{∘} + m∠U &= 180^{∘} \\
m∠U &= 180^{∘} - 160^{∘} \\
m∠U &= 20^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Identifying a Possible Angle in an Isosceles Triangle Given Another Angle

**Example:** In $\triangle A B C$, the measure of $\angle A$ is $70^{\circ}$. If $\triangle ABC$ is isosceles, which of the following is **not** a possible measure of $\angle B$?

1. $62^\circ$
2. $70^\circ$
3. $55^\circ$
4. $40^\circ$

**Explanation**

In $\triangle ABC$, the sum of the measures of the interior angles is $180^\circ{:}$

$$
m\angle A + m\angle B + m\angle C = 180^\circ
$$

Since $\triangle ABC$ is isosceles, it must have at least two equal angles. Let's consider the distinct possibilities.

- If $m\angle B = m\angle A = 70^\circ$, then $70^\circ$ is a possible measure of $\angle B$.
- If $m\angle B = m\angle C$, then we have
$m∠A + m∠B + m∠C|= 180^{∘}; m∠A + m∠B + m∠B|= 180^{∘}; m∠A + 2m∠B|= 180^{∘}; 70^{∘} + 2m∠B|= 180^{∘}; 2m∠B|= 110^{∘}; m∠B|= 55^{∘}$.
So, $55^\circ$ is a possible measure of $\angle B$.
- If $m\angle C = m\angle A = 70^\circ$, then we have
$m∠A + m∠B + m∠C|= 180^{∘}; 70^{∘} + m∠B + 70^{∘}|= 180^{∘}; 140^{∘} + m∠B|= 180^{∘}; m∠B|= 40^{∘}$.
So, $40^\circ$ is a possible measure of $\angle B$.

Therefore, among the choices, only $62^\circ$ is not a possible measure of $\angle B$.
