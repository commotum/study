## Introduction

Questions about consecutive angles often appear in geometry problems involving parallel lines, polygons, and transversals, where recognizing supplementary relationships quickly can save time.

By practicing these patterns until they become second nature, we’ll strengthen our geometric reasoning and improve test efficiency.

## Identifying Consecutive Interior Angles

**Example:** In the diagram above, lines $m$ and $n$ are parallel, and the line $t$ intersects both. What is the value of $x$?

![](<../Source/3.1.2. Consecutive Angles/Images/9eaf476a2a026ea6aed8c92189c9587a.png>)

**Explanation**

We are given that $m$ and $n$ are parallel. Since line $t$ intersects both lines $m$ and $n$, it is a transversal.

The angle labeled $60^\circ$ and the angle labeled $x^\circ$ are consecutive interior angles because they are on the same side of the transversal $t$ and between the parallel lines $m$ and $n$.

When a transversal intersects two parallel lines, consecutive interior angles are supplementary. So, we can write

$$
x^\circ + 60^\circ = 180^\circ
$$

Solving for $x$, we get

$$
x^\circ = 180^\circ - 60^\circ = 120^\circ
$$

Therefore, we can conclude that $x = 120$.

## Identifying Consecutive Exterior Angles

**Example:** In the diagram above, lines $n$ and $p$ are parallel, and the line $z$ intersects both. What is the value of $z$?

![](<../Source/3.1.2. Consecutive Angles/Images/cba31f8870931567a27ed4b38b471cb1.png>)

**Explanation**

We are given that $n$ and $p$ are parallel. Since line $h$ intersects both lines $n$ and $p$, it's a transversal.

The angle labeled $72^\circ$ and the angle labeled $z^\circ$ are consecutive exterior angles since they are on the same side of the transversal $h$ and outside the parallel lines $n$ and $p$.

When a transversal intersects two parallel lines, the consecutive exterior angles are supplementary. So, we can write

$$
z^\circ + 72^\circ = 180^\circ
$$

Solving for $z$, we get

$$
z^\circ = 180^\circ - 72^\circ = 108^\circ
$$

Therefore, we can conclude that $z=108$.

## Using Consecutive Interior Angles in Triangles

**Example:** In the diagram above, lines $m$ and $n$ are parallel. What is the value of $y$?

![](<../Source/3.1.2. Consecutive Angles/Images/d9292fe25d7b873c14618ef12ad8bb24.png>)

**Explanation**

We are given that $m$ and $n$ are parallel. Since line $k$ intersects both lines $m$ and $n$, it's a transversal.

The angle labeled $129^\circ$ and the angle labeled $x^\circ$ are consecutive interior angles since they are on the same side of the transversal $k$ and inside the parallel lines $m$ and $n$.

When a transversal intersects two parallel lines, the consecutive interior angles are supplementary. So, we can write

$$
x^\circ + 129^\circ = 180^\circ
$$

Solving for $x$, we get

$$
x^\circ = 180^\circ - 129^\circ = 51^\circ
$$

Now, consider the triangle formed by the lines $j$, $m$, and $\ell$, shown in blue below.

![](<../Source/3.1.2. Consecutive Angles/Images/a4dad2b7950116bd7072887c00336491.png>)

In any triangle, the sum of the measures of its interior angles is $180^\circ$. So, we have

$$
x^\circ + 90^\circ + y^\circ = 180^\circ
$$

Substituting the known values, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
51^{∘} + 90^{∘} + y^{∘} &= 180^{∘} \\
y^{∘} + 141^{∘} &= 180^{∘} \\
y^{∘} &= 39^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, we can conclude that $y=39$.

## Finding an Angle Measure in a Pentagon With a Pair of Parallel Sides Given Some of Its Angles

**Example:** In convex pentagon $LMNOP$, segment $\overline{LM}$ is parallel to segment $\overline{OP}$. If the measure of $\angle M$ is $132^\circ$ and the measure of $\angle O$ is $150^\circ$, what is the measure, in degrees, of $\angle N$?

**Explanation**

We proceed to represent the given information in a diagram.

![](<../Source/3.1.2. Consecutive Angles/Images/e34c317654ed88978d018cc82c487ee4.png>)

We are given that $\overline{LM}$ is parallel to $\overline{OP}$. When transversal $\overline{LP}$ cuts both, the consecutive interior angles formed are supplementary. So,

$$
m\angle{L} + m\angle{P} = 180^\circ
$$

On the other hand, the sum of the interior angles of a polygon with $n$ sides equals
$180^\circ \cdot (n-2)$.
Every pentagon has $n = 5$ sides. Applying the formula, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
180^{∘} \cdot (n - 2) &= 180^{∘} \cdot (5 - 2) \\
&= 180^{∘} \cdot 3 \\
&= 540^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, for our pentagon $LMNOP$, we have

$$
m\angle{L} + m\angle{M} + m\angle{N} + m\angle{O} + m\angle{P} = 540^\circ
$$

Finally, if we substitute the values given for $m\angle{M}$ and $m\angle{O}$ into the previous equality, take into account the relationship between $m\angle{L}$ and $m\angle{P}$, and then solve for $m\angle{N}$, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
m∠L + m∠M + m∠N + m∠O + m∠P &= 540^{∘} \\
m∠N + m∠M + m∠O + (m∠L + m∠P) &= 540^{∘} \\
m∠N + 132^{∘} + 150^{∘} + 180^{∘} &= 540^{∘} \\
m∠N &= 78^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$
