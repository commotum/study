## Introduction

Problems involving multiple connected triangles usually test the ability to identify shared angles, use geometric relationships, and combine information from several figures.

In this topic, we'll practice these multi-triangle setups to strengthen our spatial reasoning.

## Finding Angles in Connected Triangles Using Vertical Angles

**Example:** In the figure above, $\overline{RU}$ intersects $\overline{SV}$ at $T$. If $x = 105^\circ$, what is the value of $y$?

![](<../Source/3.1.8. Solving Problems With Multiple Triangles/Images/4d75b3a85b6760a61aee7e6c21fd5db5.png>)

Note: Figure not drawn to scale.

**Explanation**

In $\triangle RST$, the sum of the interior angles is always $180^\circ{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
m∠RST + m∠SRT + m∠STR &= 180^{∘} \\
x + 58^{∘} + m∠STR &= 180^{∘} \\
105^{∘} + 58^{∘} + m∠STR &= 180^{∘} \\
163^{∘} + m∠STR &= 180^{∘} \\
m∠STR &= 17^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

$\angle STR$ equals its vertical angle $\angle V T U{:}$

![](<../Source/3.1.8. Solving Problems With Multiple Triangles/Images/68b033423824e788a8fb86f87e3d1263.png>)

In $\triangle U V T$, the sum of the interior angles is always $180^\circ{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
m∠VTU + m∠TUV + m∠TVU &= 180^{∘} \\
17^{∘} + y + 63^{∘} &= 180^{∘} \\
y + 80^{∘} &= 180^{∘} \\
y &= 100^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the value of $y$ is $100^\circ$.

## Finding Angles in Connected Triangles Using Common Angles

**Example:** In the figure above, $PM = PO$. What is the value of $x$?

![](<../Source/3.1.8. Solving Problems With Multiple Triangles/Images/3777d97e23df03fa019038dbabea31a7.png>)

Note: Figure not drawn to scale.

**Explanation**

Consider $\triangle MPO$.

Since $PM=PO$, it is an isosceles triangle with the base $\overline{MO}$.
So, the base angles are equal:

$$
m\angle M = m\angle O
$$

The sum of the measures of the interior angles of a triangle is $180^\circ$. For $\triangle MPO$, this gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
m∠MPO + m∠M + m∠O &= 180^{∘} \\
132^{∘} + m∠O + m∠O &= 180^{∘} \\
132^{∘} + 2m∠O &= 180^{∘} \\
2m∠O &= 48^{∘} \\
m∠O &= 24^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, consider $\triangle ONQ$.

Using again that the sum of the measures of the interior angles of a triangle is $180^\circ$, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
m∠ONQ + m∠O + m∠Q &= 180^{∘} \\
m∠ONQ + 24^{∘} + 19^{∘} &= 180^{∘} \\
m∠ONQ + 43^{∘} &= 180^{∘} \\
m∠ONQ &= 137^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, since $\angle MON$ is a straight angle and

$$
m\angle ONQ=137^\circ
$$

we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
m∠QNM + m∠ONQ &= 180^{∘} \\
x^{∘} + 137^{∘} &= 180^{∘} \\
x^{∘} &= 43^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the value of $x$ is $43$.

## Further Finding Angles in Connected Triangles Using Common Angles

**Example:** In the figure, $RS = SP$, $m\angle TQS = 21^\circ$, and $m\angle RQT = 74^\circ$. What is the value of $x$?

![](<../Source/3.1.8. Solving Problems With Multiple Triangles/Images/180ffdb144ec6e369d3b1882943d5b2c.png>)

Note: Figure not drawn to scale.

**Explanation**

First, let's represent all the given angles on the graph.

![](<../Source/3.1.8. Solving Problems With Multiple Triangles/Images/69526715b1b58b3a2a0c3d4365c7d923.png>)

First, since $\angle PQR$ is a straight angle and

$$
m\angle RQT=74^\circ
$$

we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
m∠PQT + m∠RQT &= 180^{∘} \\
m∠PQT + 74^{∘} &= 180^{∘} \\
m∠PQT &= 106^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, consider $\triangle PQT$.

Since the sum of the measures of the interior angles of a triangle is $180^\circ$, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
m∠PQT + m∠QTS + m∠P &= 180^{∘} \\
106^{∘} + 21^{∘} + m∠P &= 180^{∘} \\
127^{∘} + m∠P &= 180^{∘} \\
m∠P &= 53^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

So, we have the following:

![](<../Source/3.1.8. Solving Problems With Multiple Triangles/Images/3c35e24444d534fd27ecadeb0ebc862e.png>)

Consider $\triangle RSP$.

Since $RS = SP$, it is an isosceles triangle with the base $\overline{RP}$.
So, the base angles are equal:

$$
m\angle R = m\angle P = 53^\circ
$$

The sum of the measures of the interior angles of a triangle is $180^\circ$. For $\triangle RSP$, this gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
m∠P + m∠R + m∠RSP &= 180^{∘} \\
2m∠P + m∠RSP &= 180^{∘} \\
2(53^{∘}) + x^{∘} &= 180^{∘} \\
106^{∘} + x^{∘} &= 180^{∘} \\
x^{∘} &= 74^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the value of $x$ is $74$.
