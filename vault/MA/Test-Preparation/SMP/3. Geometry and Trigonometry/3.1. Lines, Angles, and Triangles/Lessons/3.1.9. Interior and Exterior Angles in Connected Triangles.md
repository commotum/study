## Introduction

Problems involving connected triangles often require combining facts about both interior and exterior angles to determine unknown measures.

With consistent practice, we’ll learn to connect multiple geometric relationships to solve connected triangle problems.

## Finding an Angle Given Connected Triangles With Common Sides

**Example:** In the figure above, $\overline{LN}$ and $\overline{MO}$ intersect at point $P$, $MP = NP$, and $LP = PO$. What is the measure, in degrees, of $\angle NLO$?

![](<../Source/3.1.9. Interior and Exterior Angles in Connected Triangles/Images/20711457288b3acaded0b65fa115507c.png>)

Note: Figure not drawn to scale.

**Explanation**

First, since $\angle NLP$ is a straight angle and

$$
m\angle NPO=58^\circ
$$

we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
m∠LPO + m∠NPO &= 180^{∘} \\
m∠LPO + 58^{∘} &= 180^{∘} \\
m∠LPO &= 122^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, consider $\triangle LPO$.

Since $LP=PO$, it is an isosceles triangle with the base $\overline{LO}$.
So, the base angles are equal:

$$
m\angle PLO = m\angle OLP
$$

The sum of the measures of the interior angles of a triangle is $180^\circ$. For $\triangle LPO$, this gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
m∠PLO + m∠OLP + m∠LPO &= 180^{∘} \\
2m∠PLO + 122^{∘} &= 180^{∘} \\
2m∠PLO &= 58^{∘} \\
m∠PLO &= 29^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, since $P$ lies on $\overline{LN}$, the angle between $\overline{NL}$ and $\overline{LO}$ is the same as the angle between $\overline{PL}$ and $\overline{LO}$. Therefore,

$$
m\angle NLO = m\angle PLO = 29^\circ
$$

## Finding an Angle Given Connected Triangles With Common Angles

**Example:** In the figure above, points $B$, $C$, $D$, and $E$ lie on line segment $\overline{AF}$, line segment $\overline{CZ}$ intersects line segment $\overline{DY}$ at point $M$, $m\angle DBY = 57^\circ$, $m\angle DYB = 71^\circ$, $m\angle DMZ = 88^\circ$, and $m\angle FEZ = 148^\circ$. What is the measure, in degrees, of $\angle EZC$?

![](<../Source/3.1.9. Interior and Exterior Angles in Connected Triangles/Images/7d1e752d455a98d214c0847169ef5943.png>)

Note: *Figure not drawn to scale.*

**Explanation**

First, let's represent all the given angles on the graph.

![](<../Source/3.1.9. Interior and Exterior Angles in Connected Triangles/Images/0617fa05ca87b1d98aa4a0be15afb8f4.png>)

Consider $\triangle DBY$.

Since the sum of the measures of the interior angles of a triangle is $180^\circ$, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
m∠DBY + m∠BYD + m∠DYB &= 180^{∘} \\
57^{∘} + m∠BYD + 71^{∘} &= 180^{∘} \\
128^{∘} + m∠BYD &= 180^{∘} \\
m∠BYD &= 52^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

So, we have the following (we disregard the angles that we already used):

![](<../Source/3.1.9. Interior and Exterior Angles in Connected Triangles/Images/d114259121a7878ab501cf0f44b2baec.png>)

Since $\angle CMZ$ is a straight angle and

$$
m\angle DMZ=88^\circ
$$

we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
m∠DMC + m∠DMZ &= 180^{∘} \\
m∠DMC + 88^{∘} &= 180^{∘} \\
m∠DMC &= 92^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

Consider now $\triangle CDM$.

Using again that the sum of the measures of the interior angles of a triangle is $180^\circ$, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
m∠CDM + m∠DCM + m∠DMC &= 180^{∘} \\
52^{∘} + m∠DCM + 92^{∘} &= 180^{∘} \\
144^{∘} + m∠DCM &= 180^{∘} \\
m∠DCM &= 36^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

This gives the following (we disregard the angles that we already used):

![](<../Source/3.1.9. Interior and Exterior Angles in Connected Triangles/Images/0d2a71d852da8e8fd6b9da20c9fa8751.png>)

Since $\angle AEF$ is straight, it has a measure of $180^\circ$. At $E$ we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
m∠ZEC + m∠FEZ &= 180^{∘} \\
m∠ZEC + 148^{∘} &= 180^{∘} \\
m∠ZEC &= 32^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, in $\triangle EZC$, the sum of the measures of the interior angles is $180^\circ$. This results in

$$
\begin{aligned}
m∠ZEC + m∠EZC + m∠ECZ &= 180^{∘} \\
32^{∘} + m∠EZC + 36^{∘} &= 180^{∘} \\
68^{∘} + m∠EZC &= 180^{∘} \\
m∠EZC &= 112^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$
