## Introduction

Simplifying rational expressions using polynomial factorization tests our ability to spot, factor, and cancel common polynomial terms.

In this topic, we’ll practice simplifying rational expressions by factoring trinomials, differences of squares, and common binomials.

---

<a id="simplifying-a-rational-expression-by-factoring-a-difference-of-squares"></a>
## Simplifying a Rational Expression by Factoring a Difference of Squares

**Example:** Consider the expression
$\dfrac{k^{2} - 81}{k - 9}$,
If the expression is equivalent to $k + a$, where $k \ne 9$ and $a$ is a constant, what is the value of $a$?

**Explanation**

We are told that the expression

$$
\dfrac{k^{2} - 81}{k - 9}
$$

is equivalent to $k + a$ for some constant $a$ and

$$
k \ne 9
$$

First, we factor the numerator as a difference of squares and simplify:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{k^{2} - 81}{k - 9} &= ((k - 9)(k + 9))/(k - 9) \\
&= ((k - 9)(k + 9))/(k - 9) \\
&= k + 9
\end{aligned}
$$

Since

$$
k \ne 9
$$

we are allowed to cancel the common factor $(k - 9)$.

Therefore, by matching the final expression $k + 9$ with its given equivalent expression $k + a$, we conclude that $a = 9$.

## Simplifying a Rational Expression by Factoring a Difference of Squares With Surds

**Example:** Which expression is equivalent to $\dfrac{u^2-11}{u+\sqrt{11}}$, where $u\neq-\sqrt{11}$?

1. $u+\sqrt{11}$
2. $u+11$
3. $u-\sqrt{11}$
4. $\dfrac{u}{\sqrt{11}}$

**Explanation**

Observe that our numerator $u^2-11$ may be written as a difference of squares:

$$
u^2-11=u^2-\big(\sqrt{11}\big)^2
$$

Hence, applying the formula for factoring the difference of squares, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
u^{2} - (\sqrt{11})^{2} &= (u - \sqrt{11})(u + \sqrt{11})
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we may simplify our expression as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{u^{2} - 11}{u + \sqrt{11}} &= ((u - \sqrt{11})(u + \sqrt{11}))/(u + \sqrt{11}) \\
&= ((u - \sqrt{11})(u + \sqrt{11}))/(u + \sqrt{11}) \\
&= u - \sqrt{11}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the correct answer is expression "III."

## Simplifying a Rational Expression by Factoring a Common Binomial and a Difference of Squares

**Example:** Which expression is equivalent to $\dfrac{r^2(2r - 3) - 36(2r - 3)}{r + 6}$, where $r\neq -6$?

1. $(2r - 3)(r + 6)$
2. $(2r - 3)(r - 6)$
3. $(2r - 3)(r^2 - 36)$
4. $2r^3 - 3r^2$

**Explanation**

To simplify this quotient, we first factor the numerator. We have

$$
\operatorname{GCF}\left(r^2(2r - 3),36(2r - 3)\right)=2r - 3
$$

Therefore,

$$
\dfrac{r^2(2r - 3) - 36(2r - 3)}{r + 6} = \dfrac{(2r - 3)(r^2 - 36)}{r + 6}
$$

Now, notice that we have a difference of squares in our numerator. Factoring this difference of squares, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
((2r - 3)(r^{2} - 36))/(r + 6) &= ((2r - 3)(r^{2} - 6^{2}))/(r + 6) \\
&= ((2r - 3)(r - 6)(r + 6))/(r + 6)
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, since

$$
r\neq -6
$$

we may simplify this to

$$
\begin{aligned}
((2r - 3)(r - 6)(r + 6))/(r + 6) &= ((2r - 3)(r + 6)(r - 6))/(r + 6) \\
&= (2r - 3)(r - 6)
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the correct answer is expression "II."

## Simplifying a Rational Expression by Factoring a Trinomial

**Example:** The equation
$\dfrac{w^2 + 2w - 15}{w - 3} = bw + c$
is true for all $w \neq 3$, where $b$ and $c$ are integers. What is the value of $b + c$?

**Explanation**

We are given a rational expression that is equivalent to a linear expression for all

$$
w \neq 3
$$

To determine $b$ and $c$, we divide the numerator by the denominator

$$
\dfrac{w^2 + 2w - 15}{w - 3}
$$

First, we look to factor the numerator
$w^2 + 2w - 15$.
We search for two numbers whose product is $-15$ and whose sum is $2$

$$
5 \times (-3) = -15 \quad \text{and} \quad 5 + (-3) = 2
$$

Therefore, we can factor the expression in the numerator as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
w^{2} + 2w - 15 &= w^{2} + (5 - 3)w - 15 \\
&= w^{2} + 5w - 3w - 15 \\
&= w(w + 5) - 3(w + 5) \\
&= (w + 5)(w - 3)
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we substitute the factored form back into the original expression:

$$
\dfrac{w^2 + 2w - 15}{w - 3} = \dfrac{(w + 5)(w - 3)}{w - 3}
$$

For all

$$
w \neq 3
$$

, we can cancel the common factor $(w - 3)$ to get

$$
w + 5 = bw + c
$$

Comparing coefficients, we have $b = 1$ and $c = 5$. Therefore, the value of $b + c$ is $6$.
