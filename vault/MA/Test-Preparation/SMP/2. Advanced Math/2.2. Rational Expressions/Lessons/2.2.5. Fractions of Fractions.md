## Introduction

Problems involving fractions of fractions may also appear on the math section of the SAT exam to test the ability to simplify layered rational expressions.

Here, we’ll practice reducing complex fractional expressions step by step, focusing on recognizing structure, factoring common terms, and rewriting them as simple rational forms.

<a id="fractions-of-fractions-with-common-binomial-factors"></a>
## Fractions of Fractions With Common Binomial Factors

**Example:** Which expression is equivalent to $\dfrac{\left(\dfrac{14}{x-5}\right)}{7x-35}$, where $x\neq 5$?

1. $\dfrac{7}{(x-5)^2}$
2. $\dfrac{1}{(x-5)^2}$
3. $\dfrac{2}{(x-5)^2}$
4. $\dfrac{1}{2(x-5)^2}$

**Explanation**

Here, we wish to simplify

$$
\dfrac{\left(\dfrac{14}{x-5}\right)}{7x-35}
$$

First, notice that

$$
\operatorname{GCF}(7x,35)=7
$$

and so we may factor our denominator to obtain

$$
\dfrac{\left(\dfrac{14}{x-5}\right)}{7x-35}=\dfrac{\left(\dfrac{14}{x-5}\right)}{7(x-5)}
$$

Now, recall that division is equivalent to multiplication by the reciprocal, and so we may rewrite our complex fraction as

$$
\begin{aligned}
((\frac{14}{x - 5}))/(7(x - 5)) &= \frac{14}{x - 5} \cdot (1)/(7(x - 5)) \\
&= (14)/(7(x - 5)^{2}) \\
&= (7 \cdot 2)/(7(x - 5)^{2}) \\
&= (7 \cdot 2)/(7(x - 5)^{2}) \\
&= (2)/((x - 5)^{2})
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the correct answer is expression "III."

## The Reciprocal of a Sum of a Rational Expression and Binomial

**Example:** What is the equivalent simple fraction for the expression?

$\dfrac{1}{\left(\dfrac{2}{r + 1} + r - 4\right)}$

**Explanation**

In order to convert this to a simple fraction, we must obtain a common denominator for our fractions in the denominator of the entire fraction. The common denominator is $r + 1$, as it is the only denominator of the terms in the denominator of the whole expression.

We now multiply the entire fraction by $1$ in the form

$$
\dfrac{r + 1}{r + 1}{:}
$$

$$
(1)/((\frac{2}{r + 1} + r - 4)) \cdot \frac{r + 1}{r + 1}
$$

- First, we simplify the numerator: $1 \cdot (r + 1) = r + 1$
- Then, we simplify the denominator:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(\frac{2}{r + 1} + r - 4) \cdot (r + 1) &= (2(r + 1))/(r + 1) + (r - 4)(r + 1) \\
&= 2 + r^{2} + r - 4r - 4 \\
&= r^{2} - 3r - 2
\end{aligned}
$$

Hence, the full simplified fraction is

$$
\dfrac{r + 1}{r^2 - 3r - 2}
$$

## The Reciprocal of a Sum of Rational Expressions

**Example:** What is the equivalent simple fraction for the following expression?

$\dfrac{1}{\left(\dfrac{-2}{q + 1} + \dfrac{5}{q - 4}\right)}$

**Explanation**

In order to convert this to a simple fraction, we must obtain a common denominator for our fractions in the denominator of the entire fraction. The common denominator is $(q + 1)(q - 4)$, as the terms are distinct linear factors.

We now multiply the entire fraction by $1$ in the form

$$
\dfrac{(q + 1)(q - 4)}{(q + 1)(q - 4)}{:}
$$

$$
(1)/((\frac{-2}{q + 1} + \frac{5}{q - 4})) \cdot ((q + 1)(q - 4))/((q + 1)(q - 4))
$$

- First, we simplify the numerator: $1\cdot(q + 1)(q - 4) = q^2 - 3q - 4$
- Then, we simplify the denominator:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(\frac{-2}{q + 1} + \frac{5}{q - 4})(q + 1)(q - 4) &= (-2(q + 1)(q - 4))/(q + 1) + (5(q + 1)(q - 4))/(q - 4) \\
&=-2(q - 4) + 5(q + 1) \\
&=-2q + 8 + 5q + 5 \\
&= 3q + 13
\end{aligned}
$$

Hence, the full simplified fraction is

$$
\dfrac{q^2 - 3q - 4}{3q + 13}
$$
