## Constructing Exponential Decay Models From Descriptions

**Example:** A lighthouse installs a beacon on top of a tower that stands $50$ feet high. Due to salty sea winds, the beacon’s maximum height decreases slightly each day. After each day, the maximum height of the tip of the beacon is $70\%$ of the height it reached the day before.

Let $h(n)$ represent the estimated maximum height (in feet) of the beacon's tip after $n$ days, where $n$ is a whole number greater than $1$ and less than $10$. Which equation correctly defines $h(n)$?

**Explanation**

The height of the tip of the beacon starts at $50$ feet and reduces to $70\%$ of its previous height each day. This means we are dealing with exponential decay.

The general formula for exponential decay using a decay factor $b$ is

$$
h(n) = a \cdot b^n
$$

In our case, we have the following:

- $a = 50$ is the initial height.
- $b = 0.70$ is the decay factor (since $70\% = 0.70$).
- $n$ is the number of days.

Substituting these into the formula, we get

$$
h(n) = 50 \cdot (0.70)^n
$$

## Constructing Exponential Models From Percentage Decrease Descriptions

**Example:** A wildlife preserve had $1,200$ deer in $2020$. Starting in $2021$, the population is projected to decline by $5\%$ each year. Write the function that models this relationship, where $P(t)$ is the number of deer in the preserve $t$ years after $2020$.

**Explanation**

We're told that the wildlife preserve had $1,200$ deer in $2020$, and starting in $2021$, the population is projected to decline by $5\%$ each year.

The formula for exponential decay (in terms of a decay rate $r$) is

$$
P(t) = a(1-r)^t
$$

In our case, we have the following:

- $a=1,200$ is the initial population of deer.
- $r = 0.05$ is the decay rate ($5\%$ expressed as a decimal).
- $t$ is the time, in years, after $2020$.

Therefore, the correct expression for $P(t)$ is

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(t) &= 1, 200 \cdot (1 - 0.05)^{t} \\
&= 1, 200 \cdot (0.95)^{t}
\end{aligned}
$$

This function models the relationship where $P(t)$ is the number of deer in the preserve $t$ years after $2020$.

## Determining an Unknown Decay Rate for a Given Model

**Example:** A lab technician observes a population of $3,000$ mold spores in a sample. After $6$ hours, the number of mold spores has grown to $24,000$. Assuming exponential growth, the formula $N = A \cdot 2^{rt}$ models the number of mold spores, where $A$ and $r$ are constants and $N$ is the number of spores $t$ hours after the initial observation. What is the value of $r$?

**Explanation**

We are told that the number of mold spores follows the equation

$$
N = A \cdot 2^{rt}
$$

and that $3,000$ mold spores grow to $24,000$ after $6$ hours.

If we substitute these values into the formula, we get

$$
24,000 = 3,000 \cdot 2^{r \cdot 6}
$$

Dividing both sides by $3,000$, we obtain

$$
8 = 2^{6 \cdot r}
$$

Notice that $8=2^3$, so we can rewrite our equation as follows:

$$
2^3 = 2^{6 \cdot r}
$$

Since the bases are equal, the exponents must be equal. So, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
3 &= 6r \\
r &= \frac{1}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the value of $r$ is

$$
\dfrac{1}{2}
$$

## Constructing and Evaluating Exponential Decay Models

**Example:** A rocket is launched upward and after each stage separation it reaches $\dfrac{1}{2}$ the height it reached in the previous stage. The rocket initially reaches a height of $64.0 \, \text{m}$.

What is the maximum height the rocket reaches above the ground between the first and second stage separations?

**Explanation**

We're told that a rocket is launched upward to an initial height of $64.0\,\text{m}$. After each stage separation, it reaches

$$
\dfrac{1}{2}
$$

the height it reached on the previous stage.

This scenario represents exponential decay, where the height is multiplied by a factor of

$$
\dfrac{1}{2}
$$

after each stage. The general form for this type of relationship can be expressed as

$$
H(n) = a \cdot b^n
$$

where $H(n)$ is the height after $n$ stages, $a$ is the initial height, and $b$ is the decay factor.

In our case, we have the following:

- $a=64.0$ is the initial height of the rocket.
- $b = \dfrac{1}{2}$ is the decay factor.
- $n$ represents the number of stage separations.

So, the expression for $H(n)$ is

$$
\begin{aligned}
H(n) &= 64.0 \cdot (\frac{1}{2})^{n}
\end{aligned}
$$

We are looking for the height between the first and second stage separations. This corresponds to the height after the first separation. Hence, we need to calculate $H(1)$.

Substitute $n=1$ into the formula for $H(n)$, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
H(1) &= 64.0 \cdot (\frac{1}{2})^{1} \\
&= 64.0 \cdot \frac{1}{2} \\
&= \frac{64.0}{2} \\
&= 32.0
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the maximum height the rocket reaches above the ground between the first and second stage separations is $\bbox[4pt,border: 1px solid lightgray]{32.0}\,\text{m}$.

## Modelling With Vertically Translated Exponential Decay Functions

**Example:** In a science experiment, Priya heated a metal rod and then left it to cool in a room that maintained a constant temperature of $65^\circ \mathrm{F}$. At $3{:}00$ p.m., the rod’s temperature was $200^\circ \mathrm{F}$. After $5$ minutes, its temperature had dropped to $170^\circ \mathrm{F}$, and after $10$ minutes, it was $146.5^\circ \mathrm{F}$. The rod continued to cool over time.

Write the function that best models the temperature $T(t)$ in degrees Fahrenheit of the rod $t$ minutes after it was removed from the heat.

**Explanation**

The temperature $T(t)$ at time $t$ is modeled by the equation

$$
T(t) = a \cdot b^t + T_r
$$

where

- $T_r = 65^\circ \mathrm{F}$ is the room (ambient) temperature,
- $a$ and $b$ are constants to be determined, and
- $t$ is the time, in minutes.

At $t=0$, the temperature is $T(0) = 200$. Therefore, we can determine the value of $a{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
T(0) &= 200 \\
a \cdot b^{0} + 65 &= 200 \\
a \cdot 1 &= 135 \\
a &= 135
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus, our model becomes

$$
T(t) = 135 \cdot b^t + 65
$$

At $t=5$, the temperature is $T(5) = 170$. Hence, we can determine the value of $b{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
T(5) &= 170 \\
135 \cdot b^{5} + 65 &= 170 \\
135 \cdot b^{5} &= 105 \\
b^{5} &= \frac{105}{135} \\
b^{5} &= \frac{7}{9} \\
b &= (\frac{7}{9})^{1/5}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the function best models the temperature $T(t)$ is

$$
\begin{aligned}
T(t) &= 135 \cdot ((\frac{7}{9})^{1/5})^{t} + 65 \\
&= 135 \cdot (\frac{7}{9})^{t/5} + 65
\end{aligned}
$$
