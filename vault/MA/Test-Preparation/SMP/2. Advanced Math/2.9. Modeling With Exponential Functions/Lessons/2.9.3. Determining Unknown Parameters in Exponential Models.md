## Introduction

A common theme on the math section of the SAT is working with exponential models that contain unknown parameters, such as the growth rate or the initial value. In this topic, we'll practice solving for these unknowns given a description of a real-world situation.

## Constructing Exponential Models by Determining the Growth Rate

**Example:** An employee’s annual salary is $\[math]52,000$ when they start a new job. The salary is modeled by an exponential function $F$, where $F(t)$ represents the salary, in dollars, $t$ years after starting. After $7$ years, the salary grows to $\[/math]68,428.45$.

Rounding the base of the exponential function to two decimal places, write the equation that could define $F$.

**Explanation**

We are told that the salary starts at $\[math]52,000$ and grows to $\[/math]68,428.45$ after $7$ years.

The formula for exponential growth (in terms of a growth factor $b$) is

$$
F(t) = a b^t
$$

In our case, we have the following:

- $a=52,000$ is the initial salary.
- $t$ is the time, in years, after the job is started.
- After $t=7$ years, the salary is $F(7) = 68,428.45$.

Now, we proceed to substitute the previous values in the formula for $F(t){:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
F(7) &= 52, 000 \cdot b^{7} \\
68, 428.45 &= 52, 000 \cdot b^{7} \\
52, 000 \cdot b^{7} &= 68, 428.45 \\
b^{7} &= \frac{68, 428.45}{52, 000} \\
b^{7} &= 1.31593…
\end{aligned}
$$

To find the value of $b$, we take the $7$-th root of both sides. So, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
b &= \sqrt[7]{1.31593…} \\
&= 1.04000… \\
&\approx 1.04
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the equation that could define $F$ is

$$
F(t) = 52,000 \cdot (1.04)^t
$$

## Constructing Exponential Models by Determining the Initial Value

**Example:** A model estimates that at the end of each year from $2012$ to $2017$, the number of trees in a forest was $50\%$ more than the number of trees in the forest at the end of the previous year. The model estimates that at the end of $2013$, there were $300$ trees in the forest.

Write the equation that represents this model, where $f(t)$ is the estimated number of trees in the forest $t$ years after the end of $2012$ and $t \leq 5$.

**Explanation**

We're told that from $2012$ to $2017$, the number of trees in a forest increased by $50\%$ each year compared to the previous year. We also know that at the end of $2013$, there were $300$ trees in the forest.

The formula for exponential growth (in terms of a growth rate $r$) is

$$
f(t) = a(1+r)^t
$$

In this case, we have the following:

- $r = 0.5$ is the growth rate ($50\%$ expressed as a decimal).
- $t$ is the time, in years, after the end of $2012$.

Then, the expression for $f(t)$ is

$$
\begin{aligned}
f(t) &= a \cdot (1 + 0.5)^{t} \\
&= a \cdot (1.5)^{t}
\end{aligned}
$$

It only remains for us to determine the value of $a$.

We know that at the end of $2013$ there were $300$ trees. The end of $2013$ is $1$ year after the end of $2012$. So, when $t=1$, we have $f = 300$. Hence,

$$
\begin{aligned}
f(1) &= 300 \\
a \cdot (1.5)^{1} &= 300 \\
a \cdot 1.5 &= 300 \\
a &= \frac{300}{1.5} \\
a &= 200
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
f(t) = 200 \cdot (1.5)^t
$$
