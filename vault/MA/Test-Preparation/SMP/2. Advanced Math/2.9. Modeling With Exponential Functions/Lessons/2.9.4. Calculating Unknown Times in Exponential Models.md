## Introduction

After solving exponential models for the growth rate or the initial value, we'll now focus on solving for time. In particular, we'll practice calculating how long it takes for quantities to double or triple, identifying exponential equations given in context, and finding unknown times from tables or given models.

## Identifying the Time Needed for an Exponential Growth Quantity to Double or Triple

**Example:** The function $f(t) = 120,000 \cdot 2^{t/6}$ gives the number of followers on a social media page $t$ months after it was initially measured. How much time, in months, does it take for the number of followers to double?

**Explanation**

We are told that the number of followers is modeled by the function

$$
f(t) = 120,000 \cdot 2^{t/6}
$$

The exponent $t/6$ means that the number of followers is multiplied by $2$ when $t = 6$.

Therefore, the number of followers doubles every $\bbox[4pt,border: 1px solid lightgray]{6}$ months.

## Identifying an Exponential Growth Equation

**Example:** A university’s endowment currently has a value of $\[math]200,000$, and the endowment value is expected to increase by $6\%$ each year. Which of the following equations can be used to estimate the number of years, $t$, it will take for the endowment value to reach $\[/math]300,000$?

**Explanation**

We're told that a university’s endowment currently has a value of $\[math]200,000$, and the endowment value is expected to increase by $6\%$ each year.

The formula for exponential growth (in terms of a growth rate $r$) is

$$
E(t) = a(1+r)^t
$$

In this case, we have the following:

- $E$ is the endowment value.
- $a = 200,000$ dollars is the current value.
- $r = 0.06$ is the growth rate ($6\%$ expressed as a decimal).
- $t$ is the time, in years.

Then, the expression for $E(t)$ is

$$
\begin{aligned}
E(t) &= 200, 000 \cdot (1 + 0.06)^{t} \\
&= 200, 000 \cdot (1.06)^{t}
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we want to determine how many years it will take for the endowment value to reach $\[/math]300,000$. To represent this, we set up the equation

$$
300,000 = 200,000 \cdot (1.06)^t
$$

<a id="calculating-an-unknown-time-given-an-exponential-growth-model"></a>
## Calculating an Unknown Time Given an Exponential Growth Model

**Example:** A machine was purchased in $2015$ for $\[math]32{,}000$. Its value halves every $6$ years. In which year will the value reach $\[/math]2{,}000$?

**Explanation**

We're told that a machine was purchased in $2015$ for $\[math]32{,}000$, and its value halves every $6$ years. We want to find the year when the value will reach $\[/math]2{,}000$.

The formula for exponential decay, in terms of a halving period $d$, is

$$
y = a \cdot \left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^{t/d}
$$

In our case, we have the following:

- $a=32{,}000$ is the initial value in $2015$.
- The value halves every $6$ years. So, the halving period is $d=6$.
- $t$ is the time, in years, after $2015$.

Therefore, the expression for the value $y$ after $t$ years is

$$
y = 32{,}000 \cdot \left(\tfrac{1}{2}\right)^{t/6}
$$

Now, we need to find $t$ when

$$
y = 2{,}000
$$

Substituting $y = 2{,}000$ in the last equation and simplifying, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
2,000 &= 32,000 \cdot (\frac{1}{2})^{t/6} \\
\frac{2,000}{32,000} &= (\frac{1}{2})^{t/6} \\
\frac{1}{16} &= (\frac{1}{2})^{t/6}
\end{aligned}
$$

Notice that

$$
\dfrac{1}{16} = \left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^4
$$

Therefore,

$$
\left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^4 = \left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^{t/6}
$$

Equating the exponents, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
4 &= \frac{t}{6} \\
t &= 4 \cdot 6 \\
t &= 24
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, it will take $24$ years for the value to reach $\[math]2{,}000$.

Finally, to find the year, we add this to the initial year $2015{:}$

$$
2015 + 24 = 2039
$$

So, the value will reach $\[/math]2{,}000$ in the year ${2039}$.

## Calculating an Unknown Time Given a Table Corresponding to an Exponential Growth Model

**Example:** An online game’s player count is increasing at an exponential rate, as shown in the table above. At this rate, during which minute will the number of players reach $1.536 \cdot 10^{7}$?

| Minute | Online players |
| --- | ---: |
| $1$ | $7.5 \cdot 10^{3}$ |
| $2$ | $1.5 \cdot 10^{4}$ |
| $3$ | $3.0 \cdot 10^{4}$ |

**Explanation**

We are told that the number of players is growing exponentially. The formula for exponential growth is

$$
y(t) = a \cdot b^t
$$

From the table, we can note the following:

- At Minute $1$, there are $7.5 \cdot 10^3$ players.
- At Minute $2$, there are $1.5 \cdot 10^4$ players.
- At Minute $3$, there are $3.0 \cdot 10^4$ players.

Since the count doubles each minute (

$$
15000 \div 7500 = 2
$$

and

$$
30000 \div 15000 = 2
$$

), the growth factor is $b = 2$.

Because the count doubles per minute and the first data point is at Minute $1$, the initial count, $a$, at Minute $0$ was

$$
\begin{aligned}
a &= (7.5 \cdot 10^{3}) \div 2 \\
&= 3.75 \cdot 10^{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the correct expression for $y(t)$ is

$$
y(t) = (3.75 \cdot 10^3) \cdot 2^t
$$

Now, we want to find the value of $t$ such that

$$
y(t) = 1.536 \cdot 10^{7}
$$

that is,

$$
(3.75 \cdot 10^3) \cdot 2^t = 1.536 \cdot 10^{7}
$$

Solving the above equation for $t$, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
(3.75 \cdot 10^{3}) \cdot 2^{t} &= 1.536 \cdot 10^{7} \\
2^{t} &= \frac{1.536 \cdot 10^{7}}{3.75 \cdot 10^{3}} \\
2^{t} &= 4.096 \cdot 10^{3} \\
2^{t} &= 4096 \\
2^{t} &= 2^{12} \\
t &= 12
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the number of players will reach $1.536 \times 10^{7}$ during the $12$-th minute.
