## Evaluating Quadratic Functions

**Example:** The function $f$ is defined by $f(x) = x^{2} + x + 71$. What is the value of $f(2)$?

**Explanation**

To find the value of $f(2)$, we substitute $x=2$ into the function's definition and evaluate:

$$
\begin{aligned}
f(x) &= x^{2} + x + 71 \\
f(2) &= (2)^{2} + (2) + 71 \\
&= 4 + 2 + 71 \\
&= 77
\end{aligned}
$$

## Identifying a Table of Values for a Given Quadratic Function

**Example:** The function $z$ is defined by $z(x)=x^{2}+5$. Complete the following table for the values of $x$ and their corresponding values of $z(x)$?

| $x$ | $z(x)$ |
| --- | ---: |
| $1$ |  |
| $4$ |  |
| $5$ |  |

**Explanation**

To determine the correct table, we calculate $z(x)$ for each of the given values of $x$.

- For $x=1$, we get
$z(1)|= 1^{2} + 5; = 1 + 5; = 6$.
- For $x=4$, we get
$z(4)|= 4^{2} + 5; = 16 + 5; = 21$.
- For $x=5$, we get
$z(5)|= 5^{2} + 5; = 25 + 5; = 30$.

Therefore, the correct table is the following.

| $x$ | $z(x)$ |
| --- | ---: |
| $1$ | $6$ |
| $4$ | $21$ |
| $5$ | $30$ |

## Finding the Slope of a Secant Line

**Example:** In the $xy$-plane, the graph of $y = x^{2} - x + 1$ intersects line $q$ at $(-1, e)$ and $(3, f)$, where $e$ and $f$ are constants. What is the slope of line $q$?

**Explanation**

We are given that line $q$ intersects the graph of

$$
y = x^2 - x + 1
$$

at two points: $(-1, e)$ and $(3, f)$. Since these points lie on the graph, we can find $e$ and $f$ by substituting the $x$-values into the quadratic.

We have

$$
e = (-1)^2 - (-1) + 1 = 1 + 1 + 1 = 3
$$

and

$$
f = (3)^2 - 3 + 1 = 9 - 3 + 1 = 7
$$

The slope $m$ of a line passing through two points $(x_1, y_1)$ and $(x_2, y_2)$ is given by

$$
m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}
$$

Hence, the slope of line $q$ is

$$
\begin{aligned}
m &= (f - e)/(3 - (-1)) \\
&= \frac{7 - 3}{4} \\
&= \frac{4}{4} \\
&= 1
\end{aligned}
$$

## Determining Unknown Parameters Given a Root of a Quadratic Function

**Example:** Let $r(x) = (y - x)(x - 4)$, where $y$ is a constant. In the $xy$-plane, the graph of $y = r(x)$ passes through the point $(2, 0)$. What is the value of $r(0)$?

**Explanation**

Since the graph of $y = r(x)$ passes through the point $(2, 0)$, we have
$r(2) = 0$.

From the function expression, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
r(2) &= (y - 2)(2 - 4) \\
&= (y - 2)(-2) \\
&=-2y + 4
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we equate both expressions for $r(2)$ and solve for $y{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
-2y + 4 &= 0 \\
- 2y &= -4 \\
y &= 2
\end{aligned}
$$

So, our function can be written as

$$
r(x) = (2 - x)(x - 4)
$$

Therefore, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
r(0) &= (2 - 0)(0 - 4) \\
&= 2(-4) \\
&=-8
\end{aligned}
$$

## Determining Unknown Parameters in Quadratic Functions Given Other Information

**Example:** Let $g$ be the function defined by
$g(w) = \dfrac{(w - d)^2 + 72}{4d}$,
where $d$ is a constant. If $g(d) = 9$, what is the value of $g(15)$?

**Explanation**

We are told that

$$
g(w) = \dfrac{(w - d)^2 + 72}{4d}
$$

and that $g(d) = 9$.

First, we substitute $w = d$ into the function:

$$
\begin{aligned}
g(d) &= ((d - d)^{2} + 72)/(4d) \\
&= \frac{0 + 72}{4d} \\
&= \frac{72}{4d}
\end{aligned}
$$

We're told that this equals $9$. Therefore, we can find the value of $d$, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{72}{4d} &= 9 \\
72 &= 36d \\
d &= \frac{72}{36} \\
d &= 2
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we substitute $d = 2$ into the original function to calculate $g(15){:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
g(15) &= ((15 - 2)^{2} + 72)/(4 \cdot 2) \\
&= \frac{169 + 72}{8} \\
&= \frac{241}{8}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the value of $g(15)$ is

$$
\dfrac{241}{8}
$$
