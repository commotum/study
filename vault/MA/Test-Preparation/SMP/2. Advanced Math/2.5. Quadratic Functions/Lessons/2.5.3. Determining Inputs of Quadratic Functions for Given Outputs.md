## Determining an Input of a Quadratic Given Its Output

**Example:** The function $r$ is defined by
$r(x) = x^2 + 17$.
For which value of $x$ is $r(x) = 81$?

**Explanation**

We start by setting up the equation and then solve for $x{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
r(x) &= x^{2} + 17 \\
81 &= x^{2} + 17 \\
x^{2} &= 81 - 17 \\
x^{2} &= 64 \\
x &= ± \sqrt{64} \\
x &= ± 8
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, among the given options, the correct answer is $8$.

## Determining the Roots of a Quadratic Function in Vertex Form

**Example:** The quadratic function $s$ is defined by
$s(x) = -4(x - 6)^2 + 100$.
In the $xy$-plane, the graph of $y = s(x)$ intersects the $x$-axis at the points $(1, 0)$ and $(n, 0)$, where $n$ is a constant. What is the value of $n$?

**Explanation**

Since the function intersects the $x$-axis at $(n, 0)$, we substitute it into the given function and solve for $n{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
s(x) &= -4(x - 6)^{2} + 100 \\
0 &= -4(n - 6)^{2} + 100 \\
4(n - 6)^{2} &= 100 \\
(n - 6)^{2} &= \frac{100}{4} \\
(n - 6)^{2} &= 25 \\
n - 6 &= ± 5
\end{aligned}
$$

First, the negative root gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
n - 6 &= -5 \\
n &= -5 + 6 \\
n &= 1
\end{aligned}
$$

This implies that the graph intersects the $x$-axis at the point $(1, 0)$, which is already given.

Therefore, the positive root gives the $x$-coordinate of the other point, which is

$$
\begin{aligned}
n - 6 &= 5 \\
n &= 6 + 5 \\
n &= 11
\end{aligned}
$$
