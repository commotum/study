## Introduction

Problems involving extreme values of quadratic functions often test whether we can quickly identify the vertex of a parabola from its factored form.

Here, we’ll practice efficiently locating maximums and minimums of quadratic functions presented in factored form.

## Finding the X-Value of an Extremum Given a Factored Quadratic Function

**Example:** The function $n$ is defined by
$n(x) = -(x - 2)(x + 8)$.
For what value of $x$ does $n(x)$ reach its maximum?

**Explanation**

The given function is a quadratic in factored form. Expanding the expression results in a negative $x^2$ term, the parabola opens downward and reaches a maximum at its vertex.

The vertex (maximum) is exactly midway between the roots. To find the roots, we set $n(x) = 0$ and solve for $x{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
n(x) &= - (x - 2)(x + 8) \\
0 &= - (x - 2)(x + 8)
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus, the roots are

$$
x_1 = 2 \quad \text{and} \quad x_2 = -8
$$

So the $x$-coordinate of the vertex is the midpoint of $x_1$ and $x_2$, giving

$$
\begin{aligned}
x &= \frac{x_{1} + x_{2}}{2} \\
&= (2 + (-8))/(2) \\
&= \frac{-6}{2} \\
&=-3
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, $n(x)$ reaches its maximum at $x = -3$.

## Finding an Interval Containing the Extremum of a Factored Quadratic Function

**Example:** The function $t$ is defined by the equation
$t(x) = (-x - 5)(x + 3)$.
The graph of $t$ in the $xy$-plane is a parabola. Which of the following intervals contains the $x$-coordinate of the vertex of the graph of $t$?

1. $-5 < x < -3$
2. $-2 < x < 0$
3. $2 < x < 3$
4. $3 < x < 4$

**Explanation**

The given function is a quadratic in factored form. Since the coefficients of $x$ multiply to give a negative $x^2$ term, the parabola opens downward.

The vertex is exactly midway between the roots. To find the roots, we set $t(x) = 0$ and solve for $x{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
t(x) &= (-x - 5)(x + 3) \\
0 &= (-x - 5)(x + 3)
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus, the roots are

$$
x_1 = -5 \quad \text{and} \quad x_2 = -3
$$

So the $x$-coordinate of the vertex is the midpoint of $x_1$ and $x_2$, giving

$$
\begin{aligned}
x &= \frac{x_{1} + x_{2}}{2} \\
&= (-5 + (-3))/(2) \\
&= \frac{-8}{2} \\
&=-4
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the correct interval that contains this vertex is
$-5 < x < -3$.
