## Finding the Y-Intercept of a Quadratic Function

**Example:** The $y$-intercept of the graph of $y = 7 - 4x^{2}$ in the $xy$-plane is $(0, y)$. What is the value of $y$?

**Explanation**

To find the value of the $y$-coordinate of the $y$-intercept, we substitute $x = 0$ into the equation of the graph and solve for $y$.

Therefore, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
y &= 7 - 4x^{2} \\
&= 7 - 4(0)^{2} \\
&= 7
\end{aligned}
$$

$$

$$

## Finding Roots of Quadratic Functions in Standard Form

**Example:** The function $g(x) = x^2 - 8x - 20$ is graphed in the $xy$-plane. What are the $x$-intercepts of $g(x)$?

**Explanation**

To find the $x$-intercepts of the function

$$
g(x) = x^2 - 8x - 20
$$

, we set $g(x) = 0$ and factor:

$$
x^2 - 8x - 20 = 0
$$

$$
(x - 10)(x + 2) = 0
$$

This equation is satisfied when either factor is zero:

$$
x - 10 = 0 \quad \Rightarrow \quad x = 10
$$

$$
\text{or}
$$

$$
x + 2 = 0 \quad \Rightarrow \quad x = -2
$$

Therefore, the $x$-intercepts are the points $(10, 0)$ and $(-2, 0)$.

## Identifying the Linear and Constant Terms Given a Parabola's Graph

**Example:** The graph of $y = 2x^{2} + bx + c$ is shown, where $b$ and $c$ are constants. What is the value of $bc$?

![](<../Source/2.5.2. Graphs of Quadratic Functions/Images/858ea15fce4a03747ee42be84418fa82.png>)

**Explanation**

First, we find the $y$-intercept, which is the value of $y$ at $x = 0$. From the graph, this is $y = -4$. Substituting into the equation above, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
y &= 2x^{2} + bx + c \\
- 4 &= 2(0)^{2} + b(0) + c \\
c &= -4
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, since we know $c = -4$, we substitute one of the $x$-intercept points, say $(1, 0)$, and solve for $b{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
y &= 2x^{2} + bx - 4 \\
0 &= 2(1)^{2} + b(1) - 4 \\
0 &= 2 + b - 4 \\
0 &= b - 2 \\
b &= 2
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
bc &= (2)(-4) \\
&=-8
\end{aligned}
$$
