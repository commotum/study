## Introduction

Questions involving tables of values often test whether we can recover a quadratic pattern from data.

In this lesson, we’ll practice interpreting tabular data to determine equations of quadratic functions and their key features.

## Identifying Quadratic Functions Given Symmetric Data

**Example:** The table shows three values of $x$ and their corresponding values of $y$, where $y = p(x) + 9$ and $p$ is a quadratic function. What is the $y$-coordinate of the $y$-intercept of the graph of $y = p(x)$ in the $xy$-plane?

| $x$ | $y$ |
| --- | ---: |
| $-8$ | $6$ |
| $-5$ | $-3$ |
| $-2$ | $6$ |

$XXXXX$

**Explanation**

We are given that

$$
y = p(x) + 9
$$

so to find the values of $p(x)$, we subtract $9$ from each $y$-value:

$$
\begin{aligned}
p(-8) &= 6 - 9 =-3 \\
p(-5) &= -3 - 9 =-12 \\
p(-2) &= 6 - 9 =-3
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we observe that

$$
p(-8) = p(-2) = -3
$$

Since these values are equal and symmetric, the axis of symmetry must lie halfway between $x = -8$ and $x = -2$. Hence, the axis of symmetry is

$$
x = \dfrac{-8 + (-2)}{2} = -5
$$

So, the vertex occurs at $x = -5$, and since

$$
p(-5) = -12
$$

the vertex is the point $(-5,-12)$.

Thus, the equation of the parabola in vertex form is given by the following equation:

$$
p(x) = a(x+5)^2 - 12
$$

We can compute $a$ by substituting any of the obtained function values into the equation above. Let's take the value $p(-8)=-3{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
p(x) &= a(x + 5)^{2} - 12 \\
- 3 &= a(-8 + 5)^{2} - 12 \\
- 3 &= a(-3)^{2} - 12 \\
- 3 &= 9a - 12 \\
9a &= 9 \\
a &= 1
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the equation of the parabola $y=p(x)$ is

$$
p(x) = (x+5)^2 - 12
$$

To find the $y$-intercept of the graph of $y = p(x)$, we need to compute $p(0){:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
p(0) &= (0 + 5)^{2} - 12 \\
&= 25 - 12 \\
&= 13
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the $y$-intercept is $\bbox[3pt,border:1px solid lightgray]{13}$.

## Identifying Quadratic Functions Given Non-Symmetric Data

**Example:** For the quadratic function $g$, the table shows three values of $x$ and their corresponding values of $g(x)$. Which equation defines $g$?

| $x$ | $g(x)$ |
| --- | ---: |
| $-2$ | $0$ |
| $0$ | $2$ |
| $1$ | $-3$ |

**Explanation**

We are told that $g$ is a quadratic function, so the relationship between $x$ and $g$ must have the form

$$
g(x) = ax^2 + bx + c
$$

We will use the values from the table to write a system of equations for the parameters $a$, $b$, and $c$.

- From $x = -2$, we have
$g(-2)|= 0; a(-2)^{2} + b(-2) + c|= 0; 4a - 2b + c|= 0$.
- From $x = 1$, we have
$g(1)|=-3; a(1)^{2} + b(1) + c|=-3; a + b + c|=-3$.
- From $x = 0$, we have
$g(0)|= 2; a(0)^{2} + b(0) + c|= 2; c|= 2$.

Now, we substitute $c = 2$ into the other two equations.

- From $4a - 2b + c = 0$, we have
$4a - 2b + 2|= 0; 4a - 2b|=-2; 2a - b|=-1$.
- From $a + b + c = -3$, we have
$a + b + 2|=-3; a + b|=-5$.

So, we get the following system of equations:

$$
\begin{aligned}
2a - b &= -1 \\
a + b &= -5
\end{aligned}
$$

Adding the two equations gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
3a &= -6 \\
a &= -2
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we substitute the value $a=-2$ into

$$
a + b = -5{:}
$$

$$
-2 + b = -5\quad\Rightarrow\quad b = -3
$$

Finally, collecting $a=-2$, $b=-3$, and $c=2$, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
g(x) &= ax^{2} + bx + c \\
&=-2x^{2} - 3x + 2
\end{aligned}
$$
