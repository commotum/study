## Introduction

Problems involving the symmetry of quadratic functions often test how well we can reason about a parabola without graphing it.

In this lesson, we’ll practice using these patterns to draw logical conclusions about a quadratic function’s structure and behavior.

## Identifying Possible Ranges for Parabola Coefficients

**Example:** Consider quadratic function $g(x) = ax^{2} - 12x + c$, where $a$ and $c$ are constants. The graph of $y = g(x)$ in the $xy$-plane is a parabola that opens upward and has a vertex at the point $(h, k)$, where $h$ and $k$ are constants. If $k < 0$ and $g(3) = g(9)$, which of the following **MUST** be true?

1. $c < 0$
2. $a \gt 0$
3. $c > 0$

**Explanation**

First, recall that the parabola is symmetric about the vertical line passing through the vertex. Because $g(3) = g(9)$, the vertex is midway between $x_1 = 3$ and $x_2 = 9$.

So, the $x$-coordinate of the vertex is

$$
\begin{aligned}
h &= \frac{x_{1} + x_{2}}{2} \\
&= \frac{3 + 9}{2} \\
&= 6
\end{aligned}
$$

Since at the vertex, $g(h) = k$, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
g(6) &= a(6)^{2} - 12(6) + c \\
k &= 36a - 72 + c
\end{aligned}
$$

Given that the parabola has vertex below the $x$-axis, $k < 0$, we have
$\begin{bmatrix}36a - 72 + c & < 0 \\ 36a + c & < 72\end{bmatrix}$.

Now, we check each statement:

- Statement I is false because $c$ does not always have to be negative. For example, if the parabola opens upward with $a = 1 > 0$, we get
$\begin{bmatrix}36(1) + c & < 72 \\ c & < 36\end{bmatrix}$,
which could be positive.
- Statement II is true. Since the parabola opens upward, we must have $a > 0$.
- Statement III is false because $c$ does not always have to be positive. For example, if the parabola opens upward with $a = 3 > 0$, we get
$\begin{bmatrix}36(3) + c & < 72 \\ c & < - 36\end{bmatrix}$,
which is negative.

Therefore, the correct answer is "II only".

## Using Vertex Form to Determine a Sum of Parabola Coefficients

**Example:** $y = ax^2 + bx + c$,
In the $xy$-plane, a parabola has the vertex $(0, 3)$. If the equation of the parabola can be written in the standard form above, where $a, b$, and $c$ are constants, find the sum $a + b + c$ in terms of the parameter $a$.

**Explanation**

We know the vertex is at

$$
(h, k) = (0, 3)
$$

So, we can write the parabola in vertex form:

$$
\begin{aligned}
y &= a(x - h)^{2} + k \\
y &= a(x - 0)^{2} + 3
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we simplify:

$$
\begin{aligned}
y &= a(x - 0)^{2} + 3 \\
&= a(x^{2}) + 3 \\
&= ax^{2} + 3
\end{aligned}
$$

Comparing it with the given standard quadratic form,

$$
y = ax^2 + bx + c
$$

we get the following:

$$
\begin{aligned}
a &= a \\
b &= 0 \\
c &= 3
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, adding them up, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
a + b + c &= a + 0 + 3 \\
&= a + 3
\end{aligned}
$$

## Identifying True Statements Regarding Parabola Coefficients

**Example:** In the $xy$-plane, a parabola has vertex $(4, -8)$ and intersects the $x$-axis at two points. If the equation of the parabola is written in the form
$y = ax^2 + bx + c$,
where $a, b$, and $c$ are constants, which of the following could be the value of $a + b + c$?

1. $-8$
2. $-1$
3. $10$

**Explanation**

We know the vertex is at

$$
(h, k) = (4, -8)
$$

So, we can write the parabola in vertex form:

$$
\begin{aligned}
y &= a(x - h)^{2} + k \\
y &= a(x - 4)^{2} - 8
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we expand:

$$
\begin{aligned}
y &= a(x - 4)^{2} - 8 \\
&= a(x^{2} - 8x + 16) - 8 \\
&= ax^{2} - 8ax + 16a - 8
\end{aligned}
$$

Comparing it with the given standard quadratic form,

$$
y = ax^2 + bx + c
$$

we get the following:

$$
\begin{aligned}
a &= a \\
b &= -8a \\
c &= 16a - 8
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus, adding them up, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
a + b + c &= a + (-8a) + (16a - 8) \\
&= 9a - 8
\end{aligned}
$$

Since the parabola intersects the $x$-axis at two points and has its vertex at $(4, -8)$ below the $x$-axis, it must open upward, meaning $a > 0$.

Now, let's go through all the options and check which ones give $a > 0{:}$

- Option I, $-8{:}$
$9a - 8|=-8; 9a|=-8 + 8; 9a|= 0; a|= 0≯0\cdot$
- Option II, $-1{:}$
$9a - 8|=-1; 9a|=-1 + 8; 9a|= 7; a|= \frac{7}{9} > 0✓$
- Option III, $10{:}$
$9a - 8|= 10; 9a|= 10 + 8; 9a|= 18; a|= 2 > 0✓$

Therefore, the correct answer is "II and III only".
