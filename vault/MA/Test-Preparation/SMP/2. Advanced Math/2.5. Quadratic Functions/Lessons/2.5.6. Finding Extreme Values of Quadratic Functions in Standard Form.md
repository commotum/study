## Finding an Extremum of a Simple Quadratic Function

**Example:** The equation
$z(x) + 3 = x^{2}$
relates the value of $x$ and its corresponding value of $z(x)$ for the function $z$. What is the minimum value of the function $z$?

**Explanation**

We start by isolating $z(x){:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
z(x) + 3 &= x^{2} \\
z(x) &= x^{2} - 3
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, this equation is a quadratic function of the form:

$$
z(x) = ax^2 + bx + c
$$

where $a = 1$, $b = 0$, and $c = -3$.

Because $a > 0$, the parabola opens upward, so it has a minimum at its vertex, whose $x$-coordinate is

$$
\begin{aligned}
x &= -\frac{b}{2a} \\
&= - (0)/(2(1)) \\
&= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, substituting this value into the given function, we find that its minimum value is

$$
\begin{aligned}
z(0) &= (0)^{2} - 3 \\
&= 3
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding the X-Value of a Quadratic Function's Extremum

**Example:** The equation $y = -x^{2} + 10x - 9$ relates the variables $x$ and $y$. For what value of $x$ does the value of $y$ reach its maximum?

**Explanation**

The given equation is a quadratic function of the form:

$$
y = ax^2 + bx + c
$$

where $a = -1$, $b = 10$, and $c = -9$.

Therefore, since $a < 0$, the parabola opens downward and has a maximum at its vertex, whose $x$-coordinate is

$$
\begin{aligned}
x &= -\frac{b}{2a} \\
&= - (10)/(2(-1)) \\
&= \frac{-10}{-2} \\
&= 5
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding the Y-Value of a Quadratic Function's Extremum

**Example:** The function $k$ is defined by $k(x) = -2x^{2} + 24x - 60$. What is the maximum value of the given function?

**Explanation**

The given equation is a quadratic function of the form:

$$
y = ax^2 + bx + c
$$

where $a = -2$, $b = 24$, and $c = -60$.

Because $a < 0$, the parabola opens downward, so it has a maximum at its vertex, whose $x$-coordinate is

$$
\begin{aligned}
x &= -\frac{b}{2a} \\
&= - (24)/(2(-2)) \\
&= - (24)/((-4)) \\
&= 6
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, substituting this value back, we find the maximum value of the function is

$$
\begin{aligned}
k(6) &= -2(6)^{2} + 24(6) - 60 \\
&=-72 + 144 - 60 \\
&= (-72 + 144) - 60 \\
&= 72 - 60 \\
&= 12
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding an Unknown Parameter

**Example:** For the function $g(x)=3x^2+bx+3$, if $g(2)$ is a minimum, what is $b$?

**Explanation**

The $x$-coordinate of the minimum point is given by

$$
x = -\dfrac{b}{2a}
$$

For the function $g$, we have $a = 3$. Also, since $g(2)$ is the minimum, we have the equation

$$
\begin{aligned}
2 &= -\frac{b}{2 \cdot 3} \\
2 &= -\frac{b}{6}
\end{aligned}
$$

Solving this equation gives $b = -12$.
