## Introduction

Questions about quadratic functions in vertex form often focus on identifying or interpreting the maximum or minimum value of a parabola quickly.

Below, we’ll practice reading vertex form directly and using it to determine both the location and the value of a parabola’s highest or lowest point.

## Finding the X-Value of an Extremum Given a Quadratic Function in Vertex Form

**Example:** The function $t$ is defined by the equation
$t(x) = -6(x - 6)^2$.
For what value of $x$ does $t(x)$ reach its maximum?

**Explanation**

We are given a quadratic function that describes $t(x)$, and we want to find its maximum. The function is written in vertex form:

$$
t(x) = -6(x - 6)^2 + 0
$$

This is of the form

$$
t(x) = a(x - h)^{2} + k
$$

where

$$
a = -6, \quad h = 6, \quad k = 0
$$

Since the coefficient $a = -6$ is negative, the parabola opens downward, and the vertex represents the maximum value.

Therefore, the value of $x$ at which the function $t$ reaches its maximum is

$$
\begin{aligned}
x &= h \\
&= 6
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding the Y-Value of an Extremum Given a Quadratic Function in Vertex Form

**Example:** What is the maximum value of the function $j$ defined by
$j(x) = -2(x + 3)^2 - 5$?

**Explanation**

The function is a quadratic function with vertex at $(h, k)$, written in vertex form:

$$
j(x) = a(x - h)^{2} + k
$$

Here, we have

$$
a = -2, \quad h = -3, \quad k = -5
$$

Since the coefficient $a = -2$ is negative, the parabola opens downward, and the vertex represents the maximum value.

Therefore, the maximum value of the function $j$ is

$$
\begin{aligned}
j(h) &= k \\
&=-5
\end{aligned}
$$
