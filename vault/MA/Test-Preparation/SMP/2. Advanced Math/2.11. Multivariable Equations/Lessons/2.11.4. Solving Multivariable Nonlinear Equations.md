## Introduction

This topic focuses on solving nonlinear equations that involve multiple variables. We'll practice isolating variables in radical and quadratic equations, rearranging formulas, and applying the laws of exponents when working with radicals.

<a id="isolating-variables-in-multivariable-radical-equations"></a>
## Isolating Variables in Multivariable Radical Equations

**Example:** The equation below relates the distinct positive real numbers $a$, $b$, and $z{:}$
$\dfrac{36a}{6b} = 12\sqrt{z + 10}$.
Express $z$ in terms of $a$ and $b$.

**Explanation**

First, notice that we can simplify the fraction left-hand side since $36$ and $6$ have a common factor of $6{:}$

$$
\dfrac{36a}{6b} = \dfrac{6a}{b}
$$

So, the equation becomes

$$
\dfrac{6a}{b} = 12\sqrt{z + 10}
$$

To express our equation in terms of $z$, we first isolate the square root by dividing both sides by $12{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{6a}{b \cdot 12} &= \frac{12\sqrt{z + 10}}{12} \\
\frac{6a}{b \cdot 12} &= \frac{12\sqrt{z + 10}}{12} \\
\frac{a}{2b} &= \sqrt{z + 10}
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we square both sides:

$$
\left(\dfrac{a}{2b}\right)^2 = z + 10
$$

Finally, we solve for $z$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(\frac{a}{2b})^{2} &= z + 10 \\
(\frac{a}{2b})^{2} - 10 &= z + 10 - 10 \\
(\frac{a}{2b})^{2} - 10 &= z
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the correct answer is

$$
z = \left(\dfrac{a}{2b}\right)^2 - 10
$$

## Isolating Variables in Multivariable Quadratic Equations

**Example:** The equation
$n = 2(b - 5)^2$
relates the positive numbers $n$ and $b$. Express $b$ in terms of $n$, where $b > 5$.

**Explanation**

First, we isolate $(b - 5)^2$ by dividing both sides by $2{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
n &= 2(b - 5)^{2} \\
\frac{n}{2} &= (2(b - 5)^{2})/(2) \\
\frac{n}{2} &= (b - 5)^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we solve for $(b-5)$ by finding the square root of both sides. Notice that since $b > 5$, we have that $(b-5)>0$. So, we consider only the positive square root:

$$
\begin{aligned}
b - 5 &= \sqrt{\frac{n}{2}}
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we solve for $b$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
b &= \sqrt{\frac{n}{2}} + 5
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the correct answer is

$$
b = \sqrt{\dfrac{n}{2}} + 5
$$

## Isolating Variables in Formulas

**Example:** The volume of the container shown is given by the formula
$V = \frac{13\pi k^3}{6}$.

![](<../Source/2.11.4. Solving Multivariable Nonlinear Equations/Images/369ecd73296bb54309890a5df5d7d2ca.png>)

If the container holds $500$ cubic centimeters when completely filled, what is the value of $k$ in centimeters? Round your answer to two decimal places.

**Explanation**

We are given the volume formula

$$
V = \frac{13\pi k^3}{6}
$$

and we are told that $V = 500$.

Now, we substitute this value into the formula and solve for $k{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
500 &= \frac{13πk^{3}}{6} \\
\frac{500 \cdot 6}{13π} &= k^{3} \\
\frac{3000}{13π} &= k^{3} \\
k &= \sqrt[3]{\frac{3000}{13π}} \\
&= k \mid \approx 6.83
\end{aligned}
$$

rounded to $2$ decimal places.

So, the value of $k$ is closest to $6.83$ centimeters.

## Isolating Variables in Multivariable Radical Equations Using the Laws of Exponents

**Example:** Consider the equation
$\sqrt[6]{x^7}=\sqrt[5]{y^3}$,
where $x$, and $y$ are all positive numbers. Express $x$ in terms of $y$.

**Explanation**

Let's recall the rules of exponents:

- Negative exponents: $x^{-n} = \dfrac{1}{x^n}$
- Radicals as fractional exponents: $\sqrt[n]{x} = x^{1/n}$
- The product rule: $a^n \cdot a^m = a^{n+m}$
- The quotient rule: $\dfrac{a^n}{a^m} = a^{n-m}$
- The power rule: $\left(a^m \right)^n = a^{mn}$
- The power of product rule: $(ab)^n = a^nb^n$
- The power of quotient rule: $\left(\dfrac a b \right)^n = \dfrac{a^n}{b^n}$

First, let us convert our radicals to fractional exponents. Our equation becomes

$$
x^{7/6}=y^{3/5}
$$

Now, since we would like to isolate $x$, we take both sides to the power of

$$
\dfrac{6}{7}
$$

and apply the power rule:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^{7/6} &= y^{3/5} \\
(x^{7/6})^{6/7} &= (y^{3/5})^{6/7} \\
x^{(7/6) \cdot (6/7)} &= y^{(3/5) \cdot (6/7)} \\
x &= y^{18/35}
\end{aligned}
$$
