## Introduction

In this topic, we'll strengthen our ability to solve multivariable rational equations. We will use cross-multiplication, isolate variables appearing in numerators and denominators, and handle equations containing several rational terms.

<a id="solving-rational-equations-using-cross-multiplication"></a>
## Solving Rational Equations Using Cross-Multiplication

**Example:** The following equation relates the positive numbers $n$, $f$, and $t{:}$
$\dfrac{1}{9n} = \dfrac{2f}{t}$.
Express $f$ in terms of $t$ and $n$.

**Explanation**

We are given the equation

$$
\dfrac{1}{9n} = \dfrac{2f}{t}
$$

and asked to solve for $f$ in terms of $t$ and $n$.

To eliminate the denominators, we use *cross multiplication*:

$$
\begin{aligned}
1 \cdot t &= 2f \cdot 9n \\
t &= 18fn
\end{aligned}
$$

Now divide *both sides* by $18n$ to isolate $f{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{t}{18n} &= \frac{18fn}{18n} \\
\frac{t}{18n} &= f
\end{aligned}
$$

Rewriting with $f$ on the left-hand side, we get the correct answer:

$$
f = \dfrac{t}{18n}
$$

## Solving Rational Equations When the Required Variable Is in the Denominator

**Example:** The following equation relates the numbers $g$ and $w$, where $w\neq 0$ and $g>-12{:}$
$g = -12 + \dfrac{48}{w}$.
Express $w$ in terms of $g$.

**Explanation**

We are given the equation

$$
g = -12 + \dfrac{48}{w}
$$

and asked to solve for $w$ in terms of $g$.

First, we add $12$ to *both sides*:

$$
\begin{aligned}
g + 12 &= -12 + \frac{48}{w} + 12 \\
g + 12 &= \frac{48}{w}
\end{aligned}
$$

Now we multiply *both sides* of the equation by $w$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(g + 12) \cdot w &= \frac{48}{w} \cdot w \\
w(g + 12) &= 48
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we divide *both sides* by $g + 12$ to isolate $w{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
(w(g + 12))/(g + 12) &= \frac{48}{g + 12} \\
w &= \frac{48}{g + 12}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the correct answer is

$$
w = \dfrac{48}{g + 12}
$$

## Solving Rational Equations When the Required Variable Is in the Numerator and Denominator

**Example:** The variables $p$ and $q$ are related by the equation
$p = \dfrac{7q - 4}{q}$.
Express $q$ in terms of $p$.

**Explanation**

Multiplying both sides of the given equation by $q$ gives

$$
pq = 7q - 4
$$

Subtracting $7q$ from both sides, we get

$$
pq - 7q = -4
$$

Factoring out $q$ on the left-hand side, we get

$$
q(p - 7) = -4
$$

Finally, dividing both sides by $p - 7$ yields

$$
q = \dfrac{-4}{p - 7}
$$

So, the correct answer is

$$
q = \dfrac{-4}{p - 7}
$$

## Solving Rational Equations Containing Multiple Terms

**Example:** The following equation relates the positive variables $x$, $y$, $z$, and $w{:}$
$\dfrac{24}{x} = \dfrac{24}{y} + \dfrac{24}{z} - \dfrac{24}{w}$

Express $y$ in terms of $x$, $z$, and $w$.

**Explanation**

Question (MC, H)

Stem:

Explanation:

We are given the equation

$$
\dfrac{24}{x} = \dfrac{24}{y} + \dfrac{24}{z} - \dfrac{24}{w}
$$

First, we factor out the common factor of $24$ on the right-hand side:

$$
\dfrac{24}{x} = 24\left(\dfrac{1}{y} + \dfrac{1}{z} - \dfrac{1}{w}\right)
$$

Then, we divide both sides by $24$:

$$
\dfrac{1}{x} = \dfrac{1}{y} + \dfrac{1}{z} - \dfrac{1}{w}
$$

To isolate

$$
\dfrac{1}{y}
$$

we subtract

$$
\dfrac{1}{z}
$$

and add

$$
\dfrac{1}{w}
$$

from both sides:

$$
\dfrac{1}{y} = \dfrac{1}{x} - \dfrac{1}{z} + \dfrac{1}{w}
$$

We now combine the terms on the right-hand side using a common denominator of $xzw$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{1}{y} &= \frac{1}{x} - \frac{1}{z} + \frac{1}{w} \\
&= \frac{zw}{zw} \cdot \frac{1}{x} - \frac{xw}{xw} \cdot \frac{1}{z} + \frac{xz}{xz} \cdot \frac{1}{w} \\
&= \frac{zw - xw + xz}{xzw}
\end{aligned}
$$

Taking the reciprocal of both sides gives the desired relation:

$$
y = \dfrac{xzw}{zw - xw + xz}
$$
