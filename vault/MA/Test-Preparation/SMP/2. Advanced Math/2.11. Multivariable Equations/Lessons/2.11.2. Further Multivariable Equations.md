## Solving Multivariable Equations in Two Steps (Subtraction First)

**Example:** The cost $C$ of a certain taxi ride, in dollars, can be modeled by the formula $C = 3.6 + 2.4m$, where $m$ is the distance in miles. Express $m$ in terms of $C$.

**Explanation**

We are given the equation

$$
C = 3.6 + 2.4m
$$

and asked to solve for $m$ in terms of $C$.

First, we subtract *$3.6$ from both sides*:

$$
\begin{aligned}
C - 3.6 &= 3.6 + 2.4m - 3.6 \\
C - 3.6 &= 2.4m
\end{aligned}
$$

Now we divide *both sides* of the equation by $2.4{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{C - 3.6}{2.4} &= \frac{2.4m}{2.4} \\
\frac{C - 3.6}{2.4} &= m
\end{aligned}
$$

Rewriting with $m$ on the left-hand side, we get the correct answer:

$$
m = \dfrac{C - 3.6}{2.4}
$$

## Solving Multivariable Equations in Two Steps (Division First)

**Example:** The following equation relates the numbers $q, r$, and $s{:}$
$12q = 3(r + s)$
Express $r$ in terms of $q$ and $s$.

**Explanation**

We are given the equation

$$
12q = 3(r + s)
$$

and asked to solve for $r$ in terms of $q$ and $s$.

First, we divide *both sides* of the equation by $3$ to isolate the expression in parentheses:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{12q}{3} &= (3(r + s))/(3) \\
4q &= r + s
\end{aligned}
$$

Now we subtract $s$ from *both sides* to isolate $r{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
4q - s &= r + s - s \\
4q - s &= r
\end{aligned}
$$

Rewriting with $r$ on the left-hand side, we get the correct answer:
$r = 4q - s$

## Solving Multivariable Equations in Two Steps (Multiplication First)

**Example:** A spaceship moves through deep space, and its average speed $u$ (in kilometers per minute) during a time interval of $6$ minutes is given by the equation
$u = \dfrac{z - 30}{6}$,
where $z$ is the ship's displacement in kilometers. If the equation is rewritten in the form
$z = p u + q$,
where $p$ and $q$ are constants, what is the value of $p$?

**Explanation**

We are given the equation

$$
u = \dfrac{z - 30}{6}
$$

and asked to rewrite it in the form

$$
z = p u + q
$$

in order to determine the value of $p$.

First, we multiply *both sides* of the initial equation by $6$ to eliminate the fraction:
$6u = z - 30$

Then, we add *$30$ to both sides* to solve for $z{:}$

$$
z = 6u + 30
$$

Finally, we identify the coefficient of $u$. The equation is now in the form

$$
z = \underbrace{6}_{p} \cdot u + \underbrace{30}_{q}
$$

Therefore, the value of $p$ is $6$.

## Solving Multivariable Equations in Three Steps

**Example:** The formula
$E = D + \dfrac{4}{7}(95 - P)$
can be used to estimate the energy usage $E$ (in kilowatt-hours) of a cooling tower, where $D$ is the baseline draw (in kilowatt-hours) and $P$ is the pressure differential (in psi), provided $P < 95$. Express $P$ in terms of $E$ and $D$.

**Explanation**

We are given the equation

$$
E = D + \dfrac{4}{7}(95 - P)
$$

To isolate $P$, we first subtract $D$ from both sides:

$$
E - D = \dfrac{4}{7}(95 - P)
$$

Next, we multiply both sides by

$$
\dfrac{7}{4}
$$

to eliminate the fraction:

$$
\dfrac{7}{4}(E - D) = 95 - P
$$

Now, subtract $95$ from both sides:

$$
\dfrac{7}{4}(E - D)-95 =-P
$$

Finally, multiplying both sides by $-1$ gives

$$
95 - \dfrac{7}{4}(E - D) = P
$$

Rewriting with $P$ on the left-hand side, we get the correct answer:

$$
P = 95 - \dfrac{7}{4}(E - D)
$$
