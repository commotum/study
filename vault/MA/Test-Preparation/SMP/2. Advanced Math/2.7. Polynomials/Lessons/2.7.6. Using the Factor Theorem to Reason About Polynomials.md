## Applying the Factor Theorem to Quadratic Polynomials

**Example:** The expression $x + 2k$ is a factor of $2x^{2} + 15x + 6k$. Which of the following could be the value of $k$?

1. $k=3$ or $k=5$
2. $k=0$ only
3. $k=0$ or $k=3$
4. $k=5$ only

**Explanation**

Recall that by the factor theorem, if $(x-r)$ is a factor of a polynomial $p(x)$, then $x=r$ is a root of the polynomial.

Now, since $x + 2k$ is a factor of the polynomial

$$
p(x)=2x^{2} + 15x + 6k
$$

we must have
$p(-2k) = 0$.

So, substituting $x=-2k$ into the expression for $p(x)$, we get the following:

$$
\begin{aligned}
p(-2k) &= 2(-2k)^{2} + 15(-2k) + 6k \\
&= 8k^{2} - 30k + 6k \\
&= 8k^{2} - 24k \\
&= 8k(k - 3)
\end{aligned}
$$

Using the zero product rule, we conclude that $8k(k-3)=0$ if

$$
k=0 \qquad\text{or}\qquad k-3=0
$$

Therefore, the correct answer is "III only"

## Applying the Factor Theorem to Quadratic Polynomials With Leading Coefficients

**Example:** The expression $4x - g$ is a factor of $16x^{2} + 12x - 7g$. Which of the following could be the value of $g$?

1. $g=0$ or $g=4$
2. $g=4$ only
3. $g=-1$ or $g=0$
4. $g=0$ only

**Explanation**

Recall that by the factor theorem, if $(sx-r)$ is a factor of a polynomial $p(x)$, then

$$
x=\dfrac{r}{s}
$$

is a root of the polynomial.

Now, since $4x - g$ is a factor of the polynomial

$$
p(x)=16x^{2} + 12x - 7g
$$

we must have

$$
p\left(\dfrac{g}{4}\right) = 0
$$

So, substituting

$$
x=\dfrac{g}{4}
$$

into the expression for $p(x)$, we get the following:

$$
\begin{aligned}
p(\frac{g}{4}) &= 16(\frac{g}{4})^{2} + 12(\frac{g}{4}) - 7g \\
&= g^{2} + 3g - 7g \\
&= g^{2} - 4g \\
&= g(g - 4)
\end{aligned}
$$

Using the zero product rule, we conclude that $g(g - 4)=0$ if

$$
g=0 \qquad\text{or}\qquad g-4=0
$$

which gives $g=0$ or $g=4$.

Therefore, the correct answer is "I only"

## Applying the Factor Theorem Given Tabular Data

**Example:** The table above shows selected values of a polynomial function $t(x)$. Based on the values in the table, which of the following must be a factor of $t$?

| $x$ | $t(x)$ |
| --- | ---: |
| $-3$ | $5$ |
| $-1$ | $0$ |
| $0$ | $-2$ |
| $2$ | $0$ |
| $4$ | $7$ |

1. $(x + 1)(x - 2)$
2. $(x - 1)$
3. $(x + 2)$
4. $(x + 3)(x - 2)$

**Explanation**

We are told that $t(x)$ is a polynomial function. By the *factor theorem*, if $t(a) = 0$, then $(x - a)$ is a factor of $t(x)$.

From the table, we see:

- $t(-1) = 0$, so $(x + 1)$ is a factor
- $t(2) = 0$, so $(x - 2)$ is a factor

Both of these factors appear in the product $(x + 1)(x - 2)$, so this product must be a factor of $t(x)$.

None of the other answer choices are consistent with both of these zeros.

So, the correct answer is $(x + 1)(x - 2)$.

<a id="identifying-polynomials-with-a-given-factor"></a>
## Identifying Polynomials With a Given Factor

**Example:** Which of the following expressions has a factor of $2x - 5m$, where $m$ is a **negative integer** constant?

1. $4x^{2} + 2x + 18m$
2. $4x^{2} + 2x + 10m$
3. $4x^{2} + 2x + 20m$

**Explanation**

Recall that by the factor theorem, if $(sx-r)$ is a factor of a polynomial $p(x)$, then

$$
x=\dfrac{r}{s}
$$

is a root of the polynomial.
Thus, since $2x -5m$ is a factor of our polynomial $p(x)$, we must have

$$
p\left(\dfrac{5m}{2}\right) = 0
$$

With that in mind, let's examine the given options in turn.

- Option I, $p(x) = 4x^{2}+2x+18m$, is not valid. Here, we have
$p(\frac{3c}{2})|= 4(\frac{5m}{2})^{2} + 2(\frac{5m}{2}) + 18m; = 25m^{2} + 5m + 18m; = 25m^{2} + 23m; = m(25m + 23)$.
This equals zero only at $m=0$ or $m=-\dfrac{23}{25}$, neither of which is a negative integer. ${\color{red}\times}$

*Option II,*

$$
p(x)=4x^{2}+2x+10m
$$

is not valid. Here, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
p(\frac{3c}{2}) &= 4(\frac{5m}{2})^{2} + 2(\frac{5m}{2}) + 10m \\
&= 25m^{2} + 5m + 10m \\
&= 25m^{2} + 15m \\
&= 5m(5m + 3)
\end{aligned}
$$

This equals zero only at $m=0$ or

$$
m=-\dfrac{3}{5}
$$

neither of which is a negative integer. ${\color{red}\times}$

*Option III,*

$$
p(x)=4x^{2}+2x+20m
$$

is valid. Indeed,

$$
\begin{aligned}
p(\frac{3c}{2}) &= 4(\frac{5m}{2})^{2} + 2(\frac{5m}{2}) + 20m \\
&= 25m^{2} + 5m + 20m \\
&= 25m^{2} + 25m \\
&= 25m(m + 1)
\end{aligned}
$$

This equals zero only at $m=0$ or $m=-1$, and $m=-1$ is a negative integer. ${\color{darkgreen}\checkmark}$

Therefore, the correct answer is "III only".
