## Identifying a Shifted Parabola

**Example:** The graph shown will be translated left $5$ units. Find the resulting graph.

![](<../Source/2.7.4. Polynomial Transformations/Images/70551e28feb5dea3454c150b23d3213d.png>)

**Explanation**

We are given a graph of a parabola whose vertex is at $(-1, 2)$, and we are told it will be translated left $5$ units.

A horizontal translation to the left subtracts the translation amount from the $x$-coordinates of all the points on the graph.

So, to translate the graph left $5$ units, we subtract $5$ from the $x$-coordinates of all the points on the graph, including the vertex. This results in the same shape of the parabola but with a new vertex at

$$
(-1 - 5, 2) = (-6, 2)
$$

## Vertically Shifted Polynomials

**Example:** The function $f$ is defined by
$f(x) = (x - 3)(x + 4)(x + 1)$.
In the $xy$-plane, the graph of $y = g(x)$ is the result of translating the graph of $y = f(x)$ up $6$ units. What is the value of $g(-1)$?

**Explanation**

We are told that the graph of $g(x)$ is the result of translating the graph of $y = f(x)$ up $6$ units. This means

$$
\begin{aligned}
g(x) &= f(x) + 6 \\
&= (x - 3)(x + 4)(x + 1) + 6
\end{aligned}
$$

To calculate $g(-1)$, we substitute $x=-1$ into the expression for $g(x)$ and simplify:

$$
\begin{aligned}
g(-1) &= (-1 - 3)(-1 + 4)(-1 + 1) + 6 \\
&= (-4)(3)(0) + 6 \\
&= 0 + 6 \\
&= 6
\end{aligned}
$$

## Horizontal Translations of Quadratic Functions

**Example:** A curve is defined by $y = f(x)$, where $f(x) = -x^2 + x + 2$. If $y = g(x)$ is a horizontal translation of $y = f(x)$ by $2$ units left, what is the function $g(x)$?

**Explanation**

We're given the curve

$$
y = -x^2 + x + 2
$$

and we wish to translate this curve by $2$ units left.

For each point on the curve $y = f(x)$, we must subtract $2$ from each $x$-coordinate to get the corresponding point on the new curve $y = g(x)$. So, the $x$-coordinates of the points on the new curve are given by

$$
x_{\textrm{new}} = x - 2
$$

This equation is equivalent to

$$
x = x_{\textrm{new}} + 2
$$

To get the equation of the new curve, we substitute this into the equation of the first curve. Doing this, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
y &= -x^{2} + x + 2 \\
y &= - (x_{new} + 2)^{2} + (x_{new} + 2) + 2
\end{aligned}
$$

Dropping the $\textrm{new}$ suffix, we get

$$
y = -(x + 2)^2 + (x + 2) + 2
$$

Finally, simplifying the expression, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
y &= - (x + 2)^{2} + (x + 2) + 2 \\
y &= -x^{2} - 4x - 4 + x + 2 + 2 \\
y &= -x^{2} - 3x
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
g(x) = -x^2 - 3x
$$
