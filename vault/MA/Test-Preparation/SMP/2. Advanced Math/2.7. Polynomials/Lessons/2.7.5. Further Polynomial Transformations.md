## Determining a Table of Values of a Transformed Polynomial

**Example:** $s(x) = (x - 8)(x + 2)(x + 5)$

The function $s$ is given. Which of the following tables of values represents $y = s(x) + 1$?

**I.**

| $x$ | $y$ |
| --- | ---: |
| $-5$ | $1$ |
| $-2$ | $1$ |
| $8$ | $1$ |

**II.**

| $x$ | $y$ |
| --- | ---: |
| $-5$ | $0$ |
| $-2$ | $1$ |
| $8$ | $1$ |

**III.**

| $x$ | $y$ |
| --- | ---: |
| $-5$ | $-1$ |
| $-2$ | $1$ |
| $8$ | $1$ |

**IV.**

| $x$ | $y$ |
| --- | ---: |
| $-5$ | $0$ |
| $-2$ | $0$ |
| $8$ | $0$ |

**Explanation**

We are given the function

$$
s(x) = (x - 8)(x + 2)(x + 5)
$$

and asked to evaluate the transformed expression

$$
y = s(x) + 1
$$

for different values of $x$.

First, we note that $s(x)$ has roots at $x=-5, x=-2$, and $x=8$. Therefore, a table of values for $s(x)$ at these points is as follows:

| $x$ | $s(x)$ |
| --- | ---: |
| $-5$ | $0$ |
| $-2$ | $0$ |
| $8$ | $0$ |

Therefore, since

$$
y = s(x) + 1
$$

the correct table for $y$ must be the following:

| $x$ | $y$ |
| --- | ---: |
| $-5$ | $1$ |
| $-2$ | $1$ |
| $8$ | $1$ |

## Determining Properties of Translated Quadratic Functions

**Example:** A parabola is given by $y=f(x)$, where
$f(x) = x^2 + 8x + 5$.
The function $g$ is defined by $g(x) = f(x - 5)$. What is the vertex of $y = g(x)$?

**Explanation**

We start by noting the following:

- Since $g(x) = f(x - 5)$, the graph of $y = g(x)$ is obtained by shifting the graph of $y = f(x)$ by $5$ units to the *right.*
- Therefore, to obtain the corresponding points on $y = g(x)$, we *add* $5$ to each $x$-coordinate of $y = f(x)$ while keeping the $y$-coordinate unchanged.

Now, recall that the $x$-coordinate of the vertex of a quadratic function

$$
y = ax^2 + bx + c
$$

is

$$
x = -\dfrac{b}{2a}
$$

Here, $a = 1$ and $b = 8$, so the $x$-coordinate of the vertex of $y = f(x)$ is

$$
\begin{aligned}
x &= - (8)/(2(1)) \\
&=-4
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, to find the $y$-coordinate of the vertex of $y = f(x)$, we substitute $x = -4$ into $f(x){:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
f(x) &= x^{2} + 8x + 5 \\
f(-4) &= (-4)^{2} + 8(-4) + 5 \\
&= 16 - 32 + 5 \\
&=-11
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the vertex of $y = f(x)$ is $(-4,-11)$.

Therefore, *adding* $5$ to the $x$-coordinate, the vertex of $y = g(x)$ is

$$
(-4+5, -11) = \left(1, -11\right)
$$

## Determining Properties of the Preimage

**Example:** A parabola is given by $y = g(x)$, where
$g(x) = (x-7)(x+5)$.
Suppose the function $g$ is defined by $g(x) = f(x-6)$. What is the vertex of $y = f(x)$?

**Explanation**

We start by noting the following:

- Since $g(x) = f(x - 6)$, the graph of $y = g(x)$ is obtained by shifting the graph of $y = f(x)$ by $6$ units to the right.
- Therefore, the graph of $y = f(x)$ is obtained by shifting the graph of $y = g(x)$ by $6$ units to the *left.*
- So, to obtain the corresponding points on $y = f(x)$, we *subtract* $6$ from each $x$-coordinate of $y = g(x)$ while keeping the $y$-coordinate unchanged.

From the equation

$$
g(x) = (x-7)(x+5)
$$

we find that the roots of the parabola are $x_1=7$ and $x_2=-5$.

To find the $x$-coordinate of the vertex of $y = g(x)$, we take the average of the roots, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x &= \frac{x_{1} + x_{2}}{2} \\
&= (7 + (-5))/(2) \\
&= \frac{2}{2} \\
&= 1
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, to find the $y$-coordinate of the vertex of $y = g(x)$, we substitute $x = 1$ into $g(x){:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
g(x) &= (x - 7)(x + 5) \\
g(1) &= (1 - 7)(1 + 5) \\
&= (-6)(6) \\
&=-36
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the vertex of $y = g(x)$ is $(1,-36)$.

Therefore, *subtracting* $6$ from the $x$-coordinate, the vertex of $y = f(x)$ is

$$
(1-6, -36) = \left(-5, -36\right)
$$

## Determining Roots of Transformed Higher-Order Polynomials

**Example:** A function $f$ is defined by
$f(x)=(x+5)(x+1)(x-6)$.
Define $k$ by $k(x)=f(x+2)$. The graph $y=k(x)$ has $x$-intercepts at $(s,0)$, $(t,0)$, and $(u,0)$, where $s> t > u$ are distinct constants. What is the value of $s+t+u$?

**Explanation**

We start by noting the following:

- Since $k(x) = f(x + 2)$, the graph of $k(x)$ is obtained by shifting the graph of $f(x)$ by $2$ units to the *left.*
- Therefore, to obtain the corresponding points on $k(x)$, we *subtract* $2$ from each $x$-coordinate of $f(x)$ while keeping the $y$-coordinate unchanged.

The $x$-intercepts of $y=k(x)$ occur where $k(x)=0$, i.e., where

$$
f(x+2)=0
$$

By the zero product property, the zeros of $f$ are

$$
x = -5,\qquad x = -1, \qquad x = 6
$$

Therefore, *subtracting* $2$ from the $x$-coordinates, we get the roots of $k$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
s &= 6 - 2 = 4 \\
t &= -1 - 2 =-3 \\
u &= -5 - 2 =-7
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus, the $x$-intercepts are $(4,0)$, $(-3,0)$, and $(-7,0)$, and their $x$-coordinates sum to

$$
s+t+u=4+(-3)+(-7)=-6
$$

So,

$$
s+t+u=-6
$$
