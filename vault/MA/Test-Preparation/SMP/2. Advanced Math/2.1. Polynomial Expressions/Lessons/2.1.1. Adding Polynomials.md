## Introduction

Questions involving the simplification of polynomials usually ask to combine like terms or interpret algebraic expressions. These problems test the ability to recognize patterns and manipulate algebraic structures.

In this lesson, we’ll practice adding and subtracting polynomials of various types, while reinforcing strategies for combining like terms.

## The Sum of Two Monomials of the Same Order

**Example:** Simplify $7a^5 + 2a^5$?

**Explanation**

To simplify the expression $7a^5 + 2a^5$, we identify that both terms are like terms because they have the same variable part, $a^5$.

Now, we combine the coefficients:

$$
7a^5 + 2a^5 = (7 + 2)a^5 = 9a^5
$$

So, the simplified expression is $9a^5$.

## The Sum or Difference of Two Linear Binomials

**Example:** Simplify
$\left(\dfrac{1}{2} x + 3\right) - \left(\dfrac{2}{3} x - 5\right)$.

**Explanation**

We are asked to simplify

$$
\left(\dfrac{1}{2} x + 3\right) - \left(\dfrac{2}{3} x - 5\right)
$$

First, we distribute the negative sign across the second group. This gives

$$
\dfrac{1}{2} x + 3 - \dfrac{2}{3} x + 5
$$

Next, we group like terms as follows:

$$
\left(\dfrac{1}{2} x - \dfrac{2}{3} x \right) + \left(3 + 5\right)
$$

We now find a common denominator for the $x$ terms using the least common multiple of $2$ and $3$, which is $6{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{1}{2}x - \frac{2}{3}x &= \frac{3}{6}x - \frac{4}{6}x \\
&=-\frac{1}{6}x
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, adding the constants, we get

$$
3 + 5 = 8
$$

Therefore, the simplified expression is

$$
-\dfrac{1}{6} x + 8
$$

## The Sum or Difference of Two Cubic or Quartic Binomials

**Example:** The expression
$\left(5 x^{3} - 3\right) - \left(-4 x^{3} + 8\right)$
is equivalent to $b x^{3} - 11$, where $b$ is a constant. What is the value of $b$?

**Explanation**

We are asked to evaluate

$$
\left(5 x^{3} - 3\right) - \left(-4 x^{3} + 8\right)
$$

First, we distribute the negative sign across the second group:

$$
= 5 x^{3} - 3 + 4 x^{3} - 8
$$

Next, we group like terms as follows:

$$
(5 x^{3} + 4 x^{3}) + (-3 - 8) = 9 x^{3} - 11
$$

We compare this to the form
$b x^{3} - 11$.

Therefore, the value of $b$ is $9$.

<a id="the-sum-or-difference-of-two-trinomials"></a>
## The Sum or Difference of Two Trinomials

**Example:** Find the expression is equivalent to $(5x^2+2x-9)+(x^2-7x+3)$?

**Explanation**

When faced with simplifying expanded polynomial expressions, our strategy should consist of first checking for like terms and factorability.

Notice that there are like terms contained in separate parentheses: the $x^2$, $x$, and constant terms. We start by combining our like terms:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(5x^{2} + 2x - 9) + (x^{2} - 7x + 3) &= 5x^{2} + 2x - 9 + x^{2} - 7x + 3 \\
&= (5x^{2} + x^{2}) + (2x - 7x) + (-9 + 3) \\
&= (5 + 1)x^{2} + (-5)x - 6 \\
&= 6x^{2} - 5x - 6
\end{aligned}
$$

Since this simplified expression cannot be further simplified, we have our equivalent expression.
