## Introduction

Questions involving factoring trinomials are common on the math section of the SAT exam. Recognizing the corresponding patterns can help us solve equations and simplify expressions.

Here, we’ll practice factoring different types of trinomials, including perfect squares and more general quadratic expressions.

## Factoring Perfect Square Trinomials

**Example:** Which of the following is a factor of the polynomial $49x^2 - 112xy + 64y^2$?

1. $5x - 8y$
2. $7x - 8y$
3. $5x + 8y$
4. $7x + 8y$

**Explanation**

To find a factor of the polynomial $49x^2 - 112xy + 64y^2$, we attempt to factor it as a perfect square trinomial.

A perfect square trinomial takes the form

$$
(a - b)^2 = a^2 - 2ab + b^2
$$

In our case:

- $49x^2 = (7x)^2$
- $64y^2 = (8y)^2$
- $-112xy = -2(7x)(8y)$

So, our expression is
$(7x)^2 - 2(7x)(8y) + (8y)^2$.

Since all three conditions match the pattern of a perfect square trinomial, we can factor the expression as

$$
\begin{aligned}
49x^{2} - 112xy + 64y^{2} &= (7x)^{2} - 2(7x)(8y) + (8y)^{2} \\
&= (7x - 8y)^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the answer is $7x - 8y$ which is expression "II."

## Factoring Quadratic Trinomials With Leading Coefficients

**Example:** Which of the following expressions is (are) a factor of $6k^2 + 11k - 10$?

$\textrm{I.}2k + 5, \qquad \textrm{II.}3k - 2$

**Explanation**

We are given a quadratic trinomial and asked to determine which of the two listed binomials are factors.

To do this, we start by factoring the quadratic expression $6k^2 + 11k - 10$.

We look for two numbers that multiply to

$$
(6)(-10) = -60
$$

and sum to $11$.

The numbers $15$ and $-4$ satisfy this:

$$
15 \cdot (-4) = -60 \quad \text{and} \quad 15 + (-4) = 11
$$

We rewrite the middle term $+11k$ using $15k$ and $-4k$ as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
6k^{2} + 11k - 10 &= 6k^{2} + 15k - 4k - 10 \\
&= 3k(2k + 5) - 2(2k + 5) \\
&= (3k - 2)(2k + 5)
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the factored form is $(3k - 2)(2k + 5)$.

From this, we note the following:

- $3k - 2$ *is* a factor
- $2k + 5$ *is* a factor

Therefore, the correct answer is "I and II."
