## Introduction

Factoring by a greatest common factor (GCF) is often used as a step in solving problems that contain polynomial expressions.

In this topic, we'll practice spotting numeric and variable GCFs in polynomials and using them to factor the expressions.

---

<a id="factoring-a-common-integer-factor"></a>
## Factoring a Common Integer Factor

**Example:** Find a factor of the expression $15x^2 - 30x + 45$.

**Explanation**

To determine a factor of $15x^2 - 30x + 45$, we look for a common factor in all three terms.

The coefficients are $15$, $-30$, and $45$, which all share a common factor of $15$. So, we factor out $15$ from the expression:

$$
15x^2 - 30x + 45 = 15(x^2 - 2x + 3)
$$

This means that $15$ is a factor of the polynomial.

So, the answer is $15$.

## Factoring One-Variable Binomials

**Example:** The expression $60x^2 + 180x$ is equivalent to $rx(3x + 9)$, where $r$ is a constant. What is the value of $r$?

**Explanation**

We begin with the expression $60x^2 + 180x$ and compare it to the form $rx(3x + 9)$.

First, we factor $x$ from both terms in the original expression:

$$
\begin{aligned}
60x^{2} + 180x &= x(60x + 180)
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we factor out the greatest common factor from the binomial $60x + 180$. The GCF of $60$ and $180$ is $60$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x(60x + 180) &= x(60)(x + 3) \\
&= 60x(x + 3)
\end{aligned}
$$

We are told the expression is written as $rx(3x + 9)$. Notice that

$$
3x + 9 = 3(x + 3)
$$

so we rewrite the expression as

$$
\begin{aligned}
60x(x + 3) &= (3 \cdot \frac{60}{3})x(x + 3) \\
&= (\frac{60}{3}) \cdot x \cdot 3(x + 3) \\
&= 20x(3x + 9)
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus, the value of $r$ is $\bbox[4pt,border: 1px solid lightgray]{20}$.

## Factoring One-Variable Trinomials

**Example:** Factor out the greatest common factor and simplify the expression $18x^5 + 27x^3 - 9x^2$.

**Explanation**

We want to factor the expression $18x^5 + 27x^3 - 9x^2$. To do this, we first identify the greatest common factor (GCF) of all the terms.

Each term shares a numerical factor of $9$, and each term also contains at least two factors of $x$. So the GCF of all three terms is $9x^2$.

Now, we factor out $9x^2$ from each term:

$$
\begin{aligned}
18x^{5} + 27x^{3} - 9x^{2} &= 9x^{2}(2x^{3}) + 9x^{2}(3x) - 9x^{2}(1) \\
&= 9x^{2}(2x^{3} + 3x - 1)
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the expression $18x^5 + 27x^3 - 9x^2$ is equivalent to $9x^2(2x^3 + 3x - 1)$.

## Factoring Two-Variable Binomials and Trinomials

**Example:** Factor out the greatest common factor and simplify the expression $48 s^{4} r^{2}+60 s^{2} r$.

**Explanation**

First, we find the greatest common factor $(\textrm{GCF})$ of the two terms:

$$
\textrm{GCF}(48s^4r^2,\ 60s^2r) = 12s^2r
$$

We now rewrite each term as a product involving the $\textrm{GCF}$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
48s^{4}r^{2} + 60s^{2}r &=  \\
&= (12s^{2}r \cdot 4s^{2}r) + (12s^{2}r \cdot 5)
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we factor out the $\textrm{GCF}$ using the distributive property:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(12s^{2}r \cdot 4s^{2}r) + (12s^{2}r \cdot 5) &=  \\
&= 12s^{2}r(4s^{2}r + 5)
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the expression $48 s^{4} r^{2}+60 s^{2} r$ is equivalent to $12rs^2(4rs^2 + 5)$.

## Factoring Expressions Containing a Common Binomial

**Example:** Factor and simplify the expression $-3(q^5 + q^3) - 4(q^5 + q^3)$.

**Explanation**

Notice that we are adding two multiples of the same binomial, $q^5 + q^3$:

$$
-3(q^5 + q^3) - 4(q^5 + q^3)
$$

First, we factor out the common binomial:

$$
(-3 - 4)(q^5 + q^3) = -7(q^5 + q^3)
$$

Now, we distribute to simplify the expression, as follows:

$$
-7q^5 - 7q^3
$$

Therefore, the answer is $-7q^5 - 7q^3$.
