## Introduction

A common theme on the SAT math section is understanding how exponential functions behave, and this can include cases with restricted domains. In particular, you will need to identify when they have maximum or minimum values, determine their ranges, and describe whether they are increasing or decreasing over those domains.

## Calculating Extrema of Exponential Functions Over the Nonnegative Reals

**Example:** What is the minimum value of $f(x)=5\cdot 4^x+1$ for $x \geq 0$?

**Explanation**

Let's start by sketching the graph of $y=f(x)$ for the function

$$
f(x) = 5\cdot 4^x+1
$$

with the domain restriction

$$
x\geq 0
$$

![](<../Source/2.8.2. Maxima and Minima of Exponential Functions/Images/bca49b082395edc9e72014bd492600d5.png>)

Evaluating $f$ at $x=0$, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
f(0) &= 5 \cdot 4^{0} + 1 \\
&= 5 \cdot 1 + 1 \\
&= 6
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the minimum value of $f$ on the domain

$$
x \geq 0
$$

is $6$.

## Finding Extrema of Exponential Functions Over Other Restricted Domains

**Example:** What is the maximum value of $f(x)=-2\cdot 3^x-5$ for $x \geq 2$?

**Explanation**

Let's start by sketching the graph of $y=f(x)$ for the function

$$
f(x) = -2\cdot 3^x-5
$$

with the domain restriction

$$
x\geq 2
$$

![](<../Source/2.8.2. Maxima and Minima of Exponential Functions/Images/5d55699cc39423ced9ccdbebdd39da37.png>)

On the domain

$$
x \geq 2
$$

$f$ attains its *maximum* at $x=2$.

Evaluating $f$ at $x=2$, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
f(2) &= -2 \cdot 3^{2} - 5 \\
&=-2 \cdot 9 - 5 \\
&=-18 - 5 \\
&=-23
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the maximum value of $f$ on the domain

$$
x \geq 2
$$

is $-23$.

## Identifying True Statements Regarding Extrema of Exponential Functions Over the Reals

**Example:** Consider the function $f(x) = -6\left(\dfrac12\right)^{x} - 4$. Which of the following statements are true?

1. $f$ has a minimum value.
2. $f$ does **NOT** have a maximum value.
3. The range of $f$ is $(-\infty,-4)$.

**Explanation**

On the domain $x\in(-\infty,\infty)$, an exponential function

$$
f(x)=a\cdot b^x+c
$$

approaches, but never reaches, a horizontal asymptote on one side, and increases (or decreases) without bound on the other.

Let's start by sketching the graph of $y=f(x)$ below.

![](<../Source/2.8.2. Maxima and Minima of Exponential Functions/Images/a05d5b28a5e5ffae933bd14410237a29.png>)

From the graph, we note the following:

- Looking at the end-behavior on the left-hand side, we see that $f(x) \to -\infty$ as $x \to -\infty$.
- Looking at the end-behavior on the right-hand side, we see that $f(x) < -4$.

With this in mind, let's consider each statement in turn.

- Statement I is false. The function decreases without bound as $x\to -\infty$, so it does not have a minimum value.
- Statement II is true. Since $f(x)$ approaches the horizontal asymptote $y=-4$ from below but never reaches $-4$, $f$ does not have a maximum value.
- Statement III is true. Since $f(x)$ can be made arbitrarily negative and approaches $-4$ from below, the range is $(-\infty,-4)$.

Therefore, the correct statements are “II and III only”.

## Identifying True Statements Regarding Extrema of Exponential Functions Over the Nonnegative Reals

**Example:** Consider the function $f(x) = -4\cdot 4^x - 2$ on the domain $x\geq 0$. Which of the following statements are true?

1. The maximum value of $f$ is $-6$.
2. $f$ is increasing on its domain.
3. The range of $f$ is $[MATH: (-\infty,-6].]$

**Explanation**

On the domain $x\in(-\infty,\infty)$, an exponential function

$$
f(x)=a\cdot b^x+c
$$

approaches, but never reaches, a horizontal asymptote on one side, and increases (or decreases) without bound on the other.

Let's start by sketching the graph of $y=f(x)$ for the function

$$
f(x) = -4\cdot 4^x - 2
$$

with the domain restriction

$$
x\geq 0
$$

![](<../Source/2.8.2. Maxima and Minima of Exponential Functions/Images/6f35d3e75a0412e9672365aefbcc3236.png>)

With this in mind, let's consider each statement in turn.

- Statement I is true. The function attains a maximum at the left endpoint $x=0$, with $f(0)=-4\cdot 1-2=-6$.
- Statement II is false. We can see from the graph that $f$ is decreasing on its domain.
- Statement III is true. Because $f(0)=-6$ is the largest value and $f(x)\to -\infty$ as $x\to\infty$, the range is $[MATH: (-\infty,-6]]$ on it domain.

Therefore, the correct statements are “I and III only”.
