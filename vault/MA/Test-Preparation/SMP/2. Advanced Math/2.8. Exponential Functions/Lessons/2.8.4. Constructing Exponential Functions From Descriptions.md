## Determining an Output of an Exponential Growth Function Given a Description

**Example:** An exponential function $D$ is defined by $D(x) = 400 \cdot b^x$, where $b$ is a positive constant. If $D(2) = 100$, what is the value of $D(5)$?

**Explanation**

We are told that

$$
D(x) = 400 \cdot b^x
$$

and that $D(2) = 100$.

Substituting $x = 2$ into the function $D$ and solve for $b$, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
D(2) &= 400 \cdot b^{2} \\
100 &= 400 \cdot b^{2} \\
b^{2} &= \frac{100}{400} \\
b^{2} &= \frac{1}{4} \\
b &= ± \frac{1}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Taking into account that $b$ is positive, we get

$$
b = \dfrac{1}{2}
$$

Finally, we substitute

$$
b=\dfrac{1}{2}
$$

into the function to calculate $D(5){:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
D(5) &= 400 \cdot (\frac{1}{2})^{5} \\
&= 400 \cdot \frac{1}{32} \\
&= 12.5
\end{aligned}
$$

## Constructing an Exponential Model Given an Initial Value and Growth Factor

**Example:** Two variables, $h$ and $j$, are related such that for each increase of $1$ in the value of $h$, the value of $j$ is multiplied by $\dfrac{3}{2}$. When $h = 0$, $j = 64$. Which equation represents this relationship?

**Explanation**

We are told that the value of $j$ is multiplied by

$$
\dfrac{3}{2}
$$

for each increase of $1$ in $h$. That means the variable $j$ grows exponentially with base

$$
\dfrac{3}{2}
$$

Therefore, $h$ and $j$ are related by the expression

$$
j = a \cdot \left(\dfrac{3}{2}\right)^h
$$

Now, we know that $j = 64$ when $h = 0$. Hence, we can determine the value of $a$, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
j &= a \cdot (\frac{3}{2})^{h} \\
64 &= a \cdot (\frac{3}{2})^{0} \\
64 &= a \cdot 1 \\
a &= 64
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally,

$$
j = 64 \cdot \left(\dfrac{3}{2}\right)^h
$$

## Constructing an Exponential Model Given a Percentage Decrease

**Example:** For the function $f$, we have $f(0) = 400$, and for each increase in $x$ by $3$, the value of $f(x)$ decreases by $75\%$. What is the value of $f(6)$?

**Explanation**

We are told that

$$
f(0) = 400
$$

and that $f(x)$ decreases by $75\%$ for each increase of $3$ in $x$. This means that each time $x$ increases by $3$, the value of $f(x)$ becomes $25\%$ of the previous value.

Therefore, the function follows exponential decay with a factor of $0.25$ ($25\%$ expressed as a decimal) per $3$ units. The general form is

$$
f(x) = 400 \cdot (0.25)^{x/3}
$$

Now, we compute $f(6){:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
f(6) &= 400 \cdot (0.25)^{6/3} \\
&= 400 \cdot (0.25)^{2} \\
&= 400 \cdot \frac{1}{16} \\
&= 25
\end{aligned}
$$
