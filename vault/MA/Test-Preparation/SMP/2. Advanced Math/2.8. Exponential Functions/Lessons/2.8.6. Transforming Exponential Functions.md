## Finding a Transformed Exponential Function Corresponding to a Left Shift

**Example:** The function $h$ is defined by the equation $h(x) = 5 \cdot 2^x$. If $k(x) = h(x + 3)$, find the equation that defines the function $k$.

**Explanation**

We are told that

$$
h(x) = 5 \cdot 2^x
$$

and that

$$
k(x) = h(x + 3)
$$

To find the equation for $k(x)$, we substitute $x+3$ into the definition of $h(x)$ wherever $x$ appears. By doing this, we get

$$
k(x) = h(x + 3) = 5 \cdot 2^{x + 3}
$$

Now, we simplify and obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
k(x) &= 5 \cdot 2^{x + 3} \\
&= 5 \cdot 2^{x} \cdot 2^{3} \\
&= 5 \cdot 2^{3} \cdot 2^{x} \\
&= 5 \cdot 8 \cdot 2^{x} \\
&= 40 \cdot 2^{x}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the equation that defines $k$ is

$$
k(x) = 40 \cdot 2^x
$$

## Finding a Transformed Exponential Function Corresponding to a Right Shift

**Example:** The function $p$ is defined by the equation $p(x) = 150 \cdot 10^x$. If $q(x) = p(x - 2)$,find the equation that defines the function $q$.

**Explanation**

We are told that

$$
p(x) = 150 \cdot 10^x
$$

and that

$$
q(x) = p(x - 2)
$$

To find the equation for $q(x)$, we substitute $x-2$ into the definition of $p(x)$ wherever $x$ appears. By doing this, we get

$$
q(x) = p(x-2) = 150 \cdot 10^{x-2}
$$

Now, we simplify and obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
q(x) &= 150 \cdot 10^{x - 2} \\
&= 150 \cdot 10^{x} \cdot 10^{-2} \\
&= 150 \cdot 10^{-2} \cdot 10^{x} \\
&= 150 \cdot \frac{1}{100} \cdot 10^{x} \\
&= \frac{150}{100} \cdot 10^{x} \\
&= \frac{3}{2} \cdot 10^{x}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the equation that defines $q$ is

$$
q(x) = \dfrac{3}{2} \cdot 10^x
$$
