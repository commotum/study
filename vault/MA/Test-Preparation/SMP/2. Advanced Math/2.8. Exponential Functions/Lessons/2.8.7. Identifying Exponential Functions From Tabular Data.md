## Finding the Equation of an Exponential Function With a Fractional Base

**Example:** The table above shows values of an exponential function $h(x) = a \cdot b^x$. Determine the constants $a$ and $b$, and write the equation that defines $h(x)$.

| $x$ | $h(x)$ |
| --- | ---: |
| $-1$ | $7$ |
| $0$ | $1$ |
| $1$ | $\dfrac{1}{7}$ |
| $2$ | $\dfrac{1}{49}$ |

**Explanation**

We are told that $h$ is an exponential function, so the relationship between $x$ and $h$ must have the form

$$
h(x) = a \cdot b^x
$$

where $a$ is the initial value and $b$ is the growth factor.

From the table, we see that the value at $x = 0$ is $1$, so

$$
h(0) = a = 1
$$

Therefore, the function has the form

$$
h(x) = b^{x}
$$

To find the base $b$, we use the value at $x = 1{:}$

$$
h(1) = b = \dfrac{1}{7}
$$

So, the exponential equation is

$$
h(x) = \left(\dfrac{1}{7}\right)^x
$$

Let’s confirm that this works for other values in the table.

- When $x = 2$, we have
$h(2)= \left(\dfrac{1}{7}\right)^2 = \dfrac{1}{49}. \quad {\color{green}\checkmark}$
- When $x = -1$, we have
$h(-1) = \left(\dfrac{1}{7}\right)^{-1} = 7. \quad {\color{green}\checkmark}$

Both values match the table. So, the equation is

$$
h(x) = \left(\dfrac{1}{7}\right)^x
$$

## Finding an Exponential Function With a Decimal Base

**Example:** Maya invested in a certificate of deposit that earns interest over time. The table shows the exponential relationship between the number of years $t$ since the investment began and the total value $v$, in dollars, of the account.

| Time (years) | Value (dollars) |
| --- | ---: |
| $0$ | $950.00$ |
| $1$ | $959.50$ |
| $2$ | $969.10$ |

What is the equation that represents the relationship between $t$ and $v$ if no additional money was added or withdrawn?

**Explanation**

We are told that $v$ grows exponentially over time, so the relationship between $t$ and $v$ must have the form

$$
v(t) = a \cdot b^t
$$

where $a$ is the initial amount and $b$ is the growth factor.

From the table, we see that the value at time $t = 0$ is $950$, so
$a = 950$.

Therefore, the function has the form

$$
v(t)=950\cdot b^{t}
$$

To find the growth factor $b$, we use the value at $t=1{:}$

$$
v(1)=950\cdot b = 959.50
$$

Now, we divide both sides by $950$ to isolate $b{:}$

$$
b=\frac{959.50}{950}=1.01
$$

So, the exponential equation is

$$
v(t)=950\cdot (1.01)^t
$$

Let’s confirm that this equation works for $t=2{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
v(2) &= 950 \cdot (1.01)^{2} \\
&= 969.10
\end{aligned}
$$

which matches the value in the table.

Therefore, the equation representing the relationship is

$$
v = 950 \cdot (1.01)^t
$$

## Finding an Exponential Function Given Non-Consecutive Data Points

**Example:** The table shows the exponential relationship between the number of days, $x$, since a freediver began breath-hold training, and the average breath-hold time $f(x)$, in minutes.

| $x$ | $f(x)$ |
| --- | ---: |
| $0$ | $1.80$ |
| $2$ | $1.91$ |
| $4$ | $2.03$ |

What function represents this relationship, where $x \leq 4$?

**Explanation**

We are told that $f$ grows exponentially over time, so the relationship between $x$ and $f$ must have the form

$$
f(x) = a \cdot b^x
$$

where $a$ is the initial amount and $b$ is the growth factor.

From the table, we see that the value at time $x = 0$ is $1.80$, so

$$
f(0)=a = 1.80
$$

Therefore, the function has the form

$$
f(x) = 1.80 \cdot b^{x}
$$

To find the growth factor $b$, we use the value at $x = 2{:}$

$$
f(2)= 1.80\cdot b^{2} = 1.91
$$

Now, we solve for $b{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
b^{2} &= \frac{1.91}{1.80} \\
b^{2}\begin{vmatrix}\approx 1.06111 \\ b &= \sqrt{1.06111} \\ b\end{vmatrix}\approx 1.03
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the exponential equation is

$$
f(x) = 1.80 \cdot (1.03)^x
$$

Let’s confirm that this equation works for $x = 4{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
f(4) &= 1.80 \cdot (1.03)^{4} \\
&\approx 2.03
\end{aligned}
$$

which matches the value in the table.

So, the equation is

$$
f(x) = 1.80 \cdot (1.03)^x
$$

## Finding an Exponential Function With a Parametrically Defined Base

**Example:** For the exponential function $q$, the table above shows several values of $x$ and their corresponding values of $q(x)$, where $b$ is a constant greater than $1$. If $m$ is a constant such that $q(m) = b^{23}$, what is the value of $m$?

| $x$ | $q(x)$ |
| --- | ---: |
| $0$ | $b^{-7}$ |
| $2$ | $b^{3}$ |
| $3$ | $b^{8}$ |

$XXX$

**Explanation**

We are told that $q$ is an exponential function, so the relationship between $x$ and $q$ must have the form

$$
q(x) = a \cdot k^x
$$

where $a$ is the initial value and $k$ is the growth factor.

We will use the values from the table to write a system of equations for the parameters $a$ and $k$.

- From $x = 0$, we have
$q(0)|= b^{-7}; a \cdot k^{0}|= b^{-7}; a|= b^{-7}$.
- From $x = 2$, we have
$q(2)|= b^{3}; a \cdot k^{2}|= b^{3}$.

So, we get the following system of equations:

$$
\begin{aligned}
a &= b^{-7} \\
a \cdot k^{2} &= b^{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

Dividing the second equation by the first gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{a \cdot k^{2}}{a} &= \frac{b^{3}}{b^{-7}} \\
k^{2} &= b^{10} \\
k &= b^{5}
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we substitute $a=b^{-7}$ and $k=b^{5}$ into the model:

$$
\begin{aligned}
q(x) &= a \cdot k^{x} \\
&= b^{-7} \cdot (b^{5})^{x} \\
&= b^{-7} \cdot b^{5x} \\
&= b^{5x - 7}
\end{aligned}
$$

Let’s confirm that

$$
q(x) = b^{5x - 7}
$$

works for $x = 3{:}$

$$
q(3) = b^{5(3)-7}=b^{8}
$$

which matches the value in the table.

We are asked to find $m$ such that

$$
q(m) = b^{23}
$$

Since

$$
q(m) = b^{5m - 7}
$$

we get

$$
b^{5m - 7} = b^{23}
$$

Finally, we equate the exponents and solve for $m{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
5m - 7 &= 23 \\
5m &= 30 \\
m &= 6
\end{aligned}
$$
