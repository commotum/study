## Introduction

Quadratic equations have real solutions when their discriminant is nonnegative, and *distinct* real solutions when their discriminant is positive.

In this topic, we'll practice identifying and using these conditions.

---

<a id="determining-parameter-values-in-quadratic-equations-with-real-solutions"></a>
## Determining Parameter Values in Quadratic Equations With Real Solutions

**Example:** Given that $x(5x - k) = -10$, where $k$ is a **positive** constant, has real solutions, what is the least possible value of $k$?

**Explanation**

A quadratic equation has real solutions if the discriminant is non-negative:

$$
\mathcal{D} \geq 0
$$

First, rewrite the equation in standard quadratic form:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x(5x - k) &= -10 \\
5x^{2} - kx &= -10 \\
5x^{2} - kx + 10 &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

The coefficients of our quadratic equation are the following:

$$
a = 5, \qquad b = -k, \qquad c = 10
$$

So, computing the discriminant, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
D &= b^{2} - 4ac \\
&= (-k)^{2} - 4(5)(10) \\
&= k^{2} - 200
\end{aligned}
$$

To have real solutions, we need

$$
\mathcal{D} \geq 0{:}
$$

$$
\begin{bmatrix}k^{2} - 200 & \ge 0 \\ k^{2} & \ge 200 \\ k & \ge \sqrt{200}\end{bmatrix}
$$

Since $k$ must be positive, we conclude that the least possible value is $10\sqrt{2}$.

## Determining Parameter Values in Quadratic Equations With Distinct Real Solutions

**Example:** $4t^{2} + kt + 36 = 0$

Given that in the above equation, $k$ is a constant and the equation has more than one real solution, which of the following could be the value of $k$?

1. $0$
2. $-26$
3. $10$
4. $-22$

**Explanation**

For a quadratic equation

$$
at^2 + bt + c = 0
$$

the discriminant is

$$
\mathcal{D} = b^2 - 4ac
$$

- If $\mathcal{D} > 0$, there are two distinct real solutions.
- If $\mathcal{D} = 0$, there is exactly one real solution.
- If $\mathcal{D} < 0$, there are no real solutions (the solutions are complex).

The coefficients of our quadratic equation are the following:

$$
a = 4, \qquad b = k, \qquad c = 36
$$

Computing the discriminant, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
D &= b^{2} - 4ac \\
&= k^{2} - 4(4)(36) \\
&= k^{2} - 576
\end{aligned}
$$

To have more than one real solution, we need $\mathcal{D} > 0{:}$

$$
k^{2} - 576\begin{vmatrix}> 0 \\ k^{2}\end{vmatrix}> 576; \sqrt{k^{2}} \mid > \sqrt{576}; \mid k \mid \mid > 24
$$

Notice that among the given options, only $-26$ satisfies this condition.

Therefore, $k = -26$ is the correct answer.

## Determining Parameter Values in Factored Quadratic Equations With Real Solutions

**Example:** Given that $x(2x + 12) = -k$, where $k$ is an **integer** constant, has two distinct real solutions, what is the greatest possible value of $k$?

**Explanation**

A quadratic equation has two distinct real solutions if the discriminant is positive: $\mathcal{D} > 0$.

First, rewrite the equation in standard quadratic form:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x(2x + 12) &= -k \\
2x^{2} + 12x &= -k \\
2x^{2} + 12x + k &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

The coefficients of our quadratic equation are the following:

$$
a = 2, \qquad b = 12, \qquad c = k
$$

So, computing the discriminant, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
D &= b^{2} - 4ac \\
&= (12)^{2} - 4(2)(k) \\
&= 144 - 8k
\end{aligned}
$$

To have distinct real solutions, we need $\mathcal{D} > 0{:}$

$$
\begin{bmatrix}144 - 8k & > 0 \\ 144 & > 8k \\ 8k & < 144 \\ k & < \frac{144}{8} \\ k & < 18\end{bmatrix}
$$

Since $k$ must be an integer, we conclude that the greatest possible value is $17$.
