## Introduction

Quadratic equations have no real solutions when their discriminant is negative.

In this topic, we'll practice identifying and using this condition.

---

<a id="determining-parameter-values-in-quadratic-equations-with-no-real-solutions"></a>
## Determining Parameter Values in Quadratic Equations With No Real Solutions

**Example:** Given that $2x^2 + qx + 50 = 0$, where $q$ is an **integer** constant, has no real solution, what is the least possible value of $q$?

**Explanation**

A quadratic equation has no real roots if the discriminant is negative: $\mathcal{D} < 0$.

The coefficients of our quadratic equation are the following:

$$
a = 2, \qquad b = q, \qquad c = 50
$$

So, computing the discriminant, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
D &= b^{2} - 4ac \\
&= (q)^{2} - 4(2)(50) \\
&= q^{2} - 400
\end{aligned}
$$

To have no real solutions, we need $\mathcal{D} < 0{:}$

$$
q^{2} - 400\begin{vmatrix}< 0 \\ q^{2}\end{vmatrix}< 400; \sqrt{q^{2}} \mid < \sqrt{400}; \mid q \mid \mid < 20; - 20 < q \mid < 20
$$

Since $q$ must be an integer, we conclude that the least possible value is $-19$.

## Determining Parameter Values in Nonstandard Quadratic Equations With No Real Solutions

**Example:** $3y^{2} + 6y = k$
Given that in the equation above, $k$ is a constant and the equation has no real solutions, which of the following could be the value of $k$?

1. $-2$
2. $0$
3. $-4$
4. $3$

**Explanation**

For a quadratic equation

$$
ay^2 + by + c = 0
$$

the discriminant is

$$
\mathcal{D} = b^2 - 4ac
$$

- If $\mathcal{D} > 0$, there are two distinct real solutions.
- If $\mathcal{D} = 0$, there is one real solution.
- If $\mathcal{D} < 0$, there are no real solutions (the solutions are complex).

First, rewrite the given equation in standard form:

$$
3y^{2} + 6y - k = 0
$$

We have $a = 3$, $b = 6$, and $c = -k$.

Computing the discriminant, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
D &= b^{2} - 4ac \\
&= 6^{2} - 4(3)(-k) \\
&= 36 + 12k
\end{aligned}
$$

For no real solutions, we require $\mathcal{D} < 0{:}$

$$
\begin{bmatrix}36 + 12k & < 0 \\ 12k & < - 36 \\ k & < - 3\end{bmatrix}
$$

So, any $k$ less than $-3$ would work.
Notice that among the given options, only $-4$ is less than $-3$.

Therefore, $k = -4$ is the correct answer.

## Determining Parameter Values in Factored Quadratic Equations With No Real Solutions

**Example:** Given that $x(px - 24) = -9$, where $p$ is an **integer** constant, has no real solutions, what is the least possible value of $p$?

**Explanation**

A quadratic equation has no real solutions if the discriminant is negative: $\mathcal{D} < 0$.

First, rewrite the equation in standard quadratic form:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x(px - 24) &= -9 \\
px^{2} - 24x &= -9 \\
px^{2} - 24x + 9 &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

The coefficients of our quadratic equation are the following:

$$
a = p, \qquad b = -24, \qquad c = 9
$$

So, computing the discriminant, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
D &= b^{2} - 4ac \\
&= (-24)^{2} - 4(p)(9) \\
&= 576 - 36p
\end{aligned}
$$

To have no real solutions, we need $\mathcal{D} < 0{:}$

$$
\begin{bmatrix}576 - 36p & < 0 \\ -36p & < - 576 \\ p & > \frac{-576}{-36} \\ p & > 16\end{bmatrix}
$$

Since $p$ must be an integer, we conclude that the least possible value is $17$.
