## Introduction

Quadratic equations have exactly one real solution when their discriminant equals zero.

In this topic, we'll practice identifying and using this condition.

---

<a id="cases-that-involve-solving-a-linear-equation"></a>
## Cases That Involve Solving a Linear Equation

**Example:** Given that $kx^2 + 14x + k = 0$, where $k$ is a **positive** constant, has exactly one real solution, what is the value of $k$?

**Explanation**

A quadratic equation has exactly one real root if the discriminant is equal to zero:

$$
\mathcal{D} = 0
$$

The coefficients of our quadratic equation are the following:

$$
a = k, \qquad b = 14, \qquad c = k
$$

So, computing the discriminant, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
D &= b^{2} - 4ac \\
&= (14)^{2} - 4(k)(k) \\
&= 196 - 4k^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

To have exactly one real solution, we need

$$
\mathcal{D} = 0{:}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
196 - 4k^{2} &= 0 \\
196 &= 4k^{2} \\
k^{2} &= 49 \\
\mid k \mid &= 7
\end{aligned}
$$

Since $k$ must be positive, we conclude that $k=7$.

## Cases That Involve Solving a Quadratic Equation

**Example:** If $ax^2+3x-1=0$ has exactly one solution, what is the value of $a$?

**Explanation**

To solve this problem, let us apply the quadratic formula. The known coefficients of our quadratic equation are

$$
b = 3,\qquad c = -1
$$

So, we substitute these values into the quadratic formula:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x &= \frac{-b ± \sqrt{b^{2} - 4ac}}{2a} \\
&= (-3 ± \sqrt{(-3)^{2} - 4(a)(-1)})/((2)(a)) \\
&= \frac{-3 ± \sqrt{9 + 4a}}{2a}
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, in order for there to be exactly one solution, the discriminant must be zero. Hence

$$
\begin{aligned}
0 &= 9 + 4a \\
4a &= -9 \\
a &= -\frac{9}{4}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Cases That Reduce to Solving a Quadratic by Factoring

**Example:** Given that $-3kx^2 - 2kx - 5 = 0$, where $k$ is a **positive** constant, has exactly one real solution, what is the value of $k$?

**Explanation**

A quadratic equation has exactly one real root if the discriminant is equal to zero:

$$
\mathcal{D} = 0
$$

The coefficients of our quadratic equation are the following:

$$
a = -3k, \qquad b = -2k, \qquad c = -5
$$

So, computing the discriminant, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
D &= b^{2} - 4ac \\
&= (-2k)^{2} - 4(-3k)(-5) \\
&= 4k^{2} - 60k
\end{aligned}
$$

To have exactly one real solution, we need

$$
\mathcal{D} = 0{:}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
4k^{2} - 60k &= 0 \\
4k(k - 15) &= 0 \\
k &= 0, 15
\end{aligned}
$$

Since $k$ must be positive, we conclude that $k=15$.
