## Introduction

Recognizing when an equation can be reduced to a perfect square helps to solve it quickly without factoring or using the quadratic formula.

In this lesson, we’ll practice isolating, simplifying, and solving perfect square equations.

## Perfect Square Equations Where the Quadratic Term Is Isolated

**Example:** Consider the equation
$c^{2} = 13 \cdot 13$.
What is the negative solution to this equation?

**Explanation**

First, we rewrite the given equation as
$c^2 = 13^2$.

Next, we take the square root of both sides of the equation and solve the resulting absolute value equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
c^{2} &= 13^{2} \\
\sqrt{c^{2}} &= \sqrt{13^{2}} \\
\begin{vmatrix}c & &= & 13\end{vmatrix} \\
\mid c \mid &= 13 \\
c &= ± 13
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the **negative** solution to the given equation is

$$
c = \bbox[4pt,border: 1px solid lightgray]{-13}
$$

## Isolating the Quadratic Term in a Perfect Square Equation

**Example:** What is the negative solution to the equation $20n^2 = 20 \cdot 144$?

**Explanation**

First, we divide both sides of the equation by $20$ and simplify:

$$
\begin{aligned}
20n^{2} &= 20 \cdot 144 \\
\frac{20n^{2}}{20} &= \frac{20 \cdot 144}{20} \\
\frac{20n^{2}}{20} &= \frac{20 \cdot 144}{20} \\
n^{2} &= 144
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we take the square root of both sides of the equation and solve the resulting absolute value equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
n^{2} &= 144 \\
\sqrt{n^{2}} &= \sqrt{144} \\
\mid n \mid &= 12 \\
n &= ± 12
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the **negative** solution to the equation is

$$
n = \bbox[4pt,border: 1px solid lightgray]{-12}
$$

## Perfect Square Equations Where a Quadratic Binomial Is Isolated

**Example:** If $(r - 6)^2 = 36$, find the values of $r$.

**Explanation**

We solve the equation using the square root method:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(r - 6)^{2} &= 36 \\
\sqrt{(r - 6)^{2}} &= \sqrt{36} \\
\mid r - 6 \mid &= 6 \\
r - 6 &= ± 6 \\
r &= 6 ± 6
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we simplify the two solutions:

$$
\begin{aligned}
r &= 6 - 6 = 0 \\
r &= 6 + 6 = 12
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the values of $r$ are $0$ and $12$.

## Rearranging a Perfect Square Equation to Isolate the Quadratic Term

**Example:** Consider the equation
$y^2 - 10 = 26$.
What is the positive solution to this equation?

**Explanation**

First, we isolate the $y^2$ on one side of the equation. To do this, we add $10$ to both sides of the equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
y^{2} - 10 &= 26 \\
y^{2} - 10 + 10 &= 26 + 10 \\
y^{2} &= 36
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we take the square root of both sides of the equation and solve the resulting absolute value equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
y^{2} &= 36 \\
\sqrt{y^{2}} &= \sqrt{36} \\
\mid y \mid &= 6 \\
y &= ± 6
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the **positive** solution to the given equation is $y = 6$.
