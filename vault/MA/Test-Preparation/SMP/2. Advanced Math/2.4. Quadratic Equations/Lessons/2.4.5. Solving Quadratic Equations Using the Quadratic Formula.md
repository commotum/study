## Introduction

On the math section of the SAT exam, we may face quadratics that don’t easily factor. In such cases, developing fluency with the quadratic formula can save crucial time.

Below, we'll practice writing quadratic equations in standard form and applying the quadratic formula.

## Applying the Quadratic Formula to Equations in Standard Form

**Example:** What are the solutions to the equation $2x^2 + 5x - 1 = 0$?

**Explanation**

The standard form of a quadratic equation is

$$
ax^2 + bx + c = 0,\qquad a \neq 0
$$

For the given equation, we have $a=2$, $b=5$, and $c=-1$. Substituting the values for $a$, $b$, and $c$ into the quadratic formula, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
x &= \frac{-b ± \sqrt{b^{2} - 4ac}}{2a} \\
&= (-5 ± \sqrt{5^{2} - 4(2)(-1)})/(2(2)) \\
&= \frac{-5 ± \sqrt{25 + 8}}{4} \\
&= \frac{-5 ± \sqrt{33}}{4}
\end{aligned}
$$

We cannot simplify the two solutions. Therefore, the solutions are

$$
x = \dfrac{-5 \pm \sqrt{33}}{4}
$$

## Applying the Quadratic Formula to Equations in Nonstandard Form

**Example:** Which of the following is a solution to the equation
$v^2 + 1 = 2v + 3$?

1. $v = 1 + \sqrt{3}$
2. $v = 1 - \sqrt{5}$
3. $v = \dfrac{1 + \sqrt{3}}{2}$
4. $v = \dfrac{1 - \sqrt{3}}{2}$

**Explanation**

The standard form of a quadratic equation is

$$
av^2 + bv + c = 0,\qquad a \neq 0
$$

First, we move all the terms to the left-hand side and simplify to rewrite the equation in standard quadratic form:

$$
v^2 - 2v - 2 = 0
$$

For the last equation, we have $a=1$, $b=-2$, and $c=-2$. Substituting the values ​​for $a$, $b$, and $c$ into the quadratic formula, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
v &= \frac{-b ± \sqrt{b^{2} - 4ac}}{2a} \\
&= (- (-2) ± \sqrt{(-2)^{2} - 4(1)(-2)})/(2(1)) \\
&= \frac{2 ± \sqrt{4 + 8}}{2} \\
&= \frac{2 ± \sqrt{12}}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, notice that

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{12} &= \sqrt{4 \cdot 3} \\
&= \sqrt{4} \cdot \sqrt{3} \\
&= 2 \cdot \sqrt{3} \\
&= 2\sqrt{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, we can further simplify the solution to our equation as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
v &= \frac{2 ± \sqrt{12}}{2} \\
&= \frac{2 ± 2\sqrt{3}}{2} \\
&= 1 ± \sqrt{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the solutions to the equation are

$$
v = 1 - \sqrt{3}
$$

and

$$
v = 1 + \sqrt{3}
$$

Therefore, among the given answer options, the correct one is

$$
v = 1 + \sqrt{3}
$$

## Applying the Quadratic Formula and Simplifying the Radical Part of the Solution

**Example:** One solution to the equation
$x^2 - 6x + 2 = 0$
can be written in the form $3 - \sqrt{k}$, where $k$ is a constant. What is the value of $k$?

**Explanation**

The standard form of a quadratic equation is

$$
ax^2 + bx + c = 0,\qquad a \neq 0
$$

For the given equation, we have $a=1$, $b=-6$, and $c=2$. Substituting the values ​​for $a$, $b$, and $c$ into the quadratic formula, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
x &= \frac{-b ± \sqrt{b^{2} - 4ac}}{2a} \\
&= (- (-6) ± \sqrt{(-6)^{2} - 4(1)(2)})/(2(1)) \\
&= \frac{6 ± \sqrt{28}}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, notice that

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{28} &= \sqrt{4 \cdot 7} \\
&= \sqrt{4} \cdot \sqrt{7} \\
&= 2 \cdot \sqrt{7} \\
&= 2\sqrt{7}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, we can further simplify the solution to our equation as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x &= \frac{6 ± \sqrt{28}}{2} \\
&= \frac{6 ± 2\sqrt{7}}{2} \\
&= 3 ± \sqrt{7}
\end{aligned}
$$

Comparing the smaller solution with the given expression $3 - \sqrt{k}$, we have

$$
3 - \sqrt{\color{blue}7} = 3 - \sqrt{\color{blue}k}
$$

Therefore,

$$
{\color{blue}k} = {\color{blue}7}
$$
