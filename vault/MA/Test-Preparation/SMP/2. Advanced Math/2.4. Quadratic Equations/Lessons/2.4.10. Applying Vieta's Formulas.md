## Introduction

Vieta’s formulas are often helpful in algebra problems that ask for the sum or product of roots of a quadratic equation, but don't require the roots themselves. By utilizing Vieta's formulas, we can solve these problems quickly and avoid lengthy computations.

In this topic, we'll practice applying these formulas, helping us master one of the quickest shortcuts in quadratic problem-solving.

## Finding Sums of Solutions to Quadratic Equations in Standard Form

**Example:** What is the sum of the solutions to the equation $x^2 - 12x - 4 = 0$?

**Explanation**

The standard form of a quadratic equation is

$$
ax^2 + bx + c = 0,\qquad a \neq 0
$$

For the given equation, we have $a=1$, $b=-12$, and $c=-4$.

Now, recall that

$$
-\dfrac{b}{a}
$$

gives the value of the sum of the solutions to the quadratic equation. Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
-\frac{b}{a} &= - ((-12))/(1) = 12
\end{aligned}
$$

## Determining Unknown Parameters Given the Product of Solutions to a Quadratic Equation

**Example:** In the equation
$26x^{2} + (26m + n)x + m n = 0$,
where $m$ and $n$ are positive constants, the product of the solutions is equal to $k m n$, where $k$ is a constant. What is the value of $k$?

**Explanation**

The standard form of a quadratic equation is

$$
ax^2 + bx + c = 0,\qquad a \neq 0
$$

For the given equation, we have $a=26$,

$$
b=26m + n
$$

and $c=m n$.

Now, recall that

$$
\dfrac{c}{a}
$$

gives the value of the product of the solutions to the quadratic equation. Therefore,

$$
\dfrac{c}{a} = \dfrac{mn}{26} = \dfrac{1}{26} m n
$$

Comparing the last value with the given expression $k m n$, we get

$$
{\color{blue}\dfrac{1}{26}} mn = {\color{blue}k} mn
$$

Therefore,

$$
{\color{blue}k} = {\color{blue}\dfrac{1}{26}}
$$

## Finding Sums of Solutions to Quadratic Equations in Nonstandard Form

**Example:** What is the sum of the solutions of the equation $x(x - 3) - 14 = 3x(x - 6)$?

**Explanation**

The standard form of a quadratic equation is

$$
ax^2 + bx + c = 0,\qquad a \neq 0
$$

First, by expanding the products on both the left-hand side and the right-hand side and then simplifying, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
x(x - 3) - 14 &= 3x(x - 6) \\
x^{2} - 3x - 14 &= 3x^{2} - 18x
\end{aligned}
$$

Rewriting the above equation as a quadratic equation in standard form, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^{2} - 3x - 14 &= 3x^{2} - 18x \\
- 2x^{2} + 15x - 14 &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

For the last equation, we have $a=-2$, $b=15$, and $c=-14$.

Now, recall that

$$
-\dfrac{b}{a}
$$

gives the value of the sum of the solutions to the quadratic equation. Therefore,

$$
-\dfrac{b}{a} = -\dfrac{15}{(-2)} = \dfrac{15}{2}
$$

Therefore, the sum of the solutions to our original equation is

$$
\dfrac{15}{2}
$$

## Determining Unknown Parameters Given the Sum of Solutions to a Quadratic Equation

**Example:** In the equation
$81x^{2} - (27p+9q)x + pq = 0$,
where $p$ and $q$ are positive constants, the sum of the solutions is equal to $k(3p + q)$, where $k$ is a constant. What is the value of $k$?

**Explanation**

The standard form of a quadratic equation is

$$
ax^2 + bx + c = 0,\qquad a \neq 0
$$

For the given equation, we have $a=81$,

$$
b=-(27p+9q)
$$

and $c=pq$.

Now, recall that

$$
-\dfrac{b}{a}
$$

gives the value of the sum of the solutions to the quadratic equation. Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
-\frac{b}{a} &= - (- (27p + 9q))/(81) \\
&= \frac{27p + 9q}{81} \\
&= (9(3p + q))/(81) \\
&= \frac{3p + q}{9} \\
&= \frac{1}{9}(3p + q)
\end{aligned}
$$

Comparing the last value with the given expression $k(3p + q)$, we get

$$
{\color{blue}\dfrac{1}{9}}(3p+q) = {\color{blue}k} (3p+q)
$$

Therefore,

$$
{\color{blue}k} = {\color{blue}\dfrac{1}{9}}
$$
