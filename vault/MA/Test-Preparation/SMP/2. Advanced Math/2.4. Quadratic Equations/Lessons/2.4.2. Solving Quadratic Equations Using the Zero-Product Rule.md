## Solving Factored Quadratic Equations

**Example:** Find the solution to the equation
$(4z - 5)(z + 6) = 0$

**Explanation**

To find the solutions to the given equation, we use the zero-product property. This property states that if a product of factors equals zero, then at least one of the factors must be zero.

Setting each factor equal to zero, we get

$$
4z - 5 = 0 \qquad \text{or} \qquad z + 6 = 0
$$

Now, we proceed to solve each equation separately.

Solving the first equation, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
4z - 5 &= 0 \\
4z &= 5 \\
z &= \frac{5}{4}
\end{aligned}
$$

Solving the second equation, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
z + 6 &= 0 \\
z &= -6
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the solutions to the given equation are

$$
z = -6, \, \dfrac{5}{4}
$$

## Solving a Quadratic Equation by Rearranging and Applying the Zero-Product Rule

**Example:** Given the equation
$(v - 10)(v + 10) - 12 = -12$,
what is its negative solution?

**Explanation**

We start by adding $12$ to both sides and simplifying:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(v - 10)(v + 10) - 12 &= -12 \\
(v - 10)(v + 10) - 12 + 12 &= -12 + 12 \\
(v - 10)(v + 10) &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

To find the solutions to the last equation, we apply the zero-product property. This property states that if a product of factors equals zero, then at least one of the factors must be zero.

Setting each factor equal to zero, we get

- $v - 10 = 0$ gives $v = 10$, and
- $v + 10 = 0$ gives $v = -10$.

Therefore, we conclude that the negative solution to the given equation is $v = -10$.
