## Introduction

Solving quadratic equations by factoring tests the ability to recognize when a quadratic can be factored quickly into linear factors.

In this lesson, we’ll practice rewriting, factoring, and solving quadratic equations efficiently, developing the fluency needed to identify patterns and avoid time-consuming methods like the quadratic formula.

## Solving Quadratic Equations by Factoring

**Example:** If $k$ is a solution of the equation $t^2 - t - 30 = 0$ and $k > 0$, what is the value of $k$?

**Explanation**

To factor the left-hand side, we need to find two numbers that multiply to $-30$ and add to $-1$. These two numbers are $-6$ and $5$, so we can factor the left-hand side as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
t^{2} - t - 30 &= 0 \\
t^{2}- 6t + 5t_(⏟)_(-t) - 30 &= 0 \\
t(t - 6) + 5(t - 6) &= 0 \\
(t - 6)(t + 5) &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

Applying the zero product rule, we have the following:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}t - 6 = 0 & ⇒t = 6 \\ t + 5 = 0 & ⇒t =-5\end{bmatrix}
$$

Finally, we are given that $k$ is a solution to our equation, with $k \gt 0$. Therefore, we conclude that $k = 6$.

## Solving Quadratic Equations by Multiplying by -1 and Then Factoring

**Example:** What is the positive solution to the equation $-p^2 - 3p + 4 = 0$?

**Explanation**

First, we rewrite our equation in standard form and with a positive leading coefficient:

$$
\begin{aligned}
-p^{2} - 3p + 4 &= 0 \\
p^{2} + 3p - 4 &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

To factor the left-hand side, we need to find two numbers that multiply to $-4$ and add to $3$. These two numbers are $4$ and $-1$, so we can factor the left-hand side as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
p^{2} + 3p - 4 &= 0 \\
p^{2}+ 4p - p_(⏟)_(3p) - 4 &= 0 \\
p(p + 4) - 1(p + 4) &= 0 \\
(p + 4)(p - 1) &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

Applying the zero product rule, we have the following:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}p + 4 = 0 & ⇒p =-4 \\ p - 1 = 0 & ⇒p = 1\end{bmatrix}
$$

Therefore, the **positive** solution to the given equation is $p = 1$.

## Combining Roots of Quadratic Equations

**Example:** The solutions to $m^2 + 9m + 14 = 0$ are $r$ and $s$, with $r < s$, while the solutions to $m^2 + 5m + 4 = 0$ are $t$ and $u$ with $t < u$. If the solutions to $m^2 + 14m + k = 0$, where $k$ is a constant, are $r + t$ and $s + u$, what is the value of $k$?

**Explanation**

Let's solve the first two equations:

- To factor the left-hand side of $m^2 + 9m + 14 = 0$, we need to find two numbers that multiply to $14$ and add to $9$. These two numbers are $7$ and $2$, so we can factor the left-hand side as follows:
$m^{2} + 9m + 14|= 0; m^{2}+ 7m + 2m_(⏟)_(9m) + 14|= 0; m(m + 7) + 2(m + 7)|= 0; (m + 7)(m + 2)|= 0$
Applying the zero product rule, we have the following:
$\begin{bmatrix}m + 7 = 0 & ⇒m =-7 \\ m + 2 = 0 & ⇒m =-2\end{bmatrix}$
Thus, the solutions $r$ and $s$, with $r < s$, are the following:
$r =-7ands =-2$
- To factor the left-hand side of $m^2 + 5m + 4 = 0$, we need to find two numbers that multiply to $4$ and add to $5$. These two numbers are $1$ and $4$, so we can factor the left-hand side as follows:
$m^{2} + 5m + 4|= 0; m^{2}+ m + 4m_(⏟)_(5m) + 4|= 0; m(m + 1) + 4(m + 1)|= 0; (m + 1)(m + 4)|= 0$
Applying the zero product rule, we have the following:
$\begin{bmatrix}m + 1 = 0 & ⇒m =-1 \\ m + 4 = 0 & ⇒m =-4\end{bmatrix}$
Thus, the solutions $t$ and $u$, with $t < u$, are the following:
$t =-4andu =-1$

Consequently,

$$
\begin{aligned}
r + t &= -7 + (-4) =-11, \\
s + u &= -2 + (-1) =-3
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we are given that both $r+t$ and $s+u$ are solutions to the equation

$$
m^2 + 14m + k = 0
$$

So, substituting

$$
m = s+u = -3
$$

into this equation (it also works to substitute

$$
m=r+t=-11
$$

) and solving for $k$, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
(-3)^{2} + 14(-3) + k &= 0 \\
9 - 42 + k &= 0 \\
- 33 + k &= 0 \\
k &= 33
\end{aligned}
$$
