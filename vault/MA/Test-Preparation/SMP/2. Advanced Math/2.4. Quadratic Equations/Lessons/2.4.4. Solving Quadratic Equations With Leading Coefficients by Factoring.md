## Introduction

Solving quadratic equations with leading coefficients by factoring tests the ability to recognize when a quadratic can be factored quickly into linear factors.

In this lesson, we’ll practice factoring and solving quadratic equations with leading coefficients efficiently, developing the fluency needed to identify patterns and avoid time-consuming methods like the quadratic formula.

## Solving Quadratic Equations by Factoring

**Example:** What is the positive solution to the equation $4y^2 - 17y - 15 = 0$?

Explanation:

To factor the left-hand side, we need two numbers that multiply to $4 \cdot (-15) = -60$ and add to $-17$. These two numbers are $-20$ and $3$, so we can factor the left-hand side as follows:

$4y^{2} - 17y - 15|= 0; 4y^{2}- 20y + 3y_(⏟)_(-17y) - 15|= 0; 4y(y - 5) + 3(y - 5)|= 0; (y - 5)(4y + 3)|= 0$

Applying the zero product rule, we have the following:

$\begin{bmatrix}y - 5 = 0 & ⇒y = 5 \\ 4y + 3 = 0 & ⇒y =-\frac{3}{4}\end{bmatrix}$

Therefore, we conclude that the **positive** solution to the given equation is $y = 5$.

## Solving Quadratic Equations in Nonstandard Form by Factoring

**Example:** What is the positive solution to the equation $-12p^2 =-17p - 5$?

**Explanation**

First, we rewrite our equation in standard form and with a positive leading coefficient:

$$
\begin{aligned}
-12p^{2} &= -17p - 5 \\
- 12p^{2} + 17p + 5 &= 0 \\
12p^{2} - 17p - 5 &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

To factor the left-hand side, we need two numbers that multiply to

$$
12 \cdot (-5) = -60
$$

and add to $-17$. These two numbers are $-20$ and $3$, so we can factor the left-hand side as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
12p^{2} - 17p - 5 &= 0 \\
12p^{2}- 20p + 3p_(⏟)_(-17p) - 5 &= 0 \\
4p(3p - 5) + 1(3p - 5) &= 0 \\
(4p + 1)(3p - 5) &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

Applying the zero product rule, we have the following:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}4p + 1 = 0 & ⇒p =-\frac{1}{4} \\ 3p - 5 = 0 & ⇒p = \frac{5}{3}\end{bmatrix}
$$

Therefore, we conclude that the **positive** solution to the given equation is

$$
p = \dfrac{5}{3}
$$
