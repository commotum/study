## Introduction

In this topic, we will determine unknown parameters in quadratic systems with zero or two solutions involving horizontal or sloped lines, as well as cases where both equations of the system contain parameters.

---

<a id="quadratic-systems-with-horizontal-lines-and-two-intersection-points"></a>
## Quadratic Systems With Horizontal Lines and Two Intersection Points

**Example:** ${y = 4x^{2} - 8x + 7; y = m$

The graphs of the equations given in the system of equations above, where $m$ is an **integer** constant, intersect at two distinct points in the $xy$-plane. What is the least possible value of $m$?

**Explanation**

The line $y = m$ intersects the parabola

$$
y = 4x^2 - 8x + 7
$$

where

$$
4x^2 - 8x + 7 = m
$$

A quadratic equation has two distinct real roots if the discriminant is positive: $\mathcal{D} > 0$.

First, rewrite the equation in standard quadratic form:

$$
\begin{aligned}
4x^{2} - 8x + 7 &= m \\
4x^{2} - 8x + 7 - m &= 0 \\
4x^{2} - 8x + (7 - m) &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

The coefficients of our quadratic equation are the following:

$$
a = 4, \qquad b = -8, \qquad c = 7 - m
$$

So, computing the discriminant, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
D &= b^{2} - 4ac \\
&= (-8)^{2} - 4(4)(7 - m) \\
&= 64 - 112 + 16m \\
&=-48 + 16m
\end{aligned}
$$

For two distinct real solutions, we need $\mathcal{D} > 0{:}$
$\begin{bmatrix}-48 + 16m & > 0 \\ 16m & > 48 \\ m & > 3\end{bmatrix}$

Since $m$ must be an integer, we conclude that the least possible value is
$4$.

## Quadratic Systems With Horizontal Lines and No Intersection Points

**Example:** ${y =-3x^{2} - 6x + 9; y = k$

The graphs of the equations given in the system of equations above, where $k$ is an **integer** constant, don't intersect in the $xy$-plane. What is the smallest possible value of $k$?

**Explanation**

The line $y=k$ intersects the parabola

$$
y = -3x^2 - 6x + 9
$$

where

$$
-3x^2 - 6x + 9 = k
$$

A quadratic equation has no real roots if the discriminant is negative: $\mathcal{D} < 0$.

First, rewrite the equation in standard quadratic form:

$$
\begin{aligned}
-3x^{2} - 6x + 9 &= k \\
- 3x^{2} - 6x + 9 - k &= 0 \\
- 3x^{2} - 6x + (9 - k) &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

The coefficients of our quadratic equation are the following:

$$
a = -3, \qquad b = -6, \qquad c = 9-k
$$

So, computing the discriminant, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
D &= b^{2} - 4ac \\
&= (-6)^{2} - 4(-3)(9 - k) \\
&= 36 + 12(9 - k) \\
&= 36 + 108 - 12k \\
&= 144 - 12k
\end{aligned}
$$

For no real solutions, we need $\mathcal{D} < 0{:}$

$$
\begin{bmatrix}144 - 12k & < 0 \\ -12k & < - 144 \\ 12k & > 144 \\ k & > \frac{144}{12} \\ k & > 12\end{bmatrix}
$$

Since $k$ must be an integer, we conclude that the smallest possible value is $13$.

## Quadratic Systems With Sloped Lines and Two Intersection Points

**Example:** If the graph of the equation $y=3x^2 - 12x + 8$ intersects the line $y=k-3x$, where $k$ is an **integer** constant, at two distinct points in the $xy$-plane, what is the least possible value of $k$?

**Explanation**

The line $y=k-3x$ intersects the parabola

$$
y=3x^2 - 12x + 8
$$

where

$$
3x^2 - 12x + 8 = k - 3x
$$

A quadratic equation has two distinct real roots if the discriminant is positive: $\mathcal{D} > 0$.

First, rewrite the equation in standard quadratic form:

$$
\begin{aligned}
3x^{2} - 12x + 8 &= k - 3x \\
3x^{2} - 12x + 8 + 3x - k &= 0 \\
3x^{2} - 9x + (8 - k) &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

The coefficients of our quadratic equation are the following:

$$
a = 3, \qquad b = -9, \qquad c = 8-k
$$

So, computing the discriminant, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
D &= b^{2} - 4ac \\
&= (-9)^{2} - 4(3)(8 - k) \\
&= 81 - 96 + 12k \\
&=-15 + 12k
\end{aligned}
$$

For two distinct real solutions, we need $\mathcal{D} > 0{:}$

$$
\begin{bmatrix}-15 + 12k & > 0 \\ 12k & > 15 \\ k & > \frac{15}{12} \\ k & > 1.25\end{bmatrix}
$$

Since $k$ must be an integer, we conclude that the least possible value is $2$.

## Quadratic Systems With Sloped Lines and No Intersection Points

**Example:** If the graph of the equation $y=2x^2-4x+8$ doesn't intersect the line $y=x+k$ in the $xy$-plane, where $k$ is an **integer** constant, what is the greatest possible value of $k$?

**Explanation**

The line

$$
y=x+k
$$

intersects the parabola

$$
y = 2x^2 - 4x + 8
$$

where

$$
2x^2 - 4x + 8 = x+k
$$

A quadratic equation has no real roots if the discriminant is negative: $\mathcal{D} < 0$.

First, rewrite the equation in standard quadratic form:

$$
\begin{aligned}
2x^{2} - 4x + 8 &= x + k \\
2x^{2} - 4x + 8 - x - k &= 0 \\
2x^{2} - 5x + (8 - k) &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

The coefficients of our quadratic equation are the following:

$$
a = 2, \qquad b = -5, \qquad c = 8-k
$$

So, computing the discriminant, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
D &= b^{2} - 4ac \\
&= (-5)^{2} - 4(2)(8 - k) \\
&= 25 - 8(8 - k) \\
&= 25 - 64 + 8k \\
&= 8k - 39
\end{aligned}
$$

For no real solutions, we need $\mathcal{D} < 0{:}$

$$
\begin{bmatrix}8k - 39 & < 0 \\ 8k & < 39 \\ k & < \frac{39}{8} \\ k & < 4.875\end{bmatrix}
$$

Since $k$ must be an integer, we conclude that the greatest possible value is $4$.
