## Introduction

An important concept that features in the math section of the SAT exam is identifying when a line and a parabola intersect at exactly one point.

In this topic, we will determine unknown parameters in quadratic systems with one solution involving horizontal or sloped lines, as well as cases where both linear and quadratic equations within a system contain parameters.

---

<a id="determining-unknown-parameters-in-quadratic-systems-with-horizontal-lines"></a>
## Determining Unknown Parameters in Quadratic Systems With Horizontal Lines

**Example:** ${y =-2x^{2} + 20x - 60; y = k$

If the graphs of the equations given in the system of equations above, where $k$ is a constant, intersect at exactly one point in the $xy$-plane, what is the value of $k$?

**Explanation**

The line $y=k$ intersects the parabola

$$
y=-2x^2 + 20x - 60
$$

where

$$
-2x^2 + 20x - 60 = k
$$

We need to determine the value of $k$ such that this equation has one real solution.

A quadratic equation has exactly one real root if the discriminant is zero:

$$
\mathcal{D} = 0
$$

First, rewrite the equation in standard quadratic form:

$$
\begin{aligned}
-2x^{2} + 20x - 60 &= k \\
- 2x^{2} + 20x + (-60 - k) &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

The coefficients of our quadratic equation are the following:

$$
a = -2, \qquad b = 20, \qquad c = -60 - k
$$

So, computing the discriminant, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
D &= b^{2} - 4ac \\
&= (20)^{2} - 4(-2)(-60 - k) \\
&= 400 + 8(-60 - k) \\
&= 400 - 480 - 8k \\
&=-80 - 8k
\end{aligned}
$$

To have exactly one real solution, we need

$$
\mathcal{D} = 0{:}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
-80 - 8k &= 0 \\
- 8k &= 80 \\
k &= -\frac{80}{8} \\
k &= -10
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus, the equation of the line is $y=-10$.

## Determining Unknown Parameters in Quadratic Systems With Sloped Lines

**Example:** ${y =-5x^{2} + 10x - 1; y = 4x - k$

The graphs of the equations given in the system of equations above, where $k$ is a constant, intersect at exactly one point in the $xy$-plane. Find the point of intersection.

**Explanation**

The line $y=4x - k$ intersects the parabola

$$
y=-5x^2 + 10x - 1
$$

where

$$
-5x^2 + 10x - 1 = 4x - k
$$

We need to determine the value of $k$ such that this equation has one real solution.

A quadratic equation has exactly one real root if the discriminant is zero:

$$
\mathcal{D} = 0
$$

First, rewrite the equation in standard quadratic form:

$$
\begin{aligned}
-5x^{2} + 10x - 1 &= 4x - k \\
- 5x^{2} + 10x - 1 - 4x + k &= 0 \\
- 5x^{2} + 6x + (k - 1) &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

The coefficients of our quadratic equation are the following:

$$
a = -5, \qquad b = 6, \qquad c = k-1
$$

So, computing the discriminant, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
D &= b^{2} - 4ac \\
&= 6^{2} - 4(-5)(k - 1) \\
&= 36 + 20(k - 1) \\
&= 36 + 20k - 20 \\
&= 16 + 20k
\end{aligned}
$$

To have exactly one real solution, we need

$$
\mathcal{D} = 0{:}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
16 + 20k &= 0 \\
20k &= -16 \\
k &= -\frac{16}{20} \\
k &= -\frac{4}{5}
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus, the equation of the line is

$$
y=4x-\left(-\dfrac{4}{5}\right)=4x+\dfrac{4}{5}
$$

To find the $x$-coordinate, we solve the original quadratic equation, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
-5x^{2} + 6x + (-\frac{4}{5} - 1) &= 0 \\
- 5x^{2} + 6x - \frac{9}{5} &= 0 \\
- 25x^{2} + 30x - 9 &= 0 \\
- (25x^{2} - 30x + 9) &= 0 \\
- (5x - 3)^{2} &= 0 \\
5x - 3 &= 0 \\
x &= \frac{3}{5}
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we find the $y$-coordinate by substituting

$$
x=\dfrac{3}{5}
$$

into one of the two equations and solving:

$$
\begin{aligned}
y &= 4(\frac{3}{5}) + \frac{4}{5} \\
&= \frac{12}{5} + \frac{4}{5} \\
&= \frac{16}{5}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the point of intersection is

$$
\Big(\dfrac{3}{5},\dfrac{16}{5}\Big)
$$

## Quadratic Systems With Unknown Parameters in Both Equations

**Example:** ${y - m = 4x - 2; y + m = x^{2} + 2x$

In the given system of equations, $m$ is a constant. The system has exactly one distinct real solution. What is the value of $m$?

**Explanation**

We first rewrite the system by isolating both equations for $y{:}$

$$
{y = 4x - 2 + m; y = x^{2} + 2x - m
$$

Since both equations are now solved for $y$, we set them equal and put in standard form:

$$
\begin{aligned}
4x - 2 + m &= x^{2} + 2x - m \\
x^{2} - 2x + (2 - 2m) &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

We may now compute the discriminant with the parameters $a=1$, $b=-2$, and $c=2 - 2m{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
D &= b^{2} - 4ac \\
&= (-2)^{2} - 4(1)(2 - 2m) \\
&= 4 - 8 + 8m \\
&= 8m - 4
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, since we need a single solution, we require the discriminant to be $0$. We obtain that

$$
\begin{aligned}
0 &= 8m - 4 \\
8m &= 4 \\
m &= \frac{1}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Hence, the value of $m$ is

$$
\dfrac{1}{2}
$$
