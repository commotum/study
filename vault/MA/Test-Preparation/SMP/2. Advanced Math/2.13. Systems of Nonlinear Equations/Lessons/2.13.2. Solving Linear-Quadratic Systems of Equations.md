## Solving Systems Containing Quadratics in Standard Form

**Example:** ${y = 3x - 5; y =-2x^{2} + 3x + 3$

If $(x,y)$ is a solution to the system of equations given above, what is the smallest possible value of $x$?

**Explanation**

First, notice that we may factor the system of equations as follows:

$$
{y = 3x - 5; y =-2x^{2} + 3x + 3
$$

Since both equations are solved for $y$, we set them equal and solve for $x{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
3x - 5 &= -2x^{2} + 3x + 3 \\
0 &= -2x^{2} + 8 \\
x^{2} &= 4 \\
x &= ± 2
\end{aligned}
$$

Applying the zero product rule, we have

$$
x=-2\qquad\text{or}\qquad x=2
$$

Since $-2<2$, the smallest possible value of $x$ is $-2$.

## Solving Systems Containing Perfect Square Quadratics

**Example:** In the $xy$-plane, what is the $y$-coordinate of the point of intersection of the graphs of $y = (x - 12)^2$ and $y = 24x - 432$?

**Explanation**

To find the coordinates of the point of intersection, we need to solve the following system of equations:

$$
{y = (x - 12)^{2}; y = 24x - 432
$$

Since both equations are solved for $y$, we set them equal and put in standard form:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x - 12)^{2} &= 24x - 432 \\
x^{2} - 24x + 144 &= 24x - 432 \\
x^{2} - 48x + 576 &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, notice that the left-hand side is a perfect square. It just needs to be factored:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^{2} - 48x + 576 &= 0 \\
x^{2} - 2 \cdot 24 \cdot x + 24^{2} &= 0 \\
(x - 24)^{2} &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

Applying the zero product rule yields $x=24$, and so we substitute $x = 24$ into either equation to find the corresponding $y$-value. We use

$$
y = 24x - 432{:}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
y &= 24x - 432 \\
&= 24(24) - 432 \\
&= 576 - 432 \\
&= 144
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the value of $y$ is $144$.

## Solving Systems by Factoring a Common Binomial

**Example:** ${y = (x + 3)(x - 6); y = 9x - 54$

What is the ordered pair $(x, y)$ that satisfies the given system of equations?

**Explanation**

First, we write both equations of the given system in factored form:

$$
{y = (x + 3)(x - 6); y = 9(x - 6)
$$

Notice that $x-6$ is a common factor in both equations. To find the solution, we set the two expressions for $y$ equal to each other and factor:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x + 3)(x - 6) &= 9(x - 6) \\
(x + 3)(x - 6) - 9(x - 6) &= 0 \\
(x - 6)[(x + 3) - 9] &= 0 \\
(x - 6)(x - 6) &= 0 \\
(x - 6)^{2} &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

Applying the zero product rule then yields

$$
\begin{aligned}
x - 6 &= 0 \\
x &= 6
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we substitute $x = 6$ into either equation to find the corresponding $y$-value. Using

$$
y = 9(x - 6)
$$

we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
y &= 9(x - 6) \\
&= 9(6 - 6) \\
&= 9(0) \\
&= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the solution to the system is $(6, 0)$.
