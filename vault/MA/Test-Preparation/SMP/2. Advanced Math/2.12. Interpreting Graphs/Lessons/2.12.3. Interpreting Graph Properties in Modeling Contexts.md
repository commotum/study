## Introduction

A typical question type on the SAT math section involves interpreting key features of graphs in real-world contexts.

In this topic, we will practice determining the meaning of points and interpreting the $x$- and $y$-intercepts in terms of the situation they represent.

## Interpreting a Point on a Graph

**Example:** A football is punted into the air. The graph shows the football’s height above the ground, in meters, at time $x$ seconds after it is kicked. What is the best interpretation of the marked point $(2,\ 19.2)$?

![](<../Source/2.12.3. Interpreting Graph Properties in Modeling Contexts/Images/103a6981938c5b7de0cbd0ef5f085459.png>)

**Explanation**

We begin by interpreting each coordinate of the point $(2,\ 19.2){:}$

- $x = 2$ represents the time in seconds after the football was kicked.
- $y = 19.2$ represents the football’s height above the ground in meters at that moment.

Therefore, the point $(2,\ 19.2)$ indicates the following:

> $2$ seconds after it was kicked, the football’s height above the ground is $19.2$ meters.

## Interpreting the X-Intercept of a Graph

**Example:** A soccer ball is kicked into the air. The graph shows the ball’s height above the ground, $y$, in meters, $t$ seconds after it was kicked. What is the best interpretation of the $x$-intercept of the graph?

![](<../Source/2.12.3. Interpreting Graph Properties in Modeling Contexts/Images/35fa19be11f35eaf167b48a21fad94c4.png>)

**Explanation**

The $x$-intercept of the graph is the point where the graph intersects the $x$-axis. This happens when the height is $0$, which corresponds to the ball reaching the ground.

From the graph, we observe that the $x$-intercept occurs at approximately $t = 3$, which represents the amount of time that passed before the ball hit the ground.

Therefore, the best interpretation of the $x$-intercept of the graph is the following:

> The ball hits the ground at $3$ seconds.

## Interpreting the Y-Intercept of a Graph

**Example:** The graph shown gives the estimated number of bacteria in a refrigerated lab culture as a function of the number of hours since the culture was removed from the fridge.
What is the best interpretation of the $y$-intercept of the graph in this context?

![](<../Source/2.12.3. Interpreting Graph Properties in Modeling Contexts/Images/cb63d058f4b755a465bf2b57e0d212f7.png>)

**Explanation**

The $y$-intercept of a graph of $y=f(x)$ represents the value of $y$ when $x=0$.

In this context, the variable $x$ represents the number of hours since the culture was removed from the fridge, and $y$ is the number of bacteria present in the culture.

So the $y$-intercept represents the number of bacteria *at the moment the culture was removed from the fridge*, i.e., when $x = 0$.

Looking at the graph, we can see that the point $(0, 340)$ lies on the curve. This means there were $340$ bacteria at the start.

Therefore, the correct interpretation is as follows:

> The estimated number of bacteria in the culture was $340$ when it was removed from the fridge.
