## Introduction

A common theme on the SAT math section is finding $x$- and $y$-intercepts from graphs of functions. Here, we will interpret graphs to identify where functions cross the coordinate axes.

---

## Identifying X-Intercepts From Graphs

**Example:** What is the $x$-intercept of the graph shown?

![](<../Source/2.12.1. Identifying Intercepts of Functions From Graphs/Images/9ed8dab7e1e4a8c32bb48376ed3c8789.png>)

**Explanation**

The $x$-intercept of a graph is the point where the curve crosses the $x$-axis. This occurs when $y = 0$.

From the graph, we find that the $x$-intercept is the point $(-3, 0)$.

## Identifying the Definition of the Y-Intercept

**Example:** In the $xy$-plane, the $y$-coordinate of the $y$-intercept of the graph of the function $m$ is $e$. Which of the following must be equal to $e$?

1. $m(0)$
2. $m(1)$
3. $m(2)$
4. $m(3)$

**Explanation**

To find the $y$-coordinate of the $y$-intercept of a function, we substitute $x = 0$ into the function and solve for $y=m(0)$.

Therefore, since the $y$-coordinate of the $y$-intercept is given as $e$, we must have
$e = m(0)$.
