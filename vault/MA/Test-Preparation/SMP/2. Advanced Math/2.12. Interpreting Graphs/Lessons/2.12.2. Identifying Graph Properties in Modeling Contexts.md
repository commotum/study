## Introduction

The math section of the SAT includes questions that require us to interpret graphs modeling real-world relationships.

In this topic, we'll practice identifying points corresponding to specific descriptions, determining initial values, recognizing intervals where a function increases or decreases, and describing changes in a function’s value based on its graph.

---

## Identifying a Point on a Graph Corresponding to a Description

**Example:** The graph shows the predicted value $y$, in dollars, of a particular refrigerator $x$ years after it is first purchased. Which of the following is closest to the predicted value of the refrigerator $2$ years after it is first purchased?

![](<../Source/2.12.2. Identifying Graph Properties in Modeling Contexts/Images/711fed7c8c5a196deede1fa5bf1992f3.png>)

**Explanation**

We want the predicted value at $x=2$. Since $x$ counts years after purchase and $y$ gives the value, we read the point on the curve above $x=2$ and project it to the $y$-axis.

From the graph, the point above $x=2$ lies between the $5{,}000$ and $10{,}000$ marks on the $y$-axis. From the given options, the closest estimate is $\[math]7{,}300$.

## Identifying an Initial Value From a Graph

**Example:** The graph models the number of bike rentals recorded by a city program $x$ months after the start of January $2022$, where $0 \leq x \leq 8$. According to the model, what is the predicted number of bike rentals recorded at the start of January $2022$?

![](<../Source/2.12.2. Identifying Graph Properties in Modeling Contexts/Images/5314fa0a5b1d3ffa9b4ef26d499e8b46.png>)

**Explanation**

We want to predict the number of bike rentals recorded at the start of January $2022$.

Since $x$ represents the number of months after the start of January $2022$ and $y$ is the number of rentals, the $y$-intercept ($x=0$) corresponds to the number at the beginning of that month.

From the graph, we note that the curve passes through $(0,10)$. Substituting $x=0$ gives $y=10$, so the model predicts $10$ rentals at the start of January $2022$.

## Identifying Increasing or Decreasing Intervals in Context

**Example:** An archer shoots an arrow straight upward. The graph shown models the arrow’s height above the ground, $y$, in meters, $x$ seconds after release. What is the intervals of time was the arrow falling throughout the entire interval?

![](<../Source/2.12.2. Identifying Graph Properties in Modeling Contexts/Images/d495cce626f445a16198e8dd13361e8e.png>)

**Explanation**

We want to find when the height was decreasing continuously. The graph is a downward-opening parabola with a vertex at $x=2.8$ seconds.

The arrow rises until the peak at $x=2.8$, then descends. So, it was falling continuously for

$$
2.8\le x\le 5.2
$$

Among the given choices, the only interval lying entirely within this falling section is

$$
\text{From} x = 3 \text{to} x = 5
$$

## Identifying a Change in a Function's Value From a Graph

**Example:** A streaming platform tracks its subscriber count. The graph shows the number of subscribers, $y$, in thousands, $x$ months after a new series premiered, where $0 \le x \le 5$. What is the best describes the increase in subscribers from $x = 0$ to $x = 3$?

![](<../Source/2.12.2. Identifying Graph Properties in Modeling Contexts/Images/73f5dfd82b16dccc793c1ed6e577cefe.png>)

**Explanation**

From the graph, we have:

- At $x = 0{:}$ the number of subscribers is $3$ thousands.
- At $x = 3{:}$ the number of subscribers is approximately $9$ thousands.

To find how many times larger the value at $x = 3$ is compared to $x = 0$, we divide the number of subscribers at $x = 3$ by the number at $x = 0{:}$

$$
\frac{9{,}000}{3{,}000} = 3
$$

Therefore, the best interpretation that describes the increase from $x = 0$ to $x = 3$ is the following:

> *The number of subscribers at $x = 3$ is $3$ times the number at* $x = 0$.
