Geometry problems that involve dimensions, area, or perimeter often lead to quadratic equations.

In this lesson, we'll practice translating geometric descriptions into quadratic equations and solving them.

## Modeling Geometry Problems Using Quadratic Equations

**Example:** The area of a rectangular floor is $736$ square meters. The width of the floor is $9$ meters less than its length. What is the equation that represents this situation?

**Explanation**

Let the length be $x$ meters. Then the width is $x-9$ meters.

Since their product is $702$, we set up the equation and simplify:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x \cdot (x - 9) &= 736 \\
x^{2} - 9x &= 736 \\
x^{2} - 9x - 736 &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the equation that represents this situation is

$$
0 = x^2 - 9x - 736
$$

## Solving Geometry Problems Using the Square Root Method

**Example:** A rectangular sandbox has an area of $405$ square meters. If the length of the sandbox is $5$ times its width, what is the width of the sandbox, in meters?

**Explanation**

We are told the area of a rectangular sandbox and the relationship between its length and width. We'll use this to find the width.

Let the width be $w$ meters. Then the length is $5w$ meters.

So we set up the area expression and then solve for $w{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
area &= 405 \\
w \cdot 5w &= 405 \\
5w^{2} &= 405 \\
w^{2} &= 81 \\
w &= ± \sqrt{81} \\
w &= ± 9
\end{aligned}
$$

Note that since the width must be positive, we take the positive square root.

Therefore, the width of the sandbox is $9$ meters.

## Solving Geometry Problems Using Quadratic Equations

**Example:** A builder is constructing a rectangular patio. The width of the patio is to be $6$ meters less than its length. If the area of the patio will be $160$ square meters, what will be the width, in meters, of the patio?

**Explanation**

We are told that the area of a rectangular patio is $160$ square meters, and that the width is $6$ meters less than the length.

Let the length be $l$ meters. Then the width is $l - 6$ meters.

Since area is the product of length and width, we set up the equation and simplify:

$$
\begin{aligned}
area &= 160 \\
l \cdot (l - 6) &= 160 \\
l^{2} - 6l &= 160 \\
l^{2} - 6l - 160 &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we can factor this equation as follows:

$$
(l + 10) (l - 16) = 0
$$

So, the solutions are $l = -10$ and $l = 16$. Since the length $l$ must be positive, we discard the negative solution.

Therefore, the width is

$$
\begin{aligned}
width &= l - 6 \\
&= 16 - 6 \\
&= 10meters
\end{aligned}
$$

## Solving Geometry Problems Using the Quadratic Formula

**Example:** A rectangular mirror has an area of $168$ square feet. The length of the mirror is $2$ feet more than $5$ times the width of the mirror. What is the width of the mirror, in feet?

**Explanation**

We are told that the area of a rectangular mirror is $168$ square feet, and that the length is $2$ feet more than $5$ times the width. Let the width be $w$ feet. Then the length is $5w + 2$.

We start by writing the equation for the area and then expanding:

$$
\begin{aligned}
area &= 168 \\
w(5w + 2) &= 168 \\
5w^{2} + 2w &= 168 \\
5w^{2} + 2w - 168 &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, since this equation is a quadratic of the form

$$
aw^2 + bw + c = 0
$$

we apply the quadratic formula to solve it:

$$
\begin{aligned}
w &= \frac{-b ± \sqrt{b^{2} - 4ac}}{2a} \\
&= (-2 ± \sqrt{(2)^{2} - 4(5)(-168)})/(2(5)) \\
&= \frac{-2 ± \sqrt{4 + 3360}}{10} \\
&= \frac{-2 ± \sqrt{3364}}{10} \\
&= \frac{-2 ± 58}{10}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, since the width must be positive, we take the positive root and find that the width of the mirror is

$$
\begin{aligned}
w &= \frac{-2 + 58}{10} \\
&= \frac{56}{10} \\
&= \frac{28}{5} \\
&= 28 5feet
\end{aligned}
$$
