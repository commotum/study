## Introduction

When presented with real-world data on vertical motion, we can often fit it to a quadratic model using vertex form. In this topic, we'll practice doing just that.

<a id="constructing-quadratic-functions-given-data"></a>
## Constructing Quadratic Functions Given Data

**Example:** A soccer ball is kicked straight upward. It reaches a maximum height of $48$ feet at $2$ seconds and hits the ground at $4$ seconds. Which equation represents the height above ground $H$, in feet, of the soccer ball $t$ seconds after it is kicked?

**Explanation**

We can model the motion of the soccer ball with a downward-opening quadratic function whose vertex (maximum point) is $(h, k)$, in vertex form:

$$
H(t) = a(t - h)^2 + k
$$

First, we know the ball reached its maximum height of $48$ feet after $2$ seconds. This means the vertex of the quadratic function is at $(2, 48)$, so

$$
h = 2 \qquad\text{and}\qquad k = 48
$$

Next, we know the ball returned to the ground level at $t = 4$, so $H(4) = 0$. So we set up the equation and solve for $a{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
H(4) &= a(4 - 2)^{2} + 48 \\
0 &= a(2)^{2} + 48 \\
0 &= 4a + 48 \\
- 4a &= 48 \\
a &= -\frac{48}{4} \\
a &= -12
\end{aligned}
$$

So the height equation is

$$
H(t) = -12(t - 2)^2 + 48
$$

Therefore, rewriting it in standard form, the equation that represents the height of the soccer ball above the ground $t$ seconds after it is kicked is

$$
\begin{aligned}
H(t) &= -12(t - 2)^{2} + 48 \\
&=-12(t^{2} - 4t + 4) + 48 \\
&=-12t^{2} + 48t - 48 + 48 \\
&=-12t^{2} + 48t
\end{aligned}
$$

## Constructing and Evaluating Quadratic Functions Given Data

**Example:** The quadratic function $D$ models the depth, in meters, below the surface of the water of a research vessel’s tethered camera $t$ minutes after it was lowered. The function estimates that the camera reached its maximum depth of $225$ meters $4$ minutes after it was lowered and then reached the surface of the water $10$ minutes after it was lowered. Based on the function, what was the estimated depth, to the nearest meter, of the camera $6$ minutes after it was lowered?

**Explanation**

We model the depth of the camera below the water’s surface with a quadratic function $D(t)$, where $t$ is the number of minutes since it was lowered and $D(t)$ gives the depth in meters. Since the camera reaches a maximum depth, the function takes the vertex form:

$$
D(t) = a(t - h)^2 + k
$$

where $(h, k)$ is the vertex of the parabola.

First, we know the camera reached its maximum depth of $225$ meters after $4$ minutes. This means the vertex of the quadratic function is at $(4, 225)$, so

$$
h = 4 \qquad\text{and}\qquad k = 225
$$

Next, we know the camera reached the surface of the water ($0$ depth) at $t = 10$, which means $D(10) = 0$. So we set up the equation and solve for $a{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
D(10) &= a(10 - 4)^{2} + 225 \\
0 &= a(6)^{2} + 225 \\
0 &= 36a + 225 \\
- 36a &= 225 \\
a &= -\frac{225}{36} \\
a &= -\frac{25}{4}
\end{aligned}
$$

So the depth function is

$$
D(t) = -\frac{25}{4}(t - 4)^2 + 225
$$

Therefore, the depth at $t=6$ is

$$
\begin{aligned}
D(6) &= -\frac{25}{4}(6 - 4)^{2} + 225 \\
&=-\frac{25}{4}(2)^{2} + 225 \\
&=-\frac{25}{4}(4) + 225 \\
&=-25 + 225 \\
&= 200meters
\end{aligned}
$$

## Constructing a Quadratic Function and Determining Its Roots

**Example:** A skier launches off a ramp into the air. Her height, in meters above the ground, is modeled by a quadratic function of the time, in seconds, since she left the ramp. According to the model, she launched from a height of $8$ meters above the ground and reached her highest point of $17$ meters after $3$ seconds. After how many seconds does the model predict she will land? (Round to the nearest whole number)

**Explanation**

We can model the height of the skier above the ground with a downward-opening quadratic function whose vertex (maximum point) is $(h, k)$, in vertex form:

$$
h(t) = a(t - h)^2 + k
$$

First, we know the skier reached her maximum height of $17$ meters after $3$ seconds. This means the vertex of the quadratic function is at $(3, 17)$, so

$$
h = 3 \qquad\text{and}\qquad k = 17
$$

Next, we know the skier launched from a height of $8$ meters at $t = 0$, which means $h(0) = 8$. So we set up the equation and solve for $a{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
h(0) &= a(0 - 3)^{2} + 17 \\
8 &= a(3)^{2} + 17 \\
8 &= 9a + 17 \\
- 9a &= 17 - 8 \\
- 9a &= 9 \\
a &= -1
\end{aligned}
$$

So the height function is

$$
h(t) = -1(t - 3)^2 + 17
$$

Now, to determine when the skier lands (zero height), we set up the equation $h(t) = 0$ and solve for $t{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
h(t) &= -1(t - 3)^{2} + 17 \\
0 &= -1(t - 3)^{2} + 17 \\
(t - 3)^{2} &= 17 \\
t - 3 &= ± \sqrt{17} \\
t &= 3 ± \sqrt{17}
\end{aligned}
$$

Evaluating, we find that the roots are

$$
t\approx -1.123, \qquad t\approx 7.1234
$$

Since $t > 0$, we discard the negative option. Therefore, we have

$$
t\approx 7.1234
$$

and rounding this to the nearest second gives $t = 7$ seconds.

## Modeling Situations Using Quadratics and Systems of Equations

**Example:** The total distance $s$, in yards, glided by a skier can be modeled by a quadratic function defined in terms of $t$, where $t$ is the time in seconds. At a time of $5.0$ seconds, the skier has traveled $125.0$ yards, and at a time of $10.0$ seconds, the skier has traveled $450.0$ yards. If the skier started at a distance of $0$ yards when $t = 0$, then what is the total distance traveled, in yards, by the skier after $15.0$ seconds?

**Explanation**

We are told that the distance $s$, in yards, traveled by a skier over time $t$ seconds, follows a quadratic form

$$
s(t) = at^2 + bt + c
$$

Since the skier starts at $s = 0$ when $t = 0$, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
0 &= a(0)^{2} + b(0) + c \\
c &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

So the function simplifies to

$$
s(t) = at^2 + bt
$$

First, given that $s=125.0$ at $t=5.0$, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
125.0 &= a(5.0)^{2} + b(5.0) \\
125 &= 25a + 5b
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, given that $s=450.0$ at $t=10.0$, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
450.0 &= a(10.0)^{2} + b(10.0) \\
450 &= 100a + 10b
\end{aligned}
$$

We now have a system:

$$
{25a + 5b = 125; 100a + 10b = 450
$$

To solve the system, we first multiply the second equation by

$$
\dfrac{1}{2}
$$

then subtract the resulting equation from the first to eliminate $a$ and solve for $b{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
(25a + 5b) - (50a + 5b) &= 125 - 225 \\
25a + 5b - 50a - 5b &= -100 \\
- 25a &= -100 \\
a &= \frac{-100}{-25} \\
a &= 4
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, plugging $a = 4$ back into the first equation and solving for $b{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
25(4) + 5b &= 125 \\
100 + 5b &= 125 \\
5b &= 25 \\
b &= 5
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus, the distance function becomes

$$
s(t) = 4t^2 + 5t
$$

Therefore, by substituting $t = 15.0$, the distance traveled by the skier after $15.0$ seconds is

$$
\begin{aligned}
s(15.0) &= 4(15.0)^{2} + 5(15.0) \\
&= 4(225) + 75 \\
&= 900 + 75 \\
&= 975yards
\end{aligned}
$$
