## Identifying the Graph of the Vertically Shifted Parabola

**Example:** During the first part of a study, a marble was rolled off a $10$-inch-high table. The graph below shows the height $y$, in inches, of the marble $x$ seconds after it left the table.

![](<../Source/2.6.5. Interpreting Graphs of Quadratic Functions in Modeling Contexts/Images/c359272c42ec1226b55343f7a0e39461.png>)

During the second part of the study, the marble was rolled the same way, but from a table that is $4$ inches taller than the first table. Find the graph representing the height $y$, in inches of the marble $x$ seconds after it left the table during the second part of the study.

**Explanation**

Since the marble is rolled exactly the same way except from $4$ inches higher, the new graph should have the same shape, but the $y$-intercept and maximum point must be shifted **up** by $4$ inches.

First, the $y$-intercept on the given graph is at $y = 10$. After shifting up by $4$ inches, the new $y$-intercept is at $y = 14$.

Next, the maximum point on the given graph is approximately at $y = 11.2$. After shifting up by $4$ inches, the new maximum point is approximately at $y = 15.2$.

Therefore, the new graph that represents the new launch height is as follows:

![](<../Source/2.6.5. Interpreting Graphs of Quadratic Functions in Modeling Contexts/Images/f58c9dd4b1e36bf0bbf4eec98878f213.png>)

## Identifying the Maximum Point of a Parabola From a Graph

**Example:** The quadratic function graphed above models the profit from a marketing campaign based on the amount spent on advertising. According to the model, which of the following is closest to the advertising cost, in dollars, that results in the greatest profit?

![](<../Source/2.6.5. Interpreting Graphs of Quadratic Functions in Modeling Contexts/Images/33f587a12db1f84f9552e81dcbbc8090.png>)

1. $60$
2. $40$
3. $20$
4. $80$

**Explanation**

The graph represents a quadratic function opening downward, so the highest point gives the maximum profit.

Therefore, the profit is greatest at an advertising cost of approximately $\[math]60$ as shown below:

## Identifying a Table of Values Corresponding to the Graph

**Example:** A team of scientists modeled the height of a rocket during launch using a quadratic function. The graph below shows the height $y$, in meters, of the rocket at time $x$, in seconds. Which table correctly lists some values of $x$ and their corresponding values of $y$ based on the graph?

![](<../Source/2.6.5. Interpreting Graphs of Quadratic Functions in Modeling Contexts/Images/27390904f17f6f27b6b45aae7b9f3a15.png>)

1. $x$$y$$3$$8$$4$$9$$5$$8$
2. $x$$y$$3$$9$$4$$8$$5$$9$
3. $x$$y$$2$$0$$4$$9$$6$$0$
4. $x$$y$$2$$5$$4$$7$$6$$5$

**Explanation**

To determine which table correctly matches the graph, we identify several key points directly from the plot.

From the plot, we see the following:

- At $x = 4$, the height is $y=9$ meters.
- At $x = 3$, the height is $y=8$ meters.
- At $x = 5$, the height is $y=8$ meters.

Among the given tables, only the table shown below contains all three of these values:

| $x$ | $y$ |
| --- | ---: |
| $3$ | $8$ |
| $4$ | $9$ |
| $5$ | $8$ |
