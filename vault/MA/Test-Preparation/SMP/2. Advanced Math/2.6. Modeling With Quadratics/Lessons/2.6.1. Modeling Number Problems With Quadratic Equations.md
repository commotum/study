## Introduction

Problems that relate two or more numbers through multiplication or addition often lead to quadratic equations. These problems test our ability to translate a word problem into a quadratic equation and solve it.

In this lesson, we'll practice forming and solving quadratic equations that model number relationships. The resulting quadratic equations typically have quite large coefficients, which often make the quadratic formula an efficient solution method.

---

<a id="cases-where-the-relationship-between-two-numbers-is-additive"></a>
## Cases Where the Relationship Between Two Numbers Is Additive

**Example:** The product of a positive number $x$ and the number that is $6$ more than three times $x$ is $297$. What is the value of $x$?

**Explanation**

We are told that the product of a positive number $x$ and the number that is $6$ more than three times $x$ is $297$. This gives the equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x(3x + 6) &= 297 \\
3x^{2} + 6x &= 297 \\
3x^{2} + 6x - 297 &= 0 \\
x^{2} + 2x - 99 &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, since this equation is a quadratic of the form

$$
ax^2 + bx + c = 0
$$

we apply the quadratic formula to solve it:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x &= \frac{-b ± \sqrt{b^{2} - 4ac}}{2a} \\
&= (-2 ± \sqrt{(2)^{2} - 4(1)(-99)})/(2(1)) \\
&= \frac{-2 ± \sqrt{4 + 396}}{2} \\
&= \frac{-2 ± \sqrt{400}}{2} \\
&= \frac{-2 ± 20}{2} \\
&=-11, 9
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the two solutions are $x = -11$ and $x = 9$.

Since $x$ must be a positive number, we take the positive root. Therefore, the value of $x$ is $9$.

## Cases Where the Relationship Between Two Numbers Is Linear

**Example:** The product of two positive integers is $351$. If the first integer is $12$ less than three times the second integer, what is the smaller of the two integers?

**Explanation**

We are told that the product of two positive integers is $351$, and one is $12$ less than three times the other.

Let the smaller integer be $x$. Then the larger integer is $3x - 12$.

Since their product is $351$, we set up the equation and simplify:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x(3x - 12) &= 351 \\
3x^{2} - 12x &= 351 \\
3x^{2} - 12x - 351 &= 0 \\
x^{2} - 4x - 117 &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, since this equation is a quadratic of the form

$$
ax^2 + bx + c = 0
$$

we apply the quadratic formula to solve it:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x &= \frac{-b ± \sqrt{b^{2} - 4ac}}{2a} \\
&= (- (-4) ± \sqrt{(-4)^{2} - 4(1)(-117)})/(2(1)) \\
&= \frac{4 ± \sqrt{16 + 468}}{2} \\
&= \frac{4 ± \sqrt{484}}{2} \\
&= \frac{4 ± 22}{2} \\
&=-9, 13
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the two solutions are $x = -9$ and $x = 13$.

Since $x$ represents a positive integer, we discard the negative root and conclude that the smaller integer is $13$.

## Cases Where the Relationship Between Two Numbers Is Linear (Free-Response)

**Example:** The product of two positive integers is $585$. If the first integer is $6$ greater than three times the second integer, what is the smaller of the two integers?

**Explanation**

Let the smaller integer be $x$. Then the first integer is $3x + 6$.

Since their product is $585$, we set up the equation and simplify:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x(3x + 6) &= 585 \\
3x^{2} + 6x &= 585 \\
3x^{2} + 6x - 585 &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, since this equation is a quadratic of the form

$$
ax^2 + bx + c = 0
$$

we apply the quadratic formula to solve it:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x &= \frac{-b ± \sqrt{b^{2} - 4ac}}{2a} \\
&= (-6 ± \sqrt{6^{2} - 4(3)(-585)})/(2(3)) \\
&= \frac{-6 ± \sqrt{36 + 7020}}{6} \\
&= \frac{-6 ± \sqrt{7056}}{6} \\
&= \frac{-6 ± 84}{6}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, since $x$ must be a positive integer, we take the positive root and find that the value of the smaller integer is

$$
\begin{aligned}
x &= \frac{-6 + 84}{6} \\
&= \frac{78}{6} \\
&= 13
\end{aligned}
$$
