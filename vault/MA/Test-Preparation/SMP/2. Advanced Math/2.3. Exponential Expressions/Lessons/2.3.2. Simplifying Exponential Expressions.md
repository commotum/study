## Introduction

Problems involving simplifying exponential expressions often require applying the laws of exponents. In this topic, we’ll practice factoring, combining, and simplifying exponential terms using these laws.

<a id="factoring-exponential-expressions"></a>
## Factoring Exponential Expressions

**Example:** Simplify the expression $\dfrac{7}{8}\left(2^{-s}\right) - \left(2^{-s}\right)$.

**Explanation**

We need to write the expression

$$
\dfrac{7}{8}\left(2^{-s}\right) - \left(2^{-s}\right)
$$

in the form

$$
k\left(2^{-s}\right)
$$

Factoring out the common factor of

$$
\left(2^{-s}\right)
$$

in the expression, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{7}{8}(2^{-s}) - (2^{-s}) &= \frac{7}{8} \cdot (2^{-s}) - 1 \cdot (2^{-s}) \\
&= (\frac{7}{8} - 1) \cdot (2^{-s}) \\
&= (\frac{7}{8} - \frac{8}{8}) \cdot (2^{-s}) \\
&= (-\frac{1}{8}) \cdot (2^{-s}) \\
&=-\frac{1}{8}(2^{-s})
\end{aligned}
$$

## Using the Product and Quotient Rules to Simplify Expressions

**Example:** Express $\dfrac{64}{27}\left(\dfrac43\right)^t$ as a power of $\dfrac43$ only.

**Explanation**

Let's recall the rules of exponents:

- Negative exponents: $x^{-n} = \dfrac{1}{x^n}$
- Radicals as fractional exponents: $\sqrt[n]{x} = x^{1/n}$
- The product rule: $a^n \cdot a^m = a^{n+m}$
- The quotient rule: $\dfrac{a^n}{a^m} = a^{n-m}$
- The power rule: $\left(a^m \right)^n = a^{mn}$
- The power of product rule: $(ab)^n = a^nb^n$
- The power of quotient rule: $\left(\dfrac a b \right)^n = \dfrac{a^n}{b^n}$

Now, our expression

$$
\dfrac{64}{27}\left(\dfrac43\right)^t
$$

has an additional factor of

$$
\dfrac{64}{27}
$$

However, notice that

$$
64=4^3 \qquad\text{and}\qquad 27=3^3
$$

are both perfect cubes. Therefore, we can write our expression as

$$
\dfrac{4^3}{3^3}\left(\dfrac43\right)^t
$$

By the power of the quotient rule, we have

$$
\left(\dfrac43\right)^3\left(\dfrac43\right)^t
$$

Finally, we apply the product rule:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(\frac{4}{3})^{3}(\frac{4}{3})^{t} &= (\frac{4}{3})^{t + 3}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Using the Power Rule to Simplify Expressions

**Example:** Express $5^{2k}\,4^{k}$ in the form $n^k$, where $n$ is an integer.

**Explanation**

Let's recall the rules of exponents:

- Negative exponents: $x^{-n} = \dfrac{1}{x^n}$
- Radicals as fractional exponents: $\sqrt[n]{x} = x^{1/n}$
- The product rule: $a^n \cdot a^m = a^{n+m}$
- The quotient rule: $\dfrac{a^n}{a^m} = a^{n-m}$
- The power rule: $\left(a^m \right)^n = a^{mn}$
- The power of product rule: $(ab)^n = a^nb^n$
- The power of quotient rule: $\left(\dfrac a b \right)^n = \dfrac{a^n}{b^n}$

Now, our expression
$5^{2k}\,4^{k}$,
involves the factor $5^{2k}$. However, using the power rule, we can express this as a power of $k$ as follows:

$$
5^{2k} = \left(5^{2}\right)^k
$$

Thus, we can rewrite the given expression as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
5^{2k}4^{k} &= (5^{2})^{k}4^{k}
\end{aligned}
$$

Since $5^2 = 25$, we have

$$
\left(5^{2}\right)^k \, 4^k = 25^k \, 4^k
$$

Finally, using the power of the product rule, we can write this as

$$
25^k \, 4^k = \left(100\right)^k
$$

Hence, $n = 100$.

## Adding and Subtracting Exponential Expressions

**Example:** Given that $15(3^{x-1}) - 4(3^x) = k(3^x)$, find the value of $k$.

**Explanation**

Let's recall the rules of exponents:

- Negative exponents: $x^{-n} = \dfrac{1}{x^n}$
- Radicals as fractional exponents: $\sqrt[n]{x} = x^{1/n}$
- The product rule: $a^n \cdot a^m = a^{n+m}$
- The quotient rule: $\dfrac{a^n}{a^m} = a^{n-m}$
- The power rule: $\left(a^m \right)^n = a^{mn}$
- The power of product rule: $(ab)^n = a^nb^n$
- The power of quotient rule: $\left(\dfrac a b \right)^n = \dfrac{a^n}{b^n}$

We need to write the expression
$15(3^{x-1}) - 4(3^x)$
in the form $k(3^x)$.

First, we rewrite the first term using the product rule and negative exponents:

$$
\begin{aligned}
15(3^{x - 1}) - 4(3^{x}) &= 15 \cdot (3^{x} \cdot 3^{-1}) - 4(3^{x}) \\
&= 15 \cdot (3^{x} \cdot \frac{1}{3}) - 4(3^{x}) \\
&= 15 \cdot (\frac{1}{3} \cdot 3^{x}) - 4(3^{x}) \\
&= 5(3^{x}) - 4(3^{x})
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, combining the two negative terms, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
5(3^{x}) - 4(3^{x}) &= (5 - 4)3^{x} \\
&= 1 \cdot 3^{x} \\
&= 3^{k}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, $k=1$.
