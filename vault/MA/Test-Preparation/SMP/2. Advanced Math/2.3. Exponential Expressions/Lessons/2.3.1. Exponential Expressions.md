## Applying the Product Rule for Exponents

**Example:** Simplify the expression $\left(a^{-5} b^{6} c\right)\left(a^{3} b^{-2} c^{4}\right)$, where $a, b$, and $c$ are positive.

**Explanation**

We apply the product rule of exponents, which tells us to *add the exponents* when multiplying like bases.

First, we group the like bases:

$$
\left(a^{-5} b^{6} c\right)\left(a^{3} b^{-2} c^{4}\right) = \left(a^{-5} \cdot a^{3}\right)\left(b^{6} \cdot b^{-2}\right)\left(c \cdot c^{4}\right)
$$

Now, we apply the product rule to each group:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(a^{-5} \cdot a^{3})(b^{6} \cdot b^{-2})(c \cdot c^{4}) &= a^{-5 + 3}b^{6 + (-2)}c^{1 + 4} \\
&= a^{-2}b^{4}c^{5}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\left(a^{-5} b^{6} c\right)\left(a^{3} b^{-2} c^{4}\right) = a^{-2} b^{4} c^{5}
$$

## Applying the Product Rule With Negative Exponents

**Example:** Find the product of $\left(a^{-2} b^{5} c^{0}\right)$ and $\left(b^{-3} + a^{3} c^{4}\right)$.

**Explanation**

First, we apply the distributive property to multiply the first expression by each term in the parentheses:

$$
\left(a^{-2} b^{5} c^{0}\right)\left(b^{-3} + a^{3} c^{4}\right) = \left(a^{-2} b^{5} c^{0}\right)(b^{-3}) + \left(a^{-2} b^{5} c^{0}\right)(a^{3} c^{4})
$$

Next, we apply the product rule of exponents to each term, which tells us to *add the exponents* when multiplying like bases.

To do that, we group the like bases in each term and apply the product rule to each group:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(a^{-2}b^{5}c^{0})(b^{-3}) + (a^{-2}b^{5}c^{0})(a^{3}c^{4}) &= (a^{-2})(b^{5} \cdot b^{-3})(c^{0}) + (a^{-2} \cdot a^{3})(b^{5})(c^{0} \cdot c^{4}) \\
&= a^{-2}b^{5 - 3}c^{0} + a^{-2 + 3}b^{5}c^{0 + 4} \\
&= a^{-2}b^{2} + ab^{5}c^{4}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\left(a^{-2} b^{5} c^{0}\right)\left(b^{-3} + a^{3} c^{4}\right) = a^{-2} b^{2} + a b^{5} c^{4}
$$

## Applying the Quotient Rule for Exponents

**Example:** Simplify $\dfrac{a^{14} b^{6}}{a^{8} b^{12}}$, where $a > 0$ and $b > 0$?

**Explanation**

We apply the quotient rule of exponents, which tells us to *subtract the exponents* when dividing like bases.

First, we separate the like bases:

$$
\dfrac{a^{14} b^{6}}{a^{8} b^{12}} = \dfrac{a^{14}}{a^{8}} \cdot \dfrac{b^{6}}{b^{12}}
$$

Now, we apply the quotient rule to each fraction:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{a^{14}}{a^{8}} \cdot \frac{b^{6}}{b^{12}} &= a^{14 - 8} \cdot b^{6 - 12} \\
&= a^{6} \cdot b^{-6} \\
&= a^{6} \cdot \frac{1}{b^{6}} \\
&= \frac{a^{6}}{b^{6}}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\dfrac{a^{14} b^{6}}{a^{8} b^{12}} = \dfrac{a^6}{b^6}
$$
