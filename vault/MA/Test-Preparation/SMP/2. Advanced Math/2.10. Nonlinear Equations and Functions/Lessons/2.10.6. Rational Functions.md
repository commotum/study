## Evaluating a Rational Function

**Example:** The function $g$ is defined by $g(x) = \dfrac{x^{2} - 4x + 2}{x - 2}$. What is the value of $g(-2)$?

**Explanation**

Explanation:

To find the value of $g(-2)$, we substitute $x=-2$ into the function's definition and evaluate:

$$
\begin{aligned}
g(x) &= \frac{x^{2} - 4x + 2}{x - 2} \\
g(-2) &= ((-2)^{2} - 4(-2) + 2)/((-2) - 2) \\
&= (4 - (-8) + 2)/((-4)) \\
&= (14)/((-4)) \\
&=-\frac{7}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the value of $g(-2)$ is

$$
-\dfrac{7}{2}
$$

## Evaluating a Power Function at a Point With Negative Exponents

**Example:** The function $f$ is defined by $f(x) = 7x^{-1}$. What is the value of $f(4)$?

**Explanation**

Explanation:

To find $f(4)$, we substitute $x = 4$ into the function's definition and evaluate:

$$
\begin{aligned}
f(x) &= 7x^{-1} \\
f(4) &= 7 \cdot 4^{-1} \\
&= 7 \cdot \frac{1}{4} \\
&= \frac{7}{4}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the value of $f(4)$ is

$$
\dfrac{7}{4}
$$

## Identifying the Equation of a Shifted Reciprocal Function

**Example:** $r(x) = \dfrac{a - 5}{x} + 7$
In the given function above, $a$ is constant. The graph of the function $r$ in the $xy$-plane, where $y = r(x)$, is shifted $2$ units up and **$3$ units to the left** to produce the graph of $y = s(x)$. Which equation defines function $s$?

**Explanation**

To apply a transformation to the graph of a function, we modify its equation according to the direction of the shift:

- A horizontal shift left by $3$ units means we replace every $x$ in the original function with $(x + 3)$.
- A vertical shift up by $2$ units means we add $2$ to the entire function.

We start with the function:

$$
r(x) = \dfrac{a - 5}{x} + 7
$$

To shift left $3$ units, we replace $x$ with $x + 3{:}$

$$
\dfrac{a - 5}{x + 3} + 7
$$

Then, to shift up $2$ units, we add $2$ to the whole expression:

$$
\left(\dfrac{a - 5}{x + 3} + 7\right) + 2
$$

Therefore, by simplifying, we find the equation that defines $s$ is

$$
\begin{aligned}
s(x) &= (\frac{a - 5}{x + 3} + 7) + 2 \\
&= \frac{a - 5}{x + 3} + 9
\end{aligned}
$$

## Identifying the Graph of a Shifted Reciprocal Function

**Example:** The rational function $r$ is defined by an equation in the form $r(x) = \dfrac{a}{x + b}$, where $a$ and $b$ are constants. The partial graph of $y = r(x)$ is shown above. If $s(x) = r(x + 2)$, which equation could define the function $s$?

![](<../Source/2.10.6. Rational Functions/Images/2a313f2e5e1a04a3c9ec66df337ed639.png>)

1. $s(x) = \dfrac{6}{x + 5}$
2. $s(x) = \dfrac{6}{x + 3}$
3. $s(x) = \dfrac{6}{x + 2}$
4. $s(x) = \dfrac{6}{x - 5}$

**Explanation**

From the graph of

$$
r(x) = \dfrac{a}{x + b}
$$

we observe that the vertical asymptote occurs at $x = -3$, which means the denominator is zero when $x = -3$. Then, we must have

$$
\begin{aligned}
0 &= -3 + b \\
b &= 3
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the equation of $r(x)$ becomes

$$
r(x) = \dfrac{a}{x + 3}
$$

Now, to identify the value of $a$, we choose a convenient point on the graph. For example, when $x = -2$, the graph passes through $y = 6$. Plugging $(-2, 6)$ into $r(x)$, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
r(x) &= \frac{a}{x + 3} \\
6 &= \frac{a}{-2 + 3} \\
6 &= \frac{a}{1} \\
a &= 6
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus, the equation of $r(x)$ is

$$
r(x) = \dfrac{6}{x + 3}
$$

Therefore, to find an expression for $s(x)$, we replace each $x$ in $r(x)$ by $(x+2)$ and simplify:

$$
\begin{aligned}
s(x) &= r(x + 2) \\
&= (6)/((x + 2) + 3) \\
&= \frac{6}{x + 5}
\end{aligned}
$$
