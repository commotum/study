## Introduction

This topic provides practice in solving rational equations using three methods: cancelling common factors, applying the flip method, and multiplying through by the lowest common denominator.

---

<a id="solving-a-rational-equation-by-canceling-a-common-factor"></a>
## Solving a Rational Equation by Canceling a Common Factor

**Example:** What is the solution to the equation given below?
$\dfrac{(a - 4)(a + 2)}{a + 2} = 5$

**Explanation**

First, note that the denominator $a + 2$ must not be zero:

$$
a + 2 \neq 0 \quad\Rightarrow\quad a \neq -2
$$

Assuming that

$$
a \neq -2
$$

we simplify the rational expression by cancelling the common factor and solve for $a$, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
((a - 4)(a + 2))/(a + 2) &= 5 \\
((a - 4)(a + 2))/(a + 2) &= 5 \\
a - 4 &= 5 \\
a &= 9
\end{aligned}
$$

## Solving a Rational Equation Using the Flip Method

**Example:** $\dfrac{1}{y^{2} + 12y + 36} = 9$.
If $y$ is a solution to the given equation, which of the following is a possible value of $y + 6$?

1. $-\dfrac 2 3$
2. $\dfrac 2 3$
3. $\dfrac 1 3$
4. $\dfrac 5 3$

**Explanation**

We are given the equation

$$
\dfrac{1}{y^{2} + 12y + 36} = 9
$$

First, we observe that the denominator is a perfect square trinomial:

$$
y^{2} + 12y + 36 = (y + 6)^2
$$

So, our equation becomes

$$
\dfrac{1}{(y + 6)^2} = 9
$$

Now, we take the reciprocal of both sides:

$$
(y + 6)^2 = \dfrac{1}{9}
$$

Next, we take the square root of both sides:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{(y + 6)^{2}} &= \sqrt{\frac{1}{9}} \\
y + 6 &= ± \frac{1}{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the possible values of $y + 6$ are

$$
-\dfrac13
$$

and

$$
\dfrac13
$$

Among the listed options, only

$$
\dfrac{1}{3}
$$

is valid.

## Solving Rational Equations Using the Lowest Common Denominator

**Example:** What value of $n$ satisfies the equation given below?
$\dfrac{n^{2}}{n^{2} + 5n + 6} - \dfrac{2n}{n + 2} = -\,\dfrac{9}{n + 3}$

**Explanation**

First, we factor the denominators where possible. Note that $n^{2} + 5n + 6$ factors as $(n + 2)(n + 3)$.

So, we rewrite the expression as

$$
\dfrac{n^{2}}{(n + 2)(n + 3)} - \dfrac{2n}{n + 2} = -\,\dfrac{9}{n + 3}
$$

We now make the following observations:

- The least common denominator of our equation is given by
$\textrm{LCM}\big(n+2,\: n+3,\: (n+2)(n+3) \big) = (n+2)(n+3)$.
- The values $n=-2$ and $n=-3$ cannot be solutions to our equation. These values make at least one denominator in our original equation equal zero, and division by zero is undefined.

Now, multiplying both sides of the equation by the least common denominator, we can simplify it as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
((n^{2})/((n + 2)(n + 3)) - \frac{2n}{n + 2}) \cdot (n + 2)(n + 3) &= -\frac{9}{n + 3} \cdot (n + 2)(n + 3) \\
(n^{2})/((n + 2)(n + 3)) \cdot (n + 2)(n + 3) - \frac{2n}{n + 2} \cdot (n + 2)(n + 3) &= -\frac{9}{n + 3} \cdot (n + 2)(n + 3) \\
(n^{2})/((n + 2)(n + 3)) \cdot (n + 2)(n + 3) - \frac{2n}{n + 2} \cdot (n + 2)(n + 3) &= -\frac{9}{n + 3} \cdot (n + 2)(n + 3) \\
n^{2} - 2n(n + 3) &= -9(n + 2)
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we expand both sides, move all terms to the left-hand side, and factor the result to obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
n^{2} - 2n^{2} - 6n &= -9n - 18 \\
- n^{2} + 3n + 18 &= 0 \\
n^{2} - 3n - 18 &= 0 \\
(n - 6)(n + 3) &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

By the zero-product property, the possible solutions are $n = 6$ or $n = -3$.

However, $n = -3$ is excluded because it makes the denominator zero. Therefore, the only valid solution is $n = 6$.
