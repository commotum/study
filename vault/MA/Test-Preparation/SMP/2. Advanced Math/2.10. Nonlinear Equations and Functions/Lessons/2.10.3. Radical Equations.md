## Introduction

This topic focuses on practicing solving radical equations, including cases where the radical must be isolated first and where variables appear on both sides. We'll also practice finding unknown parameters that result in exactly one real solution.

## Solving Radical Equations With Isolated Radicals

**Example:** Given that $\sqrt{x - 7} = 6$, determine the value of $x$.

**Explanation**

To solve the given equation, we first square both sides to remove the radical. Then, we solve for $x$ as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{x - 7} &= 6 \\
(\sqrt{x - 7})^{2} &= 6^{2} \\
x - 7 &= 36 \\
x &= 43
\end{aligned}
$$

Let's check the result by plugging $x = 43$ into the original equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{43 - 7} &= ^(?)6 \\
\sqrt{36} &= ^(?)6 \\
6 &= 6✓
\end{aligned}
$$

Hence, $x = 43$ is a valid solution.

## Solving Equations by Isolating Radicals

**Example:** Given that $-8\sqrt{\dfrac{5}{2}v} = -12$, determine the value of $10v$.

**Explanation**

We are given the equation

$$
-8\sqrt{\dfrac{5}{2}v} = -12
$$

and asked to find the value of $10v$.

First, we isolate the square root term by dividing both sides by $-8{:}$

$$
\sqrt{\dfrac{5}{2}v} = \frac{-12}{-8} = \dfrac{3}{2}
$$

Next, we square both sides to remove the radical:

$$
\left(\sqrt{\dfrac{5}{2}v}\right)^2 = \left(\dfrac{3}{2}\right)^2
$$

This gives

$$
\dfrac{5}{2}v = \dfrac{9}{4}
$$

and multiplying both sides by

$$
\dfrac{2}{5}
$$

yields

$$
v = \dfrac{9}{10}
$$

Thus, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
10v &= 10 \cdot \frac{9}{10} \\
&= 9
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the correct answer is $9$.

## Radical Equations With Variables on Both Sides

**Example:** Given the equation
$\sqrt{4w + 25} + 2 = w + 3$,
determine its solution set.

**Explanation**

First, we isolate the square root:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{4w + 25} + 2 &= w + 3 \\
\sqrt{4w + 25} &= w + 1
\end{aligned}
$$

Note that the square root must be non-negative, so

$$
w + 1 \ge 0
$$

or equivalently

$$
w \ge -1
$$

First, we square both sides:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(\sqrt{4w + 25})^{2} &= (w + 1)^{2} \\
4w + 25 &= (w + 1)^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we expand the square:

$$
4w + 25 = w^{2} + 2w + 1
$$

Next, we bring all terms to one side and simplify:

$$
0 = w^{2} - 2w - 24
$$

Finally, we factor the resulting quadratic equation:

$$
(w - 6)(w + 4) = 0
$$

So, the possible solutions are $w = 6$ or $w = -4$.

However, $w = -4$ does not satisfy

$$
w \ge -1
$$

so it must be rejected.

Therefore, only $w = 6$ remains. We check this value in the original equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{4(6) + 25} + 2 &= ^(?)6 + 3 \\
\sqrt{49} + 2 &= ^(?)9 \\
9 &= 9✓
\end{aligned}
$$

So, $w = 6$ is a valid solution.

Therefore, the solution set is $\{6\}$.

## Finding Parameters Such That a Radical Equation Has One Real Solution

**Example:** $\sqrt{m - t} = 9 - 2t$

In the equation given above, $m$ is a constant. The equation has exactly one real solution. What is the minimum possible value of $16m$?

**Explanation**

We are given the equation

$$
\sqrt{m - t} = 9 - 2t
$$

To solve for $t$, we first square both sides to remove the radical:

$$
m - t = (9 - 2t)^{2}
$$

Then, expanding the square, bringing all terms to one side, and simplifying, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
m - t &= 81 - 36t + 4t^{2} \\
0 &= 4t^{2} - 35t + 81 - m
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, for this equation to have one real solution, the quadratic discriminant

$$
\mathcal{D}=b^{2} - 4ac
$$

must equal zero.

Here, $a = 4$, $b = -35$, and $c = 81 - m$. So, we must have

$$
\begin{aligned}
D &= 0 \\
(-35)^{2} - 4(4)(81 - m) &= 0 \\
1225 - 1296 + 16m &= 0 \\
- 71 + 16m &= 0 \\
16m &= 71 \\
m &= \frac{71}{16}
\end{aligned}
$$

Let's find the potential solution corresponding to

$$
m = \dfrac{71}{16}
$$

Since it corresponds to

$$
\mathcal{D}=0
$$

we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
t &= -\frac{b}{2a} \\
&= - ((-35))/(2(4)) \\
&= \frac{35}{8}
\end{aligned}
$$

Let's check that it is a valid solution by substituting it back into our original equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{m - t} &= ^(?)9 - 2t \\
\sqrt{\frac{71}{16} - t} &= ^(?)9 - 2t \\
\sqrt{\frac{71}{16} - \frac{35}{8}} &= ^(?)9 - 2 \cdot \frac{35}{8} \\
\sqrt{\frac{71}{16} - \frac{35}{8}} &= ^(?)9 - \frac{35}{4} \\
\sqrt{\frac{71}{16} - \frac{70}{16}} &= ^(?)\frac{36}{4} - \frac{35}{4} \\
\sqrt{\frac{1}{16}} &= ^(?)\frac{1}{4} \\
\frac{1}{4} &= \frac{1}{4}✓
\end{aligned}
$$

Hence,

$$
m = \dfrac{71}{16}
$$

is indeed a minimum possible value when the equation has exactly one real solution.

Finally,

$$
16m = 16 \cdot \frac{71}{16} = 71
$$
