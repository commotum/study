## Introduction

In this topic, we'll practice solving systems of linear equations using elimination. This involves multiplying one or both equations by constant factors and then adding them to eliminate one of the variables.

---

<a id="solving-a-system-by-premultiplying-one-equation"></a>
## Solving a System by Premultiplying One Equation

**Example:** ${6p - 7q = 2; - 3p + 4q = 5$

The solution to the given system of equations is $(p, q)$. What is the value of $pq$?

**Explanation**

We will solve the system using the elimination method.

Notice that if we multiply the second equation by $2$, then we will get a $-6p$ term that can cancel with the $6p$ term in the first equation:

$$
{6p - 7q = 2; - 3p + 4q = 5 \mid \mid \cdot 2
$$

When we add the two equations, we obtain:

$$
\begin{aligned}
6p - 7q &= 2 \\
- 6p + 8q &= 10 \\
(-7q + 8q) &= (2 + 10) \\
q &= 12
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, substitute $q = 12$ into one of the original equations to solve for $p$. We'll use the first equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
6p - 7q &= 2 \\
6p - 7(12) &= 2 \\
6p - 84 &= 2 \\
6p &= 86 \\
p &= \frac{43}{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

Hence, we get

$$
pq = \dfrac{43}{3} \cdot 12 = 172
$$

## Solving a System by Premultiplying Both Equations

**Example:** ${3x + 2y = 8; 4x - 3y = 5$

If $(x, y)$ is the solution to the system of equations above, what is the value of $2x + 3y$?

**Explanation**

We will solve the system using elimination.

Notice that if we multiply the first equation by $3$ and the second equation by $2$, then we will get a $6y$ term that can cancel with the $-6y$ term in the second equation:

$$
{3x + 2y = 8 \mid \mid \cdot (3); 4x - 3y = 5 \mid \mid \cdot (2)
$$

When we add the two equations, we obtain:

$$
\begin{aligned}
9x + 6y &= 24 \\
8x- 6y &= 10 \\
(9x + 8x) &= (24 + 10) \\
17x &= 34 \\
x &= 2
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, substitute $x = 2$ into one of the original equations to solve for $y$. We'll use the first equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
3x + 2y &= 8 \\
3(2) + 2y &= 8 \\
6 + 2y &= 8 \\
2y &= 2 \\
y &= 1
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we compute $2x + 3y{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
2x + 3y &= 2(2) + 3(1) \\
&= 4 + 3 \\
&= 7
\end{aligned}
$$

Hence, the value of $2x + 3y$ is $7$.

## Solving Systems Containing Fractions

**Example:** ${\frac{8}{5}x - 3y = 6; \frac{2}{5}x - 3y =-6$

The solution to the given system of equations is $(x, y)$. What is the value of $\dfrac{3}{5}x - y$?

**Explanation**

We will solve the system using the elimination method.

Notice that if we multiply the second equation by $-1$, then we will get a $+3y$ term that can cancel with the $-3y$ term in the first equation:

$$
{\frac{8}{5}x - 3y = 6; \frac{2}{5}x - 3y =-6 \mid \mid \cdot (-1)
$$

When we add the two equations, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{8}{5}x- 3y &= 6 \\
- \frac{2}{5}x+ 3y &= 6 \\
(\frac{8}{5}x - \frac{2}{5}x) &= 12 \\
\frac{6}{5}x &= 12 \\
x &= 10
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, substitute $x = 10$ into one of the original equations to solve for $y$. We'll use the second equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{2}{5}x - 3y &= -6 \\
\frac{2}{5}(10) - 3y &= -6 \\
4 - 3y &= -6 \\
- 3y &= -10 \\
y &= \frac{10}{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we compute

$$
\dfrac{3}{5}x - y{:}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{3}{5}x - y &= \frac{3}{5}(10) - \frac{10}{3} \\
&= 6 - \frac{10}{3} \\
&= \frac{18 - 10}{3} \\
&= \frac{8}{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

Hence, the value of

$$
\dfrac{3}{5}x - y
$$

is

$$
\dfrac{8}{3}
$$
