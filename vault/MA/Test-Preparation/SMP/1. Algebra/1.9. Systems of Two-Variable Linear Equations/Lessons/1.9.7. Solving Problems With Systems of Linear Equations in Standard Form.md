## Modeling and Solving Systems of Equations: One Equation Represents a Sum

**Example:** A school organized $40$ student teams for a math competition. Some teams had $3$ members, and the rest had $6$ members. If every team was full and $156$ students participated, how many of the teams had $3$ members?

**Explanation**

Let $x$ and $y$ be the number of $3$-member and $6$-member teams, respectively.

Let's establish the linear equations that model the given situation.

- The total number of teams was $40$, so
$x + y = 40$.
- The total number of students was $156$. Since $3$ students fill each $3$-member team and $6$ fill each $6$-member team, we have
$3x + 6y = 156$.

Putting these equations together, we have the following system of equations:

$$
{x + y = 40; 3x + 6y = 156
$$

We will solve the system using the substitution method. We solve for $y$ in the first equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x + y &= 40 \\
y &= 40 - x
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we can substitute $y = 40 - x$ into the second equation and solve for $x{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
3x + 6y &= 156 \\
3x + 6(40 - x) &= 156 \\
3x + 240 - 6x &= 156 \\
- 3x &= -84 \\
x &= ((-84))/((-3)) \\
x &= 28
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the number of $3$-member teams was $28$.

## Modeling and Solving Systems of Equations: One Equation Represents a Sum (Additional Step Needed)

**Example:** A market sold $25$ baskets of vegetables containing either carrots or onions. Each basket of carrots costs $\[math]3$, and each basket of onions costs $\[/math]4$. If the total revenue from all sales was $\[math]82$, how much, in dollars, was collected from selling the onion baskets?

**Explanation**

Let $c$ and $n$ be the number of carrot and onion baskets sold, respectively.

Let's establish the linear equations that model the given situation.

- The market sold $25$ baskets in total. Therefore,
$c + n = 25$.
- The total money collected was $\[math]82$. Since a carrot basket costs $\[/math]3$ and an onion basket costs $\[math]4$, we have
$3c + 4n = 82$.

Putting these equations together, we have the following system of equations:

$$
{c + n = 25; 3c + 4n = 82
$$

We will solve the system using the substitution method. We solve for $c$ in the first equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
c + n &= 25 \\
c &= 25 - n
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we can substitute $c = 25 - n$ into the second equation and solve for $n{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
3c + 4n &= 82 \\
3(25 - n) + 4n &= 82 \\
75 - 3n + 4n &= 82 \\
n + 75 &= 82 \\
n &= 82 - 75 \\
n &= 7
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, since each onion basket costs $\[/math]4$, the total amount collected from the sale of onion baskets is

$$
\[math]4 \times 7 = 28
$$

## Modeling and Solving Mixture Problems Using Systems of Equations

**Example:** A chemist prepared a $90.0\,\textrm{mL}$ salt solution that is $25.0\%$ salt by volume. This solution was created by mixing two other salt solutions. The first solution was $10.0\%$ salt, and the second was $40.0\%$ salt.

What was the volume, in milliliters, of the salt contained in the first solution used to create the final mixture?

**Explanation**

Let $x$ and $y$ be the volume (in milliliters) of the first and second solutions, respectively.

Let's establish the linear equations that model the given situation.

- The total volume was $90.0\,\text{mL}$, so
$x + y = 90.0$.
- We are given that the total salt content is $25.0\%$ of $90.0\,\text{mL}$. Therefore,
$25.0% \cdot 90.0mL|= 0.25 \cdot 90.0mL; = 22.50mL$.
We are also told that the first solution was $10.0\%$ salt and the second was $40.0\%$ salt. Therefore, we get the equation
$0.10x + 0.40y = 22.50$.

Putting the equations together, we have the following system of equations:

$$
{x + y = 90.0; 0.10x + 0.40y = 22.50
$$

We will solve the system using the substitution method. We solve for $y$ in the first equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x + y &= 90.0 \\
y &= 90.0 - x
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we can substitute

$$
y = 90.0 - x
$$

into the second equation and solve for $x{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
0.10x + 0.40y &= 22.50 \\
0.10x + 0.40(90.0 - x) &= 22.50 \\
0.10x + 36.0 - 0.40x &= 22.50 \\
- 0.30x &= -13.50 \\
x &= \frac{-13.50}{-0.30} \\
x &= 45.0
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, since the first solution was $10.0\%$ salt by volume, the volume of salt in the first solution used was

$$
\begin{aligned}
0.10x &= 0.10(45.0) \\
&= 4.5mL
\end{aligned}
$$

## Modeling and Solving Systems of Equations Using Elimination

**Example:** At a restaurant, one diner paid $\[math]155.50$ for $6$ main courses and $5$ drinks. Another diner paid $\[/math]72.50$ for $3$ main courses and $2$ drinks.

What is the cost, in dollars, of one drink?

**Explanation**

Let $m$ and $d$ be the cost of one main course and the cost of one drink, respectively.

Let's establish the linear equations that model the given situation.

- The first diner paid $\[math]155.50$ for $6$ mains and $5$ drinks, so we have
$6m + 5d = 155.50$.
- The second diner paid $\[/math]72.50$ for $3$ mains and $2$ drinks, so we have
$3m + 2d = 72.50$.

Putting these equations together, we have the following system of equations:

$$
{6m + 5d = 155.50; 3m + 2d = 72.50
$$

We will solve the above system using the elimination method. Notice that the $m$-coefficient in the first equation is ${\color{red}{6}}$, while in the second equation it is ${\color{blue}{3}}$.

If we multiply the second equation by $-{\color{red}{2}}$, then the $m$-coefficient in the first and second equations will be $6$ and $-6$, respectively, and they will cancel when we add the equations.

$$
{6m + 5d = 155.50; (3m + 2d = 72.50) \cdot (-2)
$$

Now, when we add the two equations, the variable $m$ will be eliminated:

$$
\begin{aligned}
6m + 5d &= 155.50 \\
- 6m - 4d &= -145.00 \\
(5d - 4d) &= (155.50 - 145.00) \\
d &= 10.50
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the cost of one drink is $\[math]10.50$.
