## Solving a System by Substituting a Scaled Variable

**Example:** ${\frac{1}{4}y = 12; x - \frac{1}{4}y = 2$

The system of equations above has solution $(x, y)$. What is the value of $x$?

**Explanation**

First, we solve the first equation for $y{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{1}{4}y &= 12 \\
y &= 12 \cdot 4 \\
y &= 48
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we substitute this value into the second equation to solve for $x{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
x - \frac{1}{4}y &= 2 \\
x - \frac{1}{4} \cdot 48 &= 2 \\
x - 12 &= 2 \\
x &= 2 + 12 \\
x &= 14
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the value of $x$ is $14$.

## Solving a System by Substituting an Expression

**Example:** ${x + 3y = 12; 4(x + 3y) + y = 55$

If $(x, y)$ is the solution to the given system of equations, what is the value of $y$?

**Explanation**

We can substitute

$$
x + 3y = 12
$$

from the first equation into the second equation to solve for $y{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
4(x + 3y) + y &= 55 \\
4(12) + y &= 55 \\
48 + y &= 55 \\
y &= 55 - 48 \\
y &= 7
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the value of $y$ is $7$.

## Solving Systems of Equations by Exploiting Symmetry

**Example:** ${-5p + 8q = 110; 8p - 5q =-50$

If the solution to the system above is $(p, q)$, what is the value of $p + q$?

**Explanation**

First, we add the two equations and group like terms:

$$
\begin{aligned}
-5p + 8q &= 110 \\
8p - 5q &= -50 \\
(-5p + 8p) + (8q - 5q) &= 110 - 50 \\
3p + 3q &= 60
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we solve the resulting equation for $p + q{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
3p + 3q &= 60 \\
3(p + q) &= 60 \\
p + q &= \frac{60}{3} \\
p + q &= 20
\end{aligned}
$$

Hence, the value of $p+q$ is $20$.

## Solving Systems of Equations by Introducing New Variables

**Example:** ${(2x - 3) - 4(y + 4) = 38; (2x - 3) + 4(y + 4) = 82$

The equations form a system. The solution is $(x, y)$. What is the value of $5(2x - 3)$?

**Explanation**

First, we let $w=2x-3$ and

$$
z=y+4
$$

so that the system may be written as follows:

$$
{w - 4z = 38; w + 4z = 82
$$

We will solve the system using the elimination method.

Notice that when we add the two equations, the $4z$ terms will cancel:

$$
\begin{aligned}
w- 4z &= 38 \\
w + 4z &= 82 \\
(w + w) &= (38 + 82) \\
2w &= 120 \\
w &= 60
\end{aligned}
$$

Since $w=2x-3$, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
5(2x - 3) &= 5w \\
&= 5(60) \\
&= 300
\end{aligned}
$$
