## Introduction

A common task on the math section of the SAT is solving systems of linear equations. In simple cases, this only requires a quick substitution.

In this topic, we'll practice solving systems in which one variable or equation is already solved for a variable, as well as cases that require solving for a variable before substitution.

---

<a id="solving-a-system-where-both-equations-are-solved-for-the-same-variable"></a>
## Solving a System Where Both Equations Are Solved for the Same Variable

**Example:** The equations $y = \dfrac{3}{4}x$ and $y = -\dfrac{2}{5}x$ form a system with solution $(x, y)$. What is the value of $x$?

**Explanation**

Consider the given system of equations:

$$
{y = \frac{3}{4}x; y =-\frac{2}{5}x
$$

Since both expressions equal $y$, we equate them to solve for $x{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{3}{4}x &= -\frac{2}{5}x \\
\frac{3}{4}x + \frac{2}{5}x &= 0 \\
\frac{15}{20}x + \frac{8}{20}x &= 0 \\
\frac{23}{20}x &= 0 \\
x &= \frac{20}{23} \cdot 0 \\
x &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the value of $x$ is $0$.

## Solving a System Where Both Equations Are Solved for the Same Variable (Described in Words)

**Example:** The equations $y = \dfrac{4}{7}x - 3$ and $y = -3x + 2$ form a system. The solution to the system is $(x, y)$. What is the value of $x$?

**Explanation**

Consider the given system of equations:

$$
{y = \frac{4}{7}x - 3; y =-3x + 2
$$

Since both expressions are equal to $y$, we can set them equal to each other and solve for $x{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{4}{7}x - 3 &= -3x + 2 \\
\frac{4}{7}x + 3x &= 2 + 3 \\
\frac{4}{7}x + \frac{21}{7}x &= 5 \\
\frac{25}{7}x &= 5 \\
x &= \frac{5 \cdot 7}{25} \\
x &= 7 5
\end{aligned}
$$

## Solving Systems Where One Variable is Given

**Example:** The solution to the given system of equations is $(x, y)$. What is the value of $x$?

**Explanation**

$$
{3x + 5y = 20; y = 7
$$

We know from the second equation that $y = 7$.

Therefore, we substitute this value into the first equation and solve for $x{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
3x + 5y &= 20 \\
3x + 5(7) &= 20 \\
3x + 35 &= 20 \\
3x &= 20 - 35 \\
3x &= -15 \\
x &= \frac{-15}{3} \\
x &= -5
\end{aligned}
$$

## Solving Systems Where One Equation is Solved for a Variable

**Example:** ${y =-3x - 5; 5y = 2x - 8$

The solution to the given system of equations is $(x, y)$. What is the value of $x - y$?

**Explanation**

Since the first equation is already solved for $y$, we can substitute $y = -3x - 5$ into the second equation and solve:

$$
\begin{aligned}
5y &= 2x - 8 \\
5(-3x - 5) &= 2x - 8 \\
- 15x - 25 &= 2x - 8 \\
- 15x - 2x &= -8 + 25 \\
- 17x &= 17 \\
x &= -1
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, using the equality

$$
y = -3x - 5
$$

we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
x - y &= x - (-3x - 5) \\
&= x + 3x + 5 \\
&= 4x + 5
\end{aligned}
$$

Hence,

$$
\begin{aligned}
x - y &= 4x + 5 \\
&= 4(-1) + 5 \\
&=-4 + 5 \\
&= 1
\end{aligned}
$$

## Solving Systems That Require Solving an Equation for a Variable

**Example:** ${5x = 40; 2x + y =-1$

The solution to the given system of equations is $(x, y)$. What is the value of $y$?

**Explanation**

First, we solve the first equation for $x{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
5x &= 40 \\
x &= \frac{40}{5} \\
x &= 8
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, we substitute this value into the second equation and solve for $y{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
2x + y &= -1 \\
2(8) + y &= -1 \\
16 + y &= -1 \\
y &= -1 - 16 \\
y &= -17
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the value of $y$ is $-17$.
