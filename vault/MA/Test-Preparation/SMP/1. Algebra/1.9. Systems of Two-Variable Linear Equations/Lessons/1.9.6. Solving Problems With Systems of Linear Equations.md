## Introduction

In this topic, we'll practice modeling real-world situations using systems of linear equations and then solving them to find a solution to the original problem. We'll mostly use slope-intercept form to construct our linear models.

---

<a id="modeling-and-solving-slope-intercept-systems-of-equations"></a>
## Modeling and Solving Slope-Intercept Systems of Equations

**Example:** In a school reading poll, four times as many students preferred fiction over nonfiction. It was also reported that $120$ more students preferred fiction than nonfiction. According to this data, how many students preferred nonfiction?

**Explanation**

Let $f$ be the number of students who preferred fiction, and $n$ be the number who preferred nonfiction.

Let's establish the linear equations that model the given situation.

- The poll states that four times as many students preferred fiction over nonfiction. Therefore, we have
$f = 4n$.
- It was also reported that $120$ more students preferred fiction than nonfiction. So, we have
$f = n + 120$.

Putting these equations together, we have the following system of equations:

$$
{f = 4n; f = n + 120
$$

We will solve the system using the substitution method. The first equation $f = 4n$ is already solved for $f$, so we can substitute it into the second equation and solve for $n{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
f &= n + 120 \\
4n &= n + 120 \\
4n - n &= 120 \\
3n &= 120 \\
n &= \frac{120}{3} \\
n &= 40
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, $40$ students preferred nonfiction.

## Modeling and Solving Mixed Systems of Equations

**Example:** A garden path that is $46\,\text{m}$ long is divided into two segments. One segment has a length of $x\,\text{m}$ and the other has a length of $y\,\text{m}$. The value of $x$ is $4$ more than $2$ times the value of $y$. What is the value of $x$?

**Explanation**

Let's establish the linear equations that model the given situation.

- The total length of the path is $46$ meters. Therefore, we have
$x + y = 46$.
- Since the value of $x$ is $4$ more than $2$ times the value of $y$, we have
$x = 2y + 4$.

Putting these equations together, we have the following system of equations:

$$
{x + y = 46; x = 2y + 4
$$

We will solve the system using the substitution method. The second equation

$$
x = 2y + 4
$$

is already solved for $x$, so we can substitute it into the first equation and solve for $y{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
x + y &= 46 \\
(2y + 4) + y &= 46 \\
3y + 4 &= 46 \\
3y &= 42 \\
y &= \frac{42}{3} \\
y &= 14
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we can substitute $y = 14$ back into the equation

$$
x = 2y + 4
$$

to solve for $x{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
x &= 2y + 4 \\
&= 2(14) + 4 \\
&= 28 + 4 \\
&= 32
\end{aligned}
$$

## Modeling and Solving Speed, Distance, and Time Problems

**Example:** During a bus ride through hilly terrain, a driver covered $d$ kilometers downhill at $25\,\text{km/h}$ and $u$ kilometers uphill at $10\,\text{km/h}$. The total distance of the trip was $55$ kilometers, and the bus spent three times as many hours going uphill as it did going downhill. How many kilometers did the bus travel uphill?

**Explanation**

Let's establish the linear equations that model the given situation.

- The total distance of the trip was $55$ kilometers, so
$d + u = 55$.
- Since $\text{time} = \dfrac{\text{distance}}{\text{speed}}$, the bus spent $\dfrac{d}{25}$ hours going downhill and $\dfrac{u}{10}$ hours going uphill. Because the bus spent three times as many hours uphill as downhill, we have
$\dfrac{u}{10} = 3 \cdot \dfrac{d}{25}$.

Putting these equations together, we have the following system of equations:

$$
{d + u = 55; \frac{u}{10} = 3 \cdot \frac{d}{25}
$$

We will solve the system using the substitution method. We solve for $d$ in the first equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
d + u &= 55 \\
d &= 55 - u
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we can substitute $d = 55 - u$ into the second equation and solve for $u{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{u}{10} &= 3 \cdot \frac{d}{25} \\
\frac{u}{10} &= 3 \cdot \frac{55 - u}{25} \\
u &= 30 \cdot \frac{55 - u}{25} \\
u &= (6(55 - u))/(5) \\
5u &= 6(55 - u) \\
5u &= 330 - 6u \\
11u &= 330 \\
u &= \frac{330}{11} \\
u &= 30
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the bus traveled $30$ kilometers uphill.
