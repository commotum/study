## Finding the Line Passing Through a Point and Perpendicular to a Line Given in General Form

**Example:** In the $xy$-plane, line $p$ is defined by $-3x + y = 5$. Line $q$ is perpendicular to line $p$ and has a $y$-intercept at $(0, -4)$. Find the equation of line $q$.

**Explanation**

First, we rewrite the given line in slope-intercept form to find its slope:

$$
-3x + y = 5 \quad \implies \quad y = 3x + 5
$$

So, the slope of line $p$ is $3$.

A line perpendicular to this line will have slope

$$
-\dfrac{1}{3}
$$

since the slopes of perpendicular lines are negative reciprocals.

The perpendicular line must pass through $(0, -4)$. So its equation in slope-intercept form is

$$
y = -\frac{1}{3}x - 4
$$

To write this in standard form, we multiply both sides by 3 and rearrange:

$$
x + 3y = -12
$$

Therefore, an equation of line $q$ is

$$
x + 3y = -12
$$

## Finding the Ratio of Coefficients Given a General Equation of a Perpendicular Line

**Example:** The line $ax + by = -6$ is perpendicular to the line $9y + 2x = 15$. What is the value of $\dfrac{b}{a}$?

**Explanation**

First, we rewrite both lines in slope-intercept form:

$$
by = -ax - 6 \qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad y = -\dfrac{a}{b}x - \dfrac{6}{b}
$$

$$
9y = -2x + 15 \qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad y = -\dfrac{2}{9}x + \dfrac{5}{3}
$$

So, the slopes of the lines are

$$
-\dfrac{a}{b}
$$

and

$$
-\dfrac{2}{9}
$$

respectively.

The slopes of perpendicular lines are negative reciprocals. The reciprocal of

$$
-\dfrac{2}{9}
$$

is

$$
-\dfrac{9}{2}
$$

and changing the sign gives:

$$
-\dfrac{a}{b} = \dfrac{9}{2} \quad \Longrightarrow \quad \dfrac{b}{a} = -\dfrac{2}{9}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\dfrac{b}{a} = -\dfrac{2}{9}
$$

## Identifying a Pair of Perpendicular Lines Given a General Equation of Other Perpendicular Lines

**Example:** Consider the equations $4x - y = 1$ and $mx + ny = 1$, where $m$ and $n$ are constants. The graphs of these equations in the $xy$-plane represent perpendicular lines. Which of the following pairs of equations also represents a pair of perpendicular lines?

1. $4x + y = 1$ and $mx + 2ny = 1$
2. $4x + y = 1$ and $mx - ny = 1$
3. $8x - 2y = 1$ and $2mx + ny = 1$

**Explanation**

To find the slope of the line $4x - y = 1$, we rewrite the equation in slope-intercept form:

$$
-y = -4x + 1 \quad \Rightarrow \quad y = 4x - 1
$$

So, the slope is $4$.

Now, a line is perpendicular to another line when its slope is the negative reciprocal of that other line's slope. That means the second line must have slope

$$
-\dfrac{1}{4}
$$

From the line equation

$$
mx + ny = 1
$$

it follows that the slope of the second line is

$$
-\dfrac{m}{n}
$$

So we set

$$
-\dfrac{m}{n} = -\dfrac{1}{4} \quad \Rightarrow \quad \dfrac{m}{n} = \dfrac{1}{4}
$$

Now, let's utilise this information to check which pair of lines is perpendicular:

- First, we consider the following lines:
$\begin{bmatrix}4x + y = 1 & ⇒y =-4x + 1 \\ mx + 2ny = 1 & ⇒y =-\frac{m}{2n}x + \frac{1}{2n}\end{bmatrix}$
The slope of the first line is $-4$.
The slope of the second line is
$-\frac{m}{2n}|=-\frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{m}{n}; =-\frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{4}; =-\frac{1}{8}$.
Since the product of slopes does not equal $-1$, this pair of lines is NOT perpendicular.
- Next, we consider the following lines:
$\begin{bmatrix}4x + y = 1 & ⇒y =-4x + 1 \\ mx - ny = 1 & ⇒y = \frac{m}{n}x - \frac{1}{n}\end{bmatrix}$
The slope of the first line is $-4$.
The slope of the second line is
$\dfrac m n = \dfrac 1 4$.
Since the product of the slopes equals $-1$, this pair of lines is perpendicular.
- Finally, we consider the following lines:
$\begin{bmatrix}8x - 2y = 1 & ⇒y = 4x - \frac{1}{2} \\ 2mx + ny = 1 & ⇒y =-\frac{2m}{n}x + \frac{1}{n}\end{bmatrix}$
The slope of the first line is $4$.
The slope of the second line is
$-\frac{2m}{n}|=-2 \cdot \frac{m}{n}; =-2 \cdot \frac{1}{4}; =-\frac{1}{2}$
Since the product of slopes does not equal $-1$, this pair of lines is NOT perpendicular.

Therefore, the correct answer is "II only."
