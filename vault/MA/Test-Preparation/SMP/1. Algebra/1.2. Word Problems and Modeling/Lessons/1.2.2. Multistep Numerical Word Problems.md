## Solving Problems Involving Subtraction, Division, and Rounding

**Example:** A city park begins a renovation project with $6,900$ gallons of paint. If about $84$ gallons are used per day, based on this rate, after how many days will there be $1,236$ gallons left?

**Explanation**

First, we subtract the target number of gallons from the starting amount to find how much paint is used:

$$
6,900 - 1,236 = 5,664
$$

Next, we divide that amount by $84$ to find how many days it takes:

$$
\frac{5,664}{84} = 67.42\ldots
$$

Since the number of days must be a whole number and the supply doesn’t reach the target until the full day is completed, we round up:
$68$

So, it will take *$68$ days* for the paint supply to reach $1,236$ gallons.

## Solving Problems Involving Multiplication, Subtraction, and Division

**Example:** Tara has $\[math]66.00$ at a gaming arcade. She buys $6$ accessories that each cost $\[/math]3.00$. She then uses the rest of her money to buy video game tokens that cost $\[math]3.00$ each. How many tokens can Tara buy?

**Explanation**

First, we calculate how much Tara spent on accessories:

$$
6 \times 3.00 = 18.00
$$

Next, we subtract that amount from the total to find the remaining money:

$$
66.00 - 18.00 = 48.00
$$

Finally, we divide the remaining amount by the cost per token:

$$
\frac{48.00}{3.00} = 16
$$

So, Tara can buy *$16$ tokens* with the rest of her money.

## Solving Problems Involving Fraction Multiplication

**Example:** During her marathon training, Mia runs $\dfrac{11}{20}$ of the $200$ kilometers in her plan each week. If Mia keeps up this training pace, how many kilometers will she run in $3$ weeks?

**Explanation**

First, we find how many kilometers Mia runs each week by taking

$$
\dfrac{11}{20}
$$

of $200$ kilometers. This gives

$$
\frac{11}{20} \times 200 = 110
$$

So each week, Mia runs $110$ kilometers.

We can now find the total distance run in $3$ weeks to be

$$
110 \times 3 = 330
$$

Consequently, Mia will run $\bbox[4pt,border: 1px solid lightgray]{330}$ kilometers in $3$ weeks.
