## Introduction

In the math section of the SAT test, word problems may require one step or multiple steps, sometimes involving operations such as addition, subtraction, multiplication, division, or rounding.

Make sure to double-check your arithmetic and ensure that your final answer is accurate, uses the correct units, and conforms to the requested format.

## One-Step Problems

**Example:** A student collected a total of $38$ pencils, including $14$ mechanical pencils and some wooden pencils. How many wooden pencils did she collect?

**Explanation**

The total number of pencils is the sum of the mechanical and wooden pencils. Since the total is $38$ and $14$ of those are mechanical pencils, we subtract $14$ from $38$ to find the number of wooden pencils:

$$
38 - 14 = 24
$$

So, the student collected $24$ wooden pencils.

## Computing a Minimum by Rounding

**Example:** A farm needs to store $600$ kilograms of hay. If each bale of hay weighs $45$ kilograms, what is the greatest number of **full** bales that can be stored on the farm?

**Explanation**

To find the maximum number of full bales that can be stored, we divide the total weight of the hay by the weight of each bale:

$$
\frac{600}{45} \approx 13.33
$$

Since only full bales can be stored, we must round down to the nearest whole number.

So, the greatest number of **full** bales that can be stored on the farm is $13$.

## Two-Step Problems Involving Addition and Subtraction

**Example:** A builder uses three wooden beams to support a roof. Two of the beams are each $45$ inches long. The total length of all three beams is $128$ inches. What is the length, in inches, of the third beam?

**Explanation**

Two beams are the same length. The total length is the sum of all three beam lengths.

First, we find the combined length of the two equal beams:

$$
45 + 45 = 90
$$

Next, we subtract that from the total length to find the length of the third beam:

$$
128 - 90 = 38
$$

So, the third beam is *$38$ inches* long.

## Two-Step Problems Involving Subtraction and Division

**Example:** A metal rod that is $108$ centimeters long is cut into three parts. One part is $36$ centimeters long. The other two parts are equal in length. What is the length, in centimeters, of one of the other two parts?

**Explanation**

We are told that one part is $36$ centimeters long and the total rod length is $108$ centimeters. First, subtract the known part from the total to find the combined length of the other two parts:

$$
108 - 36 = 72
$$

Since the remaining two parts are equal in length, we divide $72$ by $2$:

$$
\frac{72}{2} = 36
$$

So, the length of one of the equal parts is *$36$ centimeters*.
