## Introduction

In the math section of the SAT, you may be asked to model a real-world situation with a one-variable linear equation and then apply it to find a solution to a problem. In this topic, we'll practice doing just that.

<a id="constructing-and-solving-addition-and-subtraction-equations"></a>
## Constructing and Solving Addition and Subtraction Equations

**Example:** A language learning app charges a one-time registration fee of $\[math]28$ and a monthly fee of $\[/math]14$. If there are no additional charges, after how many months will a customer have paid a total of $\[math]196$?

**Explanation**

Let the number of months be $m$. The total cost includes a one-time fee of $\[math]28$ and a monthly cost of $\[/math]14$ for each of the $m$ months, so we write this as the following equation:

$$
196 = 14m + 28
$$

To solve for $m$, we first subtract $28$ from both sides:

$$
\begin{aligned}
196 - 28 &= 14m \\
168 &= 14m
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we divide both sides by $14{:}$

$$
m = \dfrac{168}{14} = 12
$$

So it will take *$12$ months* to reach exactly $\[math]196$ in charges.

## Solving Problems Using Linear Equations With Rounding

**Example:** A tutoring company charges a one-time registration fee of $\[math]42$ and a monthly tuition fee of $\[/math]15$. If there are no additional charges, after how many months will a student have paid a total of $\[math]175$?

**Explanation**

Let the number of months be $m$. The total cost includes a one-time fee of $\[math]42$ and a monthly cost of $\[/math]15$ for each of the $m$ months, so we write this as the following equation:

$$
175 = 15m + 42
$$

To solve for $m$, we first subtract $42$ from both sides:

$$
\begin{aligned}
175 - 42 &= 15m \\
133 &= 15m
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we divide both sides by $15{:}$

$$
m = \dfrac{133}{15} = 8.86\ldots
$$

Since the cost after exactly $8$ months would not yet reach $\[math]175$, we round up to the next whole number.

So it will take *$9$ months* to reach or exceed $\[/math]175$ in charges.

## Solving Multistep Problems Using Linear Equations

**Example:** A snack company ships boxes of chips in two sizes. Each small box contains $7$ bags of chips, and each large box contains $12$ bags. A total of $213$ bags of chips were shipped. If $9$ of the boxes contained $12$ bags each, and the remaining are $7$-bag boxes, how many $7$-bag boxes were shipped?

**Explanation**

Let $x$ represent the number of $7$-bag boxes shipped.

We are told there are $9$ boxes that each contain $12$ bags, so their total number of bags is

$$
12(9) = 108
$$

The total number of bags shipped is $213$. So, the number of bags in the $7$-bag boxes plus the bags in the $12$-bag boxes must equal $213{:}$

$$
7x + 108 = 213
$$

Now we solve for $x$. Subtracting $108$ from both sides, we get

$$
7x = 105
$$

Then, we divide both sides by $7{:}$

$$
x = 15
$$

Therefore, $15$ seven-bag boxes were shipped.

## Solving Multistep Problems Using Linear Equations With Decimal Coefficients

**Example:** A farmer bought some hens and goats. The hens cost $\[math]3.75$ each, and the goats cost $\[/math]4.80$ each. If the farmer spent $\[math]26.70$ in total and bought twice as many goats as hens, how many goats did he buy?

**Explanation**

We are told that hens cost $\[math]3.75$ each, goats cost $\[/math]4.80$ each, and the farmer spent $\[math]26.70$ in total.

Let $x$ represent the number of hens. Since he bought twice as many goats as hens, the number of goats is $2x$.

The total cost of the hens is $\[/math]3.75x$, and the total cost of the goats is

$$
\[math]4.80(2x) = \[/math]9.60x
$$

So, we can write the equation:

$$
3.75x + 9.60x = 26.70
$$

Now we combine like terms and solve for $x{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
13.35x &= 26.70 \\
x &= 2
\end{aligned}
$$

Since the number of goats is $2x$, the number of goats is $2(2) = 4$.

Therefore, the farmer bought $4$ goats.
