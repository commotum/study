## Modeling With One-Step Inequalities and Solving Using Addition

**Example:** A camera's memory card can store at most $2\,000$ photos. It currently has $1\,345$ photos stored. What is the maximum **additional number of photos** that can be taken without exceeding the memory limit?

**Explanation**

Let $p$ represent the additional number of photos that can be taken. Since the memory card currently has $1\,345$ photos stored, the total becomes

$$
1345 + p
$$

Because the memory card can hold *no more than* $2\,000$ photos, we write the inequality

$$
1345 + p \leq 2000
$$

Solving this, we get

$$
p \leq 655
$$

So, the memory card can store *at most* $655$ additional photos.

## Modeling With One-Step Inequalities and Solving Using Multiplication

**Example:** Chloe is reading a novel and wants to finish at least $18\,000$ words this evening. She reads at a pace of $300$ words per minute. What is the fewest number of minutes she must read to meet her goal?

**Explanation**

This is a rate problem involving reading speed and time. We use the relationship

$$
\text{words} = \text{rate} \times \text{time}
$$

Let $t$ be the number of minutes Chloe reads. Since she reads at a pace of $300$ words per minute, the total number of words read is

$$
300t
$$

She wants to read *at least* $18\,000$ words, so we write this as the following inequality:

$$
300t \geq 18\,000
$$

Now, we divide both sides by $3\,000$ to obtain

$$
t \geq 60
$$

So, Chloe must read for *at least* $60$ minutes. Since the question asks for the fewest number of minutes, the answer is $60$.

## Modeling and Solving With Two-Step Linear Inequalities

**Example:** Noah is buying supply kits for a robotics challenge. There is a one-time handling fee of $\[math]17$, and each kit costs $\[/math]16$. Noah must spend *at least* $\[math]113$ in total. If he can only purchase a whole number of kits, what is the minimum number of kits he must order?

**Explanation**

Let $k$ represent the number of kits Noah orders.

The total cost is given by the cost per kit times the number of kits plus the handling fee:

$$
16k + 17 \geq 113
$$

We solve the inequality step by step:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}16k + 17 & \ge 113 \\ 16k & \ge 113 - 17 \\ 16k & \ge 96 \\ k & \ge 6\end{bmatrix}
$$

Since the number of kits must be a whole number, the minimum number of kits Noah must order is $6$.

## Modeling and Solving With Two-Step Inequalities and Rounding

**Example:** A tutor is planning a weekend workshop. The venue rental is a one-time cost of $\[math]35$, and each participant receives materials costing $\[/math]18.25$. If the tutor’s total budget is $\[math]200$, what is the greatest number of participants that can attend without exceeding the budget?

**Explanation**

We are given a scenario with a fixed cost and a per-participant cost. We are told the total cost must not exceed $\[math]200$, and we are asked to find the maximum number of participants the tutor can afford.

Let $x$ be the number of participants.

The total cost is the fixed fee plus the per-participant fee:

$$
\text{Total cost} = 35 + 18.25x
$$

We are told this total must not exceed $\[/math]200$, so we write the inequality:

$$
35 + 18.25x \leq 200
$$

We now solve the inequality for $x{:}$

$$
\begin{bmatrix}35 + 18.25x & \le 200 \\ 18.25x & \le 200 - 35 \\ 18.25x & \le 165 \\ x & \le \frac{165}{18.25} \\ x & \le 9.0410…\end{bmatrix}
$$

Since $x$ must be a whole number, the greatest possible number of participants is $9$.
