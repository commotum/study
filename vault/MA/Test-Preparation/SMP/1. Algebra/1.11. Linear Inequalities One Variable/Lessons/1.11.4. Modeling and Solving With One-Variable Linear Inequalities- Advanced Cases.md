## Determining a Possible Solution to a Problem Modeled Using Inequalities

**Example:** A dance studio offers a monthly unlimited class pass for $\[math]135$. Individual classes cost $\[/math]7.00$, $\[math]8.75$, or $\[/math]9.50$, depending on the class type. What is the minimum number of classes someone must attend for the unlimited pass to be more cost-effective than paying per class?

**Explanation**

To find the fewest number of classes that makes the unlimited pass worthwhile, we assume each class costs the highest price of $\[math]9.50$.

Let $x$ represent the number of classes attended.

Without the pass, the total cost is $\[/math]9.50$ per class:
$9.50x$

With the pass, the cost is a flat $\[math]135$.

We want the pass to cost less than paying individually. This gives the inequality:

$$
135 < 9.50x
$$

Now, solve the inequality:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}135 & < 9.50x \\ \frac{135}{9.50} & < x \\ 14.210… & < x\end{bmatrix}
$$

Since $x$ must be a whole number, the smallest value that satisfies the inequality is $15$.

## Modeling and Solving a Physics Problem Using a Linear Inequality

**Example:** In an electric circuit, Ohm's law is given by the formula

$I = \dfrac{V}{R}$,

where $I$ is the current in amperes, $V$ is the potential difference in volts, and $R$ is the resistance in ohms.

Suppose a circuit has a resistance of $720$ ohms, and its potential difference comes from $n$ ten-volt batteries, producing a total of $10n$ volts. If the current in the circuit must not exceed $0.9$ amperes, what is the greatest number $n$ of batteries that can be used?

**Explanation**

We are given the formula:

$$
I = \dfrac{V}{R}
$$

and told that $V = 10n$ and $R = 720$. The current must satisfy

$$
\dfrac{10n}{720} \leq 0.9
$$

Next, we multiply both sides by $720{:}$

$$
10n \leq 648
$$

Now, we divide both sides by $10{:}$

$$
n \leq \dfrac{648}{10} = 64.8
$$

Since $n$ must be an integer, the greatest possible value is $64$.

## Modeling and Solving a Rectangular Solids Problem Using a Linear Inequality

**Example:** An aquarium builder restricts tank dimensions for a standard shelf. For tanks shaped like rectangular prisms, the sum of the perimeter of the base and the height must not exceed $160$ inches. The perimeter of the base is calculated using the width and length. If a tank has a height of $40$ inches and its length is $1.5$ times its width, find the inequality that represents the possible values of the width $w$, in inches.

**Explanation**

Let $w$ be the width of the tank. Since the length is $1.5w$, the perimeter of the base is

$$
2(w + 1.5w) = 2(2.5w) = 5w
$$

The sum of the perimeter and the height must be no more than $160$ inches. Therefore,

$$
5w + 40 \leq 160
$$

Next, we subtract $40$ from both sides:

$$
5w \leq 120
$$

Now, we divide both sides by $5$:

$$
w \leq 24
$$

Since width must be positive, the inequality is

$$
0 < w \leq 24
$$

## Modeling and Solving a Two-Step Linear Inequality With Variables on Both Sides

**Example:** In a set of three consecutive even integers, where the integers are ordered from least to greatest, the first integer is represented by $s$. The product of $11$ and the second even integer is no more than $22$ less than the sum of the first and third even integers. Find the inequality that represents this situation.

**Explanation**

Let the three consecutive even integers be

$$
s,\quad s + 2,\quad s + 4
$$

The second even integer is $s + 2$, so $11$ times that is

$$
11(s + 2)
$$

The sum of the first and third even integers is

$$
s + (s + 4)=2s + 4
$$

We are told that the product is *no more than* $22$ less than this sum, so we subtract $22$ from the sum and write the inequality:

$$
11(s + 2) \leq 2s + 4 - 22
$$
