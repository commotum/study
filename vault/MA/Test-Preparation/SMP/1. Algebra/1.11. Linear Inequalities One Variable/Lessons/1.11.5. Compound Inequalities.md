## Introduction

This topic provides practice in constructing and interpreting bounded or compound inequalities by combining multiple conditions into a single statement, determining their solutions, and applying the so-called triangle inequality to related problems.

---

<a id="constructing-compound-inequalities"></a>
## Constructing Compound Inequalities

**Example:** In a water system, the pressure in a pipe varied between a minimum of $45.5$ pounds per square inch and a maximum of $60.0$ pounds per square inch. Find the inequality that is true for all values of $p$, where $p$ is the pressure in pounds per square inch in the pipe.

**Explanation**

The pressure $p$ must be at least $45.5$ pounds per square inch and at most $60.0$ pounds per square inch. This means $p$ satisfies

$$
45.5 \leq p \leq 60.0
$$

Therefore, the correct answer is

$$
45.5 \leq p \leq 60.0
$$

## Identifying a Solution of a Compound Inequality

**Example:** A bakery packs muffins into trays. Each tray holds at least $8$ muffins but no more than $12$. Which of the following could be the total number of muffins in $6$ trays?

1. $40\qquad$ II. $45\qquad$ III. $60\qquad$ IV. $75$

**Explanation**

Each tray can hold between $8$ and $12$ muffins. For $6$ trays, the minimum total is

$$
8 \times 6 = 48
$$

muffins, and the maximum total is

$$
12 \times 6 = 72
$$

muffins.

So, the total number of muffins must be between $48$ and $72$ inclusive.

Among the choices, only $60$ is within this range.

## Applying the Triangle Inequality

**Example:** According to the triangle inequality theorem, the sum of any two sides of a triangle must be greater than the length of the third side. Given that a triangle has side lengths of $7$ and $10$, find the inequality that shows the possible lengths, $x$, of the third side.

**Explanation**

We are told that, according to the triangle inequality theorem, the sum of any two sides of a triangle must be greater than the third side.

Applying the triangle inequality to the three combinations of side lengths, we obtain the following three inequalities:
$\begin{bmatrix}7 + 10 & > x \\ 7 + x & > 10 \\ 10 + x & > 7\end{bmatrix}$

Now, we simplify each inequality:
$\begin{bmatrix}x & < 17 \\ x & > 3 \\ x & > - 3\end{bmatrix}$

Next, since side lengths are positive, the valid lower bound is $x > 3$. So, we have
$3 < x < 17$.

Therefore, the correct inequality is $3 < x < 17$.
