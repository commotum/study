## Introduction

This topic provides practice in modeling with compound inequalities. We'll construct appropriate expressions to represent multi-step word problems and linear growth relationships and solve the resulting inequalities to determine valid ranges of values.

---

<a id="modeling-with-compound-inequalities-word-problems-with-additional-steps"></a>
## Modeling With Compound Inequalities: Word Problems With Additional Steps

**Example:** A volunteer estimates that she delivers between $5$ and $8$ gallons of water per trip. Based on this estimate, find the inequality that represents the total number of gallons, $g$, she could deliver in $15$ trips.

**Explanation**

We are told that the volunteer delivers between $5$ and $8$ gallons per trip.

To find the total number of gallons delivered in $15$ trips, we multiply both the minimum $(5)$ and maximum $(8)$ gallons by $15$. This gives
$5 \times 15 \[5pt]$
and
$8 \times 15$.

So, the total number of gallons $g$ must satisfy

$$
5 \cdot 15 \leq g \leq 8 \cdot 15
$$

Therefore, the inequality is

$$
5 \cdot 15 \leq g \leq 8 \cdot 15
$$

## Modeling With Compound Inequalities Using a Linear Growth Expression

**Example:** $S = 100t + 50$
The formula above shows the relationship between the speed $S$, in kilometers per hour, and the throttle level $t$ of an electric scooter. When $t = 0$, the scooter is stationary. When $t = 1$, the scooter is at full throttle. It is recommended that $t$ be between $0.1$ and $0.7$ for safe riding. Find the inequality that describes the target speed $S$ while riding.

**Explanation**

We are asked to determine the speed range based on a given formula and a recommended throttle interval.

The formula is

$$
S = 100t + 50
$$

At minimum recommended throttle:

$$
\begin{aligned}
S &= 100(0.1) + 50 \\
&= 10 + 50 \\
&= 60
\end{aligned}
$$

At maximum recommended throttle:

$$
\begin{aligned}
S &= 100(0.7) + 50 \\
&= 70 + 50 \\
&= 120
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the target speed $S$ must satisfy

$$
60 \leq S \leq 120
$$

Therefore, the inequality is

$$
60 \leq S \leq 120
$$

## Modeling With Compound Inequalities by Constructing a Linear Growth Expression

**Example:** A chemical reaction begins at a temperature of $25^\circ$C and the temperature rises by more than $0.2^\circ$C but less than $0.4^\circ$C per hour. Find the inequality that shows all possible temperatures $t$, in degrees Celsius, after $100$ hours.

**Explanation**

We are told that the reaction starts at $25^\circ$C.

Since the temperature rises by more than $0.2^\circ$C but less than $0.4^\circ$C per hour, the minimum and maximum possible temperatures after $100$ hours are

$$
\begin{aligned}
25 + (0.2 \cdot 100) &= 25 + 20 \\
&= 45
\end{aligned}
$$

and

$$
\begin{aligned}
25 + (0.4 \cdot 100) &= 25 + 40 \\
&= 65
\end{aligned}
$$

degrees Celsius, respectively.

So, the temperature $t$ after $100$ hours must satisfy
$45 < t < 65$.

Therefore, the inequality is $45 < t < 65$.

## Modeling and Solving a Compound Inequality

**Example:** A car’s fuel efficiency includes a base rate of $e$ km/L plus an improvement of $7\%$ based on engine tuning hours $h$. The goal is for the total efficiency to be at least $1.2$ times and at most $1.8$ times the base rate. Find the inequality that shows all possible values of $h$ that meet this goal.

**Explanation**

We are told that the total efficiency is $e + 0.07h$.

To meet the goal, it must be at least $1.2e$ and at most $1.8e$. So, we have

$$
1.2e \leq e + 0.07h \leq 1.8e
$$

Then, we subtract $e$ throughout:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}1.2e - e & \le 0.07h \le 1.8e - e \\ 0.2e & \le 0.07h \le 0.8e\end{bmatrix}
$$

Finally, we divide by $0.07{:}$

$$
\dfrac{0.2e}{0.07} \leq h \leq \dfrac{0.8e}{0.07}
$$

Therefore, the inequality is

$$
\dfrac{0.2e}{0.07} \leq h \leq \dfrac{0.8e}{0.07}
$$
