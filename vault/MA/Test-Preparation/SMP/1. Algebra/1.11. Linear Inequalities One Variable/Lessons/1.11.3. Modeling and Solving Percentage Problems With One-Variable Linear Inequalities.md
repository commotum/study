## Modeling a Percentage Problem Using a One-Step Inequality

**Example:** To determine the popularity of a video game, a developer surveyed $350$ players. Among the first $210$ players, $75$ rated the game as excellent. Among the remaining $140$ players, $g$ rated it as excellent. If more than $40\%$ of all players rated the game as excellent, find the inequality that describes the possible values of $g$.

**Explanation**

The total number of players surveyed is $350$.

The number of players who rated the game as excellent is $g + 75$, where $g$ is the number from the second group.

We are told that *more than* $40\%$ of players rated it as excellent. That means

$$
g + 75 > 0.40(350)
$$

Therefore, this inequality must be satisfied, and since $g$ represents a count of people, it must also satisfy

$$
g \leq 140
$$

## Modeling and Solving a Percentage Problem Using a One-Step Inequality

**Example:** A robotics club is buying identical toolkits for each of the $50$ members. The total budget for the purchase is $\[math]7,000$, which includes a $6\%$ sales tax. What is the maximum possible price per toolkit, before sales tax, that the club can afford within the budget, rounded to two decimal places?

**Explanation**

Let $x$ represent the pre-tax price of one toolkit. The cost for $50$ toolkits before tax is $50x$. With $6\%$ sales tax, the total cost becomes

$$
50x \cdot 1.06
$$

This amount must be less than or equal to the budget:

$$
50x \cdot 1.06 \leq 7,000
$$

Now, we divide both sides by $1.06{:}$

$$
50x \leq \dfrac{7,000}{1.06} \approx 6,603.77
$$

Finally, dividing both sides by $50$ yields:

$$
x \leq \dfrac{6,603.77}{50} \approx 132.08
$$

So, the maximum possible price per toolkit, before sales tax, is approximately $\[math]132.08$.

## Modeling and Solving a Percentage Problem Using a Two-Step Inequality

**Example:** Jamal works part-time building websites. He earns $\[math]20$ per hour for the first $4$ hours each week and $\[/math]26$ per hour for any additional hours. He saves $65\%$ of his weekly earnings. What is the minimum number of additional hours he must work to save at least $\[math]325$?

**Explanation**

Let $x$ be the number of additional hours Jamal works beyond the first $4$ hours.

He earns $\[math]20$ per hour for the first $4$ hours:

$$
20 \cdot 4 = 80
$$

He earns $\[/math]26$ for each additional hour:
$26x$

His total earnings are
$80 + 26x$.

He saves $65\%$ of his earnings:
$0.65(80 + 26x)$.

He wants to save *at least* $\[math]325$. We write this as the following inequality:

$$
0.65(80 + 26x) \geq 325
$$

Then, we divide both sides by $0.65$:

$$
80 + 26x \geq \dfrac{325}{0.65} = 500
$$

Now, we subtract $80$ from both sides:

$$
26x \geq 420
$$

Finally, we divide both sides by $26$:

$$
x \geq \dfrac{420}{26} \approx 16.15
$$

Since he cannot work a fraction of an hour, he must work *at least $17$ additional hours*.
