## Introduction

A common theme on the math section of the SAT exam is to solve one-step addition and subtraction equations containing slightly larger numbers than you may be used to. Some questions may also contain decimals.

The key to solving these questions is to double-check your arithmetic before submitting your answer! This is especially important for free-response questions.

## Solving One-Step Addition Equations

**Example:** $b + 23 = 510$
What value of $b$ is the solution to the given equation?

**Explanation**

We are given the equation

$$
b + 23 = 510
$$

and are asked to solve for $b$.

Let's solve the equation for $b{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
b + 23 &= 510 \\
b &= 510 - 23 \\
b &= 487
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the solution to the given equation is $487$.

## Solving One-Step Addition Equations (Free-Response)

**Example:** $t + 32 = 90$
What value of $t$ is the solution to the given equation?

**Explanation**

We are given the equation

$$
t + 32 = 90
$$

and are asked to solve for $t$.

Let's solve the equation for $t{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
t + 32 &= 90 \\
t &= 90 - 32 \\
t &= 58
\end{aligned}
$$

## Solving One-Step Addition Equations Containing Decimals (Free-Response)

**Example:** $2.1 + z = 5.0$
What value of $z$ is the solution to the given equation?

**Explanation**

We are given the equation

$$
2.1 + z = 5.0
$$

and are asked to solve for $z$.

To isolate the variable $z$, we subtract $2.1$ from both sides of the equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
z &= 5.0 - 2.1 \\
&= 2.9
\end{aligned}
$$
