## Introduction

Sometimes, it's more efficient to solve linear equations containing binomials by factoring them, rather than applying the distributive law. In this topic, we'll practice solving these types of questions.

---

<a id="equations-with-two-binomials-on-one-side"></a>
## Equations With Two Binomials on One Side

**Example:** $6(j - 2) - 3(j - 2) = 27$
What value of $j$ is the solution to the given equation?

**Explanation**

We are given the equation

$$
6(j - 2) - 3(j - 2) = 27
$$

and are asked to find the value of $j$ that satisfies it.

We begin by simplifying the left-hand side of the equation. Notice that both terms involve the same binomial $(j - 2)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
6(j - 2) - 3(j - 2) &= 27 \\
(6 - 3)(j - 2) &= 27 \\
3(j - 2) &= 27
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we divide both sides by $3{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
3(j - 2) &= 27 \\
j - 2 &= \frac{27}{3} \\
j - 2 &= 9
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we add $2$ to both sides:

$$
\begin{aligned}
j &= 9 + 2 \\
j &= 11
\end{aligned}
$$

## Equations Containing Fractions and Two Binomials on One Side

**Example:** $\dfrac{5}{6}(9w - 1) - \dfrac{1}{3}(9w - 1) = 9$
What value of $w$ is the solution to the given equation?

**Explanation**

We are given

$$
\dfrac{5}{6}(9w - 1) - \dfrac{1}{3}(9w - 1) = 9
$$

Notice that both terms on the left-hand side involve the same binomial expression $(9w - 1)$. So we factor it out and simplify:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(\frac{5}{6} - \frac{1}{3})(9w - 1) &= 9 \\
(\frac{5}{6} - \frac{2}{6})(9w - 1) &= 9 \\
\frac{1}{2}(9w - 1) &= 9
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, multiply both sides by $2$ and solve for $w{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
9w - 1 &= 18 \\
9w &= 19 \\
w &= \frac{19}{9}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the solution is

$$
w = \dfrac{19}{9}
$$

## Equations Containing Fractions or Decimals and Two Binomials on One Side (Free-Response)

**Example:** $0.6(3d + 1) - 0.2(3d + 1) = 4.0$
What value of $3d+1$ is the solution to the given equation?

**Explanation**

Notice that $(3d+1)$ is a common factor in both terms on the left-hand side.

We begin by factoring out $(3d+1){:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
0.6(3d + 1) - 0.2(3d + 1) &= 4.0 \\
(0.6 - 0.2)(3d + 1) &= 4.0 \\
0.4(3d + 1) &= 4.0
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we multiply both sides by $10$ to remove the decimals. This gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
10 \cdot 0.4(3d + 1) &= 10 \cdot 4.0 \\
4(3d + 1) &= 40
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we divide both sides by $4$ to isolate $(3d+1){:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
4(3d + 1) &= 40 \\
(4(3d + 1))/(4) &= \frac{40}{4} \\
3d + 1 &= 10
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the value of $3d+1$ is $10$.

## Solving Linear Equations With Common Binomials on Both Sides

**Example:** Solve for $p$ given that $14 - 6(p - 4) = 2 - 4(p - 4)$.

**Explanation**

Notice that $(p-4)$ is a common factor on both sides of the equation.

We must isolate $(p-4)$ on one side of the equation. We start by adding $4(p-4)$ to both sides:

$$
\begin{aligned}
14 - 6(p - 4) &= 2 - 4(p - 4) \\
14 - 6(p - 4) + 4(p - 4) &= 2 - 4(p - 4) + 4(p - 4) \\
14 - 6(p - 4) + 4(p - 4) &= 2
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we subtract $14$ from both sides, and factor $(p-4)$ on the left-hand side:

$$
\begin{aligned}
14 - 6(p - 4) + 4(p - 4) - 14 &= 2 - 14 \\
- 6(p - 4) + 4(p - 4) &= -12 \\
(-6 + 4)(p - 4) &= -12 \\
- 2(p - 4) &= -12
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we divide both sides by $-2$ and isolate $(p - 4){:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
-2(p - 4) &= -12 \\
(-2(p - 4))/(-2) &= \frac{-12}{-2} \\
p - 4 &= 6
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we solve the obtained linear equation for $p$, as usual:

$$
\begin{aligned}
p - 4 &= 6 \\
p - 4 + 4 &= 6 + 4 \\
p &= 10
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the answer is

$$
p=\bbox[4pt, border: 1px solid lightgray]{10}
$$
