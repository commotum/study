## Equations With Binomials on One Side

**Example:** If $2(2z + 5) + 8(2z + 5) = 120$, what is the value of $2z + 5$?

**Explanation**

We are given the equation

$$
2(2z + 5) + 8(2z + 5) = 120
$$

and are asked to find the value of $2z + 5$.

We begin by combining like terms:

$$
\begin{aligned}
2(2z + 5) + 8(2z + 5) &= 120 \\
(2 + 8)(2z + 5) &= 120 \\
10(2z + 5) &= 120
\end{aligned}
$$

Now we divide both sides by $10$ to isolate $2z + 5{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
2z + 5 &= \frac{120}{10} \\
&= 12
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the value of $2z + 5$ is $12$.

## Equations With Binomials on Both Sides

**Example:** If $7(2q - 3) = 5(2q - 3) + 18$, what is the value of $2q - 3$?

**Explanation**

We are given the equation

$$
7(2q - 3) = 5(2q - 3) + 18
$$

and are asked to find the value of $2q - 3$.

We begin by combining like terms:

$$
\begin{aligned}
7(2q - 3) &= 5(2q - 3) + 18 \\
7(2q - 3) - 5(2q - 3) &= 18 \\
(7 - 5)(2q - 3) &= 18 \\
2(2q - 3) &= 18
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we divide both sides by $2$ to isolate $2q - 3{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
2q - 3 &= 9
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the value of $2q - 3$ is $9$.

## Equations With Binomials and Constants on Both Sides

**Example:** If $9 - 7(3b + 2) = 6 - 5(3b + 2)$, then find $3b+2$.

**Explanation**

We are given the equation

$$
9 - 7(3b + 2) = 6 - 5(3b + 2)
$$

Notice that both sides contain the same binomial expression $(3b + 2)$. We solve by moving all terms involving $(3b + 2)$ to one side and constants to the other:

$$
\begin{aligned}
9 - 7(3b + 2) &= 6 - 5(3b + 2) \\
- 7(3b + 2) + 5(3b + 2) &= 6 - 9 \\
(-7 + 5)(3b + 2) &= -3 \\
(-2)(3b + 2) &= -3 \\
3b + 2 &= \frac{3}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$
