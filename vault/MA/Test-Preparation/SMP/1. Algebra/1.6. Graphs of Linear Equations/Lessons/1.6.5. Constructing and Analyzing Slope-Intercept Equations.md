## Reading the Graph of a Linear Function

**Example:** The graph shows the possible combinations of the number of notebooks and pens that could be purchased for $\[math]21$ at a school supply store. If Mia purchased pens and $6$ notebooks for a total of $\[/math]21$, how many pens did she buy?

![](<../Source/1.6.5. Constructing and Analyzing Slope-Intercept Equations/Images/8e709c70e46ff959ff68d224156bfff5.png>)

**Explanation**

Since the line graph above represents all $\[math]21$ combinations of notebooks and pens, each point on the line shows a possible purchase:

- the $x$-coordinate gives the number of notebooks, and
- the $y$-coordinate gives the number of pens.

We need to find how many pens Mia bought when she purchased $6$ notebooks. Looking at the graph, for $x=6$, we have $y=12$, as shown below.

![](<../Source/1.6.5. Constructing and Analyzing Slope-Intercept Equations/Images/d7aaa1aa5cb8fd15eada696a2916eb07.png>)

Therefore, Mia bought $12$ pens.

## Finding the Y-Coordinate of a Point Given a Line That Passes Through Two Points

**Example:** In the $xy$-plane, line $r$ intersects the $y$-axis at the point $(0, -3)$ and passes through the point $(6, 3)$. If the point $(12, v)$ lies on line $r$, what is the value of $v$?

**Explanation**

We want to find the equation of the line that passes through the points

$$
(x_1, y_1) = (0, -3)
$$

and

$$
(x_2, y_2) = (6, 3)
$$

in the form

$$
y = mx + c
$$

where $m$ is the slope and $c$ is the $y$-intercept.

First, we use the two given points to find the slope $m{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
m &= \frac{y_{2} - y_{1}}{x_{2} - x_{1}} \\
&= (3 - (-3))/(6 - 0) \\
&= \frac{6}{6} \\
&= 1
\end{aligned}
$$

Since the line passes through the point $(0, -3)$, it crosses the $y$-axis at $-3$, so the $y$-intercept is
$c = -3$.

So, the equation of this line is
$y = x - 3$.

Therefore, to find the value of $v$ for the point $(12, v)$ that lies on the line, we substitute $x = 12$ and solve for $y=v{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
y &= x - 3 \\
v &= 12 - 3 \\
&= 9
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding the X-Coordinate of a Point Given a Line That Passes Through Two Points

**Example:** The point $(d, 4)$ lies on the line shown. What is the value of $d$?

![](<../Source/1.6.5. Constructing and Analyzing Slope-Intercept Equations/Images/fac70010956ce102cb9edbef635a9059.png>)

**Explanation**

We want to find the value of $d$ such that the point $(d,4)$ lies on the line.

First, we determine the equation of the line in the form

$$
y = mx + b
$$

where $m$ is the slope and $b$ is the $y$-intercept.

Looking at the graph, we see that the line crosses the $y$-axis at

$$
(x_1, y_1) = (0,10)
$$

So the $y$-intercept is
$b = 10$.

The line also crosses the $x$-axis at

$$
(x_2, y_2) = (12,0)
$$

Thus, the slope is

$$
\begin{aligned}
m &= \frac{y_{2} - y_{1}}{x_{2} - x_{1}} \\
&= \frac{0 - 10}{12 - 0} \\
&= \frac{-10}{12} \\
&=-\frac{5}{6}
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the equation of the line is

$$
y = -\dfrac{5}{6}x + 10
$$

Therefore, since the point $(d, 4)$ lies on the line, we plug in $y = 4$ and solve for $x = d{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
4 &= -\frac{5}{6}d + 10 \\
4 - 10 &= -\frac{5}{6}d \\
- 6 &= -\frac{5}{6}d \\
\frac{5}{6}d &= 6 \\
d &= 6 \cdot \frac{6}{5} \\
&= \frac{36}{5}
\end{aligned}
$$
