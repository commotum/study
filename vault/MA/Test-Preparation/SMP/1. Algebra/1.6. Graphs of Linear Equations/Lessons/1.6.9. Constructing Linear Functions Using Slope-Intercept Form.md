## Introduction

A common task on the math section of the SAT is constructing a linear function when certain values are given.

In this topic, we'll practice writing linear functions in slope-intercept form, sometimes by solving a system of equations to find the slope and intercept.

---

<a id="constructing-a-function-given-two-points-with-identical-y-values"></a>
## Constructing a Function Given Two Points With Identical Y-Values

**Example:** For the linear function $f$, it is given that $f(1) = -3$ and $f(5) = -3$. Find an equation that defines $f$.

**Explanation**

An equation defining the linear function $f$ can be written in the form

$$
f(x) = mx + b
$$

where $m$ is the slope and $b$ is the $y$-intercept of the graph of $y = f(x)$.

Note the following:

- We are given that $f(1) = -3$. Therefore, the graph $y = f(x)$ passes through the point $(1, -3)$.
- Similarly, we are given that $f(5) = -3$. Therefore, the graph passes through the point $(5, -3)$.

Notice that the two points both have $y = -3$. This means that the line is horizontal and, consequently, $m = 0$. This means that the equation of the line is
$y = -3$.

Since $y = f(x)$, we conclude that

$$
f(x) = -3
$$

## Constructing a Function Using Slope-Intercept Form

**Example:** In the linear function $g$, we have $g(0) = 5$ and $g(2) = 13$. Find an equation that defines $g$.

**Explanation**

An equation defining the linear function $g$ can be written in the form

$$
g(x) = mx + b
$$

where $m$ is the slope and $b$ is the $y$-intercept of the graph of $y = g(x)$.

We are given that $g(0) = 5$, therefore, the graph passes through the point $(0,5)$. Similarly, we are given that $g(2) = 13$, hence, the graph passes through the point $(2,13)$.

Now, we calculate the slope $m$ using the previous points:

$$
\begin{aligned}
m &= \frac{y_{2} - y_{1}}{x_{2} - x_{1}} \\
&= \frac{13 - 5}{2 - 0} \\
&= \frac{8}{2} \\
&= 4
\end{aligned}
$$

The coordinates of the $y$-intercept are always $(0,b)$. We know that the graph passes through the point $(0,5)$, thus, the $y$-intercept is $b=5$.

Substituting the slope $m=4$ and the $y$-intercept $b=5$ into

$$
g(x) = mx + b
$$

we reach

$$
g(x) = 4x+5
$$

## Constructing a Function by Solving a System of Equations

**Example:** In the linear function $k$, we have $k(6) = 1$ and $k(2) = 5$. Find an equation that defines $k$.

**Explanation**

An equation defining the linear function $k$ can be written in the form

$$
k(x) = mx + b
$$

where $m$ is the slope and $b$ is the $y$-intercept of the graph of $y = k(x)$.

We are given that $k(6) = 1$. Therefore, we can set up an equation involving $m$ and $b{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
k(x) &= mx + b \\
k(6) &= m(6) + b \\
1 &= 6m + b
\end{aligned}
$$

Similarly, we are given that $k(2) = 5$. Therefore, we can set up another equation involving $m$ and $b{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
k(x) &= mx + b \\
k(2) &= m(2) + b \\
5 &= 2m + b
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, we obtain the following system of equations:

$$
{6m + b = 1; 2m + b = 5
$$

Subtracting the second equation from the first and solving for $m$, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
(6m - 2m) + (b - b) &= 1 - 5 \\
4m + 0 &= -4 \\
4m &= -4 \\
m &= ((-4))/(4) \\
m &= -1
\end{aligned}
$$

Now we proceed to find the value of $b$. To do this, we substitute $m=-1$ into one of the equations of our system. Let's choose the second equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
2m + b &= 5 \\
2(-1) + b &= 5 \\
- 2 + b &= 5 \\
b &= 5 + 2 \\
b &= 7
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, substituting the slope $m=-1$ and the $y$-intercept $b=7$ into

$$
k(x) = mx + b
$$

we reach

$$
k(x) = -x+7
$$
