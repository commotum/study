## Introduction

This topic provides practice in finding equations of lines in the $xy$-plane using slopes and points. This could involve cases where two points are given, a slope and a point, or a description of the relationship between $x$ and $y$.

## Finding the Slope Given Two Points

**Example:** When line $u$ is graphed in the $xy$-plane, it has an $x$-intercept of $(3, 0)$ and a $y$-intercept of $\left(0, -\dfrac{9}{2}\right)$. What is the slope of line $u$?

**Explanation**

We want to find the slope of the line that passes through the two given intercepts.

Thus, we use the two given points,

$$
(x_1, y_1) = (3, 0)
$$

and

$$
(x_2, y_2) = \left(0, -\dfrac{9}{2}\right)
$$

to find the slope $m{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
m &= \frac{y_{2} - y_{1}}{x_{2} - x_{1}} \\
&= \frac{-9/2 - 0}{0 - 3} \\
&= \frac{-9/2}{-3} \\
&= \frac{9}{2} \div 3 \\
&= \frac{9}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{3} \\
&= \frac{3}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the slope of the line is

$$
m = \dfrac{3}{2}
$$

## Finding the Slope-Intercept Form Given a Slope and a Point

**Example:** For the linear function $t$, the graph of $y = t(x)$ in the $xy$-plane has a slope of $\dfrac{1}{2}$ and passes through the point $(6, -4)$. Find the equation defining $t$.

**Explanation**

We want to find the equation of the line in the form

$$
y = mx + b
$$

where $m$ is the slope and $b$ is the $y$-intercept.

We are told the line has a slope

$$
m = \dfrac{1}{2}
$$

Now, we substitute the point $(6, -4)$ into the equation to solve for $b{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
y &= mx + b \\
- 4 &= \frac{1}{2}(6) + b \\
- 4 &= 3 + b \\
b &= -7
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the equation of this line is

$$
y = \dfrac{1}{2}x - 7
$$

Therefore, since $y = t(x)$, the equation that defines $t$ is

$$
t(x) = \dfrac{1}{2}x - 7
$$

## Finding the Slope-Intercept Form Given a Description Involving a Slope and a Point

**Example:** The relationship between two variables, $x$ and $y$, is linear. For every increase in the value of $x$ by $1$, the value of $y$ decreases by $2$. When the value of $x$ is $5$, the value of $y$ is $-1$. Find the equation representing this relationship.

**Explanation**

We want to find the equation of the line in the form

$$
y = mx + b
$$

where $m$ is the slope and $b$ is the $y$-intercept.

First, since the relationship is linear and $y$ decreases by $2$ for every increase of $1$ in $x$, the slope of the line is
$m = -2$.

Now, we substitute the point $(5, -1)$ into the equation to solve for $b{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
y &= mx + b \\
- 1 &= -2(5) + b \\
- 1 &= -10 + b \\
b &= 9
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the equation representing this relationship is

$$
y = -2x + 9
$$

## Finding the Slope-Intercept Form Given Two Points

**Example:** In the $xy$-plane, the graph of the linear function $q$ contains the points $(0, -4)$ and $(8, 0)$. Find the equation defining $q$, where $y = q(x)$.

**Explanation**

We want to find which equation correctly represents the line that passes through the points

$$
(x_1, y_1) = (0, -4)
$$

and

$$
(x_2, y_2) = (8, 0)
$$

in the form

$$
y = mx + b
$$

where $m$ is the slope and $b$ is the $y$-intercept.

First, we use the two given points to find the slope $m{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
m &= (0 - (-4))/(8 - 0) \\
&= \frac{4}{8} \\
&= \frac{1}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Since the line passes through the point $(0, -4)$, it crosses the $y$-axis at $-4$, so the $y$-intercept is
$b = -4$.

So, the equation of this line is

$$
y = \dfrac{1}{2}x - 4
$$

Therefore, since $y = q(x)$, the equation that defines $q$ is

$$
q(x) = \dfrac{1}{2}x - 4
$$
