## Interpreting the Coefficients of a Slope-Intercept Form

**Example:** A line in the $xy$-plane has a slope of $-\dfrac{7}{3}$ and passes through the point $(0, 4)$. The equation $y = qx + t$ defines the line, where $q$ and $t$ are constants. What is the value of $q$?

**Explanation**

The given equation is already in the slope-intercept form

$$
y = mx + b
$$

where $m$ is the slope and $b$ is the $y$-intercept.

Thus, we can directly see that the slope is
$m = q$.

We are told the slope is

$$
m = -\dfrac{7}{3}
$$

Therefore,

$$
q = \bbox[4pt,border: 1px solid lightgray]{-\dfrac{7}{3}}
$$

## Interpreting the Coefficients of a Slope-Intercept Form: Requires Simplification

**Example:** What is the slope of the graph of
$y = \dfrac{1}{6}(18x - 24) - 4x$
in the $xy$-plane?

**Explanation**

First, we rewrite the given equation in slope-intercept form,

$$
y = mx + b
$$

where $m$ is the slope:

$$
\begin{aligned}
y &= \frac{1}{6}(18x - 24) - 4x \\
&= \frac{1}{6} \cdot 18x - \frac{1}{6} \cdot 24 - 4x \\
&= 3x - 4 - 4x \\
&=-x - 4
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the slope of the line is

$$
m = \bbox[4pt,border: 1px solid lightgray]{-1}
$$

## Finding the Slope-Intercept Form Given a Slope and the Y-Intercept

**Example:** The graph of the function $f$ is a line in the $xy$-plane. If the line has slope $\dfrac{5}{6}$ and $f(0) = -4$. Find the equation representing this line.

**Explanation**

We want to determine the equation of the function in the form

$$
y = mx + b
$$

where $m$ is the slope and $b$ is the $y$-intercept.

We are told the line has a slope

$$
m = \frac{5}{6}
$$

Since we know

$$
f(0) = -4
$$

this means the line crosses the $y$-axis at $-4$, so the $y$-intercept is
$b = -4$.

So, the equation of this line is

$$
y = \frac{5}{6}x - 4
$$

Therefore, since $y = f(x)$, the equation that defines $f$ is

$$
f(x) = \frac{5}{6}x - 4
$$

## Identifying a Possible Straight Line Graph Given Bounds on the Coefficients

**Example:** Which of the above could be the graph of $y=mx+b$, where $m$ and $b$ are constants such that $-1 \leq m \leq 2$ and $-1 \leq b \leq 3$?

![](<../Source/1.6.1. Interpreting Coefficients of Lines in Slope-Intercept Form/Images/1629a3c9618bf67fc6e7ccaeb2784e97.png>)

**Explanation**

Recall that, in an equation of a line in slope-intercept form,

$$
y=mx+b
$$

- $m$ represents the slope of the line, and
- $b$ represents the $y$-intercept of the line.

With that in mind, let's examine the given graphs in turn.

- Graph I is valid. Notice that:The line has the slope $m\approx \dfrac{3}{3} = 1$, which satisfies $-1 \leq m \leq 2$. $\color{darkgreen}\checkmark$The line has the $y$-intercept $b \approx 3$, which satisfies $-1 \leq b \leq 3$. $\color{darkgreen}\checkmark$
- Graph II is *not* valid. Notice that the line has the slope $m \approx \dfrac{3}{1} =3$, which doesn't satisfy the condition $-1 \leq m \leq 2$. $\color{red}\times$
- Graph III is *not* valid. Notice that the line has the $y$-intercept $b\approx 4$, which doesn't satisfy the condition $-1 \leq b \leq 3$. $\color{red}\times$
- Graph IV is *not* valid. Notice that the line has the $y$-intercept $b\approx-10$, which doesn't satisfy the condition $-1 \leq b \leq 3$. $\color{red}\times$

Therefore, the correct answer is "I only".
