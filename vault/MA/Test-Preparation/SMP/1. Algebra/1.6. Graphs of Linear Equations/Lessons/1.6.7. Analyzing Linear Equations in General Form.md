## Determining a Coefficient of a General Form Given Two Points

**Example:** The graph of the equation $lx + my = 30$ is a line in the $xy$-plane, where $l$ and $m$ are constants. If the line contains the points $(1, 6)$ and $(5, 8)$, what is the value of $m$?

**Explanation**

We are given that the points $(1, 6)$ and $(5, 8)$ lie on the line defined by the equation

$$
lx + my = 30
$$

To find the value of $m$, we substitute each point into the equation to create a system of two equations and then eliminate $l$ to solve for $m$.

First, we plug in the points $(1,6)$ into the equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
lx + my &= 30 \\
l(1) + m(6) &= 30 \\
l + 6m &= 30
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we plug in the points $(5,8)$ into the equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
lx + my &= 30 \\
l(5) + m(8) &= 30 \\
5l + 8m &= 30
\end{aligned}
$$

So, we have the following system of equations:

$$
{l + 6m = 30; 5l + 8m = 30
$$

Therefore, multiplying the first equation by $5$ and subtracting the second, we find that the value of $m$ is

$$
\begin{aligned}
5(l + 6m) - (5l + 8m) &= 5 \cdot 30 - 30 \\
5l + 30m - 5l - 8m &= 150 - 30 \\
22m &= 120 \\
m &= \frac{120}{22} \\
m &= \frac{60}{11}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Identifying a Graph Given a General Equation With Restrictions

**Example:** The equation $rx + sy = -s$ is given, where $r$ and $s$ are constants such that $0 < r < s$. Which of the graphs above represents the equation in the $xy$-plane?

![](<../Source/1.6.7. Analyzing Linear Equations in General Form/Images/fe50faec3e30a1c6a6b5db4a921fbf34.png>)

**Explanation**

We want to determine which graph corresponds to the equation

$$
rx + sy = -s
$$

under the condition $0 < r < s$.

First, we rewrite this in slope-intercept form,

$$
y = mx + b
$$

by solving for $y{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
rx + sy &= -s \\
sy &= -rx - s \\
y &= -\frac{r}{s}x - 1
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus, we find the slope and $y$-intercept are

$$
m = -\dfrac{r}{s} \qquad \text{and} \qquad b = -1
$$

So, the line crosses the $y$-axis at
$(0,-1)$.

Now, since $0 < r < s$, we have

$$
0 < \dfrac{r}{s} < 1
$$

Multiplying by $-1$, we get

$$
0 > -\dfrac{r}{s} > -1 \qquad \text{or} \qquad -1 < -\dfrac{r}{s} < 0
$$

So the slope

$$
m = -\dfrac{r}{s}
$$

is negative with an absolute value less than $1$, meaning the line slopes downward but is relatively flat.

Therefore, the only graph that crosses the $y$-axis at $(0,-1)$, slopes downward, and is relatively flat is

## Interpreting the Constant Coefficient of a Linear Equation in General Form

**Example:** To fund a class trip, Leo sells boxes of pencils and pens. He earns $\[math]7$ for each box of pencils sold and $\[/math]14$ for each box of pens sold. The graph above shows all possible combinations of boxes Leo could sell to earn $t$ dollars. What is the value of $t$?

![](<../Source/1.6.7. Analyzing Linear Equations in General Form/Images/446ff61d75c5e9d521eb9621fe37300c.png>)

**Explanation**

To find Leo’s total earnings $t$, we use the earnings per box and apply the coordinates from the graph to evaluate $t$ using the linear equation that models the situation.

Let $x$ be the number of boxes of pencils and $y$ be the number of boxes of pens.

Thus, the equation that translates this situation is

$$
7x + 14y = t
$$

From the graph, we see:

- The line crosses the $y$-axis at $(0,10)$, this means if he sold $10$ boxes of pens and $0$ of pencils, he earned
$t|= 7x + 14y; = 7(0) + 14(10); = 140$.
- The line crosses the $x$-axis at $(20,0)$, this means if he sold $20$ boxes of pencils and $0$ of pens, he earned
$t|= 7x + 14y; = 7(20) + 14(0); = 140$.

Therefore, in either case, Leo would have earned a total of

$$
t = \[math]140
$$
