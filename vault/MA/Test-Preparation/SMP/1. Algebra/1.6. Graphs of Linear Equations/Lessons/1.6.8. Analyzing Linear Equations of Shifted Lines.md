## Finding the Slope-Intercept Form of a Shifted Graph

**Example:** The graph of $y = g(x) + 5$ is shown above. Find the equation that defines the linear function $g$.

![](<../Source/1.6.8. Analyzing Linear Equations of Shifted Lines/Images/3c28addee686a009800a315518679ae4.png>)

**Explanation**

We are given the graph of the function

$$
y = g(x) + 5
$$

and asked to find the function $g(x)$.

We first want to determine the equation of the shown line in the form

$$
y = mx + b
$$

where $m$ is the slope and $b$ is the $y$-intercept.

Looking at the graph, we see that it crosses the $y$-axis at

$$
(x_1, y_1) = (0,3)
$$

Thus, the $y$-intercept is
$b = 3$.

The line also crosses the $x$-axis at

$$
(x_2, y_2) = (-3,0)
$$

Thus, the slope is

$$
\begin{aligned}
m &= \frac{y_{2} - y_{1}}{x_{2} - x_{1}} \\
&= \frac{0 - 3}{-3 - 0} \\
&= \frac{-3}{-3} \\
&= 1
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus, the equation of the line is

$$
y = x + 3
$$

Since this represents

$$
y = g(x) + 5
$$

we set up the equation and solve for $g(x){:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
g(x) + 5 &= x + 3 \\
g(x) &= x + 3 - 5 \\
g(x) &= x - 2
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the equation for $g$ is

$$
g(x) = x - 2
$$

## Finding the Y-Intercept of a Shifted Graph Given a General Form Linear Equation

**Example:** If the graph of $9x + 18y = 72$ is shifted down $6$ units in the $xy$-plane, what is the $y$-intercept of the resulting graph?

**Explanation**

We begin by finding the $y$-intercept of the original graph. To do this, we set $x = 0$ and solve for $y{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
9(0) + 18y &= 72 \\
18y &= 72 \\
y &= \frac{72}{18} \\
&= 4
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, shifting the graph down by $6$ units decreases all $y$-coordinates by $6$. Therefore, the new $y$-intercept becomes

$$
\begin{aligned}
y - 6 &= 4 - 6 \\
&=-2
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the $y$-intercept of the shifted graph is
$(0, -2)$.

## Finding the X-Intercept of a Shifted Graph Given a General Form Linear Equation

**Example:** The graph of $7x + 3y = 5$ is translated down $5$ units in the $xy$-plane. What is the $x$-coordinate of the $x$-intercept of the resulting graph?

**Explanation**

Translating the graph down by $5$ units means replacing $y$ with $y_n + 5$, where $y_n$ is the new $y$-coordinate:
$y_n = y - 5$

Solving for $y{:}$

$$
y = y_n + 5
$$

Thus, to rewrite the given equation in terms of $y_n$, we replace $y$ by $y_n + 5$ and simplify:

$$
\begin{aligned}
7x + 3y &= 5 \\
7x + 3(y_{n} + 5) &= 5 \\
7x + 3y_{n} + 15 &= 5 \\
7x + 3y_{n} &= -10
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, since the $x$-intercept occurs when $y_n = 0$, we set $y_n = 0$ and solve for $x{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
7x + 3y_{n} &= -10 \\
7x + 3(0) &= -10 \\
7x &= -10 \\
x &= -\frac{10}{7}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the $x$-coordinate of the $x$-intercept is

$$
-\dfrac{10}{7}
$$

## Analyzing Coefficients of the Slope-Intercept Form of a Shifted Graph

**Example:** The graph of the linear function $y = h(x) - 6$ is shown. If $c$ and $d$ are positive constants, which equation could define $h$?

![](<../Source/1.6.8. Analyzing Linear Equations of Shifted Lines/Images/842e3b99a80ee5d8123644f8dbaaca01.png>)

1. $h(x) = -d - c x$
2. $h(x) = -d + c x$
3. $h(x) = d - c x$
4. $h(x) = d + c x$

**Explanation**

To determine which equation could define $h(x)$, we analyze the graph of the function

$$
y = h(x) - 6
$$

and deduce the slope and $y$-intercept of $h(x)$ based on the vertical shift.

From the graph, we observe the following:

- The slope of the line is negative, so the slope of $h(x)$ must also be negative.
- The $y$-intercept of $h(x) - 6$ is positive, which means the $y$-intercept of $h(x)$ must be even more positive, since the graph of $h(x) - 6$ lies $6$ units below the graph of $h(x)$.

Given that $h(x)$ has a negative slope and a positive $y$-intercept, and that $c$ and $d$ are positive constants, the only equation among the options that satisfies these conditions is:

$$
h(x) = d - c x
$$
