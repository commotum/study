## Finding a Slope Given a Line in General Form

**Example:** What is the slope of the graph of $2x - 6y = 18$ in the $xy$-plane?

**Explanation**

First, we rewrite the given equation in slope-intercept form,

$$
y = mx + b
$$

where $m$ is the slope:

$$
\begin{aligned}
2x - 6y &= 18 \\
- 6y &= -2x + 18 \\
y &= \frac{1}{3}x - 3
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the slope of the line is

$$
m = \dfrac{1}{3}
$$

## Finding an X-Intercept or Y-Intercept Given a Line in General Form

**Example:** The line with the equation $\dfrac{3}{4}x + \dfrac{5}{6}y = 2$ is graphed in the $xy$-plane. What is the $x$-coordinate of the $x$-intercept of the line?

**Explanation**

Since the $x$-intercept occurs when $y = 0$, we set $y = 0$ and solve for $x{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{3}{4}x + \frac{5}{6}y &= 2 \\
\frac{3}{4}x + \frac{5}{6}(0) &= 2 \\
\frac{3}{4}x &= 2 \\
x &= \frac{4}{3} \cdot 2 \\
x &= \frac{8}{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the $x$-coordinate of the $x$-intercept is

$$
\bbox[4pt,border: 1px solid lightgray]{\dfrac{8}{3}}
$$

## Finding a General-Form Equation From a Graph

**Example:** The graph shows a linear relationship between $x$ and $y$. Find the equation of the line in standard form, ensuring the coefficient of $x$ is a positive constant $R$.

![](<../Source/1.6.6. Properties of Lines Given in Standard or General Form/Images/d4c38549ce1d0b2ae73bf91964b779e9.png>)

**Explanation**

First, we want to determine the equation of the line in the form

$$
y = mx + b
$$

where $m$ is the slope and $b$ is the $y$-intercept.

Looking at the graph, we see that the line crosses the $y$-axis at $(0,4)$, so the $y$-intercept is
$b = 4$.

Next, we determine the slope. Looking at the graph, we see that the line passes through the points

$$
(x_1, y_1) = (0,4)
$$

and

$$
(x_2, y_2) = (1,2)
$$

Thus, the slope is

$$
\begin{aligned}
m &= \frac{y_{2} - y_{1}}{x_{2} - x_{1}} \\
&= \frac{2 - 4}{1 - 0} \\
&= \frac{-2}{1} \\
&=-2
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus, the line equation is

$$
y = -2x + 4
$$

We can rewrite this in standard form as follows:

$$
2x + y = 4
$$

Therefore, an equivalent equation that represents this relationship with positive $R$ is

$$
R x + y = 4
$$
