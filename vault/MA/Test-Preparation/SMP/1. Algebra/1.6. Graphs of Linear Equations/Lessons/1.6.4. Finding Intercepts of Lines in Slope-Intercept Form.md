## Finding an X-Intercept Given a Slope-Intercept Form

**Example:** The function $v$ is defined by $v(x) = -x - 5$. The graph of $y = v(x)$ in the $xy$-plane has an $x$-intercept at $(g, 0)$ and a $y$-intercept at $(0, h)$, where $g$ and $h$ are constants. What is the value of $g + h$?

**Explanation**

We are asked to find the sum of the $x$-intercept $g$ and the $y$-intercept $h$ of the function

$$
v(x) = -x - 5
$$

First, we substitute the point $(g, 0)$ into the equation to solve for $g{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
v(x) &= -x - 5 \\
0 &= - (g) - 5 \\
g &= -5
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we substitute the point $(0, h)$ into the equation to solve for $h{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
v(x) &= -x - 5 \\
h &= -0 - 5 \\
h &= -5
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
g + h &= -5 + (-5) \\
&=-10
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding an X-Intercept Given Expressions That Require Simplification

**Example:** The functions $u$ and $v$ are defined as
$u(x) = \dfrac{4}{5}x - 8 \quad \text{and} \quad v(x) = \dfrac{2}{5}x + 6$.
If the function $w$ is defined as
$w(x) = u(x) + v(x)$,
what is the $x$-coordinate of the $x$-intercept of the graph of $y = w(x)$ in the $xy$-plane?

**Explanation**

First, we find $w(x)$ by adding $u(x)$ and $v(x){:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
w(x) &= u(x) + v(x) \\
&= (\frac{4}{5}x - 8) + (\frac{2}{5}x + 6) \\
&= (\frac{4}{5}x + \frac{2}{5}x) + (-8 + 6) \\
&= \frac{6}{5}x - 2
\end{aligned}
$$

To find the $x$-intercept of $w(x)$, we set $w(x) = 0$ and solve for $x{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
w(x) &= \frac{6}{5}x - 2 \\
0 &= \frac{6}{5}x - 2 \\
\frac{6}{5}x &= 2 \\
x &= \frac{5}{6} \cdot 2 \\
&= \frac{10}{6} \\
&= \frac{5}{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the $x$-coordinate of the $x$-intercept of $w(x)$ is

$$
\bbox[4pt,border: 1px solid lightgray]{\dfrac{5}{3}}
$$

## Finding a Y-Intercept Given a Slope and an X-Intercept

**Example:** In the $xy$-plane, line $p$ has a slope of $\dfrac{3}{2}$ and an $x$-intercept of $(4, 0)$. What is the $y$-coordinate of the $y$-intercept of line $p$?

**Explanation**

First, we find the equation of the line using point-slope form.

We know the line passes through

$$
(x_1, y_1) = (4, 0)
$$

and has slope

$$
m = \dfrac{3}{2}
$$

so the equation of the line is

$$
\begin{aligned}
y - y_{1} &= m(x - x_{1}) \\
y - 0 &= \frac{3}{2}(x - 4) \\
y &= \frac{3}{2}x - \frac{3}{2} \cdot 4 \\
y &= \frac{3}{2}x - 6
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, to find the $y$-intercept, we plug in $x=0$ and solve for $y{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
y &= \frac{3}{2}x - 6 \\
&= \frac{3}{2}(0) - 6 \\
&=-6
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the $y$-coordinate of the $y$-intercept is $\bbox[4pt,border: 1px solid lightgray]{-6}$.
