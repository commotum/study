## Evaluating Linear Functions

**Example:** Ecologists studying a population of small frogs found that the equation $p(t) = 1.25t + 8.5$, where $10 \leq t \leq 80$, models the average number of parasites, $p$, found on a frog after $t$ days of exposure to a wetland habitat. According to this model, find the predicted number of parasites on a frog after $36$ days of exposure.

**Explanation**

We predict the number of parasites after $36$ days by substituting $t = 36$ into the given function:

$$
p(t) = 1.25t + 8.5
$$

Therefore, according to this model, the predicted number of parasites after $36$ days is

$$
\begin{aligned}
p(36) &= 1.25 \cdot 36 + 8.5 \\
&= 45 + 8.5 \\
&= 53.5
\end{aligned}
$$

Among the given options, the value closest to the predicted number is $54$.

## Solving a Linear Function Model for an Input

**Example:** The function $p(y) = 12y - 500$ gives the profit, in dollars, from selling $y$ personalized T-shirts on an online store. In order to earn a profit of $\[math]700$, how many T-shirts must be sold?

**Explanation**

We want to find $y$ such that

$$
p(y) = 12y - 500 = 700
$$

Solving for $y$, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
12y - 500 &= 700 \\
12y &= 700 + 500 \\
12y &= 1, 200 \\
y &= \frac{1, 200}{12} \\
y &= 100
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, to earn a profit of $\[math]700$, we must sell $100$ T-shirts.

## Evaluating a Linear Function Containing an Unknown Parameter

**Example:** A company's total cost $h(x)$, in dollars, to produce $x$ t-shirts is given by the function

$h(x) = kx + 500$,

where $k$ is a constant and $x > 0$. The total cost to produce $40$ t-shirts is $\[math]900$. What is the total cost, in dollars, to produce $300$ t-shirts?

**Explanation**

We first use the information that $h(40) = 900$ to solve for $k$. This gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
900 &= k \cdot 40 + 500 \\
400 &= 40k \\
k &= 10
\end{aligned}
$$

So the total cost function now becomes

$$
h(x) = 10x + 500
$$

The total cost to produce $300$ t-shirts, in dollars, is $h(300)$. Therefore, we can find it by substituting $x = 300$ into the total cost function:

$$
\begin{aligned}
h(300) &= 10 \cdot 300 + 500 \\
&= 3,000 + 500 \\
&= \[math]3,500
\end{aligned}
$$
