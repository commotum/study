## Constructing a Linear Function Using Slope-Intercept Form (Intercept Given)

**Example:** A linear model estimates the fish population in a lake from $2000$ to $2015$. The model estimates the population was $220$ thousand fish in $2000$, $400$ thousand fish in $2015$, and $y$ thousand fish in $2008$. What is the value of $y$?

**Explanation**

The linear model that estimates the fish population, $F(t)$, in thousands, for $t$ years after $2000$, is

$$
F(t) = mt + b
$$

We have two known data points:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(t_{1}, y_{1}) &= (0, 220) \\
(t_{2}, y_{2}) &= (15, 400)
\end{aligned}
$$

First, we calculate the slope $m{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
m &= \frac{y_{2} - y_{1}}{x_{2} - x_{1}} \\
&= \frac{400 - 220}{15 - 0} \\
&= \frac{180}{15} \\
&= 12
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, since the population in $2000$ (when $t=0$) is $220$ thousand fish, we have $b = 220$. So we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
F(t) &= mt + b \\
&= 12t + 220
\end{aligned}
$$

To find the fish population in $2008$, we need to substitute into the above expression $t=8$.
Therefore, the population in $2008$, in thousands, is

$$
\begin{aligned}
y &= F(8) \\
&= 12 \cdot 8 + 220 \\
&= 96 + 220 \\
&= 316
\end{aligned}
$$

## Constructing a Linear Function Using Slope-Intercept Form

**Example:** A linear function $h(x)$ gives the total cost, in dollars, of producing $x$ custom bicycles. The total cost is $\[math]720$ to produce $6$ bicycles, and $\[/math]1,200$ to produce $12$ bicycles. What is the equation that defines $h(x)$?

**Explanation**

The linear function modeling this situation is

$$
h(x) = mx + b
$$

We know two points on the line:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x_{1}, y_{1}) &= (6, 720) \\
(x_{2}, y_{2}) &= (12, 1, 200)
\end{aligned}
$$

Using the given two points, we find the slope $m{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
m &= \frac{y_{2} - y_{1}}{x_{2} - x_{1}} \\
&= \frac{1, 200 - 720}{12 - 6} \\
&= \frac{480}{6} \\
&= 80
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the linear function is

$$
\begin{aligned}
h(x) &= mx + b \\
&= 80x + b
\end{aligned}
$$

To find the value of $b$, we substitute one of the points, say $(6, 720)$, into this equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
720 &= 80 \cdot 6 + b \\
720 &= 480 + b \\
b &= 240
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the linear equation that defines $h(x)$ is

$$
h(x) = 80x + 240
$$

## Constructing a Linear Function With a Decreasing Rate

**Example:** The population of pine trees in a forest dropped from $9,200$ trees in $2012$ to $7,400$ trees in $2020$. Assuming the population decreased at a constant rate, what is the linear function $Q(t)$ that best models the population, in trees, $t$ years after $2012$?

**Explanation**

The linear function that models the population, $Q(t)$, for $t$ years after $2012$, is

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q(t) &= mt + b
\end{aligned}
$$

We have two known points:

$$
\begin{aligned}
for [math]2012[/math]:(t_{1}, Q_{1}) &= (0, 9, 200) \\
for [math]2020[/math]:(t_{2}, Q_{2}) &= (8, 7, 400)
\end{aligned}
$$

We first find the slope $m{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
m &= \frac{Q_{2} - Q_{1}}{t_{2} - t_{1}} \\
&= \frac{7, 400 - 9, 200}{8 - 0} \\
&= \frac{-1, 800}{8} \\
&=-225
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, since the tree population in $2012$ (when $t=0$) is $9,200$, we have $b = 9,200$. So we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q(t) &= mt + b \\
&=-225t + 9, 200
\end{aligned}
$$

## Constructing and Evaluating a Linear Function Using Slope-Intercept Form

**Example:** An analyst modeled the demand $Q$ for a certain winter coat as a linear function of the selling price $p$. The demand was $24,000$ units when the price was $\[math]150$ per unit, and $18,000$ units when the price was $\[/math]200$ per unit. Based on this model, what is the demand, in units, when the price is $\[math]180$ per unit?

**Explanation**

The linear function that models the demand $Q$, in units, for a selling price $p$, in dollars, is

$$
Q(p) = mp + b
$$

We have two known data points:

$$
(p_{1}, Q_{1}) = (150, 24, 000); (p_{2}, Q_{2}) = (200, 18, 000)
$$

We first find the slope $m{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
m &= \frac{Q_{2} - Q_{1}}{p_{2} - p_{1}} \\
&= \frac{18, 000 - 24, 000}{200 - 150} \\
&= \frac{-6, 000}{50} \\
&=-120
\end{aligned}
$$

So the demand function is

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q(p) &= mp + b \\
&=-120p + b
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we use one of the given points, say $(150, 24,000)$, to find the value of $b{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
24, 000 &= -120 \cdot 150 + b \\
24, 000 &= -18, 000 + b \\
b &= 42, 000
\end{aligned}
$$

So we have

$$
Q(p) = -120p + 42,000
$$

To find the demand for the price $\[math]180$ per unit, we need to substitute into the above expression $p = 180$. This gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q(180) &= -120 \cdot 180 + 42, 000 \\
&=-21, 600 + 42, 000 \\
&= 20, 400
\end{aligned}
$$
