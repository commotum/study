## Constructing a Proportional Relationship

**Example:** During practice, Ava swims at an average pace of $2.4$ minutes per lap. Write a function $s(n)$ that models the total number of minutes it takes Ava to swim $n$ laps at this pace.

**Explanation**

Ava takes $2.4$ minutes to swim each lap. So for $n$ laps, the total time is

$$
2.4 \times n
$$

Therefore, the function that models the total number of minutes, $s$, for $n$ laps is

$$
s(n) = 2.4n
$$

## Constructing a Linear Function

**Example:** To service an air conditioning unit, a technician charges $\[math]60$ per hour for labor plus a fixed cost of $\[/math]150$ for parts. Write a function $C(t)$ that represents the total amount, in dollars, the technician will charge for this job if it takes $t$ hours.

**Explanation**

The total cost is made up of two parts.

- The technician charges $\[math]60$ for each hour of work. So for $t$ hours, the labor cost is

$$
60 \times t
$$

- In addition, there is a fixed cost of $\[/math]150$ for parts.

Therefore, the function that models the total cost $C$, in dollars, after $t$ hours is

$$
C(t) = 60t + 150
$$

## Constructing and Evaluating a Linear Function

**Example:** According to a model, the petal length of a magical flower, in millimeters ($\textrm{mm}$), can be estimated by adding $1.7$ to five times the flower’s seed mass, in grams. According to this model, what would be the petal length $R$ of a magical flower that has a seed mass of $0.5$ grams?

**Explanation**

Let $z$ be the seed mass of the flower in grams. Then the model for the petal length $R$, in millimeters, is

$$
R = 5z + 1.7
$$

Therefore, we find the estimated petal length for a flower of seed mass $0.5$ grams by substituting $z = 0.5$ into the above expression:

$$
\begin{aligned}
R &= 5 \cdot 0.5 + 1.7 \\
&= 2.5 + 1.7 \\
&= 4.2mm
\end{aligned}
$$

## Constructing and Evaluating a Linear Function for a Decreasing Quantity

**Example:** At sea level, the air pressure is $102.9$ (kPa). For every $500$ meters of elevation gain, the air pressure decreases by $3.6$ kPa. What is the air pressure, in kPa, at a mountain summit $2{,}500$ meters above sea level?

**Explanation**

Let $h$ be the number of $500$-meter units of elevation gain. Then the model for the air pressure $P$, in kPa, is

$$
P = 102.9 - 3.6h
$$

Therefore, we find the air pressure at a summit

$$
2{,}500 = 5 \cdot 500
$$

meters above sea level by substituting $h = 5$ into the above expression:

$$
\begin{aligned}
P &= 102.9 - 3.6(5) \\
&= 102.9 - 18 \\
&= 84.9kPa
\end{aligned}
$$
