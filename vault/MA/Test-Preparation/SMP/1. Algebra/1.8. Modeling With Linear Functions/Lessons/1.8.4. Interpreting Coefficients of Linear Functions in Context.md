## Introduction

A common theme on the math section of the SAT is interpreting the meaning of coefficients in linear functions.

In this topic, we'll practice understanding what each term represents in context, such as the $y$-intercept, the unit rate, and constants in both simplified and unsimplified relationships.

---

<a id="interpreting-a-function-statement"></a>
## Interpreting a Function Statement

**Example:** The number of users on a new educational app can be modeled by the function $U(t) = 1,\!000 + 80t$, where $U(t)$ is the number of users $t$ months after the app's launch. What is the interpretation of $U(12) = 1,\!960$?

**Explanation**

The function

$$
U(t) = 1,\!000 + 80t
$$

shows how the number of users, $U$, changes with time, $t$ (in months). Substituting $t = 12$ gives the estimated number of users $12$ months after the app was launched.

Therefore,

$$
U(12) = 1,\!960
$$

means the number of users on the app is predicted to be $1,\!960$ one year after the launch.

## Interpreting a Y-Intercept

**Example:** A scientist is tracking the number of bacteria in a petri dish. The function $b(t) = 150t + 600$ gives the estimated number of bacteria $t$ hours after the experiment began. What is the meaning of $600$ in this situation?

**Explanation**

The function

$$
b(t) = 150t + 600
$$

shows how the number of bacteria $b$ changes with time $t$ (in hours).

This means that at the initial moment of time ($t = 0$), the estimated number of bacteria was

$$
b(0) = 150(0) + 600 = 600
$$

Therefore, the meaning of $600$ in this situation is that the estimated number of bacteria at the start of the experiment was $600$.

## Interpreting a Unit Rate in a Linear Relationship

**Example:** The formula $E = 60 + 3.2s$ gives the energy consumption $E$, in kilowatt-hours, of a device that has been running for $s$ seconds. What is the meaning of the coefficient $3.2$ in this context?

**Explanation**

The formula

$$
E = 60 + 3.2s
$$

shows how the energy consumption $E$ (in kilowatt-hours) changes with time $s$ (in seconds).

The coefficient $3.2$ is multiplied by $s$, so it tells us how much energy is consumed for each one-second increase in time.

Therefore, the coefficient $3.2$ means the device uses $3.2$ kilowatt-hours of energy for each additional second it runs.

## Interpreting a Coefficient From a Description

**Example:** A model predicts that a loaf of bread was $30$ centimeters long when it was baked and that the bread shrank $0.5$ centimeters per hour afterward. This model is defined by an equation in the form $L(x) = a + bx$, where $L(x)$ is the predicted length, in centimeters, of the bread $x$ hours later, and $a$ and $b$ are constants. What is the value of $b$?

**Explanation**

In the equation

$$
L(x) = a + bx
$$

the constant $b$ represents the decrease in the bread’s length per hour. According to the model, the bread shrank $0.5$ centimeters per hour.

Therefore, we have

$$
b = \bbox[4pt,border: 1px solid lightgray]{-0.5}
$$

## Interpreting a Constant in an Unsimplified Linear Relationship

**Example:** A laboratory uses $3$ liters of distilled water for each chemical test. The relationship between the number of tests performed, $x$, and the total amount of distilled water available, $y$, in liters, is given by the equation $y - 3x = 12$. What is the interpretation of $12$ in this situation?

**Explanation**

The equation $y - 3x = 12$ shows how the total amount of distilled water $y$ (in liters) is related to the number of chemical tests $x$.

First, we rearrange the given equation to isolate $y{:}$

$$
y = 3x + 12
$$

We know that the laboratory uses $3$ liters of distilled water for each test. Therefore, $x$ tests require $3x$ liters of water.

From the equation, the laboratory has $12$ liters of distilled water more than required to perform $x$ tests.
So, $12$ represents the amount of extra distilled water the laboratory has after conducting all the tests.

Therefore, the constant $12$ shows that the laboratory had $12$ liters of distilled water in excess of what was used for the tests.
