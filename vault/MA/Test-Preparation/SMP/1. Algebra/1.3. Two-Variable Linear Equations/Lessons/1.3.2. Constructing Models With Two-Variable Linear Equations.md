## Modeling a Sum or Difference

**Example:** The number $p$ is smaller than $q$ by $14$. Find an equation representing the relationship between $p$ and $q$?

**Explanation**

The problem tells us that $p$ is smaller than $q$ by $14$. This means we take $q$ and subtract $14$ to get $p$.

Therefore, the equation that represents this relationship is

$$
p = q - 14
$$

## Constructing a Two-Variable Linear Equation

**Example:** A submarine is initially $50$ feet below the surface. It begins descending at a constant rate of $7$ feet per second. What equation gives the depth $D$, in feet, of the submarine below the surface $t$ seconds after it starts descending?

**Explanation**

The depth of the submarine below the surface is made up of two parts.

- At the beginning, the submarine is already $50$ feet below the surface.
- As it descends, it gains an additional $7$ feet for every second. This means for $t$ seconds, it increases its depth by

$$
7 \times t
$$

Therefore, the equation that models the depth $D$, in feet, of the submarine below the surface $t$ seconds after it starts descending is

$$
D = 7t + 50
$$

## Constructing a Two-Variable Linear Equation With a Negative Rate

**Example:** An electric scooter’s battery is charged at $60\%$. For every hour of riding, the battery charge decreases by $1.2\%$. What equation can be used to find the battery charge $C$, in percent, after $h$ hours of use?

**Explanation**

The battery starts at $60\%$ charge.

For each hour of riding, it loses $1.2\%$. So after $h$ hours, the total charge decrease is $1.2h$.

Therefore, after subtracting this from the original charge, the equation that models the battery charge after $h$ hours is

$$
C = 60 - 1.2h
$$

## Constructing And Modifying a Two-Variable Expression

**Example:** One liter of varnish will cover $150$ square feet of a surface. A wooden patio has a total area of $P$ square feet. Find an equation representing the total amount of varnish $S$, in liters, needed to varnish the patio three times.

**Explanation**

Since one liter covers $150$ square feet, varnishing a patio of area $P$ square feet once would require

$$
\frac{P}{150} \,\, \text{liters.}
$$

To varnish the patio three times, we simply triple this amount, so we get

$$
3 \times \frac{P}{150} = \frac{P}{50}
$$

Therefore, the equation that represents the total amount of varnish $S$, in liters, needed to varnish the patio of area $P$ three times, is

$$
S = \frac{P}{50}
$$
