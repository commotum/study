## Determining a Unit Price

**Example:** The equation $2.85m + 4.15n = 44.85$ represents the number of muffins, $m$, and sandwiches, $n$, a café sells to total exactly $\[math]44.85$. The price of each muffin is the same and the price of each sandwich is the same. What is the price of one muffin?

**Explanation**

The equation

$$
2.85m + 4.15n = 44.85
$$

models the total cost of buying muffins and sandwiches.

The term $2.85m$ shows the total cost of the muffins, so the coefficient $2.85$ represents the price, in dollars, of one muffin.

Therefore, one muffin costs $\[math]\bbox[4pt,border: 1px solid lightgray]{2.85}$.

## Identifying the Best Interpretation of a Coefficient

**Example:** Two walkways in a park are lined with decorative stones. Walkway $M$ has $m$ stones and Walkway $N$ has $n$ stones. The total length of both walkways is $1{,}140$ inches. The equation $8m + 15n = 1{,}140$ represents this situation. What is the interpretation of $15$ in this context?

**Explanation**

The equation

$$
8m + 15n = 1{,}140
$$

models the total length of the walkways. The terms $8m$ and $15n$ represent the length of Walkways $M$ and $N$, respectively.

The factor of $15$ in $15n$ shows that each stone in Walkway $N$ is $15$ inches long.

## Interpreting a Solution

**Example:** A bookstore is assembling gift bundles for two genres. Each fiction bundle contains $12$ items, and each non-fiction bundle contains $5$ items. Altogether, $795$ items were packed. The equation

$12f + 5n = 795$

represents this situation, where $f$ is the number of fiction bundles and $n$ is the number of non-fiction bundles. What is the interpretation of $(f, n) = (55, 27)$ in this context?

**Explanation**

We are given the equation

$$
12f + 5n = 795
$$

where $f$ is the number of fiction bundles and $n$ is the number of non-fiction bundles. This equation represents a total of $795$ items packed. We are told that

$$
(f, n) = (55, 27)
$$

is a solution, so we interpret these values accordingly:

- The number $f = 55$ refers to the number of fiction bundles.
- The number $n = 27$ refers to the number of non-fiction bundles.

To verify this solution, we substitute the values into the equation:

$$
12(55) + 5(27) = 660 + 135 = 795 \quad \color{green}{\checkmark}
$$

So, this ordered pair means that if $55$ fiction bundles were assembled, then $27$ non-fiction bundles were assembled, and together they used exactly $795$ items.

Therefore, the point

$$
(f, n) = (55, 27)
$$

corresponds to the scenario in which $55$ fiction bundles and $27$ non-fiction bundles were assembled.

## Identifying the Best Interpretation of a Coefficient in a Three-Variable Model

**Example:** $T = 8.60n + 3.90s$
In the equation above, $T$ represents the total amount of money, in dollars, a pet grooming service charges for $n$ nail trims and $s$ shampoo treatments. The price, in dollars, of each nail trim is the same, and the price, in dollars, of each shampoo treatment is the same. What is the interpretation of the number $3.90$ in this context?

**Explanation**

In the equation

$$
T = 8.60n + 3.90s
$$

, the term $3.90s$ represents the total cost of the shampoo treatments.

Therefore, the coefficient $3.90$ represents the price, in dollars, of one shampoo treatment.

## Interpreting a Difference of Coefficients

**Example:** During a science fair, students earned points for completing experiments and presenting findings. One student completed $x$ experiments and gave $y$ presentations, earning a total of $360$ points. The equation $12x + 24y = 360$ represents this situation. How many fewer points is an experiment worth than a presentation?

**Explanation**

In the equation

$$
12x + 24y = 360
$$

, the coefficient $12$ means each experiment is worth $12$ points, and the coefficient $24$ means each presentation is worth $24$ points.

To find how many fewer points an experiment is worth than a presentation, subtract $12$ from $24{:}$

$$
24 - 12 = 12
$$

Therefore, each experiment is worth $\bbox[4pt,border: 1px solid lightgray]{12}$ fewer points than each presentation.
