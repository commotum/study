## Introduction

On the math section of the SAT exam, some problems involve finding totals that depend on two related quantities. Here, we'll practice modeling those situations with two-variable linear equations.

---

<a id="modeling-totals-using-two-variable-equations"></a>
## Modeling Totals Using Two-Variable Equations

**Example:** A community center purchases pencils for $\[math]0.06$ each and notebooks for $\[/math]1.25$ each. The total cost of $p$ pencils and $n$ notebooks is $\[math]537.50$. Write an equation that represents this situation.

**Explanation**

We are told that the center pays $\[math]0.06$ per pencil and $\[/math]1.25$ per notebook.

If $p$ pencils and $n$ notebooks are purchased, then the total cost is

$$
0.06p + 1.25n
$$

We are told the total cost is $\[math]537.50$, so the equation that represents this situation is

$$
0.06p + 1.25n = 537.50
$$

Therefore, the correct equation is

$$
0.06p + 1.25n = 537.50
$$

## Modeling Totals Using Percentages

**Example:** A bakery is preparing a frosting blend using two types of icing. Icing A contains $35\%$ sugar, and Icing B contains $20\%$ sugar. The final mixture must contain a total of $105$ grams of sugar. Find an equation that models this relationship, where $a$ is the number of grams of Icing A and $b$ is the number of grams of Icing B.

**Explanation**

We are told that Icing A contains $35\%$ sugar, and Icing B contains $20\%$ sugar.

- If $a$ grams of Icing A is used, the sugar contributed by Icing A is $0.35a$ grams.
- If $b$ grams of Icing B is used, the sugar contributed by Icing B is $0.20b$ grams.

Together, the total amount of sugar is

$$
0.35a + 0.20b
$$

We are told this total equals $105$ grams, so the equation that models the situation is

$$
0.35a + 0.20b = 105
$$

Therefore, the correct equation is

$$
0.35a + 0.20b = 105
$$

## Modeling Totals From Tabular Data

**Example:** A nutritionist is designing a smoothie recipe using various ingredients. The table below shows four fruits that may be used. Also shown for each fruit is its weight per volume, in grams per cup $(\mathrm{g} / \mathrm{cup})$, and the cost per gram, in dollars.

| Ingredient | Weight per volume $\left(\mathrm{g} / \mathrm{cup}\right)$ | Cost per gram |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Banana | $120$ | $\[math]0.01$ |
| Strawberry | $150$ | $\[/math]0.02$ |
| Mango | $160$ | $\[math]0.03$ |
| Blueberry | $100$ | $\[/math]0.04$ |

Only banana, mango, and strawberry will be used in the smoothie. The total weight must be $950$ grams. If $240$ grams of banana is used, find an equation that could show the relationship between the amounts, $x$ and $y$, in cups, for each of the other two fruits used.

**Explanation**

We are told the total weight of the smoothie must be $950$ grams, and that $240$ grams of banana is already included. That means the *combined weight of the other two fruits* must be

$$
950 - 240 = 710 \text{g.}
$$

Let

- $x$ be the volume of *mango* in cups, and
- $y$ be the volume of *strawberry* in cups.

From the table, we have the following:

- Mango weighs $160 \ \mathrm{g}/\mathrm{cup}$, so its total weight is $160x$.
- Strawberry weighs $150 \ \mathrm{g}/\mathrm{cup}$, so its total weight is $150y$.

The total weight of both fruits must equal $710\,\textrm{g}$. This is given by the following equation:

$$
160x + 150y = 710
$$

## Modeling Totals From Tabular Data (Three Variables)

**Example:** A teacher buys pens, notebooks, and folders for a workshop. If the total cost is $\[/math]100$ for $x$ pens, $y$ notebooks, and $z$ folders, Find an expression for $y$ in terms of $x$ and $z$.

| Item | Cost per Item |
| --- | ---: |
| Pen | $\[math]2.00$ |
| Notebook | $\[/math]5.00$ |
| Folder | $\[math]3.00$ |

**Explanation**

From the table, we collect the following information:

- $x$ pens cost $2x$ dollars.
- $y$ notebooks cost $5y$ dollars.
- $z$ folders cost $3z$ dollars.

Since the total cost for $x$ pens, $y$ notebooks, and $z$ folders is $100$ dollars, we get

$$
2x + 5y + 3z = 100
$$

Now, we isolate $y$ and simplify:

$$
\begin{aligned}
5y &= 100 - 2x - 3z \\
y &= \frac{100 - 2x - 3z}{5} \\
y &= \frac{100}{5} - \frac{2x}{5} - \frac{3z}{5} \\
y &= 20 - \frac{2}{5}x - \frac{3}{5}z
\end{aligned}
$$

FInally, the required expressoin is

$$
y = 20 - \dfrac{2}{5}x - \dfrac{3}{5}z
$$
