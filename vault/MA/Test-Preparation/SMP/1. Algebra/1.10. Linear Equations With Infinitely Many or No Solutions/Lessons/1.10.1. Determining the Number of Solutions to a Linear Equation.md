## Introduction

In the math section of the SAT exam, you may be asked to identify conditions where a linear equation has zero, one, or infinitely many solutions. This concept often appears in questions such as: “If this equation has no solution, what must be true about the constants?”

---

<a id="determining-the-number-of-solutions-to-a-one-step-equation"></a>
## Determining the Number of Solutions to a One-Step Equation

**Example:** How many solutions does the equation below have?
$27x = -3x$

**Explanation**

We are given the equation
$27x = -3x$.

First, we apply the addition principle to isolate the variable terms. Adding $3x$ to both sides, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
27x + 3x &= -3x + 3x \\
30x &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we apply the multiplication principle, dividing both sides by $30$:
$x = 0$

Since we found a single unique value of $x$ that satisfies the equation, the equation has exactly one solution.

Therefore, the correct answer is that the equation has **exactly one** solution.

## Determining the Number of Solutions to a Linear Equation Containing Binomials

**Example:** How many solutions does the equation $-6(x - 10) = 2(30 - x)$ have?

**Explanation**

We start by expanding the products on both sides of the equation and simplifying:

$$
\begin{aligned}
-6(x - 10) &= 2(30 - x) \\
- 6x + 60 &= 60 - 2x \\
- 4x &= 0 \\
x &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, this equation has **exactly one** solution, $x = 0$.

## Other Cases

**Example:** How many solutions does the equation $3(x + 2) = 7(x - 1) - 4x$ have?

**Explanation**

We begin by expanding the products in our equation, and then collecting like terms:

$$
\begin{aligned}
3(x + 2) &= 7(x - 1) - 4x \\
3x + 6 &= 7x - 7 - 4x \\
3x - 7x + 4x &= -7 - 6 \\
0 &= -13
\end{aligned}
$$

Since this equality is false for any value of $x$, the equation **has no solutions**.
