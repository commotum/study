This topic provides practice in analyzing systems of linear equations with infinitely many solutions. We'll determine parameters or parameter ratios that create equivalent equations and identify alternative equations that satisfy the same system.

## Finding a Parameter in a System With Infinitely Many Solutions

**Example:** ${-2x + 9y = 7; - 6x + 27y = k$

In the system of equations above, $k$ is a constant. If the system has infinitely many solutions, what is the value of $k$?

**Explanation**

A system of equations has infinitely many solutions when both equations represent the same line. This occurs when both equations have the same slope and the same $y$-intercept.

In other words, the two equations represent the same line if one equation is a constant multiple of the other.

First, we observe that the left-hand side of the second equation appears to be a multiple of the left-hand side of the first equation. To see this, let’s multiply the first equation by $3{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
3(-2x + 9y) &= 3(7) \\
- 6x + 27y &= 21
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus, for the system to have infinitely many solutions, the second equation must be identical to

$$
-6x + 27y = 21
$$

Therefore, the constant $k$ must be

$$
k = 21
$$

## Finding a Parameter in a Two-Parameter System With Infinitely Many Solutions

**Example:** In the system of equations below, $r$ and $s$ are constants:

${\frac{5}{6}x - \frac{1}{8}y = 3; rx - 9y = s$

If the system has an infinite number of solutions $(x, y)$, what is the value of $r$?

**Explanation**

A system of equations has infinitely many solutions when both equations represent the same line. This occurs when both equations have the same slope and the same $y$-intercept.

In other words, the two equations represent the same line if one equation is a constant multiple of the other.

We start by eliminating fractions from the first equation to make comparison easier.

We multiply both sides of the first equation by $24$ (the least common multiple of $6$ and $8$):

$$
\begin{aligned}
24(\frac{5}{6}x - \frac{1}{8}y) &= 24(3) \\
20x - 3y &= 72
\end{aligned}
$$

So the system becomes

$$
{20x - 3y = 72; rx - 9y = s
$$

Now, if one equation is a constant multiple of the other, all coefficients and constants are proportional. That means

$$
\frac{r}{20} = \frac{(-9)}{(-3)} = \frac{s}{72}
$$

To find the value of $r$, we focus on the proportion

$$
\frac{r}{20}= \frac{(-9)}{(-3)} = 3
$$

Therefore, multiplying both sides by $20$, we find that the value of $r$ is

$$
r = 3 \cdot 20 = 60
$$

## Identifying a Possible Second Equation Given a System With Infinitely Many Solutions

**Example:** One of the equations in a system of linear equations is $y = -5x - 4$. The system has infinitely many solutions. Which equation could be the second equation in this system?

1. $4y = 4(-5x - 4)$
2. $4y = 4(-5x) - 4$

III. $4y = -5x + 4(-4)$

**Explanation**

A system of equations has infinitely many solutions when both equations represent the same line. This occurs when both equations have the same slope and the same $y$-intercept.

In other words, the two equations represent the same line if one equation is a constant multiple of the other.

We are given that one equation in the system is

$$
y = -5x - 4
$$

Now, we want to find another equation among the choices that is a constant multiple of this one.

We reject

$$
y = 4(-5x - 4)
$$

and

$$
y = 4(-5x) - 4
$$

because they have the same left-hand side as our equation but different right-hand sides.

Note that the remaining choices all have $4y$ on the left-hand side.

We get $4y$ on the left-hand side of our equation if we multiply both sides of
$y = -5x - 4$
by $4$. This gives the equivalent equation:

$$
4y = 4(-5x - 4)
$$

Compare this with the choices that have $4y$ on the left-hand side:

- $4y = 4(-5x - 4) \quad {\color{green}\checkmark}$
- $4y = 4(-5x) - 4 \quad {\color{red}\times}$
- $4y = -5x + 4(-4) \quad {\color{red}\times}$

Therefore, among the choices,

$$
4y = 4(-5x - 4)
$$

is a constant multiple of

$$
y = -5x - 4
$$

Therefore, I could be the second equation in the system.

## Finding a Ratio of Parameters Given a System With Infinitely Many Solutions

**Example:** ${-\frac{3}{5}x + \frac{1}{2}y = \frac{7}{10}; ax + by = \frac{7}{10}$

In the given system of equations, $a$ and $b$ are constants. The system has infinitely many solutions. What is the value of $\dfrac{a}{b}$?

**Explanation**

A system of equations has infinitely many solutions when both equations represent the same line. This occurs when both equations have the same slope and the same $y$-intercept.

In other words, the two equations represent the same line if one equation is a constant multiple of the other.

We start by eliminating fractions from both equations to make the comparison easier.

We multiply both sides of the first equation by $10$ (the least common multiple of $5$, $2$, and $10$):

$$
\begin{aligned}
10(-\frac{3}{5}x + \frac{1}{2}y) &= 10(\frac{7}{10}) \\
- 6x + 5y &= 7
\end{aligned}
$$

We also multiply both sides of the second equation by $10{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
10(ax + by) &= 10(\frac{7}{10}) \\
10ax + 10by &= 7
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the system becomes

$$
{-6x + 5y = 7; 10ax + 10by = 7
$$

Now, if one equation is a constant multiple of the other, all coefficients and constants are proportional. That means

$$
\dfrac{10a}{-6} = \dfrac{10b}{5} = \dfrac{7}{7}
$$

To find the value of

$$
\dfrac{a}{b}
$$

we focus on the proportion

$$
\dfrac{10a}{-6} = \dfrac{10b}{5}
$$

It gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
10a \cdot 5 &= 10b \cdot (-6) \\
50a &= -60b \\
\frac{a}{b} &= -\frac{60}{50} \\
\frac{a}{b} &= -6 5
\end{aligned}
$$
