## Introduction

In this topic, we'll practice analyzing linear equations with infinitely many solutions by determining the values of unknown parameters that make the two sides of an equation represent the same line.

<a id="determining-an-unknown-factor"></a>
## Determining an Unknown Factor

**Example:** $10x + 5kx = 0$

In the equation above, $k$ is a constant. If the equation has infinitely many solutions, what is the value of $k$?

**Explanation**

We are given the equation

$$
10x + 5kx = 0
$$

and told that it has infinitely many solutions. This means it must be true for all values of $x$.

Factoring out an $x$ on the left-hand side, we write our equation as

$$
(5k + 10)x = 0
$$

Now, applying the zero product rule, we have that this equation has infinitely many solutions whenever

$$
5k + 10 = 0
$$

Solving this, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
5k &= -10 \\
k &= \frac{-10}{5} \\
k &= -2
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="determining-an-unknown-constant"></a>
## Determining an Unknown Constant

**Example:** $8v - 7 = \dfrac{32v + m}{4}$

In the equation above, $m$ is a constant. If the equation has infinitely many solutions, what is the value of $m$?

**Explanation**

We are given the equation

$$
8v - 7 = \dfrac{32v + m}{4}
$$

and told that it has infinitely many solutions. This means it must be true for all values of $v$.

We start by multiplying both sides of the equation by $4$ to clear the denominator:

$$
\begin{aligned}
4 \cdot (8v - 7) &= 32v + m \\
32v - 28 &= 32v + m
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, applying the addition principle to subtract $32v$ from both sides, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
32v - 28 - 32v &= 32v + m - 32v \\
(32v - 32v) - 28 &= (32v - 32v) + m \\
- 28 &= m \\
m &= -28
\end{aligned}
$$

Hence, for $m = -28$, the equation is satisfied for any value of $v$, which means it has infinitely many solutions.

<a id="cases-when-factoring-is-required"></a>
## Cases When Factoring Is Required

**Example:** Consider the equation
$2x - 10 = \frac{f(x - 5)}{4}$
where $f$ is a constant. If the equation has infinitely many solutions, what is the value of $f$?

**Explanation**

We are given the equation

$$
2x - 10 = \frac{f(x - 5)}{4}
$$

and told that it has infinitely many solutions. This means it must be true for all values of $x$.

We begin by expanding the right-hand side:

$$
2x - 10 = \frac{fx - 5f}{4}
$$

Next, we eliminate the denominator by multiplying both sides of the equation by $4{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
4(2x - 10) &= fx - 5f \\
8x - 40 &= fx - 5f
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we isolate the variable terms. Subtracting $fx$ from both sides, we get

$$
8x - fx - 40 = -5f
$$

Then, we factor the left-hand side:

$$
(8 - f)x - 40 = -5f
$$

For the equation to be true for all values of $x$, the coefficients of corresponding terms on both sides must be equal:

$$
8 - f = 0 \quad \text{and} \quad -40 = -5f
$$

Solving the first equation, we get
$f = 8$.

We check this satisfies the second equation:

$$
-5(8) = -40 \quad {\color{green}{\checkmark}}
$$

So, both conditions are satisfied. Therefore, the value of $f$ is $8$.

## Cases Where All Terms Are Proportional to the Linear Term

**Example:** $11x = 3x - 2x(6p + 2)$

In the equation above, $p$ is a constant. What is the value of $p$ such that this equation has infinitely many solutions?

**Explanation**

Since each term has a factor of $x$, we put our equation in the form $ax = 0{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
11x - 3x + 2(6p + 2)x &= 0 \\
(11 - 3 + 2(6p + 2))x &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, since we require our equation to have infinitely many solutions, we need $a = 0$. So, we solve the following for $p{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
11 - 3 + 2(6p + 2) &= 0 \\
8 + 12p + 4 &= 0 \\
12p + 12 &= 0 \\
12p &= -12 \\
p &= -1
\end{aligned}
$$

## Cases With Two Unknown Parameters

**Example:** $\dfrac{5}{6}ax - 4 = \dfrac{5}{2}x + 8b$

In the equation above, $a$ and $b$ are constants. If the equation has infinitely many solutions, what is the value of $\dfrac{a}{b}$?

**Explanation**

For our equation to have infinitely many solutions, both sides of our equation must be equivalent expressions. So, we must match our coefficients and constants:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{5}{6}ax + (-4) &= \frac{5}{2}x + (8b)
\end{aligned}
$$

This yields the two equations

$$
\begin{aligned}
\begin{bmatrix}\frac{5}{6}a &= \frac{5}{2} & and - 4|= 8b \\ a|= \frac{6}{5} \cdot \frac{5}{2} & andb|=-\frac{4}{8} \\ a|= 3 & andb|=-\frac{1}{2}\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Hence,

$$
\dfrac{a}{b}=\displaystyle{\dfrac{3}{-1/2}=3\cdot(-2)=-6}
$$
