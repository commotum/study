## Introduction

On the math section of the SAT, you may be asked to determine how many solutions a system of two or more linear equations has.

In this topic, we'll practice identifying whether a system has one solution, no solution, or infinitely many solutions by analyzing the equations themselves or their corresponding graphs.

---

<a id="determining-the-number-of-solutions-of-a-system"></a>
## Determining the Number of Solutions of a System

**Example:** ${-5x - 15y = 25; - x - 3y = 5$

How many solutions does the given system of equations have?

**Explanation**

To determine how many solutions a system of linear equations has, we check whether the two equations represent:

- The same line, which implies there are infinitely many solutions;
- Parallel lines that never intersect, which implies there is no solution;
- Two distinct lines that intersect once, which implies there is exactly one solution.

Let’s analyze the system:

$$
{-5x - 15y = 25; - x - 3y = 5
$$

Note that if we multiply both sides of the second equation by $5$, we get exactly the first equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
5(-x - 3y) &= 5(5) \\
- 5x - 15y &= 25
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus, the two equations represent the same line, and any point that lies on one will also lie on the other.

Therefore, the system has infinitely many solutions.

## Determining the Number of Solutions of a System With Three Equations

**Example:** The line above represents the solutions to a system of two linear equations. If a new system of three equations is created using the system of equations represented by the graph above and the equation $3x+3y=3$, how many solutions will the resulting system of three equations have?

![](<../Source/1.10.4. Determining the Number of Solutions to a Linear System/Images/dc466574bec618a2af7e3241a1959a1f.png>)

**Explanation**

First, let's determine the equation of the given line.

First, we calculate the slope. Notice that the line passes through the points $(0,1)$ and $(1,0)$. Therefore, its slope is

$$
m = \dfrac{0-1}{1-0} = -1
$$

So, the equation of our line is

$$
y = -x + c
$$

and since the line passes through $(0,1)$, the $y$-intercept is $c=1$. This gives the line

$$
y = -x + 1
$$

Recall that a system of linear equations has infinitely many solutions when both equations represent the *same line.*

We're given the line

$$
3x + 3y = 3
$$

Let's compare this against the first line by writing it in slope-intercept form:

$$
\begin{aligned}
3x + 3y &= 3 \\
3y &= -3x + 3 \\
\frac{3y}{3} &= \frac{-3x + 3}{3} \\
y &= -x + 1
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the line shown in the diagram and the line represented by

$$
3x+3y = 3
$$

are the same.

This means that the system of equations has infinitely many solutions.
