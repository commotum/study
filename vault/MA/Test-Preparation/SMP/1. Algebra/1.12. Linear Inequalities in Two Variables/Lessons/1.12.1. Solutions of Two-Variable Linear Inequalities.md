## Identifying a Solution to an Inequality

**Example:** Which of the following ordered pairs $(a, b)$ satisfies the inequality $4a + b \geq 10$?

1. $(2, 3)$
2. $(1, 6)$
3. $(0, 11)$

**Explanation**

To determine which ordered pairs satisfy the inequality

$$
4a + b \geq 10
$$

we substitute the $a$ and $b$ values from each option into the inequality and check whether the result is true.

- For $(2, 3)$, we have
$\begin{bmatrix}4(2) + 3 & \ge 10 \\ 8 + 3 & \ge 10 \\ 11 & \ge 10✓\end{bmatrix}$
Hence, I is *true*.
- For $(1, 6)$, we have
$\begin{bmatrix}4(1) + 6 & \ge 10 \\ 4 + 6 & \ge 10 \\ 10 & \ge 10✓\end{bmatrix}$
Hence, II is *true*.
- For $(0, 11)$, we have
$\begin{bmatrix}4(0) + 11 & \ge 10 \\ 0 + 11 & \ge 10 \\ 11 & \ge 10✓\end{bmatrix}$
Hence, III is *true*.

Therefore, the answer is "I, II, and III."

## Identifying a Table of Values Satisfying an Inequality

**Example:** Consider the following inequality:

$3x + 2y < 100$

Which of the following tables contains only points $(x, y)$ that satisfy this inequality?

I

| $x$ | $y$ |
| --- | ---: |
| $10$ | $20$ |
| $15$ | $15$ |
| $30$ | $20$ |

II

| $x$ | $y$ |
| --- | ---: |
| $5$ | $20$ |
| $10$ | $15$ |
| $30$ | $20$ |

III

| $x$ | $y$ |
| --- | ---: |
| $10$ | $20$ |
| $15$ | $10$ |
| $5$ | $25$ |

IV

| $x$ | $y$ |
| --- | ---: |
| $15$ | $30$ |
| $10$ | $25$ |
| $20$ | $20$ |

**Explanation**

We are asked to determine which table contains only points $(x, y)$ that satisfy the inequality $3x + 2y < 100$. To do this, we substitute each point into the inequality and check whether it holds.

Notice that for the points $(10, 20)$, $(15, 10)$, and $(5, 25)$, we have:

$$
\begin{aligned}
3(10) + 2(20) &= 30 + 40 = 70 < 100✓ \\
3(15) + 2(10) &= 45 + 20 = 65 < 100✓ \\
3(5) + 2(25) &= 15 + 50 = 65 < 100✓
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the correct table is the following:

| $x$ | $y$ |
| --- | ---: |
| $10$ | $20$ |
| $15$ | $10$ |
| $5$ | $25$ |

## Identifying a Solution From a Diagram

**Example:** The shaded region shown represents solutions to an inequality. Which ordered pair $(x, y)$ is a solution to this inequality?

![](<../Source/1.12.1. Solutions of Two-Variable Linear Inequalities/Images/5e23d62918e6322541a87bfbbbec5c5e.png>)

1. $(4, 1)$
2. $(0, -3)$
3. $(-4, 1)$
4. $(2, 0)$

**Explanation**

We use the graph to check whether each point lies in the shaded region.

- The pair $(4, 1)$ lies **above** the boundary line and is outside the shaded region. $\color{red}\times$
- The pair $(-4, 1)$ also lies **above** the boundary line and is outside the shaded region. $\color{red}\times$
- The pair $(2, 0)$ lies **above** the boundary line and is not part of the shaded region. $\color{red}\times$
- The pair $(6, 2)$ lies **above** the boundary line and is not part of the shaded region. $\color{red}\times$
- The pair $(0, -3)$ lies clearly **below** the line and is inside the shaded region. $\color{green}\checkmark$

Therefore, the only point that lies in the solution region is $(0, -3)$.

## Identifying an Inequality Corresponding to a Diagram

**Example:** Find the inequality represented by the shaded region shown above.

![](<../Source/1.12.1. Solutions of Two-Variable Linear Inequalities/Images/4aea5f365e78d0d156621b33ad302da1.png>)

**Explanation**

We are given a graph of a shaded region bounded by a solid straight line. Our goal is to determine the inequality that describes this region.

First, we find the equation of the corresponding line in the slope-intercept form

$$
y=mx+b
$$

From the graph, we observe the following:

- The line intersects the $y$-axis at $(0, -3)$, so the $y$-intercept is $b = -3$.
- The line passes through the points $(0, -3)$ and $(2, -2)$, so the slope is
$m = \dfrac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1} = \dfrac{-2 - (-3)}{2 - 0} = \dfrac{1}{2}$.

Thus, the equation of the boundary line is

$$
y = \dfrac{1}{2}x - 3
$$

Finally, we need to decide which inequality symbol to use. From the graph, we observe that

- The boundary line is solid, which means the inequality includes equality. So, we will use $\geq$ or $\leq$.
- The shaded region lies above the line, so the $y$-values in the region are greater than or equal to the corresponding $y$-values on the line.

Therefore, the corresponding inequality is:

$$
y \geq \dfrac{1}{2}x - 3
$$
