## Finding an Equivalent Inequality

**Example:** Which of the following inequalities is equivalent to the inequality below?
$25p + 10q > 15$

**Explanation**

We simplify the inequality by dividing *all terms* by the greatest common factor of $5{:}$

$$
\begin{bmatrix}\frac{25p + 10q}{5} & > \frac{15}{5} \\ 5p + 2q & > 3\end{bmatrix}
$$

So, the inequality $25p + 10q > 15$ is equivalent to
$5p + 2q > 3$.

## Modeling Using Inequalities Containing Variables on Both Sides

**Example:** The greatest possible value of $u$ is $12$ less than $5$ times another number $v$. Which inequality represents the possible values of $u$?

**Explanation**

The phrase “the greatest possible value of $u$ is $12$ less than $5$ times the value of $v$” can be unpacked in parts to understand how it translates to an inequality.

- First, the expression “$5$ times the value of $v$” means we compute $5v$.
- Next, the phrase “$12$ less than $5v$” tells us to *subtract $12$* from that result, giving $5v - 12$.
- Then, the phrase *“greatest possible value”* means that $u$ must be *less than or equal to* this amount.

Putting these pieces together, we get the inequality:

$$
u \leq 5v - 12
$$

## Determining a Smallest or Largest Possible Value

**Example:** A value $m$ is not greater than $9$ less than $5$ times the value of $n$. If the value of $n$ is $4$, what is the maximum possible value of $m$?

**Explanation**

The phrase “$m$ is not greater than $9$ less than $5$ times the value of $n$” can be unpacked in parts to understand how it translates to an inequality.

- First, the expression “$5$ times the value of $n$” means we compute $5n$.
- Next, the phrase “$9$ less than $5n$” tells us to **subtract $9$** from that result, giving $5n - 9$.
- Then, the phrase *“not greater than”* means that $m$ must be *less than or equal to* this amount.

Putting these pieces together, we get the inequality:

$$
m \leq 5n - 9
$$

Substituting $n = 4$ into the inequality yields

$$
\begin{bmatrix}m & \le 5(4) - 9 \\ m & \le 20 - 9 \\ m & \le 11\end{bmatrix}
$$

So, the maximum possible value of $m$ is $\bbox[4pt,border: 1px solid lightgray]{11}$.
