## Determining the Slope of a Linear Function

**Example:** The table shows four values of $x$ and their corresponding values of $f(x)$. There is a linear relationship between $x$ and $f(x)$ that is defined by the equation $f(x) = mx + 7$, where $m$ is a constant. What is the value of $m$?

| $x$ | $3$ | $5$ | $7$ | $9$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $f(x)$ | $1$ | $-3$ | $-7$ | $-11$ |

**Explanation**

We are given that

$$
f(x) = mx + 7
$$

and we want to find the value of $m$.

To do this, we can use one of the values from the table. Substituting $x = 3$ and its corresponding value

$$
f(3) = 1
$$

into the equation yields:

$$
\begin{aligned}
1 &= m(3) + 7 \\
- 6 &= 3m \\
m &= -2
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the value of $m$ is $-2$.

## Identifying a Linear Function

**Example:** The table above shows some pairs of $x$ values and $y$ values. Find the linear equation representing the relationship between $x$ and $y$.

| $x$ | $y$ |
| --- | ---: |
| $2$ | $24$ |
| $3$ | $33$ |
| $4$ | $42$ |
| $5$ | $51$ |

**Explanation**

Since the relationship is linear, we know it must be of the form

$$
y = mx + b
$$

where

- $m$ is the slope, and
- $b$ is the $y$-intercept.

First, we find the slope using any two points. Let's use $(2,24)$ and $(3,33){:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
m &= \frac{33 - 24}{3 - 2} \\
&= \frac{9}{1} \\
&= 9
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we find the $y$-intercept using the point $(2,24){:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
24 &= 9(2) + b \\
24 &= 18 + b \\
b &= 6
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the equation is

$$
y = 9x + 6
$$

## Identifying a Decreasing Linear Function

**Example:** The table shows three values of $x$ and their corresponding values of $y$. There is a linear relationship between $x$ and $y$. Find the equation representing this relationship.

| $x$ | $y$ |
| --- | ---: |
| $1$ | $10$ |
| $2$ | $2$ |
| $3$ | $-6$ |

**Explanation**

Since the relationship is linear, we know it must be of the form

$$
y = mx + b
$$

where

- $m$ is the slope, and
- $b$ is the $y$-intercept.

First, we find the slope using the points $(1,10)$ and $(2,2){:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
m &= \frac{2 - 10}{2 - 1} \\
&= \frac{-8}{1} \\
&=-8
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we find the $y$-intercept using one point. For $(1, 10)$, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
10 &= -8(1) + b \\
10 &= -8 + b \\
b &= 18
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the equation is

$$
y = -8x + 18
$$

## Identifying a Function Then Applying a Transformation

**Example:** For the linear function $f$, the table shows three values of $x$ and their corresponding values of $f(x)$. If $g(x) = f(x) + 2$, which equation defines $g$?

| $x$ | $f(x)$ |
| --- | ---: |
| $-4$ | $-5$ |
| $2$ | $4$ |
| $6$ | $10$ |

**Explanation**

Since $f(x)$ is linear, it can be written as

$$
f(x) = mx + b
$$

First, we find the slope $m$ using the points $(-4, -5)$ and $(2, 4){:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
m &= (4 - (-5))/(2 - (-4)) \\
&= \frac{9}{6} \\
&= \frac{3}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we find the $y$-intercept $b$ using the point $(2, 4){:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
f(x) &= \frac{3}{2}x + b \\
4 &= \frac{3}{2}(2) + b \\
4 &= 3 + b \\
b &= 1
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus, we have

$$
f(x) = \dfrac{3}{2}x + 1
$$

from which it follows that

$$
\begin{aligned}
g(x) &= f(x) + 2 \\
&= (\frac{3}{2}x + 1) + 2 \\
&= \frac{3}{2}x + 3
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally,

$$
g(x) = \dfrac{3}{2}x + 3
$$
