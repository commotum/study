## Introduction

On the math section of the SAT exam, you may be asked to identify and interpret linear functions given tables that represent real-world linear relationships. This topic provides practice in relating numerical data in tables to the parameters of their corresponding linear models.

<a id="identifying-a-linear-function"></a>
## Identifying a Linear Function

**Example:** A sign maker creates square aluminum signs. The price $P$, in dollars, of a sign is a linear function of the side length $s$, in inches, as shown in the table below.

| Side length, $s$ (inches) | Price, $P$ (dollars) |
| --- | ---: |
| $3$ | $18.00$ |
| $6$ | $30.00$ |
| $9$ | $42.00$ |

Find the relationship between $s$ and $P$.

**Explanation**

Since the relationship is linear, it must be of the form

$$
P = ms + b
$$

where $m$ and $b$ are constants to be found.

First, we find the slope using any two pairs of points. Let's use $(3, 18)$ and $(6, 30){:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
m &= \frac{30 - 18}{6 - 3} \\
&= \frac{12}{3} \\
&= 4
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we find the $y$-intercept using the point $(3, 18){:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
18 &= 4(3) + b \\
18 &= 12 + b \\
b &= 6
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the equation is

$$
P = 4s + 6
$$

## Identifying a Decreasing Linear Function

**Example:** The table above shows the atmospheric CO2 level in parts per million (ppm) for the years $1990$ and $2000$. If the relationship between CO2 level and year is linear, find the function $C$ modeling the CO2 level $t$ years after $1990$.

| Year | CO2 Level (ppm) |
| --- | ---: |
| $1990$ | $355$ |
| $2000$ | $335$ |

**Explanation**

Since the relationship is linear, it can be written as

$$
C(t) = mt + b
$$

where

- $m$ is the slope, and
- $b$ is the $y$-intercept.

Let's analyze each of the given points in turn:

- The year $1990$ corresponds to $t = 0$, and the CO2 level that year was $C = 355$.
- The year $2000$ is $10$ years after $1990$, so $t = 10$, and the CO2 level was $C = 335$.

So, we find the slope using the points $(0,355)$ and $(10,335){:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
m &= \frac{335 - 355}{10 - 0} \\
&= \frac{-20}{10} \\
&=-2
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we find the $y$-intercept noting that $C = 355$ when $t = 0{:}$

$$
C(0) = b = 355
$$

Therefore, the equation is

$$
C(t) = 355 - 2t
$$

## Identifying a Model and Writing in Standard Form

**Example:** A shipping company tracks the linear relationship between the number of containers, $n$, on a truck and the total weight, $w$, in tons. Find the equation representing the linear relationship between $n$ and $w$.

| Number of Containers | Total Weight (tons) |
| --- | ---: |
| $2$ | $104$ |
| $4$ | $214$ |
| $6$ | $324$ |

**Explanation**

Since the relationship is linear, it can be written as

$$
w = mn + b
$$

where

- $m$ is the slope, and
- $b$ is the $y$-intercept.

First, we find the slope $m$ using the points $(2,104)$ and $(4,214){:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
m &= \frac{214 - 104}{4 - 2} \\
&= \frac{110}{2} \\
&= 55
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we find the $y$-intercept using the point $(2,104){:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
104 &= 55(2) + b \\
104 &= 110 + b \\
b &= -6
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the equation is

$$
w = 55n - 6
$$

or, equivalently

$$
55n - w = 6
$$

## Interpreting a Total

**Example:** A company is creating cushions using different foam types. The table shows the weights and costs of the options. The equation
$0.50(5a) + 0.70(7c) + 220 = 1,060$
gives the total material cost in dollars, where $a$ is the number of $\mathrm{ft}^3$ of Type A foam and $c$ is the number of $\mathrm{ft}^3$ of Type C foam.

| Foam type | Weight per volume $\left(\mathrm{lb} / \mathrm{ft}^{3}\right)$ | Cost per pound |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Type A | $5$ | $\[math]0.50$ |
| Type B | $6$ | $\[/math]0.60$ |
| Type C | $7$ | $\[math]0.70$ |
| Type D | $8$ | $\[/math]0.80$ |

What does the number $220$ in the equation represent?

**Explanation**

The equation

$$
0.50(5a) + 0.70(7c) + 220 = 1,060
$$

shows the total cost of the foam materials.

The terms $0.50(5a)$ and $0.70(7c)$ represent the costs for Type A and Type C foam.

The total cost of all foam is $\[math]1,060$. So, we have

$$
\text{Cost for Type A} + \text{Cost for Type C} + 220 = 1,060
$$

That leaves $\[/math]220$ unaccounted for in the expression. Since the other foam types listed are Type B and Type D, this leftover cost must come from them.

Therefore, the $\[math]220$ represents the combined cost of the Type B and Type D foam used in the project.
