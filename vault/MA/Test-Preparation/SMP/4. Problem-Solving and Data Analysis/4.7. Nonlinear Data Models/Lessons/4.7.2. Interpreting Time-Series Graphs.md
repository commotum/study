## Determining Intervals of Maximal Change in Piecewise-Linear Models

**Example:** According to the line graph above, between which two consecutive days was there the greatest change in the number of visitors to the museum?

![](<../Source/4.7.2. Interpreting Time-Series Graphs/Images/a9d1f369c8229c7913bce10491d0d5b3.png>)

**Explanation**

The greatest change corresponds to the steepest upward/downward slope on the graph.

Therefore, from the graph, the greatest change in the number of visitors to the museum occurs from Friday to Saturday.

## Determining Intervals of Maximal Change in Exponential Models

**Example:** In a lab experiment, a heated metal rod is removed from a furnace, and its temperature is measured every $2$ minutes. The plotted points (temperature in $^\circ\!F$ versus time in minutes) show a curve that decreases quickly at first and then levels off. Over which time interval does the rod cool most rapidly?

![](<../Source/4.7.2. Interpreting Time-Series Graphs/Images/3482d1dbfb958620a356da4a2998a129.png>)

**Explanation**

The average rate of change is simply the temperature difference divided by the time it took for that change to occur. In our case, the most significant change in the rod’s temperature happened between $0$ and $2$ minutes. This is because the temperature dropped more during this period than in any other $2$-minute interval.

Therefore, from the graph, the greatest temperature difference occurs between $0$ and $2$ minutes.

## Determining Intervals of Maximal Change in Scatter Plots

**Example:** The scatterplot shows the river water level $H$, in feet, measured each week $t$ at a monitoring station. During which of the following time periods did the greatest decrease in recorded water level take place?

![](<../Source/4.7.2. Interpreting Time-Series Graphs/Images/51522791b767bc8adbdc0d2bfeb78cab.png>)

**Explanation**

The scatterplot shows that there was a decrease in recorded water level from $t=2$ to $t=3$, and from $t=5$ to $t=6$.

When $t=2$, the recorded water level was approximately $16$ feet, and when $t=3$, the recorded water level was approximately $14$ feet. This means that the decrease in recorded water level from $t=2$ to $t=3$ was approximately $(16-14)$ feet, or $2$ feet.

On the other hand, when $t=5$, the recorded water level was approximately $20$ feet, and when $t=6$, the recorded water level was approximately $11$ feet. This means that the decrease in recorded water level from $t=5$ to $t=6$ was approximately $(20-11)$ feet, or $9$ feet.

Therefore, the greatest decrease in the recorded water level occurs from $t=5$ to $t=6$.

## Identifying True Statements About Speed-Time Graphs

**Example:** A call center operated for twenty-four hours, and its time and handling rate are shown on the graph above. According to the graph, which of the following statements is **not** true concerning the call center’s operation?

![](<../Source/4.7.2. Interpreting Time-Series Graphs/Images/3b9c9997f936bb4c25f272faddc8e22e.png>)

1. The handling rate increased at a constant rate during the first four hours.
2. The handling rate was constant for eight consecutive hours.
3. The handling rate reached its maximum during the last eight hours.
4. The handling rate decreased at a constant rate during the last four hours.

**Explanation**

We proceed to analyze each statement based on the graph.

- Statement I is true because the handling rate increased at a constant rate from $0$ to $4$ hours.
- Statement II is false since no single $8$-hour interval is flat; the constant stretches are $4$–$8$ hours and $12$–$16$ hours, each only $4$ hours long.
- Statement III is false because the maximum rate of $50$ calls per hour occurred from $12$ to $16$ hours, not during the last eight hours.
- Statement IV is false since the last four hours ($20$ to $24$) show a constant rate, not a decrease.

Therefore, the correct answer is "II, III, and IV only".
