## Introduction

Questions about the average rate of change often test our ability to measure how one quantity changes compared to another.

In this topic, we’ll practice estimating and calculating average rates of change from different types of plots.

## Calculating Average Velocity

**Example:** The graph shows the temperature $y$, in degrees Celsius, of water $x$ minutes after being placed on a stove, for $0 \leq x \leq 12$. What is the average rate of change, in degrees Celsius per minute, in the water’s temperature from $x=4$ to $x=12$?

![](<../Source/4.7.3. Average Rate of Change From Data Plots/Images/660a0ea1e31b0b1501df7b24f8ba5928.png>)

**Explanation**

For the graph shown, $x$ represents time, in minutes, and $y$ represents temperature, in degrees Celsius. Therefore, the average rate of change, in degrees Celsius per minute, of the temperature of the water between two $x$-values is the difference in the corresponding $y$-values divided by the difference between the $x$-values.

The graph shows that at $x=4$, the corresponding $y$-value is $40$. Similarly, the graph shows that at $x=12$, the corresponding $y$-value is $100$.

Therefore, the average rate of change from $x=4$ to $x=12$ is

$$
\frac{100-40}{12-4} = \frac{60}{8} = 7.5\,^\circ\text{C/min}
$$

## Calculating Average Rate of Change From Scatter Plots

**Example:** During an experiment, the air pressure in a tire was recorded to the nearest integer at certain times. The scatterplot shows the recorded pressure $y$, in kilopascals (kPa), of the tire $x$ seconds after the start of the experiment.

![](<../Source/4.7.3. Average Rate of Change From Data Plots/Images/7e236ded00e97bbaeb25eedebd3ee474.png>)

What was the average rate of change, in kPa per second, of the recorded pressure from $x=2$ to $x=6$?

**Explanation**

For the graph shown, $x$ represents time, in seconds, and $y$ represents pressure, in kilopascals. Therefore, the average rate of change, in kPa per second, of the pressure between two $x$-values is the difference in the corresponding $y$-values ​​divided by the difference between the $x$-values.

The graph shows that at $x = 2$, the corresponding $y$-value is $105$. Similarly, the graph shows that at $x = 6$, the corresponding $y$-value is $125$.

Therefore, the average rate of change from $x = 2$ to $x = 6$ is

$$
\frac{125-105}{6-2} = \frac{20}{4} = 5\ \text{kPa/second}
$$

## Calculating Average Rate of Change Between Two Dates

**Example:** The scatterplot shows the relationship between the time $y$, in hours, that a tagged owl spent in nocturnal flight, and the number of days, $x$, after September $1$.

![](<../Source/4.7.3. Average Rate of Change From Data Plots/Images/e3fb039629474d92f636e00756fdbd9f.png>)

What is the average rate of change, in hours per day, of the length of time the owl spent in nocturnal flight from September $3$ to September $8$?

**Explanation**

For the graph shown, $x$ represents the number of days (after September $1$) and $y$ represents the time in hours. Therefore, the average rate of change, in hours per day, of the number of hours that the tagged owl spent in nocturnal flight between two $x$-values is the difference in the corresponding $y$-values divided by the difference between the $x$-values.

Since the $x$-values represent the days after September $1$, the origin at $x = 0$ corresponds to that date.

Now, we convert the dates September $3$ and September $8$ into $x$-values.

- September $3$ corresponds to $x = 2$.
- September $8$ corresponds to $x = 7$.

Notice that the graph shows that at $x = 2$, the corresponding $y$-value is $14$. Similarly, the graph shows that at $x = 7$, the corresponding $y$-value is $9$.

Therefore, the average rate of change from $x = 2$ to $x = 7$ (from September $3$ to September $8$) is

$$
\frac{9-14}{7-2} = \frac{-5}{5} = -1\,\text{hour per day}
$$
