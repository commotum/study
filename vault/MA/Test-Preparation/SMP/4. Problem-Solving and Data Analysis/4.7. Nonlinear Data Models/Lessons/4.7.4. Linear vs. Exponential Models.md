## Introduction

In this topic, we’ll practice spotting whether a situation describes steady, additive change (linear) or rapid, multiplicative growth or decay (exponential).

---

<a id="identifying-a-model-type-from-a-description"></a>
## Identifying a Model Type From a Description

**Example:** A video's view count increases by $220\%$ each day. If $V(t)$ denotes the number of views after $t$ days, with $t\ge 0$, what type of function best models the relationship between $V(t)$ and $t$?

1. Decreasing linear
2. Increasing exponential
3. Increasing linear
4. Decreasing exponential

**Explanation**

The view count increases by $220\%$ each day. $220\%$ as a decimal is $2.20$. Therefore, an increase of $220\%$ corresponds to multiplication by a factor of

$$
1 + 2.20 = 3.20
$$

Let $V_0$ be the initial view count. Then, we have:

- After $1$ day, there will be $V_0 \cdot (3.20)$ views.
- After $\color{blue}2$ days, there will be $V_0 \cdot (3.20)^{\color{blue}2}$ views.
- After $\color{blue}3$ days, there will be $V_0 \cdot (3.20)^{\color{blue}3}$ views.$\qquad\vdots$
- After $\color{blue}t$ days, there will be $V_0 \cdot (3.20)^{\color{blue}t}$ views.

Therefore,

$$
V(t) = V_0 \cdot (3.20)^{\color{blue}t}
$$

which is an exponential function.

Finally, since the base $(3.20)$ is greater than $1$, $V$ increases as $t$ increases.
Therefore, $V$ is an increasing exponential function.

## Working With Tables Representing Functions

**Example:** The table shows selected values from the function $k$.

| $x$ | $k(x)$ |
| --- | ---: |
| $-1$ | $6$ |
| $0$ | $1$ |
| $1$ | $-4$ |
| $2$ | $-9$ |

Which of the following is the best description of function $k$?

1. Decreasing exponential
2. Increasing exponential
3. Increasing linear
4. Decreasing linear

**Explanation**

The given values show that as $x$ increases, $k(x)$ decreases, which means that $k$ is a decreasing function.

On the other hand, $k(x)$ decreases at a constant rate of $5$ for each increase of $x$ by $1$. A function with a constant rate of change is linear. Thus, the function $k$ can be described as a decreasing linear function.

## Comparing Linear and Exponential Expressions

**Example:** Which of the following is true about the values of $7x+3$ and $7^{x}$ for $x>0$?

1. For all $x \gt 0$, it is true that $7x+3 \lt 7^{x}$.
2. There is a constant $c$ such that if $0 \lt x \lt c$, then $7x+3 \lt 7^{x}$, but if $x \gt c$, then $7^x \lt 7x+3$.
3. There is a constant $c$ such that if $0 \lt x \lt c$, then $7x+3 \gt 7^{x}$, but if $x \gt c$, then $7^x \gt 7x+3$.
4. For all $x \gt 0$, it is true that $7x+3 \gt 7^{x}$.

**Explanation**

At $x = 1$, the value of $7x+3$ is greater than the value of $7^x$. Indeed,
$\begin{bmatrix}7(1) + 3 & >^(?)7^{1} \\ 7 + 3 & >^(?)7 \\ 10 & > 7.✓\end{bmatrix}$

On the other hand, at $x = 3$, the value of $7^x$ is greater than the value of $7x+3$. Indeed,
$\begin{bmatrix}7^{3} & >^(?)7(3) + 3 \\ 343 & >^(?)21 + 3 \\ 343 & > 24.✓\end{bmatrix}$

Therefore, there is a value $c$ between $1$ and $3$ such that if $0 < x < c$, we have $7x+3 > 7^x$, and if $x > c$, we have $7^x > 7x+3$.

Therefore, the correct statement is "III only".

## Comparing Linear and Exponential Models

**Example:** Two towns grow as summarized in the table. The population of City A grows by a percentage each year.

|  | Initial population | Yearly increase |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| City A | $50{,}000$ | $3\%$ per year (applied to current population) |
| City B | $55{,}000$ | $1{,}200$ people per year |

Which of the following is true about the populations?

1. City A always gains more people per year than City B.
2. City A gains more people per year than City B at first, but eventually gains less people per year.
3. City A always gains less people per year than City B.
4. City A gains less people per year than City B at first, but eventually gains more people per year.

**Explanation**

City A starts with $50{,}000$ people and increases by $3\%$ per year. So, in the first year, City A gains

$$
50{,}000 \cdot 0.03 = 1{,}500 \text{people}
$$

which is greater than the $1{,}200$ people that City B gains that year.

A percentage increase applied to the current amount can be modeled by an increasing exponential function, so each year City A will gain more people than it did the previous year. Therefore, each year City A gains at least $1{,}500$ people. Since City B always gains $1{,}200$ people per year, thus we conclude that

> City A always gains more people per year than City B.
