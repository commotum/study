## Converting Between Units of Mass

**Example:** A factory uses $2.4\,\textrm{oz}$ of plastic to mold a casing. How many pounds of plastic are needed to mold $66$ casings?

Hint: $1 \,\textrm{lb} = 16\,\textrm{oz}$

**Explanation**

We need to calculate the total amount of plastic required in ounces.

Since each casing requires $2.4\,\textrm{oz}$, the total amount of plastic needed to mold the $66$ casings is

$$
\begin{aligned}
66 \cdot 2.4 &= 158.4oz
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we are told that there are $16$ ounces per pound, which we can write as follows:

$$
\dfrac{16\,\textrm{oz}}{\textrm{lb}}
$$

However, since we're converting from a smaller unit of mass $(\textrm{oz})$ to a larger one $(\textrm{lb})$, we need to multiply $158.4 \,\text{oz}$ by the reciprocal of the above:

$$
\dfrac{\textrm{lb}}{16\,\textrm{oz}}
$$

This is our conversion factor.

Now, multiplying $158.4 \,\text{oz}$ by our conversion factor, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
158.4oz &= 158.4oz \cdot \frac{lb}{16oz} \\
&= 158.4oz \cdot \frac{lb}{16oz} \\
&= 158.4 \cdot \frac{lb}{16} \\
&= \frac{158.4}{16}lb \\
&= 9.9lb
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, $9.9\,\textrm{lb}$ of plastic are needed to mold the $66$ casings.

## Converting Between Units of Volume

**Example:** How many tablespoons are equivalent to $36$ teaspoons?

Hint: $1\,\textrm{tbsp} = 3 \, \textrm{tsp}$

**Explanation**

We want to convert the amount from teaspoons to tablespoons.

We are given that there are $3$ teaspoons per tablespoon, which we can write as follows:

$$
\dfrac{3 \, \textrm{tsp}}{\textrm{tbsp}}
$$

However, since we're converting from a smaller unit $(\textrm{tsp})$ to a larger one $(\textrm{tbsp})$, we need to multiply $36\,\textrm{tsp}$ by the reciprocal of the above:

$$
\dfrac{\textrm{tbsp}}{3 \, \textrm{tsp}}
$$

This is our conversion factor.

Now, multiplying $36\,\textrm{tsp}$ by our conversion factor, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
36tsp &= 36tsp \cdot \frac{1tbsp}{3tsp} \\
&= 36tsp \cdot \frac{1tbsp}{3tsp} \\
&= \frac{36}{3}tbsp \\
&= 12tbsp
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, $36$ teaspoons are quivalent to ${12}$ tablespoons.

## Converting Between Units of Volume and Finding a Total

**Example:** A bowl (shown above) has a capacity of $64$ fluid ounces. How many bowls can be filled with $9$ gallons of water?

![](<../Source/4.10.3. Unit Conversions Between Units of Mass and Volume/Images/7bd9e93a04195d035207c845646d9bfc.png>)

Hint: $1\,\textrm{gal} = 128\,\textrm{fl oz}$

**Explanation**

We want to find how many bowls can be filled, which first requires converting the total volume of water from gallons to fluid ounces.

We are given that there are $128$ fluid ounces per gallon, which we can write as follows:

$$
\dfrac{128 \, \textrm{fl oz}}{\textrm{gal}}
$$

This is our conversion factor.

Now, multiplying $9\,\textrm{gal}$ by our conversion factor, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
9gal &= 9gal \cdot \frac{128fl oz}{gal} \\
&= 9gal \cdot \frac{128fl oz}{gal} \\
&= 9 \cdot 128fl oz \\
&= 1, 152fl oz
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, to find how many bowls can be filled, we divide the total fluid ounces by the capacity of one bowl:

$$
\text{Number of bowls} = \dfrac{1,152 \, \textrm{fl oz}}{64 \, \textrm{fl oz/bowl}} = 18 \, \text{bowls}
$$

Therefore, $18$ bowls can be filled with $9$ gallons of water.
