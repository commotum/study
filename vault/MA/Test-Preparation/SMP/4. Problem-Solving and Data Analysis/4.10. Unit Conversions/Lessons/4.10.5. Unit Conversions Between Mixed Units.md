## Converting the Numerator in Mixed Units to Smaller Units

**Example:** A postal truck maintained an average speed of $15\,\dfrac{\textrm{mi}}{\textrm{h}}$ while delivering packages. What was its speed in yards per hour?

Hint: $1\,\textrm{mi} = 1,760\,\textrm{yd}$

**Explanation**

We want to convert the postal truck’s speed from miles per hour to yards per hour.

We are given that there are $1,760$ yards per mile, which we can write as follows:

$$
\dfrac{1,760 \, \textrm{yd}}{\textrm{mi}}
$$

This is our conversion factor.

Now, multiplying

$$
15\,\dfrac{\textrm{mi}}{\textrm{h}}
$$

by our conversion factor, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
15\frac{mi}{h} &= 15\frac{mi}{h} \cdot \frac{1, 760yd}{mi} \\
&= 15\frac{mi}{h} \cdot \frac{1, 760yd}{mi} \\
&= 15 \cdot 1, 760\frac{yd}{h} \\
&= 26, 400\frac{yd}{h}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the truck's speed is

$$
26,400\,\dfrac{\textrm{yd}}{\textrm{h}}
$$

## Converting the Numerator in Mixed Units to Larger Units

**Example:** A toy car moves at a speed of $\dfrac{1}{50}\,\dfrac{\textrm{ft}}{\textrm{s}}$. What is its speed in yards per second?

Hint: $1\,\textrm{yd} = 3\,\textrm{ft}$

**Explanation**

We want to convert the toy car's speed from feet per second to yards per second.

We are given that there are $3$ feet per yard, which we can write as follows:

$$
\dfrac{3 \, \textrm{ft}}{\textrm{yd}}
$$

However, since we're converting from a smaller unit of length $(\textrm{ft})$ to a larger one $(\textrm{yd})$, we need to multiply

$$
\dfrac{1}{50}\,\dfrac{\textrm{ft}}{\textrm{s}}
$$

by the reciprocal of the above:

$$
\dfrac{\textrm{yd}}{3 \, \textrm{ft}}
$$

This is our conversion factor.

Now, multiplying

$$
\dfrac{1}{50}\,\dfrac{\textrm{ft}}{\textrm{s}}
$$

by our conversion factor, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{1}{50}\frac{ft}{s} &= \frac{1}{50}\frac{ft}{s} \cdot \frac{1yd}{3ft} \\
&= \frac{1}{50}\frac{ft}{s} \cdot \frac{1yd}{3ft} \\
&= (\frac{1}{50} \cdot \frac{1}{3})\frac{yd}{s} \\
&= \frac{1}{150}\frac{yd}{s}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the toy car's speed is

$$
\dfrac{1}{150}\,\dfrac{\textrm{yd}}{\textrm{s}}
$$

## Converting the Denominator in Mixed Units

**Example:** A rescue boat moves at a steady pace of $0.012 \, \dfrac{\textrm{mi}}{\textrm{s}}$. What is this speed in miles per hour?

**Explanation**

We want to convert the boat's speed from miles per second to miles per hour.

There are $60\,\textrm {min}$ per $\textrm{hour}$, and also $60\,\textrm{s}$ per $\textrm{min}$. We can write this as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{60min}{hour} &= \frac{60min}{hour} \cdot \frac{60s}{min} \\
&= \frac{60min}{hour} \cdot \frac{60s}{min} \\
&= \frac{3, 600s}{hour}
\end{aligned}
$$

This is our conversion factor.

Now, multiplying

$$
0.012\,\dfrac{\textrm{mi}}{\textrm{s}}
$$

by our conversion factor, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
0.012\frac{mi}{s} &= 0.012\frac{mi}{s} \cdot \frac{3, 600s}{h} \\
&= 0.012\frac{mi}{s} \cdot \frac{3, 600s}{h} \\
&= 0.012 \cdot 3, 600\frac{mi}{h} \\
&= 43.2\frac{mi}{h} \\
&\approx 43\frac{mi}{h}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the boat's speed is approximately

$$
43\,\dfrac{\textrm{mi}}{\textrm{h}}
$$

## Converting the Numerator and Denominator in Mixed Units

**Example:** Which of the following speeds is equivalent to $18\,\dfrac{\textrm{km}}{\textrm{h}}$?

1. $2.5\,\dfrac{\textrm{m}}{\textrm{s}}$
2. $5\,\dfrac{\textrm{m}}{\textrm{s}}$
3. $4\,\dfrac{\textrm{m}}{\textrm{s}}$

Hint: $1\,\textrm{km} = 1,000\,\textrm{m}$

**Explanation**

We are given the units

$$
\dfrac{\textrm{km}}{\textrm{h}}
$$

and we want to convert this into

$$
\dfrac{\textrm{m}}{\textrm{s}}
$$

We're converting two units instead of just one, so we're going to have to multiply by two conversion factors:

$$
\dfrac{\textrm{km}}{\textrm{h}}\cdot \,\dfrac{?}{?} \cdot \,\dfrac{?}{?} = \dfrac{\textrm{m}}{\textrm{s}}
$$

One of the conversion factors should change $\textrm{km}$ to $\textrm{m}$.

$$
\dfrac{\cancel{\textrm{km}}}{\textrm{h}}\cdot \dfrac{\textrm{m}}{\cancel{\textrm{km}}} \cdot \,\dfrac{?}{?} = \dfrac{\textrm{m}}{\textrm{s}}
$$

The other conversion factor should change $\textrm{h}$ into $\textrm{s}$.

$$
\dfrac{\cancel{\textrm{km}}}{\cancel{\textrm{h}}}\cdot \dfrac{\textrm{m}}{\cancel{\textrm{km}}} \cdot \dfrac{\cancel{\textrm{h}}}{\textrm{s}} = \dfrac{\textrm{m}}{\textrm{s}}
$$

So, our conversion formula is

$$
\dfrac{\textrm{km}}{\textrm{h}}\cdot \dfrac{\textrm{m}}{\textrm{km}} \cdot \dfrac{\textrm{h}}{\textrm{s}} = \dfrac{\textrm{m}}{\textrm{s}}
$$

Now that we have our conversion formula, we need to determine the correct numbers to use and apply them.

- The rate given in the problem statement is $18 \, \dfrac{\textrm{km}}{\textrm{h}}$.
- We are given that there are $1,000$ meters per kilometer.
- The number of seconds in one hour is given by
$1h|= 60min; = 60 \cdot 1min; = 60 \cdot 60s; = 3, 600s$.
Taking the reciprocal, we find that there are $\dfrac{1}{3,600}$ hours per second.

Applying our conversion formula, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
18\frac{km}{h} &= 18\frac{km}{h} \cdot 1, 000\frac{m}{km} \cdot \frac{1}{3, 600}\frac{h}{s} \\
&= (18 \cdot 1, 000 \cdot \frac{1}{3, 600})\frac{km}{h} \cdot \frac{m}{km} \cdot \frac{h}{s} \\
&= \frac{18, 000}{3, 600}\frac{m}{s} \\
&= 5\frac{m}{s}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
18\,\dfrac{\textrm{km}}{\textrm{h}}
$$

is equivalent to

$$
5\,\dfrac{\textrm{m}}{\textrm{s}}
$$

So, the correct answer is "II only."
