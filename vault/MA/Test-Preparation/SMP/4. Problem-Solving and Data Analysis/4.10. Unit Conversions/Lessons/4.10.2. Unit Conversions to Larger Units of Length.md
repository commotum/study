## Introduction

Questions about unit conversions often appear in real-world contexts and require paying attention to conversion factors.

In this lesson, we’ll practice converting from smaller units of length to larger ones.

<a id="converting-from-inches-to-yards"></a>
## Converting From Inches to Yards

**Example:** A construction worker is installing a pipe that measures $252\,\textrm{in}$ in length. How many yards of pipe are needed?

Hint: $1\,\textrm{yd} = 36 \, \textrm{in}$

**Explanation**

We are given that there are $36$ inches per yard, which we can write as follows:

$$
\dfrac{36 \, \textrm{in}}{\textrm{yd}}
$$

However, since we're converting from a smaller unit of length $(\textrm{in})$ to a larger one $(\textrm{yd})$, we need to multiply $252\,\textrm{in}$ by the reciprocal of the above:

$$
\dfrac{\textrm{yd}}{36 \, \textrm{in}}
$$

This is our conversion factor.

Now, multiplying $252\,\textrm{in}$ by our conversion factor, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
252in &= 252in \cdot \frac{yd}{36in} \\
&= 252in \cdot \frac{yd}{36in} \\
&= 252 \cdot \frac{yd}{36} \\
&= \frac{252}{36}yd \\
&= 7yd
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the construction worker needs $7\,\textrm{yd}$ of pipe.

## Converting From Yards to Miles

**Example:** A wolf was tracked migrating a distance of $1,936 \, \textrm{yd}$ through a forest corridor. How many miles did it travel?

Hint: $1\,\textrm{mi} = 1,760 \, \textrm{yd}$

**Explanation**

We are told that there are $1,760$ yards per mile, which we can write as follows:

$$
\dfrac{1,760 \, \textrm{yd}}{\textrm{mi}}
$$

However, since we're converting from a smaller unit of length $(\textrm{yd})$ to a larger one $(\textrm{mi})$, we need to multiply $1,936\,\textrm{yd}$ by the reciprocal of the above:

$$
\dfrac{\textrm{mi}}{1,760 \, \textrm{yd}}
$$

This is our conversion factor.

Now, multiplying $1,936\,\textrm{yd}$ by our conversion factor, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
1, 936yd &= 1, 936yd \cdot \frac{mi}{1, 760yd} \\
&= 1, 936yd \cdot \frac{mi}{1, 760yd} \\
&= 1, 936 \cdot \frac{mi}{1, 760} \\
&= \frac{1, 936}{1, 760}mi \\
&= 1.1mi
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the wolf traveled $1.1\,\textrm{mi}$ through the corridor.

## Converting From Centimeters to Meters

**Example:** A swimming pool lane marker measures $2,200\,\textrm{cm}$ in length. The coach wants to log this measurement in meters. How many meters long is the lane marker?

Hint: $1\,\textrm{m} = 100\,\textrm{cm}$

**Explanation**

We want to convert the lane marker length from centimeters to meters.

We are given that there are $100$ centimeters per meter, which we can write as follows:

$$
\dfrac{100 \, \textrm{cm}}{\textrm{m}}
$$

However, since we're converting from a smaller unit of length $(\textrm{cm})$ to a larger one $(\textrm{m})$, we need to multiply $2,200\,\textrm{cm}$ by the reciprocal of the above:

$$
\dfrac{\textrm{m}}{100 \, \textrm{cm}}
$$

This is our conversion factor.

Now, multiplying $2,200\,\textrm{cm}$ by our conversion factor, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
2, 200cm &= 2, 200cm \cdot \frac{m}{100cm} \\
&= 2, 200cm \cdot \frac{m}{100cm} \\
&= 2, 200 \cdot \frac{m}{100} \\
&= \frac{2, 200}{100}m \\
&= 22m
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the lane marker is $22\,\textrm{m}$ long.
