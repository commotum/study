## Introduction

A key concept that often features on the math section of the SAT exam is understanding the relationship between the mean of a dataset and the sum of its values.

In this topic, we will practice expressing the mean as a fraction of the sum, combining means from different groups, and using sums to find unknown or constrained data values.

## Expressing the Mean as a Fraction of the Sum

**Example:** $6,\: 10,\: 14,\: 18,\: 22,\: 26$

The mean of the list of numbers above is what fraction of the sum? Write your answer in its simplified form.

**Explanation**

The mean of a dataset is the sum of the values divided by the number of values.

In this case, there are $6$ values. So, the mean is

$$
\begin{aligned}
Mean &= \frac{6 + 10 + 14 + 18 + 22 + 26}{6} \\
&= \frac{1}{6}(6 + 10 + 14 + 18 + 22 + 26)
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the mean is

$$
{\dfrac{1}{6}}
$$

of the sum.

## Combining Means

**Example:** Dataset $X$ consists of the working hours of $20$ employees and has a mean of $20$ hours. Dataset $Y$ consists of the working hours of $30$ employees and has a mean of $40$ hours. Dataset $Z$ consists of the working hours of the $50$ employees from datasets $X$ and $Y$. What is the mean of dataset $Z$?

**Explanation**

We are told that dataset $X$ contains $20$ employees and has a mean of $20$ hours. So, the sum $S_X$ of all hours in dataset $X$ is

$$
S_X = 20 \cdot 20 = 400
$$

We are also told that dataset $Y$ contains $30$ employees and has a mean of $40$ hours. So, the sum $S_Y$ of all hours in dataset $Y$ is

$$
S_Y = 30 \cdot 40 = 1\,200
$$

Now, since dataset $Z$ includes all $50$ employees from $X$ and $Y$, the sum $S_Z$ of all hours in dataset $Z$ is

$$
\begin{aligned}
S_{Z} &= S_{X} + S_{Y} \\
&= 400 + 1200 \\
&= 1600hours
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the mean of dataset $Z$ is

$$
\text{Mean} = \frac{1\,600}{50} = {32}\,\text{hours}
$$

## Using Sums to Determine an Unknown Data Point

**Example:** The mean score in a science fair with $19$ participants was $29$ points. After the lowest-scoring participant is removed, the mean score of the remaining $18$ participants becomes $30$ points. What was the lowest score?

**Explanation**

The sum of the values in a dataset is the product of the mean and the number of values.

Let $S_{\text{orig}}$ be the total points for all $19$ participants, and $S_{\text{new}}$ be the total for the remaining $18$ participants. So, the sums are as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
S_{orig} &= 19 \cdot 29 = 551.0 \\
S_{new} &= 18 \cdot 30 = 540.0
\end{aligned}
$$

The removed participant’s score is the difference:

$$
S_{\text{orig}} - S_{\text{new}} = 551.0 - 540.0 = 11
$$

Therefore, the lowest score was ${11}$ points.

## Finding the Largest Value in a Dataset Given Constraints on the Mean

**Example:** Dataset $Q$ consists of $10$ positive integers less than $70$. The list below gives $9$ of the integers from dataset $Q{:}$

$44,\: 47,\: 49,\: 57,\: 45,\: 46,\: 48,\: 47,\: 49$

The mean of these $9$ values is $48$. If the mean of dataset $Q$ is an integer greater than $48$, what is the value of the largest integer from dataset $Q$?

**Explanation**

We are told that the mean of the $9$ known values is $48$. Using this, we can compute the sum $S$ of those $9$ values, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
Mean &= \frac{S}{9} \\
S &= 9 \cdot Mean \\
&= 9 \cdot 48 \\
&= 432
\end{aligned}
$$

Every value in dataset $Q$ is a positive integer less than $70$, and the mean of dataset $Q$ is an integer that is greater than $48$. Let's consider possible values for the mean, starting with the smallest.

- If the mean of $Q$ is $49$, then the total sum of the dataset is
$10 \cdot 49 = 490$.
Subtracting the sum of the nine given integers, the missing value is
$490 - S = 490 - 432 = 58$.
Since $58 < 70$, this value *can* belong to the dataset.
- If the mean of $Q$ is $50$, then the total sum of the dataset is
$10 \cdot 50 = 500$.
Subtracting the sum of the nine given integers, the missing value is
$500 - S = 500 - 432 = 68$.
Since $68 < 70$, this value *also* can belong to the dataset.
- If the mean of $Q$ is $51$, then the total sum of the dataset is
$10 \cdot 51 = 510$.
Subtracting the sum of the nine given integers, the missing value is
$510 - S = 510 - 432 = 78$.
Since $78 > 70$, this value *cannot* belong to the dataset.

We stop here because increasing the mean any further would only increase the missing value, which would continue to violate the constraint that all values must be less than $70$.

Therefore, the largest possible missing value, and hence, the largest value in dataset $Q$, is ${68}$.
