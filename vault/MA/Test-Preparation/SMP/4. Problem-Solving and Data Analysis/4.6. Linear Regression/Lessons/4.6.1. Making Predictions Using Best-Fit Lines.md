## Introduction

Lines of best fit help us predict unknown values from data that follow a linear pattern.

In this lesson, we’ll practice making these predictions.

---

<a id="making-predictions-using-lines-of-best-fit"></a>
## Making Predictions Using Lines of Best Fit

**Example:** At $x=13$, which of the following is closest to the $y$-value predicted by the line of best fit?

![](<../Source/4.6.1. Making Predictions Using Best-Fit Lines/Images/e2097c4b8ad26a31b529537254473a0b.png>)

1. $10.3$
2. $11.6$
3. $12.4$
4. $9.1$

**Explanation**

We want to find the closest value to the $y$-value predicted by the line of best fit at $x = 13$.

From the scatterplot, we locate $x = 13$ and see where it meets the line of best fit. Visually, this point is between $10$ and $11$ on the $y$-axis.

Among the given answer choices, the closest value to this estimate is $10.3$.

Therefore, the predicted $y$-value at $x = 13$ is $10.3$.

## Making Predictions Using Lines of Best Fit: Word Problems

**Example:** The scatterplot shows the data on how many hours children of different ages slept in a day, collected by a doctor in a survey that involved $5$ participants. A line of best fit is also shown. How long (in hours) does a $7$-year-old child sleep in a day, as predicted by the line of best fit?

![](<../Source/4.6.1. Making Predictions Using Best-Fit Lines/Images/b521954ec2114ebda435be8732e21f5b.png>)

1. $8.14$ hours
2. $13.54$ hours
3. $3.77$ hours
4. $10.85$ hours

**Explanation**

We want to estimate the sleep duration (in hours) of a $7$-year-old child using the line of best fit.

From the scatterplot, we locate $x = 7$ and see where it meets the line of best fit. Visually, this point is slightly above $8$ hours on the $y$-axis.

Among the given answer choices, the closest value to this estimate is $8.14$ hours.

Therefore, a $7$-year-old child is predicted to sleep for about $8.14$ hours in a day.
