## Introduction

Questions about residuals usually test the ability to compare an actual point to the corresponding point on the line of best fit.

In this topic, we’ll practice identifying overestimates and underestimates, including identifying data points that struggle to fit the general trend.

## Counting the Number of Points Underestimated or Overestimated by a Line of Best Fit

**Example:** The scatter plot above shows $9$ data points in the relationship between two variables $x$ and $y$, along with a line of best fit. For how many of the $9$ data points is the actual $y$-value less than the $y$-value predicted by the line of best fit?

![](<../Source/4.6.5. Analyzing Residuals of Best-Fit Lines/Images/c1cac3f251a1188d6d43370f0c593335.png>)

**Explanation**

A data point's predicted $y$-value is the $y$-value of the point on the line of best fit at the same $x$-coordinate as the point.

A data point's actual $y$-value is less than the $y$-value predicted by the line of best fit if it lies below this line.

![](<../Source/4.6.5. Analyzing Residuals of Best-Fit Lines/Images/d2755fe09e240ccb5b7c6f09e57b3f27.png>)

Counting all such cases gives a total of $3$ data points below the line.

Therefore, the number of data points with an actual $y$-value less than the $y$-value predicted by the line of best fit is $3$.

## Finding a Data Point Corresponding to a Residual

**Example:** The scatterplot shows the relationship between $x$ and $y$. A line of best fit for the data is also shown. What is the difference between the $y$-coordinate of the data point with $x=5$ and the $y$-value predicted by the line of best fit at $x=5$? Rounded to one decimal place.

![](<../Source/4.6.5. Analyzing Residuals of Best-Fit Lines/Images/0f0b7bc42c3552a85f1dda2575daee3e.png>)

**Explanation**

Explanation:

We want to compare the actual $y$-value with the predicted $y$-value from the line of best fit at $x = 5$.

From the graph, we observe that:

- The data point with $x=5$ has a $y$-coordinate of $18$.
- At $x=5$, the line of best fit reaches exactly $17.5$ on the $y$-axis.

So, the difference between the data point and the predicted value is

$$
\bigl|\,18-17.5\,\bigr| \approx 0.5
$$

Among the given answer choices, the closest value to this estimate is $0.5$.

## Identifying Data Points Corresponding to a Minimum Underestimate or Overestimate

**Example:** The scatter plot above shows several data points in the relationship between two variables $x$ and $y$ along with a line of best fit.
Which of the labeled data points $A$, $B$, or $C$ has a $y$-value that is at least $10$ less than the value predicted by the line of best fit?

![](<../Source/4.6.5. Analyzing Residuals of Best-Fit Lines/Images/2ac8b6425c2fd64a6cd5d074848c5d5e.png>)

**Explanation**

A data point's predicted $y$-value is the $y$-value of the point on the line of best fit at the same $x$-coordinate as the point.

A data point's actual $y$-value is less than the $y$-value predicted by the line of best fit if it lies below this line.

With that in mind, let's consider each of the given points in turn.

- First, consider data point $A$ with coordinates $(22,35)$. At $x=22$, the line of best fit predicts a corresponding $y$-value of $y \approx 48$.![](<../Source/4.6.5. Analyzing Residuals of Best-Fit Lines/Images/7cf3b1bd4c8b75b968a59ece94a4242c.png>)On the other hand, the actual $y$-value of $A$ is $y=35$. So, the difference between the actual and predicted $y$-values is
$48 - 35 = 13 \geq 10$.
Hence, the actual $y$-value of $A$ *is* at least $10$ less than the predicted $y$-value.
- Next, consider data point $B$ with coordinates $(25,65)$. Since $B$ lies above the line, its $y$-value is *greater than* the value predicted by the line of best fit, not less than.![](<../Source/4.6.5. Analyzing Residuals of Best-Fit Lines/Images/14236ae16f642c7b19877845645e8b87.png>)Hence, the actual $y$-value of $B$ *is not* at least $10$ less than the predicted $y$-value.

- Finally, consider data point $C$ with coordinates $(27,45)$. At $x=27$, the line of best fit predicts a corresponding $y$-value of $y \approx 59$.![](<../Source/4.6.5. Analyzing Residuals of Best-Fit Lines/Images/a31d56d33e6a3613fd75c65d3105d15c.png>) On the other hand, the actual $y$-value of $C$ is $y=45$. So, the difference between the actual and predicted $y$-values is
$59 - 45 = 14 \geq 10$.
Hence, the actual $y$-value of $C$ *is* at least $10$ less than the predicted $y$-value.

Therefore, the actual $y$-values of $A$ and $C$ are at least $10$ less than the predicted $y$-values.

## Identifying the Worst Predicted Data Point

**Example:** A productivity audit tracked $27$ workdays. For each workday, the average time in meetings ($x$, in hours) and the average time in focused work ($y$, in hours) were recorded to the nearest half hour. The scatter plot above shows the data with a line of best fit.

![](<../Source/4.6.5. Analyzing Residuals of Best-Fit Lines/Images/986f18539de3f92c24d0a83fa5ba5f22.png>)

The line of best fit **overestimates** one workday’s reported focused-work time by more than $1$ hour. How many hours of meetings were recorded this day?

**Explanation**

The line of best fit predicts a day's average time in focused work ($y$) given its average time in meetings ($x$).

The line *overestimates* a day’s reported focused-work time if its corresponding point on the scatter plot lies *below* the line.

![](<../Source/4.6.5. Analyzing Residuals of Best-Fit Lines/Images/92d3817c02fba2d3311ade93534f76e1.png>)

We are told that, for one of these points, the line of best fit overestimates the day’s reported focused-work time by more than $1$ hour.

Consider the data point at $(2,3)$. For a day with $x=2$ hours of meetings, the line of best fit predicts approximately

$$
y \approx 4.2
$$

hours of focused work.

![](<../Source/4.6.5. Analyzing Residuals of Best-Fit Lines/Images/a0ebc16affc1f6ea5e17024751ba27aa.png>)

But the day corresponding to $(2,3)$ actually has $y=3$ hours of focused work. So, the line of best fit overestimates this day’s focused-work hours by

$$
4.2 - 3 = 1.2 > 1
$$

No other point is that far below the line, so this must be the day in question. Therefore, $2$ hours of meetings were recorded this day.
