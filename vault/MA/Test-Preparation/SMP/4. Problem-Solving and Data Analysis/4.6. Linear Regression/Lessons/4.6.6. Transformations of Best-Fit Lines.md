## Introduction

On the math section of the SAT exam, we may be asked to demonstrate how changes to data affect the line of best fit.

In this lesson, we’ll practice recognizing these effects so we can adjust the corresponding equations of the lines.

## Transforming the Dependent Variable of a Best-Fit Line

**Example:** The scatter plot above shows an approximate trend line for the relationship between two variables $x$ and $y$ in a dataset $R$.

![](<../Source/4.6.6. Transformations of Best-Fit Lines/Images/4b91d7cdf671d60aa3b92c4ee8eec977.png>)

A new dataset $S$ is formed by multiplying the $y$-coordinate of every data point of $R$ by $2.8$. Which of the following could be an equation of a trend line for dataset $S$?

1. $y=-0.4x+44.8$
2. $y=-1.12x+16$
3. $y=-0.4x+16$
4. $y=-1.12x+44.8$

**Explanation**

First, we find an approximate trend line for $R$ from the plot.

![](<../Source/4.6.6. Transformations of Best-Fit Lines/Images/fa956d1b8c377a5d86edca58dd9280fa.png>)

It seems that the trend line passes through the points

$$
(x_1,y_1) \approx (15,10)
$$

and

$$
(x_2,y_2) \approx (27.5,5)
$$

So, an estimate of the slope $m$ of the line is

$$
m \mid \approx \frac{y_{2} - y_{1}}{x_{2} - x_{1}}; = \frac{5 - 10}{27.5 - 15}; = ((-5))/(12.5); =-0.4
$$

So, an approximate trend line, in slope-intercept form, is

$$
y = -0.4x + b
$$

where $b$ is the $y$-intercept to be estimated. We do this by substituting in the coordinates of a point on the line, say $(15,10){:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
y &= -0.4x + b \\
10 &= -0.4(15) + b \\
10 &= -6 + b \\
16 &= b \\
b &= 16
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the equation of the trend line for dataset $R$ is

$$
y = -0.4x + 16
$$

We're told that dataset $S$ is formed by multiplying the $y$-coordinate of every data point of $R$ by $2.8$.

Let $y_R$ be a $y$-value in $R$ and $y_S$ be the corresponding $y$-value in $S$. Then, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
y_{S} &= 2.8y_{R} \\
&= 2.8(-0.4x + 16) \\
&=-1.12x + 44.8
\end{aligned}
$$

So, a suitable line of best fit for $S$ is

$$
y = -1.12x + 44.8
$$

Therefore, the correct answer is **only I.**

## Transforming the Independent Variable of a Best-Fit Line

**Example:** The scatter plot above shows an approximate trend line for the relationship between two variables $x$ and $y$ in a dataset $R$.

![](<../Source/4.6.6. Transformations of Best-Fit Lines/Images/a2d08a42d948392029f13d5a131586a1.png>)

A new dataset $S$ is formed by multiplying the $x$-coordinate of every data point of $R$ by $2$. Which of the following could be an equation of a trend line for dataset $S$?

1. $y=1.5x + \dfrac 2 3$
2. $y=0.75x+2$
3. $y=0.75x+\dfrac 2 3$
4. $y=0.5x+2$

**Explanation**

First, we find an approximate trend line for $R$ from the plot.

![](<../Source/4.6.6. Transformations of Best-Fit Lines/Images/eaf1df91b5c9eab89900f72e26da26b7.png>)

It seems that the trend line passes through the points

$$
(x_1,y_1) \approx (4,8)
$$

and

$$
(x_2,y_2) \approx (12,20)
$$

So, an estimate of the slope $m$ of the line is

$$
m \mid \approx \frac{y_{2} - y_{1}}{x_{2} - x_{1}}; = \frac{20 - 8}{12 - 4}; = \frac{12}{8}; = 1.5
$$

So, an approximate trend line, in slope-intercept form, is

$$
y=1.5x+b
$$

where $b$ is the $y$-intercept to be estimated. We do this by substituting in the coordinates of a point on the line, say $(4,8){:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
y &= 1.5x + b \\
8 &= 1.5(4) + b \\
8 &= 6 + b \\
2 &= b
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the equation of the trend line for dataset $R$ is

$$
y=1.5x+2
$$

We're told that dataset $S$ is formed by multiplying the $x$-coordinate of every data point of $R$ by $2$.

Let $x_R$ be a $x$-value in $R$ and $x_S$ be the corresponding $x$-value in $S$. Then, we have

$$
x_S = 2 x_R
$$

Subsituting

$$
x_R = \dfrac {x_S} 2
$$

into the equation for $R{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
y_{S} &= 1.5(\frac{x_{S}}{2}) + 2 \\
&= 0.75x + 2
\end{aligned}
$$

So, a suitable line of best fit for $S$ is

$$
y=0.75x+2
$$

Therefore, the correct answer is **only II.**
