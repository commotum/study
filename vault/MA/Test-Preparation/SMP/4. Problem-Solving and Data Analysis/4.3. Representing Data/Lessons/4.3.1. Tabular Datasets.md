## Finding a Mean From a Table

**Example:** The table below shows the number of items sold by six market vendors on a single day in $2023$. Based on the table, what is the mean number of items sold by these $6$ vendors? Round your answer to the nearest integer.

| Vendor | Items Sold |
| --- | ---: |
| Angela | $12$ |
| Ben | $18$ |
| Carlos | $15$ |
| Dina | $21$ |
| Ella | $16$ |
| Fred | $17$ |

**Explanation**

The mean (or average) is calculated by dividing the sum of all the data values by the number of values.

First, we add all the quantities:

$$
12 + 18 + 15 + 21 + 16 + 17 = 99
$$

Then, we divide the total by the number of data points. Since there are $6$ values, the mean is

$$
\begin{aligned}
Mean &= \frac{99}{6} \\
&= 16.5 \\
&\approx 17
\end{aligned}
$$

rounded to the nearest integer.

Therefore, the mean number of items sold is approximately $17$.

## Finding a Median From a Table

**Example:** The table above shows the number of gallons of water consumed by a household on six different days. What is the median number of gallons consumed?

| Day $1$ | Day $2$ | Day $3$ | Day $4$ | Day $5$ | Day $6$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $2.5$ | $3.1$ | $2.8$ | $2.8$ | $3.0$ | $3.5$ |

**Explanation**

The median is the middle value of a data set when the values are arranged in order from smallest to greatest.

Ordering the data points from smallest to greatest, we get
$2.5, \: 2.8, \: 2.8, \: 3.0, \: 3.1, \: 3.5$.

There are $6$ data values, which is an even number. For an even number of values, the median is the average of the two middle values. Since $6$ values are present, the median is the average of the $3$rd and $4$th ones:

$$
\begin{aligned}
median &= \frac{2.8 + 3.0}{2} \\
&= \frac{5.8}{2} \\
&= 2.9
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the median number of gallons consumed is $2.9$.

## Finding a Difference of Medians From a Table

**Example:** A quality assurance team reviewed the number of units produced at several factories in one day. The median number of units produced across all sites reviewed was $620$. What is the difference between the median number of units produced for the $5$ factories listed above and the site-wide median?

| Factory | Number of units produced in a day |
| --- | ---: |
| Alpha | $620$ |
| Beta | $615$ |
| Gamma | $612$ |
| Delta | $630$ |
| Epsilon | $618$ |

**Explanation**

The median is the middle value of a data set when the values are arranged in order from smallest to greatest.

We proceed to find the median number of units produced for the $5$ given factories.

Ordering the data points from smallest to greatest, we get
$612, \: 615, \: 618, \: 620, \: 630$.

There are $5$ data values, which is an odd number. For an odd number of values, the median is the middle value. Since $5$ values are present, the middle value is the $3$rd one:
$612, \: 615, \: \bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{618}, \: 620, \: 630$.
Therefore, the median number of units produced for the $5$ factories is $618$.

Finally, the difference between the median number of units produced for the $5$ factories and the site-wide median is

$$
620 - 618 = \bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{2}
$$

## Finding Statistics From a Table

**Example:** The high temperature, in degrees Fahrenheit $(^{\circ}\, \mathrm{F})$, in a certain town was recorded for each of $5$ days.

| Day | $1$ | $2$ | $3$ | $4$ | $5$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| High temperature | $78$ | $77$ | $77$ | $78$ | $79$ |

Over this $5$-day period, which of the following is **not** equal to $78^\circ \,\mathrm{F}$ rounded to the nearest integer?

1. Range of high temperatures
2. Mean of the high temperatures
3. Mode of the high temperatures
4. Median of the high temperatures
5. None of the listed

**Explanation**

We proceed to calculate the mean, median, mode, and range separately.

- The mean (or average) is calculated by dividing the sum of all the data values by the number of values.First, we add all the high temperatures:
$78 + 77 + 77 + 78 + 79 = 389$
Then, we divide the total by the number of data points. Since there are $5$ high temperatures, the mean high temperature is
$\text{Mean} = \dfrac{389}{5} = 77.8$.
Hence, the mean is $78^\circ\,\mathrm{F}$ rounded to the nearest integer.
- The median is the middle value of a data set when the values are arranged in order from smallest to greatest.Ordering the data in ascending order, we obtain
$77, \: 77, \: 78, \: 78, \: 79$.
There are $5$ data points, which is an odd number. When the number of values is odd, the median is the value that falls exactly in the middle. That means we take the $3$rd value:
$77,\: 77,\: \bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{78},\: 78,\: 79$
Therefore, the median of the data set is $78^\circ\, \mathrm{F}$.
- The mode is the value that occurs most often in the data set.In our case, the values $77$ and $78$ both appear more frequently than the others ($2$ times each in total). So, in particular, $78^\circ \,\mathrm{F}$ is a mode.
- The range of a data set is the difference between the greatest and smallest values.In our case, the greatest value is $79^\circ\,\mathrm{F}$ and the smallest value is $77^\circ\,\mathrm{F}$. Therefore, the range is
$79^\circ\,\mathrm{F} - 77^\circ\,\mathrm{F} = 2^\circ\,\mathrm{F}$.

Therefore, among the choices, the correct answer is I. So, the range is the only one that is not equal to $78^\circ\, \mathrm{F}$.
