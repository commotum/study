## Identifying a Category From a Bar Graph

**Example:** The bar graph above shows the number of baskets of fruit sold at a market. Which fruit was sold $30$ times?

![](<../Source/4.3.3. Bar Graphs/Images/fbb90624a84df0ecfb945df3f90bca48.png>)

**Explanation**

In a bar graph, the frequency (number) of values in each category is represented by the height of the corresponding bar.

From the graph, the bar above "Oranges" has a height of $30$.

Therefore, the fruit sold $30$ times is Oranges.

## Calculating a Difference in Frequencies From a Bar Graph

**Example:** The bar graph above shows the number of people using different transport modes.

![](<../Source/4.3.3. Bar Graphs/Images/0efd1c32a93550a60511ff382ea89773.png>)

How many more people use Cars than Bikes?

**Explanation**

In a bar graph, the frequency (number) of values in each category is represented by the height of the corresponding bar.

From the graph:

- The number of people using Cars is $25$.
- The number of people using Bikes is $15$.

Subtracting, we obtain

$$
25 - 15 = 10
$$

Therefore, there are $10$ more people who use Cars than Bikes.

## Calculating the Mean Given a Bar Graph

**Example:** A company tracked the number of laptops sold by five stores labeled $A$ through $E$. The bar graph shows the number of laptops sold. What is the mean number of laptops sold per store?

![](<../Source/4.3.3. Bar Graphs/Images/c9f352e56226f220eaf9b1c9c136c40c.png>)

**Explanation**

In a bar graph, the frequency (number) of values in each category is represented by the height of the corresponding bar.

From the graph, the numbers of laptops sold in the five stores are $24,\, 20,\, 22,\, 18$, and $22$. Thus, the mean is the sum of these values divided by the number of stores, $5{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
Mean &= \frac{24 + 20 + 22 + 18 + 22}{5} \\
&= \frac{106}{5} \\
&= 21.2
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the mean number of laptops sold per store is $21.2$.
