## Introduction

Expressing percentage change using decimals tests whether we can translate between percentage increases or decreases and their decimal multipliers.

In this topic, we’ll practice these conversions.

---

<a id="converting-a-percentage-increase-to-a-decimal"></a>
## Converting a Percentage Increase to a Decimal

**Example:** The number $p$ is $40\%$ greater than $250$. Which of the following could be the value of $k$, if $p$ is equal to $250$ times $k$?

1. $1.4$
2. $0.40$
3. $0.25$
4. $100$

**Explanation**

We are told that $p$ is $40\%$ greater than $250$.

This means we are increasing $250$ by $40\%$ of $250$. So, the increase is given by:

$$
0.40 \times 250 = 100
$$

Now, we add the increase to the original value:

$$
p = 250 + 100 = 350
$$

We are told that $p = 250k$. So, we solve for $k{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
250k &= 350 \\
k &= \frac{350}{250} \\
&= \frac{7}{5} \\
&= 1.4
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the value of $k$ is $1.4$.

## Converting a Percentage Increase to a Decimal: Word Problems

**Example:** A company’s revenue increased by $52\%$ from $2021$ to $2022$.
If the $2022$ revenue is $k$ times the $2021$ revenue, what is the value of $k$?

**Explanation**

If a quantity increases by $52\%$, then it corresponds to

$$
100\% + 52\% = 152\%
$$

of the original value.

We convert this to a decimal:

$$
152\% = \frac{152}{100} = 1.52
$$

Therefore, $k = 1.52$.

## Converting a Decimal to a Percentage Increase

**Example:** The value of $x$ is $1.18$ times $700$. The value of $x$ is what percent greater than $700$?

**Explanation**

We are told that $x$ is $1.18$ times $700$, so we compute the product:

$$
x = 1.18 \times 700 = 826
$$

To find how much greater $x$ is than $700$, we subtract:

$$
826 - 700 = 126
$$

Now, we compute the percent increase by dividing the increase by the original value:

$$
\dfrac{126}{700} = 0.18
$$

Finally, we convert to a percentage:

$$
0.18 \times 100\% = 18\%
$$

Therefore, the value of $x$ is $18\%$ greater than $700$.

## Converting a Decimal to a Percentage Decrease

**Example:** The expression $0.60c$ represents the result of decreasing a positive quantity $c$ by what percent?

**Explanation**

The expression $0.60c$ tells us that the final amount is $60\%$ of the original amount $c$.

To find how much was removed, we subtract the retained portion from the original:

$$
1 - 0.60 = 0.40
$$

So, the quantity $c$ was decreased by $0.40c$, which means it was decreased by $40\%$.
