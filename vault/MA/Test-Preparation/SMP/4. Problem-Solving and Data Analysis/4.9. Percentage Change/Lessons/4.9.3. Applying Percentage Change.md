Questions about applying a percentage change test whether we can correctly use percentage increases or decreases in real-world and algebraic settings.

In this lesson, we’ll practice applying percentage change to calculate new values, model practical scenarios, and strengthen our fluency with common exam-style percent word problems.

## Applying a Percentage Increase to a Number

**Example:** What number is $40\%$ greater than $360$?

**Explanation**

A number $40\%$ greater means applying a new percentage of

$$
100\% + 40\% = 140\%
$$

We begin by converting the percentage to a decimal:

$$
140\% = \dfrac{140}{100} = 1.40
$$

So, to find a number that is $40\%$ greater than $360$, we multiply $360$ by $1.40{:}$

$$
1.40 \cdot 360 = 504
$$

Therefore, the number that is $40\%$ greater than $360$ is $504$.

## Applying a Percentage Increase: Word Problems

**Example:** A t-shirt costs $\[math]18$ before tax. If a $9\%$ sales tax is applied, what is the total cost after tax?

**Explanation**

To find the total cost after applying the tax, we increase the price by $9\%$, which means multiplying by:

$$
100\% + 9\% = 109\% = 1.09
$$

Now, we compute

$$
18 \times 1.09 = 19.62
$$

So, the total cost after tax is $\[math]19.62$.

## Applying a Discount

**Example:** A discount of $14\%$ is offered on all hiking boots. What is the sale price of a pair of boots originally priced at $\[math]78$?

**Explanation**

To find the sale price, we calculate the discount and subtract it from the original price.

First, we calculate $14\%$ of $\[math]78{:}$

$$
0.14 \times 78 = 10.92
$$

Next, we subtract this amount from the original price to obtain

$$
78 - 10.92 = 67.08
$$

Therefore, the sale price of the boots is $\[/math]67.08$.

## Predicting a Value Given the Relationship Between Two Percentage Increases

**Example:** A hospital tracked the number of patient visits in two consecutive years, as shown below.

| Year | Patient visits |
| --- | ---: |
| $2020$ | $30,\!000$ |
| $2021$ | $31,\!500$ |

The hospital administrator estimated that the percentage increase from $2021$ to $2022$ would be four times the percentage increase from $2020$ to $2021$. How many visits did the administrator expect in $2022$?

**Explanation**

To find a percentage increase, we use the formula

$$
\text{Percentage Increase} = \dfrac{\text{New Value} - \text{Original Value}}{\text{Original Value}} \times 100\%
$$

From $2020$ to $2021$, the percentage increase is

$$
\begin{aligned}
Percentage Increase &= \frac{31, 500 - 30, 000}{30, 000} \cdot 100% \\
&= \frac{1, 500}{30, 000} \cdot 100% \\
&= 0.05 \cdot 100% \\
&= 5%
\end{aligned}
$$

Since the percentage increase from $2021$ to $2022$ would be four times $5\%$, the percentage increase is

$$
4 \times 5 =20\%
$$

So, in $2021$, the original value is $31,\!500$ and the percentage increase is $20\%$. We use it now to find the new value corresponding to the expected number of visits in $2022{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
20% &= \frac{New Value - 31, 500}{31, 500} \cdot 100% \\
0.20 &= \frac{New Value - 31, 500}{31, 500} \\
6, 300 &= New Value - 31, 500 \\
New Value &= 37, 800
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the administrator expected $37,\!800$ visits in $2022$.
