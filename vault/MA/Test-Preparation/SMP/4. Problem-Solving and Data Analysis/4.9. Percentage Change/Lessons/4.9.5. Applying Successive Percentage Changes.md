## Introduction

Questions about applying the successive percentage changes test our ability to handle multiple percent increases or decreases in sequence. These problems often appear in real-world scenarios.

In this topic, we’ll practice combining percentage changes.

## Applying a Compound Percentage Decrease

**Example:** Lena bought a drawing tablet for $\[math]480$. She estimates that the value of the tablet drops by $25\%$ each month. What is the estimated value of the tablet exactly $2$ months after she bought it?

**Explanation**

We're told that the tablet's value decreases by $25\%$ each month. This means that its value in each month is

$$
100\% - 25\% = 75\%
$$

of the value of the previous month.

We are also told that at the time of purchase, the tablet was worth $\[math]480$. So,

- the tablet's value after the first month is $(0.75)(\[/math]480) = \[math]360$, and
- the tablet's value after the second month is $(0.75)(\[/math]360) = \[math]270$.

Therefore, the estimated value of the tablet after $2$ months is $\[/math]270$.

## Applying Multiple Percentage Changes

**Example:** The number $p$ is $180\%$ greater than the number $q$. The number $q$ is $60\%$ less than $50$. What is the value of $p$?

**Explanation**

We are told that $q$ is $60\%$ less than $50$. Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
q &= 50 - 50 \cdot 60% \\
&= 50 - 50 \cdot \frac{60}{100} \\
&= 50 - 30 \\
&= 20
\end{aligned}
$$

We are also told that $p$ is $180\%$ greater than $q$. This means

$$
\begin{aligned}
p &= q + q \cdot 180% \\
&= q + q \cdot \frac{180}{100} \\
&= q + 1.8q \\
&= (1 + 1.8)q \\
&= 2.8q
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, substituting $q = 20$ into $p = 2.8q$, we get

$$
p = 2.8(20) = 56
$$

Therefore, the value of $p$ is $56$.
