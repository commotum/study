<a id="applying-two-successive-discounts-or-markups"></a>
## Applying Two Successive Discounts or Markups

**Example:** A bike shop buys helmets at a wholesale cost of $\[math]20.00$ each and sells them at a retail price that is $180\%$ of the wholesale cost. During clearance, each remaining helmet is sold at a $40\%$ discount from the retail price. What is the discounted price of a helmet?

**Explanation**

First, we are told that the retail price is $180\%$ of the wholesale cost. Here, the wholesale cost is $\[math]20.00$. So, to calculate the retail price, we multiply $20$ by $180\%{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
180% \cdot 20 &= \frac{180}{100} \cdot 20 \\
&= 1.8 \cdot 20 \\
&= 36
\end{aligned}
$$

Hence, the retail price of a helmet is $\[/math]36.00$.

Now, we're also told that there is a $40\%$ discount on the retail price. So, to calculate the amount of the discount, we need to find $40\%$ of $\[math]36.00$.

To do this, we multiply $36$ by $40\%{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
40% \cdot 36 &= \frac{40}{100} \cdot 36 \\
&= 0.40 \cdot 36 \\
&= 14.4
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the amount of the discount is $\[/math]14.40$. We subtract this amount from the retail price:

$$
36 - 14.4 = 21.6
$$

Therefore, the discounted price of a helmet is $\[math]21.60$.

## Applying Two Successive Discounts or Markups When One Percentage Is Reversed

**Example:** A pair of gloves has a regular price of $\[math]35$. During a weekend sale, the gloves are sold at a price that is $40\%$ less than the regular price. This sale price is also $40\%$ more than what the retailer paid per pair. What was the retailer’s cost for each pair of gloves?

**Explanation**

There is a $40\%$ discount on the regular price. Here, the regular price is $\[math]35$. So, to calculate the amount of the discount, we need to find $40\%$ of $\[/math]35$.

To do this, we multiply $35$ by $40\%{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
40% \cdot 35 &= \frac{40}{100} \cdot 35 \\
&= 0.40 \cdot 35 \\
&= 14
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the amount of the discount is $\[math]14$. We subtract this amount from the regular price:

$$
35 - 14 = 21
$$

Hence, the sale price of a pair of gloves is $\[/math]21$.

Now, we're also told that the sale price is $40\%$ more than the retailer’s cost per pair. So, the sale price is

$$
100\% + 40\% = 140\%
$$

of the retailer’s cost.

We found that the sale price is $\[math]21$. So, if $p$ was the retailer’s cost for each pair, then we have the following equation, which we can solve for $p{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
140% \cdot p &= 21 \\
\frac{140}{100} \cdot p &= 21 \\
1.4p &= 21 \\
p &= \frac{21}{1.4} \\
&= 15
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the retailer’s cost per pair of gloves was $\[/math]15$.

## Finding an Initial Value Given Two Successive Discounts or Markups

**Example:** A craft store sells cutting mats at a retail price that is $180\%$ of the wholesale cost. During a seasonal sale, each mat is discounted by $25\%$ from the retail price. If the final selling price of a mat is $\[math]33.75$, what is the wholesale cost per mat?

**Explanation**

Let $p$ be the wholesale cost per mat.

First, we are told that the retail price is $180\%$ of the wholesale cost. So, to calculate the retail price, we multiply $p$ by $180\%{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
180% \cdot p &= \frac{180}{100} \cdot p \\
&= 1.8p
\end{aligned}
$$

Hence, the retail price is $1.8p$ dollars.

Now, we're also told that the mat is discounted by $25\%$ from the retail price. The amount of the discount is:

$$
\begin{aligned}
25% \cdot (1.8p) &= \frac{25}{100} \cdot 1.8p \\
&= 0.45p
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the final selling price is the retail price minus the discount:

$$
1.8p - 0.45p = 1.35p
$$

We are told this equals $\[math]33.75$, so we solve for $p{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
1.35p &= 33.75 \\
p &= \frac{33.75}{1.35} \\
&= 25
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the wholesale cost per mat is $\[/math]25.00$.

## Finding an Initial Value Given Two Successive Discounts and One Percentage Is Reversed

**Example:** A luggage store lists a travel backpack with a set regular price. During a holiday promotion, the backpack is sold at a price that is $20\%$ less than the regular price. The discounted price is $140\%$ of the final selling price. If the final selling price is $\[math]32.00$, what is the regular price?

**Explanation**

Let $p$ be the regular price.

There is a $20\%$ discount on the regular price. Subtract this from the regular price:

$$
p - 0.20p = 0.80p
$$

Hence, the discounted price is $0.80p$ dollars.

We are also told that the discounted price is $140\%$ of the final selling price. Here, the final selling price is $\[math]32.00$. So,

$$
\begin{aligned}
140% \cdot 32 &= \frac{140}{100} \cdot 32 \\
&= 1.4 \cdot 32 \\
&= 44.8
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus, the discounted price is $\[/math]44.80$. Since the discounted price also equals $0.80p$, we solve for $p{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
0.80p &= 44.8 \\
p &= \frac{44.8}{0.80} \\
&= 56
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the regular price is $\[math]\bbox[4pt,border: 1px solid gainsboro]{56.00}$.
