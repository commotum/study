## Expressing Multiple Percentage Changes as Decimals

**Example:** The number $k$ is $130\%$ greater than the positive number $l$. The number $m$ is $45\%$ less than $k$. The number $m$ is how many times $l$?

**Explanation**

Since the number $k$ is $130\%$ greater than the positive number $l$, we have

$$
k = \left(1 + \dfrac{130}{100}\right) \cdot l
$$

$$
k = (1 + 1.3) \cdot l
$$

$k = 2.3l$.

Because the number $m$ is $45\%$ less than $k$, we have

$$
m = \left(1 - \dfrac{45}{100}\right) \cdot k
$$

$$
m = (1 - 0.45) \cdot k
$$

$m = 0.55k$.

Substituting $2.3l$ for $k$ in $m = 0.55k$, we obtain

$$
m = 0.55(2.3l)
$$

$$
m = (0.55 \cdot 2.3)l
$$

$m = 1.265l$.

Therefore, the number $m$ is ${1.265}$ times the number $l$.

## Expressing Multiple Percentage Increases as Decimals: Word Problems

**Example:** The size of a company’s data archive increased by $17\%$ from quarter $1$ to quarter $2$, and then by $8\%$ from quarter $2$ to quarter $3$. If the archive was $z$ times larger in quarter $3$ than in quarter $1$, what is the value of $z$?

**Explanation**

Let $D_1$, $D_2$, and $D_3$ be the archive size in quarters $1$, $2$, and $3$, respectively.

We are told that, in quarter $2$, the archive grew by $17\%$ over quarter $1$. Therefore,

$$
D_2 = \left(1 + \dfrac{17}{100}\right)D_1 = 1.17D_1
$$

We are also told that, in quarter $3$, the archive grew by $8\%$ over quarter $2$. This means

$$
D_3 = \left(1 + \dfrac{8}{100}\right) D_2 = 1.08D_2 = (1.08)(1.17D_1) = 1.2636D_1
$$

Therefore, the archive was $1.2636$ times larger in quarter $3$ than in quarter $1$.

Since it was $z$ times as large, we conclude that $z = 1.2636$.

## Finding a Net Percentage Change

**Example:** Online course enrollments increased by $70\%$ from June to July, but then dropped by $30\%$ from July to August. What was the net percentage increase in enrollments from June to August?

**Explanation**

Let $A$ be the number of enrollments in June.

We are told that enrollments increased by $70\%$ from June to July. Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
Enrollments in July &= (1 + 0.70)A \\
&= 1.70A
\end{aligned}
$$

We're also told that enrollments dropped by $30\%$ from July to August. This means

$$
\begin{aligned}
Enrollments in August &= (1 - 0.30)(1.70A) \\
&= 0.70(1.70A) \\
&= 1.19A
\end{aligned}
$$

The number of enrollments in August was $1.19$ times the number of enrollments in June. This is equivalent to $119\%$ of the original number of enrollments, which represents a net increase of $19\%$ from the original $100\%$.

Therefore, the net percentage increase in enrollments from June to August was $19\%$.
