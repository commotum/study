## Introduction

Problems about finding original values from the percentage change test our ability to work backward from a percentage increase or decrease. These problems often appear in real-world contexts.

In this topic, we’ll practice reversing percent increases and decreases to find original quantities.

---

<a id="finding-an-original-value-given-a-percentage-increase"></a>
## Finding an Original Value Given a Percentage Increase

**Example:** After a $35\%$ increase, a conference registration fee is $\[math]405$. What was the original fee?

**Explanation**

A $35\%$ increase means the final fee is $135\%$ of the original fee.

We convert this percentage to a decimal:

$$
135\%=\frac{135}{100}=1.35
$$

Let the original fee be $x$. So, after a $35\%$ increase, the new value is

$$
1.35x
$$

We are told the increased value is $\[math]405$. So, we set them equal and solve for $x{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
1.35x &= 405 \\
x &= \frac{405}{1.35} \\
x &= 300
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the original fee was $\[/math]300$.

## Finding an Original Value Given a Percentage Increase Between Dates

**Example:** An IT team reported resolved support tickets. By the end of the day, the total was $1{,}050$ tickets. The percent increase in resolved tickets from the morning update to the end of the day was $50\%$. How many tickets were reported in the morning update?

**Explanation**

Let $x$ represent the number of resolved tickets in the morning update.

A $50\%$ increase means that by the end of the day, the total became

$$
x + \dfrac{50}{100} \cdot x
$$

That gives us the equation:

$$
x + \dfrac{50}{100} \cdot x = 1{,}050
$$

Now, we solve for $x{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
x + \frac{50}{100} \cdot x &= 1,050 \\
x + 0.50 \cdot x &= 1,050 \\
1.50x &= 1,050 \\
x &= \frac{1,050}{1.50} \\
x &= 700
\end{aligned}
$$

So, there were $700$ tickets reported in the morning update.

## Finding the Original Value Given a Percentage Increase Over 100%

**Example:** After increasing a quantity $x$ by $1{,}900\%$, the result is $740$. What is the value of $x$?

**Explanation**

We are told that increasing a quantity $x$ by $1,900\%$ results in $740$. Let's find the value of $x$.

A $1{,}900\%$ increase means the final amount is

$$
100\% + 1{,}900\% = 2{,}000\%
$$

of the original.

We convert $2{,}000\%$ to a decimal:

$$
2{,}000\% = \dfrac{2{,}000}{100} = 20
$$

So, we have the equation:
$20x = 740$

Now, we solve for $x{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
x &= \frac{740}{20} \\
&= 37
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the value of $x$ is $37$.

## Finding an Original Value Given a Percentage Decrease

**Example:** A collector sold part of a marble collection. After $45\%$ of the original marbles were sold, $484$ marbles remained. How many marbles were sold?

**Explanation**

Let $x$ represent the original number of marbles.

If $45\%$ of them were removed, then $55\%$ of them remained. So we have the equation:

$$
\dfrac{55}{100} \cdot x = 484
$$

Now, we solve for $x{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{55}{100} \cdot x &= 484 \\
0.55x &= 484 \\
x &= \frac{484}{0.55} \\
x &= 880
\end{aligned}
$$

Now that we know the original number of marbles was $880$, we find the number removed by taking $45\%$ of $880{:}$

$$
\dfrac{45}{100} \cdot 880 = 396
$$

So, $396$ marbles were sold.
