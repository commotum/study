## Calculating a Probability From a Two-Way Frequency Table

**Example:** In a weekend, a sports shop had a total of $200$ customers. The table above summarizes whether or not customers bought a jersey, shoes, both, or neither. Based on the data in the table, what is the probability that a randomly selected customer did not purchase a jersey?

|  | Shoes purchased | Shoes not purchased | Total |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Jersey purchased | $80$ | $40$ | $120$ |
| Jersey not purchased | $30$ | $50$ | $80$ |
| **Total** | **$110$** | **$90$** | **$200$** |

**Explanation**

To determine the desired probability, we must find the ratio of the number of customers who did not purchase a jersey to the number of people who visited the sports shop on Sunday.

We are told that the shop had $200$ customers on that particular Sunday. Of this amount, ${\color{blue}80}$ did not purchase a jersey.

|  | Shoes purchased | Shoes not purchased | Total |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Jersey purchased | $80$ | $40$ | $120$ |
| Jersey not purchased | $30$ | $50$ | $\color{blue}80$ |
| **Total** | **$110$** | **$90$** | **$200$** |

Therefore, the desired probability is given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(Jersey not purchased) &= \frac{80}{200} \\
&= \frac{2 \cdot 40}{5 \cdot 40} \\
&= \frac{2 \cdot 40}{5 \cdot 40} \\
&= \frac{2}{5}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Calculating a Probability From a Larger Two-Way Frequency Table

**Example:** The table below shows the distribution of different types of donations collected in three neighborhoods for $100$ donations.

| Donation | North | South | East | Total |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Food | $8$ | $10$ | $12$ | $30$ |
| Clothes | $9$ | $11$ | $10$ | $30$ |
| Books | $7$ | $8$ | $9$ | $24$ |
| Toys | $6$ | $5$ | $5$ | $16$ |
| **Total** | **$30$** | **$34$** | **$36$** | **$100$** |

If one donation is selected at random, what is the probability that it was a Books donation?

**Explanation**

To determine the desired probability, we must find the ratio of the number of Books donations to the total number of donations.

We are told that there were $100$ total donations, of which ${\color{blue}24}$ were Books.

| Donation | North | South | East | Total |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Food | $8$ | $10$ | $12$ | $30$ |
| Clothes | $9$ | $11$ | $10$ | $30$ |
| Books | $7$ | $8$ | $9$ | $\color{blue}24$ |
| Toys | $6$ | $5$ | $5$ | $16$ |
| **Total** | **$30$** | **$34$** | **$36$** | **$100$** |

Therefore, the desired probability is given by

$$
P(\text{Books}) = \dfrac{\color{blue}24}{100} = \dfrac{6}{25}
$$

## Calculating the Probability of Having Two Characteristics

**Example:** An art supply shop lists $20$ items. The table below summarizes the items by type and whether they are priced at or below $\[math]12$.

| Item | Priced at no more than $\[/math]12$ | Priced greater than $\[math]12$ | Total |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Markers | $5$ | $3$ | $8$ |
| Paints | $8$ | $4$ | $12$ |
| **Total** | **$13$** | **$7$** | **$20$** |

If one of the items is selected at random, what is the probability that the selected item is Paints and is priced greater than $\[/math]12$?

**Explanation**

To determine the desired probability, we must find the ratio of the number of items that are Paints and are priced greater than $\[math]12$ to the total number of items available.

We are told that a total of $20$ items are available. Of this amount, ${\color{blue}4}$ are Paints priced greater than $\[/math]12$.

| Item | Priced at no more than $\[math]12$ | Priced greater than $\[/math]12$ | Total |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Markers | $5$ | $3$ | $8$ |
| Paints | $8$<e/td> | $\color{blue}4$ | $12$ |
| **Total** | **$13$** | **$7$** | **$20$** |

Therefore, the desired probability is given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(Paints and \ge \[math]12) &= \frac{4}{20} \\
&= \frac{1 \cdot 4}{5 \cdot 4} \\
&= \frac{1 \cdot 4}{5 \cdot 4} \\
&= \frac{1}{5}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Calculating the Probability of Having Two Characteristics (Totals Not Given)

**Example:** The table below summarizes the number of restaurants that offer outdoor seating and provide free Wi-Fi.

|  | Provides free Wi-Fi | Does not provide free Wi-Fi |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Has outdoor seating | $16$ | $4$ |
| Does not have outdoor seating | $11$ | $9$ |

If one of these restaurants is selected at random, what is the probability that it **has outdoor seating but does not provide free Wi-Fi**?

**Explanation**

To determine the desired probability, we must find the ratio of the number of restaurants that have outdoor seating but do not provide free Wi-Fi to the total number of restaurants.

We can see from the table that there are a total of

$$
16 + 4 + 11 + 9 = {\color{red}40}
$$

restaurants. Of this amount, ${\color{blue}4}$ have outdoor seating but do not provide free Wi-Fi.

|  | Provides free Wi-Fi | Does not provide free Wi-Fi |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Has outdoor seating | $16$ | $\color{blue}4$ |
| Does not have outdoor seating | $11$ | $9$ |

Therefore, the desired probability is

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(Outdoor seating but no Wi-Fi) &= \frac{4}{40} \\
&= \frac{1 \cdot 4}{10 \cdot 4} \\
&= \frac{1 \cdot 4}{10 \cdot 4} \\
&= \frac{1}{10}
\end{aligned}
$$
