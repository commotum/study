## Calculating Conditional Probabilities

**Example:** A veterinary clinic tracked incoming calls on a single day, recording whether callers inquired about appointments or pet food. The results are shown in the table below.

|  | Pet food request | No pet food request | Total |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Appointment request | $55$ | $345$ | $400$ |
| No Appointment request | $45$ | $55$ | $100$ |
| **Total** | **$100$** | **$400$** | **$500$** |

If a caller who asked about pet food is selected at random, what is the probability that the caller also asked about an appointment?

**Explanation**

To determine the desired probability, we must find the ratio of the number of callers who asked about pet food and an appointment to the total number of callers who asked about pet food. This is given by

$$
\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\! P(\text{Appointment} \mid \text{Pet food}) = \frac{\text{Number of requests for appointment and pet food}}{\text{Total number of requests for pet food}}
$$

From the table, we can see that there are a total of ${\color{red}100}$ callers who asked about pet food. Of this amount, ${\color{blue}55}$ also asked about an appointment.

|  | Pet food request | No pet food request | Total |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Appointment request | $\color{blue}55$ | $345$ | $400$ |
| No Appointment request | $45$ | $55$ | $100$ |
| **Total** | ${\color{red}100}$ | **$400$** | **$500$** |

Therefore, the desired probability is

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(Appointment∣Pet food) &= \frac{55}{100} \\
&= 0.55
\end{aligned}
$$

## Calculating Conditional Probabilities (Totals Not Given)

**Example:** The table below shows the number of teachers in the English and History departments, categorized by whether they teach honors classes.

|  | English | History |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Teach honors | $8$ | $5$ |
| Do not teach honors | $4$ | $7$ |

One teacher from the English or History department is selected at random. If the teacher is from the English department, what is the probability that they do not teach honors classes?

**Explanation**

To determine the desired probability, we must find the ratio of the number of English teachers who do not teach honors to the total number of English teachers. This is given by

$$
P(\text{Not honors} \mid \text{English})=\frac{\text{Number of English not teaching honors}}{\text{Total number of English}}
$$

From the table, we can see that a total of

$$
8+4={\color{red}12}
$$

English teachers in the department. Of this amount, ${\color{blue}4}$ do not teach honors.

|  | English | History |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Teach honors | $8$ | $5$ |
| Do not teach honors | $\color{blue}4$ | $7$ |

Therefore, the desired probability is

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(Not honors∣English) &= \frac{4}{12} \\
&= \frac{1 \cdot 4}{3 \cdot 4} \\
&= \frac{1 \cdot 4}{3 \cdot 4} \\
&= \frac{1}{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Calculating Compound Conditional Probabilities

**Example:** A group of $30$ students, during each of $3$ different practice exams, answered $5$ questions. Each correct response was worth $1$ point. The table shows how many students earned each possible score on each exam.

| Number of Students by Score and Exam | $5$ out of $5$ | $4$ out of $5$ | $3$ out of $5$ | $2$ out of $5$ | $1$ out of $5$ | $0$ out of $5$ | Total |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Exam $1$ | $6$ | $3$ | $5$ | $4$ | $7$ | $5$ | $30$ |
| Exam $2$ | $2$ | $6$ | $4$ | $7$ | $5$ | $6$ | $30$ |
| Exam $3$ | $4$ | $5$ | $6$ | $3$ | $6$ | $6$ | $30$ |
| **Total** | **$12$** | **$14$** | **$15$** | **$14$** | **$18$** | **$17$** | **$90$** |

No student received the same score on two different exams. If a student is chosen at random, what is the probability that the student earned a perfect score on Exam $1$ or Exam $3$, given that the student earned a perfect score on one of the three exams?

**Explanation**

To determine the desired probability, we must find the ratio of the number of students who received a score of $5$ on Exam $1$ or Exam $3$ to the total number of students who received a score of $5$ on one of the three exams. This is given by
$1$

From the table, we can see that a total of ${\color{red}12}$ students received a score of $5$. Of this amount,

$$
{\color{blue}6} + {\color{purple}4} = 10
$$

students received that score in either Exam $1$ or Exam $3$.

| Number of Students by Score and Exam | $5$ out of $5$ | $4$ out of $5$ | $3$ out of $5$ | $2$ out of $5$ | $1$ out of $5$ | $0$ out of $5$ | Total |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Exam $1$ | $\color{blue}6$ | $3$ | $5$ | $4$ | $7$ | $5$ | $30$ |
| Exam $2$ | $2$ | $6$ | $4$ | $7$ | $5$ | $6$ | $30$ |
| Exam $3$ | $\color{purple}4$ | $5$ | $6$ | $3$ | $6$ | $6$ | $30$ |
| **Total** | ${\color{red}12}$ | **$14$** | **$15$** | **$14$** | **$18$** | **$17$** | **$90$** |

Therefore, the desired probability is

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(Exam1or3∣Perfect Score) &= \frac{6 + 4}{12} \\
&= \frac{10}{12} \\
&= \frac{5}{6}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding an Unknown Frequency Given a Conditional Probability

**Example:** An electronics shop records whether a buyer’s main purchase was a laptop or a tablet, and how the order was placed. The data is summarized in the table below.

|  | In-store | Online | Phone |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Laptops | $35$ | $20$ | $12$ |
| Tablets | $21$ | $14$ | $x$ |

If a person who bought a tablet is selected at random, the probability that the person ordered by online is $\dfrac{1}{6}$. What is the value of $x$?

**Explanation**

To find the value of $x$, we use the given conditional probability: the probability that a person ordered by online, given they bought a tablet, is

$$
\dfrac{1}{6}
$$

The conditional probability is calculated as the ratio of the number of people who ordered by phone and bought a tablet to the total number of people who bought a tablet. This is given by

$$
P(\text{Online} \mid \text{Tablet}) = \frac{\text{Number of tablet buyers who ordered by online}}{\text{Total number who bought a tablet}}
$$

From the table, we can see that there are

$$
21 + 14 + x = {\color{red}35 + x}
$$

people who bought a tablet. Of these, ${\color{blue}14}$ buyers ordered online.

|  | In-store | Online | Phone |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Laptops | $35$ | $20$ | $12$ |
| Tablets | $21$ | $\color{blue}14$ | $x$ |

Therefore, the conditional probability is

$$
P(\text{Online} \mid \text{Tablet}) = \dfrac{\color{blue}14}{\color{red}35 + x}
$$

Since this must be equal to

$$
\dfrac{1}{6}
$$

we can solve for $x$ as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{14}{35 + x} &= \frac{1}{6} \\
\frac{35 + x}{14} &= \frac{6}{1} \\
35 + x &= 84 \\
x &= 49
\end{aligned}
$$
