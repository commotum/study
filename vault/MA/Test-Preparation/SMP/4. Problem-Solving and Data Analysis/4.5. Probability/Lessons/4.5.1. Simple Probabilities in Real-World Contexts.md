## Determining Probabilities for Events With Equally Likely Outcomes

**Example:** A jar contains $25$ balls numbered from $1$ to $25$. If one ball is randomly chosen from the jar, what is the probability that it is numbered $18$?

**Explanation**

The probability of an event is the number of favorable outcomes divided by the number of total possible outcomes.

We are told that the jar contains $25$ balls numbered from $1$ to $25$. So, there is only one ball numbered $18$.

Therefore, since the number of favorable outcomes (balls numbered $18$) is $1$, and the total number of possible outcomes (balls in the jar) is $25$, the desired probability is

$$
P(\textrm{It is numbered 18}) = \dfrac{1}{25}
$$

## Determining Part-to-Whole Probabilities

**Example:** A toy factory produces a batch of $3,600$ toy cars, of which $9$ are defective. If one toy car is selected at random for inspection, what is the probability that it is defective?

**Explanation**

The probability of an event is the number of favorable outcomes divided by the total number of possible outcomes.

We are told that a batch of toy cars has a total of $3,600$ units, of which $9$ are defective.

Therefore, since the number of favorable outcomes (defective toy cars) is $9$, and the total number of possible outcomes (all toy cars) is $3,600$, the desired probability is

$$
P(\textrm{The selected toy car is defective}) = \dfrac{9}{3,600} = 0.0025
$$

## Determining Part-to-Whole Probabilities in Terms of an Unknown

**Example:** A box contains $y$ red tiles and $18$ green tiles. If one tile is randomly selected from the box, what is the probability that it is red, in terms of $y$?

**Explanation**

The probability of an event is the number of favorable outcomes divided by the total number of possible outcomes.

We are told that the box contains $y$ red tiles and $18$ green tiles. So, the total number of tiles is the sum of the red and green tiles, which is $y+18$.

Therefore, since the number of favorable outcomes (red tiles) is $y$, and the total number of possible outcomes (all tiles) is $y+18$, the desired probability is

$$
P(\textrm{The selected tile is red}) = \dfrac{y}{y+18}
$$

## Solving a Count Given a Probability

**Example:** On January $1$, $2024$, there were $2.8$ million museum members across major London institutions. These institutions included the British Museum, Tate, the Victoria and Albert Museum, the Natural History Museum, and the Science Museum. By June $30$, $2024$, the number of museum members had increased to $3.1$ million. If a museum member in London on June $30$, $2024$, is selected at random, the probability that the person selected was a member of Tate is $0.23$. There were $p$ million Tate members in London on June $30$, $2024$. To the nearest integer, what is the value of $p$?

**Explanation**

The number of Tate members is equal to the product of the probability that a randomly selected person is a Tate member and the total population size.

On June $30$, $2024$, there were $3.1$ million museum members, and the probability that a randomly selected member belonged to Tate is $0.23$.
So, the number of Tate members is

$$
0.23\cdot 3.1 = 0.713 \approx 1\,\text{million}
$$

rounded to the nearest integer. Therefore, $p=1$.
