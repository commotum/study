## Calculating a Conditional Probability

**Example:** A bird sanctuary contains $8$ forest paths and $3$ riverside paths. Each forest path has $9$ sightings of owls and $6$ sightings of woodpeckers. Each riverside path has $5$ sightings of owls and $7$ sightings of woodpeckers. A sighting from one of these paths is selected at random. What is the probability that the sighting occurred on a riverside path, given that the animal spotted was an owl?

**Explanation**

To determine the desired probability, we must find the ratio of the number of sightings that occurred on a riverside path and were of an owl to the total number of sightings of an owl.
This is given by

$$
\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\! P(\text{Riverside path} \mid \text{Owl spotted}) = \frac{\text{Number of owl sightings on riverside paths}}{\text{Total number of owl sightings}}
$$

We begin by calculating the total number of owl sightings.

- Each of the $8$ forest paths had $9$ owl sightings. So, the number of owl sightings on the forest paths is $8 \times 9 = 72$.
- Each of the $3$ riverside paths had $5$ owl sightings. Thus, the number of owl sightings on the riverside paths is $3 \times 5 = 15$.

Therefore, the total number of owl sightings is

$$
72 + 15 = {\color{red}87}
$$

Of these, ${\color{blue}15}$ occurred on riverside paths.

Therefore, the desired probability is

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(Riverside path∣Owl spotted) &= \frac{15}{87} \\
&= \frac{5 \cdot 3}{29 \cdot 3} \\
&= \frac{5 \cdot 3}{29 \cdot 3} \\
&= \frac{5}{29}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Calculating a Conditional Probability Given a Two-Way Percentage Table

**Example:** A museum surveyed visitors about four exhibits, asking each to choose the one they enjoyed most. Each response was submitted either at an on-site kiosk or via an email link. The table below shows the distribution of preferences, in percent, by response method. The total number of email responses was four times the number of kiosk responses.

|  | Kiosk | Email |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Sculpture | $18\%$ | $22\%$ |
| Painting | $37\%$ | $28\%$ |
| Science | $22\%$ | $30\%$ |
| History | $23\%$ | $20\%$ |
| **Total** | **$100\%$** | **$100\%$** |

If a visitor who preferred the sculpture exhibit is selected at random, what is the probability that the visitor responded via email?

**Explanation**

To determine the desired probability, we must find the ratio of the number of visitors who preferred the sculpture exhibit and responded via email to the total number of visitors who preferred the sculpture exhibit. This is given by

$$
P(\text{Email} \mid \text{Sculpture}) = \frac{\text{Number of sculpture preferences via email}}{\text{Total number preferring sculpture}}
$$

Let $x$ be the number of kiosk responses. Since the number of email responses was four times the number of kiosk responses, the number of email responses is $4x$.

We begin by calculating the total number of visitors who preferred the sculpture exhibit.

- From the table, we can see that $18\%$ of kiosk respondents preferred the sculpture exhibit. This can be represented by
$0.18 \cdot x = 0.18x$.
- From the table, we can see that $22\%$ of email respondents preferred the sculpture exhibit. This can be represented by
$0.22 \cdot 4x = 0.88x$.

Therefore, the total number of visitors who preferred the sculpture exhibit is

$$
0.18x + 0.88x = {\color{red}1.06x}
$$

Of these, ${\color{blue}0.88x}$ is the number of email respondents who preferred the sculpture exhibit.

Therefore, the desired probability is

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(Email∣Sculpture) &= \frac{0.88x}{1.06x} \\
&= \frac{88x}{106x} \\
&= \frac{2 \cdot 44x}{2 \cdot 53x} \\
&= \frac{2 \cdot 44x}{2 \cdot 53x} \\
&= 44 53
\end{aligned}
$$
