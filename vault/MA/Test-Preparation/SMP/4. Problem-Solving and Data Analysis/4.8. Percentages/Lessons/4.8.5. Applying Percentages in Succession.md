## Introduction

Applying percentages in succession tests our ability to correctly combine multiple percent changes or relationships. These problems often appear in word problems or algebraic reasoning tasks.

In this topic, we’ll practice chaining percentages together and analyzing how multiple percent relationships affect each other in sequence.

## Expressing Parts of Parts as Percentages

**Example:** According to a recent report, $40\%$ of viewers said they enjoyed documentaries. Of those, $50\%$ preferred documentaries about nature. What percentage of all viewers preferred nature documentaries?

**Explanation**

Let $x$ be the total number of viewers.

We're told that $40\%$ of viewers enjoyed documentaries. Thus, the number of viewers who enjoy documentaries is $0.4x$.

We are also told that of those viewers who enjoy documentaries, $50\%$ preferred nature topics. So, the number of viewers who preferred nature documentaries is

$$
0.50(0.4x) = 0.20x
$$

The expression $0.20x$ represents $20\%$ of $x$. Therefore, $20\%$ of all viewers preferred nature documentaries.

## Comparing Two Unknowns Given Their Relationship to a Third

**Example:** A number $k$ is $180\%$ of the number $\ell$, and $k$ is $60\%$ of the number $m$. If $m$ is $q\%$ of $\ell$, what is the value of $q$?

**Explanation**

We are told that $k$ is $180\%$ of $\ell$. This can be expressed as

$$
k = \dfrac{180}{100} \cdot \ell
$$

We are also told that $k$ is $60\%$ of $m$. This can be expressed as

$$
k = \dfrac{60}{100} \cdot m
$$

Equating the two expressions for $k$, and then solving for $m$, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{60}{100} \cdot m &= \frac{180}{100} \cdot ℓ \\
60m &= 180ℓ \\
m &= \frac{180}{60} \cdot ℓ \\
m &= 3ℓ \\
m &= \frac{300}{100} \cdot ℓ
\end{aligned}
$$

The last equality tells us that $m$ is ${\color{blue}300} \%$ of $\ell$. Since $m$ is ${\color{blue}q} \%$ of $\ell$, we conclude that

$$
{\color{blue}q} = {\color{blue}300}
$$

## Further Comparing Two Unknowns Given Their Relationship to a Third

**Example:** The number $p$ is $2,\! 250\%$ of the sum of two positive numbers $r$ and $s$, and $r$ is $50\%$ of $s$. What percent of $r$ is $p$?

**Explanation**

We are told that $p$ is $2,\! 250\%$ of the sum $r + s$. This can be represented by the equation

$$
p = \dfrac{2,\! 250}{100}(r + s)
$$

or equivalently,

$$
p = 22.5(r + s)
$$

We are also told that $r$ is $50\%$ of $s$. This can be represented by the equation

$$
r = \dfrac{50}{100} \cdot s
$$

or equivalently,

$$
s = \dfrac{r}{0.5}
$$

Substituting

$$
\dfrac{r}{0.5}
$$

for $s$ in the equation

$$
p = 22.5(r + s)
$$

we get

$$
p = 22.5\left(r + \dfrac{r}{0.5}\right)
$$

$$
p = 22.5\left(r + 2r\right)
$$

$$
p = 22.5(3r)
$$

$p = 67.5r$

$$
p = \dfrac{6,\! 750}{100} \cdot r
$$

Therefore, $p$ is $6,\! 750\%$ of $r$.
