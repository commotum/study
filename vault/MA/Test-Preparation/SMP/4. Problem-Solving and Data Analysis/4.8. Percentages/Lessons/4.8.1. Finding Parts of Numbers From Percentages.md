## Finding Part of a Number Given a Percentage

**Example:** A company surveyed $300$ employees. If $36\%$ gave positive feedback, how many employees gave positive feedback?

**Explanation**

To find how many employees gave positive feedback, we calculate $36\%$ of $300{:}$

$$
0.36 \times 300 = 108
$$

So, $108$ employees gave positive feedback.

## Finding an Expression for a Part Given a Percentage

**Example:** The width of a rectangle is $42\%$ of its length. Which expression represents the width of the rectangle, where $\ell$ is the length?

1. $0.42\ell$
2. $4.2\ell$
3. $42\ell$
4. $0.042\ell$

**Explanation**

To find $42\%$ of the length, we convert the percentage to a decimal and multiply by $\ell{:}$

$$
\text{Width} = 0.42 \times \ell = 0.42\ell
$$

So, the width of the rectangle is $0.42\ell$.

## Applying a Percentage Greater Than 100%

**Example:** A restaurant served $560$ meals last week. This week, the number of meals served is $135\%$ of last week’s total. How many meals were served this week?

**Explanation**

We are given a percentage and a whole, and we are asked to find the corresponding part. The formula we can use is

$$
\textrm{part} = \textrm{percentage} \times \textrm{whole}
$$

In this case, the percentage is $135\%$ and the whole is $560$.

We begin by converting the percentage to a fraction:

$$
135\% = \dfrac{135}{100} = \dfrac{27}{20}
$$

Now, we can substitute the obtained values into our formula:

$$
\begin{aligned}
part &= percentage \cdot whole \\
&= \frac{27}{20} \cdot 560 \\
&= 27 \cdot \frac{560}{20} \\
&= 27 \cdot 28 \\
&= 756
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the restaurant served $756$ meals this week.

## Finding Part of a Number With Unit Conversions (Word Problems)

**Example:** Dylan spent $25\%$ of his $1.8$-hour workout session doing HIIT exercises. How many minutes did he spend doing HIIT?

**Explanation**

First, we find $25\%$ of $1.8$ hours:

$$
0.25 \times 1.8 = 0.45 \text{hours}
$$

Next, we convert $0.45$ hours to minutes:

$$
0.45 \times 60 = 27 \text{minutes}
$$

So, Dylan spent $27$ minutes doing HIIT exercises.

## Calculating a Compound Sample Size Given a Table of Proportions

**Example:** The table above summarizes $3,\!600$ website visits by traffic source. Based on the table, how many visits were Organic or Direct?

| Source | Percentage of<br>total visits |
| --- | ---: |
| Organic | $28\%$ |
| Direct | $22\%$ |
| Social | $20\%$ |
| Email | $12\%$ |
| Referral | $10\%$ |
| Other | $8\%$ |

**Explanation**

We begin by adding together the percentages for Organic and Direct:

$$
28\% + 22\% = 50\%
$$

This means that $50\%$ of the visits were Organic or Direct. Since $3,\!600$ visits were reported, the actual number of visits that were Organic or Direct is

$$
\begin{aligned}
Number of visits &= 50% \cdot 3, 600 \\
&= \frac{50}{100} \cdot 3, 600 \\
&= 0.50 \cdot 3, 600 \\
&= 1, 800
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, $1,\!800$ visits were Organic or Direct.
