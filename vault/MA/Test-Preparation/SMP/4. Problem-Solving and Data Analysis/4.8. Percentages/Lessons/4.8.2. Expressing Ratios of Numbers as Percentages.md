## Introduction

Questions about expressing ratios as percentages test the ability to interpret part–whole relationships and convert fractions or ratios into percent form. In this topic, we’ll practice performing these conversions.

<a id="finding-a-percentage"></a>
## Finding a Percentage

**Example:** What percent of $900$ is $495$?

**Explanation**

To find what percent of $900$ is $495$, we divide $495$ by $900$ and multiply the result by $100{:}$

$$
\dfrac{495}{900} \times 100 = \dfrac{11}{20} \times 100 = 55\%
$$

So, $495$ is $55\%$ of $900$.

## Finding a Percentage: Word Problems

**Example:** Out of $640{,}000$ cans, $384{,}000$ are recycled. What percentage of the cans are recycled?

**Explanation**

To find what percentage of the cans are recycled, we divide the number of recycled cans by the total number of cans and multiply by $100{:}$

$$
\dfrac{384{,}000}{640{,}000} \times 100 = \dfrac{3}{5} \times 100 = 60\%
$$

So, $60\%$ of the cans are recycled.

## Determining a Difference in Proportions From a Joint Frequency Table

**Example:** A total of $34,\!852$ students study at a certain university. The university conducted a survey among a randomly chosen sample of $1,\!200$ students about their preference for morning or evening classes. The results are shown in the table below.

|  | Morning | Evening | No Preference | Total |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Freshmen and Sophomores | $280$ | $190$ | $30$ | $500$ |
| Juniors and Seniors | $210$ | $150$ | $40$ | $400$ |
| Graduates | $120$ | $100$ | $80$ | $300$ |
| Total | $610$ | $440$ | $150$ | $1,\!200$ |

What is the difference between the percentage of undergraduates (freshmen, sophomores, juniors, and seniors combined) who preferred morning classes and the percentage of graduate students who preferred morning classes? Rounded to the nearest whole number.

**Explanation**

To calculate a percentage, we take the part as a fraction of the whole and then multiply the fraction by $100\%$.

We will first calculate the percentage for undergraduates, then calculate the percentage for graduate students.

- The total number of undergraduates is
$500 + 400 = 900$,
and the number of undergraduates who preferred morning classes is
$280 + 210 = 490$.
So, the percentage of undergraduates who preferred morning classes is
$\dfrac{490}{900} \times 100\% \approx 54.4\%$.
- Out of $300$ graduate students, the number who preferred morning classes is $120$. So, the percentage of graduate students who preferred morning classes is
$\dfrac{120}{300} \times 100\% = 40\%$.

Finally, the difference between the percentages is

$$
54.4\% - 40\% = 14.4\%
$$

Therefore, rounded to the nearest whole number, the difference is $14\%$.
