## Introduction

Questions about finding totals from percentages test whether we can reverse the usual percent-of-a-number process. Sometimes these problems appear in real-world contexts.

In this lesson, we’ll practice setting up and solving equations to find the total from a known part and a percentage.

---

<a id="finding-a-total-given-a-part-as-a-percentage"></a>
## Finding a Total Given a Part as a Percentage

**Example:** $63$ is $7\%$ of what number?

**Explanation**

To find the number that $63$ is $7\%$ of, we divide $63$ by $0.07{:}$

$$
\dfrac{63}{0.07} = 900
$$

So, $63$ is $7\%$ of $900$.

## Finding a Total Given a Part as a Percentage (Word Problems)

**Example:** A scientist identified $64$ known species in a survey, which represented $80\%$ of all the species present. How many species were present in total?

**Explanation**

We are told that $64$ species is $80\%$ of the total number of species.

Let $x$ represent the total number of species. We can write the equation
$0.80x = 64$
to represent the situation.

Now, we divide both sides by $0.80$ to solve for $x{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
x &= \frac{64}{0.80} \\
&= 80
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the total number of species present was $80$.
