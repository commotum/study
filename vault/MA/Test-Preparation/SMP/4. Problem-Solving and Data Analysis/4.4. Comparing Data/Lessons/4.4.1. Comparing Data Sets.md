## Identifying Datasets With Identical Medians

**Example:** $7, \: 9, \: 11, \: 13, \: 15$

Which of the following data sets has the same median as the data set above?

1. $5, \: 9, \: 11, \: 11, \: 14, \: 16$
2. $6, \: 8, \: 11, \: 11, \: 12, \: 14$
3. $4, \: 10, \: 11, \: 13, \: 13, \: 17$
4. $7, \: 9, \: 11, \: 11, \: 13, \: 20$

**Explanation**

If a data set contains an odd number of data values, the median is represented by the middle data value in the list when the data values are listed in ascending or descending order.

Since our data set
$7, \: 9, \: 11, \: 13, \: 15$,
has $5$ data values and is in ascending order, we have that the median is the third data value in the list, that is, its median is $11$.

On the other hand, if a data set contains an even number of data values, the median is between the two middle data values when the values are listed in ascending or descending order.

Since each of the choices consists of a data set with $6$ data values in ascending order, it follows that the median is between the $3$rd and $4$th data values.

- The $3$rd and $4$th data values in choice I are $11$ and $11$. Therefore, its median is $11$.
- The $3$rd and $4$th data values in choice II are $11$ and $11$. Therefore, its median is $11$.
- The $3$rd and $4$th data values in choice III are $11$ and $13$. Therefore, its median is $\dfrac{11+13}{2} = 12$.
- The $3$rd and $4$th data values in choice IV are $11$ and $11$. Therefore, its median is $11$.

Therefore, choices I, II, and IV represent data sets that have the same median as the given data set. So, the correct answer is "I, II, and IV."

## Calculating Differences Between Medians

**Example:** The table below shows the electricity generation (in terawatt-hours) in the top nine countries in both $2001$ and $2005$. The numbers are rounded to the nearest tenth of a terawatt-hour.

| Electricity Generation, in TWh |  |  |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Country | $2001$ | $2005$ |
| Country A | $120.5$ | $130.2$ |
| Country B | $98.1$ | $99.4$ |
| Country C | $76.3$ | $80.1$ |
| Country D | $135.0$ | $142.6$ |
| Country E | $88.4$ | $90.0$ |
| Country F | $110.2$ | $115.0$ |
| Country G | $101.7$ | $108.3$ |
| Country H | $95.9$ | $97.8$ |
| Country I | $140.8$ | $150.0$ |

Based on the information in the table, how much greater, in terawatt-hours, was the median electricity generation in $2005$ than in $2001$, rounded to the nearest tenth of a terawatt-hour?

The median electricity generation in $2005$ was $XXXXX$ terawatt-hours greater than in $2001$.

**Explanation**

The median of a data set is the middle value, meaning half of the data points lie above it and half lie below it, when the values are arranged from smallest to largest.

Since both data sets contain $9$ values, the median is the $5$th value after ordering the data from least to greatest.

With this in mind, we proceed to calculate the median for each data.

- $2001$ data points are not ordered, so we order them first from smallest to largest:
$76.3,\: 88.4,\: 95.9,\: 98.1,\: 101.7,\: 110.2,\: 120.5,\: 135.0,\: 140.8$
Thus, the median is
$\text{Median}(2001) = 101.7$.
- $2005$ data points are not ordered, so we order them first from smallest to largest:
$80.1,\: 90.0,\: 97.8,\: 99.4,\: 108.3,\: 115.0,\: 130.2,\: 142.6,\: 150.0$
Thus, the median is
$\text{Median}(2005) = 108.3$.

So, the difference between the medians is

$$
\begin{aligned}
Median(2005) - Median(2001) &= 108.3 - 101.7 \\
&= 6.6
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the median electricity generation in $2005$ was $\bbox[4pt,border: 1px solid lightgray]{6.6}$ terawatt-hours greater than in $2001$.

## Comparing Two Datasets Given Some Statistics

**Example:** The results of two independent surveys are shown in the table below.

| Employees' Daily Emails Sent |  |  |  |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Group | Sample size | Mean ($\textrm{emails}$)[math] | Standard deviation ($\textrm{emails}$)[/math] |
| $C$ | $1,050$ | $48$ | $11$ |
| $H$ | $1,050$ | $48$ | $15$ |

Which statement is true based on the table?

1. Group $H$ included the employee with the highest daily emails sent.
2. The Group $C$ data set was identical to the Group $H$ data set.
3. The daily emails sent in Group $H$ had a larger spread than those in Group $C$.
4. The median daily emails sent of Group $H$ is greater than the median daily emails sent of Group $C$.

**Explanation**

From the table, we notice that the two groups surveyed have the same sample size and mean value but different standard deviations.

Standard deviation tells us how spread out the data are. A bigger standard deviation means the data are more spread out.

Let’s check which statement correctly interprets this difference in standard deviation:

- Statement I is false because the table only gives the mean and the standard deviation. It does not show the maximum value for each group.
- Statement II is false because if two data sets were identical, they would have the same mean and the same standard deviation. While both groups share the same mean, their standard deviations are different.
- Statement III is true because the daily emails sent in Group $H$ have a standard deviation of $15$, which is larger than Group $C$’s $11$. This means that the daily emails sent in Group $H$ had a larger spread than those in Group $C$.
- Statement IV is false because, without more information about the distribution of the data (for example, whether it is skewed), we cannot compare the medians.

Therefore, the correct answer is "III only".

<a id="identifying-true-statements-concerning-centrality-of-two-datasets"></a>
## Identifying True Statements Concerning Centrality of Two Datasets

**Example:** Two groups of office workers, each with $9$ members, tracked their daily caffeine intake. The mean intake for Group A is $185.56$ mg, while the mean for Group B is $140$ mg.

Which of the following must be true?

1. Every member of Group A consumed more caffeine than every member of Group B.
2. The median caffeine intake in Group A is greater than the median in Group B.
3. There is at least one member of Group B who consumed less caffeine than at least one member of Group A.

**Explanation**

Let’s evaluate each statement based on the information provided.

- Statement I is not necessarily true. A lower mean does not imply that all individuals consumed less. For example, Group A’s daily caffeine intakes could be
$50, \: 60, \: 70, \: 80, \: 90, \: 300, \: 320, \: 340, \: 360$,
while Group B’s could be
$120, \: 125, \: 130, \: 135, \: 140, \: 145, \: 150, \: 155, \: 160$.
In this case, several members in Group B consumed more caffeine than some members of Group A.
- Statement II is not necessarily true. A higher mean does not guarantee a higher median. In the example above, Group A has a median of $90$, while Group B has a median of $140$.
- Statement III must be true. If every member in Group B had consumed more caffeine than any member in Group A, then the lowest caffeine intake in Group B should be greater than or equal to the highest caffeine intake in Group A. This would imply that the mean for Group A would be less than or equal to the mean for Group B. However, we know that the mean intake of Group A $(185.56)$ is greater than that of Group B $(140)$.

Therefore, the correct answer is "III only."
