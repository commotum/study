## Comparing Means When a New Data Point Is Added

**Example:** The table shows heart rates (beats per minute) measured at six time intervals during a workout.

| Interval | Heart Rate (bpm) |
| --- | ---: |
| 1 | $120$ |
| 2 | $125$ |
| 3 | $118$ |
| 4 | $122$ |
| 5 | $127$ |
| 6 | $121$ |

A seventh interval measured $140$ bpm, and this value is added to the original dataset of six time intervals to create a new dataset. Which of the following statements correctly compares the means of the two datasets?

1. The mean of the original dataset is greater than the mean of the new dataset.
2. The mean of the original dataset is less than the mean of the new dataset.
3. The means of both datasets are equal.
4. There is not enough information to compare the means.
5. Both means are undefined.

**Explanation**

When a new data point is added, the mean of the dataset moves *toward* that new data point:

- If the new value is *less* than the current mean, the overall mean decreases.
- If the new value is *greater* than the current mean, the overall mean increases.

Let’s compute the mean of the original dataset:

$$
\begin{aligned}
Original mean &= \frac{120 + 125 + 118 + 122 + 127 + 121}{6} \\
&= \frac{733}{6} \\
&\approx 122.1667
\end{aligned}
$$

The new value is $140$, Since $140>122.1667$, the overall mean will *increase* when this data point is included.

Therefore, the correct comparison of the two datasets is option II, which states that the mean of the original dataset is less than the mean of the new dataset.

## Comparing Means and Medians of Datasets Differing by One Value

**Example:** $\qquad$ Dataset $P{:}$ $5,5,5,5,5,5,5,5$

$\qquad$ Dataset $Q{:}$ $2,5,5,5,5,5,5,5,5$

Two datasets, $P$ and $Q$, are given above. Which of the following statements about the means and medians of the datasets is true?

1. Both the means and the medians are different.
2. Only the medians are different.
3. Only the means are different.
4. Neither the means nor the medians are different.
5. There is not enough information to determine.

**Explanation**

When a new data point is added, the mean of the dataset will change, and the median of the dataset may change:

- The mean of the dataset moves toward that new data point.
- The median of the dataset moves toward that new data point or stays the same.

In dataset $P$, all values are $5$, so the mean is $5$ and the median is $5$.

- Dataset $Q$ is formed by adding the value $2$ to $P$. Since $2$ is less than the mean of $P$, $5$, the mean of $Q$ must be less than $5$.
- Dataset $Q$ has nine values: eight are $5$ and one is $2$. When ordered, the middle (5th) value is $5$, so the median remains $5$.

Therefore, the correct answer is option III: "Only the means are different."

## Comparing Means and Medians When a New Data Point Is Added in a Frequency Table

**Example:** The table shows the streaming bitrates, rounded to the nearest whole number, for a group of $57$ video sessions.

| Bitrate | Number of Sessions |
| --- | ---: |
| $4$ | $6$ |
| $5$ | $7$ |
| $6$ | $8$ |
| $7$ | $8$ |
| $8$ | $10$ |
| $9$ | $9$ |
| $10$ | $9$ |

A new bitrate of $20$ is added to the dataset, making $58$ bitrates in total. Which of the following statements best compares the mean and median of the new dataset to those of the original dataset?

1. The mean of the new dataset is less than the mean of the original dataset, and the medians of the two datasets are equal.
2. The mean of the new dataset is greater than the mean of the original dataset, and the medians of the two datasets are equal.
3. The mean of the new dataset is less than the mean of the original dataset, and the median of the new dataset is less than the median of the original dataset.
4. The mean of the new dataset is greater than the mean of the original dataset, and the median of the new dataset is greater than the median of the original dataset.
5. The mean of the new dataset is greater than the mean of the original dataset, and the median of the new dataset is less than the median of the original dataset.

**Explanation**

When a new data point is added, the mean of the dataset will change, and the median of the dataset may change:

- The mean of the dataset moves toward that new data point.
- The median of the dataset moves toward that new data point or stays the same.

Since the original mean lies around $7$, and the new data point $20$ is much larger, the mean of the new dataset must be *greater* than the original mean.

Now, to compare the medians, we first calculate the cumulative frequencies.

| Bitrate | Number of Sessions | Cumulative Frequency |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| $4$ | $6$ | $6$ |
| $5$ | $7$ | $13$ |
| $6$ | $8$ | $21$ |
| $7$ | $8$ | $29$ |
| $8$ | $10$ | $39$ |
| $9$ | $9$ | $48$ |
| $10$ | $9$ | $57$ |

In the original dataset of $57$ sessions, the middle is the $29$th value. From the cumulative frequencies, the $29$th value corresponds to a bitrate of $7$. Thus, the median is $7$.

When the $20$ is added, the dataset size becomes $58$, so the middle lies between the $29$th and $30$th values. These values correspond to bitrates of $7$ and $8$, respectively. Thus, the median is

$$
\dfrac{7+8}{2}=7.5
$$

which is greater than $7$.

Therefore, the correct comparison of the two datasets is option IV, which states that the mean of the new dataset is greater than the mean of the original dataset, and the median of the new dataset is greater than the median of the original dataset.

## Comparing Means and Medians When Adding Data to a Dot Plot

**Example:** The dot plot above shows the hours of television watched per week by $15$ households. If an additional household that watched $1$ hour is added to create a new data set of $16$ households, which of the following measures will be smaller for the new data set than for the original data set?

1. The median hours
2. The mean hours

![](<../Source/4.4.6. Effects of Additional Data on Centrality Measures/Images/4cb609a4380bdcc122282e0c7b43848b.png>)

**Explanation**

In a dot plot, the median is the middle value when the data are ordered.

Let's check each statement:

- Statement I:With $15$ households, the median is the $8$th value. Counting across the frequencies, the cumulative counts for $5,10,15,20,25$ are
$3,\;7,\;11,\;14,\;15$,
so the $8$th value lies at $15$ hours.After adding a new household with $1$ hour, the data set has $16$ households. The median is now the average of the $8$th and $9$th values, which are $10$ and $15$. The median becomes $12.5$.Thus, statement I is true.
- Statement II:Since $1$ is less than the original mean, adding this new value lowers the overall average, so the mean decreases.Thus, statement II is true.

Therefore, the correct answer is “I and II.”

## Comparing Means and Medians of Integer Datasets Differing by One Value

**Example:** Dataset $M$ consists of $52$ integers between $20$ and $60$. Dataset $N$ consists of all the integers in $M$ together with the additional integer $500$. Which of the following statements is true?

1. The mean must be greater for dataset $N$ than for dataset $M$.
2. The median must be greater for dataset $N$ than for dataset $M$.
3. The mean might not be greater for dataset $N$ than for dataset $M$.
4. The median might not be greater for dataset $N$ than for dataset $M$.

**Explanation**

When a new data point is added, the mean of the dataset will change, and the median of the dataset may change:

- The mean of the dataset moves toward that new data point.
- The median of the dataset moves toward that new data point or stays the same.

In dataset $M$, all values are in the range $20$ and $60$, so the mean and the median are in this range.

- Dataset $N$ is formed by adding the value $500$ to $M$. Since $500$ is far above every value in $M$ (all are at most $60$), the new mean for $N$ must be *greater* than the mean of $M$.So, the mean must be greater for dataset $N$ than for dataset $M$, meaning statement I is true and statement III is false.
- To find the median, we list the dataset $M$ in ascending order:
$M_1 \le \dots \le M_{52}$
Dataset $M$ has $52$ values, so the median is the average of the $26$th and $27$th values, $\dfrac{M_{26}+M_{27}}{2}$. On the other hand, dataset $N$ has $53$ values:
$\underbrace{M_1}_{N_1} \le \dots \le \underbrace{M_{52}}_{N_{52}} < \underbrace{500}_{N_{53}}$
So, the median value is the $27$th value:
$\text{median}(N)=N_{27}=M_{27}\ \ \text{and}\ \ \text{median}(M)=\frac{M_{26}+M_{27}}{2}\le M_{27}=\text{median}(N)$.
The inequality can be strict (if $M_{26} < M_{27}$) or an equality (if $M_{26}=M_{27}$).As a result, the median might not be greater for dataset $N$ than for dataset $M$, meaning statement IV is true and statement II is false.

Therefore, the correct answer is **“I and IV only.”**
