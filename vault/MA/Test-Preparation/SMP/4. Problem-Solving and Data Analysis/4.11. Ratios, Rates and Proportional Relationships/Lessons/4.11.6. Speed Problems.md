## Introduction

Speed problems are among the most common applications of rate reasoning, often involving the relationship between distance, speed, and time.

In this topic, we’ll practice setting up and solving these problems systematically: translating words into equations, handling unit conversions, and mastering the logic behind distance–rate–time relationships.

## Finding Time Given Distance and Speed

**Example:** A cyclist pedals at a constant speed of $4$ meters per second. At this speed, how many seconds will it take the cyclist to travel $100$ meters?

**Explanation**

We are given the cyclist travels $100$ meters at a constant speed of $4$ meters per second.

To solve our problem, we can use the formula that relates distance, speed, and time:

$$
\textrm{Speed} = \dfrac{\textrm{Distance}}{\textrm{Time}}
$$

Substituting the known values ​​in the above formula, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
4 &= \frac{100}{Time} \\
Time &= \frac{100}{4} \\
Time &= 25
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, it will take the cyclist $25$ seconds to travel $100$ meters.

## Finding Distance Given Speed and Time

**Example:** A motorcycle travels at an average speed of $55$ meters per second. At this rate, how many meters will the motorcycle travel in $16$ seconds?

**Explanation**

We are given that the motorcycle travels for $16$ seconds at an average speed of $55$ meters per second.

To solve our problem, we can use the formula that relates distance, speed, and time:

$$
\textrm{Speed} = \dfrac{\textrm{Distance}}{\textrm{Time}}
$$

Substituting the known values ​​in the above formula, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
55 &= \frac{Distance}{16} \\
Distance &= 55 \cdot 16 \\
Distance &= 880
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the motorcycle will travel $880$ meters in $16$ seconds.

## Finding Speed Given Distance and Time: Requires Unit Conversions

**Example:** In $1932$, Wiley Post made the first solo flight around the world. Suppose he traveled $90$ miles in $72$ minutes. If he maintained this pace, what was his average speed, in miles per hour, to the nearest tenth?

**Explanation**

We're asked to determine the average speed in miles per hour. Therefore, we must first transform the given number of minutes into hours.

There are $60\,\textrm{min}$ per $\textrm{h}$, which we can write as follows:

$$
\dfrac{60\,\textrm{min}}{\textrm{h}}
$$

However, since we're converting from a smaller unit of time $(\textrm{min})$ to a larger one $(\textrm{h})$, we need to multiply $72\,\textrm{min}$ by the reciprocal of the above:

$$
\dfrac{\textrm{h}}{60\,\textrm{min}}
$$

This is our conversion factor.

Now, multiplying $72\,\textrm{min}$ by our conversion factor, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
72min &= 72min \cdot \frac{h}{60min} \\
&= 72min \cdot \frac{h}{60min} \\
&= 72 \cdot \frac{h}{60} \\
&= \frac{72}{60}h \\
&= 1.2h
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, $72\,\textrm{min}$ is equivalent to $1.2\,\textrm{h}$.

Next, we can use the formula that relates distance, speed, and time:

$$
\textrm{Speed} = \dfrac{\textrm{Distance}}{\textrm{Time}}
$$

Substituting the known values ​​in the above formula, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
Speed &= \frac{90}{1.2} \\
&= 75.0
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the average speed of the flight was $75.0$ miles per hour.

## Finding an Expression for Time Given Some Related Rates

**Example:** Two runners, $A$ and $B$, each move at constant speeds. The speed of runner $A$ is one fourth the speed of runner $B$. Runner $A$ covers a distance of $8q$ meters in $t$ seconds. Which expression represents the time, in seconds, it takes runner $B$ to travel a distance of $48q$ meters?

**Explanation**

The formula that relates distance, speed, and time is given by

$$
\text{Speed} = \frac{\text{Distance}}{\text{Time}}
$$

Let’s start with runner $A$. We’re told he travels a distance of $8q$ meters in time $t$. So, his speed is

$$
\text{Speed}_A = \dfrac{8q}{t}
$$

Now, we are given that runner $A$ moves at one-fourth the speed of runner $B$. That means runner $B$ is four times as fast as runner $A$. So, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
Speed_{B} &= 4 \cdot Speed_{A} \\
&= 4 \cdot \frac{8q}{t} \\
&= \frac{32q}{t}
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we calculate how long it takes runner $B$ to travel $48q$ meters:

$$
\begin{aligned}
Time_{B} &= (48q)/((32q/t)) \\
&= \frac{48q \cdot t}{32q} \\
&= \frac{3 \cdot 16q \cdot t}{2 \cdot 16q} \\
&= \frac{3 \cdot 16q \cdot t}{2 \cdot 16q} \\
&= \frac{3t}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the expression

$$
\dfrac{3t}{2}
$$

represents the time, in seconds, it takes runner $B$ to travel a distance of $48q$ meters.
