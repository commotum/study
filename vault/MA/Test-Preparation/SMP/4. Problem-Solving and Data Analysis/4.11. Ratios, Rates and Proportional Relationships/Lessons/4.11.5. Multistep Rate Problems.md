## Introduction

Multistep rate problems often combine two or more relationships. These questions test our ability to reason through proportional relationships logically rather than relying on a single formula.

In this topic, we’ll practice breaking down complex rate problems into smaller steps, identifying what each rate represents, and using consistent units to solve them.

## Solving Problems Using Unit Rates

**Example:** An oil extractor produces $18$ liters of oil in $7$ minutes. At this rate, how many minutes does it take the extractor to produce $90$ liters of oil?

**Explanation**

We are given that the oil extractor produces $18$ liters of oil in $7$ minutes. This rate can be written as

$$
\dfrac{18\,\textrm{L}}{7\,\textrm{min}}
$$

Let $x$ be the number of minutes it takes the oil extractor to produce $90$ liters of oil. Then, the value of $x$ can be calculated by solving the equation:

$$
\dfrac{18\,\textrm{L}}{7\,\textrm{min}} = \dfrac{90\,\textrm{L}}{x\,\textrm{min}}
$$

which can be rewritten more simply as

$$
\dfrac{18}{7} = \dfrac{90}{x}
$$

Solving for $x$, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{18}{7} &= \frac{90}{x} \\
18 \cdot x &= 90 \cdot 7 \\
18x &= 630 \\
x &= 35
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, it takes $35$ minutes to produce $90$ liters of oil.

## Finding a Rate in Two Steps

**Example:** The total area of a lakeside town is $240.0$ square miles, of which $80.0$ square miles is water. If the town had a population of $1,300,000$ people in the year $2022$, which of the following is closest to the population density, in people per square mile of land area, of the town at that time?

**Explanation**

We are given that the total area of the lakeside town is $240.0$ square miles, of which $80.0$ square miles is water, and the town had a population of $1,300,000$ people in the year $2022$.

The land area of the town can be found by subtracting the area of the water from the total area of the town:

$$
240.0 - 80.0 = 160.0
$$

Now, to calculate the population density of the town, we divide its population by the land area:

$$
\dfrac{1,300,000}{160.0} = 8125
$$

Therefore, the population density is approximately $8125$ people per square mile.

## Finding a Difference Given Two Rates

**Example:** A planet has two satellites. The first satellite orbits the planet every $300$ days, and the second satellite orbits the planet every $350$ days. How many more days does it take the second satellite to orbit the planet $60$ times than it takes the first satellite to orbit the planet $60$ times?

**Explanation**

We are given that the first satellite orbits the planet every $300$ days. Therefore, it takes the first satellite

$$
300(60) = 18000
$$

days to orbit the planet $60$ times.

We are also given that the second satellite orbits the planet every $350$ days. Therefore, it takes the second satellite

$$
350(60) = 21000
$$

days to orbit the planet $60$ times.

Since it takes the first satellite $18000$ days and the second satellite $21000$ days, it takes the second satellite

$$
21000 - 18000 = 3000
$$

more days than it takes the first satellite to orbit the planet $60$ times.

## Using a Growth Factor as a Unit Rate

**Example:** Veterinarians sometimes estimate a dog's age in years by multiplying its weight in pounds by a constant growth factor that varies depending on the breed. The table below shows the growth factors for several dog breeds.

| Dog Breed | Growth<br>factor |
| --- | ---: |
| Labrador Retriever | $5$ |
| German Shepherd | $5.5$ |
| Golden Retriever | $5$ |
| Bulldog | $5$ |
| Beagle | $4$ |
| Boxer | $5.5$ |
| Poodle | $4.5$ |
| Rottweiler | $6$ |

Suppose a Golden Retriever and a Poodle each currently weigh $14$ pounds. Which of the following is closest to the difference, in pounds, between their weights $11$ years from now?

**Explanation**

We’re told that a dog’s age can be estimated using the formula

$$
\text{Age} = \text{weight} \times \text{growth factor}
$$

For a Golden Retriever with weight $14$ pounds, we have

$$
\text{Age}_{\text{golden}} = 14 \times 5 = 70
$$

For a Poodle with the same weight, we have

$$
\text{Age}_{\text{poodle}} = 14 \times 4.5 = 63
$$

Now, after $11$ years, the Golden Retriever’s age increases to

$$
70 + 11 = 81
$$

while the Poodle’s age becomes

$$
63 + 11 = 74
$$

To compare their weights, we solve for weight:

$$
\text{weight} = \frac{\text{age}}{\text{growth factor}}
$$

For the Golden Retriever:

$$
\text{weight}_{\text{golden,new}} = \frac{81}{5} = 16.2
$$

and for the Poodle:

$$
\text{weight}_{\text{poodle,new}} = \frac{74}{4.5} \approx 16.44
$$

Finally, the difference is

$$
\mid 16.44 - 16.2 \mid \approx 0.24
$$

Therefore, among the given options, the closest value to the difference is $0.2$.
