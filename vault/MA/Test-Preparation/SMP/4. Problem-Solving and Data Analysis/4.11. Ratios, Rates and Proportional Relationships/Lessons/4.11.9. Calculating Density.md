## Introduction

Density problems often appear in the context of physical quantities (mass, volume) and sometimes in geometric settings. These problems test our ability to use proportional relationships and apply formulas in practical, real-world contexts.

In this, we’ll practice solving for density, mass, or volume using the definition of density.

## Finding Density Given Mass and Volume

**Example:** An ice cube has a mass of $288$ grams and a volume of $360$ cubic centimeters. What is the density, in grams per cubic centimeter, of the ice cube?

**Explanation**

Density is defined as the ratio of mass to volume:

$$
\rho = \dfrac{m}{V}
$$

In this problem,

- the mass is $m = 288$ grams, and
- the volume is $V = 360$ cubic centimeters.

Substituting these values into the formula, we get

$$
\rho = \frac{288}{360} = 0.8
$$

Therefore, the density is $0.8$ grams per cubic centimeter.

## Computing a Cube's Mass Given Density and Edge Length

**Example:** An aluminium cube has a density of $800$ kilograms per cubic meter, and each edge has a length of $0.7$ meters. What is the mass, in kilograms, of the cube?

**Explanation**

Density is defined as the ratio of mass to volume:

$$
\rho = \dfrac{m}{V}
$$

Here, we want to find the mass $m{:}$

$$
m = \rho \cdot V
$$

Since the object is a cube, its volume is given by

$$
V = s^3
$$

where $s$ is the length of an edge.

In this problem,

- the density is $\rho = 800 \ \text{kg/m}^3$, and
- the edge length is $s = 0.7 \ \text{m}$.

First, we evaluate the volume of the cube:

$$
\begin{aligned}
V &= s^{3} \\
&= (0.7)^{3} \\
&= 0.343m^{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we find its mass of the cube:

$$
\begin{aligned}
m &= ρ \cdot V \\
&= 800 \cdot 0.343 \\
&= 274.4kg
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the mass of the cube is $274.4$ kilograms.

## Computing a Cube's Edge Length Given Mass and Density

**Example:** An iron cube has a mass of $1{,}331$ kilograms and a density of $7{,}000$ kilograms per cubic meter. What is the length of one edge of the cube, in meters? Round your final answer to the nearest hundredth of a meter.

**Explanation**

Density is defined as the ratio of mass to volume:

$$
\rho = \dfrac{m}{V}
$$

We can use this formula to find the volume $V{:}$

$$
V = \dfrac{m}{\rho}
$$

In this problem,

- the mass is $m = 1{,}331$ kilograms, and
- the density is $\rho = 7{,}000$ kilograms per cubic meter.

Substituting these values, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
V &= \frac{m}{ρ} \\
&= \frac{1,331}{7,000} \\
&= 0.1901m^{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

Since the object is a cube, its volume is given by

$$
V = s^3
$$

where $s$ is the length of an edge.

Thus, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
s &= \sqrt[3]{V} \\
&= \sqrt[3]{0.1901} \\
&\approx 0.58m
\end{aligned}
$$

rounded to the hundredth of a meter.

Therefore, the length of the edge of the cube is approximately $0.58$ meters.
