## Writing a Ratio in Its Simplest Form

**Example:** Maya has $64$ markers and $80$ crayons. What is the ratio of markers to crayons that Maya has?

**Explanation**

The ratio of markers to crayons is $64:80$.

Now, we can simplify it by dividing both numbers by their greatest common divisor, $16{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{64}{80} &= \frac{64 \div 16}{80 \div 16} \\
&= \frac{4}{5}
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the simplified ratio is $4:5$.

## Using a Ratio to Compute a Numerator

**Example:** An astronaut sees an explosion in space and records the delay before receiving the radio signal. If the time interval is $60$ seconds and the ratio of distance in miles to time in seconds is estimated as $7$ to $20$, how far away is the explosion?

**Explanation**

We are told that the ratio of miles to seconds is $7:20$. This means for every $7$ miles, the time is $20$ seconds.

So, the distance $d$ (in miles) when the time is $60$ seconds can be found from the proportion:

$$
\dfrac{d}{60} = \dfrac{7}{20}
$$

Now, solve for $d{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{d}{60} &= \frac{7}{20} \\
d &= \frac{60 \cdot 7}{20} \\
d &= 21
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the explosion is $21$ miles away.

## Finding a Missing Value in a Ratio Table

**Example:** In the table above, the ratio of $q$ to $p$ is constant for each ordered pair. What is the value of $w$?

| $p$ | $q$ |
| --- | ---: |
| $4$ | $5$ |
| $8$ | $10$ |
| $12$ | $15$ |
| $w$ | $25$ |

**Explanation**

Since the ratio

$$
\dfrac{q}{p}
$$

is constant, we can compute it from any row.

Using $(p,q)=(4,5)$ gives

$$
\dfrac{q}{p}=\dfrac{5}{4}
$$

So,

$$
p=\dfrac{4}{5}q
$$

for every row.

For $q=25$, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
p &= \frac{4}{5}q \\
w &= \frac{4}{5} \cdot 25 \\
w &= 20
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus, $w=20$.

## Finding a Ratio of Rates From a Table

**Example:** The table above shows the amount of protein in two foods and the cost of each food. Based on the table, what is the ratio of the cost per gram of protein in oatmeal to the cost per gram of protein in quinoa?

| Food | Protein | Cost |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 1 cup oatmeal | $6$ grams | $\[math]0.54$ |
| 1 cup quinoa | $12$ grams | $\[/math]0.60$ |

**Explanation**

To compare the cost, we first calculate the cost per gram of protein for each food.

The cost per gram of protein for oatmeal is

$$
\dfrac{\[math]0.54}{6} = \[/math]0.09 \text{per gram}
$$

and for quinoa

$$
\dfrac{\[math]0.60}{12} = \[/math]0.05 \text{per gram}
$$

Now, we form the ratio of the two costs:
$0.09: 0.05$

To simplify, we multiply both numbers by $100{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{0.09}{0.05} &= \frac{0.09 \cdot 100}{0.05 \cdot 100} \\
&= \frac{9}{5}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the ratio of the cost per gram of protein in oatmeal to quinoa is $9:5$.
