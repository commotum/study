## Determining a Rate Using Equivalent Ratios

**Example:** A paint sprayer applies $132c\,\textrm{mL}$ of paint in $8d$ minutes at a constant rate. Which expression represents the number of milliliters applied in $3d$ minutes at this rate?

**Explanation**

The ratio of volume of paint applied to the number of minutes is given by

$$
132c\,\textrm{mL} \::\: 8d\,\textrm{min}
$$

We need to know the volume of paint applied in $3d$ minutes. To determine this, we

- determine the *unit rate* (milliliters of paint applied per minute), and then
- multiply by the number of minutes we're interested in $(3d)$.

Dividing the above ratio by $8d$, we have

$$
\dfrac{132c}{8d}\,\textrm{mL} \::\: 1\,\textrm{min}
$$

This tells us that

$$
\dfrac{132c}{8d}
$$

milliliters of paint are applied every minute.

Finally, multiplying this ratio by $3d$, we have

$$
3d\cdot \dfrac{132c}{8d}\,\textrm{mL} \::\: 3d\cdot 1\,\textrm{min}
$$

which gives

$$
\frac{396cd}{8d}mL:3dmin; \frac{396cd}{8d}mL:3dmin; \frac{99c}{2}mL:3dmin
$$

Therefore, in $3d$ minutes, the sprayer applies

$$
\dfrac{99c}{2}\,\textrm{mL}
$$

of paint.

## Maintaining a Ratio of Numbers

**Example:** For the values $m$ and $p$, the ratio of $m$ to $p$ is $13$ to $17$. If $m$ is multiplied by $45$, what is $p$ multiplied by in order to maintain the same ratio?

**Explanation**

We are given that

$$
\dfrac{m}{p}=\dfrac{13}{17}
$$

When $m$ is multiplied by $45$, the corresponding proportion is

$$
\dfrac{45\,m}{x\,p}=\dfrac{13}{17}
$$

where $x$ is the factor by which $p$ must be multiplied to maintain the same ratio.

Now, we solve for $x{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{45m}{xp} &= \frac{13}{17} \\
\frac{m}{p} \cdot \frac{45}{x} &= \frac{13}{17} \\
\frac{13}{17} \cdot \frac{45}{x} &= \frac{13}{17} \\
\frac{45}{x} &= 1 \\
45 &= x \\
x &= 45
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus, to maintain the same ratio, $p$ must be multiplied by $45$.

## Maintaining Fixed Ratios in Polygons and Rectangular Solids

**Example:** A solar panel mounting tray in the shape of a rectangular solid has a fixed height and a fixed length-to-width ratio of $27:18$. If the width is increased by $14$ units, how should the length be adjusted to keep the same ratio?

**Explanation**

The fixed ratio of length to width is

$27:18$.
To maintain this ratio when dimensions change, the *change* in length and width must share the same ratio.

The width is increased by $14$ units. To determine the resulting change in length, we

- determine the *unit rate* (change in length per unit change in width), and then
- multiply by the change in width we're interested in ($14$ units).

Dividing the above ratio by $18$, we have

$$
\dfrac{27}{18} \::\: 1
$$

which simplifies as

$$
\dfrac{3}{2} \::\: 1
$$

This tells us that a

$$
\dfrac32
$$

unit change in length results from a $1$ unit change in width.

Then, multiplying this ratio by $14$, we have

$$
14\cdot\dfrac{3}{2} \::\: 14 \cdot 1
$$

which gives

$$
21 \::\: 14
$$

Therefore, when the width increases by $14$ units, the length must $\bbox[4pt,border: 1px solid gainsboro]{\textrm{increase}}$ by $\bbox[4pt,border: 1px solid gainsboro]{21}$ units.

## Using Ratios to Determine Arc Length

**Example:** The circle above has center $O$, the length of arc $\overset{\frown}{ACB}$ is $7\pi$, and $x = 84$. What is the length of arc $\overset{\frown}{ADB}$?

![](<../Source/4.11.3. Working With Ratios in Various Settings/Images/594282102901b3ebc45a5c3bd6501726.png>)

**Explanation**

We'll solve this problem by considering the following ratio:

$$
\textrm{arc length} \::\: \textrm{proportion of a full turn}
$$

The arc length of $\overset{\frown}{ACB}$ is $7\pi$, which represents

$$
\dfrac{84^\circ}{360^\circ} = \dfrac{7}{30}
$$

of a full turn. Therefore, we have the following ratio of arc length to full turn proportion:

$$
7\pi \::\: \dfrac{7}{30}
$$

Thus, the remaining arc $\overset{\frown}{ADB}$ corresponds to

$$
1 - \dfrac{7}{30} = \dfrac{23}{30}
$$

of a full turn. Our goal is to manipulate the above ratio to yield the arc length corresponding to this proportion.

Dividing our ratio by $7$, we have

$$
\begin{bmatrix}7π &:\frac{7}{30} \\ \frac{7π}{7} &:\frac{1}{30} \\ π &:\frac{1}{30}\end{bmatrix}
$$

This tells us that an arc length of $\pi$ corresponds to one-thirtieth of a full turn.

Finally, multiplying this ratio by $23$, we have

$$
23 \cdot \pi \::\: \dfrac{23 \cdot 1}{30}
$$

which gives

$$
23\pi \::\: \dfrac{23}{30}
$$

Therefore, the length of the arc $\overset{\frown}{ADB}$ is $23\pi$.

## Using Ratios to Determine Angle

**Example:** The circle above has center $O$, the length of arcs $\overset{\frown}{PTQ}$ and $\overset{\frown}{QRS}$ are $20\pi$ and $15\pi$, respectively, and $x = 100$. What is the value of $y$?

![](<../Source/4.11.3. Working With Ratios in Various Settings/Images/2afffdb207ef041b771c7753c8075faf.png>)

**Explanation**

We'll solve this problem by considering the following ratio:

$$
\textrm{arc length} \::\: \textrm{proportion of a full turn}
$$

The arc length of $\overset{\frown}{PTQ}$ is $20\pi$, which represents

$$
\dfrac{100^\circ}{360^\circ}=\dfrac{5}{18}
$$

of a full turn. We can express this relationship as the following ratio:

$$
20\pi \::\: \dfrac{5}{18}
$$

On the other hand, the arc length of $\overset{\frown}{QRS}$ is $15\pi$. Our goal is to manipulate the above ratio to yield the full turn proportion corresponding to this arc length, and use that to compute the value of $y$.

Dividing the above ratio by $20$, we have

$$
\begin{bmatrix}20π &:\frac{5}{18} \\ \frac{20π}{20} &:\frac{5}{18 \cdot 20} \\ π &:\frac{1}{72}\end{bmatrix}
$$

Finally, multiplying this ratio by $15$, we have

$$
15\cdot \pi \::\: \dfrac{15\cdot 1}{72}
$$

which gives

$$
15\pi \::\: \dfrac{5}{24}
$$

This means that the arc length $\overset{\frown}{QRS}$ represents

$$
\dfrac{5}{24}
$$

of a full circle. Hence, the measure of the corresponding central angle is

$$
y^\circ=\dfrac{5}{24}\cdot 360^\circ=75^\circ
$$

Therefore, $y=75$.
