## Introduction

On the math section of the SAT exam, ratio problems often require applying a given ratio to find an unknown quantity.

In this topic, we’ll practice setting up and solving proportions to find missing values.

## Using a Ratio to Compute a Denominator

**Example:** The ratio $t$ to $u$ is equivalent to the ratio $9$ to $2$. If the value of $t$ is $144$, what is the value of $u$?

**Explanation**

To find the value of $u$, we set up a proportion using the given equivalent ratios:

$$
\dfrac{t}{u} = \dfrac{9}{2}
$$

Next, we substitute $t=144$, and solve for $u{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{t}{u} &= \frac{9}{2} \\
\frac{144}{u} &= \frac{9}{2} \\
144 \cdot 2 &= 9u \\
288 &= 9u \\
u &= \frac{288}{9} \\
u &= 32
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus, the value of $u$ is $32$.

## Using a Ratio to Compute a Denominator: Word Problems

**Example:** In a basket of fruits, the ratio of bananas to grapes is $4$ to $3$. If there are $28$ bananas in the basket, how many grapes are there?

**Explanation**

The ratio of bananas to grapes is $4:3$. This means for every $4$ bananas, there are $3$ grapes.

If the number of bananas is $28$, we can write the proportion

$$
\dfrac{28}{a} = \dfrac{4}{3}
$$

where $a$ is the number of grapes.

Now, we solve for $a{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{28}{a} &= \frac{4}{3} \\
28 \cdot 3 &= 4 \cdot a \\
84 &= 4a \\
a &= \frac{84}{4} \\
a &= 21
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the basket contains $21$ grapes.

## Using a Ratio to Compute a Denominator In Terms of an Unknown Parameter

**Example:** Laptop screen $P$ has length $32$ and width $s$. Laptop screen $Q$ has width $20$ and the same length-to-width ratio as laptop screen $P$. What is the **length** of laptop screen $Q$ in terms of $s$?

**Explanation**

The ratio of length to width for laptop screen $P$ is

$$
\dfrac{32}{s}
$$

Since laptop screen $Q$ has the same ratio, we set up the proportion

$$
\dfrac{32}{s} = \dfrac{L_Q}{20}
$$

where $L_Q$ is the length of laptop screen $Q$.

Next, we solve for $L_Q{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{32}{s} &= \frac{L_{Q}}{20} \\
\frac{32}{s} \cdot 20 &= L_{Q} \\
L_{Q} &= \frac{32 \cdot 20}{s} \\
L_{Q} &= \frac{640}{s}
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus, the length of laptop screen $Q$ is

$$
\dfrac{640}{s}
$$

## Finding a Ratio to Compute a Denominator

**Example:** A teacher distributes $12$ pencils and $18$ pens to students in a fixed ratio. Later, she prepares another set using the same ratio of pencils to pens. If she has $20$ pencils in the second set, how many pens should she include?

**Explanation**

The ratio of pencils to pens in the first set is

$$
\dfrac{12}{18}=\dfrac{2}{3}
$$

This means that for every $2$ pencils, there are $3$ pens.

In the second set, the teacher has $20$ pencils. To keep the same ratio, we set up the proportion

$$
\dfrac{2}{3}=\dfrac{20}{x}
$$

Now, we solve for $x{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
2x &= 3 \cdot 20 \\
2x &= 60 \\
x &= \frac{60}{2} \\
x &= 30
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the teacher should include $30$ pens in the second set.
