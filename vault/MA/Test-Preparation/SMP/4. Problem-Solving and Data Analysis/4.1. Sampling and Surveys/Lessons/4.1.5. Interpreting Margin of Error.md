## Introduction

In this topic, we will practice drawing conclusions based on the margin of error for population proportions and evaluate what they suggest about the accuracy and reliability of survey results.

---

<a id="drawing-conclusions-from-the-margin-of-error-of-a-population-proportion"></a>
## Drawing Conclusions From the Margin of Error of a Population Proportion

**Example:** A health survey estimated that the proportion of adults in a city who meet weekly exercise guidelines is $0.36$, with a margin of error of $0.04$.

Based on this information, what conclusion can reasonably be made about true population proportion?

**Explanation**

The estimated proportion of adults meeting exercise guidelines is $0.36$ with a margin of error of $0.04$. We calculate the plausible bounds as follows:

$$
\text{Lower bound}=0.36-0.04=0.32
$$

$$
\text{Upper bound}=0.36+0.04=0.40
$$

The margin of error provides a plausible interval for the *population proportion*, not an exact value, so values outside this interval are **not** considered plausible for the true proportion.

Therefore, it is plausible that the true proportion is between $0.32$ and $0.40$.

## Interpreting the Margin of Error of a Population Proportion

**Example:** In a survey, a random sample of $1{,}200$ households with cable television was selected. Based on the survey, it is estimated that $38\%$ of all households with cable television watch sports channels regularly, with a margin of error of $3.5\%$.

Which of the following conclusions about all households with cable television is most reasonable?

1. Between $34.5\%$ and $41.5\%$ of all households with cable television watch sports channels regularly.
2. More than $41.5\%$ of all households with cable television watch sports channels regularly.
3. Since the survey included households with cable television and not just those who watch sports channels, no conclusion can be made.
4. Since the survey did not include every household that watches sports channels, no conclusion can be made.

**Explanation**

The estimated proportion of households that watch sports channels regularly is $38\%$ with a margin of error of $3.5\%$. We calculate the plausible bounds as follows:

$$
\text{Lower bound} = 38\% - 3.5\% = 34.5\%
$$

$$
\text{Upper bound} = 38\% + 3.5\% = 41.5\%
$$

The margin of error bounds the *population proportion*, not individual households, so it is normal that not every household is included in the sample. The results allow us to make inferences about the whole population of households with cable television.

With that in mind, we have the following:

- Statement I is true, because the plausible interval is $34.5\%$ to $41.5\%$.
- Statement II is false, because the upper bound is exactly $41.5\%$, not greater.
- Statement III is false, because the sample was designed to represent households with cable television, so conclusions can be drawn.
- Statement IV is false, because not surveying every household is typical; that is precisely why margins of error are reported.

Therefore, the correct answer is "I only."

## Interpreting the Margin of Error of the Mean

**Example:** A sleep researcher measured the nightly sleep duration of adults. A random sample of $520$ adults was analyzed, and the estimated mean sleep duration was $7.3$ hours, with a margin of error of $0.4$ hours.

Which of the following interpretations is most reasonable?

1. Plausible values for the mean sleep duration of all adults are less than $6.9$ hours or greater than $7.7$ hours.
2. The mean sleep duration of all adults is less than $7$ hours.
3. Plausible values for the mean sleep duration of all adults are between $6.9$ hours and $7.7$ hours.
4. The mean sleep duration of all adults is greater than $7$ hours.

**Explanation**

The estimated mean sleep duration is $7.3$ hours with a margin of error of $0.4$ hours. We calculate the plausible bounds as follows:

$$
\text{Lower bound} = 7.3 - 0.4 = 6.9
$$

$$
\text{Upper bound} = 7.3 + 0.4 = 7.7
$$

The margin of error bounds the *population mean*, not individual sleep durations, so it gives a plausible interval for the true average across all adults.

With that in mind, we have the following:

- Statement I is false, because values outside this range are not plausible for the mean.
- Statement II is false, because the interval includes values above $7$.
- Statement III is true, because the interval is $6.9$ to $7.7$.
- Statement IV is false, because the interval includes values below $7$.

Therefore, the correct answer is "I only."
