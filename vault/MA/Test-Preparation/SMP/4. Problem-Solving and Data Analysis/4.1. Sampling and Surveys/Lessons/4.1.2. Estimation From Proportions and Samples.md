## Making Population Inferences From Sample Proportions

**Example:** There are $60$ members in the local hiking club. A random sample of hiking club members was conducted, and each participant was asked if they planned to go on the weekend hike. Of those surveyed, $55\%$ responded that they plan to go.

Based on this survey, which of the following is the best estimate of the total number of hiking club members who plan to go?

1. $30$
2. $28$
3. $36$
4. $33$

**Explanation**

We are told that there are $60$ members in the club, and $55\%$ of the surveyed members said they plan to go.

To estimate the total number of members who plan to go, we calculate $55\%$ of $60{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
60 \cdot 55% &= 60 \cdot 0.55 \\
&= 33
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the best estimate is that $33$ members plan to go.

## Making Population Inferences From Sample Data

**Example:** A company employs $900$ workers. A random sample of $75$ workers was taken, and among those surveyed, $30$ said they bike to work.

Based on the sample data, which of the following is the best estimate of the total number of workers at the company who bike to work?

1. $355$
2. $350$
3. $360$
4. $365$

**Explanation**

We are told there are $900$ workers at the company. In a sample of $75$ workers, $30$ bike to work.

First, we find the proportion of workers in the sample who bike to work:

$$
\dfrac{30}{75} = 0.4 = 40\%
$$

So, $40\%$ of the workers in our sample bike to work.

Finally, to estimate the number in the whole company who bike to work, we multiply the total number of workers by $40\%{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
900 \cdot 40% &= 900 \cdot 0.4 \\
&= 360
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the best estimate is that $360$ workers bike to work.

## Making Inferences From Sample Data in Tabular Form

**Example:** A company randomly sampled $500$ employees about a proposed remote–work policy. The results were categorized by department and are shown below.

|  | Plan to vote<br>for<br>Plan A | Plan to vote<br>for<br>Plan B |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Tech | $160$ | $90$ |
| Non-Tech | $110$ | $140$ |

The company has a total of $10{,}000$ employees. Based on the table, what is the best estimate of the number of employees who plan to vote for Plan A?

**Explanation**

First, we total the number in the sample who plan to vote for Plan A:

$$
160 + 110 = 270
$$

Of the $500$ employees, the proportion who favor Plan A is

$$
\frac{270}{500} = 0.54 = 54\%
$$

Therefore, $54\%$ of those sampled intend to vote for Plan A.

To estimate how many of the $10{,}000$ employees favor Plan A, we scale the proportion by the population:

$$
\begin{aligned}
10,000 \cdot 54% &= 10,000 \cdot 0.54 \\
&= 5,400
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the best estimate is that $5{,}400$ employees plan to vote for Plan A.

## Making Inferences From the Sample Mean

**Example:** A pasture covers $160$ acres. To estimate the total number of cows grazing, a researcher randomly selects $8$ different $1$-acre plots and counts the cows in each plot. The numbers are $12$, $15$, $14$, $10$, $16$, $13$, $11$, and $15$.

Based on the **mean** of the sample, which of the following ranges contains the best estimate for the number of cows in the entire pasture?

1. $3{,}000$ to $3{,}500$
2. $2{,}000$ to $2{,}500$
3. $1{,}500$ to $2{,}000$
4. $2{,}500$ to $3{,}000$

**Explanation**

First, we find the mean number of cows per acre in the sample. The total number of cows in the $8$ sample acres is

$$
12 + 15 + 14 + 10 + 16 + 13 + 11 + 15 = 106
$$

Dividing by the $8$ sample acres gives the sample mean:

$$
\frac{106}{8} = 13.25
$$

This suggests an average of $13.25$ cows per acre.

Now, to estimate the total number of cows in the pasture, we multiply the mean by the total acreage:

$$
\begin{aligned}
13.25 \cdot 160 &= 2,120
\end{aligned}
$$

The number $2{,}120$ lies between $2{,}000$ and $2{,}500$. Therefore, the best estimate falls in the range $2{,}000$ to $2{,}500$.
