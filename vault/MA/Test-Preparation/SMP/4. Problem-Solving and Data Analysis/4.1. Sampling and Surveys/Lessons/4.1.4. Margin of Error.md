## Introduction

In this topic, we'll strengthen our ability to quickly determine plausible ranges for population parameters, estimate actual values within those ranges, and identify the most likely outcomes supported by the data.

---

<a id="determining-plausible-bounds"></a>
## Determining Plausible Bounds

**Example:** A school survey measured the average cost of a textbook at $20$ randomly selected bookstores in New York. The mean price was estimated to be $\[math]7.75$, with a margin of error of $\[/math]0.30$.

Which of the following is a plausible statement about the mean price of a textbook for all bookstores in New York?

1. It is either less than $\[math]7.45$ or greater than $\[/math]8.05$.
2. It is less than $\[math]7.45$.
3. It is between $\[/math]7.45$ and $\[math]8.05$.
4. It is greater than $\[/math]8.05$.

**Explanation**

The estimated mean is $\[math]7.75$ with a margin of error of $\[/math]0.30$, so we calculate the plausible bounds as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
Lower bound &= 7.75 - 0.30 = 7.45 \\
Upper bound &= 7.75 + 0.30 = 8.05
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, plausible values of the mean price are between $\[math]7.45$ and $\[/math]8.05$.

<a id="calculating-plausible-population-values"></a>
## Calculating Plausible Population Values

**Example:** A national park survey recorded $24{,}000$ trees in a management zone. To estimate the percentage that are pine, a random sample was taken. The percentage of pine trees was estimated to be $47\%$, with a margin of error of $5\%$.

If $r$ is the actual number of pine trees in the zone, which of the following is most plausible?

1. $r > 13{,}000$
2. $10{,}080 < r < 12{,}480$
3. $9{,}500 < r < 9{,}900$
4. $r < 10{,}000$

**Explanation**

The estimate is $47\%$ with a margin of error of $5\%$, so we compute the plausible bounds as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
Lower percentage &= 47% - 5% = 42% \\
Upper percentage &= 47% + 5% = 52%
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we convert these percentages to totals using $24{,}000$ total trees:

$$
\begin{aligned}
Lower total &= 42% \cdot 24,000 \\
&= 0.42 \cdot 24,000 \\
&= 10,080 \\
Upper total &= 52% \cdot 24,000 \\
&= 0.52 \cdot 24,000 \\
&= 12,480
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, a plausible range for the actual number of pine trees is between $10{,}080$ and $12{,}480$.

## Determining the Most Plausible Population Value

**Example:** From a population of $64{,}000$ residents, $1{,}100$ were chosen at random and surveyed about a proposed car-free weekend. Based on the survey, it is estimated that $52\%$ of people in the population support the plan, with an associated margin of error of $3\%$.

Based on these results, which of the following is a plausible value for the total number of people in the population who support the plan?

1. $30{,}000$
2. $36{,}500$
3. $27{,}000$
4. $33{,}600$

**Explanation**

The estimate is $52\%$ with a margin of error of $3\%$, so we compute the plausible bounds as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
Lower percentage &= 52% - 3% = 49% \\
Upper percentage &= 52% + 3% = 55%
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we convert these percentages to totals using $64{,}000$ people:

$$
\begin{aligned}
Lower total &= 49% \cdot 64,000 = 0.49 \cdot 64,000 = 31,360 \\
Upper total &= 55% \cdot 64,000 = 0.55 \cdot 64,000 = 35,200
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, a plausible value for the total number of people in the population who support the plan must be between $31{,}360$ and $35{,}200$.

From the given options, the answer that lies in this range is $33{,}600$.
