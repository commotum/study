## Introduction

This topic provides practice in analyzing sampling methods for bias and validity. We will determine whether the sampling method introduces bias, evaluate flawed sampling strategies, and assess the trustworthiness of conclusions based on how the sample was gathered.

---

<a id="identifying-reasons-for-bias-in-samples"></a>
## Identifying Reasons for Bias in Samples

**Example:** The members of a city council wanted to assess the opinions of all city residents about converting an open field into a dog park. The council surveyed a sample of $500$ city residents who own dogs. The survey showed that the majority of those sampled were in favor of the dog park. Which of the following is true about the city council's survey?

- It shows that the majority of city residents are in favor of the dog park.
- The survey sample should have included more residents who are dog owners.
- The survey sample should have consisted entirely of residents who do not own dogs.
- The survey sample is biased because it is not representative of all city residents.

**Explanation**

The sample for this survey was drawn only from city residents who own dogs. This group is not representative of the entire city population, because they are more likely
to favor a dog park than non–dog owners.

Therefore, the results cannot be generalized to all city residents. The correct conclusion is that **the survey sample is biased because it is not representative of all city residents.**

## Identifying Reasons for Bias Given Survey Data

**Example:** A local newspaper ran an online poll asking, “Should taxes be raised to fund new schools?” The results showed that $19\%$ of respondents said “Yes,” and $80\%$ said “No.” Which of the following best explains why these results may not represent the views of all residents?

1. There were not enough “Yes” responses.
2. The poll did not include school officials.
3. Those who responded to the poll were not randomly selected.
4. The poll was not advertised well.

**Explanation**

The people who chose to respond to the online poll were self-selected. This means that the results are biased because they were not drawn randomly from all residents, and the people most likely to respond may have strong opinions on the topic.

Therefore, the results cannot be considered representative of all residents because **those who responded to the poll were not randomly selected.**

- [ ] A. The broadcast did not provide enough time for people to vote.
- [ ] B. The percentages do not add up to $100%$, so the conclusions are invalid.
- [ ] C. There were not $50%$ “Yes” responses and $50%$ “No” responses.
- [ ] D. Those who responded to the poll were not a random sample of all sports fans.
- [ ] E. The poll proves that all sports fans oppose lowering ticket prices.

## Identifying Unbiased Sampling Methods

**Example:** A hospital wants to estimate the average number of hours nurses work per week.

Which of the following sampling methods is most likely to produce unbiased results?

1. Randomly selecting nurses from the hospital’s complete employee database.
2. Randomly selecting nurses from the list of those assigned to the emergency department.
3. Randomly selecting nurses from the list of those who attended a recent training workshop.

**Explanation**

An unbiased sample is one in which every member of the population has an equal chance of being included. To achieve this, we must select individual units at random from a complete list of the entire population.

Let's examine each sampling method in turn.

- **Method I:** Randomly selecting from the hospital’s complete employee database gives every nurse an equal chance of being chosen, so this method can produce unbiased results.
- **Method II:** Randomly selecting from the emergency department list is biased because it excludes nurses working in other departments, leading to *undercoverage*. These nurses may have different work schedules.
- **Method III:** Randomly selecting from recent training attendees is biased because it excludes nurses who did not attend that training, also leading to *undercoverage*. These nurses may have different workloads.

Therefore, the correct answer is "I only."

Note that Methods II and III are examples of *undercoverage*, where certain members of the population are systematically excluded from the sample.

## Identifying True Statements From Survey Descriptions

**Example:** To estimate the proportion of citizens who support building a new stadium, a polling group asked $45$ attendees at a sports game. Of these $45$ people, $82\%$ were in favor. Which of the following statements **must** be true?

1. A sports game is the best location to gather opinions on stadiums.
2. The sampling method is flawed and may produce a biased estimate of public support.
3. The sample size should have been exactly $100$.
4. The sampling method is valid and random.

**Explanation**

The polling group surveyed only people at a sports game. This sample is not representative of all citizens, since these individuals are more likely to support the stadium than those who do not attend the sports game.

Therefore, **the sampling method is flawed and may produce a biased estimate of public support.**
