## Introduction

To sketch the graph of

$$
y=\dfrac{2}{x}
$$

we start with the graph of

$$
y=\dfrac1x
$$

and stretch it by a scale factor of $2$ parallel to the $y$-axis, as shown below.

![](<../Source/5.2.3. Combining Graph Transformations of Reciprocal Functions/Images/2936abad3acbc10246cdae3489fdb5fc.png>)

Note the following:

- The point $(1,1)$ on $y=\dfrac1x$ is mapped to the point $(1,2)$ on $y=\dfrac2x$.
- The point $(-1,-1)$ on $y=\dfrac1x$ is mapped to the point $(-1,-2)$ on $y=\dfrac2x$.

---

<a id="applying-a-stretch-with-a-scale-factor-less-than-one"></a>
## Applying a Stretch With a Scale Factor Less Than One

Similarly, to sketch the graph of

$$
y=\dfrac{1}{2x}
$$

we take the graph of

$$
y=\dfrac1x
$$

and stretch it by a scale factor of

$$
\dfrac12
$$

parallel to the $y$-axis.

![](<../Source/5.2.3. Combining Graph Transformations of Reciprocal Functions/Images/9c30a8687e5659b219ce739afbeb756e.png>)

Note the following:

- The point $(1,1)$ on $y=\dfrac1x$ is mapped to the point $\left(1,\dfrac12\right)$ on $y=\dfrac{1}{2x}$.
- The point $(-1,-1)$ on $y=\dfrac1x$ is mapped to the point $\left(-1,-\dfrac12\right)$ on $y=\dfrac{1}{2x}$.

<a id="graphing-a-transformed-reciprocal-function"></a>
## Graphing a Transformed Reciprocal Function

**Example:** Graph the function $y = -\dfrac{2}{x-1}-1$.

**Explanation**

To plot

$$
y = -\dfrac{2}{x-1}-1
$$

we apply the following steps:

- Take the curve $y=-\dfrac{1}{x}$.

![](<../Source/5.2.3. Combining Graph Transformations of Reciprocal Functions/Images/2c9172fb7cbb0bd0e0cc81101d0b9923.png>)

- Translate it by one unit to the right to get the graph of $y=-\dfrac{1}{x-1}$.![](<../Source/5.2.3. Combining Graph Transformations of Reciprocal Functions/Images/4be35d35282b67338f8ea012d44351e3.png>)
Notice that the vertical asymptote is now $x=1$.
- Then, stretch the result by a scale factor of $2$ parallel to the $y$-axis to get the graph of $y=-\dfrac{2}{x-1}$.

![](<../Source/5.2.3. Combining Graph Transformations of Reciprocal Functions/Images/ad086b825d99afce3979b91cf20c12ff.png>)

- Finally, translate it by $1$ unit down to get the graph of $y=-\dfrac{2}{x-1}-1$.![](<../Source/5.2.3. Combining Graph Transformations of Reciprocal Functions/Images/9e3da682b291a0c948558aca2c22a84a.png>)
Notice that the horizontal asymptote is now $y=-1$.

<a id="solving-for-some-unknowns-given-the-graph-of-a-transformed-reciprocal-function"></a>
## Solving for Some Unknowns Given the Graph of a Transformed Reciprocal Function

**Example:** The diagram above shows the graph of $y = \dfrac{a}{x-b}+k$. What is the value of $a+b+k$?

![](<../Source/5.2.3. Combining Graph Transformations of Reciprocal Functions/Images/daf6141f9c6d989c76e8b22ea3252d2b.png>)

**Explanation**

First, notice that the horizontal asymptote of the graph is $y=-1$, while the vertical asymptote is $x=2$. This means that $k=-1$ and $b=2$, and we can re-write our function as

$$
y = \dfrac{a}{x-2}+(-1) = \dfrac{a}{x-2}-1
$$

Also, we are given that the point $(3,2)$ lies on the graph. Substituting its coordinates into the equation above, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
y &= \frac{a}{x - 2} - 1 \\
2 &= \frac{a}{3 - 2} - 1 \\
3 &= \frac{a}{1} \\
a &= 3
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally,

$$
a+b+k = 3+2+(-1) = 4
$$
