## Introduction

Any decimal containing *repeating* digits is rational. This means that every decimal containing repeating digits can be written as the ratio of two integers (i.e., as a fraction).

Let's see how to convert the decimal $0.3333\ldots$ to a fraction. We proceed in four steps:

- **Step 1:** We define our number as $x$:
$x = 0.3333 \ldots$
Notice that the *repeating digit* is $3$.
- **Step 2:** We multiply our number by $10$ to create a number with the same digits after the decimal point as $x$:
$10 \cdot x|= 10 \cdot 0.3333…; 10x|= 3.3333…$
So, we now have the following two numbers:
$10x|= 3.3333…; x|= 0.3333…$
These two numbers have the same repeating digit after the decimal point. In other words, they have the same "tail." This is crucial for the next step.

- **Step 3**: We subtract $x$ from $10x$:
$10x|= 3.3333…; - x|= 0.3333…; 9x|= 3$
Notice that the tails canceled out, leaving us with the equation $9x=3$ that contains only integers.

- **Step 4**: Finally, we solve the equation for $x$:
$9x|= 3; \frac{9x}{9}|= \frac{3}{9}; x|= \frac{1}{3}$

Therefore, we conclude that

$$
0.3333 \ldots = \dfrac{1}{3}
$$

## Writing Decimals Containing One Repeating Digit as Fractions

**Example:** Write the repeating decimal $5.7777 \ldots$ as a fraction in its lowest terms.

**Explanation**

We proceed in four steps.

**Step 1:** First, we define our number as $x$:

$$
x=5.7777 \ldots
$$

Notice that the repeating digit is $7$.

**Step 2:** We multiply our number by $10$ to create a number with the same digits after the decimal point as $x$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
10 \cdot x &= 10 \cdot 5.7777… \\
10x &= 57.7777…
\end{aligned}
$$

**Step 3:** We subtract $x$ from $10x$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
10x &= 57.7777… \\
- x &= 5.7777… \\
9x &= 52
\end{aligned}
$$

**Step 4:** Finally, we solve the equation for $x$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
9x &= 52 \\
\frac{9x}{9} &= \frac{52}{9} \\
x &= \frac{52}{9}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, we conclude that

$$
5.7777 \ldots = \dfrac{52}{9}
$$

## Writing Decimals Containing Multiple Repeating Digit as Fractions

**Example:** Express $0.\overline{23}$ as a fraction in its lowest terms.

**Explanation**

We're given the repeating decimal

$$
0.\overline{23} = 0.232323\ldots
$$

To express this as a fraction, we proceed in four steps.

**Step 1:** First, we define our number as $x$:

$$
x=0.2323 \ldots
$$

Notice that the repeating digits are $2$ and $3$.

**Step 2:** Since we have two repeating digits, we multiply our number by $10^{2}=100$ to create a number with the same digits after the decimal point as $x$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
100 \cdot x &= 100 \cdot 0.2323… \\
100x &= 23.2323…
\end{aligned}
$$

**Step 3**: We subtract $x$ from $100x$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
100x &= 23.2323… \\
- x &= 0.2323… \\
99x &= 23
\end{aligned}
$$

**Step 4:** Finally, we solve the equation for $x$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
99x &= 23 \\
\frac{99x}{99} &= \frac{23}{99} \\
x &= \frac{23}{99}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, we conclude that

$$
0.2323 \ldots = \dfrac{23}{99}
$$

## Decimals With Repeating and Non-Repeating Digits

Consider the following decimal:

$$
0.67777\ldots
$$

This decimal contains a *non-repeating* digit $(6)$ followed by a *repeating digit* $(7)$.

To express this decimal as a fraction, we need to follow five steps. The extra step deals with the non-repeating digit.

- **Step 1:** We define our number as $x$:
$x = 0.67777 \ldots$

- **Step 2:** Since we have one *non-repeating* digit, we multiply $x$ by $10^1 = 10$ to create a number containing only repeating digits after the decimal point. This gives
$10x|= 6.777…$.

- **Step 3**. Since we have one *repeating* digit, we multiply $10x$ by $10^{1}=10$ to create a number with the same digits after the decimal point as $10x$:
$10 \cdot 10x|= 10 \cdot 6.77777…; 100x|= 67.777…$

- **Step 4:** We subtract $10x$ from $100x$:
$100x|= 67.7777…; - 10x|= 6.7777…; 90x|= 61$
- **Step 5:** Finally, we solve the equation for $x$:
$90x|= 61; \frac{90x}{90}|= \frac{61}{90}; x|= \frac{61}{90}$

Therefore, we conclude that

$$
0.67777 \ldots = \dfrac{61}{90}
$$

## Writing Decimals With Repeating and Non-Repeating Digits as Fractions

**Example:** Express $0.26\overline{8}$ as a fraction in its lowest terms.

**Explanation**

We're given the repeating decimal

$$
0.26\overline{8} = 0.268888\ldots
$$

To express this as a fraction, we proceed in five steps.

**Step 1:** We define our number as $x$:

$$
x=0.268888 \ldots
$$

Notice that we have two non-repeating digits after the decimal point ($2$ and $6$) followed by the repeating digit $8$.

**Step 2:** Since we have two *non-repeating* digits, we multiply $x$ by $10^2 = 100$ to create a number containing only repeating digits after the decimal point. This gives the following:

$$
\begin{aligned}
100x &= 26.8888…
\end{aligned}
$$

**Step 3**. Since we have one *repeating* digit, we multiply $100x$ by $10^{1}=10$ to create a number with the same digits after the decimal point as $100x$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
10 \cdot 100x &= 10 \cdot 26.8888… \\
1000x &= 268.8888…
\end{aligned}
$$

**Step 4:** We subtract $100x$ from $1000x$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
1000x &= 268.8888… \\
- 100x &= 26.8888… \\
900x &= 242
\end{aligned}
$$

**Step 5:** Finally, we solve the equation for $x$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
900x &= 242 \\
\frac{900x}{900} &= \frac{242}{900} \\
x &= \frac{242}{900} \\
x &= \frac{121}{450}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, we conclude that

$$
0.268888 \ldots = \dfrac{121}{450}
$$
