## Introduction

Consider a curve $y=f(x)$ between two points $x=a$ and $x=b$. The **average rate of change** of $y=f(x)$ on the interval $x\in[a,b]$ is computed as follows:

$$
\dfrac{\Delta y}{\Delta x} = \dfrac{\text{change in}y}{\text{change in}x} = \dfrac{f(b)-f(a)}{b-a}
$$

Geometrically, the average rate of change represents the slope of the **secant line** which passes through $(a,f(a))$ and $(b,f(b))$.

![](<../Source/3.1.13. The Average Rate of Change of a Function/Images/ea5624038b4812035f78f5f01fdd49f2.png>)

**Note:** In general, a secant is a line that intersects a curve at two or more distinct points.

<a id="calculating-the-average-rate-of-change-of-a-function"></a>
## Calculating the Average Rate of Change of a Function

**Example:** A curve is defined as $y = 2x^2$. Find the average rate of change of $y$ over the interval $[0,5]$.

**Explanation**

Let

$$
f(x)=2x^2
$$

Applying the formula for the average rate of change, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{Δy}{Δx} &= (f(b) - f(a))/(b - a) \\
&= (f(5) - f(0))/(5 - (0)) \\
&= ((2(5)^{2}) - (2(0)^{2}))/(5) \\
&= \frac{50}{5} \\
&= 10
\end{aligned}
$$

## Calculating the Average Rate of Change Given a Table

**Example:** Find the average rate of change of the function $f(x)$ on the interval $[-1,2]$ given the following table of values for $f(x)$:

| $x$ | $\,-1\,$ | $\,0\,$ | $\,1\,$ | $\,2\,$ | $\,3\,$ | $\,4\,$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $f(x)$ | $\,-3\,$ | $\,0\,$ | $\,1\,$ | $\,6\,$ | $\,0\,$ | $\,4\,$ |

**Explanation**

Using the formula for the average rate of change, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{Δf}{Δx} &= (f(b) - f(a))/(b - a) \\
&= (f(2) - f(-1))/(2 - (-1)) \\
&= (6 - (-3))/(2 - (-1)) \\
&= 3
\end{aligned}
$$

## Calculating the Average Rate of Change: Word Problem

**Example:** Dave plants a tree in his garden. After $x$ months the tree is $2\sqrt x + 1$ feet high. What is the average rate of growth of the tree over the first six months?

**Explanation**

Let

$$
f(x)=2\sqrt{x}+1
$$

be the height of the tree after $x$ months. The average rate of growth of the tree over the first six months is the average value of $f(x)$ on the interval $[0,6]$.

Applying the formula, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{Δf}{Δx} &= (f(b) - f(a))/(b - a) \\
&= (f(6) - f(0))/(6) \\
&= (2\sqrt{6} + 1 - (2\sqrt{0} + 1))/(6) \\
&= \frac{2\sqrt{6}}{6} \\
&= \frac{\sqrt{6}}{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the average rate of growth is

$$
\dfrac {\sqrt 6} 3 \approx 0.82
$$

feet per month.

## Determining the Interval Over Which the Average Rate of Change is Zero

**Example:** Find the interval where the average rate of change of $f(x)$ equals $0$ given the following table of values for $f(x)$:

| $x$ | $\,-2\,$ | $\,-1\,$ | $\,0\,$ | $\,1\,$ | $\,2\,$ | $\,5\,$ | $\,7\,$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $f(x)$ | $\,2\,$ | $\,1\,$ | $\,4\,$ | $\,-5\,$ | $\,-7\,$ | $\,2\,$ | $\,-2\,$ |

**Explanation**

We have

$$
\dfrac{\Delta f}{\Delta x}=0
$$

if and only if

$$
\Delta f = 0
$$

So

$$
Δf = 0; f(b) - f(a) = 0; f(b) = f(a)
$$

This means we have to find an interval $[a,b]$ such that

$$
f(a)=f(b)
$$

Since

$$
f(-2)=f(5)=2
$$

we conclude that

$$
\dfrac{\Delta f}{\Delta x}=0
$$

on the interval $[-2,5]$.
