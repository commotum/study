## Introduction

A parabola can be defined as a set of points that satisfy the **focus-directrix property.**

To demonstrate this property, consider the diagram below.

![](<../Source/7.1.5. The Focus-Directrix Property of a Parabola/Images/ad1d8aa4638529d0e8715c24365a8d4f.png>)

The point

$$
F\left(\dfrac12,0\right)
$$

is called the **focus,** and the vertical line

$$
x=-\dfrac 1 2
$$

is known as the **directrix**. Every parabola has a *unique* focus and a *unique* directrix associated with it.

The focus-directrix property states that for any point $P(x,y)$ on a parabola, the distance from $P$ to the focus $F$ equals the perpendicular distance from $P$ to the directrix line, i.e.,

$$
\begin{vmatrix}PF & = & PD\end{vmatrix}
$$

Let's use this to determine the equation of the parabola on the diagram above.

First, we find $\mid PF \mid ^2$ using the distance formula.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mid PF \mid ^{2} &= (x - \frac{1}{2})^{2} + (y - 0)^{2} \\
&= (x - \frac{1}{2})^{2} + y^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we determine $\mid PD \mid ^2$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mid PD \mid ^{2} &= (x - (-\frac{1}{2}))^{2} \\
&= (x + \frac{1}{2})^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Using the focus-directrix property, we have

$$
\begin{vmatrix}PF & = & PD\end{vmatrix}
$$

. Hence,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\begin{vmatrix}PF & ^{2} &= & PD\end{vmatrix}^{2} \\
(x - \frac{1}{2})^{2} + y^{2} &= (x + \frac{1}{2})^{2} \\
x^{2} - x + \frac{1}{4} + y^{2} &= x^{2} + x + \frac{1}{4} \\
y^{2} &= 2x
\end{aligned}
$$

The parabola on the diagram has the equation $y^2 = 2x$.

## Finding the Equation of a Left-Opening or Right-Opening Parabola From a Diagram

**Example:** The diagram above shows a parabola with focus $F(-4,0)$ and directrix $x=4$. What is the equation of the parabola?

![](<../Source/7.1.5. The Focus-Directrix Property of a Parabola/Images/5a6301020f3af91ffebf70ec1be37e33.png>)

**Explanation**

The focus-directrix property states that any parabola can be defined as the set of points $P(x,y)$ which are the same distance from both the focus and the directrix, i.e.,

$$
\begin{vmatrix}PF & = & PD\end{vmatrix}
$$

Let's use this to determine the equation of the parabola shown in the diagram above.

First, we find $\mid PF \mid ^2$ using the distance formula:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mid PF \mid ^{2} &= (x - (-4))^{2} + (y - 0)^{2} \\
&= (x + 4)^{2} + y^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we determine $\mid PD \mid ^2$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mid PD \mid ^{2} &= (x - 4)^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Using the focus-directrix property, we have

$$
\begin{vmatrix}PF & = & PD\end{vmatrix}
$$

Hence,

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x + 4)^{2} + y^{2} &= (x - 4)^{2} \\
x^{2} + 8x + 16 + y^{2} &= x^{2} - 8x + 16 \\
y^{2} &= -16x
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the equation of the parabola is $y^2 =-16x$.

## Finding the Equation of a Upward-Opening or Downward-Opening Parabola From a Diagram

**Example:** The parabola shown above has focus $F\left(0,4\right)$ and directrix $y=-4$. What is the equation of the parabola?

![](<../Source/7.1.5. The Focus-Directrix Property of a Parabola/Images/67a6a125b47ed3ea663e1ef8b2bf0125.png>)

**Explanation**

The focus-directrix property states that any parabola can be defined as the set of points $P(x,y)$ which are the same distance from both the focus and the directrix, i.e.,

$$
\begin{vmatrix}PF & = & PD\end{vmatrix}
$$

Let's use this to determine the equation of the parabola shown in the diagram above.

First, we find $\mid PF \mid ^2$ using the distance formula:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mid PF \mid ^{2} &= (x - 0)^{2} + (y - 4)^{2} \\
&= x^{2} + (y - 4)^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we determine $\mid PD \mid ^2{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mid PD \mid ^{2} &= (y - (-4))^{2} \\
&= (y + 4)^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Using the focus-directrix property, we have

$$
\begin{vmatrix}PF & = & PD\end{vmatrix}
$$

Hence,

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^{2} + (y - 4)^{2} &= (y + 4)^{2} \\
x^{2} + y^{2} - 8y + 16 &= y^{2} + 8y + 16 \\
x^{2} &= 16y
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the equation of the parabola is $x^2 =16y$.

## Finding the Equation of a Left-Opening or Right-Opening Parabola Given Its Focus and Directrix

**Example:** A parabola has focus $F\left(3, 0\right)$ and directrix $x = -1$. What is the equation of the parabola?

**Explanation**

The focus-directrix property states that any parabola can be defined as the set of points $P(x,y)$ which are the same distance from both the focus and the directrix, i.e.,

$$
\begin{vmatrix}PF & = & PD\end{vmatrix}
$$

Let's use this to determine the equation of the parabola.

First, we find $\mid PF \mid ^2$ using the distance formula:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mid PF \mid ^{2} &= (x - 3)^{2} + (y - 0)^{2} \\
&= (x - 3)^{2} + y^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we determine $\mid PD \mid ^2$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mid PD \mid ^{2} &= (x + 1)^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Using the focus-directrix property, we have

$$
\begin{vmatrix}PF & = & PD\end{vmatrix}
$$

Hence,

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x - 3)^{2} + y^{2} &= (x + 1)^{2} \\
x^{2} - 6x + 9 + y^{2} &= x^{2} + 2x + 1 \\
y^{2} &= 8x - 8 \\
y^{2} &= 8(x - 1)
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the equation of the parabola is

$$
y^2 = 8(x -1)
$$
