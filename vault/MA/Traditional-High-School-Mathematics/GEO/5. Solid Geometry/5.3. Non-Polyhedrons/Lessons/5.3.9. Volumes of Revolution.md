## Finding the Volume Generated When a Shape is Rotated About the X-Axis

**Example:** The figure shown above is rotated $360^\circ$ about the $x$-axis. What is the volume of the corresponding solid of revolution?

![](<../Source/5.3.9. Volumes of Revolution/Images/39a2c51050dc7233646f046b262a9249.png>)

**Explanation**

Our solid of revolution is a hemisphere with radius $r=2$.

![](<../Source/5.3.9. Volumes of Revolution/Images/8ff187ea85d1e3db8bcc8bbd7250c4ae.png>)

We can find the volume of a hemisphere using the formula

$$
\begin{aligned}
V &= \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{4}{3}πr^{3} \\
&= \frac{2}{3}πr^{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

Substituting $r=2$ into the formula, we obtain

$$
V = \dfrac{2}{3}\pi (2)^3 = \dfrac{16}{3} \pi
$$

## Finding the Volume Generated When a Shape is Rotated About the Y-Axis

**Example:** The figure shown above is rotated $360^\circ$ about the $y$-axis. What is the volume of the corresponding solid of revolution?

![](<../Source/5.3.9. Volumes of Revolution/Images/30753dae8005ae13f164d05068979773.png>)

**Explanation**

Our solid of revolution is a conical frustum. The radii of the bases are $r=1$ and $R=3$, and the height of the frustum is $h=5-2=3$.

![](<../Source/5.3.9. Volumes of Revolution/Images/489104ef16d7a798815259ec9fe0a319.png>)

We can find the volume of a conical frustum using the formula

$$
V = \dfrac{\pi}{3} h (R^2 + r^2 + R r)
$$

Substituting

$$
r = 1, \qquad R = 3, \qquad h = 3
$$

into the formula, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
V &= \frac{π}{3}(3)(3^{2} + 1^{2} + 3 \cdot 1) \\
&= \frac{π}{3}(3)(13) \\
&= 13π
\end{aligned}
$$
