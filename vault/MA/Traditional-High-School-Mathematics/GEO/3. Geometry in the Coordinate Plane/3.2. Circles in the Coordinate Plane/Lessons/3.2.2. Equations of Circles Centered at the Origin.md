## Introduction

What equation represents the circle of radius $5$ centered at the origin?

![](<../Source/3.2.2. Equations of Circles Centered at the Origin/Images/ff43b17b4ad7ce1e695ae03cdea15203.png>)

Let's take a generic point $(x,y)$ on the circumference. Notice that we naturally obtain a right triangle with base $x$ and height $y$, as shown above. Therefore, by applying the Pythagorean theorem, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^{2} + y^{2} &= 5^{2} \\
x^{2} + y^{2} &= 25
\end{aligned}
$$

This straightforward result gives us the **equation of the circle** of radius $5$ centered at the origin.

Similarly, given a circle of radius $r$ centered at the origin, the general equation will be

$$
x^2 + y^2 = r^2
$$

## Finding the Center and Radius of a Circle Given Its Equation

**Example:** What is the center and radius of the circle with equation $x^2 + y^2 = 9$?

**Explanation**

The general equation of a circle with radius $r$ centered at the origin is

$$
x^2 + y^2 = r^2
$$

So, the equation

$$
x^2+y^2=9
$$

represents a circle that is centered at the origin, with radius

$$
\begin{aligned}
r^{2} &= 9 \\
r &= \sqrt{9} \\
r &= 3
\end{aligned}
$$

Note that we disregarded the negative solution for the radius since the radius of a circle must be positive.

In conclusion, the circle is centered at the origin and its radius is $3$.

## Finding the Equation of a Circle Given a Graph

**Example:** What is the equation of the circle given below?

![](<../Source/3.2.2. Equations of Circles Centered at the Origin/Images/b8d596fae077045562ff9917b3557fa7.png>)

**Explanation**

The general equation of a circle with radius $r$ centered at the origin is

$$
x^2 + y^2 = r^2
$$

From the picture, we see that our circle is centered at $(0,0)$ and has radius $r = 2$. So, the equation is

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^{2} + y^{2} &= r^{2} \\
x^{2} + y^{2} &= 2^{2} \\
x^{2} + y^{2} &= 4
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding the Equation of a Circle Given a Description

**Example:** Which equation describes the set of all points $(x,y)$ in the coordinate plane that are a distance of $4$ from the origin?

**Explanation**

The set of all points $(x,y)$ that are a distance $4$ from the origin defines a circle with radius $r=4$ centered at the origin, as shown below.

![](<../Source/3.2.2. Equations of Circles Centered at the Origin/Images/18f2f791e40fb242e0ab6db9f5bd4a70.png>)

So, the required equation is

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^{2} + y^{2} &= r^{2} \\
x^{2} + y^{2} &= 4^{2} \\
x^{2} + y^{2} &= 16
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="finding-the-equation-of-a-circle-given-endpoints-of-a-diameter"></a>
## Finding the Equation of a Circle Given Endpoints of a Diameter

**Example:** Given that the points $A$ and $B$ with coordinates $(-2,4)$ and $(2,-4)$, respectively, lie on a diameter of a circle centered at the origin, what is the equation of the circle?

![](<../Source/3.2.2. Equations of Circles Centered at the Origin/Images/3342ece562d34274187b008c4c1fbdbe.png>)

**Explanation**

The equation of a circle with radius $r$, centered at the origin, is

$$
x^2 + y^2 = r^2
$$

Now, we first calculate the diameter $AB$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
AB &= \sqrt{(-2 - 2)^{2} + (4 - (-4))^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{4^{2} + 8^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{16 + 64} \\
&= \sqrt{80} \\
&= 4\sqrt{5}
\end{aligned}
$$

Since $\overline{AB}$ is a diameter, the radius is

$$
r=\dfrac{AB}{2} = \dfrac {4\sqrt{5}}{2}=2\sqrt5
$$

So, the equation of the circle is

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^{2} + y^{2} &= r^{2} \\
x^{2} + y^{2} &= (2\sqrt{5})^{2} \\
x^{2} + y^{2} &= 20
\end{aligned}
$$
