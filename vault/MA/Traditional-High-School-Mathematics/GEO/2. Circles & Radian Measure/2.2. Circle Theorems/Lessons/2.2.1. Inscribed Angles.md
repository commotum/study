## Introduction

Given a circle, an **inscribed angle** is an angle whose vertex lies on the circumference and whose sides are chords that meet at that vertex.

In the diagram below, the points $P$, $A$, and $B$ define the inscribed angle $\angle APB$.

![](<../Source/2.2.1. Inscribed Angles/Images/9b5540897b223e7a01337b1ad69d8955.png>)

The arc formed by the endpoints of an inscribed angle is called an **intercepted arc**. Here, the endpoints $A$ and $B$ determine the intercepted arc $\overset{\frown}{AB}$.

Remember that the measure of an arc is always equal to the measure of the corresponding central angle. Here, we have

$$
m\overset{\frown}{AB} = m\angle{AOB}
$$

![](<../Source/2.2.1. Inscribed Angles/Images/c28e079e17287035ddb5fec15f97240a.png>)

## Identifying Inscribed Angles and Intercepted Arcs

**Example:** Consider the diagram above. Which of the following statements are true?

![](<../Source/2.2.1. Inscribed Angles/Images/d4a22f2cdfeb016d139c62fd485537bb.png>)

1. $\angle{ACB}$ intercepts the arc $\overset{\frown}{AC}$
2. $\angle{AOC}$ is an inscribed angle
3. $\angle{ACB}$ is an inscribed angle

**Explanation**

Let's examine the statements one by one.

- Statement I is false. The angle $\angle ACB$ intercepts the arc $\overset{\frown}{AB}$, as shown below.

![](<../Source/2.2.1. Inscribed Angles/Images/4e3033d2be08ee9403f82c40d05937de.png>)

- Statement II is false. The angle $\angle{AOC}$ is a central angle of the circle, not an inscribed angle.
- Statement III is true. Notice that $\angle{ACB}$ is formed by two chords, and its vertex lies on the circumference. Hence, $\angle{ACB}$ is an inscribed angle.

Therefore, the correct answer is "III only."

<a id="the-inscribed-angle-theorem"></a>
## The Inscribed Angle Theorem

The **inscribed angle theorem** states the following:

> *The measure of an inscribed angle is half the measure of the central angle of the same intercepted arc.*

To illustrate, consider the diagram below.

![](<../Source/2.2.1. Inscribed Angles/Images/9363c6e95e2e072ee12b76e39792a042.png>)

By the inscribed angle theorem, we have

$$
m \angle APB = \dfrac{1}{2} m \angle AOB
$$

Therefore,

$$
m \angle APB = \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 100^\circ = 50^\circ
$$

This gives the following picture.

![](<../Source/2.2.1. Inscribed Angles/Images/9553e0505409858394ee9f06bdcb5859.png>)

<a id="applying-the-inscribed-angle-theorem-in-simple-situations"></a>
## Applying the Inscribed Angle Theorem in Simple Situations

**Example:** If $m\overset{\frown}{CD}= 110^\circ,\,$ then find the measure of $m\angle CED$.

![](<../Source/2.2.1. Inscribed Angles/Images/f6a4084b98585689b2c5157cf83314e6.png>)

**Explanation**

Since we're told that

$$
m\overset{\frown}{CD}= 110^\circ,\,
$$

we can add this information to the diagram:

![](<../Source/2.2.1. Inscribed Angles/Images/eba1d4b83e1726f291f89ba469f21339.png>)

According to the inscribed angle theorem, the measure of an inscribed angle is half the measure of its intercepted arc. So,

$$
\begin{aligned}
m∠CED &= \frac{1}{2}mCD^(⌢) \\
&= \frac{1}{2} \cdot 110^{∘} \\
&= 55^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Another Example of the Inscribed Angle Theorem

As a second example, let's consider the following diagram:

![](<../Source/2.2.1. Inscribed Angles/Images/96b118bd147c8e9a80bffb5182518451.png>)

Since $\angle P$ and $\angle Q$ are inscribed angles with the same intercepted arc, we have that

$$
m \angle P = m \angle Q
$$

Therefore, $x=y$, and we now have the following picture.

![](<../Source/2.2.1. Inscribed Angles/Images/c740f05349cf1a6f65fab9e7666e720e.png>)

Moreover, since

$$
m\overset{\frown}{AB}=80^\circ
$$

we have that

$$
m\angle AOB = 80^\circ
$$

and by the inscribed angle theorem,

$$
\begin{aligned}
m∠P = m∠Q &= \frac{1}{2} \cdot m∠AOB \\
&= \frac{1}{2} \cdot 80^{∘} \\
&= 40^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

Filling in this information, we get the following picture.

![](<../Source/2.2.1. Inscribed Angles/Images/54eaea43844c9dcd67ff778241df74de.png>)

## Applying the Inscribed Angle Theorem in More Advanced Situations

**Example:** Given the circle depicted below, what is the value of $x$?

![](<../Source/2.2.1. Inscribed Angles/Images/5704f170a898062d9c27fef4197c4c3b.png>)

**Explanation**

Since the inscribed angles $\angle FDC$ and $\angle FEC$ intercept the same arc $\overset{\frown}{FC}$, their measures are the same.

![](<../Source/2.2.1. Inscribed Angles/Images/d5d9020e9a2b20fd494ea2b496ec7deb.png>)

Therefore, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
m∠FDC &= m∠FEC \\
3x + 10^{∘} &= 40^{∘} \\
3x &= 30^{∘} \\
x &= 10^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="proving-the-inscribed-angle-theorem"></a>
## Proving the Inscribed Angle Theorem

Let's now prove the inscribed angle theorem.

Consider the following diagram.

![](<../Source/2.2.1. Inscribed Angles/Images/6b8110a580b4f7464d5e930f11e53447.png>)

We want to prove the relation

$$
m\angle BOA = 2m\angle BPA
$$

The radius $\overline{OP}$ splits $\angle BPA$ into the consecutive angles $\angle APO$ and $\angle BPO$. Let's call the measure of these angles

$$
m\angle APO = \alpha
$$

and

$$
m\angle BPO= \beta
$$

![](<../Source/2.2.1. Inscribed Angles/Images/4e8f921dc65f0b75b880b10a56c5a28d.png>)

$$
\triangle APO
$$

$$
\overline{AO}
$$

$$
\overline{PO}
$$

$$
\triangle APO
$$

$$
m\angle OAP = m\angle APO = \alpha
$$

$$
\triangle BPO
$$

$$
m\angle OBP = m\angle BPO = \beta
$$

![](<../Source/2.2.1. Inscribed Angles/Images/2cc56de3906b145a2610d1998c0b8e73.png>)

Since the sum of the interior angles of a triangle equals $180^\circ$, we have

$$
2\alpha + m\angle POA = 180^\circ\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad m\angle POA = 180^\circ - 2\alpha
$$

Similarly,

$$
2\beta + m\angle POB = 180^\circ \qquad\Rightarrow\qquad m\angle POB = 180^\circ - 2\beta
$$

Notice that the arcs $\overset{\frown}{PA}$, $\overset{\frown}{AB}$ and $\overset{\frown}{BP}$ partition the circle. Therefore, the sum of their angles must be a full turn:

$$
m \angle BOA + m\angle POA + m\angle POB = 360^\circ
$$

We substitute our expressions for $m\angle POA$ and $m\angle POB$ into the above and simplify as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
m∠BOA + m∠POA + m∠POB &= 360^{∘} \\
m∠BOA + (180^{∘} - 2α) + (180^{∘} - 2β) &= 360^{∘} \\
m∠BOA - 2α - 2β &= 0 \\
m∠BOA &= 2(α + β)
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, by the angle addition postulate, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
m∠BOA &= 2(α + β) \\
m∠BOA &= 2(m∠OAP + m∠OBP) \\
m∠BOA &= 2m∠BPA
\end{aligned}
$$
