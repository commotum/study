## Introduction

Consider the quadrilateral $ABCD$ that's inscribed in the circle below.

![](<../Source/2.2.6. Inscribed Quadrilaterals/Images/4e0a97753221eb4c65284af670a214a5.png>)

For any quadrilateral inscribed in a circle, we have the following theorem:

> *A quadrilateral can be inscribed in a circle if and only if both pairs of opposite angles are supplementary*.

In this case, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
m∠A + m∠C &= m∠B + m∠D = 180^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
75^{∘} + m∠C &= 180^{∘} \\
m∠C &= 180^{∘} - 75^{∘} \\
m∠C &= 105^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Calculating Angles of Inscribed Quadrilaterals

**Example:** In the diagram below, $m\angle BAD=2x+10^{\circ}$ and $m\angle BCD=2x-30^{\circ}$. What is the value of $x$?

![](<../Source/2.2.6. Inscribed Quadrilaterals/Images/2c9989dabe8de6689f9dcb55ad40972a.png>)

**Explanation**

Notice that $ABCD$ is a quadrilateral inscribed in a circle. So, its opposite angles are supplementary.

Therefore, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
m∠BAD + m∠BCD &= 180^{∘} \\
(2x + 10^{∘}) + (2x - 30^{∘}) &= 180^{∘} \\
4x - 20^{∘} &= 180^{∘} \\
4x &= 200^{∘} \\
x &= 50^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Identifying True Statements About Inscribed Quadrilaterals

**Example:** Consider the diagram above. Which of the following statements are true?

![](<../Source/2.2.6. Inscribed Quadrilaterals/Images/c921c3647c7e5cc68e528b38477e71de.png>)

1. $ABCD$ is an inscribed quadrilateral
2. $m\angle{P}+m\angle{R} = m\angle{Q}+m\angle{S}=180^\circ$
3. Both pairs of opposite angles in $EFHG$ are supplementary

**Explanation**

Let's examine the statements in turn.

- Statement I is false. $ABCD$ is not an inscribed quadrilateral since the vertex $B$ does not lie on the circle.
- Statement II is false. If we assume that
$m\angle{P}+m\angle{R} = m\angle{Q}+m\angle{S}=180^\circ$,
then $PQRS$ must be an inscribed quadrilateral, which is not true since $S$ does not lie on the circle.
- Statement III is true. Indeed, $EFHG$ is an inscribed quadrilateral since all its vertices lie on the circle. Hence, both pairs of opposite angles must be supplementary.

Therefore, the correct answer is "III only."

<a id="applying-the-inscribed-angle-theorem"></a>
## Applying the Inscribed Angle Theorem

**Example:** In the diagram below, $m\overset{\frown}{AB}=80^\circ$ and $m\overset{\frown}{DA}=70^\circ$. Find $m\angle{BAD}$.

![](<../Source/2.2.6. Inscribed Quadrilaterals/Images/a0e6e8e6f6644fa60a78b51570ef1a7f.png>)

**Explanation**

From the picture,

$$
\begin{aligned}
mDAB^(⌢) &= mDA^(⌢) + mAB^(⌢) \\
&= 70^{∘} + 80^{∘} \\
&= 150^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

Since $\angle{BCD}$ is an inscribed angle that intercepts the arc $\overset{\frown}{DAB}$, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
m∠BCD &= \frac{1}{2}mDAB^(⌢) \\
&= \frac{1}{2} \cdot 150^{∘} \\
&= 75^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, notice that $ABCD$ is a quadrilateral inscribed in a circle. So, its opposite angles are supplementary.

Therefore, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
m∠BAD + m∠BCD &= 180^{∘} \\
m∠BAD + 75^{∘} &= 180^{∘} \\
m∠BAD &= 105^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Proving That Opposite Angles in an Inscribed Quadrilateral Are Supplementary

Let's now prove that opposite angles in an inscribed quadrilateral are supplementary. Consider the following diagram.

![](<../Source/2.2.6. Inscribed Quadrilaterals/Images/ae30effec21bef753a7dbf23a0ff7351.png>)

By the definition of supplementary angles, we must prove the relation

$$
m\angle A + m\angle C = 180^\circ
$$

Let's draw the radii $\overline{OD}$ and $\overline{OB}$.
We call $\gamma$ the measure of the angle $\angle DOB$, as shown in the diagram. Notice that $\angle DOB$ is a central angle of the intercepted arc $\overset{\frown}{DB}$ (the arc in red). Moreover, $\angle C$ is an inscribed angle on the same arc.

![](<../Source/2.2.6. Inscribed Quadrilaterals/Images/7e62003b090e3be453fa48a2b612ce63.png>)

$$
\gamma
$$

$$
m\angle C{:}
$$

$$
\gamma = 2m\angle C
$$

Now, we call $\alpha$ the measure of the reflex angle $\angle DOB$.

![](<../Source/2.2.6. Inscribed Quadrilaterals/Images/9854343ae2bb97ab6b0986c489fbda27.png>)

$$
\alpha
$$

$$
\angle A
$$

$$
\angle A
$$

$$
\angle DOB{:}
$$

$$
\alpha = 2m\angle A
$$

Notice that the angle $\angle DOB$ and the reflex $\angle DOB$ make a full turn.

![](<../Source/2.2.6. Inscribed Quadrilaterals/Images/117020d8699d08b448662f3f870269b4.png>)

$$
360^\circ{:}
$$

$$
\alpha + \gamma= 360^\circ
$$

Finally, we substitute the relations

$$
\gamma = 2m\angle C
$$

and

$$
\alpha = 2m\angle A
$$

into the above, and simplify:

$$
\begin{aligned}
α + γ &= 360^{∘} \\
2m∠A + 2m∠C &= 360^{∘} \\
2(m∠A + m∠C) &= 360^{∘} \\
m∠A + m∠C &= 180^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$
