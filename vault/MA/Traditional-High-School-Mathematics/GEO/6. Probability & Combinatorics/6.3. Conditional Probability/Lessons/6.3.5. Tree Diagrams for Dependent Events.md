## Introduction

There are $4$ blue and $2$ red marbles in a bag. Suppose that two marbles are picked consecutively *without replacement.* Can we represent all possible outcomes in such a way that makes calculating the various probabilities easy?

One way to represent all possible outcomes is to use a **tree diagram.**

First, let's define the following events:

- Let $B_1$ be the event "the first marble is blue."
- Let $R_1$ be the event "the first marble is red."
- Let $B_2$ be the event "the second marble is blue."
- Let $R_2$ be the event "the second marble is red."

We represent all possible outcomes using a tree diagram, as follows:

![](<../Source/6.3.5. Tree Diagrams for Dependent Events/Images/b189a6c5eacd885ee3f7ba509d2aa7dd.png>)

We now need to add some probabilities to our tree diagram.

The probabilities on the first level of the tree correspond to the first event. Now, since there are $6$ marbles in total, $4$ blue and $2$ red, we have

$$
P(B_1)=\dfrac46,\qquad P(R_1)=\dfrac26
$$

Let's add these probabilities to the first level of our tree diagram.

![](<../Source/6.3.5. Tree Diagrams for Dependent Events/Images/67895a3059cd6dc41cdfa1de84ecdfab.png>)

We now need to add probabilities to the second level. To demonstrate, consider the branch highlighted below.

![](<../Source/6.3.5. Tree Diagrams for Dependent Events/Images/85154e246b5907862cb855f5032e8cbd.png>)

The highlighted branch corresponds to the probability that the second marble is blue *given that* the first marble is blue, i.e., $P(B_2 \mid B_1)$. Now, if the first marble is blue, there will be $3$ blue marbles remaining out of $5$ marbles in total. Therefore,

$$
P(B_2 \mid B_1)=\dfrac35
$$

Let's add this to our diagram.

![](<../Source/6.3.5. Tree Diagrams for Dependent Events/Images/e62cd225ee24a115bb97400d61410b90.png>)

Using the same method to calculate the remaining probabilities, we get

$$
P(R_2 \mid B_1)=\dfrac25, \qquad P(B_2 \mid R_1)=\dfrac45, \qquad P(R_2 \mid R_1)=\dfrac15
$$

So, we get the following completed tree diagram.

![](<../Source/6.3.5. Tree Diagrams for Dependent Events/Images/5c276b93e5c113337f7b7922c7f47642.png>)

## Calculating the Intersection of Two Events Using a Tree Diagram

If $4$ blue and $2$ red marbles are in a bag and two marbles are picked consecutively without replacement, we get the following tree diagram.

![](<../Source/6.3.5. Tree Diagrams for Dependent Events/Images/d72a8a65597a9f9d88d3b63643503656.png>)

Suppose we wish to calculate $P(R_1\cap B_2)$, the probability that the first marble is red and the second is blue. This event is highlighted below.

![](<../Source/6.3.5. Tree Diagrams for Dependent Events/Images/e5135b7ac47609ffa95b30a8baa3bb7b.png>)

By the multiplication law, we *multiply across the branches* to calculate this probability. Thus, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(R_{1}∩B_{2}) &= P(R_{1}) \cdot P(B_{2} \mid R_{1}) \\
&= \frac{2}{6} \cdot \frac{4}{5} \\
&= \frac{8}{30} \\
&= \frac{4}{15}
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="multiplying-across-branches"></a>
## Multiplying Across Branches

**Example:** The tree diagram below represents the probabilities of the dependent events $C$ and $D$. What is $P(C'\cap D')$?

![](<../Source/6.3.5. Tree Diagrams for Dependent Events/Images/d872a4f1fce4d2465719a3eb1a121b19.png>)

**Explanation**

The multiplication law states that

$$
P(C'\cap D') = P(C')\cdot P(D' \mid C')
$$

Therefore, to compute $P(C'\cap D')$ using a tree diagram, we multiply the probabilities across the appropriate branches, shown below:

![](<../Source/6.3.5. Tree Diagrams for Dependent Events/Images/5158ef148f0f0eef4fc30b75b55b99b7.png>)

Therefore, the required probability is

$$
P(C' \cap D') = 0.25 \cdot 0.1 = 0.025
$$

## Calculating the Union of Two Events Using a Tree Diagram

If $4$ blue and $2$ red marbles are in a bag and two marbles are picked consecutively without replacement, we get the following tree diagram.

![](<../Source/6.3.5. Tree Diagrams for Dependent Events/Images/87293971caf19afbe914524f396b7930.png>)

Suppose we wish to calculate $P(R_2)$, the probability that the second marble is red. This event corresponds to *two* branches of our tree diagram, highlighted below.

![](<../Source/6.3.5. Tree Diagrams for Dependent Events/Images/68cfaa9a4d8016255eac2a7dc55f151d.png>)

By the multiplication law *and* the law of total probability, we *multiply across the branches* and *add between the branches*. Thus, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(R_{2}) &= P(R_{2}∩B_{1}) + P(R_{2}∩R_{1}) \\
&= P(B_{1})P(R_{2} \mid B_{1}) + P(R_{1})P(R_{2} \mid R_{1}) \\
&= \frac{4}{6} \cdot \frac{2}{5} + \frac{2}{6} \cdot \frac{1}{5} \\
&= \frac{8}{30} + \frac{2}{30} \\
&= \frac{10}{30} \\
&= \frac{1}{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="adding-between-branches"></a>
## Adding Between Branches

**Example:** The tree diagram below represents the probabilities of two dependent events $A$ and $E$. What is $P(E')$?

![](<../Source/6.3.5. Tree Diagrams for Dependent Events/Images/47b4ac67d542f3b03d88e931c99eb00c.png>)

**Explanation**

![](<../Source/6.3.5. Tree Diagrams for Dependent Events/Images/6125e9ae5d7a34ea3c289a299b381558.png>)

The law of total probability states that

$$
P(E') = P(A \cap E') + P(A' \cap E')
$$

From the tree diagram, we have

$$
P(A\cap E') = 0.4 \cdot 0.5 = 0.2
$$

and also

$$
P(A'\cap E') = 0.6\cdot 0.75 = 0.45
$$

Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(E^{′}) &= P(A∩E^{′}) + P(A^{′}∩E^{′}) \\
&= 0.2 + 0.45 \\
&= 0.65
\end{aligned}
$$
