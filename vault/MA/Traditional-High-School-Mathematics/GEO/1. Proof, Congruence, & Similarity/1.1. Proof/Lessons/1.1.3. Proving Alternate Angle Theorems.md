## Introduction

A **mathematical proof** is a rigorous argument that a theorem (or some other mathematical statement) is true based on a set of postulates (or **axioms**), definitions, and *previously established results*. The previously established results could be other theorems or smaller results that have been proven.

Mathematical proofs follow a logical sequence of steps, starting from these foundational principles and building upon them using algebraic techniques and logic to arrive at a conclusion. Any mathematical proof aims to provide a watertight, convincing argument that a statement holds true.

By the end of this lesson, you'll know how to prove the following theorems:

- The alternate interior angles theorem
- The alternate exterior angles theorem

Our proofs of the alternate angle theorems will depend on the vertical angles theorem. We'll prove the vertical angles theorem in a separate lesson.

Let's start by proving the alternate interior angles theorem, assuming the vertical angles theorem.

<a id="the-alternate-interior-angles-theorem"></a>
## The Alternate Interior Angles Theorem

Consider the diagram below, where the parallel lines $l$ and $m$ are cut by the transversal $t$.

![](<../Source/1.1.3. Proving Alternate Angle Theorems/Images/c442604bc7e2a4911a9385dee8e792ca.png>)

Suppose we want to *prove* the following statement:

> $\angle 1 \cong \angle 2$

In other words, we want to prove the alternate interior angles theorem!

To prove this theorem, we start with $\angle 1$ and construct a sequence of statements to show that it must be congruent to $\angle 2$. Our strategy is to use the following tools:

- the vertical angles theorem
- the corresponding angles postulate
- the transitive property of congruence

We complete the proof as follows:

- **Step 1**: First, consider the angle $\angle 3$, shown in the diagram.![](<../Source/1.1.3. Proving Alternate Angle Theorems/Images/edc1073df03a1ab0ee2430b08e63d7d1.png>)
By the *vertical angles theorem*, $\angle 1$ and $\angle 3$ are congruent. Let's mark this on our diagram.![](<../Source/1.1.3. Proving Alternate Angle Theorems/Images/455e177317e89596294a0eb311139733.png>)
- **Step 2**: By the *corresponding angles postulate,* $\angle 3$ and $\angle 2$ are congruent.![](<../Source/1.1.3. Proving Alternate Angle Theorems/Images/ddb6017e1a268961bb631643c178259c.png>)

- **Step 3**: In steps 1 and 2 respectively, we showed that
$\angle 1 \cong \angle 3\quad \textrm{and}\quad \angle 3 \cong \angle 2$.
Therefore, by the *transitive property of congruence*, we conclude that
$\angle 1 \cong \angle 2$
as required.

The key idea is that we connect $\angle 1$ to $\angle 2$ through a third angle, $\angle 3$, whose relationships are already known.

It's **highly recommended** that you practice reconstructing this proof. Try to do this without referring to these notes.

## Stating the Proof Using a Two-Column Format

![](<../Source/1.1.3. Proving Alternate Angle Theorems/Images/c15b32ee6608b5c09f3337f3253eb1b0.png>)

When stating geometry proofs, we often present them in a so-called two-column format.

The table below shows the proof of the **alternate interior angles theorem** in this format.

|  | Statement | Reason |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | $\angle{1} \cong \angle{3}$ | Vertical angles theorem |
| 2 | $\angle{3} \cong \angle{2}$ | Corresponding angles postulate |
| 3 | $\angle 1 \cong \angle 2$ | Transitive property of congruence |

Let's see another example, where we prove that two alternate interior angles have the same measure.

<a id="proving-the-alternate-interior-angles-theorem"></a>
## Proving the Alternate Interior Angles Theorem

**Example:** In the diagram above, the parallel lines $l$ and $m$ are crossed by the transversal $t$.

![](<../Source/1.1.3. Proving Alternate Angle Theorems/Images/199b8578b1df48f9d099229bc3b0fe99.png>)

Consider the following statement:

> *The measure of $\angle AEF$ is $120^{\circ}$.*

The table below gives the proof of the statement. Select the correct options in the table and thus complete the proof.

|  | Statement | Reason |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | $\angle DAE \cong \angle CAB$ |  |
| 2 | $\angle CAB \cong \angle AEF$ | Corresponding angles postulate |
| 3 | $\angle DAE \cong \angle AEF$ |  |
| 4 | $\angle AEF \cong \angle DAE$ | Symmetric property of congruence |
| 5 | $m\angle AEF = 120^{\circ}$ |  |

**Explanation**

The correct proof is shown below.

|  | Statement | Reason |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | $\angle DAE \cong \angle CAB$ | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm{Vertical angles theorem}}$ |
| 2 | $\angle CAB \cong \angle AEF$ | Corresponding angles postulate |
| 3 | $\angle DAE \cong \angle AEF$ | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm{Transitive property of congruence}}$ |
| 4 | $\angle AEF \cong \angle DAE$ | Symmetric property of congruence |
| 5 | $m\angle AEF = 120^{\circ}$ | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm{Definition of congruent angles}}$ |

Let's examine the rows of the table with missing parts in turn.

- Consider row 1. Notice from the diagram that $\angle DAE$ and $\angle CAB$ are two vertical angles. The vertical angles theorem states that such angles must be congruent:
$\angle DAE \cong \angle CAB$
- Consider row 3. From rows 1 and 2, we have
${\color{blue}{\angle DAE}} \cong \angle CAB \quad\textrm{and}\quad \angle CAB \cong {\color{blue}{\angle AEF}}$.
Therefore, by the transitive property of congruence, we have
${\color{blue}{\angle DAE}} \cong {\color{blue}{\angle AEF}}$.
- Consider row 5. From row 4, we have that $\angle AEF\cong \angle DAE$. Therefore, by the definition of congruent angles, these angles have equal measure:
$m \angle AEF = m \angle DAE$
Finally, since $m \angle DAE = 120^\circ$, we have
$m \angle AEF = 120^\circ$.

<a id="proving-the-alternate-exterior-angles-theorem"></a>
## Proving the Alternate Exterior Angles Theorem

**Example:** In the diagram above, the parallel lines $n$ and $m$ are crossed by the transversal $t$.

![](<../Source/1.1.3. Proving Alternate Angle Theorems/Images/42ab05ff539b02e62f7db0c1071265ea.png>)

Consider the following statement:

> $m\angle ZSF=140^\circ$

The table below gives the proof of the statement. Determine the missing entries in the table and thus complete the proof.

|  | Statement | Reason |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | $\angle MJQ\cong \angle SJN$ |  |
| 2 | $\angle SJN\cong \angle ZSF$ |  |
| 3 | $\angle MJQ\cong \angle ZSF$ |  |
| 4 | $\angle ZSF\cong \angle MJQ$ |  |
| 5 | $m\angle ZSF=140^\circ$ |  |

**Explanation**

The completed proof is shown below.

|  | Statement | Reason |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | $\angle MJQ\cong \angle SJN$ | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm{Vertical angles theorem}}$ |
| 2 | $\angle SJN\cong \angle ZSF$ | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm{Corresponding angles postulate}}$ |
| 3 | $\angle MJQ\cong \angle ZSF$ | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm{Transitive property of congruence}}$ |
| 4 | $\angle ZSF\cong \angle MJQ$ | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm{Symmetric property of congruence}}$ |
| 5 | $m\angle ZSF=140^\circ$ | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm{Definition of congruent angles}}$ |

Let's go through this proof. First, we'll redraw the diagram.

![](<../Source/1.1.3. Proving Alternate Angle Theorems/Images/42ab05ff539b02e62f7db0c1071265ea.png>)

We wish to prove the following statement:

> $m\angle ZSF=140^\circ$

First, consider the angle $\angle MJQ$.

![](<../Source/1.1.3. Proving Alternate Angle Theorems/Images/cca9c8f3ab3f1cc9f2e50184d0e28e5c.png>)

Let's go through the proof in steps.

- **Step 1**: By the *vertical angles theorem*, $\angle MJQ$ and $\angle SJN$ are congruent.

![](<../Source/1.1.3. Proving Alternate Angle Theorems/Images/4b248c9a19fbc620796db57737707937.png>)

- **Step 2**: By the *corresponding angles postulate* $\angle SJN$ and $\angle ZSF$ are congruent.

![](<../Source/1.1.3. Proving Alternate Angle Theorems/Images/5c8c5c0fccb63f4fd0e903f2dccbe72e.png>)

- **Step 3**: In steps 1 and 2 respectively, we showed that
$\angle MJQ \cong \angle SJN\quad \textrm{and}\quad \angle SJN \cong \angle ZSF$.
Therefore, by the *transitive property of congruence*, we conclude that
$\angle MJQ \cong \angle ZSF$.
- **Step 4**: In step 3, we showed that $\angle MJQ \cong \angle ZSF$. Therefore, by the *symmetric property of congruence*, we conclude that
$\angle ZSF \cong \angle MJQ$
- **Step 5**: From step 4, we have that $\angle ZSF\cong \angle MJQ$. Therefore, by the *definition of congruent angles*, these angles have equal measure:
$m\angle ZSF= m\angle MJQ$
Finally, since $m\angle MJQ= 140^\circ$, we have
$m\angle ZSF= 140^\circ$.
