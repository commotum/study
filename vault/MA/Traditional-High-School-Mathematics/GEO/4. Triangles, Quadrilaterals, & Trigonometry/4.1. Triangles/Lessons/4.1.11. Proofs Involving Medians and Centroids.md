## Introduction

In this lesson, we'll learn how to prove results concerning medians and centroids of triangles.

We'll start by proving the centroid ratio theorem.

> *The centroid of a triangle splits any median in the proportion $2:1$ starting from the corresponding vertex.*

To prove this result, consider the diagram below, where $G$ is the point of intersection of the medians $\overline{AD}$ and $\overline{BE}$.

![](<../Source/4.1.11. Proofs Involving Medians and Centroids/Images/8dcd630db93863052eebd08a5fa7c4a9.png>)

We wish to prove the following statement:

> $AG: GD = 2: 1$

Our strategy is to show that $\triangle{ABG}$ and $\triangle{DEG}$ are similar and then use the properties of similar triangles to prove the desired result.

We proceed as follows:

- **Step 1**: We are given that $\overline{AD}$ and $\overline{BE}$ are medians of $\triangle{ABC}$.
- **Step 2**: By the *definition of a median*, $D$ and $E$ are the midpoints of $\overline{BC}$ and $\overline{AC}$, respectively.Let's add this information to our diagram.![](<../Source/4.1.11. Proofs Involving Medians and Centroids/Images/ffbe22b09a1853a19f18a180431b3f8c.png>)
- **Step 3**: Now, consider the line $\overline{DE}$, shown below![](<../Source/4.1.11. Proofs Involving Medians and Centroids/Images/91f06c8bae60e03003d9f2d09f68739e.png>)
By the *midpoint theorem for triangles*, we have
$\overline{AB} \parallel \overline{DE}, \qquad AB: DE = 2: 1$.
Let's mark the parallel lines on our diagram.![](<../Source/4.1.11. Proofs Involving Medians and Centroids/Images/19aaf44afa5981e8926e8734c572cbd2.png>)

- **Step 4**: Note that $\angle{ABG}$ and $\angle{DEG}$, as well as $\angle{BAG}$ and $\angle{EDG}$, are pairs of alternate interior angles. Then, by the *alternate interior angles theorem,* since $\overline{AB} \parallel \overline{DE}$, we get
$\angle{ABG} \cong \angle{DEG} \qquad \angle{BAG} \cong \angle{EDG}$.![](<../Source/4.1.11. Proofs Involving Medians and Centroids/Images/3b8e40a8ea959ce11981d1dfa29eecf6.png>)
- **Step 5**: In step 4, we showed that triangles $\triangle{ABG}$ and $\triangle{DEG}$ have two pairs of congruent angles.
Therefore,
$\triangle{ABG} \sim \triangle{DEG}$
by *the AA similarity criterion for triangles.*
- **Step 6**: From steps 3 and 5, we have
$AB:DE = 2:1, \qquad \triangle{ABG} \sim \triangle{DEG}$.
Therefore, since corresponding parts of similar triangles are in proportion (*CPSTP*), we conclude that
$AG:GD = 2:1$,
as required.

| 5 | $\overline{LM} \parallel \overline{AB}$ and $LM: AB = 1: 2$ |  |
| 6 | $\angle{BAK} \cong \angle{KLM}$ | Alternate interior angles theorem |
| 7 | $\angle{AKB} \cong \angle{LKM}$ |  |
| 8 | $\triangle{ABK} \sim \triangle{LMK}$ | AA similarity criterion |
| 9 | $LM: AB = 1: 2$ |  |

**Explanation**

![](<../Source/4.1.11. Proofs Involving Medians and Centroids/Images/ba4d2c1ce5e4341ee80a19f6a887db6e.png>)

The correct proof is shown below.

|  | Statement | Reason |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | $\overline{AL}$ is a median of $\triangle{ABC}$ | Given |
| 2 | $L$ is the midpoint of $\overline{BC}$ | Definition of a median |
| 3 | $\overline{BM}$ is a median of $\triangle{ABC}$ | Given |
| 4 | $M$ is the midpoint of $\overline{AC}$ | Definition of a median |
| 5 | $\overline{LM} \parallel \overline{AB} \textrm{and} LM: AB = 1: 2$ | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm{Midpoint theorem for triangles}}$ |
| 6 | $\angle{BAK} \cong \angle{KLM}$ | Alternate interior angles theorem |
| 7 | $\angle{AKB} \cong \angle{LKM}$ | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm{Vertical angles theorem}}$ |
| 8 | $\triangle{ABK} \sim \triangle{LMK}$ | AA similarity criterion |
| 9 | $LK: KA = 1: 2$ | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm{CPSTP}}$ |

Let's examine the rows of the table with missing parts in turn.

- Consider row 5. From rows 2 and 4, we have that $L$ and $M$ are the midpoints of $\overline{BC}$ and $\overline{AC}$, respectively. Then, by the midpoint theorem for triangles, $\overline{LM} \parallel \overline{AB}$ and $LM: AB = 1: 2$.
- Consider row 7. Notice that $\angle{AKB}$ and $\angle{LKM}$ are vertical angles. Therefore, $\angle{AKB} \cong \angle{LKM}$ by the vertical angles theorem.
- Consider row 9. From rows 8 and 5, we have
$\triangle{ABK} \sim \triangle{LMK}$
and
$LM: AB = 1: 2$.
Therefore, since corresponding parts of similar triangles are in proportion (CPSTP), we have that $LK: KA = 1: 2$.

## The Centroid Theorem

Let's restate the centroid theorem.

> *For any triangle, the three medians are concurrent, meaning they intersect at a single point. This point of intersection is called the centroid of the triangle.*

To prove this theorem, let's consider the following triangle.

![](<../Source/4.1.11. Proofs Involving Medians and Centroids/Images/0be90290d5d20ceb8c2fd41ea69bff0a.png>)

Here, $K, L$, and $M$ are the midpoints of the sides, as shown.

Our strategy for proving this theorem is as follows:

- First, we define the point $O$ as the intersection of the medians $\color{red}\overline{HK}$ and $\overline{IL}$.![](<../Source/4.1.11. Proofs Involving Medians and Centroids/Images/d5f35f1bc8df1f87a0d5270e467d604b.png>)
- Then, we define the point $P$ as the intersection of the medians $\color{red}\overline{HK}$ and $\overline{JM}$.![](<../Source/4.1.11. Proofs Involving Medians and Centroids/Images/a94fe5919b13a144d9529623bab1483a.png>)

- Then, applying the centroid ratio theorem to both cases, we obtain the following:The point $O$ splits $\color{red}\overline{HK}$ in the ratio $2:1$, starting from the point $H$.The point $P$ splits $\color{red}\overline{HK}$ in ratio $2:1$, starting from the point $H$.
- Since only one point can split the segment $\overline{HK}$ in the ratio $2:1$, we must have $O=P$.![](<../Source/4.1.11. Proofs Involving Medians and Centroids/Images/fe1d44566cdcc5498efc43265cbecec2.png>)

- Finally, we conclude that all three medians intersect at a single point.![](<../Source/4.1.11. Proofs Involving Medians and Centroids/Images/06243e6fb5644ad6b408c332505ddc97.png>)

That's the strategy. Now let's discuss how to construct a formal proof.

<a id="proving-the-centroid-theorem"></a>
## Proving the Centroid Theorem

**Example:** In the diagram above, $\overline{HK}$, $\overline{IL}$, and $\overline{JM}$ are the medians of $\triangle HIJ$.

![](<../Source/4.1.11. Proofs Involving Medians and Centroids/Images/d7a5925f96dd066fbe68ec3003704d11.png>)

Prove the following statement:

> *The medians of $\triangle{HIJ}$ are concurrent.*

**Explanation**

The strategy for this proof is as follows:

- Define the intersection of the medians $\overline{HK}$ and $\overline{IL}$ as the point $O$.
- Define the intersection of the medians $\overline{HK}$ and $\overline{JM}$ as the point $P$.
- Use the centroid ratio theorem to show that the points $O$ and $P$ are the same.

We proceed as follows:

> Let $O$ be the point of intersection of $\overline{HK}$ and $\overline{IL}$, and let $P$ be the point of intersection of $\overline{HK}$ and $\overline{JM}$.

Now, we apply the centroid ratio theorem to both points. First, the point $O{:}$

> Since $\overline{HK}$ and $\overline{IL}$ are medians of $\triangle{HIJ}$, by the centroid ratio theorem, we have that $O$ splits $\overline{HK}$ in the ratio $2:1$, starting from the point $H$.

Next, the point $P{:}$

> Also, since $\overline{HK}$ and $\overline{JM}$ are medians, by the centroid ratio theorem, we have that $P$ splits $\overline{HK}$ in ratio $2:1$, starting from the point $H$.

Both points split $\overline{HK}$ in the same ratio, starting from point $H$. So, they must be the same!

Finally, we make our conclusion.

> Finally, since only one point splits a given line segment in a given ratio, we obtain that $O= P$. Therefore, all three medians intersect at a single point.

The full proof is stated below:

> Let $O$ be the point of intersection of $\overline{HK}$ and $\overline{IL}$, and let $P$ be the point of intersection of $\overline{HK}$ and $\overline{JM}$.
> Since $\overline{HK}$ and $\overline{IL}$ are medians of $\triangle{HIJ}$, by the centroid ratio theorem, we have that $O$ splits $\overline{HK}$ in the ratio $2:1$, starting from the point $H$.
> Also, since $\overline{HK}$ and $\overline{JM}$ are medians, by the centroid ratio theorem, we have that $P$ splits $\overline{HK}$ in ratio $2:1$, starting from the point $H$.
> Finally, since only one point splits a given line segment in a given ratio, we obtain that $O= P$. Therefore, all three medians intersect at a single point.

## The Coordinates of the Centroid

![](<../Source/4.1.11. Proofs Involving Medians and Centroids/Images/5b1dd97996aeef5dfeba81077806d9ac.png>)

$$

$$

In a previous lesson, we saw that the coordinates of $G$ are given by

$$
\left(x_G,y_G\right) = \left(\dfrac{x_1+x_2+x_3}{3}, \dfrac{y_1+y_2+y_3}{3}\right)
$$

We can prove this result by partitioning the line segment $\overline{RJ}$ in the ratio $2:1$ as follows:

- **Step 1**: Use the *midpoint formula* to find the coordinates of $J$ in terms of the coordinates of $S$ and $T$.
$x_{J}|= \frac{x_{S} + x_{T}}{2}; y_{J}|= \frac{y_{S} + y_{T}}{2}$
- **Step 2**: Partition the segment $\overline{RJ}$ in the ratio $2:1$. To do this, we can use the following formulas (derived in a previous lesson).
$x_{G}|= x_{R} + \frac{2}{3}(x_{J} - x_{R}); y_{G}|= y_{R} + \frac{2}{3}(y_{J} - y_{R})$
- **Step 3**: Use the result from step 1 to write $x_G$ and $y_G$ in terms of the coordinates of the vertices only.

Let's now look at the full proof.

## Proving the Centroid Coordinates Formula

**Example:** In the diagram above, $\overline{RJ}$, $\overline{SI}$, and $\overline{TL}$ are the medians of $\triangle{RST}$ and $G$ is its centroid. Prove that
$x_G = \dfrac{x_R+x_S+x_T}{3}, \qquad y_G = \dfrac{y_R+y_S+y_T}{3}$.

![](<../Source/4.1.11. Proofs Involving Medians and Centroids/Images/ffd887f34d7153a96c9c6c127a1e1441.png>)

**Explanation**

First, we find the coordinates of the point $J$ in terms of the coordinates of $S$ and $T{:}$

> Since $\overline{RJ}$ is a median, we have that $J$ is the midpoint of $\overline{ST}$. So, if $(x_J,y_J)$ are the coordinates of $J$, by the
> midpoint formula, we have
> $x_{J} = \frac{x_{S} + x_{T}}{2}, y_{J} = \frac{y_{S} + y_{T}}{2}$.

Next, we write the coordinates of the centroid using the formula for partitioning a line segment in a given ratio:

> By the centroid ratio theorem, the point $G$ splits the median $\overline{RJ}$ in the ratio $2:1$, starting from the point $R$. Now, by partitioning the segment $\overline{RJ}$ in a given ratio, we obtain that
> $x_{G}|= \frac{x_{R} + 2x_{J}}{3}; = \frac{x_{R} + 2 \cdot \frac{x_{S} + x_{T}}{2}}{3}; = \frac{x_{R} + x_{S} + x_{T}}{3},; y_{G}|= \frac{y_{R} + 2y_{J}}{3}; = \frac{y_{R} + 2 \cdot \frac{y_{S} + y_{T}}{2}}{3}; = \frac{y_{R} + y_{S} + y_{T}}{3}$.
