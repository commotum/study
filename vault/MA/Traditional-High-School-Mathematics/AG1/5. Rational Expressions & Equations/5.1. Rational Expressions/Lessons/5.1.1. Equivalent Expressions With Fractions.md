## Introduction

Two expressions involving fractions are **equivalent** if they are the same.

Even if two expressions initially appear different, they might still be equivalent. For example, the expressions

$$
\dfrac{1}{2}x
$$

and

$$
\dfrac{x}{2}
$$

both represent the same thing: "half of $x$." Consequently, they are equivalent.

We can also see that these expressions are equivalent because

$$
\dfrac{1}{2}x
$$

can be transformed into

$$
\dfrac{x}{2}
$$

Writing $x$ as

$$
\dfrac{x}{1}
$$

we can multiply

$$
\dfrac 1 2
$$

and

$$
\dfrac x 1
$$

using fraction multiplication, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{1}{2}x &= \frac{1}{2} \cdot x \\
&= \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{x}{1} \\
&= \frac{1 \cdot x}{2 \cdot 1} \\
&= \frac{x}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="identifying-equivalent-expressions-with-positive-terms"></a>
## Identifying Equivalent Expressions with Positive Terms

**Example:** Find an expression that's equivalent to $\dfrac{2}3 a$.

**Explanation**

Writing $a$ as

$$
\dfrac{a}{1}
$$

we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{2}{3}a &= \frac{2}{3} \cdot a \\
&= \frac{2}{3} \cdot \frac{a}{1} \\
&= \frac{2 \cdot a}{3 \cdot 1} \\
&= \frac{2a}{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus, the expressions

$$
\dfrac{2}{3}a
$$

and

$$
\dfrac{2a}{3}
$$

are equivalent.

<a id="identifying-equivalent-expressions-with-negative-terms"></a>
## Identifying Equivalent Expressions with Negative Terms

**Example:** Find an expression equivalent to $-\dfrac{2}9 (-z)$.

**Explanation**

First, remember that the product of two negative numbers is a positive number. Thus,

$$
-\dfrac{2}9 (-z) = \dfrac{2}{9} z
$$

Now, writing $z$ as

$$
\dfrac{z}{1}
$$

we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{2}{9}z &= \frac{2}{9} \cdot z \\
&= \frac{2}{9} \cdot \frac{z}{1} \\
&= \frac{2 \cdot z}{9 \cdot 1} \\
&= \frac{2z}{9}
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus, the expressions

$$
-\dfrac{2}9 (-z)
$$

and

$$
\dfrac{2z}{9}
$$

are equivalent.

## Identifying Equivalent Expressions with Binomial Terms

**Example:** Find an expression equivalent to $\dfrac 27\, {(y-3)}$

**Explanation**

Writing $y-3$ as

$$
\dfrac{y-3}{1}
$$

we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{2}{7}(y - 3) &= \frac{2}{7} \cdot (y - 3) \\
&= \frac{2}{7} \cdot \frac{y - 3}{1} \\
&= (2 \cdot (y - 3))/(7 \cdot 1) \\
&= \frac{2y - 6}{7}
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="expressing-a-fraction-as-a-product"></a>
## Expressing a Fraction as a Product

The usual procedure also works in the reverse direction. For example, to find an equivalent expression for

$$
\dfrac{2(y+1)}5
$$

we can separate the variable part from the rest of the fraction, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(2(y + 1))/(5) &= (2 \cdot (y + 1))/(5 \cdot 1) \\
&= \frac{2}{5} \cdot ((y + 1))/(1) \\
&= \frac{2}{5} \cdot (y + 1) \\
&= \frac{2}{5}(y + 1)
\end{aligned}
$$

---

<a id="expressing-a-fraction-as-a-product"></a>
## Expressing a Fraction as a Product

**Example:** Find an expression equivalent to $\dfrac{2(3-x)}{5}$.

**Explanation**

Pulling out the variable part, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
(2(3 - x))/(5) &= (2 \cdot (3 - x))/(5 \cdot 1) \\
&= \frac{2}{5} \cdot \frac{3 - x}{1} \\
&= \frac{2}{5} \cdot (3 - x) \\
&= \frac{2}{5}(3 - x)
\end{aligned}
$$
