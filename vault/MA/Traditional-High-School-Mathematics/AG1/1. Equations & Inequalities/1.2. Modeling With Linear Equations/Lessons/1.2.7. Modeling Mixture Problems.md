## Introduction

A **mixture** is any substance made up of two other substances. We usually think of one of the substances as being mixed into the other, like salt in water.

Two numbers describe a mixture:

- the total amount of the mixture itself (in liters, grams, pounds, etc.), and
- the percentage of the mixed-in part.

We can multiply these two numbers to find the total amount of the mixed-in part:

$$
\textrm{part} = \textrm{percentage} \times \textrm{total}
$$

For instance, a $24$-ounce bottle of apple juice might be $25\%$ juice (and $75\%$ water), in which case it contains

$$
0.25 \cdot 24 = 6
$$

ounces of juice.

## Determining How Much to Add to a Mixture

**Example:** How much of a $2\%$ acid solution must be added to $300$ milliliters of a $7\%$ acid solution to get a solution that is $5\%$ acid?

**Explanation**

We will use the following formula:

$$
\textrm{part} = \textrm{percentage} \times \textrm{total}
$$

Let $x$ be the number of milliliters of the $2\%$ acid solution that must be added to the $300$ milliliters of $7\%$ acid solution. Then there will be a total of $300+x$ milliliters of the resulting $5\%$ acid solution.

We organize this information in a percentage–total–part table:

| % Acid | Total (ml) | Part Acid (ml) |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| $7$ | $300$ | $0.07 \cdot 300$ |
| $2$ | $x$ | $0.02 \cdot x$ |
| $5$ | $300+x$ | $0.05 \cdot (300+x)$ |

The parts acid in each of the $7\%$ and $2\%$ solutions add up to the part acid in the $5\%$ solution. Therefore,

$$
0.07 \cdot 300 + 0.02 \cdot x = 0.05 \cdot (300+x)
$$

Simplifying this equation, we get the following:

$$
\begin{aligned}
0.07 \cdot 300 + 0.02 \cdot x &= 0.05 \cdot (300 + x) \\
21 + 0.02x &= 15 + 0.05x \\
6 + 0.02x &= 0.05x \\
6 &= 0.03x
\end{aligned}
$$

Multiplying both sides of the equation by $100$ allows us to solve for $x$, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
100 \cdot 6 &= 100 \cdot 0.03x \\
600 &= 3x \\
200 &= x \\
x &= 200
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, $200$ milliliters of the $2\%$ solution must be added.

## Determining a Ratio

**Example:** What ratio of $20\%$ gray paint to $60\%$ gray paint must be mixed to make $50\%$ gray paint?

**Explanation**

We will use the following formula:

$$
\textrm{part} = \textrm{percentage} \times \textrm{total}
$$

Since the ratio of the two concentrations has to be the same in any amount of the $50\%$ concentrated mixture, let's assume we wish to make $100$ gallons of this mixture.

Let $x$ be the number of gallons of the $20\%$ gray paint used. Since the total mixture must be $100$ gallons, the rest of the mixture will consist of $100-x$ gallons of the $60\%$ gray paint.

We organize this information in a percentage–total–part table:

| % Gray | Total (gal) | Part Gray (gal) |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| $20$ | $x$ | $0.2 \cdot x$ |
| $60$ | $100-x$ | $0.6 \cdot (100-x)$ |
| $50$ | $100$ | $0.5 \cdot 100$ |

The parts gray in each of the $20\%$ and $60\%$ paints add up to the part gray in the $50\%$ paint:

$$
0.2 \cdot x + 0.6 \cdot (100-x) = 0.5 \cdot 100
$$

Simplifying this equation, we get:

$$
\begin{aligned}
0.2 \cdot x + 0.6 \cdot (100 - x) &= 0.5 \cdot 100 \\
0.2x + 60 - 0.6x &= 50 \\
- 0.4x &= -10
\end{aligned}
$$

Multiplying both sides of the equation by $10$ allows us to solve for $x$, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
10 \cdot (-0.4x) &= 10 \cdot (-10) \\
- 4x &= -100 \\
x &= \frac{-100}{-4} \\
x &= 25
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus, $25$ gallons of the $20\%$ gray paint must be mixed with $100-25=75$ gallons of the $60\%$ gray paint.

The required ratio of the $20\%$ concentration to the $60\%$ concentration is therefore $25:75$, or $1:3$.
