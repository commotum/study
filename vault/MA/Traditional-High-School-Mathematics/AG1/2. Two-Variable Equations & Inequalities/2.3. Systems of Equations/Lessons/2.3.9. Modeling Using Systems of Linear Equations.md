## Introduction

Solving a number problem in algebra is a three-step process.

1. First, we name each quantity mentioned in the problem statement with a variable.
2. Then, we write one or more equations relating the variables.
3. Finally, we use our knowledge of systems of equations to solve these equations and answer the question.

For example, suppose the sum of two numbers is $30$. One number is twice the other. What are the two numbers?

Let $x$ be the smaller number and $y$ the larger.

- The sum of the two numbers is $30$, so $x+y=30$.
- One number is twice the other, so $y=2x$.

Putting these equations together, we have the following system of equations:

$$
{x + y = 30; y = 2x
$$

We will solve the system using substitution. The second equation $y=2x$ is already solved for $y$, so we can substitute it into the first equation and solve for $x$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x + y &= 30 \\
x + 2x &= 30 \\
3x &= 30 \\
x &= 10
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we can substitute $x=10$ back into the equation $y=2x$ to solve for $y$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
y &= 2x \\
&= 2(10) \\
&= 20
\end{aligned}
$$

The solution to the system is $x=10$ and $y=20$. Therefore, the two numbers are $10$ and $20$.

## Solving Number Problems Using Systems of Equations

**Example:** The sum of two numbers is $50$. Three times the smaller number is $6$ less than the larger. What are the two numbers?

**Explanation**

Let $x$ be the smaller number and $y$ the larger.

- The sum of the two numbers is $50$, so $x+y=50$.
- Three times the smaller number is $6$ less than the larger, so $3x=y-6$.

Putting these equations together, we have the following system of equations:

$$
{x + y = 50; 3x = y - 6
$$

We will solve the system using substitution. We solve for $y$ in the first equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x + y &= 50 \\
y &= 50 - x
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we can substitute $y=50-x$ into the second equation and solve for $x$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
3x &= y - 6 \\
3x &= (50 - x) - 6 \\
3x &= 44 - x \\
4x &= 44 \\
x &= 11
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we can substitute $x=11$ back into the equation $y=50 - x$ to solve for $y$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
y &= 50 - x \\
&= 50 - 11 \\
&= 39
\end{aligned}
$$

The solution to the system is $x=11$ and $y=39$. Therefore, the two numbers are $11$ and $39$.

## Solving Word Problems Using Systems of Equations

**Example:** Together, Xander and Yohan have $60$ marbles. Twice the number of marbles that Xander has is $12$ more than the number of marbles Yohan has. How many marbles does Xander have, and how many marbles does Yohan have?

**Explanation**

Let $x$ be the number of marbles that Xander has, and let $y$ be the number of marbles that Yohan has.

- Together, Xander and Yohan have $60$ marbles, so $x+y=60$.
- Twice the number of marbles that Xander has is $12$ more than the number of marbles that Yohan has, so $2x=y+12$.

Putting these equations together, we have the following system of equations:

$$
{x + y = 60; 2x = y + 12
$$

We will solve the system using substitution. We solve for $y$ in the first equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x + y &= 60 \\
y &= 60 - x
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we can substitute $y=60-x$ into the second equation and solve for $x$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
2x &= y + 12 \\
2x &= (60 - x) + 12 \\
2x &= 72 - x \\
3x &= 72 \\
x &= 24
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we can substitute $x=24$ back into the equation $y=60-x$ to solve for $y$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
y &= 60 - x \\
&= 60 - 24 \\
&= 36
\end{aligned}
$$

The solution to the system is $x=24$ and $y=36$. Therefore, Xander has $24$ marbles, and Yohan has $36$ marbles.

## Solving Word Problems Using Binomials

**Example:** If Jack gives $\[math] 6$ to Ryan, both will have the same amount of dollars. But if Ryan gives $\[/math] 3$ to Jack, then Jack will have $2$ times the amount that Ryan has. How much money does Ryan have?

**Explanation**

Let $x$ be the number of dollars Jack has and let $y$ be the number of dollars Ryan has.

- If Jack gives $\[math] 6$ to Ryan, both will have the same amount of dollars, so $x-6 = y+6$.
- If Ryan gives $\[/math] 3$ to Jack, then Jack will have $2$ times what Ryan has left, so $x+3 =2(y-3)$.

Putting these equations together, we have the following system of equations:

$$
{x - 6 = y + 6; x + 3 = 2(y - 3)
$$

We will solve the system using substitution. We solve for $x$ in the second equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x + 3 &= 2(y - 3) \\
x + 3 &= 2y - 6 \\
x &= 2y - 9
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we can substitute $x=2y-9$ into the first equation and solve for $y$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x - 6 &= y + 6 \\
(2y - 9) - 6 &= y + 6 \\
2y - 15 &= y + 6 \\
y &= 21
\end{aligned}
$$

We conclude that Ryan has $\[math] 21$.

## Solving Problems Involving Cutting or Separating

**Example:** A fiberglass rod has a length of $164\,\textrm{cm}$. It is cut into two unequal pieces. Four times the longer piece is $26\,\textrm{cm}$ longer than five times the length of the shorter piece. What is the length of the shorter piece?

**Explanation**

Let $x$ be the length of the longer piece and let $y$ be the length of the shorter piece.

- Four times the longer is $26\,\textrm{cm}$ more than five times the shorter gives $4x=5y+26$.
- The total length is $164\,\textrm{cm}$, so $x+y=164$.

Putting these equations together, we have the following system of equations:

$$
{4x = 5y + 26; x + y = 164
$$

We will solve the system using substitution. We solve for $x$ in the first equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
4x &= 5y + 26 \\
\frac{1}{4} \cdot 4x &= \frac{1}{4} \cdot (5y + 26) \\
x &= \frac{5y + 26}{4}
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we can substitute

$$
x = \dfrac{5y+26}{4}
$$

into the second equation and solve for $y{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
x + y &= 164 \\
\frac{5y + 26}{4} + y &= 164 \\
\frac{5y + 26}{4} + \frac{4y}{4} &= 164 \\
\frac{5y + 26 + 4y}{4} &= 164 \\
\frac{9y + 26}{4} &= 164 \\
9y + 26 &= 656 \\
9y &= 630 \\
y &= 70
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the length of the shorter piece is $70\,\textrm{cm}$.
