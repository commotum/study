## Introduction

Up to now, all the parabolas we've seen have been written in **standard form**.

$$
y = ax^2+bx+c
$$

We can also write the equation of a parabola in the so-called **vertex form,** given by

$$
y=a(x-h)^2 + k
$$

When a parabola is written in vertex form, the pair $(h,k)$ gives the coordinates of its vertex.

For example, consider the following parabola:

$$
y=(x-2)^2 +1
$$

This parabola is written in vertex form with $a = 1, h=2$ and $k=1$. Therefore, the vertex of the parabola is at $(2,1)$.

![](<../Source/10.2.8. The Vertex Form of a Parabola/Images/9e04f8c75fe9410751e868d126e4dd60.png>)

In future lessons, we'll learn how to express any parabola in vertex form. For now, let's get some practice working with this form.

## Finding the Vertex of a Parabola Given in Vertex Form

**Example:** What are the coordinates of the vertex of the parabola $y=-3(x+5)^2-4$?

**Explanation**

Remember that the vertex form of a parabola is

$$
y=a(x-h)^2 + k
$$

where $(h,k)$ are the coordinates of the vertex of the parabola.

The given parabola

$$
y=-3(x+5)^2-4
$$

is written in vertex form with $h=-5$ and $k=-4$. To see this clearly, note that we can write the parabola as follows:

$$
y=-3(x-(-5))^2-4
$$

Therefore, the vertex of the parabola is at $(-5,-4)$.

## Finding the Axis of Symmetry of a Parabola Given in Vertex Form

**Example:** What is the axis of symmetry of the parabola $y=4(x-3)^2+2$?

**Explanation**

The axis of symmetry of the parabola is the vertical line that passes through the vertex of the parabola. To find the axis of symmetry, we need to compute the $x$-coordinate of the vertex of the parabola.

Remember that the vertex form of a parabola is

$$
y=a(x-h)^2 + k
$$

where $(h,k)$ are the coordinates of the vertex of the parabola.

The given parabola

$$
y=4(x-3)^2+2
$$

is written in vertex form with $h=3$ and $k=2$. Therefore, the vertex of the parabola is at $(3,2)$, and the axis of symmetry is $x=3$.

## Finding the Minimum or Maximum Value of a Parabola Given in Vertex Form

**Example:** Find the minimum value of the parabola $y=(x-5)^2 -4$, sketched below.

![](<../Source/10.2.8. The Vertex Form of a Parabola/Images/c4411d4911d8aa456a431a2a0037d7be.png>)

**Explanation**

The minimum value of the parabola is the $y$-coordinate of the vertex of the parabola.

Remember that the vertex form of a parabola is

$$
y = a(x-h)^2 + k
$$

where $(h,k)$ are the coordinates of the vertex of the parabola.

The given parabola

$$
y = (x-5)^2 -4
$$

is written in vertex form with $h=5$ and $k=-4$. Therefore, the vertex of the parabola is at $(5,-4)$, and its minimum value is $-4$.

## Why Does Vertex Form Give the Coordinates of the Vertex?

Let's consider our first example once more.

![](<../Source/10.2.8. The Vertex Form of a Parabola/Images/cdb1a305f401fb817f7a131632fb8af6.png>)

You might be wondering how we can tell that the vertex coordinates are $(2,1)$ just from looking at the equation.

To see this, let's label the two terms in our parabola formula:

$$
y=\underbrace{(x-2)^2}_{\textrm{quadratic part}}+\underbrace{1}_{\textrm{constant}}
$$

Now, note the following:

- The quadratic part $(x-2)^2$ is a *squared* quantity. Therefore, it can only be *positive* or *zero*.
- Moreover, the quadratic part *equals* zero when $x=2$.
- Since the constant part $(1)$ does not vary with $x$, the entire expression is *minimized* when $x=2$. Therefore, the $x$-coordinate of the minimum point (i.e., the vertex) is $2$.
- When $x=2$, the quadratic part is zero, giving $y=0^2+1 = 1$. Therefore, the $y$-coordinate of the vertex is $1$.

Following this logic, we conclude that the coordinates of the vertex are $(2,1)$.

Finally, it's possible to generalize this argument to any parabola. For $a < 0$, the coordinates $(h,k)$ give the maximum of the parabola.
