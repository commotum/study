## Introduction

To solve a two-term quadratic equation like

$$
x^2 - 7x = 0
$$

where there is no constant term, we can factor out the variable and apply the zero-product rule.

Factoring out the variable, we have:

$$
\begin{aligned} x^2 - 7x &= 0 \\ x(x - 7) &= 0 \end{aligned}
$$

We apply the zero product rule to get

$$
x=0 \qquad\text{or}\qquad x-7=0
$$

which in turn gives

$$
x=0 \qquad\text{or}\qquad x=7
$$

Therefore, the solutions are $x=0,7$.

To check that these solutions are correct, we can substitute them back into the original equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\begin{bmatrix}x &= 0 & x|= 7 \\ ⇓ & ⇓ \\ x^{2} - 7x|= 0 & x^{2} - 7x|= 0 \\ 0^{2} - 7(0)|= 0 & 7^{2} - 7(7)|= 0 \\ 0 - 0|= 0 & 49 - 49|= 0 \\ 0|= 0✓ & 0|= 0✓\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

**Warning:** It's tempting to solve the equation by dividing by $x$, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^{2} - 7x &= 0 \\
\frac{x^{2} - 7x}{x} &= \frac{0}{x} \\
x - 7 &= 0 \\
x &= 7
\end{aligned}
$$

However, if we do that, we lose the solution $x=0$. So, when solving equations, *never divide by a variable* because the variable could be zero, and division by zero is not allowed. Instead, try to factor the variable in the equation.

## Solving When the Right-Hand Side Is Zero

**Example:** Solve for $n$ where $n^2 +12n = 0$.

**Explanation**

First, we factor the left-hand side.

$$
\begin{aligned}
n^{2} + 12n &= 0 \\
n(n + 12) &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we apply the zero product rule to get

$$
n=0 \qquad\text{or}\qquad n+12=0
$$

which in turn gives

$$
n=0 \qquad\text{or}\qquad n=-12
$$

Therefore, the solutions are $n=-12,0$.

## Solving When the Right-Hand Side Is Not Zero

**Example:** Solve $3p^2 = 5p$.

**Explanation**

First, we move all of the terms to the left-hand side and then factor:

$$
\begin{aligned} 3p^2 &= 5p \\[3pt] 3p^2 - 5p &= 0 \\[3pt] p(3p - 5) &= 0 \end{aligned}
$$

So, by the zero product rule, we have

$$
p=0 \qquad\text{or}\qquad 3p-5=0
$$

which in turn gives

$$
p=0 \qquad\text{or}\qquad p=\dfrac{5}{3}
$$

Therefore, the solutions are

$$
p=0,\dfrac{5}{3}
$$

## Solving When Both Sides are Quadratic

**Example:** Solve the equation $6x^2 +2x -2= 8x^2- x -2$.

**Explanation**

First, we move all of the terms to the left-hand side and then factor:

$$
\begin{aligned}
6x^{2} + 2x - 2 &= 8x^{2} - x - 2 \\
6x^{2} + 2x - 2 - (8x^{2} - x - 2) &= 0 \\
6x^{2} + 2x - 2 - 8x^{2} + x + 2 &= 0 \\
(6x^{2} - 8x^{2}) + (2x + x) + (-2 + 2) &= 0 \\
- 2x^{2} + 3x + 0 &= 0 \\
- 2x^{2} + 3x &= 0 \\
x(-2x + 3) &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

So, by the zero product rule, we have

$$
x=0 \qquad\text{or}\qquad -2x + 3=0
$$

which in turn gives

$$
x=0 \qquad\text{or}\qquad x=\dfrac{3}{2}
$$

Therefore, the solutions are

$$
x=0,\dfrac{3}{2}
$$
