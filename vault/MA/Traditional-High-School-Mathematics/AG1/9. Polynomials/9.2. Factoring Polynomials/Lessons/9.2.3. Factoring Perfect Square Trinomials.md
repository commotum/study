## Introduction

A **perfect square trinomial** is a three-term expression that is the result of squaring a binomial.

For example, the expression
$x^2 + 6x + 9$
is a perfect square trinomial because it is the result of squaring the binomial $(x+3)$, as shown below:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x + 3)^{2} &=  \\
x^{2} + 2 \cdot 3x + 3^{2} &=  \\
&= x^{2} + 6x + 9
\end{aligned}
$$

To factor a perfect square trinomial, we have to figure out what binomial it is the square of. We can often do this by relating the perfect square trinomial to the **square of a sum**:

$$
x^2 + {\color{red}2} {\color{blue}a}x + {\color{blue}a}^2 = (x + {\color{blue}a})^2
$$

To illustrate, let's factor the expression $x^2+6x+9$ using the square of sum formula. We perform the following steps:

1. Write the constant term as a perfect square:
$x^{2} + 6x + 3^{2}$
2. Factor a $2$ out of the linear term:
$x^{2} + 2 \cdot 3x + 3^{2}$
3. Factor the trinomial using the "square of a sum formula". In this case, we have ${\color{blue}{a}}={\color{blue}{3}}$:
$(x + 3)^{2}$

This method works *only* when the trinomial is a perfect square! It will *not* work for every trinomial. We will learn more general methods in the future.

## Factoring a Trinomial Generated by the Square of a Sum

**Example:** Factor $x^2 + 12x + 36$.

**Explanation**

The "square of a sum" formula states that

$$
x^2 + {\color{red}{2}}{\color{blue}{a}} x + a^2 = (x+{\color{blue}{a}})^2
$$

We will use this formula to factor the given trinomial. Our trinomial is

$$
x^2 + 12x + 36
$$

First, we write the constant term as a perfect square:

$$
x^{2} + 12x + 6^{2}
$$

Then, we factor a $2$ out of the linear term:

$$
x^{2} + 2 \cdot 6x + 6^{2}
$$

Finally, we factor the trinomial using the square of sum formula with

$$
{\color{blue}{a}} = {\color{blue}{6}}
$$

This gives

$$
(x + 6)^{2}
$$

## Factoring a Trinomial Generated by the Square of a Difference

Other perfect square trinomials can be factored as the **square of a difference**:

$$
x^2 - {\color{red}2} {\color{blue}a}x + {\color{blue}a}^2 = (x - {\color{blue}a})^2
$$

To illustrate, let's factor the expression $x^2-10x+25$ using the square of difference formula. We perform the following steps:

1. Write the constant term as a perfect square:
$x^{2} - 10x + 5^{2}$
2. Factor a $2$ out of the linear term:
$x^{2} - 2 \cdot 5x + 5^{2}$
3. Factor the trinomial using the square of difference formula. In this case, we have ${\color{blue}{a}}={\color{blue}{5}}$, and we get
$(x - 5)^{2}$.

---

<a id="factoring-a-trinomial-generated-by-the-square-of-a-difference"></a>
## Factoring a Trinomial Generated by the Square of a Difference

**Example:** Factor $p^2 - 10p + 25$.

**Explanation**

The "square of a difference" formula states that

$$
p^2 - {\color{red}{2}}{\color{blue}{a}} p + a^2 = (p-{\color{blue}{a}})^2
$$

We will use this formula to factor the given trinomial. Our trinomial is

$$
p^2 - 10p + 25
$$

First, we write the constant term as a perfect square:

$$
p^{2} - 10p + 5^{2}
$$

Then, we factor a $2$ out of the linear term:

$$
p^{2} - 2 \cdot 5p + 5^{2}
$$

Finally, we factor the trinomial using the square of difference formula with

$$
{\color{blue}{a}} = {\color{blue}{5}}
$$

This gives

$$
(p - 5)^{2}
$$

## Factoring a Trinomial with Fractions

**Example:** Factor the expression $y^2 + \dfrac{2}{5}y + \dfrac{1}{25}$.

**Explanation**

The "square of a sum" formula states that

$$
x^2 + {\color{red}{2}}{\color{blue}{a}} x + a^2 = (x+{\color{blue}{a}})^2
$$

We will use this formula to factor the given trinomial. Our trinomial is

$$
y^2 + \dfrac{2}{5}y + \dfrac{1}{25}
$$

First, we write the constant term as a perfect square:

$$
y^{2} + \frac{2}{5}y + (\frac{1}{5})^{2}
$$

Then, we factor a $2$ out of the linear term:

$$
y^{2} + 2 \cdot \frac{1}{5}y + (\frac{1}{5})^{2}
$$

Finally, we factor the trinomial using the square of sum formula with

$$
{\color{blue}{a}} = {\color{blue}{\dfrac 1 5}}
$$

This gives

$$
(y + \frac{1}{5})^{2}
$$
