## Introduction

When simplifying an algebraic expression involving square roots, it's useful to remember that

$$
\sqrt{x^2} = \mid x \mid
$$

It's tempting to think that $\sqrt{x^2}$ is equivalent to $x$, but that's not true because $\sqrt{x^2}$ is always non-negative, even if $x$ is negative.

To illustrate, let's evaluate $\sqrt{x^2}$ for a couple of different values of $x$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{4^{2}} &= \sqrt{16} = 4 \\
\sqrt{(-4)^{2}} &= \sqrt{16} = 4
\end{aligned}
$$

In both cases, using absolute value gives the correct result:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\begin{bmatrix}\sqrt{4^{2}} = & 4 & &= 4 & ✓ \\ \sqrt{(-4)^{2}} = & -4 & |= 4 & ✓\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Expressing the Square Root of a Perfect Square Using Absolute Value

**Example:** Simplify $\sqrt{16y^2}$.

**Explanation**

Remember that the square root of a perfect square can be simplified using absolute value.

$$
\sqrt{x^2} = \mid x \mid
$$

Also, we notice that $16$ and $y^2$ are both perfect squares.

First, we write the expression under the square root as a perfect square:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{16y^{2}} &= \sqrt{4^{2} \cdot y^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{4 \cdot 4 \cdot y \cdot y} \\
&= \sqrt{4 \cdot y \cdot 4 \cdot y} \\
&= \sqrt{4y \cdot 4y} \\
&= \sqrt{(4y)^{2}}
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we take the absolute value of the squared quantity and simplify using the rules of absolute value:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{(4y)^{2}} &= \mid 4y \mid \\
&=\begin{vmatrix}4 & \cdot & y\end{vmatrix} \\
&= 4 \mid y \mid
\end{aligned}
$$

## Simplifying the Square Root of a Perfect Square With Multiple Variables

**Example:** Simplify $\sqrt{36a^2c^2}$.

**Explanation**

Remember that the square root of a perfect square can be simplified using absolute value.

$$
\sqrt{x^2} = \mid x \mid
$$

Also, we notice that $36, a^2$, and $c^2$ are perfect squares.

First, we write the expression under the square root as a perfect square:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{36a^{2}c^{2}} &= \sqrt{6^{2} \cdot a^{2} \cdot c^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{6 \cdot 6 \cdot a \cdot a \cdot c \cdot c} \\
&= \sqrt{6 \cdot a \cdot c \cdot 6 \cdot a \cdot c} \\
&= \sqrt{(6ac) \cdot (6ac)} \\
&= \sqrt{(6ac)^{2}}
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we take the absolute value of the squared quantity and simplify using the rules of absolute value:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{(6ac)^{2}} &= \mid 6ac \mid \\
&=\begin{vmatrix}6 & \cdot & ac\end{vmatrix} \\
&= 6 \mid ac \mid
\end{aligned}
$$

## Simplifying a Square Root of a Perfect Square Containing a Fraction

**Example:** Simplify $\sqrt{\dfrac{4x^2}{y^2}}$.

**Explanation**

Remember that the square root of a perfect square can be simplified using absolute value.

$$
\sqrt{x^2} = \mid x \mid
$$

Also, we notice that $4, x^2$, and $y^2$ are all perfect squares.

First, we write the expression under the square root as a perfect square:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{\frac{4x^{2}}{y^{2}}} &= \sqrt{\frac{2^{2}x^{2}}{y^{2}}} \\
&= \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot 2 \cdot x \cdot x}{y \cdot y}} \\
&= \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot x \cdot 2 \cdot x}{y \cdot y}} \\
&= \sqrt{\frac{2x}{y} \cdot \frac{2x}{y}} \\
&= \sqrt{(\frac{2x}{y})^{2}}
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we take the absolute value of the squared quantity and simplify using the rules of absolute value:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{(\frac{2x}{y})^{2}} &= \mid \frac{2x}{y} \mid \\
&= \frac{\begin{vmatrix}2x &}{\mid y\end{vmatrix}} \\
&= \frac{\begin{vmatrix}2 & \cdot & x &}{\mid y\end{vmatrix}} \\
&= \frac{2\begin{vmatrix}x &}{\mid y\end{vmatrix}}
\end{aligned}
$$
