## Introduction

Recall that the double-angle formula for tangent is given by

$$
\tan 2x = \dfrac{2\tan{x}}{1-\tan^2{x}}
$$

We can use the double-angle formula to simplify many different types of trigonometric expressions. Let's see how.

---

<a id="using-the-double-angle-formula-for-tangent"></a>
## Using the Double-Angle Formula for Tangent

**Example:** Simplify $\dfrac{8\tan{2\theta}}{1-\tan^2{2\theta}}$.

**Explanation**

First, let's recall the double-angle formula for tangent:

$$
\tan 2x = \dfrac{2\tan x}{1-\tan^2 x}
$$

Substituting $x=2\theta$ into the above, we get

$$
\tan 4θ = \frac{2\tan 2θ}{1 - \tan^{2} 2θ}
$$

Writing our expression in terms of $\tan 2\theta$, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{8\tan 2θ}{1 - \tan^{2} 2θ} &= 4 \cdot (\frac{2\tan 2θ}{1 - \tan^{2} 2θ}) = 4\tan 4θ
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\dfrac{8\tan{2\theta}}{1-\tan^2{2\theta}} = 4\tan{4\theta}
$$

## Simplifying Trigonometric Expressions Using the Double-Angle Formula for Tangent

**Example:** Simplify the expression $\dfrac{5\tan{2x}}{\cot{x}-\tan{x}}$.

**Explanation**

Substituting

$$
\cot{x}=\dfrac{1}{\tan{x}}
$$

into our expression and then simplifying, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{5\tan 2x}{\cot x - \tan x} &= (5\tan 2x)/((\frac{1}{\tan x} - \tan x)) \\
&= \frac{5\tan 2x \cdot \tan x}{1 - \tan^{2} x} \\
&= 5\tan 2x \cdot (\frac{\tan x}{1 - \tan^{2} x})
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, let's recall the double-angle formula for tangent:

$$
\tan {2x} = \dfrac {2\tan x}{1-\tan^2 x}
$$

Writing our expression in terms of $\tan2x$, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
5\tan 2x \cdot (\frac{\tan x}{1 - \tan^{2} x}) &= \frac{5}{2}\tan 2x \cdot (\frac{2\tan x}{1 - \tan^{2} x}) \\
&= \frac{5}{2}\tan 2x \cdot (\tan 2x) \\
&= \frac{5}{2}\tan^{2} 2x
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\dfrac{5\tan{2x}}{\cot{x}-\tan{x}} = \dfrac{5}{2} \tan^2 {2x}
$$

## Simplifying Advanced Trigonometric Expressions Using the Double-Angle Formula for Tangent

**Example:** Simplify $\dfrac{4\tan^2{\theta}+\left(1-\tan^2{\theta}\right)^2}{1 - 2\tan^2{\theta} + \tan^4{\theta}}$.

**Explanation**

First, we write our expression as a perfect square:

$$
1 - 2\tan^2{\theta} + \tan^4{\theta} = \left(1-\tan^2{\theta}\right)^2
$$

Rewriting the denominator of our expression using the above identity, we get

$$
\dfrac{4\tan^2{\theta}+\left(1-\tan^2{\theta}\right)^2}{1 - 2\tan^2{\theta} + \tan^4{\theta}} = \dfrac{4\tan^2{\theta}+\left(1-\tan^2{\theta}\right)^2}{\left(1-\tan^2{\theta}\right)^2}
$$

Let's now recall the double-angle formula for tangent:

$$
\dfrac{2\tan{\theta}}{1-\tan^2{\theta}}=\tan{2\theta}
$$

Now, splitting the fraction, using the double-angle formula, and applying the tangent-secant formula, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
(4\tan^{2} θ + (1 - \tan^{2} θ)^{2})/((1 - \tan^{2} θ)^{2}) &= (4\tan^{2} θ)/((1 - \tan^{2} θ)^{2}) + ((1 - \tan^{2} θ)^{2})/((1 - \tan^{2} θ)^{2}) \\
&= (4\tan^{2} θ)/((1 - \tan^{2} θ)^{2}) + ((1 - \tan^{2} θ)^{2})/((1 - \tan^{2} θ)^{2}) \\
&= (\frac{2\tan θ}{1 - \tan^{2} θ})^{2} + 1 \\
&= \tan^{2} 2θ + 1 \\
&= \sec^{2} 2θ
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\dfrac{4\tan^2{\theta}+\left(1-\tan^2{\theta}\right)^2}{1 - 2\tan^2{\theta} + \tan^4{\theta}} = \sec^2{2\theta}
$$
