## Introduction

We can multiply matrices by numbers. This type of multiplication is known as **scalar multiplication**. Here, a **scalar** is just a number.

For example, consider the matrix
$\begin{bmatrix}1 & 3 \\ 2 & 4\end{bmatrix}$

To multiply ${A}$ by the scalar $2$, we just multiply each entry of ${A}$ by $2$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
2A &= 2\begin{bmatrix}1 & 3 \\ 2 & 4\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}2 \cdot 1 & 2 \cdot 3 \\ 2 \cdot 2 & 2 \cdot 4\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}2 & 6 \\ 4 & 8\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

In general, to multiply a $m \times n$ matrix $A$ by a scalar $c$, we multiply each entry of $A$ by $c$ as follows:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}a_{11} & a_{12} & … & a_{1n} \\ a_{21} & a_{22} & … & a_{2n} \\ ⋮ & ⋮ & ⋱ & ⋮ \\ a_{m1} & a_{m2} & … & a_{mn}\end{bmatrix}
$$

## Multiplying a Matrix by a Scalar

**Example:** Calculate $-2A$, if
$A=\left\lbrack \matrix {2 & -1 \ 4 & 7} \right\rbrack$.

**Explanation**

We multiply each entry of $A$ by $-2$, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
-2A &= -2\begin{bmatrix}2 & -1 \\ 4 & 7\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}-2 \cdot 2 & -2 \cdot (-1) \\ -2 \cdot 4 & -2 \cdot 7\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}-4 & 2 \\ -8 & -14\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="calculating-the-value-of-a-scalar"></a>
## Calculating the Value of a Scalar

**Example:** If $k {A} = \left\lbrack \matrix {1 & 3 & 4 \ 6 & 0 & -2} \right\rbrack$,
where ${A} = \left\lbrack \matrix {2 & 6 & 8 \ 12 & 0 & -4} \right\rbrack$
and $k$ is a scalar, calculate the value of $k$.

**Explanation**

To express $kA$, we multiply each entry of $A$ by $k$, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
kA &= k \cdot \begin{bmatrix}2 & 6 & 8 \\ 12 & 0 & -4\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}2k & 6k & 8k \\ 12k & 0 & -4k\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we set the above result equal to the matrix given for $kA$ in the problem statement:

$$
\left\lbrack \matrix {2k & 6k & 8k \ 12k & 0 & -4k} \right\rbrack = \left\lbrack \matrix {1 & 3 & 4 \ 6 & 0 & -2} \right\rbrack
$$

We can solve for $k$ by equating any two corresponding (nonzero) elements. Let's choose the top-left elements, $2k$ and $1$. Equating these elements, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
2k &= 1 \\
k &= \frac{1}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

**Note:** We would still get the same result if we chose any other two corresponding (nonzero) elements.

<a id="properties-of-scalar-multiplication-of-matrices"></a>
## Properties of Scalar Multiplication of Matrices

Since scalar multiplication is done entry-wise, it obeys the same kind of rules as multiplication of real numbers.

For example, for any numbers $c_1, c_2$ and any matrix $A$, we can perform scalar multiplication in any order:

$$
c_1 (c_2 A) = (c_1 c_2) A = c_2 (c_1 A)
$$

Moreover, we can distribute a matrix over the sum or difference of scalars:

$$
(c_1 \pm c_2)A = c_1A \pm c_2A
$$

We can also distribute scalar multiplication over the sum or difference of two matrices:

$$
c (A \pm B) = c A \pm c B
$$

<a id="calculating-a-linear-combination-of-two-matrices"></a>
## Calculating a Linear Combination of Two Matrices

**Example:** Calculate $2 {A} + \dfrac{1}{2} {B}$, where
$\begin{bmatrix}1 & 5 \\ 1 & 2\end{bmatrix}$ and ${B}= \left\lbrack \matrix {0 & 2 \ 4 & 2} \right\rbrack$.

**Explanation**

We multiply each entry of $A$ by $2$ and each entry of $B$ by

$$
\dfrac{1}{2}
$$

and then add the resulting matrices, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
2A + \frac{1}{2}B &= 2\begin{bmatrix}1 & 5 \\ 1 & 2\end{bmatrix} + \frac{1}{2}\begin{bmatrix}0 & 2 \\ 4 & 2\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}2 \cdot 1 & 2 \cdot 5 \\ 2 \cdot 1 & 2 \cdot 2\end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{2} \cdot 0 & \frac{1}{2} \cdot 2 \\ \frac{1}{2} \cdot 4 & \frac{1}{2} \cdot 2\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}2 & 10 \\ 2 & 4\end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix}0 & 1 \\ 2 & 1\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}2 + 0 & 10 + 1 \\ 2 + 2 & 4 + 1\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}2 & 11 \\ 4 & 5\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$
