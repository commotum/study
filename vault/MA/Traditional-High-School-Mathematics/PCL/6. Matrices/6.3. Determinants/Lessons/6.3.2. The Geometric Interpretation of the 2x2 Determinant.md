## Introduction

Suppose we have two vectors

$$
\textbf{u}=\langle 1, 3 \rangle
$$

and

$$
\textbf{v}=\langle 2,-1 \rangle
$$

The parallelogram spanned by $\textbf{u}$ and $\textbf{v}$ is shown in the picture below:

![](<../Source/6.3.2. The Geometric Interpretation of the 2x2 Determinant/Images/b71bd81a49f801c9f5e115e8d2f89fb9.png>)

Now, we construct a new matrix

$$
\begin{bmatrix}1 & 2 \\ 3 & -1\end{bmatrix}
$$

where the columns are made up of the components of the corresponding vectors. Let's find the determinant of $M$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
det(M) &= \begin{vmatrix}1 & 2 \\ 3 & -1\end{vmatrix} \\
&= 1 \cdot (-1) - 2 \cdot 3 \\
&=-7
\end{aligned}
$$

It turns out that the absolute value of $\det(M)$ gives the area $A$ of the parallelogram:

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= \mid det(M) \mid \\
&= \mid - 7 \mid \\
&= 7
\end{aligned}
$$

## Calculating the Area of the Parallelogram Spanned by Two Non-Parallel Vectors

**Example:** Calculate the area of the parallelogram spanned by $\textbf{u}=\langle 1,2 \rangle$ and $\textbf{v}=\langle 3, -3 \rangle$.

**Explanation**

First, we construct a matrix where the columns are made up of the components of the corresponding vectors:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}1 & 3 \\ 2 & -3\end{bmatrix}
$$

Then, we find the determinant:

$$
\begin{aligned}
det(M) &= \begin{vmatrix}1 & 3 \\ 2 & -3\end{vmatrix} \\
&= 1 \cdot (-3) - 3 \cdot 2 \\
&=-9
\end{aligned}
$$

The absolute value of $\det(M)$ gives the area $A$ of the parallelogram:

$$
A = \mid {-9} \mid = 9
$$

## The Case When Two Vectors are Parallel

When two vectors are parallel, the parallelogram does not exist. So, it has zero area.

![](<../Source/6.3.2. The Geometric Interpretation of the 2x2 Determinant/Images/378bd116b22c5d0b45b09152520e42c0.png>)

As a result, the determinant of a matrix whose columns are parallel vectors must also be zero.

For example, in the diagram above, we have the vectors

$$
\textbf{u} = \left< 1,1 \right>
$$

and

$$
\textbf{v} = \left< 2,2 \right>
$$

Constructing a matrix where the columns are made up of the components of the corresponding vectors, we have

$$
\begin{bmatrix}1 & 2 \\ 1 & 2\end{bmatrix}
$$

Indeed, the determinant does come out to zero:

$$
\begin{aligned}
det(M) &= \begin{vmatrix}1 & 2 \\ 1 & 2\end{vmatrix} \\
&= 1 \cdot 2 - 2 \cdot 1 \\
&= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

## Calculating the Area of the Parallelogram Spanned by Two Parallel Vectors

**Example:** Calculate the area of the parallelogram spanned by $\textbf{u}=\langle 3,-2 \rangle$ and $\textbf{v}=\langle 6, -4 \rangle$.

**Explanation**

First, we construct a matrix where the columns are made up of the components of the corresponding vectors:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}3 & 6 \\ -2 & -4\end{bmatrix}
$$

Then, we find the determinant:

$$
\begin{aligned}
det(M) &= \begin{vmatrix}3 & 6 \\ -2 & -4\end{vmatrix} \\
&= 3 \cdot (-4) - 6 \cdot (-2) \\
&= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

The absolute value of $\det(M)$ gives the area $A$ of the parallelogram:

$$
A = \mid \,0\, \mid = 0
$$

<a id="general-rule"></a>
## General Rule

Why does the absolute value of the determinant represent the area of the parallelogram? Let's prove this result.

First, let's state the result precisely. Consider two vectors

$$
\textbf{u}=\langle a,b \rangle
$$

and

$$
\textbf{v}=\langle c,d \rangle
$$

The parallelogram spanned by $\textbf{u}$ and $\textbf{v}$ is shown in the picture below:

![](<../Source/6.3.2. The Geometric Interpretation of the 2x2 Determinant/Images/98f405fbc70b5a40be2c5ce94bda673f.png>)

We construct a new matrix

$$
\begin{bmatrix}a & c \\ b & d\end{bmatrix}
$$

where the columns are made up of the components of the corresponding vectors. Then the absolute value of $\det(M)$ gives the area $A$ of the parallelogram spanned by $\textbf{u}$ and $\textbf{v}$:

$$
A = \mid \det(M) \mid
$$

To prove the formula, we use the following picture:

![](<../Source/6.3.2. The Geometric Interpretation of the 2x2 Determinant/Images/9a2676dd42afc9d49fc56932dbcac477.png>)

We can compute the area by starting with the area of the outer rectangle, and then subtracting off the area of the smaller triangles and rectangles surrounding the parallelogram:

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= \mid (a + c)(b + d) - 2 \cdot \frac{1}{2}cd- 2 \cdot ad- 2 \cdot \frac{1}{2}ab \mid \\
&= \mid ab + ad + bc + cd - cd - 2ad - ab \mid \\
&= \mid bc - ad \mid \\
&= \mid ad - bc \mid \\
&= \mid det(M) \mid
\end{aligned}
$$
