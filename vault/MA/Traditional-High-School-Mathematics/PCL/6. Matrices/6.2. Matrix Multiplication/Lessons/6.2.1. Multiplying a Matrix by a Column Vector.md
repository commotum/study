## Introduction

To multiply a matrix by a column vector, we multiply each row in the matrix by the column vector using the dot product.

For example, suppose we would like to multiply
$\begin{bmatrix}1 & 3 \\ 6 & 7\end{bmatrix}$
and
$\begin{bmatrix}-3 \\ 4\end{bmatrix}$

First, we write the multiplication as follows:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}1 & 3 \\ 6 & 7\end{bmatrix}
$$

The result of the multiplication is another column vector $\mathbf{y}$ with the same dimensions as $\mathbf{x}$.
Now, we calculate the entries of the product (shown as $\ast$'s in the equation above).

To find $y_1$, the $1$st component of $\mathbf{y}$, we calculate the dot product of the $1$st row of $A$ and the vector $\mathbf{x}$:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}1 & 3 \\ 6 & 7\end{bmatrix}
$$

To find $y_2$, the $2$nd component of $\mathbf{y}$, we calculate the dot product of the $2$nd row of $A$ and the vector $\mathbf{x}$:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}1 & 3 \\ 6 & 7\end{bmatrix}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\begin{bmatrix}9 \\ 10\end{bmatrix}
$$

## Multiplying a 2x2 Matrix by a Column Vector

**Example:** Find $A\mathbf{x}$ if
$\begin{bmatrix}1 & 3 \\ 7 & -2\end{bmatrix}$
and
$\begin{bmatrix}2 \\ 5\end{bmatrix}$

**Explanation**

We multiply each row in the matrix by the column vector using the dot product. Multiplying the first row, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
\begin{bmatrix}1 & 3 \\ 7 & -2\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}2 \\ 5\end{bmatrix} &= \begin{bmatrix}1 \cdot 2 + 3 \cdot 5 \\ ∗\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}17 \\ ∗\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Multiplying the second row, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
\begin{bmatrix}1 & 3 \\ 7 & -2\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}2 \\ 5\end{bmatrix} &= \begin{bmatrix}17 \\ 7 \cdot 2 + (-2) \cdot 5\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}17 \\ 4\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,
$\begin{bmatrix}17 \\ 4\end{bmatrix}$

## Multiplying a 3x3 Matrix by a Column Vector

**Example:** Find $A\mathbf{x}$ if
$\begin{bmatrix}-3 & 1 & 1 \\ 2 & 0 & 0 \\ -1 & -2 & 5\end{bmatrix}$
and
$\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ -2 \\ 0\end{bmatrix}$

**Explanation**

We multiply each row in the matrix by the column vector using the dot product:

$$
\begin{aligned}
Ax &= \begin{bmatrix}-3 & 1 & 1 \\ 2 & 0 & 0 \\ -1 & -2 & 5\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ -2 \\ 0\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}(-3) \cdot 1 + 1 \cdot (-2) + 1 \cdot 0 \\ 2 \cdot 1 + 0 \cdot (-2) + 0 \cdot 0 \\ (-1) \cdot 1 + (-2) \cdot (-2) + 5 \cdot 0\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}-3 - 2 + 0 \\ 2 + 0 + 0 \\ -1 + 4 + 0\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}-5 \\ 2 \\ 3\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Multiplying a Matrix With Variable Entries by a Column Vector

**Example:** Find $A\mathbf{x}$ if
$\begin{bmatrix}a & c \\ b & d\end{bmatrix}$
and
$\begin{bmatrix}3 \\ 2\end{bmatrix}$

**Explanation**

We multiply each row in the matrix by the column vector using the dot product:

$$
\begin{aligned}
Ax &= \begin{bmatrix}a & c \\ b & d\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}3 \\ 2\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}a \cdot 3 + c \cdot 2 \\ b \cdot 3 + d \cdot 2\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}3a + 2c \\ 3b + 2d\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="alternative-rule"></a>
## Alternative Rule

An alternative, yet equivalent, procedure for multiplying a matrix by a column vector involves adding scaled columns of the matrix $A$ as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
Ax &= \begin{bmatrix}a & c \\ b & d\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}3 \\ 2\end{bmatrix} \\
&= 3\begin{bmatrix}a \\ b\end{bmatrix} + 2\begin{bmatrix}c \\ d\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}3a \\ 3b\end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix}2c \\ 2d\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}3a + 2c \\ 3b + 2d\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

If we denote
$\begin{bmatrix}a \\ b\end{bmatrix}$
and
$\begin{bmatrix}c \\ d\end{bmatrix}$
then we can also write the multiplication as follows:

$$
A\mathbf{x}={\color{blue}3}\mathbf{u}_1 + {\color{blue}2} \mathbf{u}_2
$$

The expression above is a **linear combination** of the vectors $\mathbf{u}_1$ and $\mathbf{u}_2$, where the corresponding coefficients are given by the entries of the vector $\mathbf{x}$.
