## Introduction

Consider the shaded region $D$ shown below.

![](<../Source/6.5.11. Area Scale Factors of Linear Transformations/Images/c76f85521d0917ecd530732e64182977.png>)

How can we calculate the area of the image of $D$ under the action of the linear transformation $\mathbf{T}$ that is given by its standard matrix
$\begin{bmatrix}0 & -2 \\ 2 & 0\end{bmatrix}$

For any linear transformation $\mathbf{T}$, we have the following connection between the area of $D$ and the area of the image $\mathbf{T}(D)$ under the action of $\mathbf{T}$:

$$
\text{Area}(\mathbf{T}(D)) = \mid \det(T) \mid \cdot \text{Area}(D)
$$

This equation is important. It tells us that $\mid \det(T) \mid$ is the **area scale factor** of the transformation.

From the diagram, we have

$$
\text{Area}(D) = 2 \cdot 3 = 6
$$

Computing the determinant of $T$, we get

$$
\det(T) = 0 \cdot 0 - (-2) \cdot 2 = 4
$$

Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
Area(T(D)) &= \begin{vmatrix}det(T) & \cdot Area(D) \\ = & 4\end{vmatrix}\cdot 6 \\
&= 24
\end{aligned}
$$

## Computing the Area Scale Factor of a Linear Transformation

**Example:** What is the area scale factor of the linear transformation $\mathbf{T}$ that is given by its standard matrix
$\begin{bmatrix}2 & 3 \\ 1 & 4\end{bmatrix}$

**Explanation**

The area scale factor of a linear transformation $\mathbf T$ with standard matrix $T$ is equal to $\mid \det(T) \mid$. Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\begin{vmatrix}det(T) & &= & 2 \cdot 4 - 3 \cdot 1\end{vmatrix} \\
&= \mid 8 - 3 \mid \\
&= 5
\end{aligned}
$$

## Calculating the Area of the Image of an Object Under a Linear Transformation Given a Diagram

**Example:** Consider the shaded region $D$ shown above. What is the area of the image of $D$ under the action of the linear transformation $\mathbf{T}$ with the standard matrix $T$, given below?
$\begin{bmatrix}4 & 2 \\ -1 & 2\end{bmatrix}$

![](<../Source/6.5.11. Area Scale Factors of Linear Transformations/Images/db07fd70ec06ccd7ac9258c2f4c75f22.png>)

**Explanation**

Recall that for any linear transformation $\mathbf{T}$, we have the following connection between the area of $D$ and the area of the image $\mathbf{T}(D)$ under the action of $\mathbf{T}$:

$$
\text{Area}(\mathbf{T}(D)) = \mid \det(T) \mid \cdot \text{Area}(D)
$$

From the diagram, we have

$$
\text{Area}(D) = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 3 \cdot 4 = 6
$$

Computing the determinant of $T$, we get

$$
\det(T) = 4 \cdot 2 - 2 \cdot (-1) = 10
$$

Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
Area(T(D)) &= \begin{vmatrix}det(T) & \cdot Area(D) \\ = & 10\end{vmatrix}\cdot 6 \\
&= 60
\end{aligned}
$$

## Calculating the Area of the Image of an Object Under a Linear Transformation

**Example:** A triangle $D$ has vertices $(1,1)$, $(4,5)$, and $(-2,1)$. What is the area of the image of $D$ under the action of the linear transformation $\mathbf{T}$ with the standard matrix $T$, given below?
$\begin{bmatrix}-7 & 5 \\ -2 & 3\end{bmatrix}$

**Explanation**

First, let's sketch our region:

![](<../Source/6.5.11. Area Scale Factors of Linear Transformations/Images/6c06e31f3407ba3e54c61c5b222b6d9d.png>)

Recall that for any linear transformation $\mathbf{T}$, we have the following connection between the area of $D$ and the area of the image $\mathbf{T}(D)$ under the action of $\mathbf{T}$:

$$
\text{Area}(\mathbf{T}(D)) = \mid \det(T) \mid \cdot \text{Area}(D)
$$

From the diagram, we have

$$
\text{Area}(D) = \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 3 \cdot 4 = 6
$$

Computing the determinant of $T$, we get

$$
\det(T) = (-7) \cdot 3 - 5 \cdot (-2) = -11
$$

Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
Area(T(D)) &= \begin{vmatrix}det(T) & \cdot Area(D) \\ = & -11\end{vmatrix}\cdot 6 \\
&= 66
\end{aligned}
$$
