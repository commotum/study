## Introduction

Suppose that the vector $\mathbf{a}$ has magnitude $7$, the vector $\mathbf{b}$ has magnitude $6$, and the angle between the vectors is

$$
\theta=45^\circ
$$

Then the magnitude of the cross product $\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b}$ is

$$
\begin{aligned}
\begin{vmatrix}a \cdot b & &= & a\end{vmatrix} \mid b \mid \sin θ \\
&= 7 \cdot 6 \cdot \sin 45^{∘} \\
&= 7 \cdot 6 \cdot \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \\
&= 21\sqrt{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Geometrically, $\mid \,\mathbf{a}\, \mid \mid \,\mathbf{b}\, \mid \sin\theta$ is the formula for the area of the parallelogram with sides $\mid \,\mathbf{a}\, \mid$ and $\mid \,\mathbf{b}\, \mid$ and the angle $\theta$ between the sides. So the magnitude of the cross product represents the area of the parallelogram spanned by the vectors $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$, as shown in the picture below.

![](<../Source/5.5.4. Finding Areas Using the Cross Product/Images/7ba2cbf2f6480a4fdb28e5571dc7ad09.png>)

**Note:** Since in the formula for the area, we are interested only in the magnitude of the cross product, it does not matter whether we take $\mid \,\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b}\, \mid$ or $\mid \,\mathbf{b}\times\mathbf{a}\, \mid$ as these vectors have the same magnitude (but opposite directions).

## Calculating the Area of the Triangle Spanned by Two Vectors

**Example:** Calculate the area of the triangle spanned by $\mathbf{a}=\langle 4,0,-3 \rangle$ and $\mathbf{b}=\langle 5,-1,2 \rangle$.

**Explanation**

Using the formula, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
a \cdot b &= \begin{vmatrix}i & j & k \\ 4 & 0 & -3 \\ 5 & -1 & 2\end{vmatrix} \\
&=\begin{vmatrix}0 & -3 \\ -1 & 2\end{vmatrix}i -\begin{vmatrix}4 & -3 \\ 5 & 2\end{vmatrix}j +\begin{vmatrix}4 & 0 \\ 5 & -1\end{vmatrix}k \\
&= (0 \cdot 2 - (-3) \cdot (-1))i - (4 \cdot 2 - (-3) \cdot 5)j + (4 \cdot (-1) - 0 \cdot 5)k \\
&=-3i - 23j - 4k \\
&= ⟨ - 3,-23,-4⟩
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the area of the corresponding parallelogram is

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= \mid a \cdot b \mid \\
&= \sqrt{(-3)^{2} + (-23)^{2} + (-4)^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{554}
\end{aligned}
$$

The area of the triangle is half of the area of the respective parallelogram. So, the area of the triangle is

$$
A_{\triangle} = \dfrac{1}{2}A = \dfrac{\sqrt{554}}{2}
$$

## Calculating the Area of a Triangle Given Its Vertices

**Example:** Calculate the area of the $\bigtriangleup\!ABC$, if $A(1,0,-2)$, $B(-2,0,1)$, $C(1,1,-1)$.

**Explanation**

First, we find the vectors $\overrightarrow{AB}$ and $\overrightarrow{AC}$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
AB^(→) &= b - a \\
&= (-2i + k) - (i - 2k) \\
&=-3i + 3k \\
&= ⟨ - 3, 0, 3⟩ \\
AC^(→) &= c - a \\
&= (i + j - k) - (i - 2k) \\
&= j + k \\
&= ⟨0, 1, 1⟩
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, using the formula to compute the cross product, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
AB^(→) \cdot AC^(→) &= \begin{vmatrix}i & j & k \\ -3 & 0 & 3 \\ 0 & 1 & 1\end{vmatrix} \\
&=\begin{vmatrix}0 & 3 \\ 1 & 1\end{vmatrix}i -\begin{vmatrix}-3 & 3 \\ 0 & 1\end{vmatrix}j +\begin{vmatrix}-3 & 0 \\ 0 & 1\end{vmatrix}k \\
&= (0 \cdot 1 - 3 \cdot 1)i - (-3 \cdot 1 - 3 \cdot 0)j + (-3 \cdot 1 - 0 \cdot 0)k \\
&=-3i + 3j - 3k \\
&= ⟨ - 3, 3,-3⟩
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the area of the parallelogram spanned by $\overrightarrow{AB}$ and $\overrightarrow{AC}$ is

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= \mid AB^(→) \cdot AC^(→) \mid \\
&= \sqrt{(-3)^{2} + 3^{2} + (-3)^{2}} \\
&= 3\sqrt{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, the area of the triangle is half of the area of the respective parallelogram. So, the area of $\bigtriangleup\!ABC$ is

$$
A_{\bigtriangleup} = \dfrac{1}{2}A = \dfrac{3\sqrt{3}}{2}
$$

## Calculating the Altitude of a Triangle Given Its Vertices

**Example:** Calculate the length of the altitude $CH$ of the $\bigtriangleup\!ABC$ if $A(-1,1,0)$, $B(1,1,2)$, $C(1,-1,1)$.

**Explanation**

Note that the length of the altitude $CH$ can be related to the area of $\bigtriangleup\!ABC$ as follows:

$$
A_{\bigtriangleup} = \dfrac{1}{2} CH \cdot AB
$$

So, to find $CH$, we first find $A_{\bigtriangleup}$. To start, we find the vectors $\overrightarrow{AB}$ and $\overrightarrow{AC}$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
AB^(→) &= b - a \\
&= (i + j + 2k) - (-i + j) \\
&= 2i + 2k \\
&= ⟨2, 0, 2⟩ \\
AC^(→) &= c - a \\
&= (i - j + k) - (-i + j) \\
&= 2i - 2j + k \\
&= ⟨2,-2, 1⟩
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
AB^(→) \cdot AC^(→) &= \begin{vmatrix}i & j & k \\ 2 & 0 & 2 \\ 2 & -2 & 1\end{vmatrix} \\
&=\begin{vmatrix}0 & 2 \\ -2 & 1\end{vmatrix}i -\begin{vmatrix}2 & 2 \\ 2 & 1\end{vmatrix}j +\begin{vmatrix}2 & 0 \\ 2 & -2\end{vmatrix}k \\
&= (0 \cdot 1 - 2 \cdot (-2))i - (2 \cdot 1 - 2 \cdot 2)j + (2 \cdot (-2) - 0 \cdot 2)k \\
&= 4i + 2j - 4k \\
&= ⟨4, 2,-4⟩
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the area of the parallelogram spanned by $\overrightarrow{AB}$ and $\overrightarrow{AC}$ is

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= \mid AB^(→) \cdot AC^(→) \mid \\
&= \sqrt{4^{2} + 2^{2} + (-4)^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{36} \\
&= 6
\end{aligned}
$$

The area of the triangle is half of the area of the respective parallelogram. So, the area of $\bigtriangleup\!ABC$ is

$$
A_{\bigtriangleup} = \dfrac{1}{2}A = \dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 6 = 3
$$

Let's also compute $AB$ since it appears in the formula:

$$
\begin{aligned}
AB &= \mid AB^(→) \mid \\
&= \sqrt{2^{2} + 0^{2} + 2^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{8} \\
&= 2\sqrt{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, using the formula

$$
A_{\bigtriangleup} = \dfrac{1}{2} CH \cdot AB
$$

we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
A_{△} &= \frac{1}{2}CH \cdot AB \\
3 &= \frac{1}{2}CH \cdot 2\sqrt{2} \\
3 &= CH \cdot \sqrt{2} \\
CH &= \frac{3}{\sqrt{2}} \\
CH &= \frac{3\sqrt{2}}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$
