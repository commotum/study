## Identifying Convergent Series Using Multiple Convergence Tests

**Example:** Which of the following series converge?

1. $\displaystyle \sum_{n=1}^\infty \dfrac{e^{2n}}{n^{99}}$
2. $\displaystyle \sum_{n=1}^\infty \dfrac{\ln(2n+1)}{n}$
3. $\displaystyle \sum_{n=1}^\infty \dfrac{4^n}{n!}$

**Explanation**

We use the flowchart below to determine which tests to use for each series.

![](<../Source/4.5.13. Selecting Procedures for Analyzing Infinite Series/Images/7513382063a8ed284cd0ef64a1b0cfc1.png>)

With that in mind, let's test each series in turn.

- For the first series, we use the $n$th term test for divergence. Let
$a_n= \dfrac{e^{2n}}{n^{99}}$.
Then, we have $\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}a_n=\infty$, since the exponential term in the numerator grows faster than the polynomial in the denominator. Therefore, by the $n$th term test, the series
$\displaystyle \sum_{n=1}^\infty \dfrac{e^{2n}}{n^{99}}$
is divergent.
- For the second series, we use the comparison test. Observe that
$\ln (2n + 1) > 1⟹(\ln (2n + 1))/(n) > \frac{1}{n}$
for all $n\geq 1$. Now, the series
$\displaystyle \sum_{n=1}^\infty \dfrac{1}{n}$
is divergent by the $p$-series test. So, by the comparison test, the series
$\displaystyle \sum_{n=1}^\infty \dfrac{\ln(2n+1)}{n}$
is also divergent.
- For the final series, we use the ratio test. Let
$a_n= \dfrac{4^n}{n!}$.
Working out the necessary limit, we get
$L|= lim_(n → ∞) \mid \frac{a_{n + 1}}{a_{n}} \mid; = lim_(n → ∞)((4^{n + 1})/((n + 1)!) \div \frac{4^{n}}{n!}); = lim_(n → ∞)((4^{n + 1})/((n + 1)!) \cdot \frac{n!}{4^{n}}); = lim_(n → ∞)((4(4^{n}))/(n!(n + 1)) \cdot \frac{n!}{4^{n}}); = lim_(n → ∞)((4(4^{n}))/(n!(n + 1)) \cdot \frac{n!}{4^{n}}); = lim_(n → ∞)(\frac{4}{n + 1}); = 0$.
Since $L=0 < 1$, we conclude that the series is convergent.

In conclusion, only series III converges.

## Identifying True Statements About Infinite Series

**Example:** If the series $\displaystyle \sum_{n=1}^\infty a_n$ is convergent and $a_n$ is a positive, strictly decreasing sequence for all $n\geq 1$, then which of the following statements *must* be true?

1. Given that $f(n) = a_n$, where $f(x)$ is continuous and strictly decreasing, then $\displaystyle\int_1^\infty f(x)\,\textrm d x$ converges.
2. $\displaystyle \sum_{n=1}^\infty (-1)^n a_n$ is convergent.
3. $\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\left| \dfrac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}\right| = 1$.

**Explanation**

Let's analyze each statement.

- Statement I is true. The sequence $a_n$ and the function $f(x)$ satisfy the conditions for the integral test. According to the integral test, since $\displaystyle \sum_{n=1}^\infty a_n$ is convergent, then $\displaystyle\int_1^\infty f(x)\,\textrm d x$ is convergent.
- Statement II is true. Since $\displaystyle \sum_{n=1}^\infty a_n$ is convergent, we must have that $\displaystyle \lim_{n\to\infty}a_n = 0$. By the alternating series test, then, $\displaystyle \sum_{n=1}^\infty (-1)^n a_n$ is convergent.
- Statement III is false. Recall that with the ratio test, $L = \displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\left| \dfrac{a_{n+1}}{a_n}\right| = 1$ gives no conclusion as to whether the series converges or diverges. In other words, there exist convergent series with $L=1$.

In conclusion, only statements I and II must be true.
