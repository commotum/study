## Introduction

A repeating decimal can be expressed as a geometric series whose common ratio is a power of

$$
\dfrac 1 {10}
$$

For example, the number $0.\overline{8}$ can be expressed as

$$
\begin{aligned}
0.8^(―) &= 0.8 + 0.08 + 0.008 + … \\
&= \frac{8}{10} + \frac{8}{100} + \frac{8}{1000} + …
\end{aligned}
$$

We have

$$
a_1 = \dfrac{8}{10}
$$

and

$$
r = \dfrac {0.08}{0.8} = \dfrac 1 {10}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
0.8^(―) &= ∑_(n = 1)^(∞)a_{1}r^{n - 1} \\
&= ∑_(n = 1)^(∞)\frac{8}{10}(\frac{1}{10})^{n - 1} \\
&= ∑_(n = 1)^(∞)8 \cdot \frac{1}{10} \cdot (\frac{1}{10})^{n} \cdot (\frac{1}{10})^{-1} \\
&= ∑_(n = 1)^(∞)8 \cdot \frac{1}{10} \cdot (\frac{1}{10})^{n} \cdot (\frac{1}{10})^{-1} \\
&= ∑_(n = 1)^(∞)8 \cdot \frac{1}{10} \cdot (\frac{1}{10})^{n} \cdot 10 \\
&= ∑_(n = 1)^(∞)8(\frac{1}{10})^{n}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Expressing a Repeating Decimal as an Infinite Geometric Series

**Example:** Write $0.\overline{23}$ as geometric series.

**Explanation**

The given repeating decimal can be written as

$$
0.\overline{23} = 0.23 + 0.002\,3 + 0.000\,023 + \dots
$$

$$
0.\overline{23} = \dfrac{23}{100} + \dfrac{23}{10\,000} + \dfrac{23}{1\,000\,000}+\dots
$$

where the first term is

$$
a_1 = \dfrac{23}{100}
$$

and the common ratio is

$$
\dfrac{1}{100}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
0.23^(―) &= ∑_(n = 1)^(∞)a_{1}r^{n - 1} \\
&= ∑_(n = 1)^(∞)\frac{23}{100} \cdot (\frac{1}{100})^{n - 1} \\
&= ∑_(n = 1)^(∞)23 \cdot \frac{1}{100} \cdot (\frac{1}{100})^{n - 1} \\
&= ∑_(n = 1)^(∞)23 \cdot \frac{1}{100} \cdot (\frac{1}{100})^{n} \cdot (\frac{1}{100})^{-1} \\
&= ∑_(n = 1)^(∞)23(\frac{1}{100})^{n}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Expressing a Repeating Decimal as the Sum of a Number and a Geometric Series

**Example:** Write $0.1\overline{4}$ as geometric series.

**Explanation**

The given repeating decimal can be written as

$$
\begin{aligned}
0.14^(―) &= 0.1444... \\
&= 0.1 + 0.04 + 0.004 + 0.0004 + … \\
&= \frac{1}{10} + (\frac{4}{100} + \frac{4}{1000} + \frac{4}{10000} + …)
\end{aligned}
$$

The series in the parentheses is a geometric series with the first term

$$
a_1 = \dfrac{4}{100}
$$

and the common ratio

$$
r=\dfrac{1}{10}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
0.14^(―) &= \frac{1}{10} + ∑_(n = 1)^(∞)a_{1}r^{n - 1} \\
&= \frac{1}{10} + ∑_(n = 1)^(∞)\frac{4}{100}(\frac{1}{10})^{n - 1} \\
&= \frac{1}{10} + ∑_(n = 1)^(∞)\frac{4 \cdot 10}{100}(\frac{1}{10})^{n} \\
&= \frac{1}{10} + ∑_(n = 1)^(∞)\frac{4}{10}(\frac{1}{10})^{n}
\end{aligned}
$$
