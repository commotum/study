## Introduction

Suppose we have a coin that we suspect is biased *against* landing on heads (i.e., the coin is *less* likely to land on heads than tails). We wish to design a hypothesis test at the

$$
\alpha = 5\%
$$

significance level to determine whether or not our suspicions are correct with the following null and alternative hypotheses:

- $H_0:\:\: p =\dfrac12$
- $H_1:\:\: p <\dfrac12$

Let the test statistic $X$ equal the number of heads we get when the coin is tossed at random $10$ times in a row. Under the null hypothesis, our test statistic $X$ can be modeled as the following binomial random variable:

$$
X\sim B\left(10,\dfrac12\right)
$$

The probability mass function $f(x)$ of $X$ is shown below.

![](<../Source/8.1.3. Critical Regions for Left-Tailed Hypothesis Tests/Images/5b981e25330127c81a3670f95619611b.png>)

The **critical region** is a set containing all possible $X$-values that are collectively unlikely under the null hypothesis and, therefore, cause the null hypothesis to be rejected. For *left-tailed* hypothesis tests like this, the critical regions lie to the *left* of the corresponding probability distribution.

Notice that getting either $0$, $1$, or $2$ heads out of $10$ coin tosses causes us to reject the null hypothesis, because

$$
P(X \le 0) \approx 0.1% < α, P(X \le 1) \approx 1.1% < α, P(X \le 2) \approx 4.4% < α
$$

All of these values lie in the critical region. However, $X=3$ does not lie in the critical region because

$$
P(X \leq 3) \approx 11.72\% > \alpha
$$

Therefore, the critical region for this hypothesis test at the $5\%$ significance level is

$$
X \in \{0, 1, {\color{red}{2}}\}
$$

We can also write this as

$$
X\leq 2
$$

The value

$$
X={\color{red}{2}}
$$

is called the **critical value**. The critical value lies on the boundary between the critical region and the region where we do not reject $H_0$.

Finally, note that the critical region and critical value depend on our choice of $\alpha{:}$

- If we set $\alpha = 1\%$, for example, this reduces the likelihood of rejecting the null hypothesis.
- Consequently, the critical region is now $X\in \{{\color{red}{0}}\}$ (or $X\leq 0$), and the critical value is $X={\color{red}{0}}$.

## Identifying Critical Regions Using CDFs

**Example:** On average, $7$ patients per week are admitted to a hospital's emergency room due to traffic incidents. The local authorities decided to install several new warning signs to alert drivers of upcoming dangers.

Let $X$ represent the number of patients admitted to the emergency department due to traffic incidents during a particular week after the new signs were installed. Under the null hypothesis that the number of patients is the same as before, what is the critical region of a one-tailed test at a significance level of $10\%$?

*Hint: You may use the table below, representing the **cumulative distribution function** of $X$ under the null hypothesis.*

| $\quad x \quad$ | $0$ | $1$ | $2$ | $3$ | $4$ | $5$ | $\quad\cdots\quad$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $P(X\leq x)$ | $0.0009$ | $0.0073$ | $0.0296$ | $0.0818$ | $0.1730$ | $0.3007$ | $\quad\cdots\quad$ |

**Explanation**

Let $\lambda$ be the average number of patients per week. Then, we have the following null and alternative hypotheses:

$$
\begin{aligned}
H_{0}: λ &= 7 \\
&= H_{1}: λ \mid < 7
\end{aligned}
$$

The critical region is the set of values of $X$ for which we reject the null hypothesis.

This is a one-sided test where the local authorities wish to conclude that the number of patients in a hospital's emergency room has decreased. This would correspond to observing an abnormally small value of $X$.

To reject the null hypothesis at a significance level of $10\%$, we must observe a value $x$ such that

$$
P(X \leq x) = 0.1
$$

under the null hypothesis.

Using the table, we have the following probabilities:

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(X \le 0) &= 0.0009 < 0.1✓ \\
P(X \le 1) &= 0.0073 < 0.1✓ \\
P(X \le 2) &= 0.0296 < 0.1✓ \\
P(X \le 3) &= 0.0818 < 0.1✓ \\
P(X \le 4) &= 0.1730≮0.1\cdot
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the values of $X$ that would cause the null hypothesis to be rejected at a significance level of $10\%$ are $0,1,2$, and $3$.

Therefore, the critical region is

$$
X\leq 3
$$

## Identifying Critical Regions Using PMFs

**Example:** An engineer knows from experience that the proportion of defective items produced by a particular machine is $1.5\%$. The machine is replaced with a new model, and the engineer thinks the proportion of defective items has decreased. Let $X$ be the number of defective items in a box of $500$ produced by the new machine. Under the null hypothesis that the proportion of defective items has not changed, what is the critical region of a one-tailed test at a significance level of $5\%$?

*Hint: You may use the table below, representing the **probability mass function** of $X$ under the null hypothesis.*

| $\quad x \quad$ | $\,0\,$ | $\,1\,$ | $\,2\,$ | $\,3\,$ | $\,4\,$ | $\,5\,$ | $\quad\cdots\quad$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $P(X = x)$ | $\,0.0005\,$ | $\,0.0040\,$ | $\,0.0151\,$ | $\,0.0382\,$ | $\,0.0723\,$ | $\,0.1092\,$ | $\quad\cdots\quad$ |

**Explanation**

Let $p$ be the probability that a randomly selected item is defective. Then, we have the following null and alternative hypotheses:

$$
\begin{aligned}
H_{0}: p &= 0.015 \\
&= H_{1}: p \mid < 0.015
\end{aligned}
$$

The critical region is the set of values of $X$ for which we reject the null hypothesis.

This is a one-sided test where the engineer wishes to conclude that the proportion of defective items has decreased. This would correspond to observing an abnormally small value of $X$.

To reject the null hypothesis at a significance level of $5\%$, we must observe a value $x$ such that

$$
P(X \leq x) = 0.05
$$

under the null hypothesis.

Using the table, we have the following probabilities:

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(X \le 0) &= P(X = 0) \\
&= 0.0005 \\
&< 0.05✓ \\
P(X \le 1) &= P(X = 0) + P(X = 1) \\
&= 0.0005 + 0.0040 \\
&= 0.0045 \\
&< 0.05✓ \\
P(X \le 2) &= P(X = 0) + P(X = 1) + P(X = 2) \\
&= 0.0005 + 0.0040 + 0.0151 \\
&= 0.0196 \\
&< 0.05✓ \\
P(X \le 3) &= P(X = 0) + P(X = 1) + P(X = 2) + P(X = 3) \\
&= 0.0005 + 0.0040 + 0.0151 + 0.0382 \\
&= 0.0578 \\
&= ≮0.05\cdot
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the values of $X$ that would cause the null hypothesis to be rejected at a significance level of $5\%$ are $0,1$, and $2$.

Therefore, the critical region is

$$
X\leq 2
$$

## Identifying Critical Values

**Example:** A gym owner suspects that the average number of customers per hour has decreased since the opening of a competing gym. Before the competing gym opened, $9$ customers per hour visited the gym. Let $X$ represent the number of customers arriving at the gym in a randomly selected one-hour period. Under the null hypothesis that the average number of customers is the same as before, what is the critical value of a one-tailed test at a significance level of $1\%$?

*Hint: You may use the table below, representing the **cumulative distribution function** of $X$ under the null hypothesis.*

| $\quad x \quad$ | $0$ | $1$ | $2$ | $3$ | $4$ | $5$ | $6$ | $\quad\cdots\quad$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $P(X\leq x)$ | $0.0001$ | $0.0012$ | $0.0062$ | $0.0212$ | $0.0550$ | $0.1157$ | $0.2068$ | $\quad\cdots\quad$ |

**Explanation**

Let $\lambda$ be the average number of customers per hour. Then, we have the following null and alternative hypotheses:

$$
\begin{aligned}
H_{0}: λ &= 9 \\
&= H_{1}: λ \mid < 9
\end{aligned}
$$

The critical region is the set of values of $X$ for which we reject the null hypothesis. A critical value lies on the boundary of the critical region.

This is a one-sided test where the owner suspects that the number of customers per hour has decreased. This would correspond to observing an abnormally small value of $X$.

To reject the null hypothesis at a significance level of $1\%$, we must observe a value $x$ such that

$$
P(X \leq x) = 0.01
$$

under the null hypothesis.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(X \le 0) &= 0.0001 < 0.01✓ \\
P(X \le 1) &= 0.0012 < 0.01✓ \\
P(X \le 2) &= 0.0062 < 0.01✓ \\
P(X \le 3) &= 0.0212≮0.01\cdot
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the values of $X$ that would cause the null hypothesis to be rejected at a significance level of $1\%$ are the values such that

$$
X \leq 2
$$

Therefore, the critical value is $X=2$.
