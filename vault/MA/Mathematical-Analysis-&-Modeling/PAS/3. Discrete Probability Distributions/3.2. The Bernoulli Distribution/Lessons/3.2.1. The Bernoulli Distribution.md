## Introduction

A discrete random variable $X$ follows a **Bernoulli distribution** if it has the following probability mass function:

$$
{p,\begin{vmatrix}x = 1 \\ 1 - p,\end{vmatrix}x = 0
$$

The Bernoulli distribution models the outcome of a **Bernoulli trial**, an experiment with only two events: "success" and "failure".
We label "success" by $X=1$ with a probability of $p$ and "failure" by $X=0$ with a probability of $1-p$.

If a random variable $X$ follows a Bernoulli distribution, we write

$$
X \sim \text{Bernoulli} (p)
$$

For example, suppose a fair die is rolled. Each roll of the die is a Bernoulli trial. Let's interpret a "success" as the event that we roll a $6$. Then, the outcome of a roll of the die $X$ can be modeled as a Bernoulli random variable

$$
X \sim \text{Bernoulli} \left(\dfrac16\right)
$$

Therefore, the probability of success ($X=1$) is

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(X = 1) &= f(1) = \frac{1}{6}
\end{aligned}
$$

while the probability of failure ($X=0$) is

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(X = 0) &= f(0) = 1 - \frac{1}{6} = \frac{5}{6}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Computing the Probability at X=1

**Example:** Given that $X \sim \textrm{Bernoulli}(0.55)$, compute $P(X = 1)$.

**Explanation**

If $X \sim \textrm{Bernoulli}(p)$, then $X$ has the following probability mass function:

$$
{p,\begin{vmatrix}x = 1 \\ 1 - p,\end{vmatrix}x = 0
$$

Here, $X \sim \textrm{Bernoulli}(0.55)$, so the distribution of $X$ in this case is

$$
{0.55,\begin{vmatrix}x = 1 \\ 0.45,\end{vmatrix}x = 0
$$

Therefore,

$$
P(X = 1) = f(1) = 0.55
$$

## Computing the Probability of an Intersection

**Example:** Given that $X\sim \textrm{Bernoulli}(0.7)$ and $Y \sim \textrm{Bernoulli}(0.6)$ are independent, calculate $P(X=1 \, \cap \, Y=1)$.

**Explanation**

Using the multiplication law for independent events, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(X = 1∩Y = 1) &= P(X = 1) \cdot P(Y = 1)
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, since $X\sim \textrm{Bernoulli}(0.7)$, we have

$$
P(X=1) = 0.7, \quad P(X=0) = 0.3
$$

and since $Y \sim \textrm{Bernoulli}(0.6)$, we have

$$
P(Y=1)=0.6, \quad P(Y=0)=0.4
$$

Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(X = 1∩Y = 1) &= P(X = 1) \cdot P(Y = 1) \\
&= 0.7 \cdot 0.6 \\
&= 0.42
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="computing-the-probability-of-a-union"></a>
## Computing the Probability of a Union

**Example:** Given that $X\sim \textrm{Bernoulli}(0.3)$ and $Y \sim \textrm{Bernoulli}(0.7)$ are independent, calculate $P(X=0 \, \cup \, Y=0)$.

**Explanation**

By the addition law, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(X = 0∪Y = 0) &= P(X = 0) + P(Y = 0) - P(X = 0∩Y = 0)
\end{aligned}
$$

Using the multiplication law for independent events, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(X = 0∩Y = 0) &= P(X = 0) \cdot P(Y = 0)
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(X = 0∪Y = 0) &= P(X = 0) + P(Y = 0) - P(X = 0) \cdot P(Y = 0)
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, since $X\sim \textrm{Bernoulli}(0.3)$, we have

$$
P(X=1) = 0.3, \quad P(X=0) = 0.7
$$

and since $Y \sim \textrm{Bernoulli}(0.7)$, we have

$$
P(Y=1)=0.7, \quad P(Y=0)=0.3
$$

Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(X = 0∪Y = 0) &= P(X = 0) + P(Y = 0) - P(X = 0) \cdot P(Y = 0) \\
&= 0.7 + 0.3 - 0.7 \cdot 0.3 \\
&= 1 - 0.21 \\
&= 0.79
\end{aligned}
$$
