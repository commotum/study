## Introduction

We can easily extend the notion of conditional probability distributions for discrete random variables to continuous random variables.

For continuous random variables $X$ and $Y$, the **conditional probability density function of $X$ given $Y = y$** is given by

$$
f_{X \mid Y} (x \mid y) =\dfrac{f(x, y)}{f_Y (y)}, \qquad f_Y(y) \neq 0
$$

and the conditional probability density function of $Y$ given $X = x$ is given by

$$
f_{Y \mid X} (y \mid x) =\dfrac{f(x, y)}{f_X (x)}, \qquad f_X(x) \neq 0
$$

Notice that these definitions are analogous to those for discrete random variables.

To compute probabilities, we work directly with these conditional PDFs. For example, to calculate the **conditional probability of $X$ given $Y$,** we use

$$
P(X \in A \mid Y = y) =\int_A f_{X \mid Y} (x \mid y)\, \textrm dx
$$

In general, the conditional distribution of $X$ given $Y$ does not equal the conditional distribution of $Y$ given $X$. That is

$$
f_{X\mid Y}(x \mid y) \ne f_{Y\mid X}(y \mid x)
$$

Finally, if $X$ and $Y$ are independent, then

$$
f_{X\mid Y}(x \mid y)=f_X(x),\qquad f_{Y\mid X}(y \mid x)=f_Y(y)
$$

## Working With the Definition of a Conditional PDF

**Example:** ${(3(x^{2} + y^{2}))/(3x^{2} + 1),\begin{vmatrix}0 \le x \le 1, 0 \le y \le 1 \\ 0,\end{vmatrix}otherwise$

Consider the continuous random variables $X$ and $Y$, for which the conditional probability density function $f_{Y \,\mid \, X}(y \, \mid \, x)$ and the marginal density function $f_X(x)$ are shown above. Find the expression of the joint probability density function $f(x,y)$ for $0 \leq x \leq 1, \: 0 \leq y \leq 1$.

**Explanation**

Recall that the conditional probability density function of $Y$ given $X=x$ for two continuous random variables $X$ and $Y$ is given by

$$
f_{Y \,\mid \, X}(y \, \mid \, x) =\dfrac{f(x, y)}{f_X (x)}
$$

where

- $f(x,y)$ is the joint probability density function for $X$ and $Y$, and
- $f_X (x)$ is the marginal density function for $X$.

From the equation above, it follows that

$$
f(x,y) = f_{Y \,\mid \, X}(y \, \mid \, x) \cdot f_X (x)
$$

Now, for

$$
0 \leq x \leq 1, \: 0 \leq y \leq 1
$$

we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
f(x, y) &= f_{Y\mid X}(y \mid x) \cdot f_{X}(x) \\
&= (3(x^{2} + y^{2}))/(3x^{2} + 1) \cdot \frac{3x^{2} + 1}{2} \\
&= (3(x^{2} + y^{2}))/(2)
\end{aligned}
$$

For other values of $x$ and $y$, the function $f_{Y \,\mid \, X}(y \, \mid \, x)$ equals $0$. As a result, the product will be equal to $0$ too.

Therefore, the joint probability density function is the following:

$$
{3 (x 2 + y 2) 2,\begin{vmatrix}0 \le x \le 1, 0 \le y \le 1 \\ 0,\end{vmatrix}otherwise
$$

## Finding a Conditional PDF

**Example:** Let $X$ and $Y$ be two continuous random variables with the following joint probability density function:
${x + y,\begin{vmatrix}0 \le x \le 1, 0 \le y \le 1 \\ 0,\end{vmatrix}otherwise$

Find the expression of the conditional probability density function $f_{X \, \mid \, Y} (x \, \mid \, y)$ for $0 \leq x \leq 1, \: 0 \leq y \leq 1$.

**Explanation**

Using the formula for the marginal density function for $Y$, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
f_{Y}(y) &= ∫_{- ∞}^{∞}f(x, y)dx \\
&= ∫_{0}^{1}(x + y)dx \\
&= [\frac{x^{2}}{2} + xy]_{x = 0}^{x = 1} \\
&= \frac{1}{2} + y - 0 \\
&= \frac{2y + 1}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the full expression for $f_Y(y)$ is the following:

$$
{\frac{2y + 1}{2},\begin{vmatrix}0 \le y \le 1 \\ 0,\end{vmatrix}otherwise
$$

Now recall that the conditional probability density function of $X$ given $Y=y$ for two continuous random variables $X$ and $Y$ is given by

$$
f_{X \, \mid \, Y} (x \, \mid \, y) = \dfrac{f(x, y)}{f_Y (y)}
$$

where

- $f(x,y)$ is the joint probability density function for $X$ and $Y$, and
- $f_Y(y)$ is the marginal density function for $Y$.

Now, for

$$
0 \leq x \leq 1, \: 0 \leq y \leq 1
$$

we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
f_{X\mid Y}(x \mid y) &= (f(x, y))/(f_{Y}(y)) \\
&= (x + y)/((\frac{2y + 1}{2})) \\
&= (2(x + y))/(2y + 1)
\end{aligned}
$$

For other values of $x$ and $y$, the function $f(x,y)$ equals $0$. As a result, $f_{X \,\mid \, Y}(x \, \mid \, y)$ will be equal to $0$ too.

Therefore, the conditional probability density function is the following:

$$
{2 (x + y) 2 y + 1,\begin{vmatrix}0 \le x \le 1, 0 \le y \le 1 \\ 0,\end{vmatrix}otherwise
$$

<a id="computing-a-conditional-probability"></a>
## Computing a Conditional Probability

**Example:** Let $X$ and $Y$ be two continuous random variables with the following joint probability density function:

${8x^{2}y,\begin{vmatrix}0 < x \le 1, 0 \le y \le \sqrt{x} \\ 0,\end{vmatrix}otherwise$

Find $P \left(Y < \dfrac{1}{2} \,\bigg \mid \, X=1\right)$.

**Explanation**

Recall that

$$
P(Y \in A \, \mid \, X = x) = \int_A f_{Y \, \mid \, X} (y \, \mid \, x) \, \textrm{d}y
$$

where $f_{Y \, \mid \, X} (y \, \mid \,x)$ is the conditional probability density function.

Using the formula for the marginal density function for $X$, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
f_{X}(x) &= ∫_{- ∞}^{∞}f(x, y)dy \\
&= ∫_{0}^{\sqrt{x}}8x^{2}ydy \\
&= 8x^{2}∫_{0}^{\sqrt{x}}ydy \\
&= 8x^{2}[\frac{1}{2}y^{2}]_{0}^{\sqrt{x}} \\
&= 4x^{2}(\sqrt{x})^{2} - 0 \\
&= 4x^{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the full expression for $f_X(x)$ is the following:

$$
{4x^{3},\begin{vmatrix}0 < x \le 1 \\ 0,\end{vmatrix}otherwise
$$

Now recall that the conditional probability density function for two continuous random variables $X$ and $Y$ is given by

$$
f_{Y \, \mid \, X} (y \, \mid \, x) =\dfrac{f(x, y)}{f_X (x)}
$$

where

- $f(x,y)$ is the joint probability density function for $X$ and $Y$, and
- $f_X (x)$ is the marginal density function for $X$.

Now, for

$$
0< x \leq 1, \: 0 \leq y \leq \sqrt{x}
$$

we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
f_{Y\mid X}(y \mid x) &= (f(x, y))/(f_{X}(x)) \\
&= \frac{8x^{2}y}{4x^{3}} \\
&= \frac{2y}{x}
\end{aligned}
$$

For other values of $x$ and $y$, the function $f(x,y)$ equals $0$. As a result, $f_{Y \, \mid \, X} (y \, \mid \, x)$ will be equal to $0$ too.

Therefore, the conditional probability density function is the following:

$$
{\frac{2y}{x},\begin{vmatrix}0 < x \le 1, 0 \le y \le \sqrt{x} \\ 0,\end{vmatrix}otherwise
$$

Finally, we compute our probability:

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(Y < \frac{1}{2} \mid X = 1) &= ∫_{0}^{1/2}f_{Y\mid X}(y \mid x = 1)dy \\
&= ∫_{0}^{1/2}\frac{2y}{1}dy \\
&= ∫_{0}^{1/2}2ydy \\
&= [y^{2}]_{0}^{1/2} \\
&= \frac{1}{4} - 0 \\
&= \frac{1}{4}
\end{aligned}
$$
