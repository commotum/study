## Introduction

We're used to seeing summations written in compact form using sigma notation.

For example, the notation

$$
\sum_{i=1}^4 (2i-3)
$$

represents the sum of the first $4$ terms of the sequence $a_i = 2i-3$. We evaluate this series as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
∑_(i = 1)^(4)(2i - 3) &= (2(1) - 3) + (2(2) - 3) + (2(3) - 3) + (2(4) - 3) \\
&= (-1) + 1 + 3 + 5 \\
&= 8
\end{aligned}
$$

We have a similar notation that compactly represents products of terms of a sequence. For this, we use the $\prod$ symbol.

For example, let's consider the following expression:

$$
\prod_{i =1}^{4} (2i - 3)
$$

This represents the *product* of the first $4$ terms of the sequence $a_i = 2i-3$. We evaluate this product as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
∏_(i = 1)^(4)(2i - 3) &= (2(1) - 3) \cdot (2(2) - 3) \cdot (2(3) - 3) \cdot (2(4) - 3) \cdot \\
&= (-1) \cdot 1 \cdot 3 \cdot 5 \\
&=-15
\end{aligned}
$$

## Evaluating a Product

**Example:** Evaluate $\displaystyle \prod_{k=1}^{4} \dfrac{3k - 1}{6k - 1}$.

**Explanation**

We expand the product by multiplying over the index $k$ from $k = 1$ to $k = 4{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
∏_(k = 1)^(4)\frac{3k - 1}{6k - 1} &= (3(1) - 1)/(6(1) - 1) \cdot (3(2) - 1)/(6(2) - 1) \cdot (3(3) - 1)/(6(3) - 1) \cdot (3(4) - 1)/(6(4) - 1) \\
&= \frac{2}{5} \cdot \frac{5}{11} \cdot \frac{8}{17} \cdot \frac{11}{23} \\
&= \frac{2}{5} \cdot \frac{5}{11} \cdot \frac{8}{17} \cdot \frac{11}{23} \\
&= \frac{2 \cdot 8}{17 \cdot 23} \\
&= \frac{16}{391}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Writing a Product Using Product Notation

**Example:** Express the following product using product notation:

$(2 - 3^2) \cdot (2 - 4^2) \cdot (2 - 5^2) \cdot \: \ldots \: \cdot (2 - 12^2)$

**Explanation**

We can collapse the given expression using product notation over the index $i$ from $i=3$ to $i = 12{:}$

$$
(2 - 3^{2}) \cdot (2 - 4^{2}) \cdot (2 - 5^{2}) \cdot … \cdot (2 - 12^{2}) = ∏_(i = 3)^(12)(2 - i^{2})
$$

## Simplifying a Product

**Example:** Express $\displaystyle \prod_{n = 3}^{m} \dfrac{n + 1}{n - 1}$ as a function of $m$ only.

**Explanation**

We expand the product by multiplying over the index $n$ from $n = 3$ to $n = m$, inclusive:

$$
\begin{aligned}
∏_(n = 3)^(m)\frac{n + 1}{n - 1} &= \frac{3 + 1}{3 - 1} \cdot \frac{4 + 1}{4 - 1} \cdot \frac{5 + 1}{5 - 1} \cdot \frac{6 + 1}{6 - 1} \cdot … \cdot \frac{m - 1 + 1}{m - 1 - 1} \cdot \frac{m + 1}{m - 1} \\
&= \frac{4}{2} \cdot \frac{5}{3} \cdot \frac{6}{4} \cdot \frac{7}{5} \cdot … \cdot \frac{m}{m - 2} \cdot \frac{m + 1}{m - 1} \\
&= \frac{4}{2} \cdot \frac{5}{3} \cdot \frac{6}{4} \cdot \frac{7}{5} \cdot … \cdot \frac{m}{m - 2} \cdot \frac{m + 1}{m - 1} \\
&= (m \cdot (m + 1))/(2 \cdot 3) \\
&= (m(m + 1))/(6)
\end{aligned}
$$
