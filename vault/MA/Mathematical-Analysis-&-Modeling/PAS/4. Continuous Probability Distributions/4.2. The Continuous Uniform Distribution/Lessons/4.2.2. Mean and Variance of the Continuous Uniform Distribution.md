## Introduction

Recall that if the random variable $X$ is uniformly distributed over the interval $[a,b]$, then $X$ has the following probability density function:

$$
{\frac{1}{b - a},\begin{vmatrix}a \le x \le b \\ 0,\end{vmatrix}otherwise
$$

It can be shown that the expected value and variance of $X$ are as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
E[X] &= \frac{a + b}{2} \\
Var[X] &= ((b - a)^{2})/(12)
\end{aligned}
$$

The result for $\textrm{E}[X]$ is intuitive as it's simply the midpoint of the interval $[a,b]$.

We will derive both of these results at the end of the lesson.

<a id="computing-the-expected-value-of-a-uniformly-distributed-random-variable"></a>
## Computing the Expected Value of a Uniformly Distributed Random Variable

**Example:** Given that $X$ is a random variable such that $X\sim U[2,8]$, find $\textrm{E}[X]$.

**Explanation**

If the random variable $X$ is distributed uniformly over the interval $[a,b]$, then

$$
\textrm{E}[X] = \dfrac{a+b}{2}
$$

In our case, we have $a=2$ and $b=8$. Therefore,

$$
\textrm{E}[X] = \dfrac{2+8}{2} =5
$$

## Computing the Variance of a Uniformly Distributed Random Variable

**Example:** Given that $X$ is a random variable such that $X\sim U[2,10]$, find $\textrm{Var}[X]$.

**Explanation**

If the random variable $X$ is distributed uniformly over the interval $[a,b]$, then

$$
\textrm{Var}[X] = \dfrac{(b-a)^2}{12}
$$

In our case, we have $a=2$ and $b=10$. Therefore,

$$
\textrm{Var}[X] = \dfrac{(10-2)^2}{12} = \dfrac{64}{12} = \dfrac{16}{3}
$$

## Computing the Standard Deviation of a Uniformly Distributed Random Variable

**Example:** Given that $X$ is a random variable such that $X\sim U[-9,-3]$, find $\textrm{SD}[X]$. Round your answer to three decimal places.

**Explanation**

If the random variable $X$ is distributed uniformly over the interval $[a,b]$, then

$$
\textrm{Var}[X] = \dfrac{(b-a)^2}{12}
$$

In our case, we have $a=-9$ and $b=-3$. Therefore,

$$
\textrm{Var}[X] = \dfrac{(-3-(-9))^2}{12} = \dfrac{36}{12} = 3
$$

Finally,

$$
\textrm{SD}[X] = \sqrt{3} \approx 1.732
$$

## Deriving the Formula for the Mean

If $X\sim U[a,b]$, then $X$ has the following probability density function:

$$
{\frac{1}{b - a},\begin{vmatrix}a \le x \le b \\ 0,\end{vmatrix}otherwise
$$

We can compute the formula for $\textrm E[X]$ using the usual definition:

$$
\begin{aligned}
E[X] &= ∫_{a}^{b}xf(x)dx \\
&= ∫_{a}^{b}\frac{x}{b - a}dx \\
&= \frac{1}{b - a}∫_{a}^{b}xdx \\
&= \frac{1}{b - a} \cdot \frac{1}{2}x^{2} \mid _{a}^{b} \\
&= (1)/(2(b - a)) \cdot (b^{2} - a^{2}) \\
&= (1)/(2(b - a)) \cdot (b - a)(b + a) \\
&= (1)/(2(b - a)) \cdot (b - a)(b + a) \\
&= \frac{a + b}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Deriving the Formula for the Variance

If $X\sim U[a,b]$, then $X$ has the following probability density function:

$$
{\frac{1}{b - a},\begin{vmatrix}a \le x \le b \\ 0,\end{vmatrix}otherwise
$$

To compute $\textrm{Var}[X]$, we use the relation

$$
\textrm{Var}[X] = \textrm{E}[X^2] - (\textrm{E}[X])^2
$$

First, we compute $\textrm E[X^2]$ as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
E[X^{2}] &= ∫_{a}^{b}x^{2}f(x)dx \\
&= ∫_{a}^{b}\frac{x^{2}}{b - a}dx \\
&= \frac{1}{b - a}∫_{a}^{b}x^{2}dx \\
&= \frac{1}{b - a} \cdot \frac{1}{3}x^{3} \mid _{a}^{b} \\
&= (1)/(3(b - a)) \cdot (b^{3} - a^{3}) \\
&= (1)/(3(b - a)) \cdot (b - a)(b^{2} + ab + a^{2}) \\
&= (1)/(3(b - a)) \cdot (b - a)(b^{2} + ab + a^{2}) \\
&= \frac{1}{3}(b^{2} + ab + a^{2})
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, since

$$
\textrm E[X] = \dfrac12(b+a)
$$

we have

$$
(\textrm E[X])^2 = \dfrac14(b^2+2ab+a^2)
$$

Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
Var[X] &= E[X^{2}] - (E[X])^{2} \\
&= \frac{1}{3}(b^{2} + ab + a^{2}) - \frac{1}{4}(b^{2} + 2ab + a^{2}) \\
&= \frac{4}{12}(b^{2} + ab + a^{2}) - \frac{3}{12}(b^{2} + 2ab + a^{2}) \\
&= \frac{1}{12}(4b^{2} + 4ab + 4a^{2}) - \frac{1}{12}(3b^{2} + 6ab + 3a^{2}) \\
&= \frac{1}{12}(b^{2} - 2ab + a^{2}) \\
&= \frac{1}{12}(b - a)^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$
