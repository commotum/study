## Introduction

The **variance** of a continuous random variable is the expected squared difference between the variable and its expected value.

For a continuous random variable $X$ with probability density function $f(x)$ defined over a set $S$, the variance is defined as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
Var[X] &= E[(X - E[X])^{2}] \\
&= ∫_{S}(x - E[X])^{2} \cdot f(x)dx
\end{aligned}
$$

In practice, though, it's easier to use the following formula:

$$
\textrm{Var}[X] = \textrm E [X^2] - \textrm E[X]^2
$$

For example, consider the continuous random variable $X$ with the following probability density function:

$$
f(x) = \dfrac{x}{4}, \quad 1 < x < 3
$$

First, we compute $\textrm E[X]{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
E[X] &= ∫_{1}^{3}x \cdot \frac{x}{4}dx \\
&= ∫_{1}^{3}\frac{x^{2}}{4}dx \\
&= \frac{x^{3}}{12} \mid _{1}^{3} \\
&= \frac{13}{6}
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we compute $\textrm E[X^2]{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
E[X^{2}] &= ∫_{1}^{3}x^{2} \cdot \frac{x}{4}dx \\
&= ∫_{1}^{3}\frac{x^{3}}{4}dx \\
&= \frac{x^{4}}{16} \mid _{1}^{3} \\
&= 5
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the variance is given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
Var[X] &= E[X^{2}] - E[X]^{2} \\
&= 5 - (\frac{13}{6})^{2} \\
&= \frac{11}{36}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Computing the Variance of a Continuous Random Variable Given a Two-Branch PDF

**Example:** The continuous random variable $X$ has the probability density function $f(x)$ stated below. Given that $\textrm E[X] = \dfrac {10}{3}$, compute $\textrm{Var}[X]$.
${8 - 2x,\begin{vmatrix}3 \le x \le 4, \\ 0,\end{vmatrix}otherwise$.

**Explanation**

For a continuous random variable $X$ with probability density function $f(x)$ defined over a set $S$, we can calculate the variance using the following formula:

$$
\textrm{Var}[X] = \textrm E [X^2] - \textrm E[X]^2
$$

We compute $\textrm E[X^2]$, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
E[X^{2}] &= ∫_{3}^{4}x^{2} \cdot (8 - 2x)dx \\
&= ∫_{3}^{4}(8x^{2} - 2x^{3})dx \\
&= (\frac{8x^{3}}{3} - \frac{x^{4}}{2}) \mid _{3}^{4} \\
&= \frac{67}{6}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the variance is given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
Var[X] &= E[X^{2}] - E[X]^{2} \\
&= \frac{67}{6} - (\frac{10}{3})^{2} \\
&= \frac{1}{18}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Computing the Variance of a Continuous Random Variable Given a Three-Branch PDF

**Example:** The continuous random variable $X$ has the probability density function given below. Given that $\textrm E[X]\approx 1.791\,67$, compute $\textrm{Var}[X]$. Round your answer to $2$ decimal places.
${\frac{1}{4},\begin{vmatrix}0 < x < 2, \\ \frac{x}{2} - \frac{3}{4},\end{vmatrix}2 \le x < 3,; 0, \mid otherwise$.

**Explanation**

For a continuous random variable $X$ with probability density function $f(x)$ defined over a set $S$, we can calculate the variance using the following formula:

$$
\textrm{Var}[X] = \textrm E [X^2] - \textrm E[X]^2
$$

We compute $\textrm E[X^2]$ as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
E[X^{2}] &= ∫_{0}^{3}x^{2} \cdot f(x)dx \\
&= ∫_{0}^{2}x^{2} \cdot f(x)dx + ∫_{2}^{3}x^{2} \cdot f(x)dx \\
&= ∫_{0}^{2}x^{2} \cdot \frac{1}{4}dx + ∫_{2}^{3}x^{2} \cdot (\frac{x}{2} - \frac{3}{4})dx \\
&= ∫_{0}^{2}\frac{x^{2}}{4}dx + ∫_{2}^{3}(\frac{x^{3}}{2} - \frac{3x^{2}}{4})dx \\
&= \frac{x^{3}}{12} \mid _{0}^{2} + (\frac{x^{4}}{8} - \frac{x^{3}}{4}) \mid _{2}^{3} \\
&\approx 4.04167
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the variance is given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
Var[X] &= E[X^{2}] - E[X]^{2} \\
&\approx 4.04167 - (1.79167)^{2} \\
&\approx 0.83
\end{aligned}
$$

rounded to $2$ decimal places.
