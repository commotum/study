## Introduction

For a set of $n$ paired observations $x_i$ and $y_i$, the trend line is the straight line that best fits the data.

![](<../Source/9.1.2. Linear Regression/Images/dd402a367c4105b60a83ce4e2b741b4e.png>)

Finding the equation of the trend line is called **linear regression**. The trend line is commonly called the **regression line** or **line of best fit.**

We can show that the equation of the trend line is given by

$$
y = mx+b
$$

where:

- The slope $m$ is given by
$m = \frac{S_{xy}}{S_{xx}}$.
- The $y$-intercept $b$ is given by
$b = \overline{y} - m \overline{x}$.
- $\overline{x}$ and $\overline{y}$ are the means of the data sets:
$\overline{x} = \dfrac1n \sum_{i=1}^n x_i, \qquad \overline{y} = \dfrac1n \sum_{i=1}^n y_i$

- The quantities $S_{xx}$ and $S_{xy}$ are given by
$S_{xx} = \sum_{i=1}^n (x_i-\overline{x})^2, \qquad S_{xy} = \sum_{i=1}^n (x_i-\overline{x})(y_i-\overline{y})$.

There's some nice intuition behind the formula for $b$. The trend line *always* passes through the point $(\overline{x}, \overline{y})$. Therefore, this point must satisfy the trend line equation

$$
y = mx+b
$$

We can then solve this equation for $b$.

Also, notice that $S_{xx}$ and $S_{xy}$ are the summation terms that feature in the formulas for the variance and covariance, respectively.

## Calculating a Regression Line Equation

**Example:** Consider the data sets above. You are given that $\overline{x}=4$ and $\overline{y}=3$. Find the equation of the corresponding linear regression line. Round the slope and the $y$-intercept to $1$ decimal place if necessary.

| $x$ | **$1$** | **$2$** | **$4$** | **$9$** |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $y$ | $6$ | $4$ | $2$ | $0$ |

**Explanation**

The equation of the regression line is given by

$$
y = mx+b
$$

where

$$
\begin{aligned}
m &= \frac{S_{xy}}{S_{xx}}, b = y^(―) - mx^(―)
\end{aligned}
$$

Let's compute $m{:}$

- First, we compute the sum in the numerator:
$S_{xy}|= ∑_(i = 1)^(n)(x_{i} - x^(―))(y_{i} - y^(―)); = (1 - 4)(6 - 3) + (2 - 4)(4 - 3); = + (4 - 4)(2 - 3) + (9 - 4)(0 - 3); = (-3)(3) + (-2)(1) + (0)(-1) + (5)(-3); =-9 - 2 - 0 - 15; =-26$
- Second, we compute the sum in the denominator:
$S_{xx}|= ∑_(i = 1)^(n)(x_{i} - x^(―))^{2}; = (1 - 4)^{2} + (2 - 4)^{2} + (4 - 4)^{2} + (9 - 4)^{2}; = 9 + 4 + 0 + 25; = 38$

Now, substituting into the formula for $m$, we obtain

$$
m = \dfrac{-26}{38} = -\dfrac{13}{19} \approx -0.7
$$

Next, we substitute into the formula for $b{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
b &= y^(―) - mx^(―) \\
&= 3 - (-\frac{13}{19}) \cdot 4 \\
&= \frac{109}{19} \\
&\approx 5.7
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the equation of the regression line is

$$
y = -0.7x + 5.7
$$

## Making Predictions by Calculating a Regression Line

**Example:** The table above gives the value of a car a few years after it was purchased (in thousands of dollars). You are given that $\overline{x}=2$ and $\overline{y}=24$. Find the equation of the corresponding linear regression line and use this to predict how long it takes for the car's value to drop to $\[math]10\,000$. Round your answer to the nearest year.

| **Years ($x$)** | $0$ | $1$ | $3$ | $4$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| **Value ($y$)** | **$35$** | **$26$** | **$19$** | **$16$** |

**Explanation**

The equation of the regression line is given by

$$
y = mx+b
$$

where

$$
\begin{aligned}
m &= \frac{S_{xy}}{S_{xx}}, b = y^(―) - mx^(―)
\end{aligned}
$$

Let's compute $m{:}$

- First, we compute the sum in the numerator:
$S_{xy}|= ∑_(i = 1)^(n)(x_{i} - x^(―))(y_{i} - y^(―)); = (0 - 2)(35 - 24) + (1 - 2)(26 - 24); = + (3 - 2)(19 - 24) + (4 - 2)(16 - 24); = (-2)(11) + (-1)(2) + (1)(-5) + (2)(-8); =-22 - 2 - 5 - 16; =-45$
- Second, we compute the sum in the denominator:
$S_{xx}|= ∑_(i = 1)^(n)(x_{i} - x^(―))^{2}; = (0 - 2)^{2} + (1 - 2)^{2} + (3 - 2)^{2} + (4 - 2)^{2}; = 4 + 1 + 1 + 4; = 10$
- Now, substituting into the formula for $m$, we obtain
$m =-\dfrac{45}{10} = -4.5$.

Next, we substitute into the formula for $b{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
b &= y^(―) - mx^(―) \\
&= 24 - (-\frac{9}{2}) \cdot 2 \\
&= 33
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the equation of the regression line is

$$
y = -4.5x+33
$$

Finally, we can estimate the required value by substituting $y=10$ into the equation of the trend line and solving for $x{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
y &= -4.5x + 33 \\
10 &= -4.5x + 33 \\
- 23 &= -4.5x \\
&= x \mid \approx 5
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, according to this model, it takes approximately $5$ years for the car's value to drop to $\[math]10\,000$.

**Note:** Since we're extrapolating (the point where $y=10$ is not within the original data range), our result *may* be unreliable.
