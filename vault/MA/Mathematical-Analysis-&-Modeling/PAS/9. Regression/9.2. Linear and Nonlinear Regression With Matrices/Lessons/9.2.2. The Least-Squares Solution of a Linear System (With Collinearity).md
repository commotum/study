## Introduction

Let's consider the following inconsistent system of equations:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}1 & -4 \\ -2 & 8\end{bmatrix}
$$

Since the system is inconsistent, we cannot find an exact solution. However, as we've previously seen, we can attempt to find the so-called least-squares solution, which is a vector ${\hat{\mathbf{x}}}$ such that

$$
\| A{\hat{\mathbf{x}}} - \mathbf{b} \|
$$

is as small as possible.

As before, the least-squares solution can be found by solving the corresponding normal equation, given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
A^{T}Ax^(^) &= A^{T}b
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, note the following:

- Previously, we solved the normal equation by left-multiplying the normal equation by $(A^T A)^{-1}$.
- However, in this case, the second column of $A$ is a multiple of the first.
- So, the columns of $A$ are linearly dependent.
- Therefore, $A^T A$ is not invertible.

To solve the normal equation in this case, we proceed as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
A^{T}Ax^(^) &= A^{T}b \\
\begin{bmatrix}1 & -2 \\ -4 & 8\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1 & -4 \\ -2 & 8\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}x_{1} \\ x_{2}\end{bmatrix} &= \begin{bmatrix}1 & -2 \\ -4 & 8\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 3\end{bmatrix} \\
\begin{bmatrix}5 & -20 \\ -20 & 80\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}x_{1} \\ x_{2}\end{bmatrix} &= \begin{bmatrix}-5 \\ 20\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Solving the last equation by Gaussian elimination, we obtain the general solution

$$
\begin{bmatrix}-1 + 4x_{2} \\ x_{2}\end{bmatrix}
$$

Notice that our solution is not unique! The second term of the least-squares solution (highlighted in blue) represents vectors from the null space of $A$.

In general, if a matrix $A$ has collinear columns, then the least-squares solution to

$$
A\mathbf x = \mathbf b
$$

is not unique.

## Constructing the Normal Equation Corresponding to a Linear System

**Example:** ${4x_{1} - 3x_{2} - x_{3} = 4; 8x_{1} - 6x_{2} - 2x_{3} = 2$

Consider the system of linear equations and its corresponding normal equation shown above. What is the value of $a-b+c$?

**Explanation**

First, we write our system in matrix form as
$\begin{bmatrix}4 & -3 & -1 \\ 8 & -6 & -2\end{bmatrix}$

The normal equation corresponding to

$$
A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{b}
$$

is given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
A^{T}Ax &= A^{T}b \\
\begin{bmatrix}4 & 8 \\ -3 & -6 \\ -1 & -2\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}4 & -3 & -1 \\ 8 & -6 & -2\end{bmatrix}x &= \begin{bmatrix}4 & 8 \\ -3 & -6 \\ -1 & -2\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}4 \\ 2\end{bmatrix} \\
\begin{bmatrix}80 & -60 & -20 \\ -60 & 45 & 15 \\ -20 & 15 & 5\end{bmatrix}x &= \begin{bmatrix}32 \\ -24 \\ -8\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, we have

$$
a-b+c=(-20)-(-24)+(-20) = -16
$$

## Finding the Least-Squares Solution of a Linear System Given in Matrix Form with Collinearity

**Example:** $\begin{bmatrix}1 & -3 \\ -1 & 3 \\ 2 & -6\end{bmatrix}$

Consider the matrix $A$ and vector $\mathbf{b}$ shown above. Find the general least-squares solution of the system $A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{b}$.

**Explanation**

The least-squares solution of

$$
A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{b}
$$

satisfies the corresponding normal equation:

$$
A^T \! A\mathbf{x} = A^T\mathbf{b}
$$

Notice that the columns of $A$ are linearly dependent. So first, let's write down the normal equation in matrix form:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\begin{bmatrix}1 & -1 & 2 \\ -3 & 3 & -6\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1 & -3 \\ -1 & 3 \\ 2 & -6\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}x_{1} \\ x_{2}\end{bmatrix} &= \begin{bmatrix}1 & -1 & 2 \\ -3 & 3 & -6\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 3 \\ -1\end{bmatrix} \\
\begin{bmatrix}6 & -18 \\ -18 & 54\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}x_{1} \\ x_{2}\end{bmatrix} &= \begin{bmatrix}-4 \\ 12\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we solve the system corresponding to the above matrix equation using Gaussian elimination, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
M &= \begin{bmatrix}6 & -18 & -4 \\ -18 & 54 & 12\end{bmatrix} \mid R_{2}:=R_{2} + 3R_{1} \\
&= ∼\begin{bmatrix}6 & -18 & -4 \\ 0 & 0 & 0\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

The reduced matrix above has one pivot column (the $1$st one). Thus, $x_2$ is a free variable. From the first equation, we have

$$
x_1=-\dfrac{2}{3}+3x_2
$$

Hence, the general solution is

$$
\begin{bmatrix}-\frac{2}{3} + 3x_{2} \\ x_{2}\end{bmatrix}
$$

## Finding the Least-Squares Solution of a Linear System With Collinearity

**Example:** ${-x_{1} + 4x_{2} = 1; 2x_{1} - 8x_{2} = 1; - 2x_{1} + 8x_{2} =-1$

Consider the system of linear equations shown above. Find the general least-squares solution of the system $A \mathbf x = \mathbf b$.

**Explanation**

First, we write our system in matrix form as

$$
A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{b}
$$

where

$$
\begin{bmatrix}-1 & 4 \\ 2 & -8 \\ -2 & 8\end{bmatrix}
$$

The least-squares solution of

$$
A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{b}
$$

satisfies the corresponding normal equation:

$$
A^T \! A\mathbf{x} = A^T\mathbf{b}
$$

Notice that the columns of $A$ are linearly dependent. So first, let's write down the normal equation in matrix form:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\begin{bmatrix}-1 & 2 & -2 \\ 4 & -8 & 8\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}-1 & 4 \\ 2 & -8 \\ -2 & 8\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}x_{1} \\ x_{2}\end{bmatrix} &= \begin{bmatrix}-1 & 2 & -2 \\ 4 & -8 & 8\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 1 \\ -1\end{bmatrix} \\
\begin{bmatrix}9 & -36 \\ -36 & 144\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}x_{1} \\ x_{2}\end{bmatrix} &= \begin{bmatrix}3 \\ -12\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we solve the system corresponding to the final matrix equation using Gaussian elimination, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
M &= \begin{bmatrix}9 & -36 & 3 \\ -36 & 144 & -12\end{bmatrix} \mid R_{2}:=R_{2} + 4R_{1} \\
&= ∼\begin{bmatrix}9 & -36 & 3 \\ 0 & 0 & 0\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

The reduced matrix above has one pivot column (the $1$st one). Thus, $x_2$ is a free variable. From the first equation, we have

$$
x_1=\dfrac{1}{3}+ 4x_2
$$

Hence, the general solution is

$$
\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{3} + 4x_{2} \\ x_{2}\end{bmatrix}
$$
