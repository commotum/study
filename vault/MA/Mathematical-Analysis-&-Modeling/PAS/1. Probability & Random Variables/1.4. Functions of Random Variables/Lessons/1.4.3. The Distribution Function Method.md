## Introduction

Let $X$ be a continuous random variable whose probability density function is known. Suppose we want to define a new random variable $Y$ as

$Y = u(X)$,
where $u = u(X)$ is a function of $X$. How do we find the probability density function of $Y$?

To find the PDF of $Y$, we can use the **distribution function method**. The distribution function method consists of two steps:

- First, we find an expression for $F_Y$ in terms of $F_X$, where $F_Y$ and $F_X$ are the cumulative distribution functions of $Y$ and $X$, respectively.
- Then, we differentiate $F_Y$ to get the probability density function $f_Y$ of $Y$.

In this lesson, we'll assume that the function $u$ is strictly monotonic (i.e., strictly increasing or decreasing). The case where $u$ is not monotonic will be dealt with in a future lesson.

Let's see an example of the distribution function method in action.

<a id="a-worked-example"></a>
## A Worked Example

Suppose the continuous random variable $X$ is defined as

${e^{-x},\begin{vmatrix}x > 0 \\ 0,\end{vmatrix}otherwise$.
Let

$$
Y= 1+2X
$$

Let's use the distribution function method to find the probability density function of $Y$.

First, note that

$$
Y = 1+2X
$$

is a strictly increasing function of $X$. Moreover, for $x > 0$, we have $y > 1$.

![](<../Source/1.4.3. The Distribution Function Method/Images/4e4c7bbd6dd72282da934bb357ffaa41.png>)

Let's consider $y > 1$. We write down the definition of the cumulative distribution of $Y$, and then use our transformation to isolate $X$ in the parentheses. This will allow us to write $F_Y$ in terms of $F_X$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
F_{Y}(y) &= P(Y \le y) \\
&= P(2X + 1 \le y) \\
&= P(2X \le y - 1) \\
&= P(X \le \frac{y - 1}{2}) \\
&= F_{X}(\frac{y - 1}{2})
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, to compute the PDF $f_Y(y)$ for $y > 1$, we differentiate $F_Y$ with respect to $y$ using the chain rule. This gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
f_{Y}(y) &= F_{Y}^{′}(y) \\
&= \frac{d}{dy}(F_{X}(\frac{y - 1}{2})) \\
&= \frac{d}{dy}(\frac{y - 1}{2}) \cdot F_{X}^{′}(\frac{y - 1}{2}) \\
&= \frac{1}{2} \cdot F_{X}^{′}(\frac{y - 1}{2}) \\
&= \frac{1}{2} \cdot f_{X}(\frac{y - 1}{2})
\end{aligned}
$$

Applying the definition of $f_X$, we finally arrive at

$$
\begin{aligned}
f_{Y}(y) &= \frac{1}{2} \cdot f_{X}(\frac{y - 1}{2}) \\
&= \frac{1}{2} \cdot e^{- (y - 1)/2} \\
&= \frac{1}{2}e^{(1 - y)/2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the full expression for the PDF of $Y$ is

$$
\begin{aligned}
f_{Y}(y) &= {\frac{1}{2}e^{(1 - y)/2},\begin{vmatrix}y > 1 \\ 0,\end{vmatrix}otherwise
\end{aligned}
$$

It's easy (and often worthwhile) to check that this is a valid probability density function.

## Computing the PDF of a Random Variable Under an Affine Transformation

**Example:** Let $X$ be a continuous random variable with the following probability density function:
${2x,\begin{vmatrix}0 < x < 1 \\ 0,\end{vmatrix}otherwise$
If $Y =1-3X$, then what is the probability density function of $Y$?

**Explanation**

To compute the probability density function of a random variable $Y$, where $Y = u(X)$ is a continuous, strictly monotonic function of a continuous random variable $X$, we follow two steps:

- First, we find an expression for $F_Y$ terms of $F_X$, where $F_Y$ and $F_X$ are the cumulative distribution functions of $Y$ and $X$, respectively.
- Then, we differentiate $F_Y$ to get the probability density function $f_Y$.

Before we start, note that $Y = 1-3X$ is a strictly decreasing function of $X$. Moreover, for $0 < x < 1$, we have $-2 \lt y \lt 1$.

![](<../Source/1.4.3. The Distribution Function Method/Images/b182aabcb351ba0f6afd21e431cca5c6.png>)

Now, using the definition of the cumulative distribution of $Y$, for $-2 \lt y \lt 1$, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
F_{Y}(y) &= P(Y \le y) \\
&= P(1 - 3X \le y) \\
&= P(-3X \le y - 1) \\
&= P(X \ge \frac{1 - y}{3}) \\
&= 1 - F_{X}(\frac{1 - y}{3})
\end{aligned}
$$

To compute the PDF $f_Y(y)$ for $-2 \lt y \lt 1$, we differentiate $F_Y$ with respect to $y$ using the chain rule. This gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
f_{Y}(y) &= F_{Y}^{′}(y) \\
&= 0 - (-\frac{1}{3}) \cdot F_{X}^{′}(\frac{1 - y}{3}) \\
&= \frac{1}{3} \cdot f_{X}(\frac{1 - y}{3})
\end{aligned}
$$

Applying the definition of $f_X$, we finally arrive at

$$
\begin{aligned}
f_{Y}(y) &= \frac{1}{3} \cdot f_{X}(\frac{1 - y}{3}) \\
&= \frac{1}{3} \cdot 2 \cdot (\frac{1 - y}{3}) \\
&= \frac{2}{9}(1 - y)
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the full expression for the PDF of $Y$ is

$$
{\frac{2}{9}(1 - y),\begin{vmatrix}-2 < y < 1 \\ 0,\end{vmatrix}otherwise
$$

## Computing the PDF of a Random Variable Under a Nonlinear Transformation

**Example:** Let $X$ be a continuous random variable with the following probability density function:
${\frac{3}{8}(2 - x)^{2},\begin{vmatrix}0 < x < 2 \\ 0,\end{vmatrix}otherwise$
If $Y = (2-X)^3$, then what is the probability density function of $Y$?

**Explanation**

To compute the probability density function of a random variable $Y$, where $Y = u(X)$ is a continuous, strictly monotonic function of a continuous random variable $X$, we follow two steps:

- First, we find an expression for $F_Y$ terms of $F_X$, where $F_Y$ and $F_X$ are the cumulative distribution functions of $Y$ and $X$, respectively.
- Then, we differentiate $F_Y$ to get the probability density function $f_Y$.

Before we start, note that $Y = (2-X)^3$ is a strictly decreasing function of $X$. Moreover, for $0 \lt x \lt 2$, we have $0 \lt y \lt 8$.

![](<../Source/1.4.3. The Distribution Function Method/Images/110c3946fcc670a315a9f111c5aae5fd.png>)

Now, using the definition of the cumulative distribution of $Y$, for $0 \lt y \lt 8$, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
F_{Y}(y) &= P(Y \le y) \\
&= P((2 - X)^{3} \le y) \\
&= P(2 - X \le y^{1/3}) \\
&= P(-X \le y^{1/3} - 2) \\
&= P(X \ge 2 - y^{1/3}) \\
&= 1 - F_{X}(2 - y^{1/3})
\end{aligned}
$$

To compute the PDF $f_Y(y)$ for $0 \lt y \lt 8$, we differentiate $F_Y$ with respect to $y$ using the chain rule. This gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
f_{Y}(y) &= F_{Y}^{′}(y) \\
&= 0 - (-\frac{1}{3y^{2/3}}) \cdot F_{X}^{′}(2 - y^{1/3}) \\
&= \frac{1}{3y^{2/3}} \cdot f_{X}(2 - y^{1/3})
\end{aligned}
$$

Applying the definition of $f_X$, we finally arrive at

$$
\begin{aligned}
f_{Y}(y) &= \frac{1}{3y^{2/3}} \cdot f_{X}(2 - y^{1/3}) \\
&= \frac{1}{3y^{2/3}} \cdot \frac{3}{8}(2 - (2 - y^{1/3}))^{2} \\
&= \frac{1}{3y^{2/3}} \cdot \frac{3}{8}(2 - 2 + y^{1/3})^{2} \\
&= \frac{1}{3y^{2/3}} \cdot \frac{3}{8}(y^{1/3})^{2} \\
&= \frac{1}{8}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the full expression for the PDF of $Y$ is

$$
{\frac{1}{8},\begin{vmatrix}0 < y < 8 \\ 0,\end{vmatrix}otherwise
$$
