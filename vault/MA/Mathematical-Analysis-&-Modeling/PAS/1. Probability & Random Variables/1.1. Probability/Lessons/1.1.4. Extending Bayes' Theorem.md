## Introduction

In this lesson, we will generalize Bayes' theorem to deal with situations where the sample space $\mathcal S$ is partitioned into a disjoint union of multiple events.

Suppose we have three events $A_1,A_2$, and $A_3$ that form a disjoint union of the sample space $\mathcal S$, as shown below.

![](<../Source/1.1.4. Extending Bayes' Theorem/Images/451c3823846faf151b85fc6f353e93be.png>)

Now consider an event $B$ that also lies in the sample space:

![](<../Source/1.1.4. Extending Bayes' Theorem/Images/d6f7f5c1be0cb59f3f9dfb6709179275.png>)

Bayes' theorem states that

$$
P(A_1 \mid B)=\dfrac{P(B \mid A_1)P(A_1)}{P(B)}
$$

Now, the law of total probability tells us that

$$
P(B)=P(B \mid A_1)P(A_1) + P(B \mid A_2)P(A_2) + P(B \mid A_3)P(A_3)
$$

Substituting this into Bayes' theorem, we get

$$
P(A_1 \mid B) = \dfrac{P(B \mid A_1) P(A_1)}{P(B \mid A_1)P(A_1) + P(B \mid A_2)P(A_2) + P(B \mid A_3)P(A_3)}
$$

Using similar arguments, we can say that for $A_k$ for $k=1,2,3$, we have

$$
P(A_k \mid B) = \dfrac{P(B \mid A_k) P(A_k)}{P(B \mid A_1)P(A_1) + P(B \mid A_2)P(A_2) + P(B \mid A_3)P(A_3)}
$$

which is usually written more compactly as

$$
P(A_k \mid B) = \dfrac{P(B \mid A_k) P(A_k)}{\sum_{i=1}^3 P(B \mid A_i)P(A_i)}
$$

It's easy to see how this generalizes. If the disjoint union $A_1 \sqcup A_2 \sqcup \ldots \sqcup A_n$ covers the entire sample space, Bayes' theorem combined with the law of total probability gives us the following:

$$
P(A_k \mid B) = \dfrac{P(B \mid A_k) P(A_k)}{\sum_{i=1}^n P(B \mid A_i)P(A_i)}
$$

Let's see an example of how to apply this theorem.

## Computing a Conditional Probability Using Bayes' Theorem

**Example:** The disjoint union $A_1 \sqcup A_2 \sqcup A_3$ of events $A_1,A_2,A_3$ covers the entire sample space. Compute $P(A_1 \mid B)$ given the following probabilities:

$P(B\begin{vmatrix}A_{1}) = 0.23, \\ P(B \mid A_{2}) = 0.26,\end{vmatrix}P(A_{2}) = 0.3; P(B \mid A_{3}) = 0.18, \mid P(A_{3}) = 0.4$

**Explanation**

If the disjoint union $A_1 \sqcup A_2 \sqcup \ldots \sqcup A_n$ covers the entire sample space, Bayes' theorem combined with the law of total probability gives us the following:

$$
P(A_k \mid B) = \dfrac{P(B \mid A_k) P(A_k)}{P(B)} = \dfrac{P(B \mid A_k) P(A_k)}{\sum_{i=1}^n P(B \mid A_i)P(A_i)}
$$

where

$$
k=1,2,\ldots,n
$$

In our case, $n=3$, and $A_1 \sqcup A_2 \sqcup A_3$ covers the entire sample space. Consequently, we have

$$
P(A_1) + P(A_2) + P(A_3) = 1
$$

As a result,

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(A_{1}) &= 1 - (P(A_{2}) + P(A_{3})) \\
&= 1 - (0.3 + 0.4) \\
&= 0.3
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(A_{1}\begin{vmatrix}B) &= (P(B \mid A_{1})P(A_{1}))/(∑_(i = 1)^(3)P(B \mid A_{i})P(A_{i})) \\ = (P(B \mid A_{1})P(A_{1}))/(P(B \mid A_{1})P(A_{1}) + P(B \mid A_{2})P(A_{2}) + P(B\end{vmatrix}A_{3})P(A_{3})) \\
&= ((0.23)(0.3))/((0.23)(0.3) + (0.26)(0.3) + (0.18)(0.4)) \\
&= \frac{0.069}{0.219} \\
&\approx 0.315
\end{aligned}
$$

## Applying Bayes’ Theorem to a Situation in Context

**Example:** A travel agency offers three itineraries, itinerary $1$, itinerary $2$, and $3$. For each itinerary, customers can choose to travel using their own car or the agency's bus.

- Itinerary $1$ is chosen by $25\%$ of the customers, and $60\%$ of those customers travel by bus.
- Itinerary $2$ is chosen by $30\%$ of the customers, and $30\%$ of those customers travel by bus.
- Itinerary $3$ is chosen by $45\%$ of the customers, and $20\%$ of those customers travel by bus.

If $65\%$ of all customers travel with children, and $40\%$ of customers with children choose the bus, what is the probability that a randomly chosen customer that chooses the bus travels with children? Round your answer to $3$ decimal places.

**Explanation**

Let $C$ be the event that a customer travels with children, $B$ be the event that a customer takes the bus, and $I_1, I_2, I_3$ be the event that a customer chooses itinerary $1$, $2$, or $3$, respectively.

Let's break down the information given in the problem statement:

- From the bullet points, we have the following information:
$\begin{bmatrix}P(I_{1}) = 0.25 & P(B \mid I_{1}) = 0.6 \\ P(I_{2}) = 0.3 & P(B \mid I_{2}) = 0.3 \\ P(I_{3}) = 0.45 & P(B \mid I_{3}) = 0.2\end{bmatrix}$
The disjoint union $I_1 \sqcup I_2 \sqcup I_3$ covers the entire sample space, and we have
$P(I_1)+ P(I_2) + P(I_3) = 0.25+0.3+0.45 = 1$.

- Since $65\%$ of customers have children, we have
$P(C) = 0.65$.
- Since $40\%$ of customers with children choose the bus, we have
$P(B \mid C) = 0.4$.

We want to find the probability that a randomly chosen customer that chooses the bus travels with children, which is represented as $P(C \mid B)$.

Using Bayes' theorem combined with the law of total probability, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(C\begin{vmatrix}B) &= (P(B \mid C)P(C))/(P(B)) \\ = (P(B \mid C)P(C))/(P(B \mid I_{1})P(I_{1}) + P(B \mid I_{2})P(I_{2}) + P(B\end{vmatrix}I_{3})P(I_{3})) \\
&= ((0.4)(0.65))/((0.6)(0.25) + (0.3)(0.3) + (0.2)(0.45)) \\
&= \frac{0.26}{0.33} \\
&\approx 0.788
\end{aligned}
$$

rounded to $3$ decimal places.

## Solving for an Unknown Conditional Probability

**Example:** Potatoes produced by a particular farm come in three different varieties, variety $1$, variety $2$, and variety $3$.

- $36\%$ of the potatoes are of variety $1$, and $20\%$ of those potatoes weigh more than $100\,\mathrm g$.
- $17\%$ of the potatoes are of variety $2$, and $6\%$ of those potatoes weigh more than $100\,\mathrm g$.
- $47\%$ of the potatoes are of the variety $3$.

If $16\%$ of potatoes from the entire farm are sold to a local restaurant, $40\%$ of potatoes sold to the restaurant weigh more than $100\,\mathrm g$, and $15\%$ of potatoes that weigh more than $100\,\mathrm g$ are sold to the restaurant, what is the probability that a randomly selected potato from variety $3$ weighs more than $100\,\mathrm g$? Round your answer to $3$ decimal places.

**Explanation**

Let $R$ be the event that a potato is sold to the restaurant, $W$ be the event that a potato weighs more than $100\,\mathrm g$, and $V_1, V_2, V_3$ be the event that a potato is of variety $1$, $2$, or $3$, respectively.

Let's break down the information given in the problem statement:

- From the bullet points, we have the following information:
$P(V_{1}) = 0.36\begin{vmatrix}P(W \mid V_{1}) = 0.2 \\ P(V_{2}) = 0.17\end{vmatrix}P(W \mid V_{2}) = 0.06; P(V_{3}) = 0.47$
The disjoint union $V_1 \sqcup V_2 \sqcup V_3$ covers the entire sample space, and we have
$P(V_1)+ P(V_2) + P(V_3) = 0.36+0.17+0.47 = 1$.
- Since $16\%$ of potatoes from the entire farm are sold to a local restaurant, we have
$P(R) = 0.16$.
- Since $40\%$ potatoes sold to the restaurant weigh more than $100\,\mathrm g$, we have
$P(W \mid R) = 0.4$.
- Since $15\%$ of potatoes that weigh more than $100\,\mathrm g$ are sold to the restaurant, we have
$P(R \mid W) = 0.15$.

We want to find the probability that a randomly chosen potato of variety $3$ weighs more than $100\,\mathrm g$, which is represented as $P(W \mid V_3)$.

Using Bayes' theorem combined with the law of total probability, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(R\begin{vmatrix}W) &= (P(W \mid R)P(R))/(P(W)) \\ P(R \mid W)|= (P(W \mid R)P(R))/(P(W \mid V_{1})P(V_{1}) + P(W \mid V_{2})P(V_{2}) + P(W \mid V_{3})P(V_{3})) \\ 0.15|= ((0.4)(0.16))/((0.2)(0.36) + (0.06)(0.17) + P(W \mid V_{3})(0.47)) \\ 0.15|= (0.064)/(0.0822 + 0.47P(W\end{vmatrix}V_{3}))
\end{aligned}
$$

Solving this equation for $P(W \mid V_3)$, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
0.15(0.0822 + 0.47P(W\begin{vmatrix}V_{3})) &= 0.064 \\ 0.0822 + 0.47P(W \mid V_{3})|= \frac{0.064}{0.15} \\ 0.0822 + 0.47P(W \mid V_{3})|= \frac{32}{75} \\ 0.47P(W \mid V_{3})|= \frac{32}{75} - 0.0822 \\ P(W \mid V_{3})|= \frac{1}{0.47}(\frac{32}{75} - 0.0822) \\ P(W \mid V_{3})\end{vmatrix}\approx 0.733
\end{aligned}
$$

rounded to three decimal places.
