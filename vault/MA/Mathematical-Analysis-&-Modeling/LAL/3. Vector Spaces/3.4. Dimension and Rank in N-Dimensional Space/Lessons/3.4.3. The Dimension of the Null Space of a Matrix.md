## Introduction

We already learned that the rank of a matrix $A$ is the number of pivot columns in $A$. Now, can we obtain any information from the *non-pivot* columns?

The answer is yes! The non-pivot columns help to find the dimension of the null space of a matrix $A$, and here's how.

Consider the matrix
$\begin{bmatrix}3 & 5 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 2 \\ 0 & 0 & 0\end{bmatrix}$

The matrix $A$ is already in row echelon form, where the $1$st and the $3$rd columns are pivot columns, while the $2$nd one is a *non-pivot* column. Now, to get the null space of $A$, we need to find the solution of the system

$$
{3x_{1} + 5x_{2} + x_{3} = 0; 2x_{3} = 0; 0 = 0
$$

where $\color{blue}x_1$ and ${\color{blue}x_3}$ are basic variables and $\color{red}x_2$ is a free variable. Hence, we obtain that

$$
\begin{bmatrix}-\frac{5}{3} \\ 1 \\ 0\end{bmatrix}
$$

is a basis of $\textrm{Null}(A)$ and, therefore,

$$
\textrm{dim(Null}(A))={\color{red}1}
$$

.

In other words,

> $\textrm{dim(Null}(A))$ is equal to the number of *non-pivot* columns in the corresponding row echelon form of $A$

or, equivalently,

> $\textrm{dim(Null}(A))$ is the number of *free variables* in the corresponding homogeneous system with the coefficient matrix $A$.

<a id="determining-the-dimension-of-the-null-space-of-a-matrix-given-the-number-of-non-pivot-columns"></a>
## Determining the Dimension of the Null Space of a Matrix Given the Number of Non-Pivot Columns

**Example:** If $A$ is a $9 \times 11$ matrix that has $9$ pivot columns, then what is $\textrm{dim(Null}(A))$?

**Explanation**

We know that $\textrm{dim(Null}(A))$ is the number of non-pivot columns in $A$.

Since $A$ has $11$ columns and $9$ of them are pivots, then the number of non-pivot columns in $A$ is
$11-9=2$.

Therefore,

$$
\textrm{dim(Null}(A))=2
$$

.

<a id="identifying-the-dimension-of-the-null-space-of-matrices-using-inspection"></a>
## Identifying the Dimension of the Null Space of Matrices Using Inspection

**Example:** Which of the following statements are true?

1. If $\begin{bmatrix}1 & 3 & 4 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 0\end{bmatrix}$ then $\textrm{dim(Null}(A))=2$
2. If $\begin{bmatrix}2 & 0 & 4 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0\end{bmatrix}$ then $\textrm{dim(Null}(B))=0$
3. If $\begin{bmatrix}2 & 6 & 7 \\ 0 & 4 & 2 \\ 0 & 0 & 0\end{bmatrix}$ then $\textrm{dim(Null}(C))=1$

**Explanation**

The dimension of the null space of a matrix is the number of non-pivot columns in the matrix.

Also, all of the given matrices are in row echelon form.

With that in mind, let's consider each of the statements.

- Statement I is false. The matrix $A$ has $2$ pivot columns and $1$ non-pivot column (namely, the $2$nd column). So, $\textrm{dim(Null}(A))=1$.
- Statement II is false. The matrix $B$ has $1$ pivot column and $2$ non-pivot columns (namely, the $2$nd and $3$rd columns). So, $\textrm{dim(Null}(B))=2$.
- Statement III is true. The matrix $C$ has $2$ pivot columns and $1$ non-pivot column (namely, the $3$nd column). So, $\textrm{dim(Null}(C))=1$.

Therefore, only statement III is true.

<a id="the-nullity-of-a-matrix"></a>
## The Nullity of a Matrix

The dimension of the null space of a matrix $A$ is often called the **nullity** of $A$. We will also use the following notation:

$$
\textrm{nullity}(A) = \textrm{dim}(\textrm{Null}(A))
$$

---

<a id="finding-the-nullity-of-a-matrix-using-row-reduction"></a>
## Finding the Nullity of a Matrix Using Row Reduction

**Example:** Given $\begin{bmatrix}2 & 1 & 3 & -2 \\ 0 & -3 & -2 & -1 \\ -6 & 0 & -7 & 4 \\ 0 & 9 & 6 & 3\end{bmatrix}$ what is $\textrm{nullity}(A)$?

**Explanation**

To find $\textrm{dim}(\textrm{Null}(A))$, we reduce $A$ to row echelon form, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= \begin{bmatrix}2 & 1 & 3 & -2 \\ 0 & -3 & -2 & -1 \\ -6 & 0 & -7 & 4 \\ 0 & 9 & 6 & 3\end{bmatrix}\begin{vmatrix}R_{3} &:=R_{3} + 3R_{1} \\ ∼\begin{bmatrix}2 & 1 & 3 & -2 \\ 0 & -3 & -2 & -1 \\ 0 & 3 & 2 & -2 \\ 0 & 9 & 6 & 3\end{bmatrix} & R_{3}\end{vmatrix}:=R_{3} + R_{2} \\
&= ∼\begin{bmatrix}2 & 1 & 3 & -2 \\ 0 & -3 & -2 & -1 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & -3 \\ 0 & 9 & 6 & 3\end{bmatrix} \mid R_{4} \mid:=R_{4} + 3R_{2} \\
&= ∼\begin{bmatrix}2 & 1 & 3 & -2 \\ 0 & -3 & -2 & -1 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & -3 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

The reduced matrix has $3$ pivot columns and $1$ non-pivot column (namely, the $3$rd column).

Therefore,

$$
\textrm{nullity}(A) = \textrm{dim}(\textrm{Null}(A)) = 1
$$
