## Introduction

The set of vectors $\{\mathbf{v}_1, \mathbf{v}_2, \ldots, \mathbf{v}_n \}$ in an abstract vector space $V$ is **linearly dependent** if there exist real scalars $x_1,x_2,\ldots,x_n$ not all zero such that

$$
x_1\mathbf{v}_1+x_2\mathbf{v}_2+\ldots+x_n\mathbf{v}_n=\mathbf{0}
$$

Otherwise, if

$$
x_1 = x_2 = \cdots = x_n = 0
$$

is the only solution, the set is **linearly independent**.

Let's consider some examples.

- First, let's consider the vector space $\mathbb{R}[t]$ of all polynomials in variable $t$, along with the set
$S = \big\{\underbrace{1}_{\color{blue}p_1(t)}, \: \underbrace{t}_{\color{blue}p_2(t)}, \: \underbrace{t^2}_{\color{blue}p_3(t)} \big\}$.
To determine whether $S$ is linearly dependent in $\mathbb{R}[t]$, we must solve the equation
$x_{1}p_{1}(t) + x_{2}p_{2}(t) + x_{3}p_{3}(t)|= 0$.
Substituting the given expressions for $p_1(t),p_2(t)$ and $p_3(t)$ into the above equation, we obtain
$x_{1} \cdot 1 + x_{2} \cdot t + x_{3} \cdot t^{2}|= 0; x_{1} + x_{2}t + x_{3}t^{2}|= 0$.
The final equation is true for every $t$ if and only if
${x_{1} = 0; x_{2} = 0; x_{3} = 0$.
Since $x_1 = x_2 = x_3 = 0$ is the only solution, $S$ is linearly independent in $\mathbb R[t]$.

- Let's consider the vector space $\mathbb R[t]$ once more, along with the set
$T = \big\{\underbrace{t^2+1}_{\color{red}q_1(t)}, \: \underbrace{-2t^2-2}_{\color{red}q_2(t)} \big\}$.
To determine whether $T$ is linearly dependent in $\mathbb{R}[t]$, we must solve the equation
$x_{1}q_{1}(t) + x_{2}q_{2}(t)|= 0; x_{1}(t^{2} + 1) + x_{2}(-2t^{2} - 2)|= 0; (x_{1} - 2x_{2}) + (x_{1} - 2x_{2})t^{2}|= 0$.
The final equation is true for every $t$ if and only if
${x_{1} - 2x_{2} = 0; x_{1} - 2x_{2} = 0$.
This system has the nonzero solution $(x_1,x_2)=(2,1)$. As a result, we get
$2 \cdot {\color{red}q_1(t)} + 1 \cdot {\color{red}q_2(t)} = 0$.
Therefore, the set $\big\{q_1(t), q_2(t) \big\}$ is linearly dependent in $\mathbb R[t]$.

## Finding the Coefficients That Show Linear Dependence Among Polynomial Vectors

**Example:** Consider the set $\big\{p_1(t), p_2(t),p_3(t) \big\}$ of polynomials from $\mathbb{R}[t]$, where
$p_1(t)=1+t, \qquad p_2(t)=-2t-4, \qquad p_3(t)=-2t-3$.
If the set is linearly dependent, find the value of $\dfrac{x_2}{x_1}$ from a non-zero solution of $x_1 \cdot p_1(t)+x_2 \cdot p_2(t) + x_3 \cdot p_3(t)=0$.

**Explanation**

If the set is linearly dependent, then there exist $x_1,x_2,x_3$ not all zero such that

$$
x_1p_1(t)+x_2p_2(t)+x_3p_3(t)=0
$$

Otherwise, if

$$
x_1 = x_2 = x_3 = 0
$$

the set is linearly independent.

Substituting the given expressions for $p_1(t),p_2(t)$, and $p_3(t)$ into the above equation, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_{1}(1 + t) + x_{2}(-2t - 4) + x_{3}(-2t - 3) &= 0 \\
(x_{1} - 4x_{2} - 3x_{3}) + (x_{1} - 2x_{2} - 2x_{3})t &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

The final equation is true for every $t$ if and only if

$$
{x_{1} - 4x_{2} - 3x_{3} = 0; x_{1} - 2x_{2} - 2x_{3} = 0
$$

Now, we consider the corresponding augmented matrix and reduce it to row echelon form using Gaussian elimination:

$$
\begin{aligned}
M &= \begin{bmatrix}1 & -4 & -3 & 0 \\ 1 & -2 & -2 & 0\end{bmatrix} \mid R_{2} \mid:=R_{2} + (-1)R_{1} \\
&= ∼\begin{bmatrix}1 & -4 & -3 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 & 1 & 0\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

The pivot columns are the $1$st and $2$nd. Hence, $x_3$ is a free variable. The system is now

$$
{x_{1} - 4x_{2} - 3x_{3} = 0; 2x_{2} + x_{3} = 0
$$

From the second equation, we get

$$
x_2=-\dfrac 12x_3
$$

Substituting this into the first equation, we get

$$
x_1-4\left(-\dfrac 12x_3\right)-3x_3=0 \qquad\Longrightarrow \qquad x_1=x_3
$$

Therefore, the general solution is
$\begin{bmatrix}x_{1} \\ x_{2} \\ x_{3}\end{bmatrix}$
Finally, for any

$$
x_3 \neq 0
$$

we obtain

$$
\dfrac{x_2}{x_1}=\dfrac{\left(-\dfrac 12x_3\right)}{x_3}=-\dfrac 12
$$

## Finding the Coefficients That Show Linear Dependence Among Matrix Vectors

**Example:** Consider the set of matrices $\big\{A, B \big\}$, where
$\begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 \\ -2 & 4\end{bmatrix}$
If the set is linearly dependent, find the value of $\dfrac{x_1}{x_2}$ from a non-zero solution of $x_1A+x_2B=O$, where $O$ is the $2 \times 2$ zero-matrix.

**Explanation**

If the set is linearly dependent, then there exist $x_1,x_2$ not both zero such that

$$
x_1A+x_2B=O
$$

Otherwise, if

$$
x_1 = x_2 = 0
$$

the set is linearly independent.

Substituting the matrices $A$ and $B$ into the above equation, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_{1}\begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 \\ -2 & 4\end{bmatrix} + x_{2}\begin{bmatrix}3 & 5 \\ 2 & -6\end{bmatrix} &= \begin{bmatrix}0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0\end{bmatrix} \\
\begin{bmatrix}x_{1} & 0 \\ -2x_{1} & 4x_{1}\end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix}3x_{2} & 5x_{2} \\ 2x_{2} & -6x_{2}\end{bmatrix} &= \begin{bmatrix}0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0\end{bmatrix} \\
\begin{bmatrix}x_{1} + 3x_{2} & 5x_{2} \\ -2x_{1} + 2x_{2} & 4x_{1} - 6x_{2}\end{bmatrix} &= \begin{bmatrix}0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Equating the corresponding entries, we obtain the following system of equations:

$$
{x_{1} + 3x_{2} = 0; 5x_{2} = 0; - 2x_{1} + 2x_{2} = 0; 4x_{1} - 6x_{2} = 0
$$

Now, we consider the corresponding augmented matrix and reduce it to row echelon form using Gaussian elimination:

$$
\begin{aligned}
M &= \begin{bmatrix}1 & 3 & 0 \\ 0 & 5 & 0 \\ -2 & 2 & 0 \\ 4 & -6 & 0\end{bmatrix}\begin{vmatrix}R_{3}:=R_{3} + 2R_{1} \\ R_{4}:=R_{4} + (-4)R_{1} \\ ∼\begin{bmatrix}1 & 3 & 0 \\ 0 & 5 & 0 \\ 0 & 8 & 0 \\ 0 & -18 & 0\end{bmatrix}\end{vmatrix}R_{3}:=R_{3} + (-\frac{8}{5})R_{2} \\
&= R_{4}:=R_{4} + \frac{18}{5}R_{2} \\
&= ∼\begin{bmatrix}1 & 3 & 0 \\ 0 & 5 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

The pivot columns are the $1$st and $2$nd columns. Therefore, we get the system

$$
{x_{1} + 3x_{2} = 0; 5x_{2} = 0
$$

which has only the zero solution $x_1=x_2=0$.

Consequently, $\big\{A, B \big\}$ is linearly independent.

<a id="an-important-property-of-linearly-dependent-sets"></a>
## An Important Property of Linearly Dependent Sets

Note the following important property of linearly dependent sets.

> *A set of vectors is linearly dependent if and only if it contains a vector that is a linear combination of **other** vectors from that set.*

To see why this is true, let $\{\mathbf{v}_1, \mathbf{v}_2,\cdots, \mathbf{v}_n\}$ be a linearly dependent set of $n$ vectors. This means that the equation

$$
x_1\mathbf{v}_1 + x_2 \mathbf{v}_2 + \cdots + x_n\mathbf{v}_n = \mathbf{0}
$$

has a non-trivial solution, i.e. a solution in which at least one of the coefficients $x_1, x_2, \ldots, x_n$ is not zero. Without loss of generality, we may assume that

$$
x_1 \ne 0
$$

and we can rewrite the above equation as

$$
\mathbf{v}_1 = -\dfrac{x_2}{x_1} \mathbf{v}_2 - \dfrac{x_3}{x_1}\mathbf{v}_3 - \cdots - \dfrac{x_n}{x_1}\mathbf{v}_n
$$

The above argument shows that the vector $\mathbf{v}_1$ can be written as a linear combination of the other vectors from the set.

## Determining if a Given Set Is Linearly Independent Given Information on Its Elements

**Example:** Consider a vector space $V$ and vectors $\mathbf{v}_1, \mathbf{v}_2, \mathbf{v}_3 \in V$. Given that $\{\mathbf{v}_1, \mathbf{v}_2, \mathbf{v}_3\}$ is linearly independent, which of the following statements are true?

1. $\{4\mathbf{v}_1, 5\mathbf{v}_2, \mathbf{v}_3\}$ is linearly independent
2. $\{\mathbf{0}, \mathbf{v}_1, 2\mathbf{v}_2 \}$ is linearly independent
3. $\{\mathbf{v}_2, \mathbf{v}_3 \}$ is linearly independent

**Explanation**

Let's examine each of the given options in turn.

- Statement I is true. Suppose that
$x_{1}(4v_{1}) + x_{2}(5v_{2}) + x_{3}v_{3}|= 0$,
which, in turn, gives
$(4x_{1})v_{1} + (5x_{2})v_{2} + x_{3}v_{3}|= 0$.
Since $\{\mathbf{v}_1, \mathbf{v}_2, \mathbf{v}_3\}$ is a linearly independent set, the final equation can be true only if
${4x_{1} = 0; 5x_{2} = 0; x_{3} = 0$.
The unique solution of the system is $x_1=x_2=x_3=0$, which means that $\{4\mathbf{v}_1, 5\mathbf{v}_2, \mathbf{v}_3\}$ is linearly independent.
- Statement II is false. Indeed, notice that
$1 \cdot \mathbf{0} + 0 \cdot \mathbf{v}_1 + 0 \cdot (2\mathbf{v}_2) = \mathbf{0}$.
As a result, $x_1\mathbf{0}+x_2\mathbf{v}_1+x_3(2\mathbf{v}_2)=\mathbf{0}$ has a non-zero solution. This means that $\{\mathbf{0}, \mathbf{v}_1, 2\mathbf{v}_2 \}$ is linearly dependent. In general, any set containing the zero vector is linearly dependent.
- Statement III is true. Indeed, $x_1\mathbf{v}_2+x_2\mathbf{v}_3=\mathbf{0}$ must have only the zero solution $x_1=x_2=0$. If this equation has a non-zero solution, $a \cdot \mathbf{v}_2 + b \cdot \mathbf{v}_3=\mathbf{0}$, then we obtain
$0 \cdot \mathbf{v}_1 + a \cdot \mathbf{v}_2 + b \cdot \mathbf{v}_3 = \mathbf{0}$,
which would mean that $\{\mathbf{v}_1, \mathbf{v}_2, \mathbf{v}_3\}$ is linearly dependent.

Therefore, the correct answer is "I and III only."
