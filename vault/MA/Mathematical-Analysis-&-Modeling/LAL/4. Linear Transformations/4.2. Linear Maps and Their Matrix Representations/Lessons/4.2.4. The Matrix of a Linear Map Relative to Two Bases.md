## Introduction

Let's suppose we have the linear map

$$
\mathbf T: {\color{blue}V} \to {\color{red}W}
$$

where the bases of the vector spaces $\color{blue}V$ and ${\color{red}W}$ respectively are given by

$$
{\color{blue}\mathcal{B}}=\{{\color{blue}\mathbf{b}_1},{\color{blue}\mathbf{b}_2}, {\color{blue}\mathbf{b}_3} \}, \qquad \qquad {\color{red}\mathcal{C}} = \{{\color{red}\mathbf{c}_1},{\color{red}\mathbf{c}_2} \}
$$

The **matrix of $\mathbf{T}$ with respect to the bases $\color{blue}\mathcal{B}$ and $\color{red}\mathcal{C}$** is the matrix whose columns are the coordinates of the vectors $\mathbf{T}({\color{blue}\mathbf{b}_1})$, $\mathbf{T}(\color{blue}{\mathbf{b}_2})$, and $\mathbf{T}({\color{blue}\mathbf{b}_3})$ relative to the basis ${\color{red}\mathcal{C}}$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
[T]_{B \mid C} &= [ \mid \mid \mid \mid \mid \\
&= [T(b_{1})]_{C} \mid [T(b_{2})]_{C} \mid [T(b_{3})]_{C} \\
&= \mid \mid \mid \mid \mid ]
\end{aligned}
$$

For example, if

$$
\begin{bmatrix}3 \\ 1\end{bmatrix}
$$

then

$$
\begin{aligned}
[T]_{B \mid C} &= \begin{bmatrix}3 & 10 & -12 \\ 1 & -1 & 0\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding the Matrix of a Linear Map Relative to Two Bases Using the Definition

**Example:** Suppose $\mathbf{T}: V \rightarrow W$ is a linear map and $\mathcal{B}=\{\mathbf{b}_1,\mathbf{b}_2, \mathbf{b}_3 \}$ and $\mathcal{C} = \{\mathbf{c}_1,\mathbf{c}_2 \}$ are the bases of the vector spaces $V$ and $W$, respectively. Find the matrix $[\mathbf{T}]_{\mathcal{B} \mid \mathcal{C}}$ of the map $\mathbf{T}$ relative to these bases, if

> $\mathbf{T}(\mathbf{b}_1)=-3\mathbf{c}_1+5\mathbf{c}_2,\quad$ $\mathbf{T}(\mathbf{b}_2)=\mathbf{c}_2-2\mathbf{c}_1\quad$ and $\quad\mathbf{T}(\mathbf{b}_3)=\mathbf{c}_1+5\mathbf{c}_2$.

**Explanation**

First, we write the images of vectors in $\mathcal{B}$ relative to the basis $\mathcal{C}$:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}T(b_{1}) =-3c_{1} + 5c_{2} & ⟹[T(b_{1})]_{C} = \begin{bmatrix}-3 \\ 5\end{bmatrix} \\ T(b_{2}) = c_{2} - 2c_{1} & ⟹[T(b_{2})]_{C} = \begin{bmatrix}-2 \\ 1\end{bmatrix} \\ T(b_{3}) = c_{1} + 5c_{2} & ⟹[T(b_{3})]_{C} = \begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 5\end{bmatrix}\end{bmatrix}
$$

Now, $[\mathbf{T}]_{\mathcal{B} \mid \mathcal{C}}$ is the matrix whose columns are the coordinates of the vectors $\mathbf{T}(\mathbf{b}_1)$, $\mathbf{T}(\mathbf{b}_2)$ and $\mathbf{T}(\mathbf{b}_3)$ relative to the basis $\mathcal{C}$:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}-3 & -2 & 1 \\ 5 & 1 & 5\end{bmatrix}
$$

## Finding the Matrix of a Linear Map Relative to Two Bases Given One Basis

**Example:** Consider the basis $\mathcal{B}=\{\mathbf{b}_1, \mathbf{b}_2, \mathbf{b}_3\}$ of the vector space $V$ and the basis $\mathcal{C}$ of $\mathbb{R}^2$ given by
$\begin{bmatrix}-1 \\ 1\end{bmatrix}$

Find the matrix $[\mathbf{T}]_{\mathcal{B} \mid \mathcal{C}}$ of the map $\mathbf{T}:V \to \mathbb{R}^2$ relative to these bases if
$\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 2\end{bmatrix}$

**Explanation**

Recall that $[\mathbf{T}]_{\mathcal{B} \mid \mathcal{C}}$ is the matrix whose columns are the coordinates of the vectors $\mathbf{T}(\mathbf{b}_1)$, $\mathbf{T}(\mathbf{b}_2)$, and $\mathbf{T}(\mathbf{b}_3)$ relative to the basis $\mathcal{C}$.

First, we need to find the coordinates of $\mathbf{T}(\mathbf{b}_i)$ relative to the basis $\mathcal{C}$ for each $i=1,2,3$. We can do this simultaneously by considering the following matrix:

$$
\mid \mid \mid \mid \mid \mid \mid \mid \mid; c_{1}\begin{vmatrix}c_{2} & T(b_{1}) & T(b_{2})\end{vmatrix}T(b_{3}); \mid \mid \mid \mid \mid \mid \mid \mid \mid
$$

We reduce the left-hand side to reduced row echelon form using Gaussian elimination:

$$
\begin{aligned}
M &= \begin{bmatrix}-1 & 4 & 1 & -3 & 7 \\ 1 & -3 & 2 & -5 & 1\end{bmatrix}\begin{vmatrix}R_{1}:=(-1)R_{1} \\ ∼\begin{bmatrix}1 & -4 & -1 & 3 & -7 \\ 1 & -3 & 2 & -5 & 1\end{bmatrix}\end{vmatrix}R_{2}:=R_{2} + (-1)R_{1} \\
&= ∼\begin{bmatrix}1 & -4 & -1 & 3 & -7 \\ 0 & 1 & 3 & -8 & 8\end{bmatrix} \mid R_{1}:=R_{1} + 4R_{2} \\
&= ∼\begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 & 11 & -29 & 25 \\ 0 & 1 & 3 & -8 & 8\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, the right-hand side gives the matrix of the linear transformation relative to the bases $\mathcal{B}$ and $\mathcal{C}$:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}11 & -29 & 25 \\ 3 & -8 & 8\end{bmatrix}
$$

<a id="the-image-of-a-vector-under-a-linear-map-given-by-a-matrix-relative-to-two-bases"></a>
## The Image of a Vector Under a Linear Map Given by a Matrix Relative to Two Bases

Given the matrix $[\mathbf{T}]_{\mathcal{B} \mid \mathcal{C}}$ of a linear map $\mathbf{T}:V \to W$ relative to the bases $\mathcal{B}$ and $\mathcal{C}$, and the coordinates $[\mathbf{x}]_{\mathcal{B}}$ of a vector $\mathbf{x}$ relative to $\mathcal{B}$, the image of $\mathbf{x}$ under $\mathbf{T}$ with respect to the basis $\mathcal{C}$ can be found as

$$
[\mathbf{T}(\mathbf{x})]_{\mathcal{C}} = [\mathbf{T}]_{{\mathcal{B}} \mid {\mathcal{C}}}\cdot [\mathbf{x}]_{\mathcal{B}}
$$

For example, if

$$
\begin{bmatrix}1 & 3 & -2 \\ 1 & 2 & 0\end{bmatrix}
$$

then the image of $\mathbf x$ under $\mathbf T$ relative to $\mathcal C$ is given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
[T(x)]_{C} &= \begin{bmatrix}1 & 3 & -2 \\ 1 & 2 & 0\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}3 \\ 0 \\ 1\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 3\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding the Image of a Vector Under a Linear Map Given the Matrix Relative to Two Bases

**Example:** $\begin{bmatrix}1 & 4 & -2 \\ 3 & 7 & 1\end{bmatrix}$

Suppose $\mathcal{B}$ and $\mathcal{C}$ are bases of the vector spaces $V$ and $W$, respectively. The matrix $[\mathbf{T}]_{\mathcal{B} \mid \mathcal{C}}$ of the linear map $\mathbf{T}:V \rightarrow W$ relative to these bases, and the coordinates of the vector $\mathbf{x} \in V$ relative to $\mathcal{B}$ are shown above. Find $[\mathbf{T}(\mathbf{x})]_{\mathcal{C}}$.

**Explanation**

The coordinates of the image of $\mathbf{x}$ under $\mathbf{T}$ relative to the basis $\mathcal{C}$ can be found as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
[T(x)]_{C} &= [T]_{B \mid C} \cdot [x]_{B} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}1 & 4 & -2 \\ 3 & 7 & 1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}3 \\ -2 \\ 1\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}-7 \\ -4\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding the Image of a Vector Given With Respect to the Standard Basis Under a Linear Map

**Example:** $\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 4\end{bmatrix}$

Consider the basis $\mathcal{B}$ of $\mathbb{R}^2$ (shown above) and a basis $\mathcal{C}$ of $\mathbb{R}^3$.
The matrix $[\mathbf{T}]_{\mathcal{B} \mid \mathcal{C}}$ of the linear map $\mathbf{T}:\mathbb{R}^2 \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^3$ relative to these bases, and the vector $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^2$, are also given. Find $[\mathbf{T}(\mathbf{x})]_{\mathcal{C}}$.

**Explanation**

We can find the coordinates of the image of $\mathbf{x}$ under $\mathbf{T}$ relative to the basis $\mathcal{C}$ as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
[T(x)]_{C} &= [T]_{B \mid C} \cdot [x]_{B}
\end{aligned}
$$

First, we need to find $[\mathbf{x}]_{\mathcal{B}}$, the coordinates of $\mathbf{x}$ relative to $\mathcal{B}$. So, let

$$
\begin{bmatrix}5 \\ 2\end{bmatrix}
$$

This gives us the system

$$
{x_{1} - 2x_{2} = 5; 4x_{1} - 5x_{2} = 2
$$

We now row-reduce the corresponding augmented matrix using Gaussian elimination, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
M &= \begin{bmatrix}1 & -2 & 5 \\ 4 & -5 & 2\end{bmatrix}\begin{vmatrix}R_{2}:=R_{2} + (-4)R_{1} \\ ∼\begin{bmatrix}1 & -2 & 5 \\ 0 & 3 & -18\end{bmatrix}\end{vmatrix}R_{2}:=\frac{1}{3}R_{2} \\
&= ∼\begin{bmatrix}1 & -2 & 5 \\ 0 & 1 & -6\end{bmatrix} \mid R_{1}:=R_{1} + 2R_{2} \\
&= ∼\begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 & -7 \\ 0 & 1 & -6\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, $\begin{bmatrix}-7 \\ -6\end{bmatrix}$

Finally, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
[T(x)]_{C} &= [T]_{B \mid C} \cdot [x]_{B} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}1 & -4 \\ 3 & -2 \\ 0 & 1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}-7 \\ -6\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}17 \\ -9 \\ -6\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$
