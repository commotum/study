## Introduction

What is the matrix of the linear transformation

$$
\begin{bmatrix}x \\ y\end{bmatrix}
$$

relative to the basis

$$
\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 1\end{bmatrix}
$$

In other words, what is the matrix $[T]_{\mathcal{B}}$ such that

$$
[\mathbf{T}(\mathbf{x})]_{\mathcal{B}} = [T]_{\mathcal{B}} \cdot[\mathbf{x}]_{\mathcal{B}}
$$

In fact, the answer is the matrix whose columns are the coordinates of the images of the basis vectors $\mathbf{b}_1, \mathbf{b}_2$ under the transformation relative to the basis:
$[\mid \mid \mid; [T(b_{1})]_{B} \mid [T(b_{2})]_{B}; \mid \mid \mid]$

This matrix is called the **matrix for $\mathbf{T}$ relative to $\mathcal{B}$** or the **$\mathcal{B}$-matrix for $\mathbf{T}$.**

As a result, for the transformation $\begin{bmatrix}x \\ y\end{bmatrix}$, with respect to the basis

$$
\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 1\end{bmatrix}
$$

we have

$$
\begin{bmatrix}7 \\ -2\end{bmatrix}
$$

Now, to write

$$
{\color{red} \mathbf{T} \left(\mathbf{b}_1 \right)}
$$

and

$$
{\color{blue} \mathbf{T} \left(\mathbf{b}_2 \right)}
$$

with respect to our basis $\mathcal{B}$, we assume that

$\begin{bmatrix}α_{1} \\ α_{2}\end{bmatrix}$
where the components $\alpha_1$, $\alpha_2$, $\beta_1$, and $\beta_2$ satisfy

$$
{\color{red}\alpha_1} \mathbf{b}_1+{\color{red}\alpha_2}\mathbf{b}_2 = {\color{red} \mathbf{T} \left(\mathbf{b}_1 \right)}\quad \text{and}\quad {\color{blue}\beta_1}\mathbf{b}_1+{\color{blue}\beta_2} \mathbf{b}_2 = {\color{blue} \mathbf{T} \left(\mathbf{b}_2 \right)}
$$

Solving these two systems gives
$\begin{bmatrix}α_{1} \\ α_{2}\end{bmatrix}$

Therefore,

$$
\begin{bmatrix}16 & 25 \\ -9 & -14\end{bmatrix}
$$

## Calculating the Image of a Vector Given the Matrix of a Linear Transformation Relative to a Basis

**Example:** Let $\mathcal{B}=\{\mathbf{b}_1,\mathbf{b}_2 \}$ be a basis of $\mathbb{R}^2$ and let $\mathbf{T}:\mathbb{R}^2 \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^2$ be a linear transformation. Find $[\mathbf{T}(\mathbf{x})]_{\mathcal{B}}$, given that
$\begin{bmatrix}3 & 1 \\ -2 & 5\end{bmatrix}$

**Explanation**

The image of $[\mathbf{x}]_{\mathcal{B}}$ relative to the basis $\mathcal{B}$ under the action of the linear transformation $\mathbf{T}$ is given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
[T(x)]_{B} &= [T]_{B}[x]_{B} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}3 & 1 \\ -2 & 5\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}-3 \\ 2\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}-7 \\ 16\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="the-general-case"></a>
## The General Case

In general, consider a basis

$$
\mathcal{B}=\{\mathbf{b}_1,\mathbf{b}_2,\dots, \mathbf{b}_n \}
$$

of a vector space $V$ and a linear transformation $\mathbf{T}:V \rightarrow V$. For any $\mathbf{x}\in V$ we have that

$$
\mathbf{x} = x_1\mathbf{b}_1+x_2\mathbf{b}_2+\cdots + x_n\mathbf{b}_n
$$

or, equivalently $\begin{bmatrix}x_{1} \\ ⋮ \\ x_{n}\end{bmatrix}$.
Then the matrix for $\mathbf{T}$ relative to $\mathcal{B}$ is given by
$[\mid \mid \mid \mid \mid; [T(b_{1})]_{B}\begin{vmatrix}[T(b_{2})]_{B} & ⋯\end{vmatrix}[T(b_{n})]_{B}; \mid \mid \mid \mid \mid]$

Therefore
$[\mid \mid \mid \mid \mid; [T(b_{1})]_{B}\begin{vmatrix}[T(b_{2})]_{B} & ⋯\end{vmatrix}[T(b_{n})]_{B}; \mid \mid \mid \mid \mid]$

## Finding the Matrix of a Linear Transformation Given the Images of the Basis Vectors

**Example:** Let $\mathcal{B}=\{\mathbf{b}_1,\mathbf{b}_2,\mathbf{b}_3 \}$ be a basis of $\mathbb{R}^3$. The matrix $[\,\mathbf{T}\,]_{\mathcal{B}}$ of the linear transformation $\mathbf{T}:\mathbb{R}^3 \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^3$ is given by

$\begin{bmatrix}1 & 1 & c \\ 0 & b & 1 \\ a & 0 & 1\end{bmatrix}$

Find the value of $\,a+b+c,\,$ given that

$T(b_{1})|= b_{1} + b_{3},; T(b_{2})|= b_{1} + b_{2},; T(b_{3})|= b_{2} + b_{3}$.

**Explanation**

First, we write down the coordinates of the images relative to the basis $\mathcal{B}$:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 0 \\ 1\end{bmatrix}
$$

Now, the $\mathcal{B}$-matrix for $\mathbf{T}$ is a matrix whose columns are the coordinates of the vectors $\mathbf{T}(\mathbf{b}_1)$, $\mathbf{T}(\mathbf{b}_2)$, and $\mathbf{T}(\mathbf{b}_3)$ relative to the basis $\mathcal{B}$. Therefore, we obtain

$$
\begin{bmatrix}1 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 & 1\end{bmatrix}
$$

Finally, $a=1$, $b=1$, $c=0$ and

$$
a+b+c = 1+1+0 = 2
$$

## Finding the Matrix of a Linear Transformation Relative to a Basis in a Two-Dimensional Vector Space

**Example:** Let $\mathcal{B}$ be a basis of $\mathbb{R}^2$ and let $\mathbf{T}: \mathbb{R}^2 \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^2$ be a linear transformation. Find $[\,\mathbf{T}\,]_{\mathcal{B}}$, given that

$\begin{bmatrix}5 \\ -4\end{bmatrix}$

**Explanation**

Let's denote the vectors of $\mathcal{B}$ as $\{\mathbf{b}_1,\mathbf{b}_2 \}$. The $\mathcal{B}$-matrix for $\mathbf{T}$ is a matrix whose columns are the coordinates of the vectors $\mathbf{T}(\mathbf{b}_1)$ and $\mathbf{T}(\mathbf{b}_2)$ relative to the basis $\mathcal{B}$.

To find the coordinates of $\mathbf{T}(\mathbf{b}_1)$ and $\mathbf{T}(\mathbf{b}_2)$ relative to the basis $\mathcal{B}$, we consider the matrix
$\mid \mid \mid \mid \mid \mid \mid; b_{1}\begin{vmatrix}b_{2} & T(b_{1})\end{vmatrix}T(b_{2}); \mid \mid \mid \mid \mid \mid \mid$

Now, we reduce the left-hand side to reduced row echelon form using Gaussian elimination. Then, on the right-hand side, we will obtain the $\mathcal{B}$-matrix for $\mathbf{T}$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
M &= \begin{bmatrix}1 & 1 & 5 & 3 \\ -1 & 1 & -4 & -7\end{bmatrix}\begin{vmatrix}R_{2}:=R_{2} + R_{1} \\ ∼\begin{bmatrix}1 & 1 & 5 & 3 \\ 0 & 2 & 1 & -4\end{bmatrix}\end{vmatrix}R_{2}:=\frac{1}{2}R_{2} \\
&= ∼\begin{bmatrix}1 & 1 & 5 & 3 \\ 0 & 1 & \frac{1}{2} & -2\end{bmatrix} \mid R_{1}:=R_{1} - R_{2} \\
&= ∼\begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 & \frac{9}{2} & 5 \\ 0 & 1 & \frac{1}{2} & -2\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, we obtain

$$
\begin{bmatrix}\frac{9}{2} & 5 \\ \frac{1}{2} & -2\end{bmatrix}
$$

## Finding the Matrix of a Linear Transformation Relative to a Basis in a Three-Dimensional Vector Space

**Example:** The basis $\mathcal{B}$ of $\mathbb{R}^3$ and the matrix $[\,\mathbf{T}\,]_{\mathcal{B}}$ of the linear transformation $\mathbf{T}: \mathbb{R}^3 \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^3$ relative to $\mathcal{B}$ are given by

$\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 0 \\ 0\end{bmatrix}$

What is the value of $\,a+b+c,\,$ given that

$\begin{bmatrix}-9 \\ 0 \\ 4\end{bmatrix}$

**Explanation**

Let's denote the vectors of $\mathcal{B}$ as $\{\mathbf{b}_1,\mathbf{b}_2,\mathbf{b}_3 \}$. The $\mathcal{B}$-matrix for $\mathbf{T}$ is a matrix whose columns are the coordinates of the vectors $\mathbf{T}(\mathbf{b}_1)$, $\mathbf{T}(\mathbf{b}_2)$, and $\mathbf{T}(\mathbf{b}_3)$ relative to the basis $\mathcal{B}$.

To find the coordinates of $\mathbf{T}(\mathbf{b}_1)$, $\mathbf{T}(\mathbf{b}_2)$, and $\mathbf{T}(\mathbf{b}_3)$ relative to the basis $\mathcal{B}$, we consider the matrix
$\mid \mid \mid \mid \mid \mid \mid \mid \mid \mid \mid; b_{1}\begin{vmatrix}b_{2} & b_{3} & T(b_{1}) & T(b_{2})\end{vmatrix}T(b_{3}); \mid \mid \mid \mid \mid \mid \mid \mid \mid \mid \mid$

Now, we reduce the left-hand side to reduced row echelon form using Gaussian elimination. Then, on the right-hand side, we will obtain the $\mathcal{B}$-matrix for $\mathbf{T}$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
M &= \begin{bmatrix}1 & -3 & 0 & -9 & 7 & 10 \\ 0 & 1 & -3 & 0 & -2 & -10 \\ 0 & 0 & 4 & 4 & 0 & 8\end{bmatrix}\begin{vmatrix}R_{3}:=\frac{1}{4}R_{3} \\ ∼\begin{bmatrix}1 & -3 & 0 & -9 & 7 & 10 \\ 0 & 1 & -3 & 0 & -2 & -10 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 & 1 & 0 & 2\end{bmatrix}\end{vmatrix}R_{2}:=R_{2} + 3R_{3} \\
&= ∼\begin{bmatrix}1 & -3 & 0 & -9 & 7 & 10 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 & 3 & -2 & -4 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 & 1 & 0 & 2\end{bmatrix} \mid R_{1}:=R_{1} + 3R_{2} \\
&= ∼\begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 1 & -2 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 & 3 & -2 & -4 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 & 1 & 0 & 2\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, we obtain
$\begin{bmatrix}0 & 1 & -2 \\ 3 & -2 & -4 \\ 1 & 0 & 2\end{bmatrix}$

Finally, $a=1$, $b=-2$, $c=-2$ and

$$
a+b+c = 1+(-2)+(-2) = -3
$$
