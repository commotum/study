## Introduction

Let's find the eigenvector corresponding to the eigenvalue

$$
\lambda=-\textrm{i}
$$

of the matrix $\begin{bmatrix}1 & -1 \\ 2 & -1\end{bmatrix}$

To compute the eigenvectors of a matrix, we need to find non-zero solutions of the matrix equation

$$
(A - \lambda I) \textbf{x}=\mathbf{0}
$$

In our case, we have

$$
\,\lambda =-\textrm{i}
$$

and

$$
\begin{aligned}
A - λI &= \begin{bmatrix}1 & -1 \\ 2 & -1\end{bmatrix} - \begin{bmatrix}-\textrm{i} & 0 \\ 0 & -\textrm{i}\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}1 + \textrm{i} & -1 \\ 2 & -1 + \textrm{i}\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

So, we have the homogeneous system of linear equations

$$
{(1 + \textrm{i})x_{1} - x_{2} = 0; 2x_{1} + (-1 + \textrm{i})x_{2} = 0
$$

Now, since the matrix $A-(-\textrm{i})I$ is singular, the system above must have a non-zero solution. This means that one equation of the system is a multiple of another.

As a result, we can drop one of the rows and take one of the variables as the free variable. For example, from the first equation, we get

$$
x_2 = (1+\textrm{i})x_1
$$

Therefore, by setting $x_1\in\mathbb C$ to be the free variable, we find that the general solution to our system is given by

$$
\begin{bmatrix}x_{1} \\ (1 + \textrm{i})x_{1}\end{bmatrix}
$$

Setting $x_1=1$, we get the eigenvector

$$
\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 1 + \textrm{i}\end{bmatrix}
$$

Notice that our eigenvector has complex-valued entries.

**Note:** To check our result, we can show that

$$
A\mathbf v = \lambda \mathbf v
$$

Computing $A\mathbf v$, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
Av &= \begin{bmatrix}1 & -1 \\ 2 & -1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 1 + \textrm{i}\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}-\textrm{i} \\ 1 - \textrm{i}\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

and computing $\lambda \mathbf v$ gives

$$
\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 1 + \textrm{i}\end{bmatrix}
$$

So

$$
A\mathbf v = \lambda \mathbf v
$$

as required.

## Finding a Complex Eigenvalue of a Matrix Given a Complex Eigenvector

**Example:** Determine whether $\begin{bmatrix}1 + 3\textrm{i} \\ 4\end{bmatrix}$ is an eigenvector of the matrix $\begin{bmatrix}2 & -5 \\ 8 & -2\end{bmatrix}$
If so, then what is the corresponding eigenvalue?

**Explanation**

The eigenvectors of $A$ are the non-zero vectors $\mathbf{v}$ such that

$$
A\mathbf{v}=\lambda \mathbf{v}
$$

So, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
Av &= \begin{bmatrix}2 & -5 \\ 8 & -2\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1 + 3\textrm{i} \\ 4\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}-18 + 6\textrm{i} \\ 24\textrm{i}\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}18\textrm{i}^{2} + 6\textrm{i} \\ 24\textrm{i}\end{bmatrix} \\
&= 6\textrm{i}\begin{bmatrix}1 + 3\textrm{i} \\ 4\end{bmatrix} \\
&= 6\textrm{i}v
\end{aligned}
$$

Since

$$
A\mathbf{v} = 6\textrm{i} \mathbf{v}
$$

we conclude that $\mathbf{v}$ is an eigenvector of $A$ and the corresponding eigenvalue is

$$
\lambda=6\textrm{i}
$$

## Finding a Complex Eigenvector of a Matrix Given a Complex Eigenvalue

**Example:** Find an eigenvector of
$\begin{bmatrix}-3 & 4 \\ -4 & -3\end{bmatrix}$
that corresponds to the eigenvalue $\lambda=-3+4\textrm{i}$.

**Explanation**

To compute the eigenvectors of a matrix, we need to find non-zero solutions of the matrix equation

$$
(A - \lambda I) \textbf{x}=\mathbf{0}
$$

In our case, we have

$$
\,\lambda =-3+4\textrm{i}
$$

and

$$
\begin{aligned}
A - λI &= \begin{bmatrix}-3 & 4 \\ -4 & -3\end{bmatrix} - \begin{bmatrix}-3 + 4\textrm{i} & 0 \\ 0 & -3 + 4\textrm{i}\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}-4\textrm{i} & 4 \\ -4 & -4\textrm{i}\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

So, we have the homogeneous system of linear equations

$$
{-4\textrm{i}x_{1} + 4x_{2} = 0; - 4x_{1} - 4\textrm{i}x_{2} = 0
$$

Now, since the matrix $A-(-3+4\textrm{i})I$ is singular, the system above must have a non-zero solution. This means that one equation of the system is a multiple of another.

As a result, we can drop one of the rows and take one of the variables to be the free variable. For example, from the second equation, we get

$$
x_1 = -\textrm{i}x_2
$$

Therefore, the general solution is given by

$$
\begin{bmatrix}-\textrm{i}x_{2} \\ x_{2}\end{bmatrix}
$$

Setting $x_2=1$, we get the eigenvector $\begin{bmatrix}-\textrm{i} \\ 1\end{bmatrix}$

## Finding Two Linearly Independent Complex Eigenvectors of a 2x2 Matrix

**Example:** Let $\begin{bmatrix}a_{1} \\ b_{1}\end{bmatrix}$ and $\begin{bmatrix}a_{2} \\ b_{2}\end{bmatrix}$ be linearly independent eigenvectors of the matrix
$\begin{bmatrix}4 & 5 \\ -5 & 4\end{bmatrix}$
Given that $\lambda_1=4+5\textrm{i}$ is an eigenvalue of $A$, find the value of ${\dfrac{a_1a_2}{b_1b_2}}$.

**Explanation**

To compute the eigenvectors of a matrix, we need to find non-zero solutions of the matrix equation

$$
(A - \lambda I) \textbf{x}=\mathbf{0}
$$

In our case, we have

$$
\,\lambda_1 =4+5\textrm{i}
$$

and

$$
\begin{aligned}
A - λ_{1}I &= \begin{bmatrix}4 & 5 \\ -5 & 4\end{bmatrix} - \begin{bmatrix}4 + 5\textrm{i} & 0 \\ 0 & 4 + 5\textrm{i}\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}-5\textrm{i} & 5 \\ -5 & -5\textrm{i}\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

So, we have the homogeneous system of linear equations

$$
{-5\textrm{i}x_{1} + 5x_{2} = 0; - 5x_{1} - 5\textrm{i}x_{2} = 0
$$

Now, since the matrix $A-(4+5\textrm{i})I$ is singular, the system above must have a non-zero solution. This means that one equation of the system is a multiple of another.

As a result, we can drop one of the rows and take one of the variables as the free variable. For example, from the first equation, we get

$$
x_2 = \textrm{i}x_1
$$

So, the general solution is given by

$$
\begin{bmatrix}x_{1} \\ \textrm{i}x_{1}\end{bmatrix}
$$

Setting $x_1=1$, we get the eigenvector

$$
\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ \textrm{i}\end{bmatrix}
$$

Now, since

$$
\lambda_1=4+5\textrm{i}
$$

is a complex eigenvalue of $A$, the second eigenvalue of $A$ must be the complex conjugate of $\lambda_1$. Therefore, we have

$$
\lambda_2=\overline{\lambda_1}=4-5\textrm{i}
$$

Moreover, the corresponding eigenvector must be the complex conjugate of $\mathbf v_1$. Therefore, we have

$$
\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ -\textrm{i}\end{bmatrix}
$$

Finally, we obtain

$$
{\dfrac{a_1a_2}{b_1b_2}} = {\dfrac{(1)(1)}{(\textrm{i})(-\textrm{i})}} = 1
$$
