## Introduction

Consider the following $3\times 3$ matrix:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}3 & 6 & -8 \\ 0 & 0 & 6 \\ 0 & 3 & -4\end{bmatrix}
$$

If we are told that one of the eigenvalues of this matrix is

$$
\lambda = 3
$$

how do we calculate the corresponding eigenvector?

To compute the eigenvectors of a matrix, we need to find non-zero solutions of the matrix equation

$$
(A - \lambda I) \textbf{x}=\mathbf{0}
$$

In our case, we have $\lambda =3$. So, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
A - λI &= \begin{bmatrix}3 & 6 & -8 \\ 0 & 0 & 6 \\ 0 & 3 & -4\end{bmatrix} - 3\begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}0 & 6 & -8 \\ 0 & -3 & 6 \\ 0 & 3 & -7\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, to find the eigenvectors, we have to solve the corresponding homogeneous system of linear equations:

$$
{6x_{2} - 8x_{3} = 0; - 3x_{2} + 6x_{3} = 0; 3x_{2} - 7x_{3} = 0
$$

So, we row-reduce the augmented matrix $M$ to row echelon form using Gaussian elimination, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
M &= \begin{bmatrix}0 & 6 & -8 & 0 \\ 0 & -3 & 6 & 0 \\ 0 & 3 & -7 & 0\end{bmatrix}\begin{vmatrix}R_{2}:=R_{2} + \frac{1}{2}R_{1} \\ R_{3}:=R_{3} + (-\frac{1}{2})R_{1} \\ ∼\begin{bmatrix}0 & 6 & -8 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & -3 & 0\end{bmatrix}\end{vmatrix}R_{3}:=R_{3} + \frac{3}{2}R_{2} \\
&= ∼\begin{bmatrix}0 & 6 & -8 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

In this reduced matrix, there are two pivot columns (the $2$nd and $3$rd). Thus, $x_1$ is a free variable. From the second equation, we have $x_3 = 0$. Substituting this into the first equation, we obtain $x_2 = 0$. Therefore, the general solution is

$$
\begin{bmatrix}x_{1} \\ 0 \\ 0\end{bmatrix}
$$

Setting $x_1=1$, we get the following eigenvector:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}a \\ b \\ c\end{bmatrix}
$$

## Calculating the Components of an Eigenvector Corresponding to a Given Eigenvalue

**Example:** The vector $\mathbf{v}$ and the matrix $A$ are given below.
If $\mathbf{v}$ is an eigenvector of $A$ that corresponds to the eigenvalue $\lambda=3$, then what is the value of $\dfrac{b}{c}$?

$\begin{bmatrix}a \\ b \\ c\end{bmatrix}$

**Explanation**

To compute the eigenvectors of a matrix, we need to find non-zero solutions of the matrix equation

$$
(A - \lambda I) \textbf{x}=\mathbf{0}
$$

In our case, we have $\lambda =3$. So, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
A - λI &= \begin{bmatrix}5 & 0 & -6 \\ 2 & 1 & -4 \\ 1 & 0 & 0\end{bmatrix} - 3\begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}2 & 0 & -6 \\ 2 & -2 & -4 \\ 1 & 0 & -3\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

To find the eigenvectors, we have to solve the corresponding homogeneous system of linear equations:

$$
{2x_{1} - 6x_{3} = 0; 2x_{1} - 2x_{2} - 4x_{3} = 0; x_{1} - 3x_{3} = 0
$$

So, we row-reduce the augmented matrix $M$ to row echelon form using Gaussian elimination, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
M &= \begin{bmatrix}2 & 0 & -6 & 0 \\ 2 & -2 & -4 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 & -3 & 0\end{bmatrix}\begin{vmatrix}R_{1}:=\frac{1}{2}R_{1} \\ ∼\begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 & -3 & 0 \\ 2 & -2 & -4 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 & -3 & 0\end{bmatrix}\end{vmatrix}R_{2}:=R_{2} + (-2)R_{1} \\
&= R_{3}:=R_{3} + (-1)R_{1} \\
&= ∼\begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 & -3 & 0 \\ 0 & -2 & 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

In the reduced matrix above, there are two pivot columns (the $1$st and the $2$nd). Thus, $x_3$ is a free variable. From the second equation, we obtain $x_2=x_3$. From the first equation, we get $x_1=3x_3$. Therefore, the general solution is

$$
\begin{bmatrix}3x_{3} \\ x_{3} \\ x_{3}\end{bmatrix}
$$

Setting, for instance, $x_3=1$, we get the following eigenvector:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}a \\ b \\ c\end{bmatrix}
$$

Finally,

$$
\dfrac{b}{c}=\dfrac{1}{1}=1
$$

## Calculating an Eigenvector Corresponding to a Given Eigenvalue

**Example:** Find an eigenvector of
$\begin{bmatrix}1 & 3 & 4 \\ 0 & -2 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & -1\end{bmatrix}$
that corresponds to the eigenvalue $\lambda=-2$.

**Explanation**

To compute the eigenvectors of a matrix, we need to find non-zero solutions of the matrix equation

$$
(A - \lambda I) \textbf{x}=\mathbf{0}
$$

In our case, we have

$$
\lambda =-2
$$

So, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
A - λI &= \begin{bmatrix}1 & 3 & 4 \\ 0 & -2 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & -1\end{bmatrix} - (-2)\begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}3 & 3 & 4 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

To find the eigenvectors corresponding to this eigenvalue, we have to solve the corresponding homogeneous system of linear equations:

$$
{3x_{1} + 3x_{2} + 4x_{3} = 0; 0 = 0; x_{3} = 0
$$

So, we row-reduce the augmented matrix $M$ to row echelon form using Gaussian elimination, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
M &= \begin{bmatrix}3 & 3 & 4 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 & 0\end{bmatrix} \mid R_{2}↔R_{3} \\
&= ∼\begin{bmatrix}3 & 3 & 4 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

In the reduced matrix above, there are two pivot columns (the $1$st and the $3$rd). Thus, $x_2$ is a free variable. From the second equation, we obtain $x_3=0$. Substituting this in the first equation, we get $x_1=-x_2$. Therefore, the general solution is

$$
\begin{bmatrix}-x_{2} \\ x_{2} \\ 0\end{bmatrix}
$$

Setting, for instance, $x_2=1$, we get the eigenvector
$\begin{bmatrix}-1 \\ 1 \\ 0\end{bmatrix}$

## Eigenvectors That Correspond to Different Eigenvalues

Note the following important theorem.

> *The set of eigenvectors $\{\mathbf{v}_1,\mathbf{v}_2, \ldots, \mathbf{v}_k \}$ of a matrix $A$, where each vector $\mathbf v_i$ corresponds to a different eigenvalue, is linearly independent.*

**Watch out!** Eigenvectors that correspond to the *same* eigenvalue *can* be (and very often *will* be) linearly dependent.

---

<a id="finding-the-eigenvectors-of-a-3x3-matrix"></a>
## Finding the Eigenvectors of a 3x3 Matrix

**Example:** $\begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 4\end{bmatrix}$

The vectors $\mathbf{v}_1$ and $\mathbf{v}_2$ are eigenvectors that correspond to the eigenvalues $\lambda_1=1$ and $\lambda_2=-1$ of the matrix $A$, given above. Find the third eigenvector $\mathbf{v}_3=\big[a, \, b, \, c \big]^T$ such that the set $\{\mathbf{v}_1,\mathbf{v}_2, \mathbf{v}_3 \}$ is linearly independent. What is the value of $\dfrac{a}{c}$?

**Explanation**

Since $A$ is a diagonal matrix, we have that its eigenvalues are the entries in the main diagonal, that is,

$$
\lambda_1=1
$$

$$
\lambda_2=-1
$$

and

$$
\lambda_3=4
$$

Also, since we obtained three distinct eigenvalues, the set of eigenvectors $\{\mathbf{v}_1,\mathbf{v}_2, \mathbf{v}_3 \}$, where each vector corresponds to different eigenvalue, will be linearly independent.

Now, recall that $\mathbf{v}_1$ and $\mathbf{v}_2$ correspond to the eigenvalues $\lambda_1=1$ and

$$
\lambda_2=-1
$$

Thus, the third eigenvector $\mathbf{v}_3$ must correspond to

$$
\lambda_3=4
$$

To compute the eigenvectors of a matrix, we need to find non-zero solutions of the matrix equation

$$
(A - \lambda I) \textbf{x}=\mathbf{0}
$$

In our case, we have $\lambda =4$. So, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
A - λI &= \begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & -1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 4\end{bmatrix} - 4\begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}-3 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & -5 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

To find the eigenvectors corresponding to this eigenvalue, we have to solve the corresponding homogeneous system of linear equations:

$$
{-3x_{1} = 0; - 5x_{2} = 0; 0 = 0
$$

Notice that the augmented matrix $M$ is already in its row echelon form:

$$
\begin{aligned}
M &= \begin{bmatrix}-3 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & -5 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

In this matrix, there are two pivot columns (the $1$st and the $2$nd). Thus, $x_3$ is a free variable. From the first and second equations, we obtain $x_1=x_2=0$. Therefore, the general solution is
$\begin{bmatrix}0 \\ 0 \\ x_{3}\end{bmatrix}$

Setting, for instance, $x_3=1$, we get the following eigenvector:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}a \\ b \\ c\end{bmatrix}
$$

Finally,

$$
\dfrac{a}{c}=\dfrac{0}{1}=0
$$
