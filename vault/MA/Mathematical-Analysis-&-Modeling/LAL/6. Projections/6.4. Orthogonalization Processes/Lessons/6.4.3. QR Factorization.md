## Introduction

The **QR factorization** of an $m\times n$ matrix $A$ with linearly independent columns is the matrix product

$$
\underset{{\large m} \times {\large n}}{A} = \underset{{\large m} \times {\large n}}{Q} \times \underset{{\large n} \times {\large n}}{R}
$$

where

- $Q$ is an $m \times n$ matrix whose columns form an orthonormal basis of $\text{Col}(A)$, and
- $R$ is an $n \times n$ upper triangular matrix with positive entries on its diagonal.

For example, the $3\times 2$ matrix

$$
\begin{bmatrix}2 & -3 \\ -4 & 3 \\ 4 & 0\end{bmatrix}
$$

has the QR factorization

$$
\begin{bmatrix}2 & -3 \\ -4 & 3 \\ 4 & 0\end{bmatrix}
$$

## Identifying Dimensions of Factors in a QR Factorization

**Example:** Given that $A=QR$ is a QR factorization of a $3 \times 2$ matrix $A$ with linearly independent columns, which of the following statements are true?

1. $Q$ is a $3 \times 3$ matrix
2. The columns of $Q$ form an orthonormal basis of $\text{Col}(A)$
3. $R$ is an upper triangular matrix

**Explanation**

Recall that if $A=QR$ is a QR factorization of an $m \times n$ matrix $A$, then

$$
\underset{{\large m} \times {\large n}}{A} = \underset{{\large m} \times {\large n}}{Q} \times \underset{{\large n} \times {\large n}}{R}
$$

where

- $Q$ is an $m \times n$ matrix whose columns form an orthonormal basis of $\text{Col}(A)$
- $R$ is an $n \times n$ upper triangular matrix

With that in mind, let's examine our statements.

- Statement I is false. $Q$ should be a $3\times 2$ matrix.
- Statement II is true. Indeed, the columns of $Q$ form an orthonormal basis of $\text{Col}(A)$.
- Statement III is true. Indeed, $R$ must be a $2 \times 2$ upper triangular matrix.

Therefore, the correct answer is "II and III only."

## Computing the Matrix R Given the Matrix Q in a QR Factorization

Given the matrix $Q$ in the QR factorization of an $m\times n$ matrix $A$, how do we find $R$?

Recall that $Q$ has orthonormal columns, so pre-multiplying both sides of $A=QR$ by $Q^T$, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q^{T} \cdot A &= Q^{T} \cdot QR \\
Q^{T}A &= Q^{T}QR
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, note that since the columns of $Q$ are orthonormal we have $Q^TQ = I_n$. Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q^{T}A &= (Q^{T}Q)R \\
Q^{T}A &= I_{n}R \\
R &= Q^{T}A
\end{aligned}
$$

So, we can find $R$ by pre-multiplying $A$ by the transpose of $Q$.

## Finding the Triangular Factor of a QR Factorization Given the Orthogonal Factor

**Example:** $\begin{bmatrix}0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \\ -1 & 2\end{bmatrix}$
Consider the matrices $A$ and $Q$ shown above. Find the matrix $R$ such that $A=QR$ is a QR factorization of $A$.

**Explanation**

Since $Q$ has orthonormal columns, pre-multiplying both sides of $A=QR$ by $Q^T$, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q^{T} \cdot A &= Q^{T} \cdot QR \\
Q^{T}A &= (Q^{T}Q)R \\
Q^{T}A &= I_{2}R \\
R &= Q^{T}A
\end{aligned}
$$

Note that $Q^TQ = I_2$ since the columns of $Q$ are orthonormal.

Now, we calculate the matrix $R$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
R &= Q^{T}A \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}0 & \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} & -\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \\ \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} & \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} & \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \\ -1 & 2\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}\sqrt{2} & -\sqrt{2} \\ 0 & \sqrt{3}\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Interpreting Relations Between Two Bases as Matrix Multiplication

Let's consider the QR factorization of the matrix $A$ shown below:

$$
[ \mid \mid \mid \mid \mid; a_{1} \mid a_{2} \mid a_{3}; \mid \mid \mid \mid \mid ]
$$

Suppose also that we are given the following system of equations that connects the columns of $Q$ and the columns of $A{:}$

$$
{q_{1} = \frac{1}{2}a_{1}; q_{2} = \frac{1}{3}a_{2} + 3q_{1}; q_{3} = \frac{1}{4}a_{3} - 2q_{1} + q_{2}
$$

How do we find $R$?
First, let's rewrite the vectors $\mathbf{a}_1, \mathbf{a}_2, \mathbf{a}_3$ as a linear combination of the vectors $\mathbf{q}_1, \mathbf{q}_2, \mathbf{q}_3$:

$$
{q_{1} = \frac{1}{2}a_{1}; q_{2} = \frac{1}{3}a_{2} + 3q_{1}; q_{3} = \frac{1}{4}a_{3} - 2q_{1} + q_{2}
$$

In this new system, the coefficients of $\mathbf{q}_1$ correspond to the first-row entries of $R$, the coefficients of $\mathbf{q}_2$ correspond to the second-row entries of $R$, and the coefficients of $\mathbf{q}_3$ correspond to the third-row entries of $R$. Therefore,

$$
[ \mid \mid \mid \mid \mid; a_{1} \mid a_{2} \mid a_{3}; \mid \mid \mid \mid \mid ]
$$

To see why this is, we can take the transpose of both sides of $A=QR{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= QR \\
A^{T} &= (QR)^{T} \\
A^{T} &= R^{T}Q^{T} \\
\begin{bmatrix}- & a_{1} & - \\ - & a_{2} & - \\ - & a_{3} & -\end{bmatrix}^(⏞)^(A^{T}) &= \begin{bmatrix}2 & 0 & 0 \\ -9 & 3 & 0 \\ 8 & -4 & 4\end{bmatrix}^(⏞)^(R^{T})\begin{bmatrix}- & q_{1} & - \\ - & q_{2} & - \\ - & q_{3} & -\end{bmatrix}^(⏞)^(Q^{T})
\end{aligned}
$$

If we were to expand this matrix product, we would obtain our system of equations.

## Finding a QR Factorization of a Matrix Given an Orthogonal Basis in Terms of the Columns of a Matrix

**Example:** $[ \mid \mid \mid \mid \mid; a_{1} \mid a_{2} \mid a_{3}; \mid \mid \mid \mid \mid ]$

Consider the QR factorization of the matrix $A$ shown above, where $\big\{\mathbf{v}_1,\mathbf{v}_2,\mathbf{v}_3 \big\}$ is an orthogonal set of vectors. You're given that $\Vert\mathbf{v}_1\Vert =2$, $\Vert\mathbf{v}_2\Vert =3$, $\Vert\mathbf{v}_3\Vert =4$, and that the system of equations below describes the relationship between the columns of $Q$ and the columns of $A$.
${v_{1} = a_{1}; v_{2} = a_{2} - v_{1}; v_{3} = a_{3} - 3v_{1} + 2v_{2}$.

What is the value of $r_{13}+r_{23}+r_{33}$?

**Explanation**

First, we normalize the vectors $\mathbf{v}_1,\mathbf{v}_2,\mathbf{v}_3$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
q_{1} &= \frac{v_{1}}{‖v_{1}‖} = \frac{v_{1}}{2}⟹v_{1} = 2q_{1} \\
q_{2} &= \frac{v_{2}}{‖v_{2}‖} = \frac{v_{2}}{3}⟹v_{2} = 3q_{2} \\
q_{3} &= \frac{v_{3}}{‖v_{3}‖} = \frac{v_{3}}{4}⟹v_{3} = 4q_{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, the set $\big\{\mathbf{q}_1,\mathbf{q}_2,\mathbf{q}_3 \big\}$ will be both orthogonal and normalized.

Next, let's rewrite the vectors $\mathbf{a}_1, \mathbf{a}_2, \mathbf{a}_3$ as a linear combination of the vectors $\mathbf{q}_1, \mathbf{q}_2, \mathbf{q}_3$:

$$
{v_{1} = a_{1}; v_{2} = a_{2} - v_{1}; v_{3} = a_{3} - 3v_{1} + 2v_{2}
$$

The final system can be written in matrix form as follows:
$[\mid \mid \mid \mid \mid; a_{1} \mid a_{2} \mid a_{3}; \mid \mid \mid \mid \mid]$

Therefore,

$$
r_{13}+r_{23}+r_{33} = 6+(-6)+4 = 4
$$

<a id="the-qr-factorization-algorithm"></a>
## The QR Factorization Algorithm

Let's now go through the QR factorization algorithm to find the factorization of the matrix

$$
\begin{bmatrix}2 & 1 \\ 4 & 3 \\ -4 & -1\end{bmatrix}
$$

**Step 1**. Orthogonalize the columns of $A$ to get the columns of $Q$. Using the Gram-Schmidt process, we obtain

$$
q_{1} = \frac{1}{3}\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 2 \\ -2\end{bmatrix}, q_{2} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{bmatrix}0 \\ 1 \\ 1\end{bmatrix}
$$

**Step 2**. Compute $R=Q^TA$. We get

$$
R = \begin{bmatrix}6 & 3 \\ 0 & \sqrt{2}\end{bmatrix}
$$

Therefore, the QR factorization of $A$ is

$$
\begin{bmatrix}2 & 1 \\ 4 & 3 \\ -4 & -1\end{bmatrix}
$$

## Finding a QR Factorization of a Matrix Given a Matrix With Orthogonal Columns

**Example:** $\begin{bmatrix}1 & -2 \\ 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 2\end{bmatrix}$

Consider the QR factorization of the matrix $A$ shown above. Given that the columns $\mathbf{a}_1,\mathbf{a}_2$ of $A$ are orthogonal, and that the columns $\mathbf{q}_1,\mathbf{q}_2$ of $Q$ were obtained by normalizing the columns of $A$, what is the value of $q_{12} \cdot r_{22}$?

**Explanation**

Since the columns of $A$ are already orthogonal, to construct a matrix $Q$, we simply normalize $\mathbf{a}_1$ and $\mathbf{a}_2\mathbf{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
q_{1} &= \frac{a_{1}}{‖a_{1}‖} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 0 \\ 1\end{bmatrix} \\
q_{2} &= \frac{a_{2}}{‖a_{2}‖} = \frac{1}{3}\begin{bmatrix}-2 \\ 1 \\ 2\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, pre-multiplying both sides of $A=QR$ by $Q^T$, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
R &= Q^{T}A \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} & 0 & \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \\ -\frac{2}{3} & \frac{1}{3} & \frac{2}{3}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1 & -2 \\ 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 2\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}\sqrt{2} & 0 \\ 0 & 3\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, we have the following QR factorization of $A$:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}1 & -2 \\ 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 2\end{bmatrix}
$$

Finally,

$$
q_{12} \cdot r_{22} = -\dfrac{2}{3} \cdot 3 = -2
$$

<a id="finding-the-qr-factorization-of-a-matrix"></a>
## Finding the QR Factorization of a Matrix

**Example:** $\begin{bmatrix}1 & 3 \\ 2 & 3 \\ 1 & 3\end{bmatrix}$

Consider the QR factorization of the matrix $A$ shown above. Given that the columns $\mathbf{q}_1,\mathbf{q}_2$ of $Q$ were obtained from the columns of $A$ using the Gram-Schmidt process, find the value of $q_{11} \cdot r_{12}$.

**Explanation**

Let $\mathbf{a}_1,\mathbf{a}_2$ be the columns of $A$.

First, we find an orthonormal basis of

$$
\textrm{Col}(A)=\textrm{Span}\{\mathbf{a}_1,\mathbf{a}_2\}
$$

using the Gram-Schmidt process.

**Step 1**. We set $\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 2 \\ 1\end{bmatrix}$

**Step 2**. We find the vector $\mathbf{v}_2$ orthogonal to $\text{Span}\{\mathbf{v}_1\}$ using the formula

$$
\begin{aligned}
v_{2} &= a_{2} - proj_{v_{1}}a_{2} \\
&= a_{2} - \frac{a_{2} \cdot v_{1}}{v_{1} \cdot v_{1}}v_{1} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}3 \\ 3 \\ 3\end{bmatrix} - \frac{12}{6}\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 2 \\ 1\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}3 \\ 3 \\ 3\end{bmatrix} - 2\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 2 \\ 1\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}1 \\ -1 \\ 1\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we normalize $\mathbf{v}_1$ and $\mathbf{v}_2$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
q_{1} &= \frac{v_{1}}{‖v_{1}‖} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 2 \\ 1\end{bmatrix} \\
q_{2} &= \frac{v_{2}}{‖v_{2}‖} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ -1 \\ 1\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we compute $R$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
R &= Q^{T}A \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}} & \frac{2}{\sqrt{6}} & \frac{1}{\sqrt{6}} \\ \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} & -\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} & \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1 & 3 \\ 2 & 3 \\ 1 & 3\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}\sqrt{6} & 2\sqrt{6} \\ 0 & \sqrt{3}\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, we obtain the following QR factorization of $A$:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}1 & 3 \\ 2 & 3 \\ 1 & 3\end{bmatrix}
$$

Finally,

$$
q_{11} \cdot r_{12} = \left(\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\right) \cdot \left({2\sqrt6}\right) = 2
$$
