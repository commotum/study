## Introduction

Suppose we want to find the shortest distance between the point $P(3,0,-2)$ and the plane $\Pi$ that is given by the equation

$$
(\mathbf{r}-\langle 2, 3, 1 \rangle) \cdot \langle 4, 4, 2 \rangle = 0
$$

First, note that

$$
\mathbf{n} = \langle 4, 4, 2 \rangle
$$

is a vector that's normal to the plane.

![](<../Source/1.3.8. The Shortest Distance Between a Plane and a Point/Images/29bc6fded08b250d82443fe75c3b5e30.png>)

Let's pick a point on the plane, for instance, $Q(2,3,1)$. Then

$$
\begin{aligned}
PQ^(→) &= ⟨2, 3, 1⟩ - ⟨3, 0,-2⟩ \\
&= ⟨ - 1, 3, 3⟩
\end{aligned}
$$

The shortest distance $d$ from the point $P$ to the plane $\Pi$ is the absolute value of the scalar projection of $\overrightarrow{PQ}$ onto the normal vector of the plane, as shown below.

![](<../Source/1.3.8. The Shortest Distance Between a Plane and a Point/Images/d1672d9ff9800e86987f2f2f955f82d5.png>)

Therefore, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
d &= \mid comp_{n}PQ^(→) \mid \\
&= (\begin{vmatrix}PQ^(→) \cdot n &)/(\mid n\end{vmatrix}) \\
&= (\mid (-1) \cdot 4 + 3 \cdot 4 + 3 \cdot 2\begin{vmatrix})/(\sqrt{4^{2} + 4^{2} + 2^{2}}) \\ = \frac{\mid - 4 + 12 + 6 \mid}{\sqrt{16 + 16 + 4}} \\ = \frac{\mid 14\end{vmatrix}}{\sqrt{36}} \\
&= \frac{7}{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

We conclude that the shortest distance from the point $P$ to the plane $\Pi$ is

$$
\dfrac{7}{3}
$$

## Finding the Shortest Distance Between a Point and a Plane Given in Dot Product Form

**Example:** Find the shortest distance between the point $P(5,2,-2)$ and the plane $\Pi$ with equation $\begin{bmatrix}4 \\ 8 \\ 2\end{bmatrix}$

**Explanation**

Let's pick a point on the plane, for instance, $Q(4,8,2)$. Then

$$
\begin{aligned}
PQ^(→) &= \begin{bmatrix}4 \\ 8 \\ 2\end{bmatrix} - \begin{bmatrix}5 \\ 2 \\ -2\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}-1 \\ 6 \\ 4\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Also, from the equation of the plane, we get a normal vector $\begin{bmatrix}2 \\ 4 \\ -4\end{bmatrix}$

Finally, the shortest distance from the point $P$ to the plane $\Pi$ is the absolute value of the scalar projection of $\overrightarrow{PQ}$ onto the normal vector of the plane:

$$
\begin{aligned}
d &= \mid comp_{n}PQ^(→) \mid \\
&= (\begin{vmatrix}PQ^(→) \cdot n &)/(\mid n\end{vmatrix}) \\
&= (\mid (-1) \cdot 2 + 6 \cdot 4 + 4 \cdot (-4)\begin{vmatrix})/(\sqrt{2^{2} + 4^{2} + (-4)^{2}}) \\ = \frac{\mid - 2 + 24 - 16 \mid}{\sqrt{4 + 16 + 16}} \\ = \frac{\mid 6\end{vmatrix}}{\sqrt{36}} \\
&= 1
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding the Shortest Distance Between a Point and a Plane Given in Simplified Dot Product Form

**Example:** Given that $Q(1,-2,-1)$ lies on the plane $\Pi:\mathbf{r} \cdot \langle 2, 0, -2 \rangle = 4$, find the shortest distance from $P(0, -1, 2)$ to $\Pi$.

**Explanation**

First, we find

$$
\begin{aligned}
PQ^(→) &= ⟨1,-2,-1⟩ - ⟨0,-1, 2⟩ \\
&= ⟨1,-1,-3⟩
\end{aligned}
$$

From the equation of a plane, we get a normal vector

$$
\mathbf{n}= \langle 2, 0, -2 \rangle
$$

Finally, the shortest distance from the point $P$ to the plane $\Pi$ is the absolute value of the scalar projection of $\overrightarrow{PQ}$ onto the normal vector of the plane:

$$
\begin{aligned}
d &= \mid comp_{n}PQ^(→) \mid \\
&= (\begin{vmatrix}PQ^(→) \cdot n &)/(\mid n\end{vmatrix}) \\
&= (\mid 1 \cdot 2 + (-1) \cdot 0 + (-3) \cdot (-2)\begin{vmatrix})/(\sqrt{2^{2} + 0^{2} + (-2)^{2}}) \\ = \frac{\mid 2 + 0 + 6 \mid}{\sqrt{4 + 0 + 4}} \\ = \frac{\mid 8\end{vmatrix}}{\sqrt{8}} \\
&= \sqrt{8} \\
&= 2\sqrt{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding the Shortest Distance Between a Point and a Plane Given in Cartesian Form

**Example:** Find the shortest distance between the point $P(3, -10, 3)$ and the plane $\Pi$ with equation $2x+y-2z = -1$.

**Explanation**

Let's start by picking a point on the plane. To do that, we simply substitute some concrete values for $x$ and $y$ into the equation and solve for $z$. Let's choose $x=4$ and $y=-5$ (though it doesn't really matter what values we choose). Solving for $z$, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
2x + y - 2z &= -1 \\
2(4) + (-5) + 1 &= 2z \\
4 &= 2z \\
z &= 2
\end{aligned}
$$

So, we get the point $Q(4, -5, 2)$ which lies on the plane.

Computing $\overrightarrow{PQ}$, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
PQ^(→) &= ⟨4,-5, 2⟩ - ⟨3,-10, 3⟩ \\
&= ⟨1, 5,-1⟩
\end{aligned}
$$

We also need a normal vector to the plane. For any Cartesian equation of a plane

$$
ax+by+cz=d
$$

the normal vector is

$$
\mathbf{n}=\langle a,b,c \rangle
$$

So, for the plane $\Pi$, we have

$$
\mathbf{n}= \langle 2, 1, -2 \rangle
$$

Finally, the shortest distance from the point $P$ to the plane $\Pi$ is the absolute value of the scalar projection of $\overrightarrow{PQ}$ onto the normal vector of the plane:

$$
\begin{aligned}
d &= \mid comp_{n}PQ^(→) \mid \\
&= (\begin{vmatrix}PQ^(→) \cdot n &)/(\mid n\end{vmatrix}) \\
&= (\mid 1 \cdot 2 + 5 \cdot 1 + (-1) \cdot (-2)\begin{vmatrix})/(\sqrt{2^{2} + 1^{2} + (-2)^{2}}) \\ = \frac{\mid 2 + 5 + 2 \mid}{\sqrt{4 + 1 + 4}} \\ = \frac{\mid 9\end{vmatrix}}{\sqrt{9}} \\
&= 3
\end{aligned}
$$
