## Introduction

We've seen how to calculate the total derivative of a function $f: \mathbb R^n \to \mathbb R$. In this lesson, we will learn how to calculate its second derivative.

For a function $f: \mathbb R^n \to \mathbb R$, given by

$$
\begin{bmatrix}x_{1} \\ x_{2} \\ ⋮ \\ x_{n}\end{bmatrix}
$$

the total derivative of $f$ is given by the matrix

$$
\begin{bmatrix}\frac{∂f}{∂x_{1}} & \frac{∂f}{∂x_{2}} & ⋯ & \frac{∂f}{∂x_{n}}\end{bmatrix}
$$

To differentiate $f$ once more, we first need to take the transpose of the first derivative:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}\frac{∂f}{∂x_{1}} \\ \frac{∂f}{∂x_{2}} \\ ⋮ \\ \frac{∂f}{∂x_{n}}\end{bmatrix}
$$

To calculate the **second derivative** of $f$, we simply apply the definition of the total derivative to

$$
\left(\boldsymbol f'(\mathbf x)\right)^T
$$

By doing this, we get the following $n\times n$ matrix:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}\frac{∂^{2}f}{∂x_{1}^{2}} & \frac{∂^{2}f}{∂x_{1}∂x_{2}} & ⋯ & \frac{∂^{2}f}{∂x_{1}∂x_{n}} \\ \frac{∂^{2}f}{∂x_{2}∂x_{1}} & \frac{∂^{2}f}{∂x_{2}^{2}} & ⋯ & \frac{∂^{2}f}{∂x_{2}∂x_{n}} \\ ⋮ & ⋮ & ⋮ & ⋮ \\ \frac{∂^{2}f}{∂x_{n}∂x_{1}} & \frac{∂^{2}f}{∂x_{n}∂x_{2}} & ⋯ & \frac{∂^{2}f}{∂x_{n}^{2}}\end{bmatrix}
$$

Notice that this matrix is *symmetric* due to the equality of mixed partial derivatives. This becomes important later.

The matrix $\boldsymbol{f}''(\mathbf x)$ is sometimes called the **Hessian matrix,** and its determinant,

$$
H(\mathbf x) = \det\big(\boldsymbol{f}''(\mathbf x) \big)
$$

is called the **Hessian determinant**.

Note that in the case of a two-variable function $f(x,y)$, the second derivative is given by

$$
\begin{bmatrix}\frac{∂^{2}f}{∂x^{2}} & \frac{∂^{2}f}{∂x∂y} \\ \frac{∂^{2}f}{∂y∂x} & \frac{∂^{2}f}{∂y^{2}}\end{bmatrix}
$$

In this lesson, we'll primarily focus on two-variable functions $f(x,y)$. However, we should remember that the techniques discussed here can be applied to scalar functions with an arbitrary number of variables.

## Finding the Second Derivative of a Function Given Its First Derivative

**Example:** Find $\boldsymbol f''(\pi,0)$ for the function $f(x,y)$ given that
$\begin{bmatrix}e^{y}\cos x & e^{y}\sin x\end{bmatrix}$

**Explanation**

For a function $f: \mathbb R^n \to \mathbb R$, given by
$\begin{bmatrix}x_{1} \\ x_{2} \\ ⋮ \\ x_{n}\end{bmatrix}$

the second derivative of $f$ is given by the $n\times n$ matrix

$$
\begin{bmatrix}\frac{∂^{2}f}{∂x_{1}^{2}} & \frac{∂^{2}f}{∂x_{1}∂x_{2}} & ⋯ & \frac{∂^{2}f}{∂x_{1}∂x_{n}} \\ \frac{∂^{2}f}{∂x_{2}∂x_{1}} & \frac{∂^{2}f}{∂x_{2}^{2}} & ⋯ & \frac{∂^{2}f}{∂x_{2}∂x_{n}} \\ ⋮ & ⋮ & ⋮ & ⋮ \\ \frac{∂^{2}f}{∂x_{n}∂x_{1}} & \frac{∂^{2}f}{∂x_{n}∂x_{2}} & ⋯ & \frac{∂^{2}f}{∂x_{n}^{2}}\end{bmatrix}
$$

In our case, we have

$$
\begin{bmatrix}\frac{∂^{2}f}{∂x^{2}} & \frac{∂^{2}f}{∂x∂y} \\ \frac{∂^{2}f}{∂y∂x} & \frac{∂^{2}f}{∂y^{2}}\end{bmatrix}
$$

First, notice that since $\begin{bmatrix}e^{y}\cos x & e^{y}\sin x\end{bmatrix}$
we must have

$$
\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial x} = e^y\cos x, \qquad \dfrac{\partial f}{\partial y} =e^y\sin x
$$

Now, we compute the second partial derivatives:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\begin{bmatrix}\frac{∂^{2}f}{∂x^{2}} &= \frac{∂}{∂x}(e^{y}\cos x) =-e^{y}\sin x & \frac{∂^{2}f}{∂x∂y}|= \frac{∂}{∂x}(e^{y}\sin x) = e^{y}\cos x \\ \frac{∂^{2}f}{∂y∂x}|= \frac{∂}{∂y}(e^{y}\cos x) = e^{y}\cos x & \frac{∂^{2}f}{∂y^{2}}|= \frac{∂}{∂y}(e^{y}\sin x) = e^{y}\sin x\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the second derivative is

$$
\begin{aligned}
f^{″}(x, y) &= \begin{bmatrix}-e^{y}\sin x & e^{y}\cos x \\ e^{y}\cos x & e^{y}\sin x\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we evaluate this matrix at $(\pi,0)$. This gives

$$
\begin{bmatrix}0 & -1 \\ -1 & 0\end{bmatrix}
$$

## Finding the Second Derivative of a Function

**Example:** Find $\boldsymbol f''(1,0)$ for the function $f(x,y) = x^2 e^y$.

**Explanation**

For a function $f: \mathbb R^n \to \mathbb R$, given by
$\begin{bmatrix}x_{1} \\ x_{2} \\ ⋮ \\ x_{n}\end{bmatrix}$

the second derivative of $f$ is given by the $n\times n$ matrix

$$
\begin{bmatrix}\frac{∂^{2}f}{∂x_{1}^{2}} & \frac{∂^{2}f}{∂x_{1}∂x_{2}} & ⋯ & \frac{∂^{2}f}{∂x_{1}∂x_{n}} \\ \frac{∂^{2}f}{∂x_{2}∂x_{1}} & \frac{∂^{2}f}{∂x_{2}^{2}} & ⋯ & \frac{∂^{2}f}{∂x_{2}∂x_{n}} \\ ⋮ & ⋮ & ⋮ & ⋮ \\ \frac{∂^{2}f}{∂x_{n}∂x_{1}} & \frac{∂^{2}f}{∂x_{n}∂x_{2}} & ⋯ & \frac{∂^{2}f}{∂x_{n}^{2}}\end{bmatrix}
$$

In our case, we have

$$
\begin{bmatrix}\frac{∂^{2}f}{∂x^{2}} & \frac{∂^{2}f}{∂x∂y} \\ \frac{∂^{2}f}{∂y∂x} & \frac{∂^{2}f}{∂y^{2}}\end{bmatrix}
$$

First, we compute the first derivative of $f(x,y)$, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
f^{′}(x, y) &= \begin{bmatrix}\frac{∂f}{∂x} & \frac{∂f}{∂y}\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}\frac{∂}{∂x}(x^{2}e^{y}) & \frac{∂}{∂y}(x^{2}e^{y})\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}2xe^{y} & x^{2}e^{y}\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we compute the second partial derivatives:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\begin{bmatrix}\frac{∂^{2}f}{∂x^{2}} &= \frac{∂}{∂x}(2xe^{y}) = 2e^{y} & \frac{∂^{2}f}{∂x∂y}|= \frac{∂}{∂x}(x^{2}e^{y}) = 2xe^{y} \\ \frac{∂^{2}f}{∂y∂x}|= \frac{∂}{∂y}(2xe^{y}) = 2xe^{y} & \frac{∂^{2}f}{∂y^{2}}|= \frac{∂}{∂y}(x^{2}e^{y}) = x^{2}e^{y}\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the second derivative is

$$
\begin{aligned}
f^{″}(x, y) &= \begin{bmatrix}2e^{y} & 2xe^{y} \\ 2xe^{y} & x^{2}e^{y}\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we evaluate this matrix at

$$
\left(1,0\right)
$$

This gives

$$
f^{″}(1, 0) = \begin{bmatrix}2 & 2 \\ 2 & 1\end{bmatrix}
$$

## Evaluating a Hessian Determinant

**Example:** Given that
$\begin{bmatrix}\ln y & \frac{x}{y}\end{bmatrix}$
evaluate the Hessian determinant of $f(x,y)$ at the point $(0,1)$.

**Explanation**

For a function $f: \mathbb R^n \to \mathbb R$, given by
$\begin{bmatrix}x_{1} \\ x_{2} \\ ⋮ \\ x_{n}\end{bmatrix}$

the second derivative of $f$ is given by the $n\times n$ matrix

$$
\begin{bmatrix}\frac{∂^{2}f}{∂x_{1}^{2}} & \frac{∂^{2}f}{∂x_{1}∂x_{2}} & ⋯ & \frac{∂^{2}f}{∂x_{1}∂x_{n}} \\ \frac{∂^{2}f}{∂x_{2}∂x_{1}} & \frac{∂^{2}f}{∂x_{2}^{2}} & ⋯ & \frac{∂^{2}f}{∂x_{2}∂x_{n}} \\ ⋮ & ⋮ & ⋮ & ⋮ \\ \frac{∂^{2}f}{∂x_{n}∂x_{1}} & \frac{∂^{2}f}{∂x_{n}∂x_{2}} & ⋯ & \frac{∂^{2}f}{∂x_{n}^{2}}\end{bmatrix}
$$

The Hessian determinant $H(x,y)$ is the determinant of the second derivative:

$$
H(\mathbf x) = \det \left(\boldsymbol{f}''(\mathbf x) \right)
$$

In our case, we have

$$
\begin{bmatrix}\frac{∂^{2}f}{∂x^{2}} & \frac{∂^{2}f}{∂x∂y} \\ \frac{∂^{2}f}{∂y∂x} & \frac{∂^{2}f}{∂y^{2}}\end{bmatrix}
$$

First, notice that since

$$
\begin{bmatrix}\ln y & \frac{x}{y}\end{bmatrix}
$$

we must have

$$
\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial x} = \ln y, \qquad \dfrac{\partial f}{\partial y} = \dfrac xy
$$

Now, we compute the second partial derivatives:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\begin{bmatrix}\frac{∂^{2}f}{∂x^{2}} &= \frac{∂}{∂x}(\ln y) = 0 & \frac{∂^{2}f}{∂x∂y}|= \frac{∂}{∂x}(\frac{x}{y}) = \frac{1}{y} \\ \frac{∂^{2}f}{∂y∂x}|= \frac{∂}{∂y}(\ln y) = \frac{1}{y} & \frac{∂^{2}f}{∂y^{2}}|= \frac{∂}{∂y}(\frac{x}{y}) =-\frac{x}{y^{2}}\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the second derivative is

$$
\begin{aligned}
f^{″}(x, y) &= \begin{bmatrix}\frac{∂^{2}f}{∂x^{2}} & \frac{∂^{2}f}{∂x∂y} \\ \frac{∂^{2}f}{∂y∂x} & \frac{∂^{2}f}{∂y^{2}}\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}0 & \frac{1}{y} \\ \frac{1}{y} & -\frac{x}{y^{2}}\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Evaluating the second derivative at $(0,1)$, we obtain

$$
f^{″}(0, 1) = \begin{bmatrix}0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0\end{bmatrix}
$$

Finally, we have

$$
H(0,1) = 0 \cdot 0 - 1\cdot 1 = -1
$$
