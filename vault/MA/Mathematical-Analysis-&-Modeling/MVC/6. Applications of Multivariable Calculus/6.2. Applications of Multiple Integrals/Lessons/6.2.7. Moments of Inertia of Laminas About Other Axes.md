## Introduction

Recall that the moments of inertia $I_x$ and $I_y$ of a thin plate $D$ about the $x$- and $y$- axes are given by

$$
I_x = \iint\limits_D y^2\,\lambda(x,y) \:\textrm dA \qquad\textrm{and}\qquad I_y = \iint\limits_D x^2\,\lambda(x,y) \:\textrm dA
$$

where the factors $y^2$ and $x^2$ represent the squared distances from a point on the plate to the $x$- and $y$-axes, respectively.

In general, the **moment of inertia about an axis $l$** of a plane lamina occupying the region $D$ with mass density function $\lambda(x,y)$ is given by

$$
I_l = \iint\limits_D \lambda(x,y) [r(x,y)]^2 \:\textrm{d}A
$$

where $r(x,y)$ is the distance from the point $(x,y)$ to the axis $l$.

For example, consider the thin plate that occupies the region

$$
D = \big\{(x,y) \:: \: -1 \leq x \leq 1, \:\: 0 \leq y \leq 1 \big\}
$$

with mass density function

$$
\lambda(x,y) = 3x+6
$$

Let's find its moment of inertia about the line $y=2$.

![](<../Source/6.2.7. Moments of Inertia of Laminas About Other Axes/Images/4f56315b9a4a6890d8474b3e10ea41b5.png>)

The distance between a point $(x,y)$ on $D$ and the line $y=2$ is given by

$$
r(x,y) = \mid 2 - y \mid
$$

Therefore, the moment of inertia about the line $y=2$ is

$$
\begin{aligned}
I_{l} &= ∬_(D)λ(x, y)[r(x, y)]^{2}dA \\
&= ∫_{0}^{1}∫_{-1}^{1}(3x + 6) \mid 2 - y \mid ^{2}dxdy \\
&= ∫_{0}^{1}∫_{-1}^{1}3(x + 2)(y - 2)^{2}dxdy \\
&= ∫_{-1}^{1}(x + 2)dx \cdot ∫_{0}^{1}3(y - 2)^{2}dy \\
&= [((x + 2)^{2})/(2)]_{-1}^{1} \cdot [(y - 2)^{3}]_{0}^{1} \\
&= (\frac{9}{2} - \frac{1}{2}) \cdot (-1 + 8) \\
&= 4 \cdot 7 \\
&= 28
\end{aligned}
$$

## Calculating the Moment of Inertia of a Lamina Given a Mass Density Function

**Example:** A plate occupies the region $D$ enclosed by the $y$-axis and the lines $x=\dfrac{y}2$ and $x=y-2$, as shown above. The mass density function of the plate is $\lambda(x,y) =\dfrac y8$. Find the moment of inertia of the plate about the line $x=2$.

![](<../Source/6.2.7. Moments of Inertia of Laminas About Other Axes/Images/11eed00af0f2d1ea5c3dd9df00a40291.png>)

**Explanation**

The moment of inertia of a plane lamina in the shape of a region $D$ about an axis of rotation $l$ is given by

$$
I_l = \iint\limits_D \lambda (x,y) [r(x,y)]^2 \:\textrm{d}A
$$

where $\lambda(x,y)$ is the mass density function of the lamina, and $r(x,y)$ is the distance from the line $l$ to the point $(x,y)$ on $D$.

First, notice that $D$ is a type I region with representation

$$
D = \left\{(x,y) \:: \: 0 \leq x \leq 2, \:\: 2x \leq y \leq x+2 \right\}
$$

as shown below.

![](<../Source/6.2.7. Moments of Inertia of Laminas About Other Axes/Images/3888077dfe4169493752346eb2247621.png>)

The distance between a point on $D$ and the line $x=2$ is given by

$$
r(x,y) = \mid x - 2 \mid
$$

Therefore, the moment of inertia about the line $x=2$ is

$$
\begin{aligned}
I &= ∬_(D)λ(x, y)[r(x, y)]^{2}dA \\
&= ∫_{0}^{2}∫_{2x}^{x + 2}(\frac{y}{8}) \mid x - 2 \mid ^{2}dydx \\
&= ∫_{0}^{2}∫_{2x}^{x + 2}(\frac{y}{8})(x - 2)^{2}dydx \\
&= ∫_{0}^{2}(x - 2)^{2}∫_{2x}^{x + 2}\frac{y}{8}dydx \\
&= ∫_{0}^{2}(x - 2)^{2}[\frac{y^{2}}{16}]_{2x}^{x + 2}dx \\
&= \frac{1}{16}∫_{0}^{2}(x - 2)^{2}((x + 2)^{2} - (2x)^{2})dx \\
&= \frac{1}{16}∫_{0}^{2}(x^{2} - 4x + 4)(4 + 4x - 3x^{2})dx \\
&= \frac{1}{16}∫_{0}^{2}(16 - 24x^{2} + 16x^{3} - 3x^{4})dx \\
&= \frac{1}{16}[16x - 8x^{3} + 4x^{4} - \frac{3x^{5}}{5}]_{0}^{2} \\
&= \frac{1}{16}(32 - 64 + 64 - \frac{96}{5}) - 0 \\
&= \frac{1}{16}(\frac{64}{5}) \\
&= \frac{4}{5}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Calculating the Moment of Inertia of a Lamina With Uniform Mass Density

**Example:** A plate with mass $M$ and uniform mass density occupies the region $D$ enclosed between the curves $y=x$ and $y=4x-x^2$, as shown above. Given that the region has an area of $\dfrac92$ square units, find the moment of inertia of the plate about the line $x=3$.

![](<../Source/6.2.7. Moments of Inertia of Laminas About Other Axes/Images/b0b9a3962cb0c3c1dde403ebea8607ff.png>)

**Explanation**

The moment of inertia of a plane lamina in the shape of a region $D$ about an axis of rotation $l$ is given by

$$
I_l = \iint\limits_D \lambda (x,y) [r(x,y)]^2 \:\textrm{d}A
$$

where $\lambda(x,y)$ is the mass density function of the lamina, and $r(x,y)$ is the distance from the line $l$ to the point $(x,y)$ on $D$.

First, notice that $D$ is a type I region with representation

$$
D = \left\{(x,y) \:: \: 0 \leq x \leq 3, \:\: x \leq y \leq 4x-x^2 \right\}
$$

as shown below.

![](<../Source/6.2.7. Moments of Inertia of Laminas About Other Axes/Images/03b423d9e4de7171ae11eedbfe4af11d.png>)

The distance between a point on $D$ and the line $x=3$ is given by

$$
r(x,y) = \mid x - 3 \mid
$$

Since the plate has uniform density, the mass density function is given by

$$
\lambda(x,y) = \dfrac{M}{\mathcal{A}} = \dfrac{2M}{9}
$$

Therefore, the moment of inertia about the line $x=3$ is

$$
\begin{aligned}
I &= ∬_(D)λ(x, y)[r(x, y)]^{2}dA \\
&= \frac{2M}{9}∫_{0}^{3}∫_{x}^{4x - x^{2}} \mid x - 3 \mid ^{2}dydx \\
&= \frac{2M}{9}∫_{0}^{3}∫_{x}^{4x - x^{2}}(x - 3)^{2}dydx \\
&= \frac{2M}{9}∫_{0}^{3}(x - 3)^{2}[y]_{y = x}^{y = 4x - x^{2}}dx \\
&= \frac{2M}{9}∫_{0}^{3}(x^{2} - 6x + 9)(3x - x^{2})dx \\
&= \frac{2M}{9}∫_{0}^{3} - x^{4} + 9x^{3} - 27x^{2} + 27xdx \\
&= \frac{2M}{9}[- \frac{x^{5}}{5} + \frac{9x^{4}}{4} - 9x^{3} + \frac{27x^{2}}{2}]_{0}^{3} \\
&= \frac{2M}{3^{2}}(-\frac{3^{5}}{5} + \frac{3^{6}}{4} - 3^{5} + \frac{3^{5}}{2}) \\
&= 2 \cdot 3^{3}M(-\frac{1}{5} + \frac{3}{4} - 1 + \frac{1}{2}) \\
&= \frac{27M}{10}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Calculating the Moment of Inertia Given a Description of a Mass Density Function

**Example:** A plate with mass $M$ occupies the region $D$ in the first quadrant enclosed between the curve $y=\sqrt x$ and the line $x=1$, as shown above.
The mass density at each point is proportional to its distance from the $x$-axis. Find the moment of inertia of the plate about the line $x=-1$.

![](<../Source/6.2.7. Moments of Inertia of Laminas About Other Axes/Images/edc9cb439dea888f2ce37f4d8ad17b5c.png>)

**Explanation**

The moment of inertia of a plane lamina in the shape of a region $D$ about an axis of rotation $l$ is given by

$$
I_l = \iint\limits_D \lambda (x,y) [r(x,y)]^2 \:\textrm{d}A
$$

where $\lambda(x,y)$ is the mass density function of the lamina, and $r(x,y)$ is the distance from the line $l$ to the point $(x,y)$ on $D$.

First, notice that $D$ is a type I region with representation

$$
D = \left\{(x,y) \:: \: 0 \leq x \leq 1, \:\: 0 \leq y \leq \sqrt x \right\}
$$

as shown below.

![](<../Source/6.2.7. Moments of Inertia of Laminas About Other Axes/Images/37d6c9a2f0b721457fa666b86f4e9c8d.png>)

Also, the distance between a point and the line $x=-1$ is given by

$$
r(x,y) = \begin{vmatrix}x-(-1) & = & x+1\end{vmatrix}
$$

Next, the mass density can be written as

$$
\lambda(x,y) = k \mid y \mid = ky
$$

where $k$ is a constant of proportionality. We can find the value of $k$ by integrating the mass density function over the plate.

$$
\begin{aligned}
M &= ∬_(D)λ(x, y)dA \\
&= ∫_{0}^{1}∫_{0}^{\sqrt{x}}kydydx \\
&= k∫_{0}^{1}[\frac{1}{2}y^{2}]_{0}^{\sqrt{x}}dx \\
&= \frac{k}{2}∫_{0}^{1}(x - 0)dx \\
&= \frac{k}{2}∫_{0}^{1}xdx \\
&= \frac{k}{2} \cdot [\frac{1}{2}x^{2}]_{0}^{1} \\
&= \frac{k}{4}
\end{aligned}
$$

Hence,

$$
\lambda(x,y) = k y = 4M y
$$

Therefore, the moment of inertia about the line $x=-1$ is

$$
\begin{aligned}
I_{l} &= ∬_(D)λ(x, y)[r(x, y)]^{2}dA \\
&= ∫_{0}^{1}∫_{0}^{\sqrt{x}}4My \mid x + 1 \mid ^{2}dydx \\
&= ∫_{0}^{1}∫_{0}^{\sqrt{x}}4My(x + 1)^{2}dydx \\
&= 4M∫_{0}^{1}(x + 1)^{2}∫_{0}^{\sqrt{x}}ydydx \\
&= 4M∫_{0}^{1}(x + 1)^{2}[\frac{1}{2}y^{2}]_{0}^{\sqrt{x}}dx \\
&= 2M∫_{0}^{1}(x + 1)^{2}(x - 0)dx \\
&= 2M∫_{0}^{1}(x^{3} + 2x^{2} + x)dx \\
&= 2M[\frac{1}{4}x^{4} + \frac{2}{3}x^{3} + \frac{1}{2}x^{2}]_{0}^{1} \\
&= 2M(\frac{1}{4} + \frac{2}{3} + \frac{1}{2}) \\
&= 2M \cdot \frac{17}{12} \\
&= \frac{17M}{6}
\end{aligned}
$$
