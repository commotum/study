## Introduction

Recall that the **probability density function** (or **pdf**) of a continuous random variable $X$ defined over a set $S$ is a function $f(x)$ that satisfies the following conditions:

- $f(x) \geq 0$ for all $x$ in $S$
- $\displaystyle \int_{S} f(x) \, \textrm dx = 1$

The probability that $X$ lies in the interval $[a,b]$ is given by

$$
P(a \leq X \leq b) = \int_a^b f(x) \, \textrm dx
$$

This probability can be interpreted as the area bounded between $f(x)$ and the $x$-axis over the interval $x\in [a,b]$, as shown below.

![](<../Source/6.3.2. Calculating Probabilities With Continuous Random Variables/Images/a9519601d1f8ace1a5ba4ecf75b73e30.png>)

Also, remember that for continuous random variables, we get the same answer whether or not we include the endpoints of an interval $[a,b]$. In other words,

$$
P(a \leq X \leq b) = P(a < X < b) = P(a < X \leq b) = P(a \leq X < b)
$$

In this lesson, we'll get some practice at computing probabilities with continuous random variables.

## Computing the Probability That a Random Variable Lies Within a Bounded Interval

**Example:** Compute $P\left(1 \lt X \lt 3 \right)$ given that the random variable $X$ has the probability density function

${\frac{1}{18}(x^{2} - 1),\begin{vmatrix}1 \le x \le 4, \\ 0,\end{vmatrix}otherwise$.

**Explanation**

If a continuous random variable $X$ has the probability density function $f(x)$, then

$$
P(a \lt X \lt b) = \int_a^b f(x) \, \textrm dx
$$

So, in our case, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(1 < X < 3) &= ∫_{1}^{3}f(x)dx \\
&= ∫_{1}^{3}\frac{1}{18}(x^{2} - 1)dx \\
&= \frac{1}{18}∫_{1}^{3}(x^{2} - 1)dx \\
&= \frac{1}{18}[\frac{1}{3}x^{3} - x]_{1}^{3} \\
&= \frac{1}{18}([\frac{1}{3}(3)^{3} - (3)] - [\frac{1}{3}(1)^{3} - (1)]) \\
&= \frac{1}{18}([9 - 3] - [\frac{1}{3} - 1]) \\
&= \frac{1}{18}(6 + \frac{2}{3}) \\
&= \frac{1}{18} \cdot \frac{20}{3} \\
&= \frac{10}{27}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Computing the Probability That a Random Variable Lies Within an Unbounded Interval

**Example:** Compute $P\left(X \leq 3 \right)$ given that the random variable $X$ has the probability density function

${\frac{2x}{45},\begin{vmatrix}2 \le x \le 7, \\ 0,\end{vmatrix}otherwise$.

**Explanation**

If a continuous random variable $X$ has the probability density function $f(x)$, then

$$
P(a \leq X \leq b) = \int_a^b f(x) \, \textrm dx
$$

Since $f(x)$ is identical to zero for all $x \lt 2$, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(X \le 3) &= ∫_{2}^{3}f(x)dx \\
&= ∫_{2}^{3}\frac{2x}{45}dx \\
&= [\frac{1}{45}x^{2}]_{2}^{3} \\
&= \frac{1}{45}(3^{2} - 2^{2}) \\
&= \frac{1}{45} \cdot 5 \\
&= \frac{1}{9}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Calculating Probabilities With Piecewise PDFs

**Example:** Compute $P\left(2 \leq X \leq 4 \right)$ given that the random variable $X$ has the probability density function

${\frac{1}{9}x,\begin{vmatrix}0 \le x < 3, \\ x - 3,\end{vmatrix}3 \le x \le 4,; 0, \mid otherwise$.

**Explanation**

If a continuous random variable $X$ has the probability density function $f(x)$, then

$$
P(a \leq X \leq b) = \int_a^b f(x) \, \textrm dx
$$

So, in our case, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(2 \le X \le 4) &= ∫_{2}^{4}f(x)dx \\
&= ∫_{2}^{3}\frac{1}{9}xdx + ∫_{3}^{4}(x - 3)dx \\
&= [\frac{1}{18}x^{2}]_{2}^{3} + [\frac{1}{2}x^{2} - 3x]_{3}^{4} \\
&= \frac{1}{18}[3^{2} - 2^{2}] + ([\frac{1}{2}(4)^{2} - 3(4)] - [\frac{1}{2}(3)^{2} - 3(3)]) \\
&= \frac{5}{18} - 4 + \frac{9}{2} \\
&= \frac{14}{18} \\
&= \frac{7}{9}
\end{aligned}
$$
