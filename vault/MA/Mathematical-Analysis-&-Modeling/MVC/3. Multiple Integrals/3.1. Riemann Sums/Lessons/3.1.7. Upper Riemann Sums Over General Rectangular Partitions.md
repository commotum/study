## Introduction

We know how to write the upper Riemann sum for a function $f(x,y)$ over a *fixed* rectangular partition. In this lesson, we will learn how to write down an expression for the upper Riemann sum for an *arbitrary* rectangular partition.

As a concrete example, let

$$
R = [0,1] \times [0,1]
$$

be a region in the $xy$-plane, and define the function $f(x,y)$ as

$$
f(x,y) = x^2+y^2
$$

Further, let $P$ be an arbitrary partition of $R$, where

$$
P_{1} = {x_{0}, x_{1}, …, x_{m}}, P_{2} = {y_{0}, y_{1}, …, y_{n}}, P = P_{1} \cdot P_{2}
$$

Our goal is to find an expression for $U(f, P)$, the upper Riemann sum of $f$ with respect to this partition. We proceed identically to the case where $P$ was fixed.

First, we consider an arbitrary subregion $R_{ij}$ of the partition. This subregion lies between $x_{i-1}$ and $x_i$ over the $x$-axis and $y_{j-1}$ and $y_j$ over the $y$-axis, as shown below.

![](<../Source/3.1.7. Upper Riemann Sums Over General Rectangular Partitions/Images/bcc8c0d0004dbbabc4ae353e02c3a507.png>)

Since we're computing the *upper* sum, we're interested in the *maximum* value of $f$ in this subregion.

Notice that

- $f(x,y) = x^2+y^2$ increases as $x$ increases on $[0,1]$, and
- $f(x,y) = x^2+y^2$ increases as $y$ increases on $[0,1]$.

![](<../Source/3.1.7. Upper Riemann Sums Over General Rectangular Partitions/Images/c842d1f4c2ce497b8c14e6bca7f487a8.png>)

Therefore, the *maximum* value of $f$ in the subregion is attained when we have the *largest* possible $x$ and the *largest* possible $y$. This is the value of $f$ at the *top right corner* of the subregion.

![](<../Source/3.1.7. Upper Riemann Sums Over General Rectangular Partitions/Images/d71a926c0d30af2c8007e0f129781a00.png>)

So, if $M_{ij}$ denotes the maximum value of $f$ in each subregion, we have

$$
M_{ij} = f(x_{i}, y_{j}) = x_{i}^2 + y_{j}^2
$$

for all

$$
1\leq i\leq m
$$

and

$$
1\leq j\leq n
$$

Since the area of $R_{ij}$ is $\Delta x_i\Delta y_j$, the volume of the rectangular solid with base $R_{ij}$ and height $M_{ij}$ is

$$
M_{ij}\Delta x_i\Delta y_j = (x_{i}^2 + y_{j}^2)\Delta x_i \Delta y_j
$$

Finally, summing up all of our rectangular solids over the entire partition $P$, we find that the upper Riemann sum is given by

$$
U(f,P) = \sum_{i=1}^m\sum_{j=1}^n (x_{i}^2 + y_{j}^2)\Delta x_i \Delta y_j \,
$$

## Writing an Upper Riemann Sum for a Strictly Increasing Function

**Example:** Let $R = [0,2]\times [1,3]$ be a region in the $xy$-plane, and let $f(x,y) = x+2y$. Further, let $P$ be a partition of $R$, where

$P_{1} = {x_{0}, x_{1}, …, x_{m}}, P_{2} = {y_{0}, y_{1}, …, y_{n}}, P = P_{1} \cdot P_{2}$.

Write down the upper Riemann sum $U(f, P)$.

**Explanation**

The upper Riemann sum is given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
U(f, P) &= ∑_(i = 1)^(m)∑_(j = 1)^(n)M_{ij}Δx_{i}Δy_{j}
\end{aligned}
$$

where $M_{ij}$ is the *maximum* value of $f$ in each subregion $R_{ij}$.

We consider an arbitrary subregion $R_{ij}$ of the partition. This subregion lies between $x_{i-1}$ and $x_i$ over the $x$-axis and $y_{i-1}$ and $y_i$ over the $y$-axis, as shown below.

![](<../Source/3.1.7. Upper Riemann Sums Over General Rectangular Partitions/Images/edad92d80f1137da0db424cf1d10358c.png>)

Notice that

- $f(x,y) = x+2y$ increases as $x$ increases, and
- $f(x,y) = x+2y$ increases as $y$ increases.

![](<../Source/3.1.7. Upper Riemann Sums Over General Rectangular Partitions/Images/963f3198c226e0fcf8688c291d514773.png>)

As a result, the *maximum* value of $f$ in each subregion is attained when we have the *largest* possible $x$ and the *largest* possible $y$. This is the value at the *top right corner* of each subregion.

![](<../Source/3.1.7. Upper Riemann Sums Over General Rectangular Partitions/Images/6870b71405e9ec1c7bd8f7feb11f4f90.png>)

Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
M_{ij} &= f(x_{i}, y_{j}) = x_{i} + 2y_{j}
\end{aligned}
$$

for all

$$
1\leq i\leq m
$$

and

$$
1\leq j\leq n
$$

Finally, the upper Riemann sum is

$$
\begin{aligned}
U(f, P) &= ∑_(i = 1)^(m)∑_(j = 1)^(n)(x_{i} + 2y_{j})Δx_{i}Δy_{j}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Writing an Upper Riemann Sum for a Strictly Decreasing Function

**Example:** Let $R = [0,1] \times [0,2]$ be a region in the $xy$-plane, and let $f(x,y) = 90-x^2-y^2$.
Further, let $P$ be a partition of $R$, where

$P_{1} = {x_{0}, x_{1}, …, x_{m}}, P_{2} = {y_{0}, y_{1}, …, y_{n}}, P = P_{1} \cdot P_{2}$.

Write down the upper Riemann sum $U(f, P)$.

**Explanation**

The upper Riemann sum is given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
U(f, P) &= ∑_(i = 1)^(m)∑_(j = 1)^(n)M_{ij}Δx_{i}Δy_{j}
\end{aligned}
$$

where $M_{ij}$ is the *maximum* value of $f$ in each subregion $R_{ij}$.

We consider an arbitrary subregion $R_{ij}$ of the partition. This subregion lies between $x_{i-1}$ and $x_i$ over the $x$-axis and $y_{i-1}$ and $y_i$ over the $y$-axis, as shown below.

![](<../Source/3.1.7. Upper Riemann Sums Over General Rectangular Partitions/Images/f0dc3e5ada5e449af8a26059d5ef0f8c.png>)

Notice that

- $f(x,y) = 90-x^2-y^2$ decreases as $x$ increases on $[0,1]$, and
- $f(x,y) = 90-x^2-y^2$ decreases as $y$ increases on $[0,2]$.

![](<../Source/3.1.7. Upper Riemann Sums Over General Rectangular Partitions/Images/3bc69da4098b8e380e39e0546cdce312.png>)

As a result, the *maximum* value of $f$ in each subregion is attained when we have the *smallest* possible $x$ and the *smallest* possible $y$. This is the value at the *bottom left corner* of each subregion.

![](<../Source/3.1.7. Upper Riemann Sums Over General Rectangular Partitions/Images/fde88ca2195c884141658dc35168ef03.png>)

Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
M_{ij} &= f(x_{i - 1}, y_{j - 1}) = 90 - x_{i - 1}^{2} - y_{j - 1}^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

for all

$$
1\leq i\leq m
$$

and

$$
1\leq j\leq n
$$

Finally, the upper Riemann sum is

$$
\begin{aligned}
U(f, P) &= ∑_(i = 1)^(m)∑_(j = 1)^(n)(90 - x_{i - 1}^{2} - y_{j - 1}^{2})Δx_{i}Δy_{j}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Writing an Upper Riemann Sum for a Function That’s Neither Strictly Increasing nor Decreasing

**Example:** Let $R = [2,4] \times [0,1]$ be a region in the $xy$-plane, and let $f(x,y) = 2x - y$. Further, let $P$ be a partition of $R$, where
$P_{1} = {x_{0}, x_{1}, …, x_{m}}, P_{2} = {y_{0}, y_{1}, …, y_{n}}, P = P_{1} \cdot P_{2}$.

Write down the upper Riemann sum $U(f,P)$.

**Explanation**

The upper Riemann sum is given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
U(f, P) &= ∑_(i = 1)^(m)∑_(j = 1)^(n)M_{ij}Δx_{i}Δy_{j}
\end{aligned}
$$

where $M_{ij}$ is the *maximum* value of $f$ in each subregion $R_{ij}$.

We consider an arbitrary subregion $R_{ij}$ of the partition. This subregion lies between $x_{i-1}$ and $x_i$ over the $x$-axis and $y_{i-1}$ and $y_i$ over the $y$-axis, as shown below.

![](<../Source/3.1.7. Upper Riemann Sums Over General Rectangular Partitions/Images/a1b215e94fff6e51b4665cd869553c5c.png>)

Notice that

- $f(x,y) = 2x - y$ increases as $x$ increases, and
- $f(x,y) = 2x - y$ decreases as $y$ increases.

![](<../Source/3.1.7. Upper Riemann Sums Over General Rectangular Partitions/Images/78607f0271a92fd1540c9ddf593f0010.png>)

As a result, the *maximum* value of $f$ in each subregion is attained when we have the *largest* possible $x$ and the *smallest* possible $y$. This is the value at the *bottom right corner* of each subregion.

![](<../Source/3.1.7. Upper Riemann Sums Over General Rectangular Partitions/Images/c7d415e021fb7f02f6aa222664e0ab60.png>)

Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
M_{ij} &= f(x_{i}, y_{j - 1}) = 2x_{i} - y_{j - 1}
\end{aligned}
$$

for all

$$
1\leq i\leq m
$$

and

$$
1\leq j\leq n
$$

Finally, the upper Riemann sum is

$$
\begin{aligned}
U(f, P) &= ∑_(i = 1)^(m)∑_(j = 1)^(n)(2x_{i} - y_{j - 1})Δx_{i}Δy_{j}
\end{aligned}
$$
