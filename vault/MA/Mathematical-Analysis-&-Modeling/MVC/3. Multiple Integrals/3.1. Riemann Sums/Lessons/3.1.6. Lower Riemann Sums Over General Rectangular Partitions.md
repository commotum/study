## Introduction

We know how to write the lower Riemann sum for a function $f(x,y)$ over a *fixed* rectangular partition. In this lesson, we will learn how to write down an expression for the lower Riemann sum for an *arbitrary* rectangular partition.

As a concrete example, let

$$
R = [0,1] \times [0,1]
$$

be a region in the $xy$-plane, and define the function $f(x,y)$ as

$$
f(x,y) = x^2+y^2
$$

Further, let $P$ be an arbitrary partition of $R$, where

$$
P_{1} = {x_{0}, x_{1}, …, x_{m}}, P_{2} = {y_{0}, y_{1}, …, y_{n}}, P = P_{1} \cdot P_{2}
$$

Our goal is to find an expression for $L(f, P)$, the lower Riemann sum of $f$ with respect to this partition. We proceed identically to the case where $P$ was fixed.

First, we consider an arbitrary subregion $R_{ij}$ of the partition. This subregion lies between $x_{i-1}$ and $x_i$ over the $x$-axis and $y_{j-1}$ and $y_j$ over the $y$-axis, as shown below.

![](<../Source/3.1.6. Lower Riemann Sums Over General Rectangular Partitions/Images/df475f12ec2f7649747a4916acdd1b37.png>)

Since we're computing the *lower* sum, we're interested in the *minimum* value of $f$ in this subregion.

Notice that

- $f(x,y) = x^2+y^2$ increases as $x$ increases on $[0,1]$, and
- $f(x,y) = x^2+y^2$ increases as $y$ increases on $[0,1]$.

![](<../Source/3.1.6. Lower Riemann Sums Over General Rectangular Partitions/Images/d52f73e64fddc857d5e31f64eacd42f1.png>)

Therefore, the *minimum* value of $f$ in the subregion is attained when we have the *smallest* possible $x$ and the *smallest* possible $y$. This is the value of $f$ at the *bottom left corner* of the subregion.

![](<../Source/3.1.6. Lower Riemann Sums Over General Rectangular Partitions/Images/0ab109da07ea8461e62e74e2a39efd4c.png>)

So, if $m_{ij}$ denotes the minimum value of $f$ in each subregion, we have

$$
m_{ij} = f(x_{i-1}, y_{j-1}) = x_{i-1}^2 + y_{j-1}^2
$$

for all

$$
1\leq i\leq m
$$

and

$$
1\leq j\leq n
$$

Since the area of $R_{ij}$ is $\Delta x_i\Delta y_j$, the volume of the rectangular solid with base $R_{ij}$ and height $m_{ij}$ is

$$
m_{ij}\Delta x_i\Delta y_j = (x_{i-1}^2 + y_{j-1}^2)\Delta x_i \Delta y_j \,
$$

Finally, summing up all of our rectangular solids over the entire partition $P$, we find that the lower Riemann sum is given by

$$
L(f,P) = \sum_{i=1}^m\sum_{j=1}^n (x_{i-1}^2 + y_{j-1}^2)\Delta x_i \Delta y_j \,
$$

## Writing the Lower Riemann Sum for a Strictly Increasing Function

**Example:** Let $R = [0,2]\times [1,3]$ be a region in the $xy$-plane, and let $f(x,y) = x+2y$. Further, let $P$ be a partition of $R$, where

$P_{1} = {x_{0}, x_{1}, …, x_{m}}, P_{2} = {y_{0}, y_{1}, …, y_{n}}, P = P_{1} \cdot P_{2}$.

Write down the lower Riemann sum $L(f, P)$.

**Explanation**

The lower Riemann sum is given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
L(f, P) &= ∑_(i = 1)^(m)∑_(j = 1)^(n)m_{ij}Δx_{i}Δy_{j}
\end{aligned}
$$

where $m_{ij}$ is the *minimum* value of $f$ in each subregion $R_{ij}$.

We consider an arbitrary subregion $R_{ij}$ of the partition. This subregion lies between $x_{i-1}$ and $x_i$ over the $x$-axis and $y_{i-1}$ and $y_i$ over the $y$-axis, as shown below.

![](<../Source/3.1.6. Lower Riemann Sums Over General Rectangular Partitions/Images/87ff4da53aaa0d24860e1ce4c87670e6.png>)

Notice that

- $f(x,y) = x+2y$ increases as $x$ increases, and
- $f(x,y) = x+2y$ increases as $y$ increases.

![](<../Source/3.1.6. Lower Riemann Sums Over General Rectangular Partitions/Images/ded242afabd5cfa40778881b586cae44.png>)

As a result, the *minimum* value of $f$ in each subregion is attained when we have the *smallest* possible $x$ and the *smallest* possible $y$. This is the value at the *bottom left corner* of each subregion.

![](<../Source/3.1.6. Lower Riemann Sums Over General Rectangular Partitions/Images/c48db414a7ab06b8e9aee0c509f1a013.png>)

Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
m_{ij} &= f(x_{i - 1}, y_{j - 1}) = x_{i - 1} + 2y_{j - 1}
\end{aligned}
$$

for all

$$
1\leq i\leq m
$$

and

$$
1\leq j\leq n
$$

Finally, the lower Riemann sum is

$$
\begin{aligned}
L(f, P) &= ∑_(i = 1)^(m)∑_(j = 1)^(n)(x_{i - 1} + 2y_{j - 1})Δx_{i}Δy_{j}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Writing the Lower Riemann Sum for a Strictly Decreasing Function

**Example:** Let $R = [0,1] \times [2,3]$ be a region in the $xy$-plane, and let $f(x,y) = 10-x^2-y^2$.
Further, let $P$ be a partition of $R$, where

$P_{1} = {x_{0}, x_{1}, …, x_{m}}, P_{2} = {y_{0}, y_{1}, …, y_{n}}, P = P_{1} \cdot P_{2}$.

Write down the lower Riemann sum $L(f, P)$.

**Explanation**

The lower Riemann sum is given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
L(f, P) &= ∑_(i = 1)^(m)∑_(j = 1)^(n)m_{ij}Δx_{i}Δy_{j}
\end{aligned}
$$

where $m_{ij}$ is the *minimum* value of $f$ in each subregion $R_{ij}$.

We consider an arbitrary subregion $R_{ij}$ of the partition. This subregion lies between $x_{i-1}$ and $x_i$ over the $x$-axis and $y_{i-1}$ and $y_i$ over the $y$-axis, as shown below.

![](<../Source/3.1.6. Lower Riemann Sums Over General Rectangular Partitions/Images/b4cb4b22e32666ef93b6b0cb8bdf196a.png>)

Notice that

- $f(x,y) =10-x^2-y^2$ decreases as $x$ increases on $[0,1]$, and
- $f(x,y) = 10-x^2-y^2$ decreases as $y$ increases on $[2,3]$.

![](<../Source/3.1.6. Lower Riemann Sums Over General Rectangular Partitions/Images/c0d4216d9454a8c106a83aaca48b9e57.png>)

As a result, the *minimum* value of $f$ in each subregion is attained when we have the *largest* possible $x$ and the *largest* possible $y$. This is the value at the *top right corner* of each subregion.

![](<../Source/3.1.6. Lower Riemann Sums Over General Rectangular Partitions/Images/3c7f764f4959d3fad12cd0df70d46b14.png>)

Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
m_{ij} &= f(x_{i}, y_{j}) = 10 - x_{i}^{2} - y_{j}^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

for all

$$
1\leq i\leq m
$$

and

$$
1\leq j\leq n
$$

Finally, the lower Riemann sum is

$$
\begin{aligned}
L(f, P) &= ∑_(i = 1)^(m)∑_(j = 1)^(n)(10 - x_{i}^{2} - y_{j}^{2})Δx_{i}Δy_{j}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Writing the Lower Riemann Sum for a Function That's Neither Strictly Increasing Nor Decreasing

**Example:** Let $R = [2,3] \times [1,2]$ be a region in the $xy$-plane, and let $f(x,y) = x - y + 3$.
Further, let $P$ be a partition of $R$, where
$P_{1} = {x_{0}, x_{1}, …, x_{m}}, P_{2} = {y_{0}, y_{1}, …, y_{n}}, P = P_{1} \cdot P_{2}$.

Write down the lower Riemann sum $L(f, P)$.

**Explanation**

The lower Riemann sum is given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
L(f, P) &= ∑_(i = 1)^(m)∑_(j = 1)^(n)m_{ij}Δx_{i}Δy_{j}
\end{aligned}
$$

where $m_{ij}$ is the *minimum* value of $f$ in each subregion $R_{ij}$.

We consider an arbitrary subregion $R_{ij}$ of the partition. This subregion lies between $x_{i-1}$ and $x_i$ over the $x$-axis and $y_{i-1}$ and $y_i$ over the $y$-axis, as shown below.

![](<../Source/3.1.6. Lower Riemann Sums Over General Rectangular Partitions/Images/4707fec393fa97f6a2a8bd379ea8574b.png>)

Notice that

- $f(x,y) = x - y + 3$ increases as $x$ increases, and
- $f(x,y) = x - y + 3$ decreases as $y$ increases.

![](<../Source/3.1.6. Lower Riemann Sums Over General Rectangular Partitions/Images/7d6f2f08ee2c75640b099bc91d36aef8.png>)

As a result, the *minimum* value of $f$ in each subregion is attained when we have the *smallest* possible $x$ and the *largest* possible $y$. This is the value at the *top left corner* of each subregion.

![](<../Source/3.1.6. Lower Riemann Sums Over General Rectangular Partitions/Images/9e0e5cae4e2067faf6025f08cbbd6685.png>)

Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
m_{ij} &= f(x_{i - 1}, y_{j}) = x_{i - 1} - y_{j} + 3
\end{aligned}
$$

for all

$$
1 \leq i \leq m
$$

and

$$
1 \leq j \leq n
$$

Finally, the lower Riemann sum is

$$
\begin{aligned}
L_{(}f, P) &= ∑_(i = 1)^(m)∑_(j = 1)^(n)(x_{i - 1} - y_{j} + 3)Δx_{i}Δy_{j}
\end{aligned}
$$
