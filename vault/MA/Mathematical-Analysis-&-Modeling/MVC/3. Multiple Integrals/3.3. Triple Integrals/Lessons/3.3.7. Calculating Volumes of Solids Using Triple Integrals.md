## Introduction

Recall that the double Integral of the function

$$
f(x,y) = 1
$$

over a domain $D\subset \mathbb R^2$ is simply the area of $D$:

$$
\textrm{Area}\ \!(D) = \displaystyle \iint\limits_{D} 1 \cdot \textrm d A = \displaystyle \iint\limits_{D} \ \textrm d A
$$

We have a similar result for triple integrals:

> *If $R$ is a region in three-dimensional space, then the volume of $R$ is given by*
> $\textrm{Volume}(R) = \iiint\limits_{R} \textrm{d}V$.

In other words, the volume of $R$ equals the triple integral of the function

$$
f(x,y,z)=1
$$

over $R$.

For example, let's use triple integrals to find the volume of the rectangular prism shown below.

![](<../Source/3.3.7. Calculating Volumes of Solids Using Triple Integrals/Images/66f4da5f969fef77ff61a6e063ea48ed.png>)

Notice that our solid can be written in set-builder notation as

$$
R = \big\{(x,y,z) \:: \: 0 \leq x \leq 1, \:\: 0 \leq y \leq 2, \:\: 0 \leq z \leq 3 \big\}
$$

Therefore, the volume of $R$ is

$$
\begin{aligned}
V &= ∭_(R)dV \\
&= ∫_{0}^{1}∫_{0}^{2}∫_{0}^{3}dzdydx \\
&= ∫_{0}^{1}dx \cdot ∫_{0}^{2}dy \cdot ∫_{0}^{3}dz \\
&= (1 - 0) \cdot (2 - 0) \cdot (3 - 0) \\
&= 6
\end{aligned}
$$

**Note:** We get the same value using the formula for the volume of a rectangular prism:

$$
\begin{aligned}
V &= length \cdot width \cdot height \\
&= 1 \cdot 2 \cdot 3 \\
&= 6
\end{aligned}
$$

Let's see some more examples.

## Finding the Volume of a Solid Given in Set Notation

**Example:** Find the volume of the solid $R$, given by
$R = \Big\{(x,y,z) \:: \: 1 \leq y \leq 2, \: 0 \leq z \leq \sqrt{2y}, \: 0 \leq x \leq yz \Big\}$.

**Explanation**

Notice that we have a three-dimensional type II region.

Therefore, the volume of $R$ is given by the following triple integral:

$$
\begin{aligned}
V &= ∭_(R)dV \\
&= ∫_{1}^{2}∫_{0}^{\sqrt{2y}}∫_{0}^{yz}dxdzdy \\
&= ∫_{1}^{2}∫_{0}^{\sqrt{2y}}[x]_{x = 0}^{x = yz}dzdy \\
&= ∫_{1}^{2}∫_{0}^{\sqrt{2y}}yzdzdy \\
&= ∫_{1}^{2}y[\frac{z^{2}}{2}]_{z = 0}^{z = \sqrt{2y}}dy \\
&= ∫_{1}^{2}\frac{y}{2}(\sqrt{2y})^{2}dy \\
&= ∫_{1}^{2}y^{2}dy \\
&= [\frac{y^{3}}{3}]_{y = 1}^{y = 2} \\
&= \frac{1}{3}(8 - 1) - 0 \\
&= \frac{7}{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding the Volume of a Solid Given a Diagram

**Example:** Find the volume of the solid region above.

![](<../Source/3.3.7. Calculating Volumes of Solids Using Triple Integrals/Images/833b93599de9c6b708f3a1261e746f92.png>)

*Hint: All surfaces, except for the top one, are parallel to the corresponding coordinate planes.*

**Explanation**

![](<../Source/3.3.7. Calculating Volumes of Solids Using Triple Integrals/Images/2298616a278bfc40c45434c5731feaf7.png>)

Notice that our solid can be written as the type I region

$$
R = \big\{(x,y,z) \:: \: (x,y) \in D, \:\: 0 \leq z \leq 4-x-y \big\}
$$

where

$$
D = \big\{(x,y) \:: \: 0 \leq x \leq 1, \:\: 0 \leq y \leq 2 \big\}
$$

is the projection of $R$ onto the $xy$-plane, shown below.

![](<../Source/3.3.7. Calculating Volumes of Solids Using Triple Integrals/Images/cc9d4db3f6570233d1e1e71d8636bcdc.png>)

Hence, the type I representation of $R$ is

$$
R = \big\{(x,y,z) \:: \: 0 \leq x \leq 1, \:\: 0 \leq y \leq 2, \:\: 0 \leq z \leq 4-x-y \big\}
$$

Therefore, the volume of $R$ is given by the following triple integral:

$$
\begin{aligned}
V &= ∭_(R)dV \\
&= ∫_{0}^{1}∫_{0}^{2}∫_{0}^{4 - x - y}dzdydx \\
&= ∫_{0}^{1}∫_{0}^{2}[z]_{z = 0}^{z = 4 - x - y}dydx \\
&= ∫_{0}^{1}∫_{0}^{2}(4 - x - y) - 0dydx \\
&= ∫_{0}^{1}∫_{0}^{2}4 - x - ydydx \\
&= ∫_{0}^{1}[(4 - x)y - \frac{y^{2}}{2}]_{y = 0}^{y = 2}dx \\
&= ∫_{0}^{1}((4 - x)(2) - \frac{2^{2}}{2}) - 0dx \\
&= ∫_{0}^{1}6 - 2xdx \\
&= [6x - x^{2}]_{x = 0}^{x = 1} \\
&= (6(1) - 1^{2}) - 0 \\
&= 5
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding the Volume of a Solid Given a Description

**Example:** Find the volume of the solid region enclosed by the surface $y=xz$, the plane $y=2$, and the planes $x=0$, $x=1$, $z=0$, $z=1$.

**Explanation**

Notice that our solid can be written as a type III region:

$$
R = \left\{(x,y,z) \:: \: 0 \leq x \leq 1, \:\: 0 \leq z \leq 1, \:\: xz \leq y \leq 2 \right\}
$$

Therefore, the volume of $R$ is given by the following triple integral:

$$
\begin{aligned}
V &= ∭_(R)dV \\
&= ∫_{0}^{1}∫_{0}^{1}∫_{xz}^{2}dydzdx \\
&= ∫_{0}^{1}∫_{0}^{1}[y]_{y = xz}^{y = 2}dzdx \\
&= ∫_{0}^{1}∫_{0}^{1}2 - xzdzdx \\
&= ∫_{0}^{1}[2z - \frac{xz^{2}}{2}]_{z = 0}^{z = 1}dx \\
&= ∫_{0}^{1}[2(1 - 0) - (x(1^{2} - 0))/(2)]dx \\
&= ∫_{0}^{1}2 - \frac{x}{2}dx \\
&= [2x - \frac{x^{2}}{4}]_{x = 0}^{x = 1} \\
&= (2 - \frac{1}{4}) - 0 \\
&= \frac{7}{4}
\end{aligned}
$$
