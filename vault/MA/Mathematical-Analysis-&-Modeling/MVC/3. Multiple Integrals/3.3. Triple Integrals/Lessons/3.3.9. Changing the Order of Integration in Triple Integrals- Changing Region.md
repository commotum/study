## Introduction

Consider the following repeated integral:
$\displaystyle \int_0^1 \int_0^{1-x} \int_0^{1-x-y} f(x,y,z) \:\textrm{d}z\:\textrm{d}y\:\textrm{d}x$

For this repeated integral, the integration domain is the type I region

$$
R = \left\{(x,y,z) \:: \: 0 \leq x \leq 1, \:\: 0 \leq y \leq 1-x, \:\: 0 \leq z \leq 1-x-y \right\}
$$

as shown below.

![](<../Source/3.3.9. Changing the Order of Integration in Triple Integrals- Changing Region/Images/37641238c24d597444c2f2095e21679a.png>)

Suppose we wish to change the order of integration so that we integrate first with respect to $y$, then over a region $D$ in the $xz$-plane. In other words, we wish to express our repeated integral in the form

$$
\int_0^1 \int_0^{1-x} \int_0^{1-x-y} f(x,y,z) \:\textrm{d}z\:\textrm{d}y\:\textrm{d}x = \iint\limits_D \left[ \int_{u_1(x,z)}^{u_2(x,z)} f(x,y,z) \:\textrm{d}y \right] \textrm{d}A
$$

To change the order of integration from $\textrm{d}z\:\textrm{d}y\:\textrm{d}x$ to $\textrm{d}y\:\textrm{d}A$, we write $R$ as a type III region.

To visualize $R$ as a type III region, imagine firing an arrow through the region parallel to the $y$-axis and in the direction of increasing $y$, as shown below.

![](<../Source/3.3.9. Changing the Order of Integration in Triple Integrals- Changing Region/Images/531040d25ff73e6692c538475d1839c3.png>)

The arrow enters the region through the back boundary and leaves through the front boundary:

- The front boundary of the region is
$z = 1-x-y$,
and writing this in the form $y = u_2(x,z)$, we have
$y = 1-x-z$.
- The back boundary of the region is $y = 0$.

Therefore, the type III representation of $R$ is

$$
R = \left\{(x,y,z) \:: \: (x,z) \in D, \:\: 0 \leq y \leq 1-x-z \right\}
$$

as shown in the diagram below.

![](<../Source/3.3.9. Changing the Order of Integration in Triple Integrals- Changing Region/Images/6b533dc1c5d94d74a1c63db6ffe20dbd.png>)

Therefore, by swapping the order of integration so that it is integrated first with respect to $y$ and then over $D$, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
∫_{0}^{1}∫_{0}^{1 - x}∫_{0}^{1 - x - y}f(x, y, z)dzdydx &= ∬_(D)[∫_{0}^{1 - x - z}f(x, y, z)dy]dA
\end{aligned}
$$

## Rewriting a Repeated Integral as a Mixed Integral

**Example:** Change the order of integration in the integral
$\displaystyle \int_0^1 \int_0^{2} \int_0^{\sqrt[3]{z}} f(x,y,z) \:\textrm{d}y\:\textrm{d}x\:\textrm{d}z$
so that it is integrated first with respect to $z$, then over a region $D$ in the $xy$-plane. The integration region $R$ is shown above.

![](<../Source/3.3.9. Changing the Order of Integration in Triple Integrals- Changing Region/Images/dc30042ba8467a4dd3b57e4729cf275e.png>)

**Explanation**

We wish to find the limits of integration $u_1(x,y)$ and $u_2(x,y)$ such that

$$
\int_0^1 \int_0^{2} \int_0^{\sqrt[3]{z}} f(x,y,z) \:\textrm{d}y\:\textrm{d}x\:\textrm{d}z = \iint\limits_D \left[ \int_{u_1(x,y)}^{u_2(x,y)} f(x,y,z) \:\textrm{d}z\right] \textrm{d}A
$$

From the repeated integral, we see that the region of integration $R$ can be written as a type III region as follows:

$$
R = \big\{(x,y,z) \:\: {:} \:\: 0 \leq x \leq 2, \:\: 0 \leq z \leq 1, \:\: 0 \leq y \leq \sqrt[3]{z} \big\}
$$

To change the order of integration from $\textrm{d}y\:\textrm{d}x\:\textrm{d}z$ to $\textrm{d}z\:\textrm{d}A$, we write $R$ as a type I region.

To visualize this, imagine firing an arrow through the region parallel to the $z$-axis and in the direction of increasing $z$, as shown below.

![](<../Source/3.3.9. Changing the Order of Integration in Triple Integrals- Changing Region/Images/20ba647fd3e3402dc4ba57a1efe199b3.png>)

The arrow enters the region through the bottom boundary and leaves through the top boundary:

- The bottom boundary of the region, written in the form $z=z(x,y)$, is
$y = \sqrt[3]{z}, \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad z = y^3$.
- The top boundary of the region is $z = 1$.

Therefore, the type I representation of $R$ is

$$
R = \big\{(x,y,z) \:\: {:} \:\: (x,y) \in D, \:\: y^3\leq z \leq 1 \big\}
$$

as shown in the diagram below.

![](<../Source/3.3.9. Changing the Order of Integration in Triple Integrals- Changing Region/Images/54727f66b4a3d3b312c032189ed08a48.png>)

Therefore, by swapping the order of integration so that it is integrated first with respect to $z$ and then over $D$, we obtain

$$
\int_0^1 \int_0^{2} \int_0^{\sqrt[3]{z}} f(x,y,z) \:\textrm{d}y\:\textrm{d}x\:\textrm{d}z = \iint\limits_D \left[\int_{y^3}^{1} f(x,y,z) \:\textrm{d}z\right] \textrm{d}A
$$

## Swapping the Order of Integration in a Triple Integral

**Example:** Change the order of integration in the integral
$\displaystyle \int_0^{1} \int_0^{1} \int_0^{1-z^2} f(x,y,z) \:\textrm{d}y\:\textrm{d}z\:\textrm{d}x$.
so that it is integrated first with respect to $z$, then $y$, and then $x$.

**Explanation**

We wish to find limits of integration $u_1(x,y)$, $u_2(x,y)$, $v_1(x)$, $v_2(x)$, $a$, and $b$ such that

$$
\int_0^{1} \int_0^{1} \int_0^{1-z^2} f(x,y,z) \:\textrm{d}y\:\textrm{d}z\:\textrm{d}x = \int_a^b \int_{v_1(x)}^{v_2(x)} \int_{u_1(x,y)}^{u_2(x,y)} f(x,y,z) \:\textrm{d}z\:\textrm{d}y\:\textrm{d}x
$$

From the repeated integral, we see that the region of integration $R$ can be written as a type III region as follows:

$$
R = \left\{(x,y,z) \:: \: 0 \leq x \leq 1, \:\: 0 \leq z \leq 1, \:\: 0 \leq y \leq 1-z^2 \right\}
$$

Now, let's sketch the region. Since it is a type III region, we first draw the projection onto the $xz$-plane, given by

$$
E = \left\{(x,z) \:: \: 0 \leq x \leq 1, \:\: 0 \leq z \leq 1 \right\}
$$

as shown below.

![](<../Source/3.3.9. Changing the Order of Integration in Triple Integrals- Changing Region/Images/0898e2ab1dad983d6db2c113b25a7b9b.png>)

Then, the region $R$ is shown in the diagram below, where

$$
0 \leq y \leq 1-z^2
$$

![](<../Source/3.3.9. Changing the Order of Integration in Triple Integrals- Changing Region/Images/7bf3173bf663a363a02d25d6025c92dc.png>)

To change the order of integration from $\textrm{d}y\:\textrm{d}z\:\textrm{d}x$ to $\textrm{d}z\:\textrm{d}y\:\textrm{d}x$, we write $R$ as a type I region and its projection $D$ onto the $xy$-plane as a type I *plane* region.

To visualize this, imagine firing an arrow through the region parallel to the $z$-axis and in the direction of increasing $z$, as shown below.

![](<../Source/3.3.9. Changing the Order of Integration in Triple Integrals- Changing Region/Images/54b1cbed41d7992355278fe388a141c6.png>)

The arrow enters the region through the bottom boundary and leaves through the top boundary:

- The top boundary of the region, written in the form $z=z(x,y)$, is
$y = 1-z^2 \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad z = \sqrt{1- y}$.
- The bottom boundary of the region is $z=0$.

Therefore, the type I representation of $R$ is

$$
R = \big\{(x,y,z) \:: \: (x,y) \in D, \:\: 0 \leq z \leq \sqrt{1-y} \big\}
$$

as shown in the diagram below.

![](<../Source/3.3.9. Changing the Order of Integration in Triple Integrals- Changing Region/Images/5a04ed84a421e394756b01cbdd473210.png>)

Now, we consider the projection $D$ as a type I plane region in the $xy$-plane with the top curve given by $y=1$.

![](<../Source/3.3.9. Changing the Order of Integration in Triple Integrals- Changing Region/Images/84fb222f81aaade0074f70c558fa3b30.png>)

Hence, we have that

$$
D = \left\{(x,y) \:: \: 0 \leq x \leq 1, \:\: 0 \leq y \leq 1 \right\}
$$

Therefore, by swapping the order of integration so that it is integrated first with respect to $z$, then $y$, then $x$, we obtain

$$
\int_0^{1} \int_0^{1} \int_0^{1-z^2} f(x,y,z) \:\textrm{d}y\:\textrm{d}z\:\textrm{d}x = \int_0^{1} \int_0^{\bbox[2pt,Gainsboro]{1}} \int_{\bbox[2pt,Gainsboro]{0}}^{\bbox[2pt,Gainsboro]{\sqrt{1-y}}} f(x,y,z) \:\textrm{d}z\:\textrm{d}y\:\textrm{d}x
$$

## Evaluating a Triple Integral by Swapping the Order of Integration

**Example:** Evaluate $\displaystyle \int_{0}^{1} \int_{0}^{2} \int_{z}^{2} e^{x^2} \, \mathrm{d}x \: \mathrm{d}z \: \mathrm{d}y$.

*Hint: Change the order of integration to $\textrm{d}z\:\textrm{d}x\:\textrm{d}y$.*

**Explanation**

We cannot integrate the given integral because we cannot find a simple antiderivative of the integrand. So, we change the order of integration.

We wish to find the limits of integration $u_1(x,y)$, $u_2(x,y)$, $v_1(y)$, $v_2(y)$, $a$, and $b$ such that

$$
\int_{0}^{1} \int_{0}^{2} \int_{z}^{2} e^{x^2} \, \mathrm{d}x \: \mathrm{d}z \: \mathrm{d}y = \int_{a}^{b} \int_{v_1(y)}^{v_2(y)} \int_{u_1(x,y)}^{u_2(x,y)} e^{x^2} \, \textrm{d}z \: \textrm{d}x \: \textrm{d}y
$$

From the repeated integral, we see that the region of integration $R$ can be written as a type II region as follows:

$$
R = \left\{(x,y,z) \:: \: 0 \leq y \leq 1, \:\: 0 \leq z \leq 2, \:\: z \leq x \leq 2 \right\}
$$

Now, let's sketch the region. Since it is a type II region, we first sketch the projection onto the $yz$-plane, given by

$$
E = \left\{(y,z) \:: \: 0 \leq y \leq 1, \:\: 0 \leq z \leq 2 \right\}
$$

as shown below.

![](<../Source/3.3.9. Changing the Order of Integration in Triple Integrals- Changing Region/Images/50867af2658a2fd8436bc8f1c49b01d9.png>)

Then, the region $R$ is shown in the diagram below, where

$$
z \leq x \leq 2
$$

![](<../Source/3.3.9. Changing the Order of Integration in Triple Integrals- Changing Region/Images/183a99ad9f325c9c75801f18e667d562.png>)

To change the order of integration from $\mathrm{d}x \: \mathrm{d}z \: \mathrm{d}y$ to $\textrm{d}z\:\textrm{d}x\:\textrm{d}y$, we write $R$ as a type I region and its projection $D$ onto the $xy$-plane as a type II *plane* region.

To visualize this, imagine firing an arrow through the region parallel to the $z$-axis and in the direction of increasing $z$, as shown below.

![](<../Source/3.3.9. Changing the Order of Integration in Triple Integrals- Changing Region/Images/0f5cb134455e0cfceef74be513903e4a.png>)

The arrow enters the region through the bottom boundary and leaves through the top boundary:

- The bottom boundary of the region is
$z=0$.
- The top boundary of the region, written in the form $z = z(x,y)$, is
$x = z \qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad z = x$.

Therefore, the type I representation of $R$ is

$$
R = \left\{(x,y,z) \:: \: (x,y) \in D, \:\: 0 \leq z \leq x \right\}
$$

as shown in the diagram below.

![](<../Source/3.3.9. Changing the Order of Integration in Triple Integrals- Changing Region/Images/e4de33214185ed8a7bebf8a9db1b1051.png>)

Now, we consider the projection $D$ as a type II plane region in the $xy$-plane with the left curve given by $x = 0$.

![](<../Source/3.3.9. Changing the Order of Integration in Triple Integrals- Changing Region/Images/8b095ecbfe344bc149ece073098d6a15.png>)

Hence,

$$
D = \left\{(x,y) \:: \: 0 \leq x \leq 2, \:\: 0 \leq y \leq 1 \right\}
$$

Therefore, by swapping the order of integration, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
∫_{0}^{1}∫_{0}^{2}∫_{z}^{2}e^{x^{2}}dxdzdy &= ∫_{0}^{1}∫_{0}^{2}∫_{0}^{x}e^{x^{2}}dzdxdy \\
&= ∫_{0}^{1}∫_{0}^{2}e^{x^{2}}[∫_{0}^{x}dz]dxdy \\
&= ∫_{0}^{1}∫_{0}^{2}e^{x^{2}}[z]_{0}^{x}dxdy \\
&= ∫_{0}^{1}∫_{0}^{2}xe^{x^{2}}dxdy \\
&= ∫_{0}^{1}[∫_{0}^{2}xe^{x^{2}}dx]dy \\
&= ∫_{0}^{1}[\frac{1}{2}e^{x^{2}}]_{0}^{2}dy \\
&= \frac{1}{2}∫_{0}^{1}(e^{4} - 1)dy \\
&= \frac{1}{2}(e^{4} - 1) \cdot [y]_{0}^{1} \\
&= \frac{1}{2}(e^{4} - 1)
\end{aligned}
$$
