## Introduction

Let's evaluate the triple integral

$$
\displaystyle \iiint\limits_R 2y \: \mathrm{d}V
$$

where the region $R$ is shown below.

![](<../Source/3.3.5. Triple Integrals Over Type II Regions/Images/26e7bab8989c9c5aa619a9ce2d222c25.png>)

The region $R$ is a type II region. So, our first task is to fully describe this region.

We start by drawing an arrow parallel to the $x$-axis that

- enters the region through the *back* surface $x=0$, and
- leaves the region through the *front* surface $x=5-y-z$,

as shown below.

![](<../Source/3.3.5. Triple Integrals Over Type II Regions/Images/1aac8042efc786ce5addfa1b50630a09.png>)

So, our solid can be written as the type II region

$$
R = \big\{(x,y,z) \:: \: (y,z) \in D, \:\: 0 \leq x \leq 5-y-z \big\}
$$

where

$$
D = \big\{(y,z) \:: \: 0 \leq y \leq 2, \:\: 0 \leq z \leq 2 \big\}
$$

is the projection of $R$ onto the $yz$-plane, shown below.

![](<../Source/3.3.5. Triple Integrals Over Type II Regions/Images/0342247f8d971a505afcd68847a251b1.png>)

To evaluate our triple integral, we start by expressing it as a mixed integral:

$$
\iiint \limits_{R} 2y \: \textrm{d}V = \iint\limits_D \left[ \int_{0}^{5-y-z} 2y \: \textrm{d}x \right] \textrm{d}A
$$

Now, since the region $D$ is a type I plane region, we can express the region $R$ as

$$
R = \big\{(x,y,z) \:: \: 0 \leq y \leq 2, \:\: 0 \leq z \leq 2, \:\: 0 \leq x \leq 5-y-z \big\}
$$

and therefore, our mixed integral can be written as the following repeated integral:

$$
\iint\limits_D \left[ \int_{0}^{5-x-z} 2y \: \mathrm{d}y \right] \mathrm{d}A = \int_{0}^{2} \int_{0}^{2} \int_{0}^{5-y-z} 2y \: \mathrm{d}x \: \mathrm{d}z \: \mathrm{d}y
$$

Evaluating this using the usual methods, we get

$$
\int_{0}^{2} \int_{0}^{2} \int_{0}^{5-y-z} 2y \: \mathrm{d}x \: \mathrm{d}z \: \mathrm{d}y = \dfrac{64}{3}
$$

Therefore, we conclude that

$$
\iiint \limits_{R} 2y \: \textrm{d}V = \dfrac{64}{3}
$$

<a id="representing-a-triple-integral-as-a-mixed-or-repeated-integral"></a>
## Representing a Triple Integral as a Mixed or Repeated Integral

**Example:** Find the missing limits in the repeated integral below

$\displaystyle \iiint \limits_{R} f(x,y,z) \: \textrm{d}V = \int_{\ast}^{\ast} \int_{\ast}^{\ast} \int_{\ast}^{\ast} f(x,y,z) \: \mathrm{d}x \: \mathrm{d}z \: \mathrm{d}y$

where $R$ is the type II region defined as

$R=\left\{(x,y,z) \:: \: 1 \leq y \leq 4, \: -y^2 \leq z \leq \sqrt{y}, \: -2 \leq x \leq ye^{z+2} \right\}$.

**Explanation**

A type II region in three-dimensional space can be written as

$$
R = \big\{(x,y,z) \:: \: (y,z) \in D, \: u_1(y,z) \leq x \leq u_2(y,z) \big\}
$$

A typical three-dimensional type II region can be visualized schematically, as shown below.

![](<../Source/3.3.5. Triple Integrals Over Type II Regions/Images/83d78c3a80d9921c38438e2967052d69.png>)

If $R$ is a type II region, we can express the triple integral of $f(x,y,z)$ over $R$ as a mixed integral, as follows:

$$
\iiint\limits_{R} f(x,y,z)\,\textrm d V = \iint\limits_{D} \left[\int_{u_1(y,z)}^{u_2(y,z)} f(x,y,z)\,\textrm{d}x \right]\,\textrm d A
$$

In our case, we have

$$
u_1(y,z) = -2, \qquad u_2(y,z) = ye^{z+2}
$$

Therefore, our triple integral can be written as

$$
\displaystyle \iiint \limits_{R} f(x,y,z) \: \textrm{d}V = \iint\limits_{D} \left[ \int_{-2}^{ye^{z+2}} f(x,y,z) \: \mathrm{d}x \right] \mathrm{d}A
$$

Now, the region $D$ is given by

$$
D =\left\{(x,y) \:: \: 1 \leq y \leq 4, \: -y^2 \leq z \leq \sqrt{y} \right\}
$$

Therefore, by expressing the double integral over $D$ as a repeated integral, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
∬_(D)[∫_{-2}^{ye^{z + 2}}f(x, y, z)dx]dA &= ∫_{1}^{4}∫_{-y^{2}}^{\sqrt{y}}[∫_{-2}^{ye^{z + 2}}f(x, y, z)dx]dzdy \\
&= ∫_{1}^{4}∫_{-y^{2}}^{\sqrt{y}}∫_{-2}^{ye^{z + 2}}f(x, y, z)dxdzdy
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="evaluating-a-mixed-integral"></a>
## Evaluating a Mixed Integral

**Example:** Evaluate the mixed integral
$\displaystyle \iint\limits_D \left[\int_{0}^{z} \dfrac{\cos (2y)}{1-y} \,\textrm{d}x \right] \textrm{d}A$
where the region $D$ in the $yz$-plane is given by
$D = \left\{(y,z) \,: \, 0 \leq y \leq \dfrac{\pi}{6}, \: 0 \leq z \leq 2\sqrt{1-y} \right\}$.

**Explanation**

First, we evaluate the inner integral with respect to $x$, treating $y$ and $z$ as constants:

$$
\begin{aligned}
∬_(D)[∫_{0}^{z}(\cos (2y))/(1 - y)dx]dA &= ∬_(D)(\cos (2y))/(1 - y)[x]_{x = 0}^{x = z}dA \\
&= ∬_(D)(\cos (2y))/(1 - y)(z - 0)dA \\
&= ∬_(D)(z\cos (2y))/(1 - y)dA
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we evaluate the double integral:

$$
\begin{aligned}
∬_(D)(z\cos (2y))/(1 - y)dA &= ∫_{0}^{π/6}∫_{0}^{2\sqrt{1 - y}}(z\cos (2y))/(1 - y)dzdy \\
&= ∫_{0}^{π/6}(\cos (2y))/(1 - y)[\frac{z^{2}}{2}]_{z = 0}^{z = 2\sqrt{1 - y}}dy \\
&= ∫_{0}^{π/6}(\cos (2y))/(1 - y)((4(1 - y))/(2) - 0)dy \\
&= ∫_{0}^{π/6}2\cos (2y)dy \\
&= [\sin (2y)]_{y = 0}^{y = π/6} \\
&= \sin (\frac{π}{3}) - \sin (0) \\
&= \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="evaluating-a-triple-integral-over-a-type-ii-region"></a>
## Evaluating a Triple Integral Over a Type II Region

**Example:** Evaluate the triple integral

$\displaystyle \iiint\limits_R 4x^3 \: \mathrm{d}V$

where the region $R$ is given by
$R = \left\{(x,y,z) \:: \: (y,z) \in D, \: 0 \leq x \leq \sqrt{y} \right\}$,
and $D$ is a finite region in $yz$-plane enclosed between the $y$-axis, the $z$-axis, and the line $z = 4-y$ in the first quadrant.

**Explanation**

Writing down our triple integral as a mixed integral, we obtain

$$
\displaystyle \iiint \limits_{R} 4x^3 \, \textrm{d}V = \iint\limits_{D} \left[ \int_{0}^{\sqrt{y}} 4x^3 \, \mathrm{d}x \right] \mathrm{d}A
$$

First, we evaluate the inner integral with respect to $x$, treating $y$ and $z$ as constants:

$$
\begin{aligned}
∬_(D)[∫_{0}^{\sqrt{y}}4x^{3}dx]dA &= ∬_(D)[x^{4}]_{x = 0}^{x = \sqrt{y}}dA \\
&= ∬_(D)(\sqrt{y})^{4}dA \\
&= ∬_(D)y^{2}dA
\end{aligned}
$$

We must now evaluate this double integral over the region $D$ in the $yz$-plane shown below.

![](<../Source/3.3.5. Triple Integrals Over Type II Regions/Images/f91b4d0b9ed33b8d327459a73fe31766.png>)

We can evaluate our double integral as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
∬_(D)y^{2}dA &= ∫_{0}^{4}∫_{0}^{4 - y}y^{2}dzdy \\
&= ∫_{0}^{4}[∫_{0}^{4 - y}y^{2}dz]dy \\
&= ∫_{0}^{4}[y^{2}z]_{z = 0}^{z = 4 - y}dy \\
&= ∫_{0}^{4}y^{2}(4 - y)dy \\
&= ∫_{0}^{4}4y^{2} - y^{3}dy \\
&= [\frac{4}{3}y^{3} - \frac{1}{4}y^{4}]_{y = 0}^{y = 4} \\
&= \frac{4}{3}(64 - 0) - \frac{1}{4}(256 - 0) \\
&= \frac{64}{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="skipping-the-intermediate-step"></a>
## Skipping the Intermediate Step

When writing down a triple integral as a repeated integral, we can often skip the intermediate step of expressing our triple integral as a mixed integral.

For example, suppose we wish to evaluate

$$
\displaystyle \iiint\limits_R f(x,y,z) \: \mathrm{d}V
$$

where $R$ is the type II region given by

$$
R = \left\{(x,y,z) \:: \: {\color{red}a} \leq y \leq {\color{red}b}, \: {\color{blue}v_1(y)} \leq z \leq {\color{blue}v_2(y)}, \: {\color{purple}u_1(y,z)} \leq x \leq {\color{purple}u_2(y,z)} \right\}
$$

Notice that the projection of $R$ onto the $yz$-plane is a type I plane region. Therefore, we can immediately express our triple integral as a repeated integral as follows:

$$
\displaystyle \iiint \limits_{R} f(x,y,z)\: \textrm{d}V = \int_{\color{red}a}^{\color{red}b} \int_{\color{blue}v_1(y)}^{\color{blue}v_2(y)} \int_{\color{purple}u_1(y,z)}^{\color{purple}u_2(y,z)} f(x,y,z)\: \mathrm{d}x \: \mathrm{d}z \: \mathrm{d}y
$$

<a id="evaluating-a-triple-integral-over-a-type-ii-region-given-a-diagram"></a>
## Evaluating a Triple Integral Over a Type II Region Given a Diagram

**Example:** Evaluate the triple integral
$\displaystyle \iiint\limits_R 12z^2 \: \mathrm{d}V$
over the region $R$, shown above.

![](<../Source/3.3.5. Triple Integrals Over Type II Regions/Images/e1e0a37a01b5ecf240851daf1ff21f56.png>)

*Hint: All surfaces, except for the front one, are parallel to the corresponding coordinate planes.*

**Explanation**

![](<../Source/3.3.5. Triple Integrals Over Type II Regions/Images/a4f8d3e95c46f652b90983ba8bfa9d97.png>)

Notice that our solid can be written as the type II region

$$
R = \big\{(x,y,z) \:: \: (y,z) \in D, \:\: 0 \leq x \leq 3-y-z \big\}
$$

where

$$
D = \big\{(y,z) \:: \: 0 \leq y \leq 1, \:\: 0 \leq z \leq 1 \big\}
$$

is the projection of $R$ onto the $yz$-plane, shown below.

![](<../Source/3.3.5. Triple Integrals Over Type II Regions/Images/5b209d6360c4381ea8e24a7a857c8cba.png>)

Hence, we can express the region $R$ as

$$
R = \big\{(x,y,z) \:: \: 0 \leq y \leq 1, \:\: 0 \leq z \leq 1, \:\: 0 \leq x \leq 3-y-z \big\}
$$

As a result, by writing down our triple integral as a repeated integral, we obtain

$$
\iiint \limits_{R} 12z^2 \: \textrm{d}V = \int_{0}^{1} \int_{0}^{1} \int_{0}^{3-y-z} 12z^2 \: \mathrm{d}x \: \mathrm{d}z \: \mathrm{d}y
$$

First, we evaluate the inner integral with respect to $x$, treating $y$ and $z$ as constants:

$$
\begin{aligned}
∫_{0}^{1}∫_{0}^{1}∫_{0}^{3 - y - z}12z^{2}dxdzdy &= ∫_{0}^{1}∫_{0}^{1}[∫_{0}^{3 - y - z}12z^{2}dx]dzdy \\
&= ∫_{0}^{1}∫_{0}^{1}12z^{2}[x]_{x = 0}^{x = 3 - y - z}dzdy \\
&= ∫_{0}^{1}∫_{0}^{1}12z^{2}(3 - y - z)dzdy \\
&= ∫_{0}^{1}∫_{0}^{1}(36z^{2} - 12yz^{2} - 12z^{3})dzdy
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we evaluate the inner integral with respect to $z$, treating $y$ as a constant:

$$
\begin{aligned}
∫_{0}^{1}∫_{0}^{1}(36z^{2} - 12yz^{2} - 12z^{3})dzdy &= ∫_{0}^{1}[∫_{0}^{1}(36z^{2} - 12yz^{2} - 12z^{3})dz]dy \\
&= ∫_{0}^{1}[12z^{3} - 4yz^{3} - 3z^{4}]_{z = 0}^{z = 1}dy \\
&= ∫_{0}^{1}(12 - 4y - 3)dy \\
&= ∫_{0}^{1}(9 - 4y)dy
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we integrate with respect to $y{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
∫_{0}^{1}(9 - 4y)dy &= [9y - 2y^{2}]_{y = 0}^{y = 1} \\
&= (9 - 2) - 0 \\
&= 7
\end{aligned}
$$
