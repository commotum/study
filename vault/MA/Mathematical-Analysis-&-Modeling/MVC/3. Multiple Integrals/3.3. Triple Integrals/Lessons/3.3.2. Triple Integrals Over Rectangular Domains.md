## Introduction

Recall that the double integral of a continuous function $f(x,y)$ over a rectangular region

$$
R = [a,b]\times[c,d]
$$

denoted

$\iint\limits_{R}{f(x,y) \, \mathrm{d}A}$,
represents the total *signed* volume bounded between the surface $z=f(x,y)$ and the planes

$$
x=a, x=b, y=c
$$

and $y=d$.

We can extend the concept of multiple integrals to functions of three variables.

Now, suppose that $f(x,y,z)$ be a continuous function of three variables defined over the rectangular domain $R\subset \mathbb R^3$, given by

$$
R = [a,b] \times [c,d] \times [e,f]
$$

Then, the **triple integral** of $f(x,y,z)$ over $R$, denoted

$$
\iiint\limits_{R}{f(x,y,z) \, \mathrm{d}V}
$$

can be interpreted as a four-dimensional signed *hypervolume* bounded by the function $f$ and the *hyperplanes* $x=a$, $x=b$, $y=c$, $y=d$, $z=e$, and $z=f$ in four-dimesional space.

Similar to the case of double integrals, it can be shown that the triple integral

$$
\iiint\limits_R f(x,y,z) \: \mathrm{d}V
$$

can be evaluated using the repeated integral

$$
\int_a^b\int_c^d\int_e^f f(x,y,z) \: \mathrm{d}z\,\textrm d y\,\textrm d x
$$

Note that when the domain $R$ is rectangular, we can freely change the order of integration. For example, we can write

$$
\int_{\color{red}a}^{\color{red}b}\int_c^d\int_{\color{blue}e}^{\color{blue}f} f(x,y,z) \: \mathrm{d}{\color{blue}z}\,\textrm d y\,\textrm d {\color{red}x} = \int_{\color{blue}e}^{\color{blue}f}\int_c^d \int_{\color{red}a}^{\color{red}b} f(x,y,z) \: \textrm d {\color{red}x}\,\textrm d y\,\mathrm{d}{\color{blue}z}
$$

There are six possible orders in which we can evaluate a triple integral using a repeated integral:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}dxdydz & dydxdz & dzdxdy \\ dxdzdy & dydzdx & dzdydx\end{bmatrix}
$$

## A Worked Example

Let's evaluate the triple integral

$$
\iiint\limits_R x + y + z \:\textrm d V
$$

where the integration domain

$$
R = [0,1] \times [0,2] \times [0,2]
$$

First, we write our triple integral as a repeated integral. Let's integrate first with respect to $x$, then $y$, and then $z{:}$

$$
\int_0^2 \int_0^2 \int_0^1 x+ y +z \: \textrm{d}x\, \textrm{d}y\, \textrm{d}z
$$

Applying the sum rule, we get

$$
=∫_{0}^{2}∫_{0}^{2}∫_{0}^{1}xdxdydz + ∫_{0}^{2}∫_{0}^{2}∫_{0}^{1}ydxdydz + ∫_{0}^{2}∫_{0}^{2}∫_{0}^{1}zdxdydz
$$

Since $R$ is rectangular, we can apply the product rule, which gives

$$
∫_{0}^{1}xdx∫_{0}^{2}dy∫_{0}^{2}dz + ∫_{0}^{1}dx∫_{0}^{2}ydy∫_{0}^{2}dz + ∫_{0}^{1}dx∫_{0}^{2}dy∫_{0}^{2}zdz
$$

Let's evaluate each of these separately:

- For the first product, we have
$∫_{0}^{1}xdx∫_{0}^{2}dy∫_{0}^{2}dz|= [\frac{1}{2}x^{2}]_{0}^{1} \cdot (2 - 0) \cdot (2 - 0); = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 4; = 2$.
- For the second product, we have
$∫_{0}^{1}dx∫_{0}^{2}ydy∫_{0}^{2}dz|= (1 - 0) \cdot [\frac{1}{2}y^{2}]_{0}^{2} \cdot (2 - 0); = 1 \cdot 2 \cdot 2; = 4$.
- For the third product, we have
$∫_{0}^{1}dx∫_{0}^{2}dy∫_{0}^{2}zdz|= (1 - 0) \cdot (2 - 0) \cdot [\frac{1}{2}z^{2}]_{0}^{2}; = 2 \cdot 2; = 4$.

Therefore, we conclude that

$$
\iiint\limits_R x + y + z \:\textrm d V = 2+4+4 = 10
$$

## Writing a Triple Integral as a Repeated Integral

**Example:** Express the triple integral
$\displaystyle \iiint\limits_{R} (x+y+z)^2 \: \textrm{d}V$
over the box
$R = \bigg\{(x,y,z) \:: \: 0 \leq x \leq 1, \: -1 \leq y \leq 2, \: 0\leq z \leq 2 \bigg\}$
as a repeated integral of the form
$\displaystyle \int_a^b \int_c^d \int_e^f \ldots \: \textrm{d}y\: \textrm{d}z\: \textrm{d}x$.

**Explanation**

Given the limits for $x,y$, and $z$, we can express our triple integral as a repeated integral as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
∭_(R)(x + y + z)^{2}dV &= ∫_{0}^{1}∫_{0}^{2}∫_{-1}^{2}(x + y + z)^{2}dydzdx
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="evaluating-a-triple-integral-using-the-product-rule"></a>
## Evaluating a Triple Integral Using the Product Rule

**Example:** Evaluate the triple integral
$\displaystyle \iiint\limits_{R} y \cos{x} \sin{z} \: \textrm{d}V$
over the box $R = \bigg\{(x,y,z) \:: \: 0 \leq x \leq \dfrac{\pi}{2}, \: 0 \leq y \leq 1, \: 0 \leq z \leq \pi \bigg\}$.

**Explanation**

First, we express our triple integral as a repeated integral:

$$
\begin{aligned}
∭_(R)y\cos x\sin zdV &= ∫_{0}^{π}∫_{0}^{1}∫_{0}^{π/2}y\cos x\sin zdxdydz
\end{aligned}
$$

Since the domain of integration

$$
R = \left[0,\pi/2\right]\times[0,1]\times\left[0,\pi\right]
$$

is a rectangular box, and the integrand can be expressed as $f(x)\cdot g(y)\cdot h(z)$, we can rewrite our repeated integral as follows:

$$
∫_{0}^{π/2}\cos xdx∫_{0}^{1}ydy∫_{0}^{π}\sin zdz
$$

Evaluating each integral separately, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
∫_{0}^{π/2}\cos xdx∫_{0}^{1}ydy∫_{0}^{π}\sin zdz &= [\sin x]_{0}^{π/2} \cdot [\frac{y^{2}}{2}]_{0}^{1} \cdot [- \cos z]_{0}^{π} \\
&= (1 - 0) \cdot (\frac{1}{2} - 0) \cdot (1 - (-1)) \\
&= 1 \cdot \frac{1}{2} \cdot 2 \\
&= 1
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="evaluating-a-triple-integral"></a>
## Evaluating a Triple Integral

**Example:** By integrating first with respect to $z$, then $x$, and then $y$, evaluate the triple integral
$\displaystyle \iiint\limits_R z-xy \: \mathrm{d}V$
over the rectangular box $R = \left\{(x,y,z) \:: \:0 \leq x \leq 2, \: -1 \leq y \leq 1, \: 1 \leq z \leq 3 \right\}$.

**Explanation**

First, we express our triple integral as a repeated integral:

$$
\displaystyle \iiint \limits_{R} z-xy \: \textrm{d}V = \int_{-1}^{1} \int_{0}^{2} \int_{1}^{3} z-xy \: \mathrm{d}z \: \mathrm{d}x \: \mathrm{d}y
$$

Since we're integrating first with respect to $z$, then $x$, and then $y$, we write

$$
\textrm d V = \textrm d z\:\textrm d x\:\textrm d y
$$

First, we evaluate the inner integral with respect to $z$, treating $x$ and $y$ as constants:

$$
\begin{aligned}
∫_{-1}^{1}∫_{0}^{2}∫_{1}^{3}z - xydzdxdy &= ∫_{-1}^{1}∫_{0}^{2}[∫_{1}^{3}z - xydz]dxdy \\
&= ∫_{-1}^{1}∫_{0}^{2}[\frac{1}{2}z^{2} - xyz]_{z = 1}^{z = 3}dxdy \\
&= ∫_{-1}^{1}∫_{0}^{2}\frac{1}{2}(9 - 1) - xy(3 - 1)dxdy \\
&= ∫_{-1}^{1}∫_{0}^{2}4 - 2xydxdy
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we evaluate the inner integral with respect to $x$, treating $y$ as a constant:

$$
\begin{aligned}
∫_{-1}^{1}∫_{0}^{2}4 - 2xydxdy &= ∫_{-1}^{1}[∫_{0}^{2}4 - 2yxdx]dy \\
&= ∫_{-1}^{1}[4x - yx^{2}]_{x = 0}^{x = 2}dy \\
&= ∫_{-1}^{1}4(2 - 0) - y(2^{2} - 0)dy \\
&= ∫_{-1}^{1}8 - 4ydy
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we integrate with respect to $y{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
∫_{-1}^{1}8 - 4ydy &= [8y - 2y^{2}]_{y =-1}^{y = 1} \\
&= 8(1 - (-1)) - 2(1^{2} - (-1)^{2}) \\
&= 8 \cdot 2 - 2(1 - 1) \\
&= 16 - 0 \\
&= 16
\end{aligned}
$$
