## Introduction

Suppose that the transformation $\mathbf T$, given by

$$
\mathbf T: (u,v,w) \longrightarrow \left(x(u,v,w), \, y(u,v,w), \, z(u,v,w) \right)
$$

is a $C^1$ transformation that has no critical points (i.e., it is invertible) inside some region $\Delta\subset \mathbb R^3$, and that

$$
\mathbf T(\Delta) = R
$$

as shown below.

![](<../Source/3.5.7. Computing Triple Integrals Using a Change of Variables/Images/c576965ed01ce8c9f5f4f30fd2b9a7f7.png>)

To compute the integral of a function $f(x,y,z)$ over $R$, we use the change of variables formula

$$
\iiint\limits_{R} f(x,y,z) \ \mathrm{d}V = \iiint\limits_{\Delta} \ f(x(u,v,w), y(u,v,w), z(u,v,w)) \: \left| \dfrac{\partial (x, y, z)}{\partial (u, v, w)} \right| \: \textrm{d}u \textrm{d}v \textrm{d}w
$$

where

$$
\dfrac{\partial (x, y, z)}{\partial (u, v, w)}
$$

is the Jacobian determinant corresponding to $\mathbf{T}$.

Note the similarity with the change of variables formula for double integrals.

## Performing a Change of Variables in a Triple Integral

**Example:** Consider the finite region $R$ in Cartesian $xyz$-space, given by
$R = \big\{(x,y,z) \:: \:1 \leq xy \leq 2, \: 1 \leq y \leq 2, \: 0 \leq z\leq 1 \big\}$.

By performing the change of variables

$u=xy, \qquad v=y, \qquad w=2z$,

write down the triple integral

$\displaystyle \iiint\limits_R 3xy^2+6yz \, \mathrm{d}V$

as a repeated integral in terms of $u$, $v$, and $w$.

**Explanation**

Let's define a transformation $\mathbf{T}$ as follows:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}u \\ v \\ w\end{bmatrix}
$$

This transformation maps some region $\Delta$ in the $uvw$-space to our region $R$ in the $xyz$-space.

To find the required integral, we can use the change of variables formula

$$
\iiint\limits_{R} f(x,y,z) \ \mathrm{d}V = \iiint\limits_{\Delta} \ f(x(u,v,w), y(u,v,w), z(u,v,w)) \: \left| \dfrac{\partial (x, y, z)}{\partial (u, v, w)} \right| \: \textrm{d}u \textrm{d}v \textrm{d}w
$$

where

$$
\dfrac{\partial (x, y, z)}{\partial (u, v, w)}
$$

is the Jacobian determinant corresponding to $\mathbf{T}$.

Note that the change of variables

$$
u=xy, \qquad v=y, \qquad w=2z
$$

gives us the *inverse* function $\mathbf T^{-1}$, that is

$$
\begin{bmatrix}x \\ y \\ z\end{bmatrix}
$$

To find the required integral, we proceed in four steps:

**Step 1**: Find $\Delta$, which is the image of $R$ under the action of $\mathbf{T}^{-1}$.

Our domain in the $uvw$-space is

$$
\begin{aligned}
Δ &= {(u, v, w): 1 \le u \le 2, 1 \le v \le 2, 0 \le w \le 2}
\end{aligned}
$$

**Step 2**: Compute the Jacobian determinant corresponding to $\mathbf T^{-1}{:}$

The Jacobian determinant corresponding to $\mathbf T^{-1}$ is

$$
\begin{aligned}
(∂(u, v, w))/(∂(x, y, z)) &= \begin{vmatrix}\frac{∂u}{∂x} & \frac{∂u}{∂y} & \frac{∂u}{∂z} \\ \frac{∂v}{∂x} & \frac{∂v}{∂y} & \frac{∂v}{∂z} \\ \frac{∂w}{∂x} & \frac{∂w}{∂y} & \frac{∂w}{∂z}\end{vmatrix} \\
&=\begin{vmatrix}y & x & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 2\end{vmatrix} \\
&= 2\begin{vmatrix}y & x \\ 0 & 1\end{vmatrix} \\
&= 2(y - 0) \\
&= 2y \\
&= 2v
\end{aligned}
$$

**Step 3**: Compute the Jacobian determinant corresponding to $\mathbf T{:}$

The Jacobian determinant corresponding to $\mathbf T$ is

$$
\dfrac{\partial (x, y, z)}{\partial (u, v, w)} = \left(\dfrac{\partial (u, v, w)}{\partial (x, y, z)} \right)^{-1} =\dfrac{1}{2v}
$$

Note that

$$
\dfrac{\partial (x, y, z)}{\partial (u, v, w)} \neq 0
$$

everywhere inside $\Delta$. In other words, $\mathbf{T}$ has no critical points inside $\Delta$.

**Step 4**: Perform the change of variables:

$$
\begin{aligned}
∭_(R)3xy^{2} + 6yzdV &= ∭_(Δ)(3uv + 3vw) \cdot\begin{vmatrix}(∂(x, y, z))/(∂(u, v, w)) & dudvdw \\ = ∭_(Δ)3v(u + w) \cdot & \frac{1}{2v}\end{vmatrix}dudvdw \\
&= ∫_{1}^{2}∫_{1}^{2}∫_{0}^{1}\frac{3}{2}(u + w)dwdvdu
\end{aligned}
$$

## Evaluating a Triple Integral Using a Change of Variables

**Example:** Use the change of variables
$u = x+y^2, \qquad v= y+z, \qquad w = 2z$
to evaluate the triple integral
$\displaystyle \iiint \limits_R (x+y^2)^{15}z \: \mathrm{d}V$,
where the region $R$ is given by
$R = \big\{(x,y,z) \:: \: 0 \leq x+y^2 \leq 1, \: 0 \leq y+z \leq 4, \: 0\leq z\leq 2 \big\}$.

**Explanation**

Let's define a transformation $\mathbf{T}$ as follows:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}u \\ v \\ w\end{bmatrix}
$$

This transformation maps some region $\Delta$ in the $uvw$-space to our region $R$ in the $xyz$-space.

To compute the required integral, we can use the change of variables formula

$$
\iiint\limits_{R} f(x,y,z) \ \mathrm{d}V = \iiint\limits_{\Delta} \ f(x(u,v,w), y(u,v,w), z(u,v,w)) \: \left| \dfrac{\partial (x, y, z)}{\partial (u, v, w)} \right| \: \textrm{d}u \textrm{d}v \textrm{d}w
$$

where

$$
\dfrac{\partial (x, y, z)}{\partial (u, v, w)}
$$

is the Jacobian determinant corresponding to $\mathbf{T}$.

Note that the change of variables

$$
u = x+y^2, \qquad v= y+z, \qquad w = 2z
$$

gives us the *inverse* function $\mathbf T^{-1}$, that is

$$
\begin{bmatrix}x \\ y \\ z\end{bmatrix}
$$

To compute the required integral, we proceed in four steps:

**Step 1**: Find $\Delta$, which is the image of $R$ under the action of $\mathbf{T}^{-1}$.

Our domain in the $uvw$-space is

$$
\begin{aligned}
Δ &= {(u, v, w): 0 \le u \le 1, 0 \le v \le 4, 0 \le w \le 4}
\end{aligned}
$$

**Step 2**: Compute the Jacobian determinant corresponding to $\mathbf T^{-1}{:}$

The Jacobian determinant corresponding to $\mathbf T^{-1}$ is

$$
\begin{aligned}
(∂(u, v, w))/(∂(x, y, z)) &= \begin{vmatrix}\frac{∂u}{∂x} & \frac{∂u}{∂y} & \frac{∂u}{∂z} \\ \frac{∂v}{∂x} & \frac{∂v}{∂y} & \frac{∂v}{∂z} \\ \frac{∂w}{∂x} & \frac{∂w}{∂y} & \frac{∂w}{∂z}\end{vmatrix} \\
&=\begin{vmatrix}1 & 2y & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 2\end{vmatrix} \\
&= 1 \cdot 1 \cdot 2 \\
&= 2
\end{aligned}
$$

**Step 3**: Compute the Jacobian determinant corresponding to $\mathbf T{:}$

The Jacobian determinant corresponding to $\mathbf T$ is

$$
\dfrac{\partial (x, y, z)}{\partial (u, v, w)} = \left(\dfrac{\partial (u, v, w)}{\partial (x, y, z)} \right)^{-1} = \dfrac{1}{2}
$$

Note that

$$
\dfrac{\partial (x, y, z)}{\partial (u, v, w)} \neq 0
$$

everywhere inside $\Delta$. In other words, $\mathbf{T}$ has no critical points inside $\Delta$.

**Step 4**: Perform the change of variables:

$$
\begin{aligned}
∭_(R)(x + y^{2})^{15}zdV &= ∭_(Δ)u^{15} \cdot \frac{w}{2} \cdot \mid \frac{1}{2} \mid dudvdw \\
&= \frac{1}{4}∭_(Δ)u^{15}wdudvdw \\
&= \frac{1}{4}∫_{0}^{1}∫_{0}^{4}∫_{0}^{4}u^{15}wdwdvdu \\
&= \frac{1}{4}∫_{0}^{1}u^{15}du \cdot ∫_{0}^{4}dv \cdot ∫_{0}^{4}wdw \\
&= \frac{1}{4} \cdot [\frac{u^{16}}{16}]_{u = 0}^{u = 1} \cdot [v]_{v = 0}^{v = 4} \cdot [\frac{w^{2}}{2}]_{w = 0}^{w = 4} \\
&= \frac{1}{4} \cdot (\frac{1}{16} - 0) \cdot (4 - 0) \cdot (8 - 0) \\
&= \frac{1}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Computing the Volume of a Region Using a Change of Variables

**Example:** Find the volume bounded by the region $R$, given by
$R = \big\{(x,y,z) \:: \: 0 \leq x+y \leq 1, \:-2 \leq y-2x \leq 2, \: 1 \leq z^3 \leq 8 \big\}$.

**Explanation**

Recall that the volume of $R$ can be found as

$$
V = \iiint\limits_R \textrm{d}V
$$

Let's define a transformation $\mathbf{T}$ as follows:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}u \\ v \\ w\end{bmatrix}
$$

This transformation maps some region $\Delta$ in the $uvw$-space to our region $R$ in the $xyz$-space.

To compute the required volume, we can use the change of variables formula

$$
\iiint\limits_{R} f(x,y,z) \ \mathrm{d}V = \iiint\limits_{\Delta} \ f(x(u,v,w), y(u,v,w), z(u,v,w)) \: \left| \dfrac{\partial (x, y, z)}{\partial (u, v, w)} \right| \: \textrm{d}u \textrm{d}v \textrm{d}w
$$

where

$$
\dfrac{\partial (x, y, z)}{\partial (u, v, w)}
$$

is the Jacobian determinant corresponding to $\mathbf{T}$.

The inequalities that define the region $R$,

$$
0 \leq x+y \leq 1, \qquad -2 \leq y-2x \leq 2, \qquad 1 \leq z^3 \leq 8
$$

suggest that we use the change of variables

$$
u = x+y, \qquad v = y-2x, \qquad w = z^3
$$

This change of variables gives us the *inverse* function $\mathbf T^{-1}$, that is

$$
\begin{bmatrix}x \\ y \\ z\end{bmatrix}
$$

To compute the required volume, we proceed in four steps:

**Step 1**: Find $\Delta$, which is the image of $R$ under the action of $\mathbf{T}^{-1}$.

Our domain in the $uvw$-space is

$$
\begin{aligned}
Δ &= {(u, v, w): 0 \le u \le 1,-2 \le v \le 2, 1 \le w \le 8}
\end{aligned}
$$

**Step 2**: Compute the Jacobian determinant corresponding to $\mathbf T^{-1}{:}$

The Jacobian determinant corresponding to $\mathbf T^{-1}$ is

$$
\begin{aligned}
(∂(u, v, w))/(∂(x, y, z)) &= \begin{vmatrix}\frac{∂u}{∂x} & \frac{∂u}{∂y} & \frac{∂u}{∂z} \\ \frac{∂v}{∂x} & \frac{∂v}{∂y} & \frac{∂v}{∂z} \\ \frac{∂w}{∂x} & \frac{∂w}{∂y} & \frac{∂w}{∂z}\end{vmatrix} \\
&=\begin{vmatrix}1 & 1 & 0 \\ -2 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 3z^{2}\end{vmatrix} \\
&= 3z^{2}(-1)^{3 + 3}\begin{vmatrix}1 & 1 \\ -2 & 1\end{vmatrix} \\
&= 9z^{2} \\
&= 9w^{2/3}
\end{aligned}
$$

**Step 3**: Compute the Jacobian determinant corresponding to $\mathbf T{:}$

The Jacobian determinant corresponding to $\mathbf T$ is

$$
\dfrac{\partial (x, y, z)}{\partial (u, v, w)} = \left(\dfrac{\partial (u, v, w)}{\partial (x, y, z)} \right)^{-1} = \dfrac 19 w^{-2/3}
$$

Note that

$$
\dfrac{\partial (x, y, z)}{\partial (u, v, w)} \neq 0
$$

everywhere inside $\Delta$. In other words, $\mathbf{T}$ has no critical points inside $\Delta$.

**Step 4**: Perform the change of variables:

$$
\begin{aligned}
∭_(R)dV &= ∭_(Δ)1 \cdot \mid \frac{1}{9}w^{-2/3} \mid dudvdw \\
&= ∫_{0}^{1}∫_{-2}^{2}∫_{1}^{8}\frac{1}{9}w^{-2/3}dwdvdu \\
&= \frac{1}{9}∫_{0}^{1}du \cdot ∫_{-2}^{2}dv \cdot ∫_{1}^{8}w^{-2/3}dw \\
&= \frac{1}{9}[u]_{0}^{1} \cdot [v]_{-2}^{2} \cdot [3w^{1/3}]_{1}^{8} \\
&= \frac{1}{9} \cdot (1 - 0) \cdot (2 - (-2)) \cdot 3(8^{1/3} - 1^{1/3}) \\
&= \frac{4}{3}
\end{aligned}
$$
