## Introduction

Consider the planar curve $y=e^{-x}$ defined on the interval $x \in [0,2]$, as shown below.

![](<../Source/5.2.5. Surfaces of Revolution/Images/538b0648008dc51ca23212958fc9b36e.png>)

By rotating this curve $2\pi$ radians about the $x$-axis, we get a **surface of revolution**:

![](<../Source/5.2.5. Surfaces of Revolution/Images/aac7635321d34cda1accf713655e35a4.png>)

We can parametrize this surface in the following way:

- We take a point $P$ on the surface with $x$-coordinate equal to $x_0$ for some fixed $x_0 \in [0,2]$.
- Now, by taking the intersection of the surface with the plane $x=x_0$, we get a circle of radius $e^{-x_0}$.![](<../Source/5.2.5. Surfaces of Revolution/Images/ebc823814cb857960a0f4df53c84f833.png>)
This circle can be parameterized as follows:
$[MATH: \mathbf r(x_0, \theta) = \big\langle {\color{black}x_0}, \: e^{-x_0} \, \cos{\theta}, \: e^{-x_0} \, \sin{\theta} \big\rangle, \quad \theta \in [0,2\pi).]$

- Finally, by allowing $x_0$ to vary over the interval $[0, 2]$, these circles will trace out the surface of revolution. Therefore, our parameterization is

$[MATH: \mathbf{r}(x,\theta) = \big\langle x, \: e^{-x} \cos{\theta}, \: e^{-x}\sin{\theta} \big\rangle, \quad x \in [0,2], \quad \theta \in [0,2\pi).]$

Let's now describe some general formulas for parametrizing surfaces of revolution.

<a id="parametrizing-a-surface-generated-by-rotating-yyx-about-the-x-axis"></a>
## Parametrizing a Surface Generated by Rotating y=y(x) About the x-Axis

Consider the point $(x,y(x),0)$ that lies on the curve $y=y(x)$ in the $xy$-plane. Rotating this point by $2\pi$ radians about the $x$-axis generates a circle, as shown below.

![](<../Source/5.2.5. Surfaces of Revolution/Images/f2051d3bcd02a1cef1abe16f067f18b9.png>)

In the diagram above, the pair $(\textrm{circle}_1,\textrm{circle}_2)$ denotes any parametrization of a circle of radius $y(x)$ with parameter $x$.

In general, given a curve

$$
y=y(x)\geq 0
$$

for $x \in [a,b]$ that is rotated $2\pi$ radians about the $x$-axis, the corresponding surface of revolution can be parametrized as
$[MATH: \mathbf{r} = \big\langle x, \: y(x) \cos\theta, \: y(x) \sin\theta \big\rangle, \quad x\in[a,b], \quad \theta\in [0,2\pi).]$

Since a parametrization of a circle is not unique, we can obtain different parameterizations of our surface of revolution. For example, the following is also a valid parameterization:
$[MATH: \mathbf{r} = \big\langle x, \: y(x) \sin\theta, \: y(x) \cos\theta \big\rangle, \quad x\in[a,b], \quad \theta\in [0,2\pi)]$

## Parametrizing a Surface Generated by Rotating z=z(x) About the x-Axis

Now consider the point $(x,0,z(x))$ that lies on a curve $z=z(x)$ in the $xz$-plane. Rotating this point by $2\pi$ radians about the $x$-axis generates a circle, as shown below.

![](<../Source/5.2.5. Surfaces of Revolution/Images/bd7044fb5ed4555ee3142b645d642e98.png>)

In general, given a curve

$$
z=z(x)\geq 0
$$

for $x \in [a,b]$ that is rotated $2\pi$ radians about the $x$-axis, the corresponding surface of revolution can be parametrized as
$[MATH: \mathbf{r} = \big\langle x, \: z(x) \cos\theta, \: z(x) \sin\theta \big\rangle, \quad z\in[a,b], \quad \theta\in [0,2\pi).]$

Again, this parametrization is not unique. An alternative parametrization is
$[MATH: \mathbf{r} = \big\langle x, \: z(x) \sin\theta, \: z(x) \cos\theta \big\rangle, \quad z\in[a,b], \quad \theta\in [0,2\pi).]$

## Finding a Parametrization of a Surface of Revolution: Rotation About the x-Axis

**Example:** The vector-valued function $\mathbf{r}(x,\theta)$ parametrizes the surface of revolution generated by rotating the curve $y=x^2$ by $2\pi$ radians about the $x$-axis for $x \in \left[0, 1\right]$. What is $\mathbf{r}$?

**Explanation**

![](<../Source/5.2.5. Surfaces of Revolution/Images/34f3efad20bb1b36ba124e1cf3ca11d1.png>)

A point $(x,y,z)$ on the surface generated by rotating the graph of $y=f(x)$ by $2\pi$ radians about the $x$-axis for $x\in[a,b]$ has the position vector

$[MATH: r = ⟨x, f(x)\cos θ, f(x)\sin θ⟩, x ∈ [a, b], θ ∈ [0, 2π).]$

First, notice that

$$
y=f(x) = x^2 \geq 0
$$

for all

$$
x \in \left[0,1\right]
$$

So, the parametrization of our surface of revolution is

$[MATH: \mathbf{r}(x,\theta) = \big\langle x, \: x^2 \cos\theta, \: x^2\sin\theta \big\rangle, \quad x \in \left[0, 1\right], \quad \theta \in [0,2\pi).]$

<a id="the-fundamental-vector-product-of-a-surface-of-revolution"></a>
## The Fundamental Vector Product of a Surface of Revolution

**Example:** The fundamental vector product of the surface of revolution generated by rotating the curve $y=e^{x/2}$ by $2\pi$ radians about the $x$-axis for $x \in [0,2]$ is given by
$\big\langle \text{_______}\,, \:-e^{x/2} \cos\theta, \: -e^{x/2}\sin\theta \big\rangle$.

What is the $1$st component of the product?

**Explanation**

Notice that

$$
y=e^{x/2} \geq 0
$$

for all $x \in [0,2]$. The parametrization of our surface of revolution is

$$
r = ⟨x, f(x)\cos θ, f(x)\sin θ⟩, (x, θ) ∈ D
$$

where

$$
f(x)=e^{x/2}
$$

and

$$
D = \big\{(x,\theta) \:: \: 0 \leq x \leq 2, \: 0 \leq \theta \lt 2\pi \big\}
$$

We now compute the tangent vectors to the grid curves:

$$
\begin{aligned}
r_{x}^{′} &= ⟨\frac{∂x}{∂x}, \frac{∂y}{∂x}, \frac{∂z}{∂x}⟩ = ⟨1, f^{′}\cos θ, f^{′}\sin θ⟩ \\
r_{θ}^{′} &= ⟨\frac{∂x}{∂θ}, \frac{∂y}{∂θ}, \frac{∂z}{∂θ}⟩ = ⟨0,-f\sin θ, f\cos θ⟩
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, the fundamental vector product is

$$
\begin{aligned}
r_{x}^{′} \cdot r_{θ}^{′} &= \begin{vmatrix}i & j & k \\ 1 & f^{′}\cos θ & f^{′}\sin θ \\ 0 & -f\sin θ & f\cos θ\end{vmatrix} \\
&= ⟨\begin{vmatrix}f^{′}\cos θ & f^{′}\sin θ \\ -f\sin θ & f\cos θ\end{vmatrix}, -\begin{vmatrix}1 & f^{′}\sin θ \\ 0 & f\cos θ\end{vmatrix},\begin{vmatrix}1 & f^{′}\cos θ \\ 0 & -f\sin θ\end{vmatrix}⟩ \\
&= ⟨ff^{′}\cos^{2} θ + ff^{′}\sin^{2} θ,-f\cos θ,-f\sin θ⟩ \\
&= ⟨ff^{′},-f\cos θ,-f\sin θ⟩
\end{aligned}
$$

Since

$$
f=e^{x/2}
$$

we obtain

$$
f' = \dfrac12 e^{x/2}
$$

So, finally, we conclude that the fundamental vector product is

$$
r_{x}^{′} \cdot r_{θ}^{′} = ⟨\frac{1}{2}e^{x},-e^{x/2}\cos θ,-e^{x/2}\sin θ⟩
$$

Therefore, the $1$st component of the product is

$$
\dfrac{1}{2}e^x
$$

<a id="computing-a-surface-area-of-revolution"></a>
## Computing a Surface Area of Revolution

Recall that if $S$ is a surface parametrized by a differentiable vector-valued function

$$
\mathbf{r}(u,v) = \big\langle x(u,v), \: y(u,v), \: z(u,v) \big\rangle
$$

defined over a region $D$ in the $uv$-plane, then the surface area of $S$ is given by

$$
\iint\limits_{S} \: \textrm{d}S = \iint\limits_{D} \| \mathbf{r}'_ u \times \mathbf{r}'_v \| \: \textrm{d}A
$$

where $\mathbf{r}'_ u \times \mathbf{r}'_v$ is the fundamental vector product of the surface.

Let's apply this formula to find the surface area generated by a surface of revolution.

---

<a id="calculating-a-surface-of-revolution"></a>
## Calculating a Surface of Revolution

**Example:** Use a fundamental vector product to find an integral expression for the surface area generated when the curve $x=\sin z$ is rotated by $2\pi$ radians about the $z$-axis for $z \in [0, \pi]$.

**Explanation**

Notice that

$$
x=\sin z \geq 0
$$

for all $z \in [0, \pi]$. The parametrization of our surface of revolution is

$$
r = ⟨f(z)\cos θ, f(z)\sin θ, z⟩, (z, θ) ∈ D
$$

where

$$
f(z)=\sin z
$$

and

$$
D = \big\{(z,\theta) \,: \, 0 \leq z \leq \pi, \, 0 \leq \theta \leq 2\pi\big\}
$$

We now compute the tangent vectors to the grid curves:

$$
\begin{aligned}
r_{z}^{′} &= ⟨\frac{∂x}{∂z}, \frac{∂y}{∂z}, \frac{∂z}{∂z}⟩ = ⟨f^{′}\cos θ, f^{′}\sin θ, 1⟩ \\
r_{θ}^{′} &= ⟨\frac{∂x}{∂θ}, \frac{∂y}{∂θ}, \frac{∂z}{∂θ}⟩ = ⟨ - f\sin θ, f\cos θ, 0⟩
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, the fundamental vector product is

$$
\begin{aligned}
r_{z}^{′} \cdot r_{θ}^{′} &= \begin{vmatrix}i & j & k \\ f^{′}\cos θ & f^{′}\sin θ & 1 \\ -f\sin θ & f\cos θ & 0\end{vmatrix} \\
&= ⟨\begin{vmatrix}f^{′}\sin θ & 1 \\ f\cos θ & 0\end{vmatrix}, -\begin{vmatrix}f^{′}\cos θ & 1 \\ -f\sin θ & 0\end{vmatrix},\begin{vmatrix}f^{′}\cos θ & f^{′}\sin θ \\ -f\sin θ & f\cos θ\end{vmatrix}⟩ \\
&= ⟨ - f\cos θ,-f\sin θ, f^{′}f\cos^{2} θ + f^{′}f\sin^{2} θ⟩ \\
&= ⟨ - f\cos θ,-f\sin θ, f^{′}f⟩
\end{aligned}
$$

and

$$
\begin{aligned}
‖r_{z}^{′} \cdot r_{θ}^{′}‖ &= \sqrt{(-f\cos θ)^{2} + (-f\sin θ)^{2} + (f^{′}f)^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{f^{2}(\cos^{2} θ + \sin^{2} θ) + f^{2}(f^{′})^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{f^{2} + f^{2}(f^{′})^{2}} \\
&= f\sqrt{1 + (f^{′})^{2}}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the surface area is

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= ∬_(S)dS \\
&= ∬_(D)‖r_{z}^{′} \cdot r_{θ}^{′}‖dzdθ \\
&= ∬_(D)f\sqrt{1 + (f^{′})^{2}}dzdθ \\
&= ∫_{0}^{π}f\sqrt{1 + (f^{′})^{2}}∫_{0}^{2π}dθdz \\
&= ∫_{0}^{π}f\sqrt{1 + (f^{′})^{2}} \cdot θ \mid _{0}^{2π}dz \\
&= 2π∫_{0}^{π}f\sqrt{1 + (f^{′})^{2}}dz
\end{aligned}
$$

Since

$$
f(z)=\sin z
$$

we obtain

$$
f'(z) = \cos z
$$

So, finally, we conclude that our expression for the surface area is

$$
\begin{aligned}
2π∫_{0}^{π}f\sqrt{1 + (f^{′})^{2}}dz &= 2π∫_{0}^{π}\sin z\sqrt{1 + (\cos z)^{2}}dz \\
&= 2π∫_{0}^{π}\sin z\sqrt{1 + \cos^{2} z}dz
\end{aligned}
$$
