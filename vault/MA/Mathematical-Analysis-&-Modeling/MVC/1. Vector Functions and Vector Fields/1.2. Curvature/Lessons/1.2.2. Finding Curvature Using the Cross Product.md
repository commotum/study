## Introduction

Recall that if the curve $\mathbf r(s)$ is parametrized by arc length $s$, then the curvature of $\mathbf{r}$ is defined as

$$
\kappa(s) = \left\| \dfrac{\textrm{d}\mathbf T}{\textrm{d}s} \right\|
$$

Moreover, if $\mathbf r(t)$ is parametrized by a general parameter $t$, we have the following formula for the curvature:

$$
\kappa(t) = \dfrac{\|\mathbf T'(t)\|}{\|\mathbf r'(t)\|}
$$

where

$$
\mathbf T(t) = \dfrac{\mathbf r'(t)}{\|\mathbf r'(t)\|}
$$

is the unit tangent vector to the curve.

It can be shown that the curvature of $\mathbf r(t)$ can also be found using the following cross product formula:

$$
\kappa(t) = \dfrac{\|\mathbf r'(t) \times \mathbf r''(t)\|}{\|\mathbf r'(t)\|^3}
$$

We'll derive this formula at the end of the lesson. For now, let's get some practice at applying it.

<a id="finding-the-curvature-of-a-curve-at-a-point"></a>
## Finding the Curvature of a Curve at a Point

**Example:** Using the cross product curvature formula, find the curvature of $\mathbf r(t) = \langle t, -2t, t^2 \rangle$ at the point where $t = 1$.

**Explanation**

To compute the curvature $\kappa (t)$, we will use the following formula:

$$
\kappa(t) = \dfrac{\| \mathbf r'(t) \times \mathbf r''(t) \|}{\|\mathbf r'(t)\|^3}
$$

First, we calculate $\mathbf r'(t)$, $\mathbf r'(1)$, and $\|\mathbf r'(1)\|$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
r^{′}(t) &= ⟨\frac{d}{dt}(t), \frac{d}{dt}(-2t), \frac{d}{dt}(t^{2})⟩ \\
&= ⟨1,-2, 2t⟩ \\
r^{′}(1) &= ⟨1,-2, 2⟩ \\
‖r^{′}(1)‖ &= \sqrt{1^{2} + (-2)^{2} + 2^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{9} \\
&= 3
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we find $\mathbf r''(t)$ and $\mathbf r''(1)$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
r^{″}(t) &= ⟨\frac{d}{dt}(1), \frac{d}{dt}(-2), 2\frac{d}{dt}(t)⟩ \\
&= ⟨0, 0, 2⟩ \\
r^{″}(1) &= ⟨0, 0, 2⟩
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we compute the cross product and its magnitude:

$$
\begin{aligned}
r^{′}(1) \cdot r^{″}(1) &= \begin{vmatrix}i & j & k \\ 1 & -2 & 2 \\ 0 & 0 & 2\end{vmatrix} \\
&=\begin{vmatrix}-2 & 2 \\ 0 & 2\end{vmatrix}i -\begin{vmatrix}1 & 2 \\ 0 & 2\end{vmatrix}j +\begin{vmatrix}1 & -2 \\ 0 & 0\end{vmatrix}k \\
&=-4i - 2j \\
‖r^{′}(1) \cdot r^{″}(1)‖ &= \sqrt{(-4)^{2} + (-2)^{2} + 0^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{20} \\
&= 2\sqrt{5}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the curvature at $t=1$ is

$$
\begin{aligned}
κ(1) &= (‖r^{′}(1) \cdot r^{″}(1)‖)/(‖r^{′}(1)‖^{3}) = \frac{2\sqrt{5}}{3^{3}} = \frac{2\sqrt{5}}{27}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding the Curvature of a Curve at a General Point

**Example:** Given the curve $\mathbf r(t)=\left\langle t^2, \: 2t, \: 1 \right\rangle$, use the cross product curvature formula to find the curvature of this curve as a function of $t$.

**Explanation**

The curvature $\kappa (t)$ is given by

$$
\kappa(t) = \dfrac{\| \mathbf r'(t) \times \mathbf r''(t) \|}{\|\mathbf r'(t)\|^3}
$$

First, we calculate $\mathbf r'(t)$ and $\| \mathbf r'(t) \|$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
r^{′}(t) &= ⟨\frac{d}{dt}(t^{2}), \frac{d}{dt}(2t), \frac{d}{dt}(1)⟩ \\
&= ⟨2t, 2, 0⟩ \\
‖r^{′}(t)‖ &= \sqrt{(2t)^{2} + (2)^{2} + 0^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{4t^{2} + 4} \\
&= \sqrt{4(t^{2} + 1)} \\
&= 2\sqrt{t^{2} + 1}
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we find the second derivative $\mathbf{r}''(t)$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
r^{″}(t) &= ⟨\frac{d}{dt}(2t), \frac{d}{dt}(2), \frac{d}{dt}(0)⟩ = ⟨2, 0, 0⟩
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we compute the cross-product:

$$
\begin{aligned}
r^{′}(t) \cdot r^{″}(t) &= ⟨2t, 2, 0⟩ \cdot ⟨2, 0, 0⟩ \\
&=\begin{vmatrix}i & j & k \\ 2t & 2 & 0 \\ 2 & 0 & 0\end{vmatrix} \\
&=\begin{vmatrix}2 & 0 \\ 0 & 0\end{vmatrix}i -\begin{vmatrix}2t & 0 \\ 2 & 0\end{vmatrix}j +\begin{vmatrix}2t & 2 \\ 2 & 0\end{vmatrix}k \\
&=-4k
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we compute the magnitude of the above:

$$
\begin{aligned}
‖r^{′}(t) \cdot r^{″}(t)‖ &= 4
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the curvature is

$$
\begin{aligned}
κ(t) &= (‖r^{′}(t) \cdot r^{″}(t)‖)/(‖r^{′}(t)‖^{3}) = (4)/(8(t^{2} + 1)^{3/2}) = (1)/(2(t^{2} + 1)^{3/2})
\end{aligned}
$$

## Applying the Cross Product Formula to Plane Curves

Suppose we have a plane curve $C$ that is parametrized by the vector-valued function

$$
\mathbf{r}(t) = \langle x(t), \: y(t) \rangle
$$

At first glance, it looks as though the cross product curvature formula cannot be applied since cross products apply to three-dimensional vectors, whereas the given vector $\mathbf{r}(t)$ has only two dimensions.

However, this curve can also be viewed as a curve in three-dimensional space that lies on the plane $z=0$. In this case, $\mathbf{r}(t)$ can be considered as a vector-valued function with $z$-component equal to zero:

$$
\mathbf r(t)= \langle x(t), \: y(t), \: 0 \rangle
$$

As a result, the curvature $\kappa$ of our plane curve $C$ can be calculated using our cross product formula:

$$
\kappa(t)=\dfrac{\|{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}(t)}\times {\mathbf{r}^{\prime\prime}(t)}\|}{\|{\mathbf{r}^{\prime}(t)}\|^3}
$$

<a id="finding-the-curvature-of-a-plane-curve"></a>
## Finding the Curvature of a Plane Curve

**Example:** Using the cross product formula for curvature, find the curvature of $\mathbf r(t) = \langle 2t, 1+t^2\rangle$ as a function of $t$.

**Explanation**

To compute the curvature $\kappa (t)$, we will use the following formula:

$$
\kappa(t) = \dfrac{\| \mathbf r'(t) \times \mathbf r''(t) \|}{\|\mathbf r'(t)\|^3}
$$

First, we calculate $\mathbf r'(t)$ and $\|\mathbf r'(t)\|$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
r^{′}(t) &= ⟨\frac{d}{dt}(2t), \frac{d}{dt}(1 + t^{2})⟩ \\
&= ⟨2, 2t⟩ \\
‖r^{′}(t)‖ &= \sqrt{2^{2} + (2t)^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{4 + 4t^{2}} \\
&= 2\sqrt{1 + t^{2}}
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we find the second derivative:

$$
\begin{aligned}
r^{″}(t) &= ⟨\frac{d}{dt}(2), \frac{d}{dt}(2t)⟩ = ⟨0, 2⟩
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we compute the cross product by considering $\mathbf r'(t)$ and $\mathbf r''(t)$ as $3$-dimensional vectors with zero $z$-components:

$$
\begin{aligned}
r^{′}(t) \cdot r^{″}(t) &= ⟨2, 2t, 0⟩ \cdot ⟨0, 2, 0⟩ \\
&=\begin{vmatrix}i & j & k \\ 2 & 2t & 0 \\ 0 & 2 & 0\end{vmatrix} \\
&= 2\begin{vmatrix}2t \\ 0\end{vmatrix}2k \\
&= 4k
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the magnitude of the cross product is

$$
\| \mathbf r'(t) \times \mathbf r''(t) \| = 4
$$

and therefore, the curvature is

$$
\begin{aligned}
κ(t) &= (‖r^{′}(t) \cdot r^{″}(t)‖)/(‖r^{′}(t)‖^{3}) = (4)/((2\sqrt{1 + t^{2}})^{3}) = (1)/(2(1 + t^{2})^{3/2})
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="deriving-the-cross-product-formula"></a>
## Deriving the Cross Product Formula

Recall that if the curve $\mathbf r(t)$ is parametrized by a general parameter $t$, then the curvature of $\mathbf{r}$ is given by

$$
\kappa(t) = \dfrac{\|\mathbf T'(t)\|}{\|\mathbf r'(t)\|}
$$

where

$$
\mathbf T(t) = \dfrac{\mathbf r'(t)}{\|\mathbf r'(t)\|}
$$

is the unit tangent vector to the curve.

Here, $\mathbf r'(t)$ is a vector function and $\|\mathbf r'(t)\|$ is a scalar function.
So, we differentiate $\mathbf T(t)$ by using the chain rule:

$$
\begin{aligned}
T^{′}(t) &= \frac{∂}{∂t}(T) \\
&= \frac{∂}{∂t}((r^{′}(t))/(‖r^{′}(t)‖)) \\
&= \frac{∂}{∂t}((1)/(‖r^{′}(t)‖) \cdot r^{′}(t)) \\
&= \frac{∂}{∂t}((1)/(‖r^{′}(t)‖)) \cdot r^{′}(t) + (1)/(‖r^{′}(t)‖) \cdot \frac{∂}{∂t}(r^{′}(t)) \\
&= \frac{∂}{∂t}((1)/(‖r^{′}(t)‖)) \cdot r^{′}(t) + (1)/(‖r^{′}(t)‖) \cdot r^{″}(t)
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, since the cross product of a vector with itself equals $\mathbf{0}$, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
T(t) \cdot T^{′}(t) &= (r^{′}(t))/(‖r^{′}(t)‖) \cdot (\frac{∂}{∂t}((1)/(‖r^{′}(t)‖)) \cdot r^{′}(t) + (1)/(‖r^{′}(t)‖) \cdot r^{″}(t)) \\
&= (1)/(‖r^{′}(t)‖^{2})(r^{′}(t) \cdot r^{″}(t))
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we find the magnitude of both sides and use the fact that $\mathbf T$ is a unit vector:

$$
\begin{aligned}
‖T(t) \cdot T^{′}(t)‖ &= (1)/(‖r^{′}(t)‖^{2})‖r^{′}(t) \cdot r^{″}(t)‖ \\
‖T(t)‖ \cdot ‖T^{′}(t)‖ &= (1)/(‖r^{′}(t)‖^{2})‖r^{′}(t) \cdot r^{″}(t)‖ \\
1 \cdot ‖T^{′}(t)‖ &= (1)/(‖r^{′}(t)‖^{2})‖r^{′}(t) \cdot r^{″}(t)‖ \\
‖T^{′}(t)‖ &= (‖r^{′}(t) \cdot r^{″}(t)‖)/(‖r^{′}(t)‖^{2})
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\kappa(t) = \dfrac{\|\mathbf T'(t)\|}{\|\mathbf r'(t)\|} = \dfrac{\left\| \mathbf r'(t) \times \mathbf r''(t) \right\|}{\|\mathbf r'(t)\|^3}
$$
