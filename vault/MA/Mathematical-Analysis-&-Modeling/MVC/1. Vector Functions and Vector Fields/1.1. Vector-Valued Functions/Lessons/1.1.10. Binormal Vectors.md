## Introduction

The **binormal vector** to a curve $C$ defined by the vector-valued function $\mathbf{r}(t)$ is defined as

$$
\mathbf{B}(t) = \mathbf{T}(t) \times \mathbf{N}(t)
$$

where $\mathbf{T}$ and $\mathbf{N}$ denote the unit tangent and principal normal vectors, respectively.

The diagram below shows the binormal vector to a curve at some point $P$ on the curve.

![](<../Source/1.1.10. Binormal Vectors/Images/822e82e0569c0682bf32ce9f8cdd4f1f.png>)

Since

$$
\mathbf B = \mathbf T\times \mathbf N
$$

we can conclude that $\mathbf B$ is perpendicular to both $\mathbf T$ and $\mathbf N$, and it's pointing downward.

Also, since $\mathbf T$ and $\mathbf N$ are perpendicular *unit* vectors, it follows that $\mathbf B$ is also a unit vector.

## Calculating the General Binormal Vector to a Curve Given the Unit Tangent and Principal Normal

**Example:** Find the binormal vector to the curve $\mathbf r(t)$ at an arbitrary point, given that
$T(t)|= \frac{1}{\sqrt{t^{2} + 16}}⟨0, t, 4⟩,; N(t)|= \frac{1}{\sqrt{t^{2} + 16}}⟨0, 4,-t⟩$.

Note that $\mathbf T$ and $\mathbf N$ denote the unit tangent and principal normal, respectively.

**Explanation**

The binormal vector is given by

$$
\mathbf B(t) = \mathbf T(t) \times \mathbf N(t)
$$

Therefore, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
B(t) &= T(t) \cdot N(t) \\
&= \frac{1}{\sqrt{t^{2} + 16}}⟨0, t, 4⟩ \cdot \frac{1}{\sqrt{t^{2} + 16}}⟨0, 4,-t⟩ \\
&= \frac{1}{t^{2} + 16}i\begin{vmatrix}j & k \\ 0 & t\end{vmatrix}4 \\
&= 0 \mid 4\begin{vmatrix}-t \\ = \frac{1}{t^{2} + 16}[t\begin{vmatrix}4 \\ 4\end{vmatrix}- ti - 0\begin{vmatrix}4 \\ 0\end{vmatrix}- tj + 0 \mid t \\
&= 0\end{vmatrix}4k] \\
&= \frac{1}{t^{2} + 16}⟨(-t^{2} - 16), - (0 - 0), (0 - 0)⟩ \\
&= \frac{1}{t^{2} + 16}⟨ - (t^{2} + 16), 0, 0⟩ \\
&= ⟨ - 1, 0, 0⟩
\end{aligned}
$$

## Calculating a Specific Binormal Vector to a Curve Given the Unit Tangent and Principal Normal

**Example:** Find the binormal vector to the curve $\mathbf r(t)$ at the point $t=\pi$, given that

$T(π)|= \frac{1}{5}⟨0,-3, 4⟩,; N(π)|= ⟨1, 0, 0⟩$.

Note that $\mathbf T$ and $\mathbf N$ denote the unit tangent and principal normal, respectively.

**Explanation**

The binormal vector is given by

$$
\mathbf B(t) = \mathbf T(t) \times \mathbf N(t)
$$

Therefore, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
B(π) &= T(π) \cdot N(π) \\
&= \frac{1}{5}⟨0,-3, 4⟩ \cdot ⟨1, 0, 0⟩ \\
&= \frac{1}{5}i\begin{vmatrix}j & k \\ 0 & -3\end{vmatrix}4 \\
&= 1 \mid 0\begin{vmatrix}0 \\ = \frac{1}{5}[- 3\begin{vmatrix}4 \\ 0\end{vmatrix}0i - 0\begin{vmatrix}4 \\ 1\end{vmatrix}0j + 0 \mid - 3 \\
&= 1\end{vmatrix}0k] \\
&= \frac{1}{5}⟨(0 - 0), - (0 - 4), (0 - (-3))⟩ \\
&= \frac{1}{5}⟨0, 4, 3⟩
\end{aligned}
$$

## Calculating the General Binormal Vector to a Curve Given the Unit Tangent

**Example:** Find the binormal vector to the curve $\mathbf r(t)$ at an arbitrary point, given that

$T(t)|= \frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}⟨\sin 4t, 2, \cos 4t⟩$.

Note that $\mathbf T$ denotes the unit tangent vector.

**Explanation**

The binormal vector is given by

$$
\mathbf B(t) = \mathbf T(t) \times \mathbf N(t)
$$

where the principal normal vector $\mathbf N(t)$ is given by

$$
\mathbf N(t)= \dfrac{\mathbf T'(t)}{\|\mathbf T'(t)\|}
$$

First, we find the principal normal $\mathbf{N}(t)$. To do this, we calculate $\mathbf T'(t)$ and its magnitude as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
T^{′}(t) &= \frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}⟨\frac{d}{dt}(\sin 4t), \frac{d}{dt}(2), \frac{d}{dt}(\cos 4t)⟩ \\
&= \frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}⟨4\cos 4t, 0,-4\sin 4t⟩ \\
&= \frac{4}{\sqrt{5}}⟨\cos 4t, 0,-\sin 4t⟩ \\
‖T^{′}(t)‖ &= \frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}\sqrt{(4\cos 4t)^{2} + 0^{2} + (-4\sin 4t)^{2}} \\
&= \frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}\sqrt{16\cos^{2} 4t + 16\sin^{2} 4t} \\
&= \frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}\sqrt{16(\cos^{2} 4t + \sin^{2} 4t)} \\
&= \frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}\sqrt{16} \\
&= \frac{4}{\sqrt{5}}
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we normalize $\mathbf T'(t)$ and obtain the principal normal to the curve:

$$
\begin{aligned}
N(t) &= (T^{′}(t))/(‖T^{′}(t)‖) \\
&= (\frac{4}{\sqrt{5}}⟨\cos 4t, 0,-\sin 4t⟩)/((\frac{4}{\sqrt{5}})) \\
&= ⟨\cos 4t, 0,-\sin 4t⟩
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we calculate the binormal vector, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
B(t) &= T(t) \cdot N(t) \\
&= \frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}⟨\sin 4t, 2, \cos 4t⟩ \cdot ⟨\cos 4t, 0,-\sin 4t⟩ \\
&= \frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}i\begin{vmatrix}j & k \\ \sin 4t & 2\end{vmatrix}\cos 4t \\
&= \cos 4t \mid 0\begin{vmatrix}-\sin 4t \\ = \frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}[2\begin{vmatrix}\cos 4t \\ 0\end{vmatrix}- \sin 4ti - \sin 4t\begin{vmatrix}\cos 4t \\ \cos 4t\end{vmatrix}- \sin 4tj + \sin 4t \mid 2 \\
&= \cos 4t\end{vmatrix}0k] \\
&= \frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}⟨ - 2\sin 4t - 0, - (-\sin^{2} 4t - \cos^{2} 4t), 0 - 2\cos 4t⟩ \\
&= \frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}⟨ - 2\sin 4t, 1,-2\cos 4t⟩
\end{aligned}
$$
