## Introduction

Can we apply Euler's method to approximate solutions to second-order initial value problems, such as the one below?

$$
xy'' - y' - y = 2x, \qquad y(1) = -1, \quad y'(1) = 1
$$

Well, for a second-order initial value problem,

$$
y'' = f(x, y, y'), \qquad y(a) = c, \quad y'(a) = d
$$

we can use Euler's method to approximate a solution by first reducing the second-order ODE to a system of first-order ODEs,

$$
{y^{′} = z,\begin{vmatrix}y(a) = c, \\ z^{′} = f(x, y, z),\end{vmatrix}z(a) = d
$$

before applying the update rule component-wise to each equation:

$$
{Δy = y^{′} \cdot Δx; Δz = z^{′} \cdot Δx
$$

The new values are then given by

$$
y_\text{new} = y + \Delta y
$$

and

$$
z_\text{new} = z + \Delta z
$$

In the example above, we set up Euler's method by first isolating $y''$ so it can serve as the derivative of a new variable $z{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
xy^{″} - y^{′} - y &= 2x \\
xy^{″} &= y + y^{′} + 2x \\
y^{″} &= \frac{y + y^{′}}{x} + 2
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we substitute our new variable $z=y'$, $z'=y''$ in the above. This gives the system

$$
{y^{′} = z,\begin{vmatrix}y(1) =-1 \\ z^{′} = \frac{y + z}{x} + 2,\end{vmatrix}z(1) = 1
$$

On the next slide, we will approximate values for this system using step size

$$
\Delta x = \dfrac12
$$

## A Worked Example

We now apply the update rules

$$
\Delta y = y' \cdot \Delta x
$$

and

$$
\Delta z = z' \cdot \Delta x
$$

to our system below starting at $x=1$ with step size

$$
\Delta x = \dfrac12
$$

$$
{y^{′} = z,\begin{vmatrix}y(1) =-1 \\ z^{′} = \frac{y + z}{x} + 2,\end{vmatrix}z(1) = 1
$$

We'll start by creating a table that includes the initial condition data $y(1)=-1$ and $z(1)=1$.

| $x$ | $y$ | $y'$ | $\Delta y$ | $z$ | $z'$ | $\Delta z$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $1$ | $-1$ |  |  | $1$ |  |  |

We compute $y'$ and $\Delta y$, $z'$ and $\Delta z$ (using the values of $x$, $y$, and $z$, in the first row), and the values of $x_\text{new}$, $y_\text{new}$, and $z_\text{new}$ using Euler's method:

$$
\begin{aligned}
y^{′} &= z = 1 \\
z^{′} &= \frac{y + z}{x} + 2 = \frac{-1 + 1}{1} + 2 = 2 \\
Δy &= y^{′} \cdot Δx = 1 \cdot \frac{1}{2} = \frac{1}{2} \\
Δz &= z^{′} \cdot Δx = 2 \cdot \frac{1}{2} = 1 \\
x_{new} &= x + Δx = 1 + \frac{1}{2} = \frac{3}{2} \\
y_{new} &= y + Δy =-1 + \frac{1}{2} =-\frac{1}{2} \\
z_{new} &= z + Δz = 1 + 1 = 2
\end{aligned}
$$

We add these values to our table.

| $x$ | $y$ | $y'$ | $\Delta y$ | $z$ | $z'$ | $\Delta z$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $1$ | $-1$ | $1$ | $\dfrac12$ | $1$ | $2$ | $1$ |
| $\dfrac32$ | $-\dfrac12$ |  |  | $2$ |  |  |

After several steps, we obtain the following table of values.

| $x$ | $y$ | $y'$ | $\Delta y$ | $z$ | $z'$ | $\Delta z$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $1$ | $-1$ | $1$ | $\dfrac12$ | $1$ | $2$ | $1$ |
| $\dfrac32$ | $-\dfrac12$ | $2$ | $1$ | $2$ | $3$ | $\dfrac32$ |
| $2$ | $\dfrac12$ | $\dfrac72$ | $\dfrac74$ | $\dfrac72$ | $4$ | $2$ |
| $\dfrac52$ | $\dfrac94$ |  |  | $\dfrac{11}2$ |  |  |

Therefore, we conclude that

$$
y\left(\dfrac52\right) \approx \dfrac94
$$

and

$$
y'\left(\dfrac52\right) = z\left(\dfrac52\right) \approx \dfrac{11}2
$$

Notice that this approach of splitting a second-order ODE into a system of first-order ODEs is not specific to Euler's method. Indeed, we could apply any numerical method component-wise to approximate the solution to a second-order ODE once split into a system of first-order ODEs!

## Completing a Row of a Table Using Euler's Method for Second-Order ODEs

**Example:** Consider the following initial value problem:
$y'' + 4xy' - 3y = 2x, \qquad y(0) = 1, \quad y'(0) = -2$
We wish to approximate the solution using Euler's method by introducing $z = y'$ and reducing the equation to a system of first-order ODEs. What entries should be placed in the columns for $\Delta y$ and $\Delta z$ in the table below if the step size is $\Delta x = 1$?

| $x$ | $y$ | $y'$ | $\Delta y$ | $z$ | $z'$ | $\Delta z$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $0$ |  |  |  |  |  |  |

Use the information above to approximate the value of $y(1)$.

**Explanation**

For a second-order initial value problem

$$
y'' = f(x,y,y'), \qquad y(a) = c, \quad y'(a) = d
$$

we can use Euler's method to approximate a solution by first reducing the second-order ODE to a system of first-order ODEs,

$$
{y^{′} = z,\begin{vmatrix}y(a) = c, \\ z^{′} = f(x, y, z)\end{vmatrix}z(a) = d
$$

before applying the update rule component-wise to each equation.

To apply Euler’s method, we first isolate $y''$ so it can serve as the derivative of a new variable $z{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
y^{″} + 4xy^{′} - 3y &= 2x \\
y^{″} &= -4xy^{′} + 3y + 2x
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we substitute our new variable

$$
z = y', z' = y''
$$

in the right-hand side of the above equation. This gives

$$
z' = -4xz + 3y + 2x
$$

Note that the initial value of $z$ is

$$
z(0) = y'(0) = -2
$$

So, the initial value problem reduces to the following system of first-order ODEs:

$$
{y^{′} = z,\begin{vmatrix}y(0) = 1, \\ z^{′} =-4xz + 3y + 2x,\end{vmatrix}z(0) =-2
$$

Now, we can proceed with Euler's method by applying the update rule component-wise.

First, because the initial conditions are $y(0)=1$ and $z(0)=-2$, we place $y=1$ and $z=-2$ in the table.

| $x$ | $y$ | $y'$ | $\Delta y$ | $z$ | $z'$ | $\Delta z$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $0$ | $1$ |  |  | $-2$ |  |  |

Next, we compute both $y'$ and $z'$ according to the given rules:

$$
\begin{aligned}
y^{′} &= z =-2 \\
z^{′} &= -4xz + 3y + 2x \\
&=-4 \cdot 0 \cdot (-2) + 3 \cdot 1 + 2 \cdot 0 \\
&= 0 + 3 + 0 \\
&= 3
\end{aligned}
$$

So, we add these to our table.

| $x$ | $y$ | $y'$ | $\Delta y$ | $z$ | $z'$ | $\Delta z$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $0$ | $1$ | $-2$ |  | $-2$ | $3$ |  |

Finally, we compute $\Delta y$ and $\Delta z$ using Euler's method:

$$
\begin{aligned}
Δy &= y^{′} \cdot Δx \\
&= (-2) \cdot 1 \\
&=-2 \\
Δz &= z^{′} \cdot Δx \\
&= 3 \cdot 1 \\
&= 3
\end{aligned}
$$

We add these to our table.

| $x$ | $y$ | $y'$ | $\Delta y$ | $z$ | $z'$ | $\Delta z$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $0$ | $1$ | $-2$ | $-2$ | $-2$ | $3$ | $3$ |

To get the new values of $x$, $y$, and $z$ in the *next* row, we add $\Delta x$ to $x$, $\Delta y$ to $y$, and $\Delta z$ to $z$, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_{new} &= x + Δx = 0 + 1 = 1 \\
y_{new} &= y + Δy = 1 + (-2) =-1 \\
z_{new} &= z + Δz =-2 + 3 = 1
\end{aligned}
$$

Adding these values to our table in a new row gives the following:

| $x$ | $y$ | $y'$ | $\Delta y$ | $z$ | $z'$ | $\Delta z$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $0$ | $1$ | $-2$ | $-2$ | $-2$ | $3$ | $3$ |
| $1$ | $-1$ |  |  | $1$ |  |  |

Therefore, we conclude that

$$
y(1) \approx -1
$$

## Approximating the Solution to a Second-Order ODE Using Euler's Method With One Step

**Example:** Consider the following initial value problem:
$x^2y'' + 2y' = 4x^2, \qquad y(1) = -1, \qquad y'(1) = -1$
Use Euler's Method with one step to approximate $y\left(\dfrac32\right)$ and $y'\left(\dfrac32\right)$.

**Explanation**

For a second-order initial value problem

$$
y'' = f(x,y,y'), \qquad y(a) = c, \quad y'(a) = d
$$

we can use Euler's method to approximate a solution by first reducing the second-order ODE to a system of first-order ODEs,

$$
{y^{′} = z,\begin{vmatrix}y(a) = c, \\ z^{′} = f(x, y, z)\end{vmatrix}z(a) = d
$$

before applying the update rule component-wise to each equation.

To apply Euler’s method, we first isolate $y''$ so it can serve as the derivative of a new variable $z{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^{2}y^{″} + 2y^{′} &= 4x^{2} \\
x^{2}y^{″} &= 4x^{2} - 2y^{′} \\
y^{″} &= \frac{4x^{2} - 2y^{′}}{x^{2}}
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we substitute our new variable

$$
z = y', z' = y''
$$

in the right-hand side of the above equation. This gives

$$
z' = \dfrac{4x^2 - 2z}{x^2}
$$

Note that the initial value of $z$ is

$$
z(1) = y'(1) = -1
$$

So, the initial value problem reduces to the following system of first-order ODEs:

$$
{y^{′} = z,\begin{vmatrix}y(1) =-1, \\ z^{′} = \frac{4x^{2} - 2z}{x^{2}},\end{vmatrix}z(1) =-1
$$

Now, we can proceed with Euler's method by applying the update rule component-wise.

Since we want to find the values of $y$ and $y'=z$ at

$$
x=\dfrac32
$$

we will use a step size of

$$
\Delta x = \dfrac32 - 1 = \dfrac12
$$

First, we place the initial condition data in the table.

| $x$ | $y$ | $y'$ | $\Delta y$ | $z$ | $z'$ | $\Delta z$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $1$ | $-1$ |  |  | $-1$ |  |  |

Next, we compute both $y'$ and $z'$ according to the given rules:

$$
\begin{aligned}
y^{′} &= z =-1 \\
z^{′} &= \frac{4x^{2} - 2z}{x^{2}} \\
&= (4 \cdot 1^{2} - 2 \cdot (-1))/(1^{2}) \\
&= \frac{4 + 2}{1} \\
&= 6
\end{aligned}
$$

So, we add these to our table.

| $x$ | $y$ | $y'$ | $\Delta y$ | $z$ | $z'$ | $\Delta z$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $1$ | $-1$ | $-1$ |  | $-1$ | $6$ |  |

Next, we compute $\Delta y$ and $\Delta z$ using Euler's method:

$$
\begin{aligned}
Δy &= y^{′} \cdot Δx \\
&= (-1) \cdot \frac{1}{2} \\
&=-\frac{1}{2} \\
Δz &= z^{′} \cdot Δx \\
&= 6 \cdot \frac{1}{2} \\
&= 3
\end{aligned}
$$

We add these to our table.

| $x$ | $y$ | $y'$ | $\Delta y$ | $z$ | $z'$ | $\Delta z$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $1$ | $-1$ | $-1$ | $-\dfrac12$ | $-1$ | $6$ | $3$ |

To get the new values of $x$, $y$, and $z$ in the *next* row, we add $\Delta x$ to $x$, $\Delta y$ to $y$, and $\Delta z$ to $z$, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_{new} &= x + Δx = 1 + \frac{1}{2} = \frac{3}{2} \\
y_{new} &= y + Δy =-1 + (-\frac{1}{2}) =-\frac{3}{2} \\
z_{new} &= z + Δz =-1 + 3 = 2
\end{aligned}
$$

Adding these values to our table in a new row gives the following:

| $x$ | $y$ | $y'$ | $\Delta y$ | $z$ | $z'$ | $\Delta z$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $1$ | $-1$ | $-1$ | $-\dfrac12$ | $-1$ | $6$ | $3$ |
| $\dfrac32$ | $-\dfrac32$ |  |  | $2$ |  |  |

Therefore, we conclude that

$$
y\left(\dfrac32\right) \approx -\dfrac32
$$

and

$$
y'\left(\dfrac32\right) = z\left(\dfrac32\right) \approx 2
$$

## Approximating the Solution to a Second-Order ODE Using Euler's Method With Two Steps

**Example:** Consider the following initial value problem:
$2x y'' - (y')^2 + 4y = 0, \qquad y(1) = -1, \quad y'(1) = 2$
Use Euler's Method with two steps to approximate $y(3)$ and $y'(3)$.

**Explanation**

For a second-order initial value problem

$$
y'' = f(x,y,y'), \qquad y(a) = c, \quad y'(a) = d
$$

we can use Euler's method to approximate a solution by first reducing the second-order ODE to a system of first-order ODEs,

$$
{y^{′} = z,\begin{vmatrix}y(a) = c, \\ z^{′} = f(x, y, z)\end{vmatrix}z(a) = d
$$

before applying the update rule component-wise to each equation.

To apply Euler’s method, we first isolate $y''$ so it can serve as the derivative of a new variable $z{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
2xy^{″} - (y^{′})^{2} + 4y &= 0 \\
2xy^{″} &= (y^{′})^{2} - 4y \\
y^{″} &= ((y^{′})^{2} - 4y)/(2x)
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we substitute our new variable $z = y'$, $z' = y''$ in the right-hand side of the above equation. This gives

$$
z' = \dfrac{z^2 - 4y}{2x}
$$

Note that the initial value of $z$ is

$$
z(1) = y'(1) = 2
$$

So, the initial value problem reduces to the following system of first-order ODEs:

$$
{y^{′} = z,\begin{vmatrix}y(1) =-1, \\ z^{′} = \frac{z^{2} - 4y}{2x},\end{vmatrix}z(1) = 2
$$

Now, we can proceed with Euler's method by applying the update rule component-wise.

First, note that we are given the values of $y$ and $z=y'$ at $x=1$ and asked to find their values at $x=3$. The distance from $x=1$ to $x=3$ is $2$, and since we are asked to use two steps of equal size, our step size must be

$$
\Delta x = \dfrac{3 - 1}2 = 1
$$

Now, we'll start by creating a table that includes the initial condition data $y(1)=-1$ and $z(1)=2$.

| $x$ | $y$ | $y'$ | $\Delta y$ | $z$ | $z'$ | $\Delta z$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $1$ | $-1$ |  |  | $2$ |  |  |

**Step 1**.

We compute $y'$ and $\Delta y$, $z'$ and $\Delta z$ (using the values of $x$, $y$, and $z$ in the first row), and the values of $x_\text{new}$, $y_\text{new}$, and $z_\text{new}$ using Euler's method:

$$
\begin{aligned}
y^{′} &= z = 2 \\
z^{′} &= \frac{z^{2} - 4y}{2x} = (2^{2} - 4(-1))/(2 \cdot 1) = 4 \\
Δy &= y^{′} \cdot Δx = 2 \cdot 1 = 2 \\
Δz &= z^{′} \cdot Δx = 4 \cdot 1 = 4 \\
x_{new} &= x + Δx = 1 + 1 = 2 \\
y_{new} &= y + Δy =-1 + 2 = 1 \\
z_{new} &= z + Δz = 2 + 4 = 6
\end{aligned}
$$

We add these values to our table.

| $x$ | $y$ | $y'$ | $\Delta y$ | $z$ | $z'$ | $\Delta z$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $1$ | $-1$ | $2$ | $2$ | $2$ | $4$ | $4$ |
| $2$ | $1$ |  |  | $6$ |  |  |

**Step 2**.

We compute $y'$ and $\Delta y$, $z'$ and $\Delta z$ (using the values of $x$, $y$, and $z$ in the second row), and the values of $x_\text{new}$, $y_\text{new}$, and $z_\text{new}$ using Euler's method:

$$
\begin{aligned}
y^{′} &= z = 6 \\
z^{′} &= \frac{z^{2} - 4y}{2x} = \frac{6^{2} - 4 \cdot 1}{2 \cdot 2} = 8 \\
Δy &= y^{′} \cdot Δx = 6 \cdot 1 = 6 \\
Δz &= z^{′} \cdot Δx = 8 \cdot 1 = 8 \\
x_{new} &= x + Δx = 2 + 1 = 3 \\
y_{new} &= y + Δy = 1 + 6 = 7 \\
z_{new} &= z + Δz = 6 + 8 = 14
\end{aligned}
$$

We add these values to our table.

| $x$ | $y$ | $y'$ | $\Delta y$ | $z$ | $z'$ | $\Delta z$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $1$ | $-1$ | $2$ | $2$ | $2$ | $4$ | $4$ |
| $2$ | $1$ | $6$ | $6$ | $6$ | $8$ | $8$ |
| $3$ | $7$ |  |  | $14$ |  |  |

Therefore, we conclude that

$$
y(3) \approx 7
$$

and

$$
y'(3) = z(3) \approx 14
$$
