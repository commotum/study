## Introduction

Let's consider the differential equation $y' = -4xy$. The slope field for this equation is shown below.

![](<../Source/1.3.6. Analyzing Slope Fields for Nonautonomous Differential Equations/Images/6b3a366dc2b3a37d03251b825dc169e1.png>)

Let's see how much information we can gather about the differential equation by referring *only* to its slope field.

- First, note that the slope field has neither vertical nor horizontal symmetry. Therefore, the associated differential equation must be of the form $y' = f(x,y)$. Indeed, the equation $y' =-4xy$ is of this form.
- Second, notice that the solution curves appear to have stationary points along the lines $x=0$ and $y=0$. This means that the right-hand side of our differential equation must evaluate to zero on these lines. Indeed, this is true of our equation.![](<../Source/1.3.6. Analyzing Slope Fields for Nonautonomous Differential Equations/Images/60bfa29549744e236422265c6242ebbf.png>)
- Third, the lines $x=0$ and $y=0$ split the plane into four regions. The slopes of the solution curves in each of these regions are summarized in the table below.$y > 0$$\nearrow$

$({\color{blue}{+}})$$\searrow$

$({\color{red}{-}})$$y < 0$$\searrow$

$({\color{red}{-}})$$\nearrow$

$({\color{blue}{+}})$$x< 0$ $x> 0$

Notice that the sign of $f(x,y)$ in the four regions agrees with the above table:

- $f(x,y)=-4xy$ is positive in the second and fourth quadrants, and
- $f(x,y)=-4xy$ is negative in the first and third quadrants.

## Identifying an Equation That Generates a Slope Field Using Stationary Points

**Example:** The differential equation that generates the slope field shown above is given by $y' = (x-a)(y-b)$. Find the value of $a + b$.

![](<../Source/1.3.6. Analyzing Slope Fields for Nonautonomous Differential Equations/Images/fd5112fd081ff4dbdf41ceb7d6b6ccc4.png>)

**Explanation**

![](<../Source/1.3.6. Analyzing Slope Fields for Nonautonomous Differential Equations/Images/3327d1834e198b8eab50a19a54e5b9b1.png>)

Notice that the slopes of the slope field are horizontal along the lines $x = -2$ and $y = 2$, as shown above. Therefore, the right-hand side of the differential equation should evaluate to zero on both of these lines.

So, our differential equation must be

$$
y' = (x+2)(y-2)
$$

Therefore, we conclude that $a = -2$, $b = 2$, and

$$
a + b=0
$$

## Identifying an Equation That Generates a Slope Field Using the Sign of the Slopes

**Example:** Which of the following equations could have generated the slope field shown above?

![](<../Source/1.3.6. Analyzing Slope Fields for Nonautonomous Differential Equations/Images/77e64d6b091c27df883b55b93e628c77.png>)

1. $y' = 2xy$
2. $y' = -x(y-1)^2$
3. $y' = \dfrac{1}{2}x^2(y-1)$

**Explanation**

![](<../Source/1.3.6. Analyzing Slope Fields for Nonautonomous Differential Equations/Images/b06ebab58bfb746f35ecba54930526a3.png>)

Notice the following properties of the given slope field:

- First, the slopes of the slope field are horizontal along the lines $x=0$ and $y=1$, as shown above. Therefore, the right-hand side of the differential equation should evaluate to zero on both of these lines.
- Second, the lines $x=0$ and $y=1$ divide the plane into $4$ regions. The sign of the slopes in each of the $4$ regions are easily deduced by considering the behavior of the solution curves in these regions. This information is summarized in the following table:$y > 1$$\nearrow$

$({\color{blue}{+}})$$\nearrow$

$({\color{blue}{+}})$ $y < 1$$\searrow$

$({\color{red}{-}})$ $\searrow$

$({\color{red}{-}})$$x< 0$ $x> 0$

With that in mind, let's consider each equation.

- Equation I does *not* give the required behavior. The right-hand side of $y' = 2xy$ evaluates to zero at $x=0$ and $y=0$.
- Equation II does *not* give the required behavior. The right-hand side of $y' = -x(y-1)^2$ evaluates to zero at $x=0$ and $y=1$, but the sign of the slopes in the required regions (summarized below) do not correspond to the given slope field.$y > 1$${\color{blue}{+}}$${\color{red}{-}}$ $y < 1$${\color{blue}{+}}$ ${\color{red}{-}}$$x< 0$ $x> 0$
- Equation III gives the required behavior. The right-hand side of $y' = \dfrac{1}{2}x^2(y-1)$ evaluates to zero at $x=0$ and $y=1$, and the sign of the slopes in the required regions (summarized below) correspond to the given slope field.$y > 1$${\color{blue}{+}}$${\color{blue}{+}}$ $y < 1$${\color{red}{-}}$ ${\color{red}{-}}$$x< 0$ $x> 0$

Therefore, the correct answer is "III only."

## Identifying an Equation That Generates a Slope Field Using the Sign of the Slopes and Factoring

**Example:** The diagram shown above is the slope field for which of the following differential equations?

![](<../Source/1.3.6. Analyzing Slope Fields for Nonautonomous Differential Equations/Images/321b6eb66ca267d3844c0960f615026d.png>)

1. $y' = x^2(y+1)+2(y+1)$
2. $y' = x(y+1)+2(y+1)$
3. $y' = x(y+1)^2+2(y+1)^2$

**Explanation**

![](<../Source/1.3.6. Analyzing Slope Fields for Nonautonomous Differential Equations/Images/e8dd09383c592fafe22b0fd1c05a391a.png>)

Notice the following properties of the given slope field:

- First, the slopes of the slope field are horizontal along the lines $x=-2$ and $y=-1$, as shown above. Therefore, the right-hand side of the differential equation should evaluate to zero on both of these lines.
- Second, the lines $x=-2$ and $y=-1$ divide the plane into $4$ regions. The sign of the slopes in each of the $4$ regions are easily deduced by considering the behavior of the solution curves in these regions. This information is summarized in the following table:$y > -1$$\searrow$

$({\color{red}{-}})$$\nearrow$

$({\color{blue}{+}})$$y < -1$$\nearrow$

$({\color{blue}{+}})$$\searrow$

$({\color{red}{-}})$$x< -2$ $x> -2$

With that in mind, let's consider each equation.

- Equation I does *not* give the required behavior. The right-hand side of
$y' = x^2(y+1)+2(y+1) = (x^2 + 2)(y + 1)$
evaluates to zero at $y = -1$ only.
- Equation II gives the required behavior. The right-hand side of
$y' = x(y+1)+2(y+1) = (x+2)(y+1)$
evaluates to zero at $x = -2$ and $y = -1$, and the sign of the slopes in the required regions (summarized below) correspond to the given slope field.$y > -1$${\color{red}{-}}$${\color{blue}{+}}$$y < -1$${\color{blue}{+}}$${\color{red}{-}}$$x< -2$ $x> -2$
- Equation III does *not* give the required behavior. The right-hand side of
$y' = x(y+1)^2+2(y+1)^2 = (x+2)(y+1)^2$
evaluates to zero at $x=-2$ and $y=-1$, but the sign of the slopes in the required regions (summarized below) do not correspond to the given slope field.$y > -1$${\color{red}{-}}$${\color{blue}{+}}$$y < -1$${\color{red}{-}}$${\color{blue}{+}}$$x< -2$ $x> -2$

Therefore, the correct answer is equation II.

## Identifying an Equation That Generates a Slope Field Using Asymptotes

**Example:** Which of the following equations could have generated the slope field shown above?

![](<../Source/1.3.6. Analyzing Slope Fields for Nonautonomous Differential Equations/Images/42556eddb08beba40a46abcc4af06f69.png>)

1. $y' = x + y + 1$
2. $y' = \dfrac{xy}{y+1}$
3. $y' = \dfrac{y}{x+1}$

**Explanation**

![](<../Source/1.3.6. Analyzing Slope Fields for Nonautonomous Differential Equations/Images/c1cf6111a0a453b8105ef790e0748df2.png>)

Notice the following properties of the given slope field:

- The slopes of the slope field become vertical as $x \to -1$.
- The slopes of the slope field become horizontal at $y=0$.

With that in mind, let's consider each equation.

- Equation I does *not* give the required behavior. The right-hand side of $y' = x + y + 1$ does not evaluate to zero on $y=0$ (the value depends on $x$).
- Equation II does *not* give the required behavior. The right-hand side of $y' = \dfrac{xy}{y+1}$ evaluates to zero on $y=0$, but
$y' = \dfrac{xy}{y+1} \to -\dfrac{y}{y+1} \quad \textrm{as} \quad x \to -1$,
and hence $y'$ depends on $y$ along the line $x=-1$.
- Equation III gives the required behavior. The right-hand side of $y' = \dfrac{y}{x+1}$ evaluates to zero on $y=0$, and
$y' = \bigg \mid \dfrac{y}{x+1} \bigg \mid \to \infty \quad \textrm{as} \quad x \to -1$.

Therefore, the correct answer is "III only."
