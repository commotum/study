## Introduction

For a $2 \times 2$ matrix differential equation

$$
\mathbf{x}'(t) = A \mathbf{x}(t)
$$

we know that if $A$ has distinct eigenvalues, the general solution is

$$
\mathbf{x}(t) = c_1{\color{black}\mathbf{v}_1} e^{{\color{black}\lambda_1} t} + c_2{\color{black}\mathbf{v}_2} e^{{\color{black}\lambda_2} t}
$$

This formula works even when the eigenvalues $\lambda_1, \lambda_2$ are complex. However, using it directly requires working with complex exponentials, which can be cumbersome.

A more direct method is to find just *one* of the complex solutions, say

$$
\mathbf x_1(t) = \mathbf{v}_1 e^{{\color{black}\lambda_1} t}
$$

and separate it into its *real and imaginary parts*.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_{1}(t) &= v_{1}e^{λ_{1}t} \\
&= w_{1}(t)_(⏟)_(Real Part) + \textrm{i}w_{2}(t)_(⏟)_(Imaginary Part)
\end{aligned}
$$

The general *real-valued* solution is then a linear combination of these two parts:

$$
\mathbf x(t) = c_1 \mathbf w_1(t) + c_2 \mathbf w_2(t)
$$

**Note:** This method may seem surprising. Why can we construct the general solution using only *one* of the complex solutions, $\mathbf x_1(t)$?

The reason is that the second solution, $\mathbf x_2(t)$, doesn't provide any new information. For a real matrix $A$, the complex eigenvalues and eigenvectors come in *conjugate pairs*, which means $\mathbf x_2(t)$ is simply the complex conjugate of $\mathbf x_1(t)$.

Since

$$
\mathbf x_2(t) = \mathbf w_1 (t) - \textrm{i} \, \mathbf w_2(t)
$$

it is also a linear combination of the real and imaginary parts of $\mathbf x_1(t)$.

To build our general solution, we just need two *linearly independent* real solutions. The key facts are the following:

- For a system with complex eigenvalues, the real part $\mathbf w_1(t)$ and the imaginary part $\mathbf w_2(t)$ are *always* linearly independent.
- Since they are two linearly independent solutions, they can form the basis for the general solution.

So, we can use them directly and bypass the need to work with $\mathbf x_2(t)$ at all.

Let's see a concrete example.

## Finding the General Solution to a System of Linear Equations: Purely Imaginary Eigenvalues

**Example:** $\begin{bmatrix}1 & -1 \\ 2 & -1\end{bmatrix}$
Consider the system of linear differential equations above.
Find the unknown function $f(t)$ in the general solution to the system, shown below.
$\begin{bmatrix}\cos t - \sin t \\ 2\cos t\end{bmatrix}$

*Hint: The matrix has the eigenvalue $\lambda_1 = \textrm{i}$ with the corresponding eigenvector $\begin{bmatrix}1 + \textrm{i} \\ 2\end{bmatrix}$*

**Explanation**

We need to find the eigenvalues and associated eigenvectors of the matrix $\begin{bmatrix}1 & -1 \\ 2 & -1\end{bmatrix}$

We are given the eigenvalue

$$
\lambda_1 = \textrm{i}
$$

and the corresponding eigenvector $\begin{bmatrix}1 + \textrm{i} \\ 2\end{bmatrix}$

**Note:** The eigenvector corresponding to the second eigenvalue

$$
\lambda_2 = \overline{\lambda}_1
$$

is

$$
\mathbf{v}_2 = \overline{\mathbf{v}}_1
$$

But we do not require this eigenvector for solving our system.

The solution that we obtain for the eigenvalue

$$
\lambda_1 = \textrm{i}
$$

and its associated eigenvector is
$\begin{bmatrix}1 + \textrm{i} \\ 2\end{bmatrix}$

We now express this as a real function. Using Euler's formula, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_{1}(t) &= \begin{bmatrix}1 + \textrm{i} \\ 2\end{bmatrix}e^{\textrm{i}t} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}1 + \textrm{i} \\ 2\end{bmatrix} \cdot (\cos t + \textrm{i}\sin t) \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}\cos t + \textrm{i}\sin t + \textrm{i}\cos t - \sin t \\ 2\cos t + 2\textrm{i}\sin t\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}(\cos t - \sin t) + \textrm{i}(\cos t + \sin t) \\ 2\cos t + 2\textrm{i}\sin t\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}\cos t - \sin t \\ 2\cos t\end{bmatrix} + \textrm{i}\begin{bmatrix}\cos t + \sin t \\ 2\sin t\end{bmatrix} \\
&= w_{1}(t) + \textrm{i}w_{2}(t)
\end{aligned}
$$

The vectors $\mathbf w_1$ and $\mathbf w_2$ are linearly independent. Therefore, the general solution of the system is

$$
\begin{aligned}
x(t) &= c_{1}w_{1}(t) + c_{2}w_{2}(t) \\
&= c_{1}\begin{bmatrix}\cos t - \sin t \\ 2\cos t\end{bmatrix} + c_{2}\begin{bmatrix}\cos t + \sin t \\ 2\sin t\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally,

$$
f(t) = \cos{t} + \sin{t}
$$

## Solving an Initial Value Problem: Purely Imaginary Eigenvalues

**Example:** Solve the initial value problem
$\begin{bmatrix}2 & -4 \\ 2 & -2\end{bmatrix}$
*Hint: The matrix has the eigenvalue $\lambda_1 = 2\textrm{i}$ with the corresponding eigenvector $\begin{bmatrix}1 + \textrm{i} \\ 1\end{bmatrix}$*

**Explanation**

We need to find the eigenvalues and associated eigenvectors of the matrix $\begin{bmatrix}2 & -4 \\ 2 & -2\end{bmatrix}$

We are given the eigenvalue

$$
\lambda_1 = 2\textrm{i}
$$

and the corresponding eigenvector $\begin{bmatrix}1 + \textrm{i} \\ 1\end{bmatrix}$

**Note:** The eigenvector corresponding to the second eigenvalue

$$
\lambda_2 = \overline{\lambda}_1
$$

is

$$
\mathbf{v}_2 = \overline{\mathbf{v}}_1
$$

But we do not require this eigenvector for solving our system.

The solution that we obtain for the eigenvalue

$$
\lambda_1 = 2\textrm{i}
$$

and its associated eigenvector is
$\begin{bmatrix}1 + \textrm{i} \\ 1\end{bmatrix}$

We now express this as a real function. Using Euler's formula, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_{1}(t) &= \begin{bmatrix}1 + \textrm{i} \\ 1\end{bmatrix}e^{2\textrm{i}t} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}1 + \textrm{i} \\ 1\end{bmatrix}(\cos (2t) + \textrm{i}\sin (2t)) \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}\cos (2t) + \textrm{i}\sin (2t) + \textrm{i}\cos (2t) - \sin (2t) \\ \cos (2t) + \textrm{i}\sin (2t)\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}(\cos (2t) - \sin (2t)) + \textrm{i}(\cos (2t) + \sin (2t)) \\ \cos (2t) + \textrm{i}\sin (2t)\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}\cos (2t) - \sin (2t) \\ \cos (2t)\end{bmatrix} + \textrm{i}\begin{bmatrix}\cos (2t) + \sin (2t) \\ \sin (2t)\end{bmatrix} \\
&= w_{1}(t) + \textrm{i}w_{2}(t)
\end{aligned}
$$

The vectors $\mathbf w_1$ and $\mathbf w_2$ are linearly independent. Therefore, the general solution of the system is

$$
\begin{aligned}
x(t) &= c_{1}w_{1}(t) + c_{2}w_{2}(t) \\
&= c_{1}\begin{bmatrix}\cos (2t) - \sin (2t) \\ \cos (2t)\end{bmatrix} + c_{2}\begin{bmatrix}\cos (2t) + \sin (2t) \\ \sin (2t)\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we need to find the values of $c_1$ and $c_2$ using the initial condition $\begin{bmatrix}-1 \\ 1\end{bmatrix}$ Substituting $t=0$ into the general solution gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
x(0) &= c_{1}\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 1\end{bmatrix} + c_{2}\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 0\end{bmatrix} \\
\begin{bmatrix}-1 \\ 1\end{bmatrix} &= \begin{bmatrix}c_{1} + c_{2} \\ c_{1}\end{bmatrix} \\
\begin{bmatrix}c_{1} \\ c_{2}\end{bmatrix} &= \begin{bmatrix}1 \\ -2\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, the solution to our initial value problem is given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
x(t) &= 1\begin{bmatrix}\cos (2t) - \sin (2t) \\ \cos (2t)\end{bmatrix} - 2\begin{bmatrix}\cos (2t) + \sin (2t) \\ \sin (2t)\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}-\cos (2t) - 3\sin (2t) \\ \cos (2t) - 2\sin (2t)\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding the General Solution to a System of Linear Equations: Complex Eigenvalues

**Example:** $\begin{bmatrix}1 & -1 \\ 1 & 1\end{bmatrix}$

Consider the system of linear differential equations above.
Find the unknown function $f(t)$ in the general solution to the system, shown below.
$\begin{bmatrix}-\sin t \\ \cos t\end{bmatrix}$

*Hint: One of the eigenvalues of the matrix is $\lambda_1=1+\textrm{i}$.*

**Explanation**

We need to find the eigenvalues and associated eigenvectors of the matrix $\begin{bmatrix}1 & -1 \\ 1 & 1\end{bmatrix}$

We are given that the eigenvalue is

$$
\lambda_1=1+\textrm{i}
$$

Let's go through the process of finding the eigenvectors. For

$$
\lambda_1 =1+\textrm{i}
$$

we get
$\begin{bmatrix}-\textrm{i} & -1 \\ 1 & -\textrm{i}\end{bmatrix}$
So, we get an eigenvector
$\begin{bmatrix}\textrm{i} \\ 1\end{bmatrix}$

**Note:** The eigenvector corresponding to the second eigenvalue

$$
\lambda_2 = \overline{\lambda}_1
$$

is

$$
\mathbf{v}_2 = \overline{\mathbf{v}}_1
$$

But we do not require this eigenvector for solving our system.

The solution that we obtain for the eigenvalue

$$
\lambda_1 = 1+\textrm{i}
$$

and its associated eigenvector is
$\begin{bmatrix}\textrm{i} \\ 1\end{bmatrix}$

We now separate this solution into its real and imaginary parts. Using Euler's formula, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_{1}(t) &= \begin{bmatrix}\textrm{i} \\ 1\end{bmatrix}e^{(1 + \textrm{i})t} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}\textrm{i} \\ 1\end{bmatrix}e^{t}(\cos t + \textrm{i}\sin t) \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}\textrm{i}(e^{t}\cos t) - e^{t}\sin t \\ e^{t}\cos t + \textrm{i}(e^{t}\sin t)\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}-\sin t \\ \cos t\end{bmatrix}e^{t} + \textrm{i}\begin{bmatrix}\cos t \\ \sin t\end{bmatrix}e^{t} \\
&= w_{1}(t) + \textrm{i}w_{2}(t)
\end{aligned}
$$

The vectors $\mathbf w_1$ and $\mathbf w_2$ are linearly independent. Therefore, the general solution of the system is

$$
\begin{aligned}
x(t) &= c_{1}w_{1}(t) + c_{2}w_{2}(t) \\
&= c_{1}\begin{bmatrix}-\sin t \\ \cos t\end{bmatrix}e^{t} + c_{2}\begin{bmatrix}\cos t \\ \sin t\end{bmatrix}e^{t}
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally,

$$
f(t) = \cos{t}
$$

## Solving an Initial Value Problem: Complex Eigenvalues

**Example:** Solve the initial value problem
$\begin{bmatrix}3 & -1 \\ 1 & 3\end{bmatrix}$

*Hint: One of the eigenvalues of the matrix is $\lambda_1=3+\textrm{i}$.*

**Explanation**

We need to find the eigenvalues and associated eigenvectors of the matrix $\begin{bmatrix}3 & -1 \\ 1 & 3\end{bmatrix}$

We are given that the eigenvalue is

$$
\lambda_1=3+\textrm{i}
$$

Let's go through the process of finding the eigenvectors. For

$$
\lambda_1 =3+\textrm{i}
$$

we get
$\begin{bmatrix}-\textrm{i} & -1 \\ 1 & -\textrm{i}\end{bmatrix}$
So, we get an eigenvector
$\begin{bmatrix}\textrm{i} \\ 1\end{bmatrix}$

**Note:** The eigenvector corresponding to the second eigenvalue

$$
\lambda_2 = \overline{\lambda}_1
$$

is

$$
\mathbf{v}_2 = \overline{\mathbf{v}}_1
$$

But we do not require this eigenvector for solving our system.

The solution that we obtain for the eigenvalue

$$
\lambda_1 = 3 + \textrm{i}
$$

and its associated eigenvector is
$\begin{bmatrix}\textrm{i} \\ 1\end{bmatrix}$

We now express this as a real function. Using Euler's formula, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_{1}(t) &= \begin{bmatrix}\textrm{i} \\ 1\end{bmatrix}e^{(3 + \textrm{i})t} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}\textrm{i} \\ 1\end{bmatrix}e^{3t}(\cos t + \textrm{i}\sin t) \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}\textrm{i}(e^{3t}\cos t) - e^{3t}\sin t \\ e^{3t}\cos t + \textrm{i}(e^{3t}\sin t)\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}-\sin t \\ \cos t\end{bmatrix}e^{3t} + \textrm{i}\begin{bmatrix}\cos t \\ \sin t\end{bmatrix}e^{3t} \\
&= w_{1}(t) + \textrm{i}w_{2}(t)
\end{aligned}
$$

The vectors $\mathbf w_1$ and $\mathbf w_2$ are linearly independent. Therefore, the general solution of the system is

$$
\begin{aligned}
x(t) &= c_{1}w_{1}(t) + c_{2}w_{2}(t) \\
&= c_{1}\begin{bmatrix}-\sin t \\ \cos t\end{bmatrix}e^{3t} + c_{2}\begin{bmatrix}\cos t \\ \sin t\end{bmatrix}e^{3t}
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we need to find the values of $c_1$ and $c_2$ using the initial condition $\begin{bmatrix}-3 \\ 1\end{bmatrix}$ Substituting $t=0$ into the general solution gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
x(0) &= c_{1}\begin{bmatrix}0 \\ 1\end{bmatrix} + c_{2}\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 0\end{bmatrix} \\
\begin{bmatrix}-3 \\ 1\end{bmatrix} &= \begin{bmatrix}c_{2} \\ c_{1}\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, the solution to our initial value problem is given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
x(t) &= 1\begin{bmatrix}-\sin t \\ \cos t\end{bmatrix}e^{3t} - 3\begin{bmatrix}\cos t \\ \sin t\end{bmatrix}e^{3t} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}-\sin t - 3\cos t \\ \cos t - 3\sin t\end{bmatrix}e^{3t}
\end{aligned}
$$
