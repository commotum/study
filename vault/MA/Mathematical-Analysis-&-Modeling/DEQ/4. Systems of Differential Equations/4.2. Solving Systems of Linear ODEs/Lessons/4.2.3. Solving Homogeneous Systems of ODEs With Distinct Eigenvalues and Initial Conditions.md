## Introduction

An **initial value problem (IVP)** for a homogeneous linear system has the form

$$
\mathbf{x}'(t)=A\mathbf{x}(t), \qquad \mathbf{x}(t_0)=\mathbf{x}_0
$$

where $A$ is a constant $2 \times 2$ matrix and $\mathbf{x}_0\in\mathbb{R}^2$ is the given initial state at $t=t_0$. In other words, in an IVP we seek the solution of

$$
\mathbf{x}'(t)=A\mathbf{x}(t)
$$

that passes through the point $\mathbf{x}_0$ at $t=t_0$.

To solve an IVP, we proceed as follows:

1. Find the general solution of the system $\mathbf{x}'(t)=A\mathbf{x}(t){:}$
$\mathbf{x}(t)=c_1\mathbf{x}_1(t)+c_2\mathbf{x}_2(t)$
2. Substitute $t=t_0$ into the general solution and use the initial condition $\mathbf{x}(t_0)=\mathbf{x}_0{:}$
$\mathbf{x}(t_0)=c_1\mathbf{x}_1(t_0)+c_2\mathbf{x}_2(t_0) \qquad\Rightarrow\qquad c_1\mathbf{x}_1(t_0)+c_2\mathbf{x}_2(t_0)=\mathbf{x}_0$
3. Solve the resulting linear system for $c_1$ and $c_2$, then substitute those values back into $\mathbf{x}(t)$.

The definition (and the procedure) above generalizes directly to the $n\times n$ case.

Let's see some concrete examples of solving an IVP.

## Solving an Initial Value Problem Given the General Solution

**Example:** Solve the initial value problem
$\begin{bmatrix}7 \\ -1\end{bmatrix}$
given that the general solution to our differential equation is
$\begin{bmatrix}3 \\ 1\end{bmatrix}$

The solution to the initial value problem is

$\begin{bmatrix}[math]\phantom{X \mid}[/math] \\ [math]\phantom{X \mid}[/math]\end{bmatrix}$

**Explanation**

To find the values of $c_1$ and $c_2$, we substitute $t=0$ in the general solution:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x(0) &= \begin{bmatrix}7 \\ -1\end{bmatrix} \\
c_{1}\begin{bmatrix}3 \\ 1\end{bmatrix}e^{0} + c_{2}\begin{bmatrix}2 \\ -1\end{bmatrix}e^{0} &= \begin{bmatrix}7 \\ -1\end{bmatrix} \\
\begin{bmatrix}3c_{1} + 2c_{2} \\ c_{1} - c_{2}\end{bmatrix} &= \begin{bmatrix}7 \\ -1\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

So, we have a system of linear equations

$$
{3c_{1} + 2c_{2} = 7; c_{1} - c_{2} =-1
$$

Multiplying the second equation by $2$ and adding the result to the first equation, we get

$$
5c_1= 5 \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad c_1 = 1
$$

Substituting this into the second equation, we obtain

$$
1 - c_2 = -1 \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad c_2 = 2
$$

Therefore, the solution to the initial value problem is

$$
\begin{aligned}
x(t) &= 1 \cdot \begin{bmatrix}3 \\ 1\end{bmatrix}e^{t} + 2 \cdot \begin{bmatrix}2 \\ -1\end{bmatrix}e^{-t} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}3 \\ 1\end{bmatrix}e^{t} + \begin{bmatrix}4 \\ -2\end{bmatrix}e^{-t}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Solving an Initial Value Problem Given the Eigenvectors and Eigenvalues of the Corresponding Matrix

**Example:** Let $\lambda_1=3$ and $\lambda_2=-2$ be eigenvalues of the $2\times 2$ matrix $A$ that correspond to eigenvectors
$\begin{bmatrix}6 \\ -2\end{bmatrix}$
Given that the position of a particle at time $t$ seconds is given by the vector $\mathbf{x}(t)$ and that $\mathbf{x}(t)$ is the solution to the initial value problem
$\begin{bmatrix}-11 \\ 1\end{bmatrix}$
find the position of the particle after $2$ seconds.

**Explanation**

Given a system of first-order linear ODEs in the form

$$
\mathbf{x}'(t)=A\mathbf{x}(t)
$$

where $A$ has two distinct eigenvalues ${\color{red}\lambda_1}, {\color{blue}\lambda_2}$ with corresponding eigenvectors ${\color{red}\mathbf{v}_1}$ and ${\color{blue}\mathbf{v}_2}$, the formula for the general solution is

$$
\mathbf{x}(t) = c_1{\color{red}\mathbf{v}_1} e^{{\color{red}\lambda_1} t} + c_2{\color{blue}\mathbf{v}_2} e^{{\color{blue}\lambda_2} t}, \qquad c_1,c_2 \in \mathbb{R}
$$

Therefore, the general solution to our matrix differential equation is

$$
x(t) = c_{1}\begin{bmatrix}6 \\ -2\end{bmatrix}e^{3t} + c_{2}\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ -3\end{bmatrix}e^{-2t}, c_{1}, c_{2} ∈ R
$$

Now, to find the values of $c_1$ and $c_2$, we substitute $t=0$ into the general solution:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x(0) &= \begin{bmatrix}-11 \\ 1\end{bmatrix} \\
c_{1}\begin{bmatrix}6 \\ -2\end{bmatrix}e^{0} + c_{2}\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ -3\end{bmatrix}e^{0} &= \begin{bmatrix}-11 \\ 1\end{bmatrix} \\
\begin{bmatrix}6c_{1} + c_{2} \\ -2c_{1} - 3c_{2}\end{bmatrix} &= \begin{bmatrix}-11 \\ 1\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

So, we have a system of linear equations

$$
{6c_{1} + c_{2} =-11; - 2c_{1} - 3c_{2} = 1
$$

Multiplying the first equation by $3$ and adding the result to the second equation, we get

$$
16c_1 = -32 \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad c_1=-2
$$

Substituting this into the second equation, we obtain

$$
-2\cdot(-2) -3c_2 = 1 \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad c_2=1
$$

Therefore, the solution for the initial value problem is

$$
\begin{aligned}
x(t) &= -2 \cdot \begin{bmatrix}6 \\ -2\end{bmatrix}e^{3t} + 1 \cdot \begin{bmatrix}1 \\ -3\end{bmatrix}e^{-2t} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}-12 \\ 4\end{bmatrix}e^{3t} + \begin{bmatrix}1 \\ -3\end{bmatrix}e^{-2t}
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we calculate $\mathbf x(2)$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x(2) &= \begin{bmatrix}-12 \\ 4\end{bmatrix}e^{3 \cdot 2} + \begin{bmatrix}1 \\ -3\end{bmatrix}e^{-2 \cdot 2} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}-12e^{6} + e^{-4} \\ 4e^{6} - 3e^{-4}\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Solving an Initial Value Problem for a Linear System of Differential Equations

**Example:** Find the solution to the initial value problem
${x_{1}^{′}(t) = 3x_{1}(t) - x_{2}(t); x_{2}^{′}(t) = 6x_{1}(t) - 4x_{2}(t)$

*Hint: The eigenvalues of the system's matrix are $\lambda_1=2$ and $\lambda_2=-3$.*

**Explanation**

Let's construct the matrix differential equation

$$
\mathbf{x}'(t)=A\mathbf{x}(t)
$$

for the given system
$\begin{bmatrix}x_{1}^{′}(t) \\ x_{2}^{′}(t)\end{bmatrix}$

- We are given that the eigenvalues are
$\lambda_1= 2 \qquad\text{and}\qquad \lambda_2= -3$.
- Let's go through the process of finding the eigenvectors.For $\lambda_1 =2$, we get
$\begin{bmatrix}1 & -1 \\ 6 & -6\end{bmatrix}$
So, we get an eigenvector
$\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 1\end{bmatrix}$For $\lambda_2 =-3$, we get
$\begin{bmatrix}6 & -1 \\ 6 & -1\end{bmatrix}$
So, we get an eigenvector
$\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 6\end{bmatrix}$

Now, we write down the general solution to the given linear system of differential equations:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x(t) &= c_{1}v_{1}e^{λ_{1}t} + c_{2}v_{2}e^{λ_{2}t} \\
&= c_{1}\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 1\end{bmatrix}e^{2t} + c_{2}\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 6\end{bmatrix}e^{-3t}, c_{1}, c_{2} ∈ R
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, to find the values of $c_1$ and $c_2$, we substitute $t=0$ in the general solution and use the initial values:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x(0) &= \begin{bmatrix}5 \\ 0\end{bmatrix} \\
c_{1}\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 1\end{bmatrix}e^{0} + c_{2}\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 6\end{bmatrix}e^{0} &= \begin{bmatrix}5 \\ 0\end{bmatrix} \\
\begin{bmatrix}c_{1} + c_{2} \\ c_{1} + 6c_{2}\end{bmatrix} &= \begin{bmatrix}5 \\ 0\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

So, we have a system of linear equations

$$
{c_{1} + c_{2} = 5; c_{1} + 6c_{2} = 0
$$

Subtracting the first equation from the second equation, we get

$$
5c_{2} =-5⟹c_{2} =-1
$$

Substituting $c_2 = -1$ into the first equation, we obtain

$$
c_{1} - 1 = 5⟹c_{1} = 6
$$

Finally, we write down the solution to the initial value problem:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x(t) &= 6\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 1\end{bmatrix}e^{2t} - \begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 6\end{bmatrix}e^{-3t}
\end{aligned}
$$
