## Introduction

Let's calculate the flux of the vector field

$$
\mathbf{F}(x,y,z)=\left\langle (x-y)^2, \:(x+y)^2, \: 12xy \right\rangle
$$

through the surface $S$ defined by the Cartesian equation

$$
z=2y-2x
$$

for

$$
0 \leq x \leq 1
$$

and

$$
0 \leq y \leq 1
$$

For surfaces like this, substituting $x=u$ and $y=v$, we obtain a parametrization

$$
\mathbf{r}(u,v) = \left\langle u,\: v,\: 2v-2u \right\rangle
$$

over the domain

$$
D = \big\{(u,v) \:: \: 0 \leq u \leq 1, \: 0 \leq v \leq 1 \big\}
$$

Recall that for a surface $S$ with parametrization $\mathbf{r}(u,v)$ for $(u,v) \in D$, the flux of a vector field $\mathbf{F}$ over $S$ is given by

$$
\iint\limits_S \mathbf{F} \cdot \textrm{d}\mathbf{S} = \iint\limits_S \mathbf{F} \cdot \mathbf{n} \,\textrm{d}S = \iint\limits_D \mathbf{F}(\mathbf{r}(u,v)) \cdot (\mathbf{r}'_u \times \mathbf{r}'_v) \,\textrm{d}A
$$

We now compute the tangent vectors to the grid curves:

$$
\begin{aligned}
r_{u}^{′} &= ⟨\frac{∂x}{∂u}, \frac{∂y}{∂u}, \frac{∂z}{∂u}⟩ \\
&= ⟨\frac{∂}{∂u}(u), \frac{∂}{∂u}(v), \frac{∂}{∂u}(2v - 2u)⟩ \\
&= ⟨1, 0,-2⟩ \\
r_{v}^{′} &= ⟨\frac{∂x}{∂v}, \frac{∂y}{∂v}, \frac{∂z}{∂v}⟩ \\
&= ⟨\frac{∂}{∂v}(u), \frac{∂}{∂v}(v), \frac{∂}{∂v}(2v - 2u)⟩ \\
&= ⟨0, 1, 2⟩
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the fundamental vector product is

$$
\begin{aligned}
r_{u}^{′} \cdot r_{v}^{′} &= \begin{vmatrix}i & j & k \\ 1 & 0 & -2 \\ 0 & 1 & 2\end{vmatrix} \\
&= ⟨\begin{vmatrix}0 & -2 \\ 1 & 2\end{vmatrix}, -\begin{vmatrix}1 & -2 \\ 0 & 2\end{vmatrix},\begin{vmatrix}1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1\end{vmatrix}⟩ \\
&= ⟨2,-2, 1⟩
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we substitute the coordinate expressions of $\mathbf{r}(u,v)$ into $\mathbf{F}(x,y,z){:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
F(r(u, v)) &= ⟨(x - y)^{2}, (x + y)^{2}, 12xy⟩ \mid _{x = u, y = v, z = 2v - 2u} \\
&= ⟨(u - v)^{2}, (u + v)^{2}, 12uv⟩
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we calculate the dot product of $\mathbf{F}$ and $\mathbf{r}'_u \times \mathbf{r}'_v{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
F(r(u, v)) \cdot (r_{u}^{′} \cdot r_{v}^{′}) &= ⟨(u - v)^{2}, (u + v)^{2}, 12uv⟩ \cdot ⟨2,-2, 1⟩ \\
&= (u - v)^{2} \cdot 2 + (u + v)^{2} \cdot (-2) + 12uv \cdot 1 \\
&= 4uv
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the flux of $\mathbf F$ through $S$ is given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
∬_(S)⟨(x - y)^{2}, (x + y)^{2}, 12xy⟩ \cdot dS &= ∬_(D)F(r(u, v)) \cdot (r_{u}^{′} \cdot r_{v}^{′})dA \\
&= ∬_(D)4uvdA \\
&= ∫_{0}^{1}∫_{0}^{1}4uvdvdu \\
&= ∫_{0}^{1}2udu \cdot ∫_{0}^{1}2vdv \\
&= [u^{2}]_{0}^{1} \cdot [v^{2}]_{0}^{1} \\
&= (1^{2} - 0^{2}) \cdot (1^{2} - 0^{2}) \\
&= 1
\end{aligned}
$$

For a surface $S$ with parametrization $\mathbf{r}(u,v)$ for $(u,v) \in D$, the flux of a vector field $\mathbf{F}$ over $S$ is given by

$$
\iint\limits_S \mathbf{F} \cdot \textrm{d}\mathbf{S} = \iint\limits_S \mathbf{F} \cdot \mathbf{n} \,\textrm{d}S = \iint\limits_D \mathbf{F}(\mathbf{r}(u,v)) \cdot (\mathbf{r}'_u \times \mathbf{r}'_v) \,\textrm{d}A

\end{aligned}
$$

Now, the fundamental vector product is

$$
\begin{aligned}
r_{u}^{′} \cdot r_{v}^{′} &= \begin{vmatrix}i & j & k \\ 1 & 0 & -3 \\ 0 & 1 & 2\end{vmatrix} \\
&= ⟨\begin{vmatrix}0 & -3 \\ 1 & 2\end{vmatrix}, -\begin{vmatrix}1 & -3 \\ 0 & 2\end{vmatrix},\begin{vmatrix}1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1\end{vmatrix}⟩ \\
&= ⟨3,-2, 1⟩
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we substitute the coordinate expressions of $\mathbf{r}(u,v)$ into $\mathbf{F}(x,y,z){:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
F(r(u, v)) &= ⟨z + 2y, 3y + z, x - z⟩ \mid _{x = u, y = v, z =-3u + 2v} \\
&= ⟨(-3u + 2v) + 2v, 3v + (-3u + 2v), u - (-3u + 2v)⟩ \\

\begin{aligned}

&= u
\end{aligned}

$$
\begin{aligned}
∬_(S)⟨z + 2y, 3y + z, x - z⟩ \cdot dS &= ∬_(D)F(r(u, v)) \cdot (r_{u}^{′} \cdot r_{v}^{′})dA \\
&= ∬_(D)udA \\
&= ∫_{0}^{2}∫_{0}^{1}udvdu
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the missing expression is ${\color{blue}u}$.

## Formulas for Flux Over Cartesian Surfaces

Let's now derive a general formula for the flux through an *upward-oriented* Cartesian surface.

For a surface $S$ with parametrization $\mathbf{r}(u,v)$ for $(u,v) \in D$, the flux of a vector field $\mathbf{F}$ over $S$ is given by

$$
\iint\limits_S \mathbf{F} \cdot \textrm{d}\mathbf{S} = \iint\limits_S \mathbf{F} \cdot \mathbf{n} \,\textrm{d}S = \iint\limits_D \mathbf{F}(\mathbf{r}(u,v)) \cdot (\mathbf{r}'_u \times \mathbf{r}'_v) \,\textrm{d}A
$$

Consider a surface $S$ given by the equation $z=g(x,y)$. The canonical parameterization of the surface is

$$
\mathbf{r}(x,y) = \left\langle x,\: y,\: g(x,y) \right\rangle
$$

Computing the tangent vectors to the grid curves, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
r_{x}^{′} &= ⟨1, 0, \frac{∂g}{∂x}⟩ \\
r_{y}^{′} &= ⟨0, 1, \frac{∂g}{∂y}⟩
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, the fundamental vector product is

$$
\begin{aligned}
r_{x}^{′} \cdot r_{y}^{′} &= \begin{vmatrix}i & j\end{vmatrix}k \\
1\begin{vmatrix}0 & \frac{∂g}{∂x} \\ 0 & 1\end{vmatrix}\frac{∂g}{∂y} &= ⟨ - \frac{∂g}{∂x},-\frac{∂g}{∂y}, 1⟩
\end{aligned}
$$

Notice that the $\mathbf k$-component is positive, which indicates that our surface is upward-oriented.

Therefore, the surface integral can be written as

$$
\begin{aligned}
∬_(S)F \cdot dS &= ∬_(D)F(r(x, y)) \cdot (r_{x}^{′} \cdot r_{y}^{′})dA \\
&= ∬_(D)⟨P, Q, R⟩ \cdot ⟨ - \frac{∂g}{∂x},-\frac{∂g}{∂y}, 1⟩dA \\
&= ∬_(D)(-P\frac{∂g}{∂x} - Q\frac{∂g}{∂y} + R)dA
\end{aligned}
$$

We summarize as follows:

- *For a surface $S$ given by $z = g(x,y)$ for $(x,y) \in D$, the flux of a vector field $\mathbf{F}(x,y,z) = \left\langle P,\: Q,\: R \right\rangle$ over $S$ is given by*
$\iint\limits_S \mathbf{F} \cdot \textrm{d}\mathbf{S} = \iint\limits_D \left(-P\dfrac{\partial g}{\partial x} - Q\dfrac{\partial g}{\partial y} + R \right) \textrm{d}A$.

We have analogous results for surfaces of the form $y = h(x,z)$ and $x=k(y,z){:}$

- *For a surface $S$ given by $y = h(x,z)$ for $(x,z) \in D$, the flux of a vector field $\mathbf{F}(x,y,z) = \left\langle P,\: Q,\: R \right\rangle$ over $S$ is given by*
$\iint\limits_S \mathbf{F} \cdot \textrm{d}\mathbf{S} = \iint\limits_D \left(-P\dfrac{\partial h}{\partial x} + Q - R\dfrac{\partial h}{\partial z} \right) \textrm{d}A$,
*where the unit normal to the surface is chosen such that the $\mathbf j$-component is positive.*
- *For a surface $S$ given $x = k(y,z)$ for $(y,z) \in D$, the flux of a vector field $\mathbf{F}(x,y,z) = \left\langle P,\: Q,\: R \right\rangle$ over $S$ is given by*
$\iint\limits_S \mathbf{F} \cdot \textrm{d}\mathbf{S} = \iint\limits_D \left(P - Q\dfrac{\partial k}{\partial y} - R\dfrac{\partial k}{\partial z} \right) \textrm{d}A$,
*where the unit normal to the surface is chosen such that the $\mathbf i$-component is positive.*

## Expressing Flux Over a Surface as a Repeated Integral Using a Formula

**Example:** For a surface $S$ given by $y = h(x,z)$ for $(x,z) \in D$, the flux of the vector field $\mathbf{F}(x,y,z) = \left\langle P,\: Q,\: R \right\rangle$ over $S$ is given by
$\iint\limits_S \mathbf{F} \cdot \textrm{d}\mathbf{S} = \iint\limits_D \Bigg(\bbox[3pt, border:1pt solid black]{\phantom{A}} \dfrac{\partial h}{\partial x} + Q - \bbox[3pt, border:1pt solid black]{\phantom{A}} \dfrac{\partial h}{\partial z} \Bigg) \: \textrm{d}A$,

From left to right, what are the missing parts of the formula?

*Hint: The orientation of $S$ should be chosen such that the unit normal vector has a positive $\mathbf j$-component.*

**Explanation**

For a surface $S$ with parametrization $\mathbf{r}(u,v)$ for $(u,v) \in D$, the flux of a vector field $\mathbf{F}$ over $S$ is given by

$$
\iint\limits_S \mathbf{F} \cdot \textrm{d}\mathbf{S} = \iint\limits_S \mathbf{F} \cdot \mathbf{n} \,\textrm{d}S = \iint\limits_D \mathbf{F}(\mathbf{r}(u,v)) \cdot (\mathbf{r}'_u \times \mathbf{r}'_v) \,\textrm{d}A
$$

Since our surface $S$ is of the form $y = h(x,z)$, it can be parameterized as

$$
\mathbf{r}(x,z) = \left\langle x, \: h(x,z),\: z \right\rangle
$$

We now compute the tangent vectors to the grid curves:

$$
\begin{aligned}
r_{x}^{′} &= ⟨1, \frac{∂h}{∂x}, 0⟩ \\
r_{z}^{′} &= ⟨0, \frac{∂h}{∂z}, 1⟩
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, the fundamental vector product is

$$
\begin{aligned}
r_{x}^{′} \cdot r_{z}^{′} &= \begin{vmatrix}i & j\end{vmatrix}k \\
1\begin{vmatrix}\frac{∂h}{∂x} & 0 \\ 0 & \frac{∂h}{∂z}\end{vmatrix}1 &= ⟨\frac{∂h}{∂x},-1, \frac{∂h}{∂z}⟩
\end{aligned}
$$

However, notice that the $\mathbf j$-component is negative. Therefore, we select the normal vector that points in the *opposite* direction:

$$
\mathbf{r}'_z \times \mathbf{r}'_x =-\mathbf{r}'_x \times \mathbf{r}'_z = \left \langle -\dfrac{\partial h}{\partial x}, \: 1, \: -\dfrac{\partial h}{\partial z} \right \rangle
$$

Therefore, the surface integral can be written as

$$
\begin{aligned}
∬_(S)F \cdot dS &= ∬_(D)F(r(x, z)) \cdot (r_{x}^{′} \cdot r_{z}^{′})dA \\
&= ∬_(D)⟨P, Q, R⟩ \cdot ⟨ - \frac{∂h}{∂x}, 1,-\frac{∂h}{∂z}⟩dA \\
&= ∬_(D)(-P\frac{∂h}{∂x} + Q - R\frac{∂h}{∂z})dA
\end{aligned}
$$

## Evaluating Flux Over a Surface

**Example:** Calculate the flux of the vector field

$\mathbf F(x, y, z) = \left\langle -y, \: x, \: z+2 \right\rangle$,

where the surface $S$ is defined by $z=x^2+y^2$ for $0 \leq x \leq 1$ and $0 \leq y \leq 1$.

**Explanation**

For a surface $S$ given by $z = g(x,y)$ for $(x,y) \in D$, the flux of a vector field

$$
\mathbf{F}(x,y,z) = \left\langle P,\: Q,\: R \right\rangle
$$

over $S$ is given by

$$
\iint\limits_S \mathbf{F} \cdot \textrm{d}\mathbf{S} = \iint\limits_D \left(-P\dfrac{\partial g}{\partial x} - Q\dfrac{\partial g}{\partial y} + R \right) \textrm{d}A
$$

We first compute the partial derivatives of the graph $z=g(x,y){:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{∂g}{∂x} &= \frac{∂}{∂x}(x^{2} + y^{2}) = 2x \\
\frac{∂g}{∂y} &= \frac{∂}{∂y}(x^{2} + y^{2}) = 2y
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we substitute the equation $z=g(x,y)$ into $\mathbf F(x,y,z){:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
F(x, y, g(x, y)) &= ⟨ - y, x, z + 2⟩ \mid _{z = x^{2} + y^{2}} \\
&= ⟨- y_(⏟)_(P), x_(⏟)_(Q), x^{2} + y^{2} + 2_(⏟)_(R)⟩
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, we can evaluate our surface integral as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
∬_(S)⟨ - y, x, z + 2⟩ \cdot dS &= ∬_(D)(-P\frac{∂g}{∂x} - Q\frac{∂g}{∂y} + R)dA \\
&= ∬_(D)(- (-y) \cdot (2x) - (x) \cdot (2y) + x^{2} + y^{2} + 2)dA \\
&= ∬_(D)(2xy - 2xy + x^{2} + y^{2} + 2)dA \\
&= ∫_{0}^{1}∫_{0}^{1}(x^{2} + y^{2} + 2)dydx \\
&= ∫_{0}^{1}[x^{2}y + \frac{y^{3}}{3} + 2y]_{0}^{1}dx \\
&= ∫_{0}^{1}(x^{2} + \frac{1}{3} + 2)dx \\
&= ∫_{0}^{1}(x^{2} + \frac{7}{3})dx \\
&= [\frac{x^{3}}{3} + \frac{7x}{3}]_{0}^{1} \\
&= \frac{1}{3} + \frac{7}{3} \\
&= \frac{8}{3}
\end{aligned}
$$
