## Introduction

Recall that the flux of the vector field $\mathbf F$ across a smooth, orientable surface $S$ with respect to the unit normal vector $\mathbf{n}$ is given by

$$
\iint\limits_S \mathbf F \cdot \textrm{d} \mathbf{S} = \iint\limits_S \mathbf F \cdot {\color{blue}\mathbf{n}} \,\textrm{d}S
$$

where ${\color{blue}\mathbf{n}}$ is a unit normal vector that defines the orientation of the surface.

![](<../Source/5.3.5. Calculating Flux Through Parametric Surfaces/Images/2de37c52acafc23a3af759d57fbb9866.png>)

If $S$ is given by a parametric equation of the form

$$
\mathbf{r}=\mathbf{r}(u,v), \qquad (u,v)\in D\subseteq \mathbb{R}^2
$$

we can determine the unit normal vector using the fundamental product as follows:

$$
{\color{blue}\mathbf{n}} = \dfrac{\mathbf{r}'_u \times \mathbf{r}'_v}{\| \mathbf{r}'_u \times \mathbf{r}'_v \|}
$$

Substituting this into the formula for the flux, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
∬_(S)F \cdot dS &= ∬_(S)F \cdot ndS \\
&= ∬_(S)F \cdot \frac{r_{u}^{′} \cdot r_{v}^{′}}{‖r_{u}^{′} \cdot r_{v}^{′}‖}dS \\
&= ∬_(D)F(r(u, v)) \cdot \frac{r_{u}^{′} \cdot r_{v}^{′}}{‖r_{u}^{′} \cdot r_{v}^{′}‖}‖r_{u}^{′} \cdot r_{v}^{′}‖dA \\
&= ∬_(D)F(r(u, v)) \cdot (r_{u}^{′} \cdot r_{v}^{′})dA
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, if $S$ is a smooth, orientable surface given in parametric form by

$$
\mathbf{r}=\mathbf{r}(u,v)
$$

for $(u,v)\in D \subseteq \mathbb{R}^2$, then the flux of the vector field $\mathbf{F}$ across $S$ is given by

$$
\iint\limits_S \mathbf F \cdot \textrm{d} \mathbf{S} = \iint\limits_D \mathbf F(\mathbf{r}(u,v)) \cdot (\mathbf{r}'_u \times \mathbf{r}'_v) \: \textrm{d}A
$$

Let's see some examples.

## Expressing Flux Through a Surface as a Double Integral

**Example:** Consider the vector field $\mathbf F$ and the surface $S$ with parametrization $\mathbf r(u,v)$, where

$\mathbf{F}(x,y,z) =\left\langle x+y,\: x-y,\: xyz \right\rangle, \quad \mathbf{r}(u,v) = \left\langle u^2,\: v^2,\: 1 \right\rangle, \quad (u,v)\in D\subset \mathbb{R}^2$.

If the flux of $\mathbf F$ across $S$ with respect to $\mathbf{n}$ is given by

$AAAAAAA$

what is the missing expression?

**Explanation**

For a surface $S$ with parametrization $\mathbf{r}(u,v)$ for $(u,v) \in D$, the flux of a vector field $\mathbf{F}$ over $S$ is given by

$$
\iint\limits_S \mathbf{F} \cdot \textrm{d}\mathbf{S} = \iint\limits_S \mathbf{F} \cdot \mathbf{n} \,\textrm{d}S = \iint\limits_D \mathbf{F}(\mathbf{r}(u,v)) \cdot (\mathbf{r}'_u \times \mathbf{r}'_v) \,\textrm{d}A
$$

We first compute the tangent vectors to the grid curves:

$$
\begin{aligned}
r_{u}^{′} &= ⟨\frac{∂x}{∂u}, \frac{∂y}{∂u}, \frac{∂z}{∂u}⟩ \\
&= ⟨\frac{∂}{∂u}(u^{2}), \frac{∂}{∂u}(v^{2}), \frac{∂}{∂u}(1)⟩ \\
&= ⟨2u, 0, 0⟩ \\
r_{v}^{′} &= ⟨\frac{∂x}{∂v}, \frac{∂y}{∂v}, \frac{∂z}{∂v}⟩ \\
&= ⟨\frac{∂}{∂v}(u^{2}), \frac{∂}{∂v}(v^{2}), \frac{∂}{∂v}(1)⟩ \\
&= ⟨0, 2v, 0⟩
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, the fundamental vector product is

$$
\begin{aligned}
r_{u}^{′} \cdot r_{v}^{′} &= \begin{vmatrix}i & j & k \\ 2u & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 2v & 0\end{vmatrix} \\
&= ⟨\begin{vmatrix}0 & 0 \\ 2v & 0\end{vmatrix}, -\begin{vmatrix}2u & 0 \\ 0 & 0\end{vmatrix},\begin{vmatrix}2u & 0 \\ 0 & 2v\end{vmatrix}⟩ \\
&= ⟨0, 0, 4uv⟩
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we substitute the coordinate expressions of $\mathbf{r}(u,v)$ into $\mathbf{F}(x,y,z){:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
F(r(u, v)) &= ⟨x + y, x - y, xyz⟩ \mid _{x = u^{2}y = v^{2}z = 1} \\
&= ⟨u^{2} + v^{2}, u^{2} - v^{2}, u^{2}v^{2}⟩
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we calculate the dot product of $\mathbf{F}$ and $\mathbf{r}'_u \times \mathbf{r}'_v{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
F(r(u, v)) \cdot (r_{u}^{′} \cdot r_{v}^{′}) &= ⟨u^{2} + v^{2}, u^{2} - v^{2}, u^{2}v^{2}⟩ \cdot ⟨0, 0, 4uv⟩ \\
&= (u^{2} + v^{2}) \cdot 0 + (u^{2} - v^{2}) \cdot 0 + u^{2}v^{2} \cdot 4uv \\
&= 4u^{3}v^{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the surface integral can be written as

$$
\begin{aligned}
∬_(S)F \cdot dS &= ∬_(D)F(r(u, v)) \cdot (r_{u}^{′} \cdot r_{v}^{′})dA \\
&= ∬_(D)4 u 3 v 3dA
\end{aligned}
$$

## Evaluating Flux Through a Surface

**Example:** Evaluate the surface integral
$\iint\limits_S \left\langle z, \: x, \: y \right\rangle \cdot \textrm{d}\mathbf{S}$

where the parametric surface $S$ is defined by the equations

$\mathbf{r}(u,v) = \left\langle 1+2u+v, \: u+v, \: u-v \right\rangle$

for $0 \leq u \leq 2$ and $0 \leq v \leq 1$.

**Explanation**

For a surface $S$ with parametrization $\mathbf{r}(u,v)$ for $(u,v) \in D$, the flux of a vector field $\mathbf{F}$ over $S$ is given by

$$
\iint\limits_S \mathbf{F} \cdot \textrm{d}\mathbf{S} = \iint\limits_S \mathbf{F} \cdot \mathbf{n} \,\textrm{d}S = \iint\limits_D \mathbf{F}(\mathbf{r}(u,v)) \cdot (\mathbf{r}'_u \times \mathbf{r}'_v) \,\textrm{d}A
$$

We first compute the tangent vectors to the grid curves:

$$
\begin{aligned}
r_{u}^{′} &= ⟨\frac{∂x}{∂u}, \frac{∂y}{∂u}, \frac{∂z}{∂u}⟩ \\
&= ⟨\frac{∂}{∂u}(1 + 2u + v), \frac{∂}{∂u}(u + v), \frac{∂}{∂u}(u - v)⟩ \\
&= ⟨2, 1, 1⟩ \\
r_{v}^{′} &= ⟨\frac{∂x}{∂v}, \frac{∂y}{∂v}, \frac{∂z}{∂v}⟩ \\
&= ⟨\frac{∂}{∂v}(1 + 2u + v), \frac{∂}{∂v}(u + v), \frac{∂}{∂v}(u - v)⟩ \\
&= ⟨1, 1,-1⟩
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, the fundamental vector product is

$$
\begin{aligned}
r_{u}^{′} \cdot r_{v}^{′} &= \begin{vmatrix}i & j & k \\ 2 & 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 & -1\end{vmatrix} \\
&= ⟨\begin{vmatrix}1 & 1 \\ 1 & -1\end{vmatrix}, -\begin{vmatrix}2 & 1 \\ 1 & -1\end{vmatrix},\begin{vmatrix}2 & 1 \\ 1 & 1\end{vmatrix}⟩ \\
&= ⟨ - 2, 3, 1⟩
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we substitute the coordinate expressions of $\mathbf{r}(u,v)$ into $\mathbf{F}(x,y,z){:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
F(r(u, v)) &= ⟨z, x, y⟩ \mid _{x = 1 + 2u + vy = u + vz = u - v} \\
&= ⟨u - v, 1 + 2u + v, u + v⟩
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we calculate the dot product of $\mathbf{F}$ and $\mathbf{r}'_u \times \mathbf{r}'_v{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
F(r(u, v)) \cdot (r_{u}^{′} \cdot r_{v}^{′}) &= ⟨u - v, 1 + 2u + v, u + v⟩ \cdot ⟨ - 2, 3, 1⟩ \\
&= (u - v) \cdot (-2) + (1 + 2u + v) \cdot 3 + (u + v) \cdot 1 \\
&=-2u + 2v + 3 + 6u + 3v + u + v \\
&= 5u + 6v + 3
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the surface integral evaluates to

$$
\begin{aligned}
∬_(S)⟨z, x, y⟩ \cdot dS &= ∬_(D)F(r(u, v)) \cdot (r_{u}^{′} \cdot r_{v}^{′})dA \\
&= ∬_(D)(5u + 6v + 3)dA \\
&= ∫_{0}^{2}∫_{0}^{1}(5u + 6v + 3)dvdu \\
&= ∫_{0}^{2}[5uv + 3v^{2} + 3v]_{v = 0}^{v = 1}du \\
&= ∫_{0}^{2}(5u + 6)du \\
&= [\frac{5}{2}u^{2} + 6u]_{0}^{2} \\
&= 10 + 12 \\
&= 22
\end{aligned}
$$
