## Introduction

In a previous lesson, we saw how Newton’s method can be used to find local minima or maxima of a smooth function $f: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ by approximating the function near the current guess with a parabola.

Recall that in one variable, Newton’s update rule is

$$
x_{n+1} = x_n - \frac{f'(x_n)}{f''(x_n)}
$$

where $f'(x)$ is the first derivative, and $f''(x)$ is the second derivative.

To generalize this idea to higher dimensions, we need analogues of the first and second derivatives.

Recall that the gradient vector $\nabla f(\mathbf{x})$ generalizes the first derivative to multivariable functions, and the Hessian matrix $H_f(\mathbf{x})$ generalizes the second derivative.

Like before, we aim to find critical points, which are the points where the gradient vanishes:

$$
\nabla f(\mathbf{x}) = \mathbf{0}
$$

So once again, optimization becomes a root-finding problem, but now for the gradient vector $\nabla f(\mathbf{x})$.

Just like in one dimension, we approximate $f(\mathbf{x})$ near our current guess $\mathbf{x}_n$ using a second-order Taylor expansion

$$
f(\mathbf{x}) \approx f(\mathbf{x}_n) + \nabla f(\mathbf{x}_n)^T (\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{x}_n) + \frac{1}{2} (\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{x}_n)^T H_f(\mathbf{x}_n) (\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{x}_n)
$$

This is the multivariable analogue of a parabola, a *quadratic surface*.

To find the minimum of this quadratic surface, we compute its gradient with respect to $\mathbf{x}$ and set it equal to zero. The gradient of the approximation is

$$
\nabla f(\mathbf{x}) \approx \nabla f(\mathbf{x}_n) + H_f(\mathbf{x}_n)(\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{x}_n)
$$

We then set the gradient equal to the zero vector:

$$
\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_n) + H_f(\mathbf{x}_n)(\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{x}_n) = \mathbf{0}
$$

Finally, we isolate $\mathbf{x}$, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
H_{f}(x_{n})(x - x_{n}) &= - ∇f(x_{n}) \\
x - x_{n} &= - [H_{f}(x_{n})]^{-1}∇f(x_{n}) \\
x &= x_{n} - [H_{f}(x_{n})]^{-1}∇f(x_{n})
\end{aligned}
$$

This new value becomes our next guess, which we call $\mathbf{x}_{n+1}$. So, we write

$$
\bbox[4pt,border: 1px solid lightgray]{\mathbf{x}_{n+1} = \mathbf{x}_n - [H_f(\mathbf{x}_n)]^{-1} \nabla f(\mathbf{x}_n).}
$$

Newton’s method can be extremely fast, but only under the following conditions:

- The function $f$ is twice continuously differentiable (i.e., smooth).
- The starting point $\mathbf{x}_0$ is close to a critical point.
- The Hessian $H_f(\mathbf{x})$ is invertible and preferably positive definite near the solution.

When these conditions are met, Newton’s method often converges in just a few iterations.

## A Worked Example

Suppose we wish to find a critical point of the function $f(x,y)$ in the region $[0,2]\times[0,2] \subseteq \mathbb{R}^2$, where

$$
f(x,y) =x^2y-2xy^2+x^3-4x
$$

Let’s calculate one iteration of the Newton-Raphson method, starting at the point

$$
\mathbf x_0 = \big[1, \: 1 \big]^T
$$

The Newton-Raphson method of optimizing a multivariable function $f: \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}$ is

$$
\mathbf{x}_{n+1} = \mathbf{x}_n - [H_f(\mathbf{x}_n)]^{-1} \nabla{f}(\mathbf{x}_n)
$$

where $H_f(\mathbf{x}_n)$ and $\nabla{f}(\mathbf{x}_n)$ are the Hessian and gradient of $f$, respectively, evaluated at $\mathbf{x}_n$.

In our case, the gradient is

$$
\begin{aligned}
∇f(x) &= \begin{bmatrix}\frac{∂}{∂x}(x^{2}y - 2xy^{2} + x^{3} - 4x) \\ \frac{∂}{∂y}(x^{2}y - 2xy^{2} + x^{3} - 4x)\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}2xy - 2y^{2} + 3x^{2} - 4 \\ x^{2} - 4xy\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

and the Hessian is

$$
\begin{aligned}
H_{f}(x) &= \begin{bmatrix}\frac{∂}{∂x}(2xy - 2y^{2} + 3x^{2} - 4) & \frac{∂}{∂x}(x^{2} - 4xy) \\ \frac{∂}{∂y}(2xy - 2y^{2} + 3x^{2} - 4) & \frac{∂}{∂y}(x^{2} - 4xy)\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}2y + 6x & 2x - 4y \\ 2x - 4y & -4x\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, our Newton-Raphson formula is

$$
\begin{bmatrix}2y + 6x & 2x - 4y \\ 2x - 4y & -4x\end{bmatrix}
$$

Starting with
$\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 1\end{bmatrix}$ we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_{1} &= x_{0} - \begin{bmatrix}2y + 6x & 2x - 4y \\ 2x - 4y & -4x\end{bmatrix}_{x_{0}}^{-1}\begin{bmatrix}2xy - 2y^{2} + 3x^{2} - 4 \\ x^{2} - 4xy\end{bmatrix}_{x_{0}} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 1\end{bmatrix} - \begin{bmatrix}2(1) + 6(1) & 2(1) - 4(1) \\ 2(1) - 4(1) & -4(1)\end{bmatrix}^{-1}\begin{bmatrix}2(1)(1) - 2(1)^{2} + 3(1)^{2} - 4 \\ (1)^{2} - 4(1)(1)\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 1\end{bmatrix} - \begin{bmatrix}8 & -2 \\ -2 & -4\end{bmatrix}^{-1}\begin{bmatrix}-1 \\ -3\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 1\end{bmatrix} - (1)/((8) \cdot (-4) - (-2) \cdot (-2))\begin{bmatrix}-4 & 2 \\ 2 & 8\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}-1 \\ -3\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 1\end{bmatrix} - \frac{1}{-36}\begin{bmatrix}-2 \\ -26\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 1\end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix}-\frac{1}{18} \\ -\frac{13}{18}\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}\frac{17}{18} \\ \frac{5}{18}\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Computing One Iteration of the Newton-Raphson Method

**Example:** Suppose we wish to find a critical point of the function $f(x,y)$ in the region $[0,1] \times [0,1] \subseteq \mathbb{R}^2$, where
$f(x,y) = 2x^2y-2x^2+3xy^2+3xy+1$.
Calculate one iteration of the Newton-Raphson process for optimization that starts at $\mathbf x_0 = \big[1, \: 1 \big]^T$. Round your final answer to three decimal places (if needed).

**Explanation**

The Newton-Raphson method of optimizing a multivariable function $f: \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}$ is

$$
\mathbf{x}_{n+1} = \mathbf{x}_n - [H_f(\mathbf{x}_n)]^{-1} \nabla{f}(\mathbf{x}_n)
$$

where $H(\mathbf{x}_n)$ and $\nabla{f}(\mathbf{x}_n)$ are the Hessian and gradient of $f$, respectively, evaluated at $\mathbf{x}_n$.

In our case, the gradient is

$$
\begin{aligned}
∇f(x) &= \begin{bmatrix}\frac{∂}{∂x}(2x^{2}y - 2x^{2} + 3xy^{2} + 3xy + 1) \\ \frac{∂}{∂y}(2x^{2}y - 2x^{2} + 3xy^{2} + 3xy + 1)\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}4xy - 4x + 3y^{2} + 3y \\ 2x^{2} + 6xy + 3x\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

and the Hessian is

$$
\begin{aligned}
H_{f}(x) &= \begin{bmatrix}\frac{∂}{∂x}(4xy - 4x + 3y^{2} + 3y) & \frac{∂}{∂x}(2x^{2} + 6xy + 3x) \\ \frac{∂}{∂y}(4xy - 4x + 3y^{2} + 3y) & \frac{∂}{∂y}(2x^{2} + 6xy + 3x)\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}4y - 4 & 4x + 6y + 3 \\ 4x + 6y + 3 & 6x\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, our Newton-Raphson formula is

$$
\begin{bmatrix}4y - 4 & 4x + 6y + 3 \\ 4x + 6y + 3 & 6x\end{bmatrix}
$$

Starting with
$\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 1\end{bmatrix}$ we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_{1} &= x_{0} - \begin{bmatrix}4y - 4 & 4x + 6y + 3 \\ 4x + 6y + 3 & 6x\end{bmatrix}_{x_{0}}^{-1}\begin{bmatrix}4xy - 4x + 3y^{2} + 3y \\ 2x^{2} + 6xy + 3x\end{bmatrix}_{x_{0}} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 1\end{bmatrix} - \begin{bmatrix}4(1) - 4 & 4(1) + 6(1) + 3 \\ 4(1) + 6(1) + 3 & 6(1)\end{bmatrix}^{-1}\begin{bmatrix}4(1)(1) - 4(1) + 3(1)^{2} + 3(1) \\ 2(1)^{2} + 6(1)(1) + 3(1)\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 1\end{bmatrix} - \begin{bmatrix}0 & 13 \\ 13 & 6\end{bmatrix}^{-1}\begin{bmatrix}6 \\ 11\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 1\end{bmatrix} - \frac{1}{0 \cdot 6 - 13 \cdot 13}\begin{bmatrix}6 & -13 \\ -13 & 0\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}6 \\ 11\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 1\end{bmatrix} + \frac{1}{169}\begin{bmatrix}-107 \\ -78\end{bmatrix} \\
&\approx \begin{bmatrix}0.367 \\ 0.538\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Computing Two Iterations of the Newton-Raphson Method

**Example:** Suppose we wish to find a critical point of the function $f(x,y)$ in the region $[1,2] \times [1,2] \subseteq \mathbb{R}^2$, where
$f(x,y) = x^3+y^3-3x-6y+9$.
Calculate the second iteration of the Newton-Raphson process for optimization that starts at $\mathbf x_0 = \big[1, \: 2 \big]^T$ if the first iteration of the algorithm gives $\mathbf x_1 = \big[1, \: 1.5 \big]^T$. Round your final answer to three decimal places (if needed).

**Explanation**

The Newton-Raphson method of optimizing a multivariable function $f: \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}$ is

$$
\mathbf{x}_{n+1} = \mathbf{x}_n - [H_f(\mathbf{x}_n)]^{-1} \nabla{f}(\mathbf{x}_n)
$$

where $H(\mathbf{x}_n)$ and $\nabla{f}(\mathbf{x}_n)$ are the Hessian and gradient of $f$, respectively, evaluated at $\mathbf{x}_n$.

In our case, the gradient is

$$
\begin{aligned}
∇f(x) &= \begin{bmatrix}\frac{∂}{∂x}(x^{3} + y^{3} - 3x - 6y + 9) \\ \frac{∂}{∂y}(x^{3} + y^{3} - 3x - 6y + 9)\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}3x^{2} - 3 \\ 3y^{2} - 6\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

and the Hessian is

$$
\begin{aligned}
H_{f}(x) &= \begin{bmatrix}\frac{∂}{∂x}(3x^{2} - 3) & \frac{∂}{∂x}(3y^{2} - 6) \\ \frac{∂}{∂y}(3x^{2} - 3) & \frac{∂}{∂y}(3y^{2} - 6)\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}6x & 0 \\ 0 & 6y\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, our Newton-Raphson formula is

$$
\begin{bmatrix}6x & 0 \\ 0 & 6y\end{bmatrix}
$$

For the second iteration, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_{2} &= x_{1} - \begin{bmatrix}6x & 0 \\ 0 & 6y\end{bmatrix}_{x_{1}}^{-1}\begin{bmatrix}3x^{2} - 3 \\ 3y^{2} - 6\end{bmatrix}_{x_{1}} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 1.5\end{bmatrix} - \begin{bmatrix}6(1) & 0 \\ 0 & 6(1.5)\end{bmatrix}^{-1}\begin{bmatrix}3(1)^{2} - 3 \\ 3(1.5)^{2} - 6\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 1.5\end{bmatrix} - \begin{bmatrix}6 & 0 \\ 0 & 9\end{bmatrix}^{-1}\begin{bmatrix}0 \\ 0.75\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 1.5\end{bmatrix} - \frac{1}{6 \cdot 9 - 0 \cdot 0}\begin{bmatrix}9 & 0 \\ 0 & 6\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}0 \\ 0.75\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 1.5\end{bmatrix} - \frac{1}{54}\begin{bmatrix}0 \\ 4.5\end{bmatrix} \\
&\approx \begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 1.417\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Alternative to Newton's Method for Multivariable Functions Using Linear Systems

Recall that the standard Newton’s method update for multivariable optimization is

$$
\mathbf{x}_{n+1} = \mathbf{x}_n - [H_f(\mathbf{x}_n)]^{-1} \nabla f(\mathbf{x}_n)
$$

In higher dimensions, it’s often more useful to avoid calculating the inverse of a large Hessian matrix by rewriting the update as a linear system instead.

Let’s see how.

We start with the original update formula:

$$
\mathbf{x}_{n+1} = \mathbf{x}_n - [H_f(\mathbf{x}_n)]^{-1} \nabla f(\mathbf{x}_n)
$$

Next, we subtract $\mathbf{x}_n$ from both sides:

$$
\mathbf{x}_{n+1} - \mathbf{x}_n = -[H_f(\mathbf{x}_n)]^{-1} \nabla f(\mathbf{x}_n)
$$

Then, we multiply both sides by $H_f(\mathbf{x}_n){:}$

$$
H_f(\mathbf{x}_n)(\mathbf{x}_{n+1} - \mathbf{x}_n) = -\nabla f(\mathbf{x}_n)
$$

This is a linear system of equations. It’s mathematically equivalent to the original update rule, but much better for actual computation, since we no longer need to invert the Hessian; we just need to solve for $(\mathbf{x}_{n+1} - \mathbf{x}_n)$.

## A Worked Example

Recall the example we worked on, where we computed one iteration of the Newton–Raphson method.

We aim to find a critical point of the function

$$
f(x,y) =x^2y-2xy^2+x^3-4x
$$

in the region $[0,2]\times[0,2] \subseteq \mathbb{R}^2$, starting at the point $\big[1, \: 1 \big]^T$.

Now, let’s carry out one iteration of Newton–Raphson using linear systems.

The Newton-Raphson method of optimizing a multivariable function $f: \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}$ is

$$
\mathbf{x}_{n+1} = \mathbf{x}_n - [H_f(\mathbf{x}_n)]^{-1} \nabla{f}(\mathbf{x}_n)
$$

where $H_f(\mathbf{x}_n)$ and $\nabla{f}(\mathbf{x}_n)$ are the Hessian and gradient of $f$, respectively, evaluated at $\mathbf{x}_n$.

Equivalently, at each iteration, the above equation can be rewritten as

$$
H_f(\mathbf{x}_n)(\mathbf{x}_{n+1} - \mathbf{x}_n) = -\nabla{f}(\mathbf{x}_n)
$$

In our case, the gradient is

$$
\begin{aligned}
∇f(x) &= \begin{bmatrix}\frac{∂}{∂x}(x^{2}y - 2xy^{2} + x^{3} - 4x) \\ \frac{∂}{∂y}(x^{2}y - 2xy^{2} + x^{3} - 4x)\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}2xy - 2y^{2} + 3x^{2} - 4 \\ x^{2} - 4xy\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

and the Hessian is

$$
\begin{aligned}
H_{f}(x) &= \begin{bmatrix}\frac{∂}{∂x}(2xy - 2y^{2} + 3x^{2} - 4) & \frac{∂}{∂x}(x^{2} - 4xy) \\ \frac{∂}{∂y}(2xy - 2y^{2} + 3x^{2} - 4) & \frac{∂}{∂y}(x^{2} - 4xy)\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}2y + 6x & 2x - 4y \\ 2x - 4y & -4x\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, our Newton-Raphson system of equations is

$$
\begin{bmatrix}2y + 6x & 2x - 4y \\ 2x - 4y & -4x\end{bmatrix}
$$

Starting with $\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 1\end{bmatrix}$ we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
\begin{bmatrix}2y + 6x & 2x - 4y \\ 2x - 4y & -4x\end{bmatrix}_{x_{0}}(x_{1} - x_{0}) &= -\begin{bmatrix}2xy - 2y^{2} + 3x^{2} - 4 \\ x^{2} - 4xy\end{bmatrix}_{x_{0}} \\
\begin{bmatrix}2(1) + 6(1) & 2(1) - 4(1) \\ 2(1) - 4(1) & -4(1)\end{bmatrix}(x_{1} - x_{0}) &= -\begin{bmatrix}2(1)(1) - 2(1)^{2} + 3(1)^{2} - 4 \\ (1)^{2} - 4(1)(1)\end{bmatrix} \\
\begin{bmatrix}8 & -2 \\ -2 & -4\end{bmatrix}(x_{1} - x_{0}) &= \begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 3\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Solving this system of equations, we get $\begin{bmatrix}-1/18 \\ -13/18\end{bmatrix}$ Hence,

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_{1} &= x_{0} + \begin{bmatrix}-\frac{1}{18} \\ -\frac{13}{18}\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 1\end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix}-\frac{1}{18} \\ -\frac{13}{18}\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}\frac{17}{18} \\ \frac{5}{18}\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

This is the same result we obtained previously.

## Computing Iterations of the Newton-Raphson Method Using Linear Systems

**Example:** Suppose we wish to find a critical point of the function $f(x,y)$ in the region $[0,1]\times[0,1] \subseteq \mathbb{R}^2$, where
$f(x,y) = xy^2+3x^2-xy-x$.
Find the matrix equation that defines the first iteration of the Newton-Raphson process starting at the point $\big[1, \: 1 \big]^T$, and compute the components of the first iteration

**Explanation**

The Newton-Raphson method of optimizing a multivariable function $f: \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}$ is

$$
\mathbf{x}_{n+1} = \mathbf{x}_n - [H_f(\mathbf{x}_n)]^{-1} \nabla{f}(\mathbf{x}_n)
$$

where $H(\mathbf{x}_n)$ and $\nabla{f}(\mathbf{x}_n)$ are the Hessian and gradient of $f$, respectively, evaluated at $\mathbf{x}_n$.

Equivalently, at each iteration, the above equation can be rewritten as

$$
H_f(\mathbf{x}_n)(\mathbf{x}_{n+1} - \mathbf{x}_n) = -\nabla{f}(\mathbf{x}_n)
$$

In our case, the gradient is

$$
\begin{aligned}
∇f(x) &= \begin{bmatrix}\frac{∂}{∂x}(xy^{2} + 3x^{2} - xy - x) \\ \frac{∂}{∂y}(xy^{2} + 3x^{2} - xy - x)\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}y^{2} + 6x - y - 1 \\ 2xy - x\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

and the Hessian is

$$
\begin{aligned}
H_{f}(x) &= \begin{bmatrix}\frac{∂}{∂x}(y^{2} + 6x - y - 1) & \frac{∂}{∂x}(2xy - x) \\ \frac{∂}{∂y}(y^{2} + 6x - y - 1) & \frac{∂}{∂y}(2xy - x)\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}6 & 2y - 1 \\ 2y - 1 & 2x\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, our Newton-Raphson system of equations is

$$
\begin{bmatrix}6 & 2y - 1 \\ 2y - 1 & 2x\end{bmatrix}
$$

Starting with $\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 1\end{bmatrix}$ we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
\begin{bmatrix}6 & 2y - 1 \\ 2y - 1 & 2x\end{bmatrix}_{x_{0}}(x_{1} - x_{0}) &= -\begin{bmatrix}y^{2} + 6x - y - 1 \\ 2xy - x\end{bmatrix}_{x_{0}} \\
\begin{bmatrix}6 & 2(1) - 1 \\ 2(1) - 1 & 2(1)\end{bmatrix}(x_{1} - x_{0}) &= -\begin{bmatrix}(1)^{2} + 6(1) - (1) - 1 \\ 2(1)(1) - 1\end{bmatrix} \\
\begin{bmatrix}6 & 1 \\ 1 & 2\end{bmatrix}(x_{1} - x_{0}) &= \begin{bmatrix}-5 \\ -1\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Solving this system of equations, we get $\begin{bmatrix}-9/11 \\ -1/11\end{bmatrix}$ Hence,

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_{1} &= x_{0} + \begin{bmatrix}-9/11 \\ -1/11\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 1\end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix}-9/11 \\ -1/11\end{bmatrix} \\
&\approx \begin{bmatrix}0.182 \\ 0.909\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$
