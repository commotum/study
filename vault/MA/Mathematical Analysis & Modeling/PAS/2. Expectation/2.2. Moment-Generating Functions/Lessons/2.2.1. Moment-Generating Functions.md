## Introduction

Let $X$ be a discrete random variable with probability mass function $f(x)$ and support $S$. **The moment-generating function** (or **MGF** or **Laplace transform**) of $X$ is defined as

$$
M(t) = \textrm E \left[ e^{tX} \right] = \sum_{x \in S} f(x) e^{tx}
$$

The MGF of a random variable exists if $M(t)$ is finite in some open neighborhood of $t=0$.

For example, consider the random variable $X$ with the following probability mass function.

| $x$ | $1$ | $2$ | $3$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $f(x)$ | $\dfrac12$ | $\dfrac13$ | $\dfrac16$ |

The MGF of $X$ is given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
M(t) &= ∑_(x ∈ S)f(x)e^{tx} \\
&= ∑_(x = 1)^(3)f(x)e^{tx} \\
&= f(1)e^{t} + f(2)e^{2t} + f(3)e^{3t} \\
&= \frac{1}{2}e^{t} + \frac{1}{3}e^{2t} + \frac{1}{6}e^{3t}
\end{aligned}
$$

which is finite for every $t \in \mathbb{R}$.

We can use moment-generating functions to calculate the moments of a probability distribution. More importantly, we also use them to prove many important theorems about probability distributions and random variables. We'll see some examples of this in future lessons.

## Computing a Moment-Generating Function for a Discrete Random Variable

**Example:** Calculate the moment-generating function (MGF) for the discrete random variable $X$, given that $X$ has the probability mass function $f(x)$ shown in the table below.

| $x$ | $0$ | $1$ | $2$ | $3$ | $4$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $f(x)$ | $0.1$ | $0.2$ | $0.4$ | $0.2$ | $0.1$ |

**Explanation**

For a discrete random variable $X$ with probability mass function $f(x)$ and support $S$, the MGF is defined as

$$
M(t) = \textrm E \left[ e^{tX} \right] = \sum_{x \in S} f(x) e^{tx}
$$

Let

$$
S = \{0, 1, 2, 3, 4 \}
$$

be the support. Then, the MGF is given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
M(t) &= f(0)e^{0t} + f(1)e^{t} + f(2)e^{2t} + f(3)e^{3t} + f(4)e^{4t} \\
&= 0.1 + 0.2e^{t} + 0.4e^{2t} + 0.2e^{3t} + 0.1e^{4t}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Moment-Generating Functions for Continuous Random Variables

Let $X$ be a continuous random variable with probability density function $f(x)$. The MGF of $X$ is defined as

$$
M(t) =\textrm E \left[ e^{tX} \right] =\int^{\infty}_{-\infty} f (x) e^{tx} \textrm dx
$$

For example, suppose that the continuous random $X$ has the following probability distribution:

$$
{1,\begin{vmatrix}0 \le x \le 1 \\ 0,\end{vmatrix}otherwise
$$

Then, the MGF of $X$ is given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
M(t) &= ∫_{0}^{1}f(x)e^{tx}dx \\
&= ∫_{0}^{1}e^{tx}dx
\end{aligned}
$$

Calculating our integral, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
M(t) &= ∫_{0}^{1}e^{tx}dx \\
&= \frac{e^{tx}}{t} \mid _{0}^{1} \\
&= \frac{e^{t} - 1}{t}
\end{aligned}
$$

Notice that this result is not valid for $t=0$. For $t=0$, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
M(0) &= ∫_{0}^{1}e^{0x}dx \\
&= ∫_{0}^{1}dx \\
&= 1
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, the MGF for our continuous random variable $X$ is

$$
{\frac{e^{t} - 1}{t},\begin{vmatrix}t \ne 0 \\ 1,\end{vmatrix}t = 0
$$

## Computing a Moment-Generating Function for a Continuous Random Variable

**Example:** Calculate the moment-generating function (MGF) for the random variable $X$, given that $X$ has the following probability distribution:

${\frac{2}{3}x,\begin{vmatrix}0 \le x \le 1 \\ \frac{2}{3},\end{vmatrix}1 < x \le 2; 0, \mid otherwise$

**Explanation**

Let $X$ be a continuous random variable with probability density function $f(x)$. The MGF of $X$ is defined as

$$
M(t) =\textrm E \left[ e^{tX} \right] =\int^{\infty}_{-\infty} f (x) e^{tx} \textrm dx
$$

So, for the given random variable, the MGF is given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
M(t) &= ∫_{0}^{2}f(x)e^{tx}dx \\
&= ∫_{0}^{1}\frac{2}{3}xe^{tx}dx + ∫_{1}^{2}\frac{2}{3}e^{tx}dx
\end{aligned}
$$

First, we calculate the integrals for

$$
t \neq 0
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
M(t) &= ∫_{0}^{1}\frac{2}{3}xe^{tx}dx + ∫_{1}^{2}\frac{2}{3}e^{tx}dx \\
&= \frac{2}{3}∫_{0}^{1}xe^{tx}dx + \frac{2e^{tx}}{3t} \mid _{1}^{2} \\
&= \frac{2}{3}∫_{0}^{1}xe^{tx}dx + (2(e^{2t} - e^{t}))/(3t)
\end{aligned}
$$

To solve $\displaystyle \int_{0}^{1} x e^{tx}$, we first find $\displaystyle \int xe^{tx} \,\textrm{d}x$ using the by-parts formula, given by

$$
\int uv'\,\textrm{d}x= uv- \int u'v\,\textrm{d}x
$$

We set

$$
\begin{bmatrix}u = x & ⟹u^{′} = 1 \\ v^{′} = e^{tx} & ⟹v = \frac{e^{tx}}{t}\end{bmatrix}
$$

Substituting the above into the integration by parts formula, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
∫xe^{tx}dx &= \frac{xe^{tx}}{t} - ∫\frac{e^{tx}}{t}dx \\
&= \frac{xe^{tx}}{t} - \frac{e^{tx}}{t^{2}} + C \\
&= ((xt - 1)e^{tx})/(t^{2}) + C
\end{aligned}
$$

So, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
M(t) &= \frac{2}{3}∫_{0}^{1}xe^{tx}dx + (2(e^{2t} - e^{t}))/(3t) \\
&= (2(xt - 1)e^{tx})/(3t^{2}) \mid _{0}^{1} + (2(e^{2t} - e^{t}))/(3t) \\
&= (2e^{t}(t - 1) + 2)/(3t^{2}) + (2(e^{2t} - e^{t}))/(3t) \\
&= (2e^{t}(t - 1) + 2)/(3t^{2}) + (2t(e^{2t} - e^{t}))/(3t^{2}) \\
&= \frac{2te^{t} - 2e^{t} + 2 + 2te^{2t} - 2te^{t}}{3t^{2}} \\
&= \frac{2te^{2t} - 2e^{t} + 2}{3t^{2}} \\
&= (2(te^{2t} - e^{t} + 1))/(3t^{2})
\end{aligned}
$$

If $t=0$, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
M(0) &= ∫_{0}^{2}f(x)dx \\
&= ∫_{0}^{1}\frac{2}{3}xdx + ∫_{1}^{2}\frac{2}{3}dx \\
&= 1
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, the MGF for the random variable $X$ is

$$
{(2(e^{2t} - e^{t} + 1))/(3t^{2})\begin{vmatrix}t \ne 0 \\ 1\end{vmatrix}t = 0
$$
