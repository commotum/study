## Introduction

Let's recall the multiplication law for conditional probability for two events $A$ and $B$:

$$
P(A \mid B) = \dfrac{P(A\cap B)}{P(B)}
$$

Now suppose that $X$ and $Y$ are discrete random variables. The conditional probability that $X=x$ *given that* $Y = y$ follows immediately from the multiplication law and is given by

$$
P(X = x \, \mid \, Y = y) = \dfrac{P(X =x, \, Y = y)}{P(Y = y)} = \dfrac{f(x,y)}{f_Y(y)}
$$

where $f(x,y)$ is the joint PMF of $X$ and $Y$, and $f_Y(y)$ is the marginal mass function of $Y$.

Note the following:

- To avoid getting a zero in the denominator, this formula assumes that $P(Y = y) = f_Y(y) \neq 0$.
- In the case where $P(Y=y) = 0$, the corresponding conditional probability for $X$ does not exist. It makes no sense to ask about the event of $X=x$ occurring *given that* $Y=y$ has occurred if the event $Y=y$ is impossible!

In the similar manner, the conditional probability that $Y=y$ given that $X = x$ can be calculated as

$$
P(Y = y \, \mid \, X = x) = \dfrac{P(X = x, \, Y = y)}{P(X = x)} = \dfrac{f(x,y)}{f_X(x)}
$$

where $f_X(x)$ is the marginal mass function of $X$. This formula assumes that

$$
P(X=x)\neq 0
$$

## Computing a Conditional Probability

**Example:** Compute $P(Y=0 \, \mid \, X=1)$ given that the discrete random variables $X$ and $Y$ have the joint probability mass function $f(x,y)$ shown in the table above.

| $f$ | $Y=0$ | $Y=1$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| $X=0$ | $0.5$ | $0.05$ |
| $X=1$ | $0.15$ | $0.3$ |

**Explanation**

By the multiplication law for conditional probability, the required probability is given by

$$
P(Y = 0 \, \mid \, X = 1) = \dfrac{P(X = 1, Y = 0)}{P(X = 1)} = \dfrac{f(1,0)}{f_X(1)}
$$

where $f(x,y)$ is the joint probability mass function of $X$ and $Y$, and $f_X(x)$ is the marginal distribution of $X$.

Recall that the marginal distribution for $X$ corresponds to the row totals. Thus,

$$
\begin{aligned}
f_{X}(1) &= f(1, 0) + f(1, 1) \\
&= 0.15 + 0.3 \\
&= 0.45
\end{aligned}
$$

From the given table, we have that

$$
f(1,0) = 0.15
$$

Finally, substituting

$$
f_X(1) = 0.45
$$

and

$$
f(1,0) = 0.15
$$

in our formula above, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(Y = 0 \mid X = 1) &= (f(1, 0))/(f_{X}(1)) \\
&= \frac{0.15}{0.45} \\
&= \frac{1}{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Computing a Conditional Probability Over an Interval

**Example:** Compute $P(0\lt Y \lt 3\, \mid \, X=0)$ given that the discrete random variables $X$ and $Y$ have the joint probability mass function $f(x,y)$ shown in the table above. Round your answer to two decimal places.

| $f$ | $Y=0$ | $Y=1$ | $Y=2$ | $Y=3$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $X=0$ | $0.2$ | $0$ | $0.05$ | $0.1$ |
| $X=2$ | $0.1$ | $0.05$ | $0.15$ | $0$ |
| $X=4$ | $0.2$ | $0.05$ | $0$ | $0.1$ |

**Explanation**

By the multiplication law for conditional probability, the required conditional probability is given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(0 < Y < 3 \mid X = 0) &= (P((0 < Y < 3), X = 0))/(P(X = 0)) \\
&= (P(X = 0, (0 < Y < 3)))/(P(X = 0)) \\
&= (P(X = 0, Y = 1) + P(X = 0, Y = 2))/(P(X = 0)) \\
&= (f(0, 1) + f(0, 2))/(f_{X}(0))
\end{aligned}
$$

where $f(x,y)$ is the joint probability mass function of $X$ and $Y$, and $f_X(x)$ is the marginal distribution of $X$.

Recall that the marginal distribution for $X$ corresponds to the row totals. Thus,

$$
\begin{aligned}
f_{X}(0) &= f(0, 0) + f(0, 1) + f(0, 2) + f(0, 3) \\
&= 0.2 + 0 + 0.05 + 0.1 \\
&= 0.35
\end{aligned}
$$

From the given table, we have that

$$
f(0,1)=0
$$

and

$$
f(0,2)=0.05
$$

Finally, substituting our values into the formula above, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(0 < Y < 3 \mid X = 0) &= (f(0, 1) + f(0, 2))/(f_{X}(0)) \\
&= \frac{0 + 0.05}{0.35} \\
&= \frac{0.05}{0.35} \\
&\approx 0.14
\end{aligned}
$$

## Conditional Probability Mass Functions

We can now define two more probability mass functions as follows:

- The **conditional probability mass function of $X$ given that $Y=y$,** denoted $f_{X\mid Y}(x \mid y)$, is defined as
$f_{X\mid Y}(x \mid y) = P(X = x \, \mid \, Y = y) = \dfrac{P(X = x, \, Y = y)}{P(Y=y)} = \dfrac{f(x,y)}{f_Y(y)}$,
where $f(x,y)$ is the joint probability mass function of $X$ and $Y$, and $f_Y(y)$ is the marginal mass function of $Y$.

- The **conditional probability mass function of $Y$ given that $X=x$,** denoted $f_{Y\mid X}(y \mid x)$, is defined as
$f_{Y\mid X}(y \mid x) = P(Y = y \, \mid \, X = x) = \dfrac{P(X = x,\, Y = y)}{P(X=x)} = \dfrac{f(x,y)}{f_X(x)}$,
where $f(x,y)$ is a joint probability mass function of $X$ and $Y$, and $f_X(x)$ is the marginal mass function of $X$.

**Watch out!** The conditional distribution of $X$ given $Y$ does *not* equal the conditional distribution of $Y$ given $X$. That is, in general,

$$
f_{X\mid Y}(x \mid y) \ne f_{Y\mid X}(y \mid x)
$$

Finally, if $X$ and $Y$ are independent, then

$$
f_{X\mid Y}(x \mid y)=f_X(x),\qquad f_{Y\mid X}(y \mid x)=f_Y(y)
$$

In other words, if $X$ and $Y$ are independent, then knowing the value of one random variable does not affect the probability distribution of the other.

## Computing the Value of a Conditional Probability Mass Function at a Point

**Example:** Compute $f_{X\mid Y}(2 \, \mid \, 2)$ given that the discrete random variables $X$ and $Y$ have the joint probability mass function $f(x,y)$ shown in the table above.

| $f$ | $Y=0$ | $Y=1$ | $Y=2$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $X=1$ | $\dfrac{1}{6}$ | $\dfrac{1}{9}$ | $\dfrac{1}{6}$ |
| $X=2$ | $\dfrac{1}{18}$ | $\dfrac{1}{18}$ | $\dfrac{1}{6}$ |
| $X=3$ | $\dfrac{1}{9}$ | $\dfrac{1}{9}$ | $\dfrac{1}{18}$ |

**Explanation**

The conditional probability mass function $f_{X\mid Y}(x\, \mid \,y)$ is defined as

$$
f_{X\mid Y}(x\, \mid \,y) = P(X = x \, \mid \, Y=y)
$$

Therefore, we're required to compute the conditional probability

$$
f_{X\mid Y}(2 \, \mid \, 2) = P(X = 2 \, \mid \, Y = 2)
$$

By the multiplication law for conditional probability, we have

$$
P(X = 2 \, \mid \, Y = 2) = \dfrac{P(X = 2, Y = 2)}{P(Y = 2)} = \dfrac{f(2,2)}{f_Y(2)}
$$

where $f(x,y)$ is the joint probability mass function of $X$ and $Y$, and $f_Y(y)$ is the marginal distribution of $Y$.

Recall that the marginal distribution for $Y$ corresponds to the column totals. Thus,

$$
\begin{aligned}
f_{Y}(2) &= f(1, 2) + f(2, 2) + f(3, 2) \\
&= \frac{1}{6} + \frac{1}{6} + \frac{1}{18} \\
&= \frac{7}{18}
\end{aligned}
$$

From the given table,

$$
f(2,2) = \dfrac{1}{6}
$$

Finally, substituting

$$
f_Y(2) = \dfrac{7}{18}
$$

and

$$
f(2,2) = \dfrac{1}{6}
$$

in our formula above, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
f_{X\mid Y}(2 \mid 2) &= P(X = 2 \mid Y = 2) \\
&= (f(2, 2))/(f_{Y}(2)) \\
&= ((\frac{1}{6}))/((\frac{7}{18})) \\
&= \frac{3}{7}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Computing a Conditional Probability Mass Function

**Example:** Find the conditional probability mass function $f_{X\mid Y}(x\, \mid \, 1)$ given that the random variables $X$ and $Y$ have the joint probability mass function $f(x,y)$ shown in the table above.

| $f$ | $Y=1$ | $Y=3$ | $Y=5$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $X=0$ | $0.1$ | $0$ | $0.2$ |
| $X=2$ | $0.3$ | $0.1$ | $0$ |
| $X=4$ | $0.1$ | $0$ | $0.2$ |

**Explanation**

The conditional probability mass function $f_{X\mid Y}(x\, \mid \,y)$ is defined as

$$
f_{X\mid Y}(x\, \mid \,y) = P(X = x \, \mid \, Y=y)
$$

Therefore, we're required to compute the following set of conditional probabilities:

$$
f_{X\mid Y}(x \, \mid \, 1) = P(X = x \, \mid \, Y=1)
$$

By the multiplication law for conditional probability, we have

$$
P(X = x \, \mid \, Y=1) = \dfrac{P(X = x, Y=1)}{P(Y=1)} = \dfrac{f(x,1)}{f_Y(1)}
$$

where $f(x,y)$ is the joint probability mass function of $X$ and $Y$, and $f_Y(y)$ is the marginal distribution of $Y$.

Recall that the marginal distributions for $X$ and $Y$ correspond to the row and column totals, respectively. So, let's compute these:

| $f$ | $Y=1$ | $Y=3$ | $Y=5$ | $f_X(x)$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $X=0$ | $0.1$ | $0$ | $0.2$ | $0.3$ |
| $X=2$ | $0.3$ | $0.1$ | $0$ | $0.4$ |
| $X=4$ | $0.1$ | $0.2$ | $0$ | $0.3$ |
| $f_Y(y)$ | $0.5$ | $0.3$ | $0.2$ | $1$ |

Therefore, for $x\in \{0,2,4\}$, we obtain the following:

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(X = 0\begin{vmatrix}Y = 1) &= (f(0, 1))/(f_{Y}(1)) = \frac{0.1}{0.5} = \frac{1}{5} \\ P(X = 2 \mid Y = 1)|= (f(2, 1))/(f_{Y}(1)) = \frac{0.3}{0.5} = \frac{3}{5} \\ P(X = 4\end{vmatrix}Y = 1)|= (f(4, 1))/(f_{Y}(1)) = \frac{0.1}{0.5} = \frac{1}{5}
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, the conditional probability mass function can be represented by the following table:

| $x$ | $0$ | $2$ | $4$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $f_{X\mid Y}(x \, \mid \, 1)$ | $\dfrac{1}{5}$ | $\dfrac{3}{5}$ | $\dfrac{1}{5}$ |
