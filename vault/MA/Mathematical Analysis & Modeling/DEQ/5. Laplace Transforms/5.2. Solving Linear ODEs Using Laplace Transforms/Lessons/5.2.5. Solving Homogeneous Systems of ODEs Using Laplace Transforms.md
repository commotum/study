## Introduction

In this topic, we'll learn how to use Laplace transforms to solve homogeneous systems like the one below.

$$
{x_{1}^{′}(t) = 5x_{1}(t); x_{2}^{′}(t) =-2x_{1}(t) + 6x_{2}(t)
$$

The process can be split into three steps.

- **Step 1.** Apply the Laplace transform $\mathcal{L}$ to both sides of each equation, to get a system with variables
$X_1(s) = \mathcal{L}\left\{x_1(t) \right\} \qquad \text{and} \qquad X_2(s) = \mathcal{L}\left\{x_2(t) \right\}$.
- **Step 2.** Solve the obtained system for variables
$X_1(s) \qquad \text{and} \qquad X_2(s)$.
- **Step 3.** To find the initial variables
$x_1(t) \qquad \text{and} \qquad x_2(t)$
satisfying the original initial value problem, apply the inverse Laplace transform to
$X_1(s) \qquad \text{and} \qquad X_2(s)$.

Let's see how this works in practice.

## A Worked Example

$$
{x_{1}^{′}(t) = 5x_{1}(t); x_{2}^{′}(t) =-2x_{1}(t) + 6x_{2}(t)
$$

**Step 1**. In the initial value problem above, we apply the Laplace transform $\mathcal{L}$ to both sides of each equation.

First, we find the Laplace transforms of both sides of each equation in our system:

$$
{L{x_{1}^{′}(t)} = L{5x_{1}(t)}; L{x_{2}^{′}(t)} = L{-2x_{1}(t) + 6x_{2}(t)}
$$

Using the derivative property of the Laplace transform, we obtain the system

$$
{sL{x_{1}(t)} - x_{1}(0) = 5L{x_{1}(t)}; sL{x_{2}(t)} - x_{2}(0) =-2L{x_{1}(t)} + 6L{x_{2}(t)}
$$

which can be simplified to the following:

$$
{sX_{1}(s) + 3 = 5X_{1}(s); sX_{2}(s) - 2 =-2X_{1}(s) + 6X_{2}(s)
$$

where

$$
X_1(s) = \mathcal{L}\left\{x_1(t) \right\}
$$

and

$$
X_2(s) = \mathcal{L}\left\{x_2(t) \right\}
$$

**Step 2**. Now, solving the first equation for $X_1(s)$, we get

$$
s X_1(s) + 3 = 5 X_1(s) \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad X_1(s) = -\dfrac{3}{s-5}
$$

Substituting this in the second equation and solving for $X_2(s)$, we have

$$
s X_2(s) - 2 = -2\left(-\dfrac{3}{s-5}\right) + 6 X_2(s) \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad X_2(s) = \dfrac{2s-4}{(s-5)(s-6)}
$$

**Step 3**. To find $x_1(t)$ and $x_2(t)$ satisfying the original initial value problem, we apply the inverse Laplace transform to $X_1(s)$ and $X_2(s){:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_{1}(t) &= L^{-1}{X_{1}(s)} \\
&= L^{-1}{-\frac{3}{s - 5}} \\
&=-3L^{-1}{\frac{1}{s - 5}} \\
&=-3e^{5t} \\
x_{2}(t) &= L^{-1}{X_{2}(s)} \\
&= L^{-1}{(2s - 4)/((s - 5)(s - 6))} \\
&= L^{-1}{\frac{8}{s - 6} - \frac{6}{s - 5}} \\
&= 8L^{-1}{\frac{1}{s - 6}} - 6L^{-1}{\frac{1}{s - 5}} \\
&= 8e^{6t} - 6e^{5t}
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we write down the solution to the initial value problem:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x(t) &= \begin{bmatrix}x_{1}(t) \\ x_{2}(t)\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}-3e^{5t} \\ 8e^{6t} - 6e^{5t}\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}0 \\ 8\end{bmatrix}e^{6t} + \begin{bmatrix}-3 \\ -6\end{bmatrix}e^{5t}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding the Solution of a Laplace Transformed Initial Value Problem

**Example:** ${x_{1}^{′}(t) =-3x_{1}(t) + x_{2}(t); x_{2}^{′}(t) = 3x_{1}(t) - x_{2}(t)$,
Consider the initial value problem above. By applying the Laplace transform $\mathcal{L}$ to both sides of each equation in our system, we get a system with variables $X_1(s) = \mathcal{L}\!\left\{x_1(t) \right\}$ and $X_2(s) = \mathcal{L}\!\left\{x_2(t) \right\}$.
Find the solution of the new system in terms of the parameter $s$.

**Explanation**

First, we find the Laplace transforms of both sides of each equation in our system:

$$
{L{x_{1}^{′}(t)} = L{-3x_{1}(t) + x_{2}(t)}; L{x_{2}^{′}(t)} = L{3x_{1}(t) - x_{2}(t)}
$$

Using the derivative property of the Laplace transform, we obtain the system

$$
{sL{x_{1}(t)} - x_{1}(0) =-3L{x_{1}(t)} + L{x_{2}(t)}; sL{x_{2}(t)} - x_{2}(0) = 3L{x_{1}(t)} - L{x_{2}(t)}
$$

which simplifies to the following system:

$$
{sX_{1}(s) - 4 =-3X_{1}(s) + X_{2}(s); sX_{2}(s) - 4 = 3X_{1}(s) - X_{2}(s)
$$

where

$$
X_1(s) = \mathcal{L}\!\left\{x_1(t) \right\}
$$

and

$$
X_2(s) = \mathcal{L}\!\left\{x_2(t) \right\}
$$

Rewriting the system in standard form, we get

$$
{(s + 3)X_{1}(s) - X_{2}(s) = 4; - 3X_{1}(s) + (s + 1)X_{2}(s) = 4
$$

Now, from the first equation, we have

$$
X_2(s) = (s+3)X_1(s) - 4
$$

Substituting this into the second equation, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
-3X_{1}(s) + (s + 1)[(s + 3)X_{1}(s) - 4] &= 4 \\
- 3X_{1}(s) + (s + 1)(s + 3)X_{1}(s) - 4(s + 1) &= 4 \\
[(s + 1)(s + 3) - 3]X_{1}(s) &= 4 + 4(s + 1) \\
s(s + 4)X_{1}(s) &= 4s + 8 \\
&= X_{1}(s) = (4(s + 2))/(s(s + 4))
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we find $X_2(s)$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
X_{2}(s) &= (s + 3)X_{1}(s) - 4 \\
&= (s + 3)((4(s + 2))/(s(s + 4))) - 4 \\
&= (4(s + 3)(s + 2) - 4s(s + 4))/(s(s + 4)) \\
&= (4(s^{2} + 5s + 6) - 4(s^{2} + 4s))/(s(s + 4)) \\
&= (4s^{2} + 20s + 24 - 4s^{2} - 16s)/(s(s + 4)) \\
&= (4(s + 6))/(s(s + 4))
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, we have

$$
X_1(s) =\dfrac{4(s+2)}{s(s+4)}, \qquad X_2(s) = \dfrac{4(s+6)}{s(s+4)}
$$

## Solving an Initial Value Problem Given the Laplace Transformed Solution

**Example:** ${x_{1}^{′}(t) = 5x_{1}(t) - x_{2}(t); x_{2}^{′}(t) =-5x_{1}(t) + x_{2}(t)$,
Consider the initial value problem above. By applying the Laplace transform to both sides of each equation in our system, we get a transformed system with the solution
$X_1(s) = \mathcal{L}\left\{x_1(t) \right\} = \dfrac{4}{s} + \dfrac{1}{s-6}, \qquad X_2(s) = \mathcal{L}\left\{x_2(t) \right\} = \dfrac{20}{s} - \dfrac{1}{s-6}$.
Find the solution to the original initial value problem.

**Explanation**

To find $x_1(t)$ and $x_2(t)$ satisfying the original initial value problem, we apply the inverse Laplace transform to $X_1(s)$ and $X_2(s){:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_{1}(t) &= L^{-1}{X_{1}(s)} \\
&= L^{-1}{\frac{4}{s} + \frac{1}{s - 6}} \\
&= 4L^{-1}{\frac{1}{s}} + L^{-1}{\frac{1}{s - 6}} \\
&= 4 \cdot 1 + e^{6 \cdot t} \\
&= 4 + e^{6t} \\
x_{2}(t) &= L^{-1}{X_{2}(s)} \\
&= L^{-1}{\frac{20}{s} - \frac{1}{s - 6}} \\
&= 20L^{-1}{\frac{1}{s}} - L^{-1}{\frac{1}{s - 6}} \\
&= 20 \cdot 1 - e^{6 \cdot t} \\
&= 20 - e^{6t}
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we write down the solution to the initial value problem:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x(t) &= \begin{bmatrix}x_{1}(t) \\ x_{2}(t)\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}4 + e^{6t} \\ 20 - e^{6t}\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}4 \\ 20\end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix}1 \\ -1\end{bmatrix}e^{6t}
\end{aligned}
$$
