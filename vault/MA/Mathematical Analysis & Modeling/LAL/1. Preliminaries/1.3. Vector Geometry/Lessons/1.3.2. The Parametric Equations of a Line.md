## Introduction

Suppose that we have a straight line passing through the point $P(3,-1,7)$ and that's parallel to the direction vector $\begin{bmatrix}0 \\ 5 \\ -3\end{bmatrix}$ As we know, we can write the vector equation of the line as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
r &= p + tv \\
r &= \begin{bmatrix}3 \\ -1 \\ 7\end{bmatrix} + t\begin{bmatrix}0 \\ 5 \\ -3\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

where $t \in (-\infty,\infty)$.

Using the vector equation above, we can create a set of **parametric equations** where we express a general point $(x,y,z)$ in terms of the parameter $t$.

If we let $\begin{bmatrix}x \\ y \\ z\end{bmatrix}$ be the position vector of the general point $(x,y,z)$, then the above equation can be written as

$$
\begin{aligned}
r &= \begin{bmatrix}3 \\ -1 \\ 7\end{bmatrix} + t\begin{bmatrix}0 \\ 5 \\ -3\end{bmatrix} \\
\begin{bmatrix}x \\ y \\ z\end{bmatrix} &= \begin{bmatrix}3 \\ -1 \\ 7\end{bmatrix} + t\begin{bmatrix}0 \\ 5 \\ -3\end{bmatrix} \\
\begin{bmatrix}x \\ y \\ z\end{bmatrix} &= \begin{bmatrix}3 \\ -1 + 5t \\ 7 - 3t\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

So, we have the following system of equations:

$$
x = 3; y =-1 + 5t; z = 7 - 3t
$$

The equations in the above system are called the **parametric equations** of the straight line.
Geometrically, the equation states that a point belongs to the line if and only if its coordinates have the form

$$
(3, \: -1+5t, \: 7-3t)
$$

where $t \in (-\infty, \infty)$.

## Finding the Vector Equation of a Straight Line Given Parametrically

**Example:** Consider the straight line given by the following parametric equations:
$x = 2 + 2t; y = 5t; z = 1 - t$
What would be the vector equation of the line?

**Explanation**

These parametric equations are equivalent to

$$
\begin{aligned}
\begin{bmatrix}x \\ y \\ z\end{bmatrix} &= \begin{bmatrix}2 + 2t \\ 5t \\ 1 - t\end{bmatrix} \\
\begin{bmatrix}x \\ y \\ z\end{bmatrix} &= \begin{bmatrix}2 \\ 0 \\ 1\end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix}2t \\ 5t \\ -t\end{bmatrix} \\
\begin{bmatrix}x \\ y \\ z\end{bmatrix} &= \begin{bmatrix}2 \\ 0 \\ 1\end{bmatrix} + t\begin{bmatrix}2 \\ 5 \\ -1\end{bmatrix} \\
r &= \begin{bmatrix}2 \\ 0 \\ 1\end{bmatrix} + t\begin{bmatrix}2 \\ 5 \\ -1\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

where $t \in (-\infty,\infty)$.

## Finding a Vector Parallel To a Straight Line Given Parametrically

**Example:** Consider the straight line given by the following parametric equations:
$x = 5 - t; y = 3; z = 1$
Find a vector that is parallel to the line.

**Explanation**

First, let's find the corresponding vector equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\begin{bmatrix}x \\ y \\ z\end{bmatrix} &= \begin{bmatrix}5 - t \\ 3 \\ 1\end{bmatrix} \\
\begin{bmatrix}x \\ y \\ z\end{bmatrix} &= \begin{bmatrix}5 \\ 3 \\ 1\end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix}-t \\ 0 \\ 0\end{bmatrix} \\
\begin{bmatrix}x \\ y \\ z\end{bmatrix} &= \begin{bmatrix}5 \\ 3 \\ 1\end{bmatrix} + t\begin{bmatrix}-1 \\ 0 \\ 0\end{bmatrix} \\
r &= \begin{bmatrix}5 \\ 3 \\ 1\end{bmatrix} + t\begin{bmatrix}-1 \\ 0 \\ 0\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

where $t \in (-\infty,\infty)$.

This implies that the line passes through the point $(5,3,1)$ and is parallel to the vector $\begin{bmatrix}-1 \\ 0 \\ 0\end{bmatrix}$

## Finding the Parametric Equations of a Line

**Example:** Find the parametric equations of the line shown in the figure below.

![](<../Source/1.3.2. The Parametric Equations of a Line/Images/fc5afc43c642374b8c9fc33cd5cbe982.png>)

**Explanation**

We know that the line passes through the points $P(3,3,-5)$ and $Q(1,0,-1)$. Now, we need to find a direction vector that is parallel to the given line. We can use the vector $\overrightarrow{PQ}$ as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
v &= PQ^(→) \\
&= OQ^(→) - OP^(→) \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 0 \\ -1\end{bmatrix} - \begin{bmatrix}3 \\ 3 \\ -5\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}-2 \\ -3 \\ 4\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the vector equation of the line is

$$
\begin{aligned}
r &= p + tv \\
r &= \begin{bmatrix}3 \\ 3 \\ -5\end{bmatrix} + t\begin{bmatrix}-2 \\ -3 \\ 4\end{bmatrix} \\
\begin{bmatrix}x \\ y \\ z\end{bmatrix} &= \begin{bmatrix}3 \\ 3 \\ -5\end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix}-2t \\ -3t \\ 4t\end{bmatrix} \\
\begin{bmatrix}x \\ y \\ z\end{bmatrix} &= \begin{bmatrix}3 - 2t \\ 3 - 3t \\ -5 + 4t\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

where $t \in (-\infty,\infty)$. And finally, the parametric equations:

$$
{x = 3 - 2t; y = 3 - 3t; z =-5 + 4t
$$

## Finding the Parametric Equations of a Segment

**Example:** Find the parametric equations that describe all the points on the line segment $\overline{PQ}$, where $P(1,-5,3)$ and $Q(0, 4, -4)$.

**Explanation**

First, we need to find a direction vector that is parallel to the line passing through the given points. We can use the vector $\overrightarrow{PQ}$ as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
v &= PQ^(→) \\
&= OQ^(→) - OP^(→) \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}0 \\ 4 \\ -4\end{bmatrix} - \begin{bmatrix}1 \\ -5 \\ 3\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}-1 \\ 9 \\ -7\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the vector equation of the line is

$$
\begin{aligned}
r &= p + tv \\
r &= \begin{bmatrix}1 \\ -5 \\ 3\end{bmatrix} + t\begin{bmatrix}-1 \\ 9 \\ -7\end{bmatrix} \\
\begin{bmatrix}x \\ y \\ z\end{bmatrix} &= \begin{bmatrix}1 \\ -5 \\ 3\end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix}-t \\ 9t \\ -7t\end{bmatrix} \\
\begin{bmatrix}x \\ y \\ z\end{bmatrix} &= \begin{bmatrix}1 - t \\ -5 + 9t \\ 3 - 7t\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

where $t \in (-\infty,\infty)$.

To get the equations for the line segment, we take only $t \in [0,1]$. Notice that substitution of $t=0$ in the above equation gives the coordinates of $P$ and substitution of $t=1$ gives the coordinates of $Q$.

Therefore, the parametric equations of the line segment $\overline{PQ}$ are the following:

$$
{x = 1 - t; y =-5 + 9t; z = 3 - 7t
$$
