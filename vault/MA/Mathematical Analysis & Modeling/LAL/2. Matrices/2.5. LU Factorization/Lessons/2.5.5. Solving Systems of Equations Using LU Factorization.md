## Introduction

There is an algorithm that uses $LU$ factorization to solve systems of equations of the form

$$
A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{b}
$$

For example, let's solve the following equation:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}1 & -3 \\ -1 & 2\end{bmatrix}
$$

Since the LU factorization of $A$ is

$\begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 \\ -1 & 1\end{bmatrix}$
our equation

$$
A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{b}
$$

can be written as

$$
\begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 \\ -1 & 1\end{bmatrix}
$$

i.e.,

$$
LU\mathbf{x}= \mathbf{b}
$$

Now, suppose we set

$$
U\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{y}
$$

Substituting this into the above, we have

$$
L\mathbf{y}= \mathbf{b}
$$

In other words, we can reduce the problem to the following system of matrix equations:

$$
{Ly = b; Ux = y
$$

Since $L$ and $U$ are both triangular, each of these equations is easily solvable using back and forward substitution.

- First, we solve $L\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{b}$. Let's assume that $\begin{bmatrix}y_{1} \\ y_{2}\end{bmatrix}$ Then, we have
$\begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 \\ -1 & 1\end{bmatrix}$
which gives the system of equations
${y_{1} =-1; - y_{1} + y_{2} = 0$.
Solving this equation using *forward* substitution, we get the solution
$\begin{bmatrix}y_{1} \\ y_{2}\end{bmatrix}$
- Finally, we solve $U\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{y}$. We have
$\begin{bmatrix}1 & -3 \\ 0 & -1\end{bmatrix}$
which gives the system of equations
${x_{1} - 3x_{2} =-1; - x_{2} =-1$.
Solving this equation using *back* substitution, we get the solution
$\begin{bmatrix}x_{1} \\ x_{2}\end{bmatrix}$

## Finding the Solution of an Intermediate System Using the LU Factorization of a 2x2 Matrix

**Example:** Consider the matrix $L$ and the vector $\mathbf b$, given by

$\begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 \\ 15 & 1\end{bmatrix}$

If $A=LU$ is the LU factorization of some $2\times 2$ matrix $A$, find the vector $\mathbf{y}$ such that

${Ly = b; Ux = y$
is equivalent to $A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{b}$.

**Explanation**

First, notice that since $A=LU$, we can write

$$
Ax = b⟹(LU)_(⏟)_(A)x = b
$$

Now, setting

$$
U\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{y}
$$

we obtain

$$
{Ly = b; Ux = y
$$

We now solve

$$
L\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{b}
$$

Writing this equation in matrix form, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
\begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 \\ 15 & 1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}y_{1} \\ y_{2}\end{bmatrix} &= \begin{bmatrix}5 \\ 66\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

This gives the system of equations

$$
{y_{1} = 5; 15y_{1} + y_{2} = 66
$$

The solution to this system is $y_1=5$ and $y_2=-9$.

Therefore, $\begin{bmatrix}5 \\ -9\end{bmatrix}$

## Finding the Solution of an Intermediate System Using the LU Factorization of a 3x3 Matrix

**Example:** Consider the matrix $L$ and the vector $\mathbf b$, given by
$\begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 & 0 \\ 1 & 1 & 0 \\ 1 & -2 & 1\end{bmatrix}$
If $A=LU$ is the LU factorization of some $3\times 3$ matrix $A$, find the vector $\mathbf{y}$ such that

${Ly = b; Ux = y$

is equivalent to $A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{b}$.

**Explanation**

First, notice that since $A=LU$, we can write

$$
Ax = b⟹(LU)_(⏟)_(A)x = b
$$

Now, setting

$$
U\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{y}
$$

we obtain

$$
{Ly = b; Ux = y
$$

We now solve

$$
L\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{b}
$$

Writing this equation in matrix form, we get

$$
\begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 & 0 \\ 1 & 1 & 0 \\ 1 & -2 & 1\end{bmatrix}
$$

This gives the system of equations

$$
{y_{1} = 2; y_{1} + y_{2} = 4; y_{1} - 2y_{2} + y_{3} = 1
$$

We can compute the solution to this system using forward substitution, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
y_{1} &= 2, \\
y_{2} &= 4 - y_{1} \\
&= 4 - (2) \\
&= 2, \\
y_{3} &= 1 - y_{1} + 2y_{2} \\
&= 1 - (2) + 2(2) \\
&= 3
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,
$\begin{bmatrix}2 \\ 2 \\ 3\end{bmatrix}$

## Solving a 2x2 Linear System Using LU Factorization

**Example:** Consider the LU decomposition of the matrix $A$ and the vector $\mathbf{b}$, given by
$\begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 \\ -2 & 1\end{bmatrix}$
If $\langle x_1,x_2 \rangle$ and $\langle y_1,y_2 \rangle$ are the solutions to the systems $A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{b}$ and $L\mathbf{y}=\mathbf{b}$ respectively, what is the value of $x_1 \cdot y_2$?

**Explanation**

First, notice that since $A=LU$, we can write

$$
Ax = b⟹(LU)_(⏟)_(A)x = b
$$

Now, setting

$$
U\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{y}
$$

we obtain

$$
{Ly = b; Ux = y
$$

Now, we solve the two systems in turn.

- First, we solve $L\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{b}$. Writing this equation in matrix form, we get
$\begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 \\ -2 & 1\end{bmatrix}$
This gives the system of equations
${y_{1} = 6; - 2y_{1} + y_{2} =-8$.
The solution to this system is $y_1=6$ and $y_2=4$. Therefore,
$\begin{bmatrix}6 \\ 4\end{bmatrix}$
- Next, we solve $U\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{y}$. Writing this equation in matrix form, we get
$\begin{bmatrix}7 & 4 \\ 0 & -2\end{bmatrix}$
This gives the system of equations
${7x_{1} + 4x_{2} = 6; - 2x_{2} = 4$.
The solution to this system is $x_2=-2$ and $x_1=2$. Therefore,
$\begin{bmatrix}2 \\ -2\end{bmatrix}$

Finally,

$$
x_1 \cdot y_2 = 2 \cdot 4 = 8
$$
