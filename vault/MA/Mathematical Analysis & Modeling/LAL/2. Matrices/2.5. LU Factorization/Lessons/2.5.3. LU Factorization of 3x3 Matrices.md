## Introduction

Recall that LU factorization involves writing a matrix $A$ as a product of two matrices $L$ and $U$, where

- $\begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 & 0 \\ ∗ & 1 & 0 \\ ∗ & ∗ & 1\end{bmatrix}$ is a *unit lower triangular matrix* (a lower triangular matrix that has only $1$'s on the main diagonal), and
- $\begin{bmatrix}∗ & ∗ & ∗ \\ 0 & ∗ & ∗ \\ 0 & 0 & ∗\end{bmatrix}$ is an *upper triangular matrix*.

For example, suppose we're given the matrix $\begin{bmatrix}1 & -1 & -2 \\ 0 & 2 & -3 \\ -2 & 2 & 7\end{bmatrix}$ To perform LU factorization, we need to write $A$ as

$$
\begin{bmatrix}1 & -1 & -2 \\ 0 & 2 & -3 \\ -2 & 2 & 7\end{bmatrix}
$$

As usual, to find the matrices $L$ and $U$, we can reduce the matrix $A$ to *echelon form* while simultaneously performing the same elementary row operations on the identity matrix:

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= \begin{bmatrix}1 & -1 & -2 \\ 0 & 2 & -3 \\ -2 & 2 & 7\end{bmatrix}\begin{vmatrix}R_{3} &:=R_{3} + 2R_{1} & ⇔\end{vmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 2 & 0 & 1\end{bmatrix}^(⏞)^(E_{1}) \cdot A \\
&= ∼\begin{bmatrix}1 & -1 & -2 \\ 0 & 2 & -3 \\ 0 & 0 & 3\end{bmatrix} = U
\end{aligned}
$$

The reduced matrix is upper triangular, so it is our matrix $U$. Now, since $E_1A=U$, we can invert $E_1$ to arrive at the desired LU factorization:

$$
\begin{aligned}
E_{1}A &= U \\
A &= E_{1}^{-1}U \\
A &= \begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 2 & 0 & 1\end{bmatrix}^{-1}U \\
A &= \begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ -2 & 0 & 1\end{bmatrix}_(⏟)_(L)\begin{bmatrix}1 & -1 & -2 \\ 0 & 2 & -3 \\ 0 & 0 & 3\end{bmatrix}_(⏟)_(U)
\end{aligned}
$$

**Caution:** In order to get a unit lower triangular matrix at the end, we have the following restriction while reducing a matrix $A$ to its *echelon form*:

- we can only add multiples of higher rows to lower rows ($R_j:=R_j+k R_i$ where $i < j$), and
- the other elementary row operations (swapping or multiplying a row by a number) are not allowed.

## Finding the LU Factorization Using a Single Elementary Row Operation

**Example:** Given that
$\begin{bmatrix}4 & 1 & -6 \\ 0 & 3 & 1 \\ 0 & 6 & -2\end{bmatrix}$
has the LU decomposition $A=LU$, then $L=$

**Explanation**

To find the matrices $L$ and $U$, we reduce the matrix $A$ to row echelon form. Whilst doing this, we simultaneously write down the row operations applied to $A$ in terms of matrix multiplication using elementary matrices.

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= \begin{bmatrix}4 & 1 & -6 \\ 0 & 3 & 1 \\ 0 & 6 & -2\end{bmatrix}\begin{vmatrix}R_{3} &:=R_{3} + (-2)R_{2} & ⟺\end{vmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -2 & 1\end{bmatrix}^(⏞)^(E_{1}) \cdot A \\
&= ∼\begin{bmatrix}4 & 1 & -6 \\ 0 & 3 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & -4\end{bmatrix} \\
&= U
\end{aligned}
$$

The reduced matrix is upper triangular, so it is our matrix $U$. Now, since $E_1A=U$, we can invert $E_1$ to arrive at the desired LU factorization:

$$
\begin{aligned}
E_{1}A &= U \\
A &= E_{1}^{-1}U \\
A &= \begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -2 & 1\end{bmatrix}^{-1}U \\
A &= \begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 & 1\end{bmatrix}_(⏟)_(L)\begin{bmatrix}4 & 1 & -6 \\ 0 & 3 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & -4\end{bmatrix}_(⏟)_(U)
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding the Lower Triangular Matrix Given the Elementary Row Operations

**Example:** Suppose that a $3 \times 3$ matrix $F$ is reduced to row echelon form using the following elementary row operations, applied in the order in which they appear:
$R_2:=R_2+R_1, \qquad R_3:=R_3+2R_1$
Given that $F$ has the LU factorization $F=LU$, then $L=$

**Explanation**

Applying

$$
R_2:=R_2 + {\color{red}1} \cdot R_1
$$

and then

$$
R_3:=R_3+ {\color{red}2} \cdot R_1
$$

is equivalent to multiplying $F$ from the left by the following elementary matrices:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 2 & 0 & 1\end{bmatrix}
$$

Now, since $E_2E_1F=U$, we can invert $E_2$ and $E_1$ to arrive at the desired LU factorization:

$$
\begin{aligned}
E_{2}E_{1}F &= U \\
E_{1}F &= E_{2}^{-1}U \\
F &= E_{1}^{-1}E_{2}^{-1}U \\
F &= \begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 & 0 \\ 1 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1\end{bmatrix}^{-1}\begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 2 & 0 & 1\end{bmatrix}^{-1}U \\
F &= (\begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 & 0 \\ -1 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ -2 & 0 & 1\end{bmatrix})U \\
F &= \begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 & 0 \\ -1 & 1 & 0 \\ -2 & 0 & 1\end{bmatrix}_(⏟)_(L) \cdot U
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding the LU Factorization Using Two or More Elementary Row Operations

**Example:** Given that
$\begin{bmatrix}4 & 8 & 0 \\ 5 & 11 & -6 \\ 0 & -4 & 16\end{bmatrix}$
has the LU decomposition $A=LU$, then $L=$

**Explanation**

To find the matrices $L$ and $U$, we reduce the matrix $A$ to row echelon form. Whilst doing this, we simultaneously write down the row operations applied to $A$ in terms of matrix multiplication using elementary matrices.

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= \begin{bmatrix}4 & 8 & 0 \\ 5 & 11 & -6 \\ 0 & -4 & 16\end{bmatrix}\begin{vmatrix}R_{2} &:=R_{2} + (-\frac{5}{4})R_{1} & ⟺ & \begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 & 0 \\ -\frac{5}{4} & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1\end{bmatrix}^(⏞)^(E_{1}) \cdot A \\ ∼\begin{bmatrix}4 & 8 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & -6 \\ 0 & -4 & 16\end{bmatrix} & R_{3} &:=R_{3} + 4R_{2} & ⟺\end{vmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 4 & 1\end{bmatrix}^(⏞)^(E_{2}) \cdot E_{1} \cdot A \\
&= ∼\begin{bmatrix}4 & 8 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & -6 \\ 0 & 0 & -8\end{bmatrix} \\
&= U
\end{aligned}
$$

The reduced matrix is upper triangular, so it is our matrix $U$. Now, since $E_2E_1A=U$, we can invert $E_2$ and $E_1$ to arrive at the desired LU factorization:

$$
\begin{aligned}
E_{2}E_{1}A &= U \\
E_{1}A &= E_{2}^{-1}U \\
A &= E_{1}^{-1}E_{2}^{-1}U \\
A &= \begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 & 0 \\ -\frac{5}{4} & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1\end{bmatrix}^{-1}\begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 4 & 1\end{bmatrix}^{-1}U \\
A &= (\begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 & 0 \\ \frac{5}{4} & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -4 & 1\end{bmatrix})U \\
A &= \begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 & 0 \\ \frac{5}{4} & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & -4 & 1\end{bmatrix}_(⏟)_(L)\begin{bmatrix}4 & 8 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & -6 \\ 0 & 0 & -8\end{bmatrix}_(⏟)_(U)
\end{aligned}
$$
