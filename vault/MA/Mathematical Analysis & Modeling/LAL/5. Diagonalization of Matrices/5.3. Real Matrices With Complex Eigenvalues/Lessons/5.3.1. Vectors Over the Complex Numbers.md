## Introduction

**Complex vectors** are vectors whose entries are *complex numbers*, such as $\begin{bmatrix}-1 + \textrm{i} \\ 3 + 2\textrm{i}\end{bmatrix}$ In general, $\mathbb{C}^n$ is the space of complex vectors with $n$ entries.

Complex vectors work mostly the same as real vectors (we can add them, multiply them by a scalar, etc). However, complex vectors have a few extra properties:

- We can take the complex conjugate of a vector by taking the complex conjugate of each component:
$\begin{bmatrix}-1 + \textrm{i} \\ 3 + 2\textrm{i}\end{bmatrix}$
- Complex vectors have a real and imaginary part. This means that we can write a vector in the form $\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{\color{blue}a} + \textrm{i}\mathbf{\color{red}b}$, where ${\color{blue}\mathbf{a}} = {\color{blue}\textrm{Re}(\mathbf{x})}$ and ${\color{red}\mathbf{b}} = {\color{red}\textrm{Im}(\mathbf{x})}$.

The real part of the vector contains the real part of each entry, and the imaginary part of the vector contains the imaginary part of each entry:
$\begin{bmatrix}-1 + \textrm{i} \\ 3 + 2\textrm{i}\end{bmatrix}$

## Calculating the Complex Conjugate of a Complex Vector

**Example:** If $\begin{bmatrix}-2 + 5\textrm{i} \\ 5 - 9\textrm{i} \\ -7 + 8\textrm{i}\end{bmatrix}$ find $\,\overline{\mathbf{x}}$.

**Explanation**

To find the complex conjugate of a vector, we take the complex conjugate of the entries of the vector. So, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^(―) &= \begin{bmatrix}-2 + 5\textrm{i} \\ 5 - 9\textrm{i} \\ -7 + 8\textrm{i}\end{bmatrix}^(―) \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}-2 + 5\textrm{i}^(―) \\ 5 - 9\textrm{i}^(―) \\ -7 + 8\textrm{i}^(―)\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}-2 - 5\textrm{i} \\ 5 + 9\textrm{i} \\ -7 - 8\textrm{i}\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Computing Components of the Real and Imaginary Parts of a Complex Vector

**Example:** If $\begin{bmatrix}a \\ b\end{bmatrix}$ and $\begin{bmatrix}c \\ d\end{bmatrix}$ are the real and imaginary parts of the vector $\begin{bmatrix}-3 + 7\textrm{i} \\ 5 - 2\textrm{i}\end{bmatrix}$ what is the value of $ab+cd$?

**Explanation**

For the given vector $\begin{bmatrix}-3 + 7\textrm{i} \\ 5 - 2\textrm{i}\end{bmatrix}$ we have that

$$
\begin{aligned}
x &= \begin{bmatrix}-3 + 7\textrm{i} \\ 5 - 2\textrm{i}\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}-3 \\ 5\end{bmatrix}_(⏟)_(Re(x)) + \textrm{i}\begin{bmatrix}7 \\ -2\end{bmatrix}_(⏟)_(Im(x))
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore

$$
a=-3, b=5, c=7
$$

and $d=-2$, which implies that

$$
ab+cd=(-3) \cdot 5 + 7 \cdot (-2) = -29
$$

## Properties of Vectors Over the Complex Numbers

The complex conjugate of vectors has a few useful properties:

- We can distribute the complex conjugate over individual vectors in a sum:
$\overline{\mathbf{z}_1 + \mathbf{z}_2} = \overline{\mathbf{z}_1} + \overline{\mathbf{z}_2}$
- We can distribute the complex conjugate over the scalar and the corresponding vector:
$\overline{k \mathbf{z}} = \overline{k} \cdot \overline{\mathbf{z}}$

## Finding the Real or Imaginary Part of a Scalar Given the Conjugate of a Scaled Vector

**Example:** Find $\textrm{Im}(k)$ given that
$\begin{bmatrix}-4\textrm{i} \\ 0\end{bmatrix}$
and
$\begin{bmatrix}8\textrm{i} - 12 \\ 0\end{bmatrix}$

**Explanation**

Since

$$
\overline{k\mathbf{v}} = \overline{k} \, \overline{\mathbf{v}}
$$

we get the following equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
kv^(―) &= k^(―)v^(―) \\
\begin{bmatrix}8\textrm{i} - 12 \\ 0\end{bmatrix} &= k^(―)\begin{bmatrix}-4\textrm{i} \\ 0\end{bmatrix}^(―) \\
\begin{bmatrix}8\textrm{i} - 12 \\ 0\end{bmatrix} &= k^(―)\begin{bmatrix}-4\textrm{i}^(―) \\ 0^(―)\end{bmatrix} \\
\begin{bmatrix}8\textrm{i} - 12 \\ 0\end{bmatrix} &= k^(―)\begin{bmatrix}4\textrm{i} \\ 0\end{bmatrix} \\
\begin{bmatrix}8\textrm{i} - 12 \\ 0\end{bmatrix} &= \begin{bmatrix}4\textrm{i}k^(―) \\ 0\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

So, we obtain that

$$
4\textrm{i}\,\overline{k} = 8\textrm{i}-12 \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \overline{k}=\dfrac{8\textrm{i}-12}{4\textrm i} = 2+3\textrm{i}
$$

Therefore,

$$
k=\overline{2+3\textrm{i}}=2-3\textrm{i}
$$

Finally,

$$
\textrm{Im}(k)=-3
$$
