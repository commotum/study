## Introduction

To determine the slope of a line that is given in standard form, we need to convert the equation to slope-intercept form.

To demonstrate, let's calculate the slope of the following line:

$$
2x + y = 8
$$

To write this equation in slope-intercept form, we solve for $y$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
2x + y &= 8 \\
2x + y - 2x &= 8 - 2x \\
y &= 8 - 2x \\
y &= -2x + 8
\end{aligned}
$$

From the slope-intercept equation, we can tell that the slope of the line is $m=-2$.

---

<a id="finding-the-slope-of-a-line-given-standard-form"></a>
## Finding the Slope of a Line Given Standard Form

**Example:** What is the slope of the line $4x-2y=1$?

**Explanation**

We can find the slope of the line if we can express its equation in slope-intercept form. To do this, we solve for $y$, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
4x - 2y &= 1 \\
- 2y &= -4x + 1 \\
2y &= 4x - 1 \\
\frac{2y}{2} &= \frac{4x - 1}{2} \\
y &= \frac{4x}{2} - \frac{1}{2} \\
y &= 2x - \frac{1}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

From the slope-intercept equation, we can tell that the slope of the line is $m=2$.

## Determining Intercepts of Lines Given in Standard Form

Suppose we want to find the $x$- and $y$-intercepts of the following line:

$$
2x + y = 8
$$

Let's find each of them in turn.

- To find the $x$-intercept, we substitute $y=0$ into the equation and solve for $x{:}$
$2x + (0)|= 8; 2x|= 8; \frac{2x}{2}|= \frac{8}{2}; x|= 4$
Therefore, the line intersects the $x$-axis at the point $(4,0)$.

- To find the $y$-intercept, we substitute $x=0$ into the equation and solve for $y{:}$
$2(0) + y|= 8; y|= 8$
Therefore, the line intersects the $y$-axis at the point $(0,8)$.

<a id="finding-the-x-intercept-of-a-line"></a>
## Finding the X-Intercept of a Line

**Example:** Find the point where the line $4x-3y=8$ intersects the $x$-axis.

**Explanation**

To find the $x$-intercept, we substitute $y=0$ into the equation and solve for $x$.

$$
\begin{aligned} 4x-3y&=8 \\[5pt] 4x-3(0)&=8 \\[5pt] 4x&=8 \\[5pt] \dfrac{4x}{4}&=\dfrac{8}{4} \\[5pt] x&=2 \end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the $x$-intercept is $2$, and we conclude that the line intersects the $x$-axis at

$$
\left(2,0\right)
$$

## Finding the Y-Intercept of a Line

**Example:** Find the point where the line $5x -4y=-8$ intersects the $y$-axis.

**Explanation**

To find the $y$-intercept, we substitute $x=0$ into the equation and solve for $y$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
5x - 4y &= -8 \\
5(0) - 4y &= -8 \\
- 4y &= -8 \\
y &= 2
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the $y$-intercept is $2$, and we conclude that the line intersects the $y$-axis at

$$
\left(0,2\right)
$$
