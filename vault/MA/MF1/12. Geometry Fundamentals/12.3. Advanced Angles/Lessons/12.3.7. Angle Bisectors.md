## Introduction

An angle **bisector** is a line that divides an angle into two equal parts.

For example, in the diagram below, $\overset{\longrightarrow}{AC}$ is the bisector of the angle $\angle BAD$.

![](<../Source/12.3.7. Angle Bisectors/Images/0b672caf28f48b0d46dc2dc922fcbbbd.png>)

The resulting angles $\angle BAC$ and $\angle CAD$ are congruent (they have the same measure).

---

<a id="using-the-property-of-a-bisection"></a>
## Using the Property of a Bisection

**Example:** In the figure below, $\overset{\longrightarrow}{AC}$ is the bisector of $\angle DAB$ and $m\angle DAB = 79^\circ$. What is the measure of $\angle DAC$?

![](<../Source/12.3.7. Angle Bisectors/Images/e0280ddcb295a30b26cbdbf1cfc42504.png>)

**Explanation**

An angle bisector divides the angle into two congruent angles with equal measures, so

$$
m\angle BAC = m\angle DAC
$$

![](<../Source/12.3.7. Angle Bisectors/Images/6e3c9f09f48f2677908090d1ddb78a74.png>)

Each of these congruent angles has a measure that is half the measure of the larger bisected angle. So, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
m∠DAC &= \frac{m∠DAB}{2} \\
&= \frac{79^{∘}}{2} \\
&= 39.5^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Calculating an Unknown Angle Given an Angle Bisector, an Angle Measurement, and a Straight Angle

**Example:** In the figure below, $\overset{\longrightarrow}{AG}$ is the bisector of $\angle CAD$ and $m\angle BAC = 40^\circ$. What is the measure of $\angle GAD$?

![](<../Source/12.3.7. Angle Bisectors/Images/b09208b3e0ad560f217a9e32afcf9e6a.png>)

**Explanation**

Since the angles $\angle CAD$ and $\angle BAC$ are supplementary, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
m∠CAD &= 180^{∘} - m∠BAC \\
&= 180^{∘} - 40^{∘} \\
&= 140^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

Since $\overset{\longrightarrow}{AG}$ is the bisector of $\angle CAD$, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
m∠GAD &= \frac{m∠CAD}{2} \\
&= \frac{140^{∘}}{2} \\
&= 70^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Calculating an Unknown Angle Given an Angle Bisector and Two Angle Measurements

**Example:** In the figure below, $\overrightarrow{OC}$ is the bisector of $\angle AOB$. If $m \angle AOD = 90^\circ$ and $m \angle COD = 35^\circ$, find the measure of $\angle BOD$.

![](<../Source/12.3.7. Angle Bisectors/Images/d208b5e0faf56a9dda25b4b2e7c43e2b.png>)

**Explanation**

![](<../Source/12.3.7. Angle Bisectors/Images/8e73eb1d382130856d934617336f03ae.png>)

By the angle addition postulate, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
m∠AOC &= m∠AOD - m∠COD \\
&= 90^{∘} - 35^{∘} \\
&= 55^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

The bisector of an angle divides the angle into two congruent angles. Therefore,

$$
m \angle AOB =2 m \angle AOC = 110^\circ
$$

Finally, the angle addition postulate gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
m∠BOD &= m∠AOB - m∠AOD \\
&= 110^{∘} - 90^{∘} \\
&= 20^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Calculating an Unknown Angle Given Two Angle Bisectors

**Example:** In the figure below, $\angle BAD$ is a straight angle, $\overset{\longrightarrow}{AG}$ is the bisector of $\angle CAD$ and $\overset{\longrightarrow}{AF}$ is the bisector of $\angle BAC$. What is the measure of $\angle GAF$?

![](<../Source/12.3.7. Angle Bisectors/Images/352d1d1647663efdd0ecf77db9809d58.png>)

**Explanation**

![](<../Source/12.3.7. Angle Bisectors/Images/dab7bd4e42ee35a12c2ebbfc1bad3a7a.png>)

Let

$$
m \angle FAC=x
$$

and

$$
m \angle CAG=y
$$

Then,

$$
m \angle GAF=m\angle FAC + m \angle CAG=x+y
$$

Since $\overset{\longrightarrow}{AF}$ is the bisector of $\angle BAC$, we have

$$
m \angle BAC=2x
$$

Since $\overset{\longrightarrow}{AG}$ is the bisector of $\angle CAD$, we have

$$
m \angle CAD=2y
$$

Finally, since $\angle BAD$ is a straight angle, we obtain:

$$
\begin{aligned}
m∠BAC + m∠CAD &= m∠BAD \\
2x + 2y &= 180^{∘} \\
x + y &= 90^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

Consequently,

$$
m \angle GAF=x+y=90^\circ
$$
