## Introduction

What is the value of $x$ that satisfies the following equation?

$$
3^x = 3^4
$$

We call this an **exponential equation** because the variable is contained within the exponent.

Now, this equation is pretty easy to solve. Because the bases on both sides of the equation are the same (they are both equal to $3$), we can simply equate the powers.

$$
\begin{aligned}
3^{x} &= 3^{4} \\
x &= 4
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the solution to this equation is $x=4$.

---

<a id="solving-an-exponential-equation"></a>
## Solving an Exponential Equation

**Example:** What is the solution to the equation $6^x - 6^5= 0$?

**Explanation**

First, we isolate the term with the variable exponent by adding $6^5$ to both sides:

$$
\begin{aligned}
6^{x} - 6^{5} &= 0 \\
6^{x} - 6^{5} + 6^{5} &= 0 + 6^{5} \\
6^{x} - 6^{5} + 6^{5} &= 0 + 6^{5} \\
6^{x} &= 6^{5}
\end{aligned}
$$

Since both sides of the equation share the same base $(6)$, we can equate the exponents.

$$
\begin{aligned}
6^{x} &= 6^{5} \\
x &= 5
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the solution is

$$
{\color{blue}x} = {\color{blue}5}
$$

## Changing the Base of an Expression

Let's take a look at the following equation:

$$
5^x = 25
$$

We can't solve this equation straight off the bat because the two sides of the equation don't share a common base.

However, notice that $25$ can be written as a power of $5$:

$$
25=5^2
$$

Therefore, we can rewrite the equation as

$$
5^x = 5^2
$$

Since both sides of the equation are now written as an exponential expression with a common base of $5$, we can set the exponents equal to each other and solve:

$$
\begin{aligned}
5^{x} &= 5^{2} \\
x &= 2
\end{aligned}
$$

To check that the solution is correct, we plug $x=2$ into the original equation:

$$
\begin{aligned} 5^{2} &= 25 \\ 25 &= 25 \; {\color{green}{\checkmark}} \end{aligned}
$$

<a id="solving-an-exponential-equation-by-changing-the-base"></a>
## Solving an Exponential Equation by Changing the Base

**Example:** What is the solution to $3^x = \dfrac{1}{9}$?

**Explanation**

First, we rewrite $9$ as $3^2$ and then proceed as before:

$$
\begin{aligned}
3^{x} &= \frac{1}{9} \\
3^{x} &= \frac{1}{3^{2}} \\
3^{x} &= 3^{-2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Since both sides of the equation share the same base $(3)$, we equate the exponents and get $x=-2$.

## Rearranging and Solving an Exponential Equation

**Example:** Solve the equation $2\cdot 3^x=54$.

**Explanation**

First, we divide both sides of the equation by $2$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
2 \cdot 3^{x} &= 54 \\
\frac{2 \cdot 3^{x}}{2} &= \frac{54}{2} \\
\frac{2 \cdot 3^{x}}{2} &= 27 \\
3^{x} &= 27
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we can rewrite $27$ as $3^3$ so that both sides have the same base:

$$
3^{x} = 3^{3}
$$

Equating the exponents, we get

$$
{\color{blue}x}={\color{blue}3}
$$
