## Introduction

We can use our knowledge of fraction multiplication to square fractions.

For example, let's evaluate the following squared fraction:

$$
\left(\dfrac{3}{5}\right)^2
$$

First, we write

$$
\left(\dfrac{3}{5}\right)^2
$$

with multiplication signs:

$$
(\frac{3}{5})^{2} = \frac{3}{5} \cdot \frac{3}{5}_(⏟)_([math]\color{blue}2[/math] times)
$$

Now, we calculate the product of the two fractions. We multiply the numerators, and we multiply the denominators:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(\frac{3}{5})^{2} &= \frac{3}{5} \cdot \frac{3}{5} \\
&= \frac{3 \cdot 3}{5 \cdot 5} \\
&= \frac{9}{25}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, we conclude that

$$
\left(\dfrac{3}{5}\right)^2= \dfrac{9}{25}
$$

<a id="squaring-a-fraction"></a>
## Squaring a Fraction

**Example:** Find the value of $\left(\dfrac{4}{5}\right)^2$.

**Explanation**

We proceed in two steps:

- First, we write $\left(\dfrac{4}{5}\right)^2$ with multiplication signs:
$(\frac{4}{5})^{2}|= \frac{4}{5} \cdot \frac{4}{5}_(⏟)_([math]{\color{blue}{2}}[/math] times)$
- Then, we multiply the numerators, and we multiply the denominators:
$(\frac{4}{5})^{2}|= \frac{4}{5} \cdot \frac{4}{5}; = \frac{4 \cdot 4}{5 \cdot 5}; = \frac{16}{25}$

Therefore, we conclude that

$$
\left(\dfrac{4}{5}\right)^2= \dfrac{16}{25}
$$

## Cubing a Fraction

**Example:** Expressed as a fraction in its simplest form, $\left(\dfrac{3}{5}\right)^3 =$

**Explanation**

We proceed in two steps:

- First, we write $\left(\dfrac{3}{5}\right)^3$ with multiplication signs:
$(\frac{3}{5})^{3}|= \frac{3}{5} \cdot \frac{3}{5} \cdot \frac{3}{5}_(⏟)_([math]{\color{blue}{3}}[/math] times)$
- Then, we multiply the numerators, and we multiply the denominators:
$(\frac{3}{5})^{3}|= \frac{3}{5} \cdot \frac{3}{5} \cdot \frac{3}{5}; = \frac{3 \cdot 3 \cdot 3}{5 \cdot 5 \cdot 5}; = \frac{9 \cdot 3}{25 \cdot 5}; = \frac{27}{125}$

Therefore, we conclude that

$$
\left(\dfrac{3}{5}\right)^3=\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue} \dfrac{27}{125}}
$$

## Raising a Fraction to the Fourth Power

**Example:** Calculate the value of $\left(\dfrac{3}{8}\right)^4$.

**Explanation**

We proceed in two steps:

- First, we write $\left(\dfrac{3}{8}\right)^4$ with multiplication signs:
$4$
- Then, we multiply the numerators, and we multiply the denominators:
$(\frac{3}{8})^{4}|= \frac{3}{8} \cdot \frac{3}{8} \cdot \frac{3}{8} \cdot \frac{3}{8}; = \frac{3 \cdot 3 \cdot 3 \cdot 3}{8 \cdot 8 \cdot 8 \cdot 8}; = (3 \cdot 3^(⏞)^(9) \cdot 3 \cdot 3^(⏞)^(9))/(8 \cdot 8_(⏟)_(64) \cdot 8 \cdot 8_(⏟)_(64)); = \frac{9 \cdot 9}{64 \cdot 64}; = \frac{81}{4096}$

Therefore, we conclude that

$$
\left(\dfrac{3}{8}\right)^4= \bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\dfrac{81}{4\,096}}
$$
