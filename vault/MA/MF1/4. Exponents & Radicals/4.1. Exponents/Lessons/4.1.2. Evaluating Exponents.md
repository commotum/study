## Introduction

To evaluate a power, we write it out with multiplication signs and then compute the resulting product.

So, to find the value of $9^2$, we first write it out with multiplication signs as follows:
$2$

Then, we compute the product:

$$
9^{2} = 9 \cdot 9 = 81
$$

Raising a number to the power of $2$ is known as **squaring** a number. Therefore, the following are equivalent.

- $9^2 = 81$
- The **square** of $9$ is $81$.
- $9$ raised to the *second* power equals $81$.

---

<a id="squaring-a-number"></a>
## Squaring a Number

**Example:** Find the value of $4$ raised to the second power.

**Explanation**

We need to evaluate $4^2$.

To evaluate $4^2$, we write it out with multiplication signs and then compute the product:

$$
4^{2} = 4 \cdot 4_(⏟)_([math]2[/math] times) = 16
$$

## The Cube of a Number

Let's evaluate the following exponent:

$$
2^{\color{red}{3}}
$$

We start by writing our exponent as repeated multiplication:
$3$

Now, we compute the product.

Since

$$
2 \times 2 = 4
$$

we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
2 \cdot 2_(⏟)_(4) \cdot 2 &= 4 \cdot 2
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, since

$$
{\color{blue}{4}}\times 2=8
$$

we have $2^3=8$.

Raising a number to the power of $3$ is known as **cubing** a number. Therefore, the following are equivalent.

- $2^3 = 8$
- The **cube** of $2$ is $8$.
- $2$ raised to the *third* power equals $8$.

---

<a id="cubing-a-number"></a>
## Cubing a Number

**Example:** What is the value of $5$ raised to the power of $3$?

**Explanation**

Here, $5$ raised to the power of $3$ means $5^3$.

To compute this power, we start by writing it out with multiplication signs:
$3$

Now, we compute the product. Since

$$
5 \times 5 = 25
$$

we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
5 \cdot 5_(⏟)_(25) \cdot 5 &= 25 \cdot 5
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we work out ${\color{blue}25} \times 5$.
$_(2); 2\begin{vmatrix}5 \\ \cdot\end{vmatrix}5; 1 \mid 2 \mid 5$

Therefore, $5^3=125$.

## Raising a Number to the First Power

**Example:** Find the value of $6^1$.

**Explanation**

Here, ${\color{blue}6}^{\color{red}1}$ is in exponent form. The base is $\color{blue}6$ and the exponent is ${\color{red}1}$.

This means that $\color{blue}6$ is taken as a factor only once. Therefore,
$6^1 = 6$.
