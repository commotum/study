## Introduction

To raise a power to another power, we multiply the exponents. This is the **power rule for exponents**.

To demonstrate, let's simplify the expression

$$
\left(3^5\right)^4
$$

by applying the power rule:

$$
\left(3^5\right)^4 = 3^{5\cdot 4} = 3^{20}
$$

**Note:** The power rule for exponents is just a quicker alternative to using repeated multiplication, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(3^{5})^{4} &=  \\
3^{5} \cdot 3^{5} \cdot 3^{5} \cdot 3^{5}_(⏟)_(4copies) &=  \\
3^{5 + 5 + 5 + 5} &=  \\
3^{5 \cdot 4} &=  \\
&= 3^{20}
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="applying-the-power-rule-with-positive-integer-exponents"></a>
## Applying the Power Rule With Positive Integer Exponents

**Example:** Express $\left(2\cdot 4^3\right)^2$ as a base raised to a single exponent.

**Explanation**

To raise a power to another power, we simply multiply the exponents:

$$
\left(2\cdot4^3\right)^2=2^{2}\cdot 4^{3\cdot 2}=4 \cdot 4^{6} = 4^{7}
$$

## Applying the Power Rule With Negative Integer Exponents

**Example:** Express $\left(\left(\dfrac{2}{5}\right)^{6}\right)^{-2}$ as a base raised to a single exponent.

**Explanation**

To raise a power to another power, we simply multiply the exponents:

$$
\left(\left(\dfrac{2}{5}\right)^{6}\right)^{-2}=\left(\dfrac{2}{5}\right)^{(6)\cdot (-2)}=\left(\dfrac{2}{5}\right)^{-12}
$$

## Applying the Power Rule With Negative Integer Exponents and Formatting the Result

**Example:** Express $\left(2^{-4}\right)^{2}$ as a base raised to a single, positive exponent.

**Explanation**

To raise a power to another power, we simply multiply the exponents:

$$
\left(2^{-4}\right)^{2}=2^{(-4)\cdot 2}=2^{-8}
$$

To evaluate $2^{-8}$, we find the reciprocal of the base $(2)$ and then raise that reciprocal to the same power but with the opposite sign:

$$
2^{-8} = \left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^{8}
$$
