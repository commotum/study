## Introduction

As we already know, to evaluate numerical expressions with a mix of different operations, we must follow the order of operations (PEMDAS).

The same rules apply to numerical expressions with negative numbers. For example, let's compute the value of

$$
(-2) \times (-3)^2
$$

To evaluate this expression, we use PEMDAS. First, we compute the exponents:

$$
(-2)\,\times\overbrace{(-3)^2}^{\large\color{blue} 9} = (-2) \times {\color{blue} 9}
$$

Then, we multiply:

$$
(-2) \times 9 = -18
$$

Therefore,

$$
(-2) \times (-3)^2 = -18
$$

## Evaluating Expressions With Stand-Alone Exponents

**Example:** Calculate $(-3)^3 \div 6$.

**Explanation**

To evaluate this expression, we use PEMDAS. First, we compute the exponents:

$$
(-3)^{3}_(⏟)_(-27) \div 6 = (-27) \div 6
$$

Then, we divide:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(-27) \div 6 &=  \\
((-27))/(6) &=  \\
- \frac{27}{6} &=  \\
- \frac{27 \div 3}{6 \div 3} &=  \\
&= - \frac{9}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Evaluating Expressions With Exponents Outside Parentheses

**Example:** Evaluate $(1-4)^2 \times \dfrac{1}{12}$.

**Explanation**

To evaluate this expression, we use PEMDAS. First, we perform the operation inside the parentheses:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(1 - 4_(⏟)_(-3))^{2} \cdot \frac{1}{12} &= (-3)^{2} \cdot \frac{1}{12}
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we compute the exponents:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(-3)^{2}_(⏟)_(9) \cdot \frac{1}{12} &= 9 \cdot \frac{1}{12}
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we multiply:

$$
\begin{aligned}
9 \cdot \frac{1}{12} &=  \\
\frac{9}{1} \cdot \frac{1}{12} &=  \\
\frac{9 \cdot 1}{1 \cdot 12} &=  \\
\frac{9}{12} &=  \\
\frac{9 \div 3}{12 \div 3} &=  \\
&= \frac{3}{4}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Evaluating Expressions With Exponents Inside Parentheses

**Example:** What is the value of $\left(5 \div (-2)^2 \right) \times \left(-\dfrac{4}{5} \right)$.

**Explanation**

To evaluate this expression, we use PEMDAS. First, consider the expression inside the parenthesis:

$$
5 \div (-2)^{2}
$$

Applying PEMDAS to this expression, we obtain:

$$
\begin{aligned}
5 \div (-2)^{2} &=  \\
5 \div 4 &=  \\
&= \frac{5}{4}
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we multiply:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(5 \div (-2)^{2}^(⏞)^(\frac{5}{4})) \cdot (-\frac{4}{5}) &=  \\
\frac{5}{4} \cdot (-\frac{4}{5}) &=  \\
- \frac{5}{4} \cdot \frac{4}{5} &=  \\
- \frac{5 \cdot 4}{4 \cdot 5} &=  \\
- \frac{5 \cdot 4}{4 \cdot 5} &=  \\
&= - 1
\end{aligned}
$$
