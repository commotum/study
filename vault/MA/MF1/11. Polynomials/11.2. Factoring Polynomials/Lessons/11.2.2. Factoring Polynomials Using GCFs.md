## Introduction

To factor a polynomial expression like $2a^2+6a$, we follow the three steps below:

1. First, we find the greatest common factor (or $\textrm{GCF}$) of both terms. In this case,
$\textrm{GCF}(2a^2,6a) = 2a$.
2. Then, we rewrite each term in the original expression as a product with the $\textrm{GCF}$:
$2a^{2} + 6a|=; (2a \cdot a) + (2a \cdot 3)$
3. Finally, we write the $\textrm{GCF}$ outside the parentheses and keep the remaining terms inside:
$(2a \cdot a) + (2a \cdot 3)|=; 2a(a + 3)$

Therefore, the factored form of $2a^2+6a$ is $2a(a+3)$.

We can check that we factored correctly by multiplying it out:

$$
\begin{aligned}
2a(a + 3) &=  \\
2a \cdot a + 2a \cdot 3 &=  \\
&= 2a^{2} + 6a \mid ✓
\end{aligned}
$$

## Factoring a Monomial out of a Binomial Containing One Variable

**Example:** Factor $4x^2+10x$.

**Explanation**

First, we find the $\textrm{GCF}$ of the two terms:

$$
\textrm{GCF}(4x^2,10x) = 2x
$$

Then, we rewrite each term in the original expression as a product with the $\textrm{GCF}$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
4x^{2} + 10x &=  \\
&= (2x \cdot 2x) + (2x \cdot 5)
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we write the $\textrm{GCF}$ outside the parentheses and keep the remaining terms inside:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(2x \cdot 2x) + (2x \cdot 5) &=  \\
&= 2x(2x + 5)
\end{aligned}
$$

## Factoring a Monomial out of a Binomial Containing Two Variables

**Example:** Factor $3t^2+12tw$.

**Explanation**

First, we find the $\textrm{GCF}$ of the two terms:

$$
\textrm{GCF}(3t^2,12tw) = 3t
$$

Then, we rewrite each term in the original expression as a product with the $\textrm{GCF}$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
3t^{2} + 12tw &=  \\
&= (3t \cdot t) + (3t \cdot 4w)
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we write the $\textrm{GCF}$ outside the parentheses and keep the remaining terms inside:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(3t \cdot t) + (3t \cdot 4w) &=  \\
&= 3t(t + 4w)
\end{aligned}
$$

## Factoring a Monomial From a Trinomial

**Example:** Factor $15x^2-5x-25xy$.

**Explanation**

First, we find the $\textrm{GCF}$ of the three terms:

$$
\textrm{GCF}(15x^2, 5x, 25xy) = 5x
$$

Then, we rewrite each term in the original expression as a product with the $\textrm{GCF}$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
15x^{2} - 5x - 25xy &=  \\
&= (5x \cdot 3x) - (5x \cdot 1) - (5x \cdot 5y)
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we write the $\textrm{GCF}$ outside the parentheses and keep the remaining terms inside:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(5x \cdot 3x) - (5x \cdot 1) - (5x \cdot 5y) &=  \\
&= 5x(3x - 1 - 5y)
\end{aligned}
$$

## Factoring a Binomial

**Example:** Factor the polynomial expression $8a(b^2-3)-c(b^2-3)$.

**Explanation**

First, we find the $\textrm{GCF}$ of the two terms:

$$
\textrm{GCF}(8a(b^2-3),c(b^2-3)) = b^2-3
$$

Each term is already written as a product with the $\textrm{GCF}$:

$$
8a \cdot (b^{2} - 3) - c \cdot (b^{2} - 3)
$$

Finally, we write the $\textrm{GCF}$ outside the parentheses and keep the remaining terms inside:

$$
\begin{aligned}
8a \cdot (b^{2} - 3) - c \cdot (b^{2} - 3) &=  \\
&= (8a - c)(b^{2} - 3)
\end{aligned}
$$
