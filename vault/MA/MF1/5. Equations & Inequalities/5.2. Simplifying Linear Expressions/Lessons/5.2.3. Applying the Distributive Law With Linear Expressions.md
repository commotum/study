## Introduction

The **distributive law** states that multiplying a group of terms by a number is the same as multiplying each term separately by that same number and adding the products.

For example, let's take a look at the following expression:

$$
2(3+4)
$$

We can evaluate this expression using the distributive law:

![](<../Source/5.2.3. Applying the Distributive Law With Linear Expressions/Images/23586c10e3598384c3c2a1d23d916d90.png>)

Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
2(3 + 4) &=  \\
2 \cdot 3 + 2 \cdot 4 &=  \\
6 + 8 &=  \\
&= 14 \mid
\end{aligned}
$$

The distributive law also applies when the multiplication occurs on the right of the parentheses:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(3 + 4) \cdot 2 &=  \\
3 \cdot 2 + 4 \cdot 2 &=  \\
6 + 8 &=  \\
&= 14
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="distributing-a-positive-number-over-the-sum-of-two-terms"></a>
## Distributing a Positive Number Over the Sum of Two Terms

**Example:** Expand $3(4x+2)$.

**Explanation**

Using the distributive law, we multiply each term inside the parentheses by the number $3$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
3(4x + 2) &=  \\
3 \cdot 4x + 3 \cdot 2 &=  \\
&= 12x + 6
\end{aligned}
$$

## Distributing a Negative Number Over the Sum of Two Terms

**Example:** Expand $(2y+7) \cdot (-3)$.

**Explanation**

Using the distributive law, we multiply each term inside the parentheses by the number
$(-3)$:

$$
\begin{aligned} (2y+7) \cdot (-3) \\[5pt] &= 2y\cdot(-3)+7\cdot(-3) \\[5pt] &= (-6y) + (-21) \\[5pt] &= -6y-21 \end{aligned}
$$

## Dealing With a Minus Inside the Parentheses

The distributive law also works when there is subtraction inside the parentheses.

For example, we can expand the expression $2(3-4)$ as follows:

![](<../Source/5.2.3. Applying the Distributive Law With Linear Expressions/Images/31bdd08501d1684bcf9c165278cd126c.png>)

Notice that we keep a plus ($+$) sign between the two terms. This helps us to avoid mistakes when multiplying with negative numbers.

Now, evaluating our expression, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
2(3 - 4) &=  \\
2 \cdot 3 + 2 \cdot (-4) &=  \\
(6) + (-8) &=  \\
6 - 8 &=  \\
&= - 2 \mid
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, the distributive law also applies when the multiplication occurs on the right of the parentheses:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(3 - 4) \cdot 2 &=  \\
3 \cdot 2 + (-4) \cdot 2 &=  \\
(6) + (-8) &=  \\
6 - 8 &=  \\
&= - 2 \mid
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="distributing-a-positive-number-over-the-difference-of-two-terms"></a>
## Distributing a Positive Number Over the Difference of Two Terms

**Example:** Expand $7(5x-3y)$.

**Explanation**

Using the distributive law, we multiply each term inside the parentheses by the number $7\mathbin$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
7(5x - 3y) &=  \\
7 \cdot 5x + 7 \cdot (-3y) &=  \\
(35x) + (-21y) &=  \\
&= 35x - 21y
\end{aligned}
$$

## Distributing a Negative Number Over the Difference of Two Terms

**Example:** Expand $-11(4x - 3y)$.

**Explanation**

Using the distributive law, we multiply each term inside the parentheses by the number $(-11)$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
-11(4x - 3y) &=  \\
(-11) \cdot 4x + (-11) \cdot (-3y) &=  \\
(-44x) + (33y) &=  \\
&= - 44x + 33y
\end{aligned}
$$
