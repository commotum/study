## Introduction

The **greatest common factor** (or $\textrm{GCF}$) of $12$ and $18x$ is the largest expression that will divide *both* $12$ and $18x$ with no remainder.

To find the GCF, we first decompose each expression into its separate factors. We express each constant as a product of its prime factors, and we treat each variable in the product as a separate factor.

$$
\begin{aligned}
12 &= 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 3 \\
18x &= 2 \cdot 3 \cdot 3 \cdot x
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we pair off the individual factors as much as possible.

$$
\begin{aligned}
12 &= 2_(―) \cdot 2 \cdot 3_(―) \\
18x &= 2_(―) \cdot 3_(―) \cdot 3 \cdot x
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we take one factor from each pair and multiply.

$$
\textrm{GCF}(12,18x) = {\color{red}2} \cdot {\color{blue}3} = 6
$$

Thus, the greatest common factor of $12$ and $18x$ is $6$.

## Finding the Greatest Common Factor when it is a Positive Constant

**Example:** Find the greatest common factor of $2x$ and $10y$.

**Explanation**

First, we decompose each expression into its separate factors. We express each constant as a product of its prime factors, and we treat each variable in the product as a separate factor.

$$
\begin{aligned}
2x &= 2 \cdot x \\
10y &= 2 \cdot 5 \cdot y
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we pair off the individual factors as much as possible.

$$
\begin{aligned}
2x &= 2_(―) \cdot x \\
10y &= 2_(―) \cdot 5 \cdot y
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we take one factor from each pair. Here, there is only one pair, so we just need to take that factor. There are no other factors to multiply it with.

$$
\textrm{GCF}(2x,10y) = {\color{red}{2}}
$$

Thus, the greatest common factor of $2x$ and $10y$ is $2$.

## Finding the Greatest Common Factor when it Contains Variables

**Example:** Find the $\textrm{GCF}$ of $16a$ and $4a$.

**Explanation**

First, we decompose each expression into its separate factors. We express each constant as a product of its prime factors, and we treat each variable in the product as a separate factor.

$$
\begin{aligned}
16a &= 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 \cdot a \\
4a &= 2 \cdot 2 \cdot a
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we pair off the individual factors as much as possible.

$$
\begin{aligned}
16a &= 2_(―) \cdot 2_(―) \cdot 2 \cdot 2 \cdot a_(―) \\
4a &= 2_(―) \cdot 2_(―) \cdot a_(―)
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we take one factor from each pair and multiply.

$$
\textrm{GCF}(16a, 4a) = \underline{2} \cdot \underline{2} \cdot \underline{a} = 4a
$$

## Finding the Greatest Common Factor when there are No Common Factors

**Example:** What is the $\textrm{GCF}$ of $4a$ and $15b$?

**Explanation**

First, we decompose each expression into its separate factors. We express each constant as a product of its prime factors, and we treat each variable in the product as a separate factor.

$$
\begin{aligned}
4a &= 2 \cdot 2 \cdot a \\
15b &= 3 \cdot 5 \cdot b
\end{aligned}
$$

If we try to pair off the individual factors, we can't find any common factors. The only common factor is one. Thus,

$$
\textrm{GCF}(4a, 15b) = 1
$$

## Finding the Greatest Common Factor of Three Expressions

**Example:** Find the $\textrm{GCF}$ of $8a$, $4c$ and $12c$.

**Explanation**

First, we decompose each expression into its separate factors. We express each constant as a product of its prime factors, and we treat each variable in the product as a separate factor.

$$
\begin{aligned}
8a &= 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 \cdot a \\
4c &= 2 \cdot 2 \cdot c \\
12c &= 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 3 \cdot c
\end{aligned}
$$

We now match up the individual factors as much as possible.

$$
\begin{aligned}
8a &= 2_(―) \cdot 2_(―) \cdot 2 \cdot a \\
4c &= 2_(―) \cdot 2_(―) \cdot c \\
12c &= 2_(―) \cdot 2_(―) \cdot 3 \cdot c
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we take one factor from each pair and multiply.

$$
\textrm{GCF}(8a, 4c, 12c) = 2 \cdot 2 = 4
$$
