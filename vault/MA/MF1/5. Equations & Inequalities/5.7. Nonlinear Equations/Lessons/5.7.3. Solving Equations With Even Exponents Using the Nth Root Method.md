## Introduction

How do we solve an equation with a variable raised to an even exponent, such as

$$
x^6 = 64
$$

We can solve this equation by taking the $6$th root of each side and solving for $x$.

Since the exponent $(6)$ is even, there will be two real solutions (positive and negative):

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^{6} &= 64 \\
\sqrt[6]{x^{6}} &= ± \sqrt[6]{64} \\
x &= ± \sqrt[6]{2^{6}} \\
x &= ± 2
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the solutions are $x=\pm 2$.

In general, we have the following rule of thumb:

> *When $n$ is even, and we take the $n$th root of both sides of $x^n = a$ with $a \geq 0$, we get two solutions (positive and negative):* $x = \pm\sqrt[n]{a}$

**Watch out!** If $a<0$ and $n$ is even, equation $x^n = a$ has no solution since, in this case,

$$
x^n \geq 0
$$

for any real $x$.

## Solving Equations With Even Exponents

**Example:** Find all of the real solutions to the equation $9x^4 = \dfrac{1}{9}$.

**Explanation**

We can solve this equation by isolating the $x^4$ term and then taking the $4$th root of each side.

First, let's isolate the $x^4$ term on the left-hand side:

$$
\begin{aligned}
9x^{4} &= \frac{1}{9} \\
x^{4} &= \frac{1}{81}
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we take the $4$th root of each side and solve for $x$. Since the exponent $(4)$ is even, we consider both the positive and negative solutions:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^{4} &= \frac{1}{81} \\
\sqrt[4]{x^{4}} &= ± \sqrt[4]{\frac{1}{81}} \\
x &= ± \sqrt[4]{(\frac{1}{3})^{4}} \\
x &= ± \frac{1}{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the solutions are

$$
x=\pm \dfrac13
$$

## Solving Equations Containing an Expression Raised to an Even Power

**Example:** Find all of the real solutions to the equation $(x-3)^4=-\dfrac{1}{81}$.

**Explanation**

Notice that

$$
(x-3)^4 \geq 0
$$

for all values of $x$ because the exponent ($4$) is even. This expression can never be negative.

Therefore, there are no real solutions to this equation.
