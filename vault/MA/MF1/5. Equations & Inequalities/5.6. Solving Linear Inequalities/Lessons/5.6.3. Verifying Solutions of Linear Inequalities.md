## Introduction

We can find which values satisfy an inequality by trial and error, which involves substituting a number for the variable and checking to see if we get a true statement.

For example, which of the following values of $y$ satisfy the inequality $y - 1 > 3\,$?

1. $y=6$
2. $y=2$
3. $y=3$

Let's go through the possibilities one at a time.

- Substituting the value ${\color{blue}y}={\color{blue}6}$ into the given equation, we get:
$\begin{bmatrix}y - 1 & > 3 \\ 6 - 1 & > 3 \\ 5 & > 3✓\end{bmatrix}$
This is a **true** statement, so $y=6$ **satisfies** this inequality.
- Substituting the value ${\color{blue}y}={\color{blue}2}$ into the given equation, we get:
$\begin{bmatrix}y - 1 & > 3 \\ 2 - 1 & > 3 \\ 1 & > 3\cdot\end{bmatrix}$
This is a **false** statement, so $y=2$ **does not satisfy** this inequality.
- Substituting the value ${\color{blue}y}={\color{blue}3}$ into the given equation, we get:
$\begin{bmatrix}y - 1 & > 3 \\ 3 - 1 & > 3 \\ 2 & > 3\cdot\end{bmatrix}$
This is a **false** statement, so $y=3$ **does not satisfy** this inequality.

Therefore, only I satisfies the inequality.

## Identifying Values that Satisfy a Given Inequality

**Example:** Which of the following values of $x$ satisfy the inequality $2x < 10\,$?

1. $x=0$
2. $x=2$
3. $x=5$

**Explanation**

We substitute each value into the given inequality and check to see if the resulting statement is true or false.

- We substitute the value ${\color{blue}x}={\color{blue}0}$ into the given inequality. We get:
$\begin{bmatrix}2x & < 10 \\ 2 \cdot x & < 10 \\ 2 \cdot 0 & < 10 \\ 0 & < 10✓\end{bmatrix}$
So, substituting $x=0$ into the inequality leads to a **true** statement. Therefore, $x=0$ **satisfies** this inequality.
- We substitute the value ${\color{blue}x}={\color{blue}2}$ into the given inequality. We get:
$\begin{bmatrix}2x & < 10 \\ 2 \cdot x & < 10 \\ 2 \cdot 2 & < 10 \\ 4 & < 10✓\end{bmatrix}$
So, substituting $x=2$ into the inequality leads to a **true** statement. Therefore, $x=2$ **satisfies** this inequality.
- We substitute the value ${\color{blue}x}={\color{blue}5}$ into the given inequality. We get:
$\begin{bmatrix}2x & < 10 \\ 2 \cdot x & < 10 \\ 2 \cdot 5 & < 10 \\ 10 & < 10\cdot\end{bmatrix}$
So, substituting $x=5$ into the inequality leads to a **false** statement. Therefore, $x=5$ **does not satisfy** this inequality.

## Identifying Values that Satisfy a Given Inequality with Fractions

**Example:** Which of the following values of $t$ satisfy the inequality $\dfrac{2t}{3} < 8\,$?

1. $t=9$
2. $t=6$
3. $t=3$

**Explanation**

We substitute each value into the given inequality and check to see if the resulting statement is true or false.

- We substitute the value ${\color{blue}t}={\color{blue}9}$ into the given inequality. We get:
$\begin{bmatrix}\frac{2t}{3} & < 8 \\ \frac{2 \cdot t}{3} & < 8 \\ \frac{2 \cdot 9}{3} & < 8 \\ \frac{18}{3} & < 8 \\ 6 & < 8✓\end{bmatrix}$
So, substituting $t = 9$ into the inequality leads to a **true** statement. Therefore, $t=9$ **satisfies** this inequality.
- We substitute the value ${\color{blue}t}={\color{blue}6}$ into the given inequality. We get:
$\begin{bmatrix}\frac{2t}{3} & < 8 \\ \frac{2 \cdot t}{3} & < 8 \\ \frac{2 \cdot 6}{3} & < 8 \\ \frac{12}{3} & < 8 \\ 4 & < 8✓\end{bmatrix}$
So, substituting $t=6$ into the inequality leads to a **true** statement. Therefore, $t=6$ **satisfies** this inequality.
- We substitute the value ${\color{blue}t}={\color{blue}3}$ into the given inequality. We get:
$\begin{bmatrix}\frac{2t}{3} & < 8 \\ \frac{2 \cdot t}{3} & < 8 \\ \frac{2 \cdot 3}{3} & < 8 \\ \frac{6}{3} & < 8 \\ 2 & < 8✓\end{bmatrix}$
So, substituting $t=3$ into the inequality leads to a **true** statement. Therefore, $t=3$ **satisfies** this inequality.

## Identifying Values that Satisfy a Given Compound Inequality

**Example:** Which of the following values of $x$ satisfy the inequality $1 \leq \dfrac{x}{2}-1 < 3\,$?

1. $x=0$
2. $x=4$
3. $x=10$

**Explanation**

We substitute each value into the given inequality and check to see if the resulting statement is true or false.

- We substitute the value ${\color{blue}x}={\color{blue}0}$ into the given inequality. We get:
$\begin{bmatrix}1 & \le \frac{x}{2} - 1 < 3 \\ 1 & \le \frac{0}{2} - 1 < 3 \\ 1 & \le 0 - 1 < 3 \\ 1 & \le - 1 < 3\cdot\end{bmatrix}$
So, substituting $x=0$ into the inequality leads to a **false** statement. Therefore, $x=0$ **does not satisfy** this inequality.
- We substitute the value ${\color{blue}x}={\color{blue}4}$ into the given inequality. We get:
$\begin{bmatrix}1 & \le \frac{x}{2} - 1 < 3 \\ 1 & \le \frac{4}{2} - 1 < 3 \\ 1 & \le 2 - 1 < 3 \\ 1 & \le 1 < 3✓\end{bmatrix}$
So, substituting $x=4$ into the inequality leads to a **true** statement. Therefore, $x=4$ **satisfies** this inequality.
- We substitute the value ${\color{blue}x}={\color{blue}10}$ into the given inequality. We get:
$\begin{bmatrix}1 & \le \frac{x}{2} - 1 < 3 \\ 1 & \le \frac{10}{2} - 1 < 3 \\ 1 & \le 5 - 1 < 3 \\ 1 & \le 4 < 3\cdot\end{bmatrix}$
So, substituting $x=10$ into the inequality leads to a **false** statement. Therefore, $x=10$ **does not satisfy** this inequality.

## Identifying Inequalities that are Satisfied by a Given Value

**Example:** The value $a = 2$ satisfies which of the following inequalities?

1. $\dfrac{a}{2} + 2 > 5$
2. $1 \leq 2a < 6$
3. $3a - 1 < 10$

**Explanation**

We substitute the given value into each of the inequalities and check to see if the resulting statement is true or false.

- We substitute the value ${\color{blue}a} = {\color{blue}2}$ into the first inequality. We get:
$\begin{bmatrix}\frac{a}{2} + 2 & > 5 \\ \frac{2}{2} + 2 & > 5 \\ 1 + 2 & > 5 \\ 3 & > 5\cdot\end{bmatrix}$
So, substituting $a = 2$ leads to a **false** statement. Therefore, $a = 2$ **does not satisfy** this inequality.
- We substitute the value ${\color{blue}a}={\color{blue}2}$ into the second inequality. We get:
$\begin{bmatrix}1 & \le 2a < 6 \\ 1 & \le 2 \cdot a < 6 \\ 1 & \le 2 \cdot 2 < 6 \\ 1 & \le 4 < 6✓\end{bmatrix}$
So, substituting $a = 2$ leads to a **true** statement. Therefore, $a = 2$ **satisfies** this inequality.
- We substitute the value ${\color{blue}a} = {\color{blue}2}$ into the third inequality. We get:
$\begin{bmatrix}3a - 1 & < 10 \\ 3 \cdot a - 1 & < 10 \\ 3 \cdot 2 - 1 & < 10 \\ 5 & < 10✓\end{bmatrix}$
So, substituting $a = 2$ leads to a **true** statement. Therefore, $a = 2$ **satisfies** this inequality.
