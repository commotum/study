## Introduction

To solve an equation with a decimal coefficient, such as
$0.2x = -1$,

we start by getting rid of the decimal. To do this, we multiply both sides of the equation by ${\color{blue}10}$. Multiplying a decimal by $10$ shifts the decimal place one spot over to the right.

$$
\begin{aligned}
0.2x &= -1 \\
10 \cdot (0.2x) &= 10 \cdot (-1) \\
2x &= -10
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we solve the equation using the multiplication principle, as usual:

$$
\begin{aligned}
2x &= -10 \\
\frac{2x}{2} &= \frac{-10}{2} \\
\frac{2x}{2} &= -\frac{10}{2} \\
x &= -5
\end{aligned}
$$

## Solving Linear Equations Containing Single-Digit Decimals

**Example:** What is the solution to the equation $0.9s=3.6$?

**Explanation**

First, we multiply both sides of the equation by $10$ to get rid of the decimals:

$$
\begin{aligned}
0.9s &= 3.6 \\
10 \cdot (0.9s) &= 10 \cdot (3.6) \\
9s &= 36
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we solve the equation using the multiplication principle, as usual:

$$
\begin{aligned}
9s &= 36 \\
\frac{9s}{9} &= \frac{36}{9} \\
\frac{9s}{9} &= \frac{36}{9} \\
s &= 4
\end{aligned}
$$

## Solving Linear Equations with More than One Decimal Place

How do we get rid of the decimal in an equation like

$$
0.25x = 2
$$

when there is more than one number behind the decimal place? If we multiply by $10$ as usual, we shift the decimal place over one spot to the right:

$$
\begin{aligned}
0.25x &= 2 \\
10 \cdot (0.25x) &= 10 \cdot (2) \\
2.5x &= 20
\end{aligned}
$$

However, there is still one number behind the decimal place. So, we can multiply by $10$ again:

$$
\begin{aligned}
2.5x &= 20 \\
10 \cdot (2.5x) &= 10 \cdot (20) \\
25x &= 200
\end{aligned}
$$

Now that our equation contains only whole numbers, we can continue with the usual method of applying the multiplication principle:

$$
\begin{aligned}
25x &= 200 \\
\frac{25x}{25} &= \frac{200}{25} \\
\frac{25x}{25} &= \frac{200}{25} \\
x &= 8
\end{aligned}
$$

Looking back, we can see a faster way to apply the same method. We multiplied by $10$ twice, which means that overall, we multiplied by $100$. In general, multiplying a decimal by $100$ shifts the decimal place over two spots to the right. So, we could have solved the equation faster by multiplying by $100$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
0.25x &= 2 \\
100 \cdot (0.25x) &= 100 \cdot (2) \\
25x &= 200 \\
\frac{25x}{25} &= \frac{200}{25} \\
\frac{25x}{25} &= \frac{200}{25} \\
x &= 8
\end{aligned}
$$

## Solving Linear Equations Containing Two-Digit Decimals

**Example:** What is the solution to the equation $0.25s=0.5$?

**Explanation**

First, we multiply both sides of the equation by $100$ to get rid of the decimals:

$$
\begin{aligned}
0.25s &= 0.5 \\
100 \cdot (0.25s) &= 100 \cdot (0.5) \\
25s &= 50
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we solve the equation using the multiplication principle, as usual:

$$
\begin{aligned}
25s &= 50 \\
\frac{25s}{25} &= \frac{50}{25} \\
\frac{25s}{25} &= \frac{50}{25} \\
s &= 2
\end{aligned}
$$

## Solving Two-Step Equations

**Example:** If $0.25+0.03z=1$, then what is the value of $z$?

**Explanation**

First, we multiply both sides of the equation by $100$ to get rid of the decimals:

$$
\begin{aligned}
0.25 + 0.03z &= 1 \\
100 \cdot (0.25 + 0.03z) &= 100 \cdot (1) \\
100 \cdot (0.25) + 100 \cdot (0.03z) &= 100 \\
25 + 3z &= 100
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we solve the equation using the addition and multiplication principles, as usual:

$$
\begin{aligned}
25 + 3z &= 100 \\
3z + 25 &= 100 \\
3z + 25 - 25 &= 100 - 25 \\
3z &= 75 \\
\frac{3z}{3} &= \frac{75}{3} \\
z &= 25
\end{aligned}
$$
