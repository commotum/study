## Introduction

Equations can contain more than one variable. For example, the following equation has two variables, $\color{blue}x$ and ${\color{red}y}{:}$

$$
{\color{blue}x} - 7{\color{red}y} = 1
$$

To **solve the equation for the variable** ${\color{blue}{x}}$, we need to isolate $\color{blue}x$ on one side of the equation:

In this case, we apply the addition principle and add $7{\color{red}y}$ to both sides:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x - 7y &= 1 \\
x - 7y + 7y &= 1 + 7y \\
x + 0 &= 1 + 7y \\
x &= 1 + 7y
\end{aligned}
$$

And that's it! We have successfully isolated the variable ${\color{blue}{x}}$.

Let's now look at an example where we must apply the multiplication principle.

## Solving One-Step Equations

**Example:** Solve the equation $4x = y$ for the variable $x$.

**Explanation**

We need to isolate the variable $x$. To do this, we treat $y$ as though it were a constant and apply the addition and multiplication principles as usual.

In this case, we simply divide both sides of the equation by $4$:

$$
\begin{aligned} \require{cancel} 4x &= y \\[5pt] \dfrac {4x} {4} &= \dfrac y 4 \\[5pt] \dfrac {{\cancel{4}}x} {\cancel{4}} &= \dfrac y 4 \\[5pt] x &= \dfrac y4 \end{aligned}
$$

## Solving Two-Step Equations

**Example:** Given that $\dfrac{x}{3} + 2y = 1$, express $x$ in terms of $y$.

**Explanation**

We need to isolate the variable $x$. To do this, we treat $y$ as though it were a constant and apply the addition and multiplication principles as usual.

First, we subtract $2y$ from both sides of the equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{x}{3} + 2y &= 1 \\
\frac{x}{3} + 2y - 2y &= 1 - 2y \\
\frac{x}{3} + 0 &= 1 - 2y \\
\frac{x}{3} &= 1 - 2y
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we multiply both sides of the equation by $3$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{x}{3} &= 1 - 2y \\
3 \cdot \frac{x}{3} &= 3 \cdot (1 - 2y) \\
3 \cdot \frac{x}{3} &= 3 \cdot (1 - 2y) \\
x &= 3 \cdot (1 - 2y) \\
x &= 3 \cdot 1 - 3 \cdot 2y \\
x &= 3 - 6y
\end{aligned}
$$

## Solving Multi-Step Equations

**Example:** Solve the following equation for the variable $x$:

$\dfrac{3x-2y}{3}=\dfrac{1+2y}{4}$

**Explanation**

We need to isolate the variable $x$. First, we cross-multiply:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{3x - 2y}{3} &= \frac{1 + 2y}{4} \\
4 \cdot (3x - 2y) &= 3 \cdot (1 + 2y) \\
12x - 8y &= 3 + 6y
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we add $8y$ to both sides of the equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
12x - 8y &= 3 + 6y \\
12x - 8y + 8y &= 3 + 6y + 8y \\
12x &= 3 + 14y
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we divide both sides of the equation by $12$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
12x &= 3 + 14y \\
\frac{12x}{12} &= \frac{3 + 14y}{12} \\
\frac{12x}{12} &= \frac{3 + 14y}{12} \\
x &= \frac{3 + 14y}{12}
\end{aligned}
$$
