## Introduction

When we divide a whole number by a non-unit fraction, the answer is often not a whole number.

In this lesson, we'll learn how to solve division problems in these cases using models. The process is similar to cases when the answer is a whole number (we saw this in a previous lesson). The difference now is that we have to deal with a remainder.

To demonstrate, let's use a model to solve the following division problem:

$$
{\color{blue}{1}} \div \dfrac{2}{3}
$$

We start with a model that represents $\color{blue}1$ whole:

![](<../Source/1.5.7. Dividing Whole Numbers by Fractions Using Models- Fractional Results/Images/55e6bf6b05087b9a9b42f0a5654ca19c.png>)

We need to determine how many times

$$
\dfrac{\bbox[2pt, lightgray]{2}}3
$$

fits into $\color{blue}1$ whole. So, we proceed as follows:

- First, we split our whole into *thirds* (the denominator of our fraction):![](<../Source/1.5.7. Dividing Whole Numbers by Fractions Using Models- Fractional Results/Images/4930c8f77c863578220addb26efe8d88.png>)
- Next, we break this into groups of $\bbox[2pt, lightgray]{2}$:![](<../Source/1.5.7. Dividing Whole Numbers by Fractions Using Models- Fractional Results/Images/f4e5e41fb609202e6b1a0dd850792800.png>)

Note the following:

- We have $\fbox{1}$ full group. This is the whole number part of our answer.
- We see that there is $\bbox[2pt, Moccasin]{1}$ piece left over out of a possible group of $\bbox[2pt, lightgray]{2}$. So, the fractional part of our answer is
$\dfrac{\bbox[2pt, Moccasin]{1}}{\bbox[2pt, lightgray]{2}}$.

Therefore, the solution to our division problem is as follows:

$$
1 \div \dfrac{2}{3} = \fbox{1} \, \dfrac{\bbox[2pt, Moccasin]{1}}{\bbox[2pt, lightgray]{2}}
$$

Let's see another example.

## Finding the Remainder in a Division Problem

**Example:** Use the model above to determine the missing fraction in the following division problem.
$0$

![](<../Source/1.5.7. Dividing Whole Numbers by Fractions Using Models- Fractional Results/Images/9804779de28bd3a1955c927f68854da8.png>)

**Explanation**

Let's start by interpreting our model:

- The model shows $2$ wholes split into sixths.![](<../Source/1.5.7. Dividing Whole Numbers by Fractions Using Models- Fractional Results/Images/19aeb7ee3b53fe89931cfa88c8e26f47.png>)
- There are $\bbox[2pt, lightgray]{5}$ sixths in each group, with $\bbox[2pt, Moccasin]{2}$ remaining.![](<../Source/1.5.7. Dividing Whole Numbers by Fractions Using Models- Fractional Results/Images/f52e812944c57c1082cb055a15c47a9b.png>)

Note the following:

- There are $\color{red}2$ full groups.
- There are $\bbox[2pt, Moccasin]{2}$ sixths left over out of a possible group of $\bbox[2pt, lightgray]{5}$. So the fractional part of our answer is
$\dfrac{\bbox[2pt, Moccasin]{2}}{\bbox[2pt, lightgray]{5}}$.

Therefore,

$$
2 \div \dfrac{5}{6} = {\color{red}{2}} \, \dfrac{\bbox[2pt, Moccasin]{2}}{\bbox[2pt, lightgray]{5}}
$$

## Dividing a Whole Number by a Non-Unit Fraction

**Example:** Use the model above to calculate the value of $2 \div \dfrac{3}{7}$.

![](<../Source/1.5.7. Dividing Whole Numbers by Fractions Using Models- Fractional Results/Images/9e2f8f3867d89863a5f7254c04612122.png>)

**Explanation**

To calculate

$$
{\color{red}2} \div \dfrac{\bbox[2pt, lightgray]{3}}{\color{blue}7}
$$

, we first need to divide each of the $\color{red}2$ wholes into $\color{blue}7$ equal parts.

![](<../Source/1.5.7. Dividing Whole Numbers by Fractions Using Models- Fractional Results/Images/088dda7dcbf1963d5beb0eff8e304690.png>)

Next, we break this into groups of $\bbox[2pt, lightgray]{3}$:

![](<../Source/1.5.7. Dividing Whole Numbers by Fractions Using Models- Fractional Results/Images/56612b9bd764a85b4da174bd38894d9e.png>)

Note the following:

- We got $\fbox{4}$ full groups.
- There are $\bbox[2pt, Moccasin]{2}$ pieces left over out of a possible group of $\bbox[2pt, lightgray]{3}$. So the fractional part of our answer is
$\dfrac{\bbox[2pt, Moccasin]{2}}{\bbox[2pt, lightgray]{3}}$.

Therefore,

$$
2 \div \dfrac{3}{7} = \fbox{4} \, \dfrac{\bbox[2pt, Moccasin]{2}}{\bbox[2pt, lightgray]{3}}
$$
