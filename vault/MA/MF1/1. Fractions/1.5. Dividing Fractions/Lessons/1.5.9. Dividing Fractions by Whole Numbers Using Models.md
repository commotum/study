## Introduction

When we divide a fraction by a whole number, say ${\color{red}{2}}$, the idea is to

- split the fraction into ${\color{red}{2}}$ equal parts, and
- determine the fraction that makes up each part.

For example, suppose we wish to find the value of

$$
\dfrac{4}{5} \div 2
$$

Let's start by drawing the fraction model for

$$
\dfrac45
$$

![](<../Source/1.5.9. Dividing Fractions by Whole Numbers Using Models/Images/f345c5d4630be0995779cde0327f55b4.png>)

We need to divide this fraction by ${\color{red}{2}}$. To do this, we draw a horizontal line through our fraction that splits it into $\color{red}2$ *equal parts*.

![](<../Source/1.5.9. Dividing Fractions by Whole Numbers Using Models/Images/5381a543704612749e4f2b2712e1f7d7.png>)

We only need to determine the fraction that makes up one of the parts. Therefore, we can focus on only one row (i.e., the bottom row).

Now, we remove all the shaded parts not in the bottom row.

![](<../Source/1.5.9. Dividing Fractions by Whole Numbers Using Models/Images/54a9e7ebe3c900b2bdcfe928092fb128.png>)

From the picture on the right, we obtain that the shaded part represents

$$
\dfrac{4}{10}
$$

of the whole. Therefore, we conclude that

$$
\dfrac{4}{5} \div 2 = \dfrac{4}{10} \,
$$

## Interpreting a Division Model

**Example:** What division problem is represented by the model below?

![](<../Source/1.5.9. Dividing Fractions by Whole Numbers Using Models/Images/3e427c7d8255c9a7b0b15be2594a2639.png>)

**Explanation**

We have

$$
\dfrac{5}{7}
$$

on the left and

$$
\dfrac{5}{21}
$$

on the right.

![](<../Source/1.5.9. Dividing Fractions by Whole Numbers Using Models/Images/c5933117803b710f958eb877577cf9d6.png>)

Therefore, the division problem shown is

$$
\dfrac{5}{7} \div 3 = \dfrac{5}{21}
$$

## Finding a Missing Divisor From a Division Model

**Example:** What number is missing from the division problem below?

![](<../Source/1.5.9. Dividing Fractions by Whole Numbers Using Models/Images/51f8dfbb0868c70d4f4b2ec20d147495.png>)

**Explanation**

We have

$$
\dfrac{3}{4}
$$

on the left and

$$
\dfrac{3}{20}
$$

on the right.

![](<../Source/1.5.9. Dividing Fractions by Whole Numbers Using Models/Images/0d6fb8d2b1b46929b76a8cf5a9f82850.png>)

The division operation splits each vertical line into $\color{blue}5$ equal parts. So the shaded parts on the left are $\color{blue}5$ times larger than the shaded parts on the right.

Therefore, the division problem shown is

$$
\dfrac 3 4 \div {\color{blue}{5}} = \dfrac{3}{20}
$$

So, the missing number is $\color{blue}5$.

## Dividing a Fraction by a Whole Number Using a Division Model

**Example:** Use the model below to calculate $\dfrac{5}{6} \div 4$.

![](<../Source/1.5.9. Dividing Fractions by Whole Numbers Using Models/Images/59a0c04d06716ee4dd66241ee7baa8dd.png>)

**Explanation**

Since we need to divide by $\color{blue}4$, we split the whole into $\color{blue}4$ equal parts horizontally.

![](<../Source/1.5.9. Dividing Fractions by Whole Numbers Using Models/Images/95cd947116f76b63c65e33ac3a928251.png>)

From the picture on the right, we obtain that the shaded part represents

$$
\dfrac{5}{24}
$$

of the whole.

Therefore,

$$
\dfrac{5}{6} \div 4 = \dfrac{5}{24} \,
$$
