## Introduction

We can also subtract fractions with unlike denominators using fraction models. To illustrate, let's use a fraction model to find the value of

$$
\dfrac{2}{3} - \dfrac{1}{6}
$$

![](<../Source/1.2.6. Subtracting Fractions With Unlike Denominators Using Models/Images/3ae8ea5a677b70fdc70e1139de0139b6.png>)

The denominators are $3$ and $6$. Since the denominators are not the same, we need to make a common denominator before subtracting.

Since $6$ is a multiple of $3$, we can make a common denominator of ${\color{blue}{6}}$. To do this, we split the shape on the left into $2$ parts vertically. This turns the fraction

$$
\dfrac2 3
$$

into the equivalent fraction

$$
\dfrac 4 {\color{blue}{6}}
$$

![](<../Source/1.2.6. Subtracting Fractions With Unlike Denominators Using Models/Images/6934a96372a64f0302333a8609221ea8.png>)

We can now subtract the fractions:

![](<../Source/1.2.6. Subtracting Fractions With Unlike Denominators Using Models/Images/69b8f91dc8a80c86ecd2b4bb606709ee.png>)

So our answer is

$$
\dfrac 3 6
$$

Notice that we can simplify this answer further by dividing the numerator and denominator by $3$. However, in this lesson, we'll focus just on subtraction and not worry about simplifying the final answers.

## Subtracting Two Fractions When One Split is Required

**Example:** Use the model above to find the value of $\dfrac{3}{4} - \dfrac{4}{8}$.

![](<../Source/1.2.6. Subtracting Fractions With Unlike Denominators Using Models/Images/e0bc34b50a2410ab84c111714b1cc6c0.png>)

**Explanation**

The denominators are $4$ and $8$. Since $8$ is a multiple of $4$, we can make a common denominator of $\color{blue}8$.

We split the shape on the left of the minus sign into $2$ parts horizontally:

![](<../Source/1.2.6. Subtracting Fractions With Unlike Denominators Using Models/Images/443a050f8c3fbbe5dd538229f3fe86cc.png>)

Now, the shape to the left shows

$$
\dfrac{6}{\color{blue}8}
$$

and the shape to the right shows

$$
\dfrac{4}{\color{blue}8}
$$

Next, we subtract the number of shaded parts on the right from the number on the left.

$$
6 - 4 = {\color{red}{2}}
$$

This gives the picture shown below.

![](<../Source/1.2.6. Subtracting Fractions With Unlike Denominators Using Models/Images/3390f18446f9721d46ce597e8dbd9e96.png>)

Therefore, we have:

$$
\dfrac{3}{4} - \dfrac{4}{8} = \dfrac{6}{\color{blue}8} - \dfrac{4}{\color{blue}8} = \dfrac{\color{red}2}{\color{blue}8}
$$

## Subtracting Two Fractions When Multiple Splits are Required

**Example:** Use the model above to find the value of $\dfrac{3}{4} - \dfrac{1}{12}$.

![](<../Source/1.2.6. Subtracting Fractions With Unlike Denominators Using Models/Images/35ba560898b98e7770e7e85794559d01.png>)

**Explanation**

The denominators are $4$ and $12$. Since $12$ is a multiple of $4$, we can make a common denominator of $\color{blue}12$.

We split the shape on the left of the minus sign into $3$ parts vertically.

![](<../Source/1.2.6. Subtracting Fractions With Unlike Denominators Using Models/Images/7d3ba0f68873eaa85db7b982e06d2df9.png>)

Now, the shape to the left shows

$$
\dfrac{9}{\color{blue}12}
$$

and the shape to the right shows

$$
\dfrac{1}{\color{blue}12}
$$

Next, we subtract the number of shaded parts on the right from the number on the left.

$$
9 - 1 = {\color{red}{8}}
$$

This gives the picture shown below.

![](<../Source/1.2.6. Subtracting Fractions With Unlike Denominators Using Models/Images/89004b29689eca7062014a4231c6b26b.png>)

Therefore, we have

$$
\dfrac{3}{4} - \dfrac{1}{12} = \dfrac{9}{\color{blue}12} - \dfrac{1}{\color{blue}12} = \dfrac{\color{red}8}{\color{blue}12}
$$

## Making a Common Denominator by Altering Both Fractions

**Example:** Use the model above to determine the number that is missing in the statement below.
$0$

![](<../Source/1.2.6. Subtracting Fractions With Unlike Denominators Using Models/Images/ae33bb9ee056c2d75f51e4653d3d9f03.png>)

**Explanation**

The denominators are $2$ and $3$. So we can make a common denominator of

$$
2\times 3 = \color{blue}6
$$

We split the shape on the left of the minus sign into $3$ parts horizontally, and we split the shape on the right into $2$ parts vertically:

![](<../Source/1.2.6. Subtracting Fractions With Unlike Denominators Using Models/Images/0fc8089c1757b02251c48fe1ae4077e9.png>)

Now, the shape to the left shows

$$
\dfrac{3}{\color{blue}6}
$$

and the shape to the right shows

$$
\dfrac{2}{\color{blue}6}
$$

Next, we subtract the number of shaded parts on the right from the number on the left.

$$
3-2 = {\color{red}{1}}
$$

This gives the picture shown below.

![](<../Source/1.2.6. Subtracting Fractions With Unlike Denominators Using Models/Images/ff145bbcb2246430be11f82cde7a2c15.png>)

Therefore, we have
$1$

So, the missing number is $\color{red}1$.
