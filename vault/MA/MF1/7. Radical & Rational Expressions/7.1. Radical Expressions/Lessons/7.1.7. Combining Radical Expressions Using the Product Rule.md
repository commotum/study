## Introduction

The **product rule for radicals** states that the square root of a product equals the product of the square roots:

$$
\begin{aligned} \sqrt{a} \cdot \sqrt{b} = \sqrt{ab} \end{aligned}
$$

We can use the product rule to combine radical expressions.

For example, let's suppose we want to combine the radicals in the following expression:

$$
\sqrt{5x} \cdot \sqrt{6z}
$$

To combine the radicals, we apply the product rule as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{5x} \cdot \sqrt{6z} &=  \\
\sqrt{5x \cdot 6z} &=  \\
&= \sqrt{30xz}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Combining Radical Expressions

**Example:** Find an expression that's equal to $\sqrt{5w^3} \cdot \sqrt{uv}$ by combining the radicals.

**Explanation**

We apply the product rule for radicals, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{5w^{3}} \cdot \sqrt{uv} &=  \\
\sqrt{5w^{3} \cdot uv} &=  \\
&= \sqrt{5uvw^{3}}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Combining Radical Expressions Using the Rules of Absolute Value

**Example:** Simplify $\sqrt{x} \cdot \sqrt{x^3}$.

**Explanation**

We perform the multiplication under a single radical and then simplify the result:

$$
\begin{aligned} \sqrt{x} \cdot \sqrt{x^3} &= \ \sqrt{x \cdot x^3} &= \ \sqrt{x^4} \\ &= \mid x^2 \mid \end{aligned}
$$

A squared number is always positive or zero, and the absolute value of any positive (or zero) number is itself, so we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mid x^{2} \mid &= x^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="the-product-rule-for-cube-roots"></a>
## The Product Rule for Cube Roots

The product rule for radicals also works for higher-order roots, like the cube root. In the case of the cube root, we have

$$
\sqrt[3]{ab} = \sqrt[3]{a}\cdot \sqrt[3]{b}
$$

In general,

$$
\sqrt[n]{ab} = \sqrt[n]{a}\cdot \sqrt[n]{b}
$$

---

<a id="simplifying-a-cube-root-expression"></a>
## Simplifying a Cube Root Expression

**Example:** Simplify $\sqrt[3]{16x^2}\cdot \sqrt[3]{4x}$.

**Explanation**

We perform the multiplication under a single radical and then simplify the result:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt[3]{16x^{2}} \cdot \sqrt[3]{4x} &=  \\
\sqrt[3]{16x^{2} \cdot 4x} &=  \\
\sqrt[3]{64x^{3}} &=  \\
\sqrt[3]{(4x)^{3}} &=  \\
&= 4x
\end{aligned}
$$
