## Introduction

Sometimes we need to find a number that is a certain percentage *greater than* a given amount. This corresponds to applying a so-called **percentage increase.**

To apply a percentage increase, we add the increase to $100\%$, convert the result to a decimal multiplier, and then find the product of the multiplier and the original number.

To demonstrate, let's consider the following problem.

> What number is $25\%$ *greater than* $160$?

The key words here are "$\boldsymbol{25\%}$ **greater than.**" This means we need to apply a percentage increase, increasing the original number by $25\%$.

To find a number $25\%$ greater than $160$, we apply a new percentage of

$$
100\% + 25\% = 125\%
$$

This means that our new number is $125\%$ of $160$.

So, first, we convert this percentage to a decimal:

$$
125\% = \dfrac{125}{100} = 1.25
$$

To find a number that is $25\%$ greater than $160$, we multiply $160$ by $1.25{:}$

$$
1.25 \cdot 160 = 200
$$

Therefore, the number that is $25\%$ *greater than* $160$ is $200$.

This method applies to *any* percentage increase, including those greater than $100\%{;}$ add the increase to $100\%$ before converting to a decimal multiplier.

## Applying a Percentage Increase

**Example:** What number is $40\%$ greater than $360$?

**Explanation**

To find a number $40\%$ *greater than* $360$, we apply a new percentage of

$$
100\% + 40\% = 140\%
$$

We begin by converting the percentage to a decimal:

$$
140\% = \dfrac{140}{100} = 1.40
$$

So, to find a number that is $40\%$ greater than $360$, we multiply $360$ by $1.40{:}$

$$
1.40 \cdot 360 = 504
$$

Therefore, the number that is $40\%$ greater than $360$ is $504$.

## Applying a Percentage Increase: Word Problems

**Example:** A t-shirt costs $\[math]18$ before tax. If a $9\%$ sales tax is applied, what is the total cost after tax?

**Explanation**

To find the total cost after tax, we apply a $9\%$ increase to the original price.

Increasing by $9\%$ means that we must apply a new percentage of

$$
100\% + 9\% = 109\% = 1.09
$$

Now, we multiply the original price by $1.09{:}$

$$
18 \times 1.09 = 19.62
$$

So, the total cost after tax is $\[math]19.62$.

## Percentage Increases Greater Than 100%

It's possible to increase a number by $100\%$ or more.

For example, what number is $150\%$ greater than $90$?

To find a number $150\%$ *greater than* $90$, we apply a new percentage of

$$
100\% + 150\% = 250\%
$$

We begin by converting the percentage to a decimal:

$$
250\% = \dfrac{250}{100} = 2.5
$$

So, to find a number that is $150\%$ greater than $90$, we multiply $90$ by $2.5$:

$$
2.5 \cdot 90 = 225
$$

Therefore, the number that is $150\%$ greater than $90$ is $225$.

## Solving Word Problems Involving Percentage Increases Greater Than 100%

**Example:** A gym had $80$ members last year. This year, membership is $400\%$ greater than last year. How many members does the gym have this year?

**Explanation**

To find this year's gym membership, we apply a $400\%$ increase to last year's figure.

Increasing by $400\%$ means that we must apply a new percentage of

$$
100\% + 400\% = 500\%
$$

We begin by converting the percentage to a decimal:

$$
500\% = \dfrac{500}{100} = 5
$$

Now, we multiply the original amount by $5{:}$

$$
80 \times 5 = 400
$$

So, the gym has $\bbox[4pt, border: 1px solid lightgray]{400}$ members this year.
