## Introduction

Percentages are *very* common in everyday life. For example, they are used in retail, finance, and sports! In this lesson, we will learn how to calculate the percentage of a whole number in real-world situations.

For example, suppose Daniel and Luisa traveled $200$ kilometers in their car. If Luisa drove $40 \%$ of the way, how many kilometers did she drive?

To solve this problem, we can use the following formula:

$$
\textrm{part} = \textrm{percentage} \times \textrm{whole}
$$

In this case, the whole is the number of kilometers traveled, which is $200$ kilometers. The percentage is $40 \%$, and the part is the distance Luisa drove.

As usual, we can use two methods to solve this problem:

**Method 1**

First, we convert the percentage to a decimal:

$$
40 \% = 40 \div 100 = 0.4
$$

Applying our formula, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
part &= percentage \cdot whole \\
&= 40% \cdot 200 \\
&= 0.4 \cdot 200 \\
&= 80
\end{aligned}
$$

So Luisa drove $80$ kilometers.

**Method 2**

First, we convert the percentage to a fraction:

$$
40 \% = \dfrac{40}{100} = \dfrac{40 \div 20}{100 \div 20} = \dfrac{2}{5}
$$

Applying the formula, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
part &= percentage \cdot whole \\
&= 40% \cdot 200 \\
&= \frac{2}{5} \cdot 200 \\
&= \frac{400}{5} \\
&= 80
\end{aligned}
$$

Again, we find that Luisa drove $80$ kilometers.

## Finding Part of a Number Given a Whole and a Percentage

**Example:** In a garden, there are $40$ flowers, $30 \%$ of which are roses. How many roses are there in the garden?

**Explanation**

To solve this problem, we can use the following formula:

$$
\textrm{part}= \textrm{percentage}\times \textrm{whole}
$$

In our case, the whole is the number of flowers in the garden, which is $40$ flowers. The percentage is $30 \%$, and the part is the number of roses in the garden.

**Method 1**

First, we convert the percentage to a decimal:

$$
30 \% = 30 \div 100 = 0.3
$$

Applying our formula, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
part &= percentage \cdot whole \\
&= 30% \cdot 40 \\
&= 0.3 \cdot 40 \\
&= 12
\end{aligned}
$$

So, there are $12$ roses in the garden.

**Method 2**

First, we convert the percentage to a fraction:

$$
30 \% = \dfrac{30}{100} = \dfrac{30 \div 10}{100 \div 10} = \dfrac{3}{10}
$$

Applying our formula, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
part &= percentage \cdot whole \\
&= 30% \cdot 40 \\
&= \frac{3}{10} \cdot 40 \\
&= 3 \cdot \frac{40}{10} \\
&= 3 \cdot 4 \\
&= 12
\end{aligned}
$$

So, there are $12$ roses in the garden.

## Finding Part of a Number Given a Whole and a Decimal Percentage

**Example:** Dora works $800$ hours per year. She rests for $12.5 \%$ ​​of her total working hours. How much time does Dora rest per year at work?

**Explanation**

To solve this problem, we can use the following formula:

$$
\textrm{part}= \textrm{percentage}\times \textrm{whole}
$$

In our case, the whole is the total number of hours Dora works yearly, which is $800$ hours. The percentage is $12.5 \%$, and the part is the number of hours she rests.

**Method 1**

First, we convert the percentage to a decimal:

$$
12.5 \% = 12.5 \div 100 = 0.125
$$

Applying our formula, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
part &= percentage \cdot whole \\
&= 12.5% \cdot 800 \\
&= 0.125 \cdot 800 \\
&= 100
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, Dora rests for $100$ hours.

**Method 2**

First, we convert the percentage to a fraction:

$$
12.5 \% = \dfrac{12.5}{100}
$$

Applying our formula, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
part &= percentage \cdot whole \\
&= 12.5% \cdot 800 \\
&= \frac{12.5}{100} \cdot 800 \\
&= 12.5 \cdot \frac{800}{100} \\
&= 12.5 \cdot 8 \\
&= 100
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, Dora rests for $100$ hours.

## Finding Part of a Number Given a Whole and a Percentage Greater Than 100%

**Example:** Marcus has a tank with a capacity of $1,500$ liters. He decides to buy a second tank whose capacity is $130 \%$ of the capacity of the first tank. What is the capacity of the second tank?

**Explanation**

To solve this problem, we can use the following formula:

$$
\textrm{part}= \textrm{percentage}\times \textrm{whole}
$$

In our case, the whole is the capacity of the first tank, which is $1,500$ liters. The percentage is $130 \%$, and the part is the capacity of the second tank.

**Method 1**

First, we convert the percentage to a decimal:

$$
130 \% = 130 \div 100 = 1.3
$$

Applying our formula, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
part &= percentage \cdot whole \\
&= 130% \cdot 1, 500 \\
&= 1.3 \cdot 1, 500 \\
&= 1, 950
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the second tank has a capacity of $1,950$ liters.

**Method 2**

First, we convert the percentage to a fraction:

$$
130 \% = \dfrac{130}{100}
$$

Applying our formula, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
part &= percentage \cdot whole \\
&= 130% \cdot 1, 500 \\
&= \frac{130}{100} \cdot 1, 500 \\
&= 130 \cdot \frac{1, 500}{100} \\
&= 130 \cdot 15 \\
&= 1, 950
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the second tank has a capacity of $1,950$ liters.
