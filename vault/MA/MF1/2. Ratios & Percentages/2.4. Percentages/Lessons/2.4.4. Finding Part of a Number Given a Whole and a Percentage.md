## Introduction

We can find a percentage part of a number using the following formula:

$$
\textrm{part} = \textrm{percentage} \times \textrm{whole}
$$

To illustrate, let's calculate $16 \%$ of $300$.

Here, the whole is $300$ and the percentage is $16 \%$. So, the part is given by

$$
\textrm{part} = 16\% \times 300
$$

But how do we multiply a percentage by a number? We have two methods of doing this. Let's apply both and check that they give the same answer.

**Method 1**

First, we convert the percentage to a decimal:

$$
16\% = 16 \div 100 = 0.16
$$

Then, we apply our formula as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
part &= percentage \cdot whole \\
&= 16% \cdot 300 \\
&= 0.16 \cdot 300 \\
&= 48
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, $16\%$ of $300$ is $48$.

**Method 2**

First, we convert the percentage to a fraction:

$$
16\% = \dfrac{16}{100}
$$

Then, we apply our formula as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
part &= percentage \cdot whole \\
&= 16% \cdot 300 \\
&= \frac{16}{100} \cdot 300 \\
&= 16 \cdot \frac{300}{100} \\
&= 16 \cdot 3 \\
&= 48
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, $16 \%$ of $300$ is $48$.

## Finding Part of a Number Given a Whole and a Percentage

**Example:** What is $4 \%$ of $35$?

**Explanation**

To solve this problem, we can use the following formula:

$$
\textrm{part}= \textrm{percentage}\times \textrm{whole}
$$

In our case, the whole is $35$, and the percentage is $4 \%$.

**Method 1**

First, we convert the percentage to a decimal:

$$
4 \% = 4 \div 100 = 0.04
$$

Then, we apply our formula as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
part &= percentage \cdot whole \\
&= 4% \cdot 35 \\
&= 0.04 \cdot 35 \\
&= 1.4
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, $4 \%$ of $35$ is $1.4$.

**Method 2**

First, we convert the percentage to a fraction:

$$
4 \% = \dfrac{4}{100}
$$

Then, we apply our formula as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
part &= percentage \cdot whole \\
&= 4% \cdot 35 \\
&= \frac{4}{100} \cdot 35 \\
&= \frac{140}{100} \\
&= 1.4
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, $4 \%$ of $35$ is $1.4$.

## Finding Part of a Number Given a Whole and a Percentage Greater Than 100%

**Example:** What is $140 \%$ of $30$?

**Explanation**

To solve this problem, we can use the following formula:

$$
\textrm{part}= \textrm{percentage}\times \textrm{whole}
$$

In our case, the whole is $30$, and the percentage is $140\%$.

**Method 1**

First, we convert the percentage to a decimal:

$$
140 \% = 140 \div 100 = 1.4
$$

Then, we apply our formula as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
part &= percentage \cdot whole \\
&= 140% \cdot 30 \\
&= 1.4 \cdot 30 \\
&= 42
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, $140 \%$ of $30$ is $42$.

**Method 2**

First, we convert the percentage to a fraction:

$$
140 \% = \dfrac{140}{100} = \dfrac{140 \div 20}{100\div 20} = \dfrac{7}{5}
$$

Then, we apply our formula as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
part &= percentage \cdot whole \\
&= 140% \cdot 30 \\
&= \frac{7}{5} \cdot 30 \\
&= 7 \cdot \frac{30}{5} \\
&= 7 \cdot 6 \\
&= 42
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, $140 \%$ of $30$ is $42$.

## Finding Part of a Number Given a Whole and a Decimal Percentage

**Example:** What is $3.6 \%$ of $25$?

**Explanation**

To solve this problem, we can use the following formula:

$$
\textrm{part}= \textrm{percentage}\times \textrm{whole}
$$

In our case, the whole is $25$, and the percentage is $3.6 \%$.

**Method 1**

First, we convert the percentage to a decimal:

$$
3.6 \% = 3.6 \div 100 = 0.036
$$

Then, we apply our formula as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
part &= percentage \cdot whole \\
&= 3.6% \cdot 25 \\
&= 0.036 \cdot 25 \\
&= 0.9
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, $3.6 \%$ of $25$ is $0.9$.

**Method 2**

First, we convert the percentage to a fraction:

$$
3.6 \% = \dfrac{3.6}{100} = \dfrac{3.6 \times 10}{100 \times 10} = \dfrac{36}{1,000}
$$

Then, we apply our formula as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
part &= percentage \cdot whole \\
&= 3.6% \cdot 25 \\
&= \frac{36}{1, 000} \cdot 25 \\
&= \frac{900}{1, 000} \\
&= 0.9
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, $3.6 \%$ of $25$ is $0.9$.
