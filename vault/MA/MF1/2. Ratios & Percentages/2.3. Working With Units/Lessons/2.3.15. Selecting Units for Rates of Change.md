## Introduction

Sometimes, when we are choosing an appropriate unit, we are not given any formula to use.

This often occurs when we measure the **rate of change** of something. A rate of change measures how much something changes as we change something else.

In such cases, we can use a quotient to work out an appropriate unit. The numerator is what we measure the change of, and the denominator is what we change:

$$
\dfrac{\color{blue}\textrm{what we measure the change of}}{\color{red}\textrm{what we change}}
$$

For instance, suppose that a newly opened store wants to measure the rate at which the number of customers increases each day.

We *measure the change* of the number of customers, as *we change* the number of days, so the most appropriate unit is

$$
\dfrac{\color{blue}\textrm{customers}}{\color{red}\textrm{day}}
$$

or, customers per day.

## Choosing Appropriate Units When Base Units Are Not Required

**Example:** A tank is being filled with water. What is an appropriate unit for measuring the rate of change of water volume in the tank, as time goes on?

**Explanation**

We *measure the change* of the volume as *we change* the amount of time. Consequently, any appropriate unit for measuring the rate of change of water volume in the tank must take the form

$$
\dfrac{\textrm{unit of volume}}{\textrm{unit of time}}
$$

Some examples of appropriate units include

$$
\dfrac{\textrm{liters}}{\textrm{hour}}
$$

$$
\dfrac{\textrm{m}^3}{\textrm{min}}
$$

and

$$
\dfrac{\textrm{cm}^3}{\textrm{s}}
$$

## Choosing Appropriate Units When Base Units Are Required

**Example:** Density $D$ is the rate that mass $M$ changes as volume $V$ changes. What are the units of density in terms of base units?

**Explanation**

We *measure the change* in the mass, as *we change* the volume. Consequently, any appropriate unit must take the form

$$
\dfrac{\textrm{unit of mass}}{\textrm{unit of volume}}
$$

The base unit for mass is $\textrm{kg}$, and the base unit for volume is $\textrm{m}^3$. Therefore, the units of density are

$$
\dfrac{\textrm{kg}}{\textrm{m}^3}
$$

or, kilograms per cubic meter.
