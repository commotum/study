## Introduction

The **natural numbers** are the positive whole numbers:
${\color{blue}1},\: {\color{blue}2},\: {\color{blue}3},\: {\color{blue}4},\: {\color{blue}5},\ldots$
We also refer to them as the **counting numbers**.

There's some debate among mathematicians about whether zero should be considered a natural number. However, for our purposes, zero is *not* a natural number.

---

<a id="classifying-numbers-as-natural"></a>
## Classifying Numbers as Natural

**Example:** Which of the following are natural numbers?

1. $-\dfrac1 3$
2. $0$
3. $4$

**Explanation**

The natural numbers consist of all positive whole numbers:

$$
{\color{blue}1},\: {\color{blue}2},\: {\color{blue}3},\: {\color{blue}4}, \:{\color{blue}5},\ldots
$$

Of the given numbers, $4$ is a natural number, but

$$
-\dfrac13
$$

and $0$ are not.

Therefore, the correct answer is "III only."

## The Integers

When we extend the natural numbers by joining them with zero and the whole negative numbers, we get the **integers**.
$\overbrace{\ldots, {\color{red}-5},\: {\color{red}-4},\: {\color{red}-3},\: {\color{red}-2},\: {\color{red}-1},\: {\color{red}0}, \: \!\underbrace{{\color{blue}1},\: {\color{blue}2}, \: {\color{blue}3},\: {\color{blue}4},\: {\color{blue}5},\ldots}_{\large\textrm{Natural numbers}}}^{\large\textrm{Integers}}$

We can represent the relationship between the natural numbers and integers using an inclusion diagram:

![](<../Source/3.3.8. Natural Numbers, Integers, and Rational Numbers/Images/c13f4c827ad9be617efa7cebabfed701.png>)

Notice that the natural numbers are *contained within* the integers. In other words, *every* natural number is also an integer.

Therefore, the correct answer is "I and III only."

<a id="the-rational-numbers"></a>
## The Rational Numbers

The **rational numbers** consist of all numbers that can be written as the ratio of two integers. For example:

$$
{\color{purple}-\dfrac{1}{2}}, \quad {\color{purple}\dfrac{1}{2}}, \quad {\color{purple}\dfrac{1}{3}}, \quad {\color{purple}-\dfrac{2}{3}}, \quad {\color{purple}\dfrac{2}{3}}
$$

Every integer is also a rational number because every integer can be expressed as a ratio of two integers. For example,

$$
{\color{blue}2} = {\color{black}\dfrac{2}{1}}, \qquad {\color{red}-9} = {\color{black}\dfrac{(-9)}{\phantom{-}1}}
$$

Likewise, any mixed number is a rational number because it can be written as an improper fraction. For example,

$$
2 \, \dfrac{1}{3}
$$

is a rational number because

$$
2 \, \dfrac{1}{3} = \dfrac{2 \times 3 + 1}{3} = \dfrac{7}{3}
$$

We can now add the rational numbers to our inclusion diagram.

![](<../Source/3.3.8. Natural Numbers, Integers, and Rational Numbers/Images/1def656c2c3b8452cec425563ee6bf73.png>)

Notice that the integers (and therefore the natural numbers) are contained within the rational numbers.

<a id="classifying-numbers-as-rational"></a>
## Classifying Numbers as Rational

**Example:** Which of the following statements are true?

1. $2$ is a natural number
2. $2$ is a rational number
3. $\dfrac12$ is a rational number

**Explanation**

Let's recall our definitions.

- The natural numbers consist of all positive whole numbers: ${\color{blue}1},\: {\color{blue}2},\: {\color{blue}3},\: {\color{blue}4}, \:{\color{blue}5},\ldots$
- The integers consist of all of the positive and negative whole numbers, and zero: $\ldots, {\color{red}-3},\: {\color{red}-2},\: {\color{red}-1},\: {\color{black}0},\: {\color{blue}1},\: {\color{blue}2},\: {\color{blue}3}, \ldots$
- The rationals consist of all numbers that can be written as the ratio of two integers.

Now, let's go through each number in turn.

- $2$ is a natural number.
- $2$ is a rational number because it can be written as $\dfrac{2}{1}$.
- $\dfrac12$ is a rational number because it is the ratio of two integers.

Therefore, the correct answer is "I, II, and III."

<a id="repeating-decimals"></a>
## Repeating Decimals

Let's consider the following rational number:

$$
\dfrac13
$$

We can find the decimal representation of this rational number by dividing $1$ by $3$ using long division, as follows:

| $\,\,0 \,. 3 {\:\phantom{\mid}} 3 {\:\phantom{\mid}} 3$$\:\ldots$$3$$\!\require{enclose}\enclose{longdiv}{1 {\,. \,} 0 {\:\phantom{\mid}} 0 {\:\phantom{\mid}} 0}$$-\!\!\!\!\!\!\!$$9$$1$$0$$-\!\!\!\!\!$$9$$1$$0$$-\!\!\!\!\!$$9$$1$$\,\ldots$ |
| --- |

Note that, in this case

- the long division process never ends, and
- the digit $3$ after the decimal point repeats *forever*.

Therefore, we have that

$$
\dfrac13 = 0.333\,333\ldots
$$

This is an example of a **repeating decimal**. A repeating decimal is a decimal where the digits after the decimal point continue forever but in a repeating pattern.

It's convenient to use the following special notation to indicate decimals that repeat indefinitely.

$$
0.\overline{3} = 0.333\,333\ldots
$$

Some decimals have two or more digits that repeat indefinitely. For example:

$$
\begin{aligned}
0.78^(―) &= 0.78787878… \\
0.675^(―) &= 0.675675675…
\end{aligned}
$$

Some repeating decimals have non-repeating digits before the repeating part. For example:

$$
0.333\overline{67} = 0.333\,67\,67\,67\,67\ldots
$$

The key takeaway for this section is that every repeating decimal is rational! In other words, every repeating decimal can be expressed as a ratio of two integers.

For example:

$$
\begin{aligned}
0.3^(―) &= \frac{1}{3} \\
0.78^(―) &= \frac{26}{33} \\
0.33367^(―) &= \frac{16517}{49500}
\end{aligned}
$$

In a future lesson, we'll learn how to express a repeating decimal as a fraction. For now, you can verify these results using a calculator.

<a id="identifying-rational-decimals"></a>
## Identifying Rational Decimals

**Example:** Which of the following numbers are rational?

1. $1.577$
2. $8.163452 \ldots$
3. $2.\overline{73}$

**Explanation**

Let's examine each of our numbers in turn.

- $1.577$ is a rational number because it can be expressed as a ratio of two integers:
$1.577 = \dfrac{1\,577}{1\,000}$
- $8.163452 \ldots$ is not a rational number. The notation "$\ldots$" tells us that digits after the decimal point extend forever. However, there are no repeating digits.
- $2.\overline{73}$ is a rational number. The notation $\overline{73}$ tells us that the digits $7$ and $3$ after the decimal point repeat indefinitely.
$2.\overline{73} = 2.73737373\ldots$
Any decimal with repeating digits can be represented as a ratio of two integers. In this case,
$2.\overline{73} = \dfrac{271}{99}$.

Therefore, the correct answer is "I and III only."
