## Introduction

A **composite polygon** is a polygon that is made up of other polygons, such as triangles, parallelograms, rectangles, squares, and others.

For example, the polygon below is composite. How can we find its area?

![](<../Source/13.3.4. Areas and Perimeters of Composite Shapes/Images/e9ee72fb423022655fed82b4d3bfe887.png>)

First, notice that the polygon consists of four squares and one rectangle in the middle.

![](<../Source/13.3.4. Areas and Perimeters of Composite Shapes/Images/4295c67a47a9d643e16621d0f7b0e8ee.png>)

To calculate the area of the composite polygon above, we add the areas of the four squares and the rectangle:

$$
A = 2A_{1} + 2A_{2} + A_{3}
$$

The areas of the squares and the rectangle are

$$
\begin{aligned}
A_{1} &= 3^{2} = 9, \\
A_{2} &= 2^{2} = 4, \\
A_{3} &= 3 \cdot 2 = 6
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the area of the entire figure is

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= 2 \cdot 9 + 2 \cdot 4 + 6 \\
&= 18 + 8 + 6 \\
&= 32
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding the Area of a Composite Shape Made Up of Rectangles

**Example:** Find the area of ​​the shaded region in the figure above.

![](<../Source/13.3.4. Areas and Perimeters of Composite Shapes/Images/a97fd26926e7eea8f33bcf21a55cc0f4.png>)

**Explanation**

Note that the figure is formed by $7$ squares with the same dimensions. The area of ​​each square is

$$
\mathcal{A}_s= s^2 = 3^2 = 9 \textrm{in}^2
$$

Therefore, the area of ​​the shaded region is given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= 7 \cdot A_{s} \\
&= 7 \cdot 9 \\
&= 63in^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding the Perimeter of a Composite Shape Made Up of Rectangles

**Example:** Find the perimeter of ​​the figure above.

![](<../Source/13.3.4. Areas and Perimeters of Composite Shapes/Images/8793579490153de2ebd4cf105c3b218f.png>)

*(Note: Figure not drawn to scale)*

**Explanation**

The figure is composed of three squares, $R_1$, $R_2$, and $R_3$, and one rectangle, $R_4$.

![](<../Source/13.3.4. Areas and Perimeters of Composite Shapes/Images/acda490421e35221c5acb19b2547692a.png>)

The perimeter of the figure is determined by the sum of the lengths of

- $3$ sides of $R_1$ (each of length $1\,\textrm{cm}$),
- $1$ side of $R_2$ (of length $1\,\textrm{cm}$),
- $3$ sides of $R_3$ (of length $1\,\textrm{cm}$),
- $3$ sides of $R_4$ (two of length $3\,\textrm{cm}$ and one of length $1\,\textrm{cm}$).

Therefore, the perimeter is given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
p &= 3 \cdot 1 + 1 + 3 \cdot 1 + (3 + 1 + 3) \\
&= 3 + 1 + 3 + 7 \\
&= 14cm
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding the Area of a Composite Shape Made of Triangles and Trapezoids

**Example:** The figure above is composed of a trapezoid and an isosceles triangle. Find the area of ​​the figure.

![](<../Source/13.3.4. Areas and Perimeters of Composite Shapes/Images/1d84bc3ec2f5b6e1fb051a3baf436789.png>)

*(Note: Figure not drawn to scale)*

**Explanation**

The area of ​​the trapezoid is

$$
\begin{aligned}
A_{1} &= ((2 + 4) \cdot 1)/(2) = 3cm^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

The area of ​​the isosceles triangle is

$$
\begin{aligned}
A_{2} &= \frac{4 \cdot 3}{2} = 6cm^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the area of ​​the entire figure is

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= A_{1} + A_{2} \\
&= 3 + 6 \\
&= 9cm^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding the Perimeter of a Composite Shape Given Its Area

**Example:** The figure above is obtained by taking a square with an area of $16\,\textrm{cm}^2$ and cutting out an equilateral triangle. Find the perimeter of ​​the figure.

![](<../Source/13.3.4. Areas and Perimeters of Composite Shapes/Images/2c9b28799f3f62222831c0b0253de6df.png>)

*(Note: Figure not draw to scale)*

**Explanation**

Let $a$ be the length of the sides of the square. Then, the length of each side of the triangle is also $a$ since it is equilateral and the square and the triangle have a common side.

![](<../Source/13.3.4. Areas and Perimeters of Composite Shapes/Images/07602bf09bdda323b4ad7c3f8c708ecd.png>)

We're given that the area of a square is $16\,\textrm{cm}^2$. Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
a^{2} &= 16 \\
a &= 4cm
\end{aligned}
$$

The perimeter of the figure is the sum of the lengths of

- $3$ sides of the square (each of length $a$), and
- $2$ sides of the triangle (each of length $a$).

So, we have

$$
p = 3a + 2a = 5a
$$

Finally, substituting

$$
a=4\,\textrm{cm}
$$

into the above, we get that the perimeter of ​​the given shape is

$$
\begin{aligned}
p &= 5a \\
&= 5 \cdot 4 \\
&= 20cm
\end{aligned}
$$
