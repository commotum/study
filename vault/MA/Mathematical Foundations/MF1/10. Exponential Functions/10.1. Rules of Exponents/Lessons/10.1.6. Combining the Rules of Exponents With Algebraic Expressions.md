## Introduction

Sometimes, we might need to use more than one rule of exponents to simplify an expression. First, let's remind ourselves of all of the rules of exponents.

- Radicals as fractional exponents: $\sqrt[n]{x} = x^{1/n}$
- The product rule: $a^n \cdot a^m = a^{n+m}$
- The quotient rule: $\dfrac{a^n}{a^m} = a^{n-m}$
- The power rule: $\left(a^m \right)^n = a^{mn}$
- The power of product rule: $(ab)^n = a^nb^n$
- The power of quotient rule: $\left(\dfrac a b \right)^n = \dfrac{a^n}{b^n}$

As an example, let's simplify the following expression:

$$
\left(u^2 v\right)^3
$$

We start by using the power of product rule:

$$
(u^{2}v)^{3} = (u^{2})^{3} \cdot v^{3}
$$

Then, we use the power rule:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(u^{2})^{3} \cdot v^{3} &=  \\
u^{(2 \cdot 3)} \cdot v^{3} &=  \\
u^{6} \cdot v^{3} &=  \\
&= u^{6}v^{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Combining the Rules of Exponents Without Fractions

**Example:** Simplify the expression $\left(x^2y\right)^3\cdot x^2y$.

**Explanation**

We start by using the power of product rule:

$$
(x^{2}y)^{3} \cdot x^{2}y =; (x^{2})^{3} \cdot y^{3} \cdot x^{2}y
$$

Then, we use the power rule:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^{2 \cdot 3} \cdot y^{3} \cdot x^{2}y &=  \\
&= x^{6} \cdot y^{3} \cdot x^{2}y
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we use the product rule:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^{6} \cdot y^{3} \cdot x^{2}y = \\
&= x^{6} \cdot x^{2} \cdot y^{3} \cdot y = \\
x^{6 + 2} \cdot y^{3 + 1} &=  \\
x^{8} \cdot y^{4} &=  \\
&= x^{8}y^{4}
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="combining-the-rules-of-exponents-with-fractions"></a>
## Combining the Rules of Exponents With Fractions

**Example:** Given that $a=2x^3$ and $b=\dfrac{x}{y}$, express $ab^2$ in terms of $x$ and $y$.

**Explanation**

First, we substitute for $a$ and $b$:

$$
ab^{2} = 2x^{3}(\frac{x}{y})^{2}
$$

Then, we apply the power of quotient rule:

$$
2x^{3}(\frac{x}{y})^{2} = 2x^{3}(\frac{x^{2}}{y^{2}})
$$

Finally, we multiply and apply the product rule:

$$
\begin{aligned}
2x^{3}(\frac{x^{2}}{y^{2}}) &=  \\
\frac{2x^{3} \cdot x^{2}}{y^{2}} &=  \\
\frac{2x^{3 + 2}}{y^{2}} &=  \\
&= \frac{2x^{5}}{y^{2}}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Rules of Exponents and Radicals

To simplify the expression $x\cdot \sqrt[3]{x^2}$, we first rewrite the radical as a fractional exponent:

$$
x \cdot \sqrt[3]{x^2} = x \cdot \left(x^2 \right)^{1/3}
$$

Now, we apply the power rule for exponents:

$$
x \cdot \left(x^2 \right)^{1/3} = x \cdot x^{2(1/3)} = x \cdot x^{2/3}
$$

Then, we apply the product rule:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x \cdot x^{2/3} &=  \\
x^{1 + 2/3} &=  \\
&= x^{5/3}
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we express the result as a radical:

$$
x^{5/3} = \sqrt[3]{x^5}
$$

Notice that, by combining the radicals as fractional exponents and power rules, we get the following formula:

$$
\sqrt[n]{x^m} = x^{m/n}
$$

<a id="combining-the-rules-of-exponents-with-radicals"></a>
## Combining the Rules of Exponents With Radicals

**Example:** Simplify the expression $\dfrac {\sqrt{y}} {\sqrt[3]{y}}$.

**Explanation**

First, we rewrite the radicals as fractional exponents:

$$
\frac{\sqrt{y}}{\sqrt[3]{y}} = \frac{y^{1/2}}{y^{1/3}}
$$

Then, we apply the quotient rule:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{y^{1/2}}{y^{1/3}} &=  \\
y^{1/2 - 1/3} &=  \\
y^{3/6 - 2/6} &=  \\
&= y^{1/6}
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we express the result as a radical:

$$
y^{1/6} = \sqrt[6]{y}
$$
