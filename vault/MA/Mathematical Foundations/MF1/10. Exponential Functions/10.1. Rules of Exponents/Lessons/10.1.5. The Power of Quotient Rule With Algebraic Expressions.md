## Introduction

The **power of quotient rule** states that if we want to raise the quotient

$$
\dfrac{x}{y}
$$

to some power $n$, we must raise the numerator and denominator to the power $n$ as follows:

$$
\left(\dfrac{x}{y}\right)^n=\dfrac{x^n}{y^n}
$$

For example,

$$
\left(\dfrac{x}{y}\right)^4=\dfrac{x^4}{y^4}
$$

To see why the power of quotient rule is true, let's take the above example and work it out again the long way. We get the same result:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(\frac{x}{y})^{4} &=  \\
\frac{x}{y} \cdot \frac{x}{y} \cdot \frac{x}{y} \cdot \frac{x}{y} &=  \\
\frac{x \cdot x \cdot x \cdot x}{y \cdot y \cdot y \cdot y} &=  \\
&= \frac{x^{4}}{y^{4}}
\end{aligned}
$$

The power of quotient rule is just a shortcut for the process above.

<a id="using-the-power-of-quotient-rule-with-one-fraction"></a>
## Using the Power of Quotient Rule with One Fraction

**Example:** Calculate $\left(\dfrac{2x}{3}\right)^4$.

**Explanation**

First, we apply the power of quotient rule:

$$
(\frac{2x}{3})^{4} = ((2x)^{4})/(3^{4})
$$

Then, we apply the power rule and simplify:

$$
\begin{aligned}
((2x)^{4})/(3^{4}) &=  \\
\frac{2^{4}x^{4}}{3^{4}} &=  \\
&= \frac{16x^{4}}{81}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Using the Power of Quotient Rule with Two Fractions

**Example:** Simplify $\left(\dfrac{y}{2}\right)^3 \left(\dfrac{2y}{3}\right)^2$.

**Explanation**

First, we apply the power of quotient rule:

$$
(\frac{y}{2})^{3}(\frac{2y}{3})^{2} = \frac{y^{3}}{2^{3}} \cdot ((2y)^{2})/(3^{2})
$$

Next, we apply the power rule:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{y^{3}}{2^{3}} \cdot ((2y)^{2})/(3^{2}) &=  \\
\frac{y^{3}}{8} \cdot \frac{2^{2}y^{2}}{9} &=  \\
&= \frac{y^{3}}{8} \cdot \frac{4y^{2}}{9}
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we apply the product rule and simplify:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{y^{3}}{8} \cdot \frac{4y^{2}}{9} &=  \\
(4y^{(3 + 2)})/(72) &=  \\
\frac{4y^{5}}{72} &=  \\
&= \frac{y^{5}}{18}
\end{aligned}
$$
