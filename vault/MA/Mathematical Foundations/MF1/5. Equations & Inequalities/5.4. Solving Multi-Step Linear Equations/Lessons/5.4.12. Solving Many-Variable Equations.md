## Introduction

Sometimes we might be given an equation that contains more than one variable and asked to isolate one of the variables. This means that we need to put the variable we're interested in on one side of the equation (on its own) and everything else on the other.

For example, suppose we want to isolate $n$ in the equation

$$
2n - k = q + 5k
$$

The equation has three variables, $n$, $k$, and $q$. However, we will treat the variables $k$ and $q$ as constants because we are solving for $n$ only. We can use the addition and multiplication principles to solve for $n\mathbin{.}$

First, we add $k$ to both sides:

$$
\begin{aligned} 2n - k &= q + 5k \\[5pt] 2n - k + k &= q + 5k +k \\[5pt] 2n &= q + 6k \end{aligned}
$$

Then, we divide both sides of the equation by $2$:

$$
\begin{aligned} 2n &= q + 6k \\[5pt] \dfrac {2n} {2} &= \dfrac {q+6k} {2} \\ n &= \dfrac {q+6k} {2} \end{aligned}
$$

Because the equation is now solved for $n$, we say that we've made $n$ **the subject** of the equation.

## Solving Equations When the Variables are Separate

**Example:** Solve for $x$ where $2x - y = 5y - z$.

**Explanation**

We need to isolate $x$, so we will treat the other variables $y$ and $z$ as constants. We can use the addition and multiplication principles to solve for $x$.

First, we add $y$ to both sides:

$$
\begin{aligned} 2x - y &= 5y - z \\[5pt] 2x - y + y &= 5y - z + y \\[5pt] 2x &= 6y - z \end{aligned}
$$

Then, we divide both sides by $2$:

$$
\begin{aligned} 2x &= 6y - z \\[5pt] \dfrac {2x} 2 &= \dfrac{6y - z} {2} \\[5pt] x &= \dfrac{6y - z} {2} \end{aligned}
$$

## Solving Equations When the Variables are Multiplied

**Example:** The energy $E$ of a body at rest is related to its mass $m$ by the equation $E=mC$, where $C$ is a constant. What is an expression for the mass ($m$) in terms of the energy ($E$) and the constant ($C$)?

**Explanation**

We need to isolate $m$, so we will treat the other variables $E$ and $C$ as constants. Since $m$ is being multiplied by $C$, we need to divide both sides of the equation by $C$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
E &= mC \\
\frac{E}{C} &= \frac{mC}{C} \\
\frac{E}{C} &= m
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus,

$$
m = \dfrac E C
$$

**Note:** This result assumes that

$$
C\neq 0
$$

## Solving Equations Requiring Manipulation of Binomials

**Example:** The total energy $E$ of a body moving with a (squared) velocity $V$ can be approximated using the equation $E = mC + \dfrac{1}{2}mV$. Find an expression for $V$ in terms of the other quantities.

**Explanation**

We need to isolate $V$, so we will treat the other variables $E$, $C$, and $m$ as constants. We can use the addition and multiplication principles to solve for $V$.

First, we subtract $mC$ from both sides of the equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
E &= mC + \frac{1}{2}mV \\
E - mC &= mC + \frac{1}{2}mV - mC \\
E - mC &= \frac{1}{2}mV
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we multiply by $2$ to get rid of the fraction:

$$
\begin{aligned}
E - mC &= \frac{1}{2}mV \\
2 \cdot (E - mC) &= 2 \cdot \frac{1}{2}mV \\
2(E - mC) &= mV
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we divide by $m$ to get $V$ alone:

$$
\begin{aligned}
2(E - mC) &= mV \\
(2(E - mC))/(m) &= \frac{mV}{m} \\
(2(E - mC))/(m) &= V
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus,

$$
V = \dfrac{2(E-mC)}{m}
$$

**Note:** This result assumes that

$$
m\neq 0
$$
