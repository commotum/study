## Introduction

Let's have a look at the diagram below.

![](<../Source/9.1.9. Stretches of Absolute Value Graphs/Images/442c602e99f0ab84b8e060359f032643.png>)

The green curve

$$
y = 3 \mid x \mid
$$

represents a **vertical scaling** of

$$
y = \mid x \mid
$$

by a factor of $3$. It takes all of the $y$-coordinates on

$$
y= \mid x \mid
$$

and multiplies them by $3$. This has the effect of **stretching** the curve upwards.

Now consider the following diagram:

![](<../Source/9.1.9. Stretches of Absolute Value Graphs/Images/bb2478e15f0b5d7b1b9e9faf9de852c6.png>)

The blue curve

$$
y = \dfrac 1 2 \left|x\right|
$$

is a vertical scaling of

$$
y = \mid x \mid
$$

by a factor of

$$
\dfrac{1}{2}
$$

. It takes all of the $y$-coordinates on

$$
y= \mid x \mid
$$

and multiplies them by

$$
\dfrac{1}{2}
$$

This has the effect of squashing, or **compressing**, the curve downwards.

In general, the curve

$$
y = k \mid x \mid
$$

is a vertical scaling of the curve

$$
y= \mid x \mid
$$

by a scale factor of $k$ units. If $k>1$ we have stretching, and if $0 < k < 1$ we have compression (squashing).

## Graphs of Scaled Absolute Value Functions

**Example:** The graph of $y=k \mid x \mid$ is shown below. What is the value of $k$?

![](<../Source/9.1.9. Stretches of Absolute Value Graphs/Images/054a28af666dd5bb00d14d82bda35bb0.png>)

**Explanation**

To find the value of $k$, we can pick a point on the curve

$$
y=k \mid x \mid
$$

and substitute it into the equation.

Notice that the curve passes through the point $(1,2)$. So, we can substitute $x=1$ and $y=2$ into the equation and solve for $k$, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
y &= k \mid x \mid \\
2 &= k \cdot \mid 1 \mid \\
2 &= k \cdot 1 \\
2 &= k
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, $k =2$.

## Graphs of Scaled and Reflected Absolute Value Functions

**Example:** Sketch the graph of $y = -\dfrac53 \mid x \mid$.

**Explanation**

To get the graph of

$$
y=-\dfrac53 \mid x \mid
$$

we proceed as follows:

- Take the graph of $y=-\mid x \mid$.
- Stretch the graph vertically by a scale factor of $\dfrac53$ to get the graph of $y=-\dfrac53 \mid x \mid$.

![](<../Source/9.1.9. Stretches of Absolute Value Graphs/Images/dc7892db1c5e11a241ad22f6554e26e5.png>)

The result is the solid blue curve, shown above.

Notice that the point $(3,-5)$ lies on the curve because when $x=3$, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
y &= -\frac{5}{3} \cdot \mid 3 \mid \\
&=-\frac{5}{3} \cdot 3 \\
&=-5
\end{aligned}
$$

## Graphing a Scaled Absolute Value Function Using the Rules of Absolute Value

**Example:** Plot the graph of $y = \mid 2x \mid$.

**Explanation**

First, let's re-write the equation using the rules of absolute value, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
y &= \mid 2x \mid \\
&=\begin{vmatrix}2 & \cdot & x\end{vmatrix} \\
&= 2 \mid x \mid
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, to plot the graph of

$$
y=2 \mid x \mid
$$

we proceed as follows:

- Take the graph of $y= \mid x \mid$.
- Stretch it vertically by a scale factor of $2$ to get the graph of $y=2 \mid x \mid$.

The result is the solid blue curve, shown below.

![](<../Source/9.1.9. Stretches of Absolute Value Graphs/Images/0bdd4e566b179309403f1c9afd9dd3f5.png>)

Notice that the point $(1,2)$ lies on the curve because when $x=1$, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
y &= \mid 2 \cdot 1 \mid \\
&= \mid 2 \mid \\
&= 2
\end{aligned}
$$
