## Introduction

The **greatest common factor** (or $\textrm{GCF}$) of two integers is the largest positive integer that divides both of them.

For example, the greatest common factor of $12$ and $30$ is $6$. This is because $6$ is the greatest integer that divides *both* $12$ *and* $30$. We can write this as

$$
\textrm{GCF}(12,30) = 6
$$

There is a general technique that we can use to find the greatest common factor of any two integers. All we need to do is find the prime factorization of both numbers and multiply the prime factors that appear in *both* factorizations.

For example, the prime factorizations of $12$ and $30$ are as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
12 &= 2_(―) \cdot 2 \cdot 3_(―) \\
30 &= 2_(―) \cdot 3_(―) \cdot 5
\end{aligned}
$$

Notice that $12$ and $30$ both have the factors of ${\color{blue}2}$ and ${\color{red}3}$ in common. Therefore,

$$
\textrm{GCF}(12,30) = {\color{blue}2}\cdot {\color{red}3} = 6
$$

Sometimes, we write "$\textrm{gcd}$" instead of $\textrm{GCF}$. They mean the same thing, but $\textrm{gcd}$ stands for **greatest common divisor.**

## Finding the Greatest Common Factor of Two Integers

**Example:** Calculate $\textrm{GCF}(420,84)$.

**Explanation**

To find the greatest common factor $(\textrm{GCF})$ of two numbers, we first find the prime factorization of both numbers.

- First, let's find the prime decomposition of $420{:}$![](<../Source/3.3.6. Finding Greatest Common Factors Using Prime Factorization/Images/09c7d92556704f2c7ad9551ac350a7f1.png>)
So, $420=2\cdot 2\cdot 3\cdot 5\cdot 7$.
- Next, we find the prime factorization of $84{:}$![](<../Source/3.3.6. Finding Greatest Common Factors Using Prime Factorization/Images/807db3d8672d6902eb8997189191ff50.png>)
So, $84=2\cdot 2\cdot 3\cdot 7$.

Comparing the decompositions side-by-side, we pair off the factors as much as possible:

$$
\begin{aligned}
420 &= 2_(―) \cdot 2_(―) \cdot 3_(―) \cdot 5 \cdot 7_(―) \\
84 &= 2_(―) \cdot 2_(―) \cdot 3_(―) \cdot 7_(―)
\end{aligned}
$$

The common factors are $2, 2, 3$, and $7$. Notice that the common factor $2$ appears twice in both factorizations. Therefore,

$$
\textrm{GCF}(420,84) = 2\cdot 2 \cdot 3 \cdot 7 = 84
$$

## Finding the Greatest Common Factor of Three Integers

**Example:** What is the greatest common factor of $36$, $90$, and $396$.

**Explanation**

To find the greatest common factor $(\textrm{GCF})$ of the three numbers, we first find their prime factorizations:

- First, let's find the prime decomposition of $36{:}$![](<../Source/3.3.6. Finding Greatest Common Factors Using Prime Factorization/Images/1e661409f287e051ddbb4011592b1b5b.png>)
So, $36 = 2 \cdot 2\cdot 3 \cdot 3$.
- Next, we find the prime decomposition of $90{:}$![](<../Source/3.3.6. Finding Greatest Common Factors Using Prime Factorization/Images/c9abbc17a279db74ba7a6b47a9cb3ce6.png>)
So, $90= 2 \cdot 3 \cdot 3 \cdot 5$.
- Finally, we find the prime decomposition of $396{:}$![](<../Source/3.3.6. Finding Greatest Common Factors Using Prime Factorization/Images/70ab983e756025935246505b57efb980.png>)
So, $396= 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 3 \cdot 3 \cdot 11$.

Then, comparing our factorizations side-by-side, we find the factors that are common to all three:

$$
\begin{aligned}
36 &= 2_(―) \cdot 2 \cdot 3_(―) \cdot 3_(―) \\
90 &= 2_(―) \cdot 3_(―) \cdot 3_(―) \cdot 5 \\
396 &= 2_(―) \cdot 2 \cdot 3_(―) \cdot 3_(―) \cdot 11
\end{aligned}
$$

The common factors are $2$, $3$, and $3$. Therefore,

$$
\textrm{GCF}(36,90,396) = 2\cdot 3 \cdot 3 = 18
$$

## Finding the Greatest Common Factor of Two Powers

**Example:** What is $\textrm{GCF}(3^3,21^2)$?

**Explanation**

To find the greatest common factor of two numbers, we first find the prime factorization of both numbers.

- Since $3$ is a prime number, we immediately obtain the prime decomposition:

$$
3^3 = 3 \cdot 3 \cdot 3
$$

- Since $21= 3\cdot 7$, the prime decomposition $21^2$ can be obtained as follows:
$21^{2}|= 21 \cdot 21; = (3 \cdot 7) \cdot (3 \cdot 7); = 3 \cdot 3 \cdot 7 \cdot 7$.

Comparing the decompositions side-by-side, we pair off the factors as much as possible:

$$
\begin{aligned}
3^{3} &= 3_(―) \cdot 3_(―) \cdot 3 \\
21^{2} &= 3_(―) \cdot 3_(―) \cdot 7 \cdot 7
\end{aligned}
$$

We conclude that

$$
\textrm{GCF}(3^{3}, 21^{2}) = 3^{2}
$$
