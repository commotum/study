## Introduction

To raise the product of two numbers to a power, we can raise each number to that power, and then multiply. This is the **power of product rule for exponents**.

To demonstrate, let's simplify the expression $(5 \times 3)^4$ by applying the power of product rule:

$$
(5\times 3)^4=5^4\times 3^4
$$

**Note:** The power of product rule for exponents is really just a quicker alternative to using repeated multiplication, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(5 \cdot 3)^{4} &=  \\
(5 \cdot 3) \cdot (5 \cdot 3) \cdot (5 \cdot 3) \cdot (5 \cdot 3)_(⏟)_(4copies) &=  \\
(5 \cdot 5 \cdot 5 \cdot 5)_(⏟)_(4copies) \cdot (3 \cdot 3 \cdot 3 \cdot 3)_(⏟)_(4copies) &=  \\
&= 5^{4} \cdot 3^{4}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Applying the Power of Product Rule

**Example:** Express $(3\times 2)^2$ as the product of two powers.

**Explanation**

To raise the product of two numbers to the power of $2$, we can raise each number to the power of $2$, and then multiply:

$$
(3\times 2)^2=3^2\times 2^2
$$

## Applying the Power of Product Rule to Expressions with Fractions

**Example:** Express $\left(2\times \dfrac{1}{3}\right)^2$ as the product of two powers.

**Explanation**

To raise the product of two numbers to the power of $2$, we can raise each number to the power of $2$, and then multiply:

$$
\left(2\times \dfrac{1}{3}\right)^2=2^2 \times \left(\dfrac{1}{3}\right)^2
$$

## Applying the Power of Product Rule to Expressions with Negative Numbers

**Example:** Express $\left(-2\times 4\right)^3$ as the product of two powers.

**Explanation**

To raise the product of two numbers to the power of $3$, we can raise each number to the power of $3$, and then multiply:

$$
\left(-2\times 4\right)^3=\left(\left(-2\right)\times 4\right)^3= \left(-2\right)^3\times 4^3
$$
