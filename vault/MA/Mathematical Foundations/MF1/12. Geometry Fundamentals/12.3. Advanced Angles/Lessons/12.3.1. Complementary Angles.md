## Introduction

**Complementary angles** are two angles whose measures have a sum of $90^\circ$, a right angle.

![](<../Source/12.3.1. Complementary Angles/Images/e24a1c61d390e459a47c909375ae3891.png>)

For example, the angles in the diagram above are complementary because the sum of their measures is $90^\circ$:

$$
60^\circ +30^\circ = 90^\circ
$$

<a id="finding-an-unknown-complementary-angle-given-the-other-angle"></a>
## Finding an Unknown Complementary Angle Given the Other Angle

**Example:** The angles $\angle a$ and $\angle b$ are complementary angles. If $m \angle a = 37^\circ$, what is the measure of $\angle b$?

**Explanation**

![](<../Source/12.3.1. Complementary Angles/Images/f92f794ac7a486ceff984e4ef8f1a9ce.png>)

Since $\angle a$ and $\angle b$ are complementary, the sum of their measures is equal to $90^\circ$. So, we have:

$$
\begin{aligned}
m∠a + m∠b &= 90^{∘} \\
37^{∘} + m∠b &= 90^{∘} \\
m∠b &= 90^{∘} - 37^{∘} \\
m∠b &= 53^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="finding-an-unknown-quantity-using-complementary-angles"></a>
## Finding an Unknown Quantity Using Complementary Angles

**Example:** Calculate $x$, given that $\angle{A}$ and $\angle{B}$ are complementary, and that
$m\angle{A} = 3x, \qquad m\angle{B} = 4x-8^\circ\,$.

**Explanation**

Since angles $\angle{A}$ and $\angle{B}$ are complementary, we have

$$
m \angle A + m \angle B = 90^\circ
$$

Hence, we can solve for $x$, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
m∠A + m∠B &= 90^{∘} \\
3x + (4x - 8^{∘}) &= 90^{∘} \\
7x - 8^{∘} &= 90^{∘} \\
7x &= 98^{∘} \\
x &= 14^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="finding-an-unknown-complementary-angle-given-an-equation"></a>
## Finding an Unknown Complementary Angle Given an Equation

**Example:** Let $\angle A$ and $\angle B$ be two complementary angles. If $m \angle B = 2m \angle A$, find $m \angle B$.

**Explanation**

Since $\angle A$ and $\angle B$ are complementary angles, we have

$$
m \angle A + m \angle B = 90^\circ
$$

Substituting

$$
m \angle B = 2m \angle A
$$

we obtain:

$$
\begin{aligned}
m∠A + m∠B &= 90^{∘} \\
m∠A + 2m∠A &= 90^{∘} \\
3m∠A &= 90^{∘} \\
m∠A &= 30^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
m∠B &= 2m∠A \\
&= 2(30^{∘}) \\
&= 60^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Using a Diagram and Properties of Complementary Angles to Find an Unknown Angle

**Example:** In the figure below, $\angle AOC$ and $\angle COB$ are complementary angles. If $\angle AOC \cong \angle BOD$ and $m \angle COB = 50^\circ$, find $m \angle AOD$.

![](<../Source/12.3.1. Complementary Angles/Images/88e1cf88acdc08e6348603512c13413f.png>)

**Explanation**

Since $\angle AOC$ and $\angle COB$ are complementary angles, we have:

$$
\begin{aligned}
m∠AOC + m∠COB &= 90^{∘} \\
m∠AOC + 50^{∘} &= 90^{∘} \\
m∠AOC &= 40^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, since $\angle AOC \cong \angle BOD$, we have

$$
m \angle BOD = m \angle AOC=40^\circ
$$

Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
m∠AOD &= m∠AOC + m∠COB + m∠BOD \\
&= 40^{∘} + 50^{∘} + 40^{∘} \\
&= 130^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$
