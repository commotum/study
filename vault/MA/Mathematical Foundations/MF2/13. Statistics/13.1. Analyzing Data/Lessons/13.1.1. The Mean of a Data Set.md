## Introduction

When summarizing a data set, we often want to produce a *single* number that best represents *all* numbers in the data set. One way to do this is to find the **mean** of the data set. The mean can be considered the "center" of the data set.

If we have a data set that consists of the points $x_1, x_2, \ldots, x_n$, then the mean, denoted $\overline{x}$, is given by the following formula:

$$
\overline{x} = \dfrac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n x_i
$$

To demonstrate, let's compute the mean of the data set below:

$$
1, \: 7, \: 3, \: 9
$$

Here, there are $n=4$ data points. Adding up all the points in the data set, we get

$$
\sum_{i=1}^n x_i = 1 + 7 + 3 + 9 = 20
$$

Therefore, the mean is

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^(―) &= \frac{1}{n}∑_(i = 1)^(n)x_{i} \\
&= \frac{1}{4} \cdot 20 \\
&= 5
\end{aligned}
$$

In general, to compute the mean of a set of numbers, all we need to do is add up all the numbers and divide the result by how many numbers we added.

## Computing the Mean of a Data Set From the Sum

**Example:** Given a data set with $12$ data points and $\displaystyle \sum_{i=1}^{12} x_i = 96$, compute $\overline{x}$.

**Explanation**

If we have a data set that consists of the points $x_1, x_2, \ldots, x_n$, then the mean is denoted by $\overline{x}$ and is given by the following formula:

$$
\overline{x} = \dfrac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n x_i
$$

Substituting $n=12$ and

$$
\displaystyle \sum_{i=1}^{12} x_i = 96
$$

we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^(―) &= \frac{1}{12} \cdot 96 = 8
\end{aligned}
$$

## Computing the Mean of a Data Set

**Example:** What is the mean of the following data set?
$9, \: 10, \: 8, \: 11, \: 12$

**Explanation**

If we have a data set that consists of the points $x_1, x_2, \ldots, x_n$, then the mean is denoted by $\overline{x}$ and is given by the following formula:

$$
\overline{x} = \dfrac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n x_i
$$

Here, there are $n=5$ data points. Adding up all the points in the data set, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
∑_(i = 1)^(5)x_{i} &= 9 + 10 + 8 + 11 + 12 \\
&= 50
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the mean is

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^(―) &= \frac{1}{n}∑_(i = 1)^(n)x_{i} \\
&= \frac{1}{5} \cdot 50 \\
&= 10
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding Missing Values in a Data Set Given the Mean

**Example:** Given that the mean of the following data set is $9$, find the value of $a$:
$5, \: 3, \: a, \: a, \: a, \: 7$

**Explanation**

If we have a data set that consists of the points $x_1, x_2, \ldots, x_n$, then the mean is denoted by $\overline{x}$ and is given by the following formula:

$$
\overline{x} = \dfrac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n x_i
$$

Here, there are $n=6$ data points. Adding up all the points in the data set, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
∑_(i = 1)^(6)x_{i} &= 5 + 3 + a + a + a + 7 \\
&= 15 + 3a
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the mean is given by the expression

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^(―) &= \frac{1}{n}∑_(i = 1)^(n)x_{i} \\
&= \frac{1}{6} \cdot (15 + 3a) \\
&= \frac{15 + 3a}{6}
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, since we're told that

$$
\overline{x} = 9
$$

we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
9 &= \frac{15 + 3a}{6} \\
54 &= 15 + 3a \\
39 &= 3a \\
a &= 13
\end{aligned}
$$
