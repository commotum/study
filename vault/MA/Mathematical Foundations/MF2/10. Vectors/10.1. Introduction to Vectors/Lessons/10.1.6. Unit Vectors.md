## Introduction

A **unit vector** is a vector whose magnitude is equal to $1$.

Given any vector $\mathbf a$, we can create a unit vector $\mathbf u$ by dividing $\mathbf a$ by its magnitude $\mid \, \mathbf{a} \, \mid$, as follows:

$$
\mathbf{u} = \dfrac{\mathbf{a}}{\mid \,\mathbf{a}\, \mid}
$$

For example, if

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mid \,\mathbf{a}\, &= 2
\end{aligned}
$$

then the unit vector is

$$
\mathbf{u} = \dfrac{\mathbf{a}}{2} = \dfrac{1}{2} \mathbf{a}
$$

## Finding a Unit Vector Parallel to a Given Vector

**Example:** The vector $\mathbf{a}$ has magnitude $3$. Find a unit vector that is parallel to $\mathbf{a}$.

**Explanation**

To obtain the unit vector that is parallel to $\mathbf{a}$, we divide $\mathbf{a}$ by its length:

$$
\mathbf{u}=\dfrac{\mathbf{a}}{\mid \,\mathbf{a}\, \mid}=\dfrac{1}{3}\mathbf{a}
$$

## Finding a Unit Vector Parallel to a Given Vector in a Diagram

**Example:** Consider the picture shown above. Find a unit vector that is parallel to $\mathbf a$.

![](<../Source/10.1.6. Unit Vectors/Images/b16da2f9bc2d3afa2d52cafb894da27b.png>)

**Explanation**

![](<../Source/10.1.6. Unit Vectors/Images/766132fe7dad96d52256ee9db5c4e858.png>)

In $\bigtriangleup AHB$ we have

$$
\angle AHB = 90^\circ
$$

, $AH=3$ and $BH=2$. So, the Pythagorean theorem gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
AB &= \sqrt{AH^{2} + BH^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{3^{2} + 2^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{9 + 4} \\
&= \sqrt{13}
\end{aligned}
$$

Hence, the magnitude of $\mathbf{a}$ is $\sqrt{13}$. Consequently, the unit vector is

$$
\begin{aligned}
u &= \frac{1}{\mid a \mid} \cdot a \\
&= \frac{1}{\sqrt{13}}a
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="finding-a-unit-vector-parallel-to-a-vector-sum"></a>
## Finding a Unit Vector Parallel to a Vector Sum

**Example:** The vector $\mathbf{a}$ is directed horizontally to the right and has magnitude $10$, while the vector $\mathbf{b}$ is directed vertically down and has magnitude $20$. Find a unit vector that is parallel to $\mathbf{a+b}$.

**Explanation**

First, we have to find $\mathbf{a+b}$. According to the triangle law of addition, the resultant $\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b}$ goes from the tail of $\mathbf{a}$ to the head of $\mathbf{b}$:

![](<../Source/10.1.6. Unit Vectors/Images/76f1d93be3bbbf7c5435f00304403b1d.png>)

To find the unit vector corresponding to $\mathbf{a+b}$, we have to divide by the magnitude:

$$
\begin{aligned}
u &= \frac{a + b}{\mid a + b \mid}
\end{aligned}
$$

To find the magnitude, first notice that we obtain a right triangle. So, we can use the Pythagorean theorem:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\begin{vmatrix}a + b & ^{2} &= & a & ^{2} + & b\end{vmatrix}^{2} \\
&= 10^{2} + 20^{2} \\
&= 100 + 400 \\
&= 500
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the magnitude is

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mid \mathbf{a+b} &= \sqrt{500}=10\sqrt{5}
\end{aligned}
$$

and the corresponding unit vector is

$$
\begin{aligned}
u &= \frac{a + b}{\mid a + b \mid} \\
&= \frac{1}{10\sqrt{5}}(a + b)
\end{aligned}
$$
