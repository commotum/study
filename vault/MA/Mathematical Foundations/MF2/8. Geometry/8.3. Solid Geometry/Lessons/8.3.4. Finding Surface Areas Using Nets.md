## Introduction

The **surface area** of a polyhedron is the combined area of all its faces.

To calculate the surface area of a polyhedron, we use nets.

Let's consider the net of the rectangular pyramid below. What is the surface area of this pyramid?

![](<../Source/8.3.4. Finding Surface Areas Using Nets/Images/f184ad90d33e51aa5e147b079ff0b071.png>)

The surface area of our pyramid consists of one rectangle (the base) and four triangles (the side faces).

Also, notice that there are two different triangular faces:

- there are two triangular faces with an area of $60\,\text{in}^2$, and
- there are two triangular faces with an area of $30\,\text{in}^2$.

Calculating the total area, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
Surface area &= 1 \cdot 120in^{2} + 2 \cdot 60in^{2} + 2 \cdot 30in^{2} \\
&= 120in^{2} + 120in^{2} + 60in^{2} \\
&= 300in^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, we conclude that the surface area of the pyramid is $300 \, \text{in}^2$.

## Calculating the Surface Area of a Polyhedron Given the Areas of the Faces

**Example:** What is the surface area of the triangular prism represented by the net below?

![](<../Source/8.3.4. Finding Surface Areas Using Nets/Images/2be33da597597d270a7469cb5512e3f4.png>)

**Explanation**

The surface area of our prism consists of $2$ triangles (base and top) and $3$ rectangles (side faces).

Adding the areas, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
Surface area &= 2 \cdot (Area of base) + 3 \cdot (Area of side) \\
&= 2 \cdot 12 + 3 \cdot 48 \\
&= 24 + 144 \\
&= 168cm^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Calculating the Surface Area of a Polyhedron Given the Areas of the Faces: Word Problems

**Example:** Maggy is building a pyramid from a cardboard net, shown below. What will be the total surface area of the pyramid?

![](<../Source/8.3.4. Finding Surface Areas Using Nets/Images/25b8cd579c4544b8df59da93a28b6eb0.png>)

**Explanation**

The net shown corresponds to a triangular pyramid. The surface area of our pyramid consists of $1$ triangular base and $3$ triangular sides.

Adding the areas, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
Surface area &= 1 \cdot (Area of base) + 3 \cdot (Area of side) \\
&= 1 \cdot 10 + 3 \cdot 12 \\
&= 10 + 36 \\
&= 46in^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding Surface Areas of Polyhedra Given Some Lengths

If we know a polyhedron's measurements, we can calculate its surface area by first drawing the net of the polyhedron. Then, we calculate the areas of its polygonal faces using known formulas and then sum the results.

To demonstrate, let's find the surface area of the rectangular prism shown below.

![](<../Source/8.3.4. Finding Surface Areas Using Nets/Images/9384f4f5540ef61df98b1a287ae8baf9.png>)

First, we consider the net of our prism.

![](<../Source/8.3.4. Finding Surface Areas Using Nets/Images/948e762e99c6ebe20a48713c6ea2d956.png>)

From the diagram, we see that the prism is made up of squares and rectangles.

- The rectangles are $8\,\text{ft}$ long and $2\,\text{ft}$ high. So, the area of one rectangle is
$\text{Area of rectangle} = 8 \times 2 = 16 \, \text{ft}^2$.
- The squares have sides of length $2\, \text{ft}$. So, the area of one square is
$\text{Area of square} = 2 \times 2 = 4 \, \text{ft}^2$.

Finally, the surface area of the prism consists of $4$ rectangles and $2$ squares.

Adding the areas, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
Surface area &= 4 \cdot (Area of rectangle) + 2 \cdot (Area of square) \\
&= 4 \cdot 16 + 2 \cdot 4 \\
&= 64 + 8 \\
&= 72ft^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="calculating-the-surface-area-of-a-polyhedron-given-some-lengths"></a>
## Calculating the Surface Area of a Polyhedron Given Some Lengths

**Example:** By assuming that all of the side faces have the same height $(8\,\text{in})$, find the total surface area of the square pyramid above by considering its net.

![](<../Source/8.3.4. Finding Surface Areas Using Nets/Images/6455bf199b1787e8e87e60f7216f0736.png>)

**Explanation**

The net of our pyramid is as follows:

![](<../Source/8.3.4. Finding Surface Areas Using Nets/Images/99f9776f48362797204b09a2d28ece42.png>)

First, notice that the base of the pyramid is a square with sides equal to $5 \, \text{in}$. Therefore, we have

$$
\text{Area of square} = 5 \times 5 = 25 \, \text{in}^2
$$

Each side face of our solid is triangle, where the base is $5\,\text{in}$ and the height is $8\,\text{in}$. Therefore, we have

$$
\text{Area of triangle} = \dfrac{1}{2} \times 5 \times 8 = 20 \, \text{in}^2
$$

Finally, the surface area of the pyramid consists of $1$ square (the base) and $4$ triangles (the side faces).

Adding the areas, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
Surface area &= 1 \cdot (Area of the square) + 4 \cdot (Area of the triangle) \\
&= 1 \cdot 25 + 4 \cdot 20 \\
&= 25 + 80 \\
&= 105in^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$
