## Introduction

A **complex number** is a combination of a real number and an imaginary number.
If we add the real number $\color{blue}2$ and the imaginary number ${\color{red}3\textrm{i}}$, we get the complex number ${\color{blue}2} +{\color{red}3\textrm{i}}$.

This complex number has **real part** equal to $\color{blue}2$ and **imaginary part** equal to ${\color{red}3}$, which we write as

$$
\begin{aligned}
Re(2 + 3\textrm{i}) &= 2, \\
Im(2 + 3\textrm{i}) &= 3
\end{aligned}
$$

In general, we write a complex number as ${\color{blue}a} + {\color{red}b\textrm{i}}$, where $\color{blue}a$ is the real part and $\color{red}b$ is the imaginary part.

## Identifying the Real and Imaginary Parts of a Complex Number

**Example:** What are the real and imaginary parts of the complex number $5 - 2\textrm{i}$?

**Explanation**

For a given complex number

$$
z = a + b \textrm{i}
$$

- the number $a$ is called the **real part** of $z$, and
- the number $b$ is called the **imaginary part** of $z$.

Therefore, the real part of the given complex number is $5$, and the imaginary part is $-2$.

## Identifying Real and Imaginary Parts of a Complex Number Written in Nonstandard Form

**Example:** What is the real part of the complex number $\sqrt 3+6\textrm{i}-2$?

**Explanation**

For a given complex number

$$
z = a + b \textrm{i}
$$

- the number $a$ is called the **real part** of $z$, and
- the number $b$ is called the **imaginary part** of $z$.

Rewriting the given complex number in the form $a + b \textrm{i}$, we get

$$
\sqrt 3+6\textrm{i}-2=\left(\sqrt 3-2\right)+6\textrm{i}
$$

Therefore, the real part of the given complex number is $\sqrt 3-2$ and the imaginary part is $6$.

## Complex Numbers Where the Real or Imaginary Part is Zero

Any real number is a complex number ${\color{blue}a} + {\color{red}b\textrm{i}}$ with the imaginary part $\color{red}b$ equal to $0$. For instance, $3$ is a complex number, because

$$
3 = 3+ {\color{red}0\textrm{i}}
$$

In such a case, we say the complex number is **real**.

Similarly, an imaginary number is a complex number ${\color{blue}a} + {\color{red}b\textrm{i}}$ with the real part $\color{blue}a$ equal to $0$. For example, $2\textrm{i}$ is a complex number, because

$$
2\textrm{i} = {\color{blue}0} + 2\textrm{i}
$$

In such a case, we say the complex number is **purely imaginary**.

Ultimately, this means that the real numbers and the imaginary numbers are both subsets of the complex numbers.

<a id="identifying-the-real-part-of-a-purely-imaginary-number"></a>
## Identifying the Real Part of a Purely Imaginary Number

**Example:** What is the real part of the complex number $-\sqrt 7 \,\textrm{i}$?

**Explanation**

For a given complex number

$$
z = a + b \textrm{i}
$$

- the number $a$ is called the **real part** of $z$, and
- the number $b$ is called the **imaginary part** of $z$.

We can think of the number $-\sqrt 7 \,\textrm{i}$ as having a real part of zero:

$$
-\sqrt 7\,\textrm{i} = 0 - \sqrt 7\,\textrm{i}
$$

Therefore, the real part of the given complex number is $0$ and the imaginary part is $-\sqrt 7$.

## Identifying the Imaginary Part of a Real Number

**Example:** What is the imaginary part of the complex number $-6$?

**Explanation**

For a given complex number

$$
z = a + b \textrm{i}
$$

- the number $a$ is called the **real part** of $z$, and
- the number $b$ is called the **imaginary part** of $z$.

We can think of the number $-6$ as having an imaginary part of zero:

$$
-6 = -6+ 0\textrm{i}
$$

Therefore, for the complex number $-6$, the imaginary part is $0$ and the real part is $-6$.
