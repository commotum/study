## Introduction

Consider the exponential function

$$
f(x)=2^x
$$

Its domain is given by

$x \in (-\infty, \infty)$.
Because the domain consists of all real numbers, if we were to apply graph transformations to $y = f(x)$, the domain would remain unchanged.

However, applying graph transformations could affect other properties of $f(x)$, such as its range, intercepts, end-behavior, and roots. In this lesson, we will learn how to calculate the properties of transformed exponential functions.

<a id="finding-the-y-intercept-of-a-transformed-exponential-function"></a>
## Finding the Y-Intercept of a Transformed Exponential Function

**Example:** What is the $y$-intercept of $y = -2e^{-2x}+3$?

**Explanation**

To find the $y$-intercept, we substitute $x = 0$ into the equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
y &= -2e^{-2(0)} + 3 \\
&=-2e^{0} + 3 \\
&=-2(1) + 3 \\
&= 1
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the $y$-intercept is $1$.

## Finding the Range of a Transformed Exponential Function

**Example:** What is the range of $y = 2(4)^x+3$?

**Explanation**

The range of $4^x$ is

$$
4^x \gt 0
$$

Multiplying the above inequality by $2$ and then adding $3$, we get the following:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}4^{x} & > 0 \\ 2(4)^{x} & > 0 \\ 2(4)^{x} + 3 & > 0 + 3 \\ y & > 3\end{bmatrix}
$$

Therefore, the range is $y \in (3,\infty)$.

We can also see that this is true from the graph of the function. The graph of

$$
f(x) = 2(4)^x+3
$$

is shown below.

<a id="calculating-the-root-of-a-transformed-exponential-function"></a>
## Calculating the Root of a Transformed Exponential Function

**Example:** Find the root of $f(x) = 3(2)^{x} - 48$.

**Explanation**

To find the root of $f(x)$, we must solve

$$
f(x)=0
$$

$$
3(2)^{x} - 48 = 0
$$

First, we isolate the term with the variable exponent by adding $48$ to both sides:

$$
\begin{aligned}
3(2)^{x} - 48 &= 0 \\
3(2)^{x} - 48 + 48 &= 0 + 48 \\
3(2)^{x} &= 48
\end{aligned}
$$

Dividing both sides by $3$, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
3(2)^{x} &= 48 \\
2^{x} &= 16
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we rewrite $16$ as $2^4$ to get
$2^x = 2^4$.

Finally, since both sides of the equation share the same base $(2)$, we equate the exponents. This gives
$x = 4$.

Therefore, the root of $f(x)$ is $x=4$.

## Finding the End-Behavior of a Transformed Exponential Function

**Example:** Suppose that $f(x) = 3(e^{5x}) + 2$. Given that $f(x) \to a$ as $x \to -\infty$, then what is the value of $a$?

**Explanation**

Let's sketch the graph of $y=f(x)$:

![](<../Source/5.5.5. Properties of Transformed Exponential Functions/Images/4f7eca5fb85704c4c86b65a5d83bbab9.png>)

Since the function approaches the line $y=2$ as $x\to -\infty$, we conclude that
$f(x)\to 2 \quad \textrm{as}\quad x\to -\infty$.

Therefore, $a=2$.

Finally, we note that the horizontal asymptote of $y=f(x)$ is $y=2$.
