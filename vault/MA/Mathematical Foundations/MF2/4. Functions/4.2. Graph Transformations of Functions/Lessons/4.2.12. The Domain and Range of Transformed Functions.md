## Introduction

The diagram below shows the function $y=f(x)$.

![](<../Source/4.2.12. The Domain and Range of Transformed Functions/Images/66b8adc19a78261f09cecc603927f282.png>)

Suppose we want to calculate the domain and range of

$$
y = f(2x) -4
$$

To do this, we first sketch it by applying graph transformations, and then we read the domain and range from the graph of the transformed function.

To plot the graph of

$$
y = f(2x) -4
$$

we apply the following steps:

- Take the curve $y=f(x)$.
- Then, stretch the graph by a scale factor of $\dfrac{1}{2}$ parallel to the $x$-axis and get the graph of $y = f(2x)$.

![](<../Source/4.2.12. The Domain and Range of Transformed Functions/Images/b5f1c5aa1de5667d9931744ead359799.png>)

- Finally, we shift the result by $4$ units down and obtain the graph of $y = f(2x) -4$.

![](<../Source/4.2.12. The Domain and Range of Transformed Functions/Images/943cc2113d506ed2465f1d2f4123d3ca.png>)

From here, we may deduce the following about the function $y=f(2x) -4$:

- Its domain is $[MATH: x\in (-3, 3].]$
- For the range, we note the following:The minimum value is indicated by the $y$-coordinate of the point $(1, -7)$, and this value is included in the range (since this point has a filled circle).The maximum value is indicated by the $y$-coordinate of the point $(-3,4)$, and this value is *not* included in the range (since this point has an open circle).Therefore, the range is $[MATH: y\in [-7, 4).]$

## Finding the Domain of a Transformed Function

**Example:** The diagram above shows the graph of $y=f(x)$. What is the domain of $y = f(2-x)$?

![](<../Source/4.2.12. The Domain and Range of Transformed Functions/Images/8d28fbaae7d6760efe0e1f517b516ff9.png>)

**Explanation**

First, let's write the function in a form that is convenient for transformations:

$$
y = f(2-x) = f(-(x-2))
$$

When performing graph transformations, we must remember the following rule:

1. First, we perform the transformations *inside* the argument of $f$. We first carry out horizontal stretches and reflections, followed by horizontal translations.
2. Then, we perform the transformations *outside* the argument of $f$. We first carry out vertical stretches and reflections, followed by vertical translations.

So, to plot the graph of

$$
y = f(-(x-2))
$$

we apply the following steps:

- Take the curve $y=f(x)$.
- Then, reflect the graph across the $y$-axis and get the graph of $y = f(-x)$.

![](<../Source/4.2.12. The Domain and Range of Transformed Functions/Images/f1d0b16ca0a95f652d367607b66975e6.png>)

- Finally, we shift the result horizontally by $2$ units to the right and obtain the graph of $y = f(-(x-2))$.

![](<../Source/4.2.12. The Domain and Range of Transformed Functions/Images/2fb2f7fd3932d3b2400a61f1fdd40c85.png>)

Therefore, the domain of $f(2-x)$ is $[MATH: [0,5).]$

## Finding the Range of a Transformed Function

**Example:** The diagram above shows the graph of $y=f(x)$. What is the range of $y = -2-f(x)$?

![](<../Source/4.2.12. The Domain and Range of Transformed Functions/Images/086b894e5978f39627153b7081d6fbdd.png>)

**Explanation**

First, let's write the function in a form that is convenient for transformations:

$$
y = -2 - f(x) =-f(x) - 2
$$

1. First, we perform the transformations *inside* the argument of $f$. We first carry out horizontal stretches and reflections, followed by horizontal translations.
2. Then, we perform the transformations *outside* the argument of $f$. We first carry out vertical stretches and reflections, followed by vertical translations.

So, to plot the graph of

$$
y = -f(x) -2
$$

we apply the following steps:

- Take the curve $y=f(x)$.
- Then, reflect the graph across the $x$-axis and get the graph of $y = -f(x)$.

![](<../Source/4.2.12. The Domain and Range of Transformed Functions/Images/9a38756fa24a40954c88632675f6597f.png>)

- Finally, we shift the result by $2$ units down and obtain the graph of $y = -f(x) -2$.

![](<../Source/4.2.12. The Domain and Range of Transformed Functions/Images/bfe711fcff9956c8dc14c741c934a15f.png>)

Therefore, the range of $-f(x) -2$ is $[MATH: [-5,-1).]$
