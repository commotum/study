## Introduction

We know that we can write a system of three linear equations in three variables in the form

$$
A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{b}
$$

where

- $A$ is the $3\times 3$ coefficient matrix,
- $\mathbf{x}$ is the column vector of variables, and
- $\mathbf{b}$ is the column vector of constants.

If the $3\times 3$ matrix $A$ is invertible, then we can find a solution to the system

$$
A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b}
$$

by "pre-multiplying" both sides of the equation by $A^{-1}{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
Ax &= b \\
A^{-1}Ax &= A^{-1}b \\
I_{3}x &= A^{-1}b \\
x &= A^{-1}b
\end{aligned}
$$

So, to find the solution of

$$
A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{b}
$$

we simply calculate

$$
\mathbf{x}= A^{-1}\mathbf{b}
$$

Let's see how this works for a particular system in the next example.

## Solving a 3x3 System of Equations Given the Inverse of the Coefficient Matrix

**Example:** Consider the following system of linear equations:

${-2x + 5y - 2z =-2; - 3y + z =-2; 3x - 7y + 3z = 3$

The system above has the matrix representation $A \mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b}$. Given that $\mathbf x = \langle x,y,z\rangle$ is unique, what is the value of $x+y+z$?

*Hint: The inverse of the coefficient matrix is given by*
$\begin{bmatrix}-2 & -1 & -1 \\ 3 & 0 & 2 \\ 9 & 1 & 6\end{bmatrix}$

**Explanation**

First, let's write our system in matrix form as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
Ax &= b \\
\begin{bmatrix}-2 & 5 & -2 \\ 0 & -3 & 1 \\ 3 & -7 & 3\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}x \\ y \\ z\end{bmatrix} &= \begin{bmatrix}-2 \\ -2 \\ 3\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Recall that the solution to

$$
A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{b}
$$

is given by

$$
\mathbf{x}= A^{-1}\mathbf{b}
$$

Therefore, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
x &= A^{-1}b \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}-2 & -1 & -1 \\ 3 & 0 & 2 \\ 9 & 1 & 6\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}-2 \\ -2 \\ 3\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}3 \\ 0 \\ -2\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the solution is

$$
x=3, y=0, z=-2
$$

Finally,

$$
x+y+z=3+0+(-2)=1
$$

## Solving a 3x3 System of Equations By Calculating the Inverse of the Coefficient Matrix

**Example:** Solve the following system of linear equations using matrices:
${3y + x + z = 0; z + 4y - 5 = 2; 1 + 2x - y =-3$

**Explanation**

First, we organize the system so that the variables are in the correct order and every variable has a coefficient:

$$
{x + 3y + z = 0; 0x + 4y + z = 7; 2x - y + 0z =-4
$$

Next, we write the system in the form

$$
A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b}
$$

, where $A$ is a $3\times 3$ matrix and $\mathbf{x}$ and $\mathbf{b}$ are $3\times 1$ column vectors. We can do this as follows:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}1 & 3 & 1 \\ 0 & 4 & 1 \\ 2 & -1 & 0\end{bmatrix}
$$

Now, the solution to

$$
A\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{b}
$$

is given by

$$
\mathbf{x}= A^{-1}\mathbf{b}
$$

So, we compute $A^{-1}$ as follows:

*Step 1*: Find the determinant of $A$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
det(A) =\begin{vmatrix}1 & 3 & 1 \\ 0 & 4 & 1 \\ 2 & -1 & 0\end{vmatrix} &= 1\begin{vmatrix}4 & 1 \\ -1 & 0\end{vmatrix}- 3\begin{vmatrix}0 & 1 \\ 2 & 0\end{vmatrix}+ 1\begin{vmatrix}0 & 4 \\ 2 & -1\end{vmatrix} \\
&= (1) - 3(-2) + (-8) \\
&= 1 + 6 - 8 \\
&=-1
\end{aligned}
$$

*Step 2*: Compute the matrix of minors.

$$
\begin{aligned}
M &= [\begin{vmatrix}4 & 1 \\ -1 & 0\end{vmatrix} \mid \mid 0\begin{vmatrix}1 \\ 2\end{vmatrix}0 \mid \mid \mid 0\begin{vmatrix}4 \\ 2\end{vmatrix}- 1 \mid \\
&= \begin{vmatrix}3 & 1 \\ -1 & 0\end{vmatrix} \mid \mid 1\begin{vmatrix}1 \\ 2\end{vmatrix}0 \mid \mid \mid 1\begin{vmatrix}3 \\ 2\end{vmatrix}- 1 \mid \\
&= \begin{vmatrix}3 & 1 \\ 4 & 1\end{vmatrix} \mid \mid 1\begin{vmatrix}1 \\ 0\end{vmatrix}1 \mid \mid \mid 1\begin{vmatrix}3 \\ 0\end{vmatrix}4 \mid ] \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}1 & -2 & -8 \\ 1 & -2 & -7 \\ -1 & 1 & 4\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

*Step 3*: Find the matrix of cofactors.

$$
\begin{bmatrix}1 & 2 & -8 \\ -1 & -2 & 7 \\ -1 & -1 & 4\end{bmatrix}
$$

*Step 4*: Transpose the matrix of cofactors.

$$
\begin{bmatrix}1 & -1 & -1 \\ 2 & -2 & -1 \\ -8 & 7 & 4\end{bmatrix}
$$

*Step 5*: Apply the inverse matrix formula.

$$
\begin{bmatrix}1 & -1 & -1 \\ 2 & -2 & -1 \\ -8 & 7 & 4\end{bmatrix}
$$

Finally, we can calculate the solution as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x &= A^{-1}b \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}-1 & 1 & 1 \\ -2 & 2 & 1 \\ 8 & -7 & -4\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}0 \\ 7 \\ -4\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}3 \\ 10 \\ -33\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the solution is $x=3$, $y=10$, $z=-33$.

## Solving a Matrix Equation Using Algebraic Manipulation

**Example:** Consider a matrix equation $B\,\mathbf{x} = A^{-1}\,\mathbf{d}$, where $A$ and $B$ are $10 \times 10$ non-singular matrices, while $\mathbf{x}$ and $\mathbf{d}$ are $10 \times 1$ column vectors. Solve the equation for $\mathbf{x}$.

**Explanation**

Since $B$ is not singular, it must have an inverse $B^{-1}$. Now, pre-multiplying both sides of the equation

$$
B\,\mathbf{x} = A^{-1}\,\mathbf{d}
$$

by $B^{-1}$, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
Bx &= A^{-1}d \\
B^{-1}Bx &= B^{-1}A^{-1}d \\
I_{10}x &= B^{-1}A^{-1}d \\
x &= B^{-1}A^{-1}d
\end{aligned}
$$
