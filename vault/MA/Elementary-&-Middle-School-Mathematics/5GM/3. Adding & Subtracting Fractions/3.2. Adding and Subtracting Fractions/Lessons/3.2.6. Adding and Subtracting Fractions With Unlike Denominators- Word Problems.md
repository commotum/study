## Introduction

We can apply our knowledge of adding and subtracting fractions with unlike denominators to real-world situations.

For example, suppose William went to the grocery store to buy flour. He purchased

$$
\dfrac{1}{3}
$$

pounds of brown flour and

$$
\dfrac{1}{6}
$$

pounds of white flour. He'd like to know how much flour he bought in total.

To find the total amount of flour that William bought, we need to add

$$
\dfrac{1}{3}
$$

(the amount of brown flour) to

$$
\dfrac{1}{6}
$$

(the amount of white flour). Therefore, we have the following sum:

$$
\dfrac{1}{3} + \dfrac{1}{6}
$$

To add two fractions with unlike denominators, we need to express each fraction as an equivalent fraction with a common denominator.

Here, we can make a common denominator of $6$.

To put

$$
\dfrac{1}{3}
$$

over a denominator of $6$, we multiply the numerator and denominator by $2{:}$

$$
\dfrac{1}{3} = \dfrac{1 \times 2}{3 \times 2} = \dfrac{2}{6}
$$

We can now add the fractions. We keep the denominator the same and we add the numerators:

$$
\frac{2}{6} + \frac{1}{6} = \frac{3}{6}
$$

We can simplify this fraction by dividing the numerator and denominator by $3$:

$$
\dfrac{3}{6} = \dfrac{3\div 3}{6\div 3} = \dfrac12
$$

Therefore, William bought

$$
\dfrac12
$$

pounds of flour in total.

## Adding Fractions With Unlike Denominators

**Example:** A wooden slat was cut into two parts. One of the pieces is $\dfrac{6}{5}\,\textrm{ft}$ long and the other is $\dfrac{7}{4}\,\textrm{ft}$ long. How long was the original slat? Express your final answer as a mixed number.

**Explanation**

To find the length of the original slat, we need to calculate the sum

$$
\dfrac{6}{5} + \dfrac{7}{4}
$$

To add two fractions with unlike denominators, we need to express each fraction as an equivalent fraction with a common denominator.

Let's look at the denominators:

$$
\dfrac{6}{\color{red}5} + \dfrac{7}{\color{blue}4}
$$

In this question, we can make a common denominator of

$$
{\color{red}5} \times {\color{blue}4} = 20
$$

To put

$$
\dfrac{6}{5}
$$

over a denominator of $20$, we multiply the numerator and denominator by $4$:

$$
\dfrac{6}{5} = \dfrac{6 \times 4}{5 \times 4} = \dfrac{24}{20}
$$

To put

$$
\dfrac{7}{4}
$$

over a denominator of $20$, we multiply the numerator and denominator by $5$:

$$
\dfrac{7}{4} = \dfrac{7 \times 5}{4 \times 5} = \dfrac{35}{20}
$$

We can now add the fractions. We keep the denominator the same, and we add the numerators:

$$
\dfrac{24}{20} + \dfrac{35}{20} = \dfrac{59}{20}
$$

Converting this to a mixed number, we get

$$
\dfrac{59}{20} = 2\dfrac{19}{20}
$$

Therefore, the slat was originally

$$
2\dfrac{19}{20}\,\textrm{ft}
$$

long.

## Subtracting Fractions When One Denominator Is a Multiple of the Other

**Example:** John spends $\dfrac{5}{4}$ of an hour in the gym. During this time, he's either on the exercise bike or lifting weights. One day, he spent $\dfrac{1}{2}$ on the exercise bike. How much time did John spend lifting weights?

**Explanation**

To find how much time John spent lifting weights, we need to calculate the difference

$$
\dfrac{5}{4} - \dfrac{1}{2}
$$

To subtract two fractions with unlike denominators, we need to express each fraction as an equivalent fraction with a common denominator.

Since $4$ is a multiple of $2$, we can make a common denominator of $4$.

To put

$$
\dfrac{1}{2}
$$

over a denominator of $4$, we multiply the numerator and denominator by $2$:

$$
\dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{1 \times 2}{2 \times 2} = \dfrac{2}{4}
$$

We can now subtract the fractions. We keep the denominator the same and we subtract the numerators:

$$
\frac{5}{4} - \frac{2}{4} = \frac{3}{4}
$$

Therefore, John spent

$$
\dfrac{3}{4}
$$

of an hour lifting weights.

## Subtracting Fractions When Neither Denominator Is a Multiple of the Other

**Example:** Tara cuts a piece of fabric that is $\dfrac{9}{4}\,\textrm{yd}$ long into two smaller pieces. If one of the pieces is $\dfrac{15}{14}\,\textrm{yd}$ long, how long is the other piece? Express your final answer as a mixed number.

**Explanation**

To determine the length of the other piece, we need to calculate the difference

$$
\dfrac{9}{4} - \dfrac{15}{14}
$$

To subtract two fractions with unlike denominators, we need to express each fraction as an equivalent fraction with a common denominator.

Let's look at the denominators:

$$
\dfrac{9}{\color{red}4} - \dfrac{15}{\color{blue}14}
$$

The least common denominator of $\color{red}4$ and $\color{blue}14$ is $28$.

To put

$$
\dfrac{9}{4}
$$

over a denominator of $28$, we multiply the numerator and denominator by $7$:

$$
\dfrac{9}{4} = \dfrac{9 \times 7}{4 \times 7} = \dfrac{63}{28}
$$

To put

$$
\dfrac{15}{14}
$$

over a denominator of $28$, we multiply the numerator and denominator by $2$:

$$
\dfrac{15}{14} = \dfrac{15 \times 2}{14 \times 2} = \dfrac{30}{28}
$$

We can now subtract the fractions. We keep the denominator the same and subtract the numerators:

$$
\frac{63}{28} - \frac{30}{28} = \frac{33}{28}
$$

Converting this to a mixed number, we get

$$
\dfrac{33}{28} = 1\dfrac{5}{28}
$$

Therefore, Tara's other piece is

$$
1\dfrac{5}{28}\,\textrm{yd}
$$

long.
