## Introduction

We can use fraction models to subtract fractions from whole numbers.

For example, let's find the value of

$$
1 - \dfrac{1}{6}
$$

using the model below:

![](<../Source/3.1.3. Subtracting Fractions and Whole Numbers Using Models/Images/b3ed0e684818e6c815efb9606125067f.png>)

We cannot subtract the two numbers straight away because they are split into different numbers of parts (i.e., they have different denominators).

In order to subtract, we split the whole number ($1$ in this case) into $\color{red}6$ equal parts, as follows:

![](<../Source/3.1.3. Subtracting Fractions and Whole Numbers Using Models/Images/5ede9929ac641e3be9352e9ed8bb9a56.png>)

Now, both shapes are split into $\color{red}6$ equal parts, and we have

$$
6-1={\color{blue}{5}}
$$

parts in total. Therefore:

$$
1 - \dfrac{1}{6} = \dfrac{\color{blue}5}{\color{red}6}
$$

## Subtracting Fractions From Whole Numbers

**Example:** Use the model below to find the value of $2 - \dfrac{1}{3}$ as an improper fraction.

![](<../Source/3.1.3. Subtracting Fractions and Whole Numbers Using Models/Images/273e7da539b52aa841e3c4ffd79a4733.png>)

**Explanation**

All three shapes are already split into $\color{red}3$ equal parts, and we have

$$
3+3-1 = {\color{blue}{5}}
$$

parts in total.

Therefore,

$$
2 - \dfrac{1}{3} = \dfrac{{\color{blue}5}}{{\color{red}3}}
$$

## Splitting Whole Numbers Into Parts

**Example:** Use the model above to determine the missing number in the statement below.

![](<../Source/3.1.3. Subtracting Fractions and Whole Numbers Using Models/Images/f9f2fcf4adff700d2c82cc55f0e2f27b.png>)

$0$

**Explanation**

The shape on the right is split into $\color{red}5$ equal parts. We can split the first and second shapes into $\color{red}5$ equal parts as follows:

![](<../Source/3.1.3. Subtracting Fractions and Whole Numbers Using Models/Images/a1858e5b9d9e3f2114fa0cb452d43169.png>)

We have

$$
5 + 5 - 3=\color{blue}7
$$

shaded parts in total.

Therefore,

$$
2 - \dfrac{3}{5} = \dfrac{\color{blue}7}{5}
$$

So, the missing number is $\color{blue}7$.

## Solving a Subtraction Problem Using a Fraction Model

**Example:** Use a visual fraction model to find the value of $2 - \dfrac{1}{6}$ as an improper fraction.

**Explanation**

Writing

$$
2 - \dfrac{1}{6}
$$

as a visual fraction model, we get:

![](<../Source/3.1.3. Subtracting Fractions and Whole Numbers Using Models/Images/ed39340879c466186f7303bd77610d36.png>)

The shape on the right is split into $\color{red}6$ equal parts. We can split the other two shapes into $\color{red}6$ equal parts as follows:

![](<../Source/3.1.3. Subtracting Fractions and Whole Numbers Using Models/Images/c14eafe8ce4406a4c9efb72b7b380c00.png>)

We have

$$
6 + 6 - 1 = \color{blue}11
$$

shaded parts in total.

Therefore,

$$
2 - \dfrac{1}{6} = \dfrac{\color{blue}11}{\color{red}6}
$$
