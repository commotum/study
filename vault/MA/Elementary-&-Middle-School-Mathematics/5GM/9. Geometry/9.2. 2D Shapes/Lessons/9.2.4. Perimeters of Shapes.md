## Introduction

The **perimeter** of a polygon is the total distance around its boundary. So, to find the perimeter, we add the lengths of all the polygon's sides.

For example, let's find the perimeter of the triangle shown below.

![](<../Source/9.2.4. Perimeters of Shapes/Images/ba4075436f4982c28288204eac4eda3b.png>)

Therefore, the perimeter of the triangle is

$$

## Perimeters of Rhombuses and Squares

Recall the following properties of rhombuses and squares:

- A square is a quadrilateral with two pairs of parallel sides, where *all four sides are equal*, and all four angles are right angles.![](<../Source/9.2.4. Perimeters of Shapes/Images/5bf5f0b36a8fd7f0d3fb8f9f2bbacc61.png>)
- A rhombus is a quadrilateral with two pairs of parallel sides, where *all four sides are equal*.![](<../Source/9.2.4. Perimeters of Shapes/Images/fc6ae61486ddde2f8742e9df417be7dc.png>)

The special properties of these shapes allow us to find their perimeters even more easily than usual.

For example, the perimeter of a square with a side of length $7\:\text{m}$ is given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
Perimeter &= 4 \cdot (Side Length) \\
&= 4 \cdot 7 \\
&= 28m
\end{aligned}
$$

Let's see another example.

---

<a id="finding-the-perimeter-of-a-rhombus"></a>
## Finding the Perimeter of a Rhombus

**Example:** What is the perimeter of a rhombus with side length $9\:\text{cm}$?

**Explanation**

The perimeter of a polygon is the total distance around its boundary, so we add the lengths of all its sides.

Recall that a rhombus is a quadrilateral with two pairs of parallel sides, where all four sides are equal.

![](<../Source/9.2.4. Perimeters of Shapes/Images/f472afdf3f8ecb070eb13871f1746055.png>)

Therefore, the perimeter of the rhombus is

$$
\begin{aligned}
Perimeter &= 4 \cdot (Side Length) \\
&= 4 \cdot 9 \\
&= 36cm
\end{aligned}
$$

## Perimeters of Rectangles

Now, let's see how to find the perimeter of the rectangle shown below.

![](<../Source/9.2.4. Perimeters of Shapes/Images/c5344d0b911d65baede6cc32e11dc989.png>)

To find the perimeter, we add the lengths of all the rectangle's sides.

Recall that a rectangle is a quadrilateral with two pairs of equal parallel sides and four right angles.

![](<../Source/9.2.4. Perimeters of Shapes/Images/36d8710d444b47754ebf895dcee62aab.png>)

Therefore, the perimeter of the rectangle is

$$
\begin{aligned}
Perimeter &= 2 \cdot (Length + Height) \\
&= 2 \cdot (9 + 4) \\
&= 2 \cdot 13 \\
&= 26in
\end{aligned}
$$

---

<a id="finding-the-perimeter-of-a-rectangle"></a>
## Finding the Perimeter of a Rectangle

**Example:** What is the perimeter of a rectangle with a length of $14\:\text{ft}$ and a height of $9\:\text{ft}$?

**Explanation**

The perimeter of a polygon is the total distance around its boundary, so we add the lengths of all its sides.

Recall that a rectangle is a quadrilateral with two pairs of equal parallel sides and four right angles.

![](<../Source/9.2.4. Perimeters of Shapes/Images/8e7e5de378b698519a35f450820668da.png>)

Therefore, the perimeter of the rectangle is

$$
\begin{aligned}
Perimeter &= 2 \cdot (Length + Height) \\
&= 2 \cdot (14 + 9) \\
&= 2 \cdot 23 \\
&= 46ft
\end{aligned}
$$
