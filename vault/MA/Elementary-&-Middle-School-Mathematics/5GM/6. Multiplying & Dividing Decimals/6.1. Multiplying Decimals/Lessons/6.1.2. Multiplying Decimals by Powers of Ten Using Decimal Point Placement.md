## Introduction

To multiply a decimal by $10$, we shift the decimal point one space to the right.

To demonstrate, let's find the value of

$$
0.31 \times 10
$$

First, we write down our number. We can attach as many trailing zeros as we want to the decimal part.

![](<../Source/6.1.2. Multiplying Decimals by Powers of Ten Using Decimal Point Placement/Images/71f35cfe67b148ab963521af855bab67.png>)

Now, we count the zeros in $10$. Since $10$ contains $1$ zero, we move the decimal point $1$ place to the right:

![](<../Source/6.1.2. Multiplying Decimals by Powers of Ten Using Decimal Point Placement/Images/0e5f51eea07d58981b1c894049756878.png>)

This gives the following diagram:

![](<../Source/6.1.2. Multiplying Decimals by Powers of Ten Using Decimal Point Placement/Images/879eb5f03fd4a9d221be598d10ca6f27.png>)

We ignore the leading zeros in the whole number part and trailing zeros in the decimal part. This gives the following number:

$$
3.1
$$

Therefore:

$$
0.31 \times 10 = 3.1
$$

## Multiplying Numbers by Larger Powers of Ten

To multiply decimals by larger powers of ten, we shift the decimal point $n$ spaces to the right, where $n$ is the number of zeros in the power of ten.

To illustrate, let's compute the value of

$$
5.723 \times 100
$$

First, we write down our number. We can attach as many trailing zeros as we want to the decimal part.

![](<../Source/6.1.2. Multiplying Decimals by Powers of Ten Using Decimal Point Placement/Images/9c784664c8d074cf1e89a0b838991fb8.png>)

Now, we count the zeros in $100$. Since $100$ contains $2$ zeros, we move the decimal point $2$ places to the right:

![](<../Source/6.1.2. Multiplying Decimals by Powers of Ten Using Decimal Point Placement/Images/ddaad24c4d0b47143e43d89bfb24a583.png>)

This gives the following diagram:

![](<../Source/6.1.2. Multiplying Decimals by Powers of Ten Using Decimal Point Placement/Images/2eb3e2d5de90c06e7b5989e9af802bb5.png>)

We ignore the leading zeros in the whole number part and trailing zeros in the decimal part. This gives the following number:

$$
572.3
$$

Therefore:

$$
5.723 \times 100 = 572.3
$$
