## Introduction

A number's **significant figures** are the digits that contribute most to the number's magnitude. In other words, they are the digits that tell us (approximately) how large the number is.

We find a number's first few significant figures by looking at its *leftmost* part.

For example:

- To identify the first *two* significant figures of $2\,015$, we highlight the first *two* digits on the *leftmost* part of the number.
$20$
- To identify the first *four* significant figures of $45\,953$, we highlight the first *four* digits on the *leftmost* part of the number.
$4595$

It's important to remember that any number can be written with **leading zeros** included. We always ignore leading zeros because they do not contribute to the number's magnitude.

For example:

- The following two numbers are equivalent:
$000\,901\,220, \qquad 901\,220$
Ignoring the leading zeros in the first number, we find that the first three significant digits of the two numbers above are the same.
$901$
- It's important to ignore only *leading* zeros! For example, the first *three* significant figures of
$001\,000\,001$
are shown below:
$00\fbox{100}0001$

<a id="rounding-down-with-significant-figures"></a>
## Rounding Down With Significant Figures

We've already seen how to round numbers to the nearest ten, hundred, thousand, etc. We can also round numbers using significant figures.

For example, let's round the following number to *two* significant figures.

$$
42\,137
$$

We proceed as follows:

- **Step 1:** We separate the first *two* significant digits from the other digits:
$42\, \mid \, {\color{blue}1}37$
- **Step 2:** We inspect the digit to the bar's right. In this case, it is ${\color{blue}1}$, which is smaller than $5$. So, we do not change the digit to the bar's left.
- **Step 3:** Finally, we change digits to the bar's right to zeros:
$42\, \mid \,000$

Therefore, $42\,137$ rounded to *two* significant figures is $42\,000$.

---

<a id="rounding-a-number-down"></a>
## Rounding a Number Down

**Example:** Round the number $45\,843$ to three significant figures.

**Explanation**

Since we are rounding to *three* significant figures, we separate the first *three* digits from the left from the other digits:

$$
45\,{\color{black}8} \, \mid \, {\color{blue}4}3
$$

The digit to the bar's right is ${\color{blue}4}$, which is smaller than $5$. So, we do not change the digit to the bar's left.

$$
45\,{\color{black}8} \, \mid \, {\color{blue}4}3
$$

Finally, we change all of the digits on the bar's right to zeros:

$$
45\,8\, \mid \,00
$$

Therefore, $45\,843$ rounded to three significant figures is $45\,800$.

<a id="rounding-up-with-significant-figures"></a>
## Rounding Up With Significant Figures

In cases where the digit to the bar's right is greater than or equal to $5$, we should round *up*.

For example, let's round the following number to *two* significant figures:

$$
86\,613
$$

We proceed as follows:

- **Step 1**: We separate the first *two* significant digits from the other digits:
$8{\color{red}6}\, \mid \,{\color{blue}6}13$
- **Step 2**: We inspect the digit to the bar's right. In this case, it is ${\color{blue}6}$, which is larger than (or equal to) $5$. So, we increase the digit to the bar's left by one. Since the digit to bar's left is ${\color{red}6}$, it changes to ${\color{red}7}{:}$
$8{\color{red}7}\, \mid \,{\color{blue}6}13$
- **Step 3**: Finally, we change all of the digits on the bar's right to zeros:
$8{\color{red}7}\, \mid \,000$

Therefore, $86\,613$ rounded to *two* significant figures is $87\,000$.

<a id="rounding-a-number-up"></a>
## Rounding a Number Up

**Example:** Round the number $1\,047\,850$ to four significant figures.

**Explanation**

Since we are rounding to *four* significant figures, we separate the first *four* digits from the left from the other digits:

$$
1\,04{\color{red}7} \, \mid \, {\color{blue}8}50
$$

The digit to the bar's right is ${\color{blue}8}$, which is larger than (or equal to) $5$. So, we increase the digit to the bar's left by one. Since the digit to bar's left is ${\color{red}7}$, it changes to ${\color{red}8}{:}$

$$
1\,04{\color{red}8} \, \mid \, {\color{blue}8}50
$$

Finally, we change all of the digits on the bar's right to zeros:

$$
1\,048\, \mid \, 000
$$

Therefore, $1\,047\,850$ rounded to four significant figures is $1\,048\,000$.
