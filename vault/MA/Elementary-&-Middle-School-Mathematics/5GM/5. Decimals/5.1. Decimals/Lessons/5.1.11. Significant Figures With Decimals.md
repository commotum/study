## Introduction

Similar to whole numbers, the significant figures of a decimal are the digits that contribute most to the decimal's magnitude.

We find a decimal's first few significant figures by looking at its *leftmost* part, ignoring any leading zeros.

For example:

- To identify the first *two* significant figures of $3.265\,8$, we highlight the first *two* digits on the leftmost part of the number.
$3.2$
- To identify the first *four* significant figures of $1.002\,45$, we highlight the first *four* digits on the leftmost part of the number.
$1.002$
- To identify the first *three* significant figures of $0.000\,126\,7$, we highlight the first *three* digits on the leftmost part of the number, ignoring any leading zeros.
$126$

Let's see some more examples.

## Rounding With Significant Figures

We can round decimals to a given number of significant figures.

For example, let's round the following number to *two* significant figures.

$$
2.714
$$

We proceed as follows:

- **Step 1:** Since we are rounding to *two* significant figures, we separate the first *two* significant digits on the left from the others:
$2.7 \, \mid \, {\color{blue}1}4$
- **Step 2:** We inspect the digit to the bar's right. In this case, it is ${\color{blue}1}$, which is smaller than $5$. So, we do not change the digit to the bar's left.
- **Step 3:** Finally, we change digits to the bar's right to zeros:
$2.7\, \mid \,00$
This gives the number $2.700$, which is equivalent to $2.7$.

Therefore, $2.714$ rounded to two significant figures is $2.7$.

Let's now look at an example where we need to round *up*.

<a id="rounding-a-decimal-with-a-whole-number-part"></a>
## Rounding a Decimal With a Whole Number Part

**Example:** Round the number $45.363$ to three significant figures.

**Explanation**

Notice that our number has a whole number part and a decimal part.

Since we are rounding to *three* significant figures, we separate the first *three* digits on the left from the others:

$$
45.{\color{red}3} \, \mid \, {\color{blue}6}3
$$

The digit to the bar's right is ${\color{blue}6}$, which is greater than (or equal to) $5$. So, we increase the digit to the bar's left by one. Since the digit to bar's left is ${\color{red}3}$, it changes to ${\color{red}4}{:}$

$$
45.{\color{red}4} \, \mid \, {\color{blue}6}3
$$

Finally, we remove all digits to the bar's right:

$$
45.4 \, \mid \,
$$

Therefore, $45.363$ rounded to three significant figures is $45.4$.

<a id="rounding-a-decimal-with-no-whole-number-part"></a>
## Rounding a Decimal With No Whole Number Part

**Example:** What is $0.023\,94$ rounded to three significant figures?

**Explanation**

First, we ignore all the leading zeros:

$$
{\color{lightgray}0.0}23\,94
$$

Since we are rounding to *three* significant figures, we separate the first *three* nonzero digits from the left from the other digits:

$$
{\color{lightgray}0.0}23\,9 \, \mid \, {\color{blue}4}
$$

The digit to the bar's right is ${\color{blue}4}$, which is smaller than $5$. So, we do not change the digit to the bar's left.

Finally, we remove all digits to the right of the bar:

$$
{\color{lightgray}0.0}23\,9 \, \mid \,
$$

Therefore, $0.023 \, 94$ rounded to three significant figures is $0.023\,9$.

<a id="rounding-the-digit-nine"></a>
## Rounding the Digit Nine

Let's round the following number to *two* significant figures.

$$
7\,961
$$

Since we are rounding to *two* significant figures, we separate the first *two* digits on the left from the others:

$$
{\color{red}7}\,{\color{purple}9} \, \mid \, {\color{blue}6}1
$$

The digit to the bar's right is ${\color{blue}6}$. So, we increase the digit to the bar's left by one. Since the digit to the bar's left is ${\color{purple}9}$, we turn it into ${\color{purple}0}$ and add one to the digit left of it. So, ${\color{red}7}$ changes to ${\color{red}8}{:}$

$$
{\color{red}8}\,{\color{purple}0} \, \mid \, {\color{blue}6}1
$$

Finally, we change all of the digits on the bar's right to zeros:
$8\,0 \, \mid \, 00$

Therefore, $7\,961$ rounded to two significant figures is $8\,000$.

## Rounding a Number With The Digit Nine

**Example:** What is $14.963$ rounded to three significant figures?

**Explanation**

Notice that our number has a whole number part and a decimal part.

Since we are rounding to *three* significant figures, we separate the first *three* digits from the left from the other digits:
$1 {\color{red}4}.{\color{purple}9} \, \mid \, {\color{blue}6}3$

The digit to the bar's right is ${\color{blue}6}$. So, we increase the digit to the bar's left by one. Since the digit to the bar's left is ${\color{purple}9}$, we turn it into ${\color{purple}0}$ and add one to the digit left of it. So, ${\color{red}4}$ changes to ${\color{red}5}{:}$
$1 {\color{red}5}.{\color{purple}0} \, \mid \, {\color{blue}6} 3$

Finally, we remove all digits to the bar's right:
$15.0 \, \mid \,$

Therefore, $14.963$ rounded to three significant figures is $15.0$.
