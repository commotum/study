## Introduction

A decimal number is in expanded form when it is written as the sum of place values. For instance, the following number is in expanded form:

$$
3 + 0.5
$$

Suppose that we need to convert the given number from expanded form to standard form. To do this, notice that:

$\qquad$ $3$ is $3$ ones.

$\qquad$ $0.5$ is $5$ tenths.

We can put this information into a place value chart. By doing this, we get:

| **ones** |  | **tenths** |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| $3$ | **.** | $5$ |

Therefore, the standard form representation of our decimal is $3.5$.

<a id="converting-from-decimal-expanded-form-to-standard-form"></a>
## Converting From Decimal Expanded Form to Standard Form

**Example:** Which number is equivalent to the expanded form below?

$2\times 10 + 4\times 1 + 1\times 0.1 + 8\times 0.01$

**Explanation**

We need to convert the given number from expanded form to standard form. First, notice that:

$\qquad$ $2\times 10$ is $2$ tens.

$\qquad$ $4\times 1$ is $4$ ones.

$\qquad$ $1\times 0.1$ is $1$ tenth.

$\qquad$ $8\times 0.01$ is $8$ hundredths.

Putting this into a place value chart, we get:

| **tens** | **ones** |  | **tenths** | **hundredths** |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $2$ | $4$ | **.** | $1$ | $8$ |

Therefore, the given expanded form is equivalent to $24.18$.

## Converting From Fractional Expanded Form to Standard Form

**Example:** Which number is equivalent to the expanded form below?

$(5\times 10) + (3\times 1) + \left(8\times\dfrac{1}{10}\right)+\left(7\times\dfrac{1}{100}\right)$

**Explanation**

We need to convert the given number from expanded form to standard form. First, notice that:

$\qquad$ $5\times 10$ is $5$ tens.

$\qquad$ $3\times 1$ is $3$ ones.

$\qquad$

$$
8\times\dfrac{1}{10}
$$

is $8$ tenths.

$\qquad$

$$
7\times\dfrac{1}{100}
$$

is $7$ hundredths.

Putting this into the place value chart, we get:

| **tens** | **ones** |  | **tenths** | **hundredths** |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $5$ | $3$ | **.** | $8$ | $7$ |

Therefore, the given expanded form is equivalent to $53.87$.

<a id="converting-from-expanded-form-to-standard-form-with-missing-place-values"></a>
## Converting From Expanded Form To Standard Form With Missing Place Values

In the expanded form

$$
(2\times 1) + \left(5\times\dfrac{1}{10}\right)+\left(7\times\dfrac{1}{1,000}\right)
$$

there is no place value for the hundredths place. We have:

$\qquad$ $2\times 1$ is $2$ ones.

$\qquad$

$$
5\times\dfrac{1}{10}
$$

is $5$ tenths.

$\qquad$

$$
7\times\dfrac{1}{1,000}
$$

is $7$ thousandths.

Since there are no hundredths in the given expression, we say we have $\color{blue}0$ hundredths.

Putting this into the place value chart, we get:

| **ones** |  | **tenths** | **hundredths** | **thousandths** |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $2$ | **.** | $5$ | $\color{blue}0$ | $7$ |

Therefore, the given expanded form is equivalent to $2.5{\color{blue}0}7$.

<a id="converting-from-decimal-expanded-form-to-standard-form-with-missing-place-values"></a>
## Converting From Decimal Expanded Form to Standard Form With Missing Place Values

**Example:** What are the missing digits in the following equality?

$0$

**Explanation**

We need to convert the given number from expanded form to standard form. First, notice that:

$\qquad$ $70$ is $7$ tens.

$\qquad$ $8$ is $8$ ones.

$\qquad$ $0.04$ is $4$ hundredths.

$\qquad$ $0.002$ is $2$ thousandths.

There are no tenths in the given expression which means that we have $0$ tenths.

Putting this into the place value chart, we get:

| **tens** | **ones** |  | **tenths** | **hundredths** | **thousandths** |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $7$ | $8$ | **.** | $\color{blue}0$ | $4$ | $\color{blue}2$ |

Therefore, the resulting number is $78.042$ and the missing digits are $0$ and $2$.

## Converting From Fractional Expanded Form to Standard Form With Missing Place Values

**Example:** Which number is equivalent to the expanded form below?

$(5\times 1,000) + \dfrac{1}{1,000}$

**Explanation**

We need to convert the given number from expanded form to standard form. We notice that:

$\qquad$ $5\times 1,000$ is $5$ thousands.

$\qquad$

$$
\dfrac{1}{1,000}
$$

is $1$ thousandth.

There are no hundreds, tens, ones, tenths and hundredths in the given expression, which means that we have $0$ hundreds, $0$ tens, $0$ ones, $0$ tenths, and $0$ hundredths.
Putting this into the place value chart, we get:

| **thousands** | **hundreds** | **tens** | **ones** |  | **tenths** | **hundredths** | **thousandths** |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $5$ | $0$ | $0$ | $0$ | **.** | $0$ | $0$ | $1$ |

Therefore, the given expanded form is equivalent to $5,000.001$.
