## Introduction

Suppose we want to subtract two numbers with different numbers of decimal places, such as

$$
4.7-3.16 \,
$$

Here, there is only $\color{blue}1$ decimal place in $4.7$, while there are $\color{blue}2$ decimal places in $3.16$.

In order to subtract the two numbers, we need the two numbers to have the same number of decimal places. We can achieve this by attaching an extra zero behind the last digit in the first number, so that it has $\color{blue}2$ decimal places:

$$
4.7=4.7{\color{red}0}
$$

Now, we line up the decimal points:
$4\begin{vmatrix}. & 7 & 0 \\ - & 3 &.\end{vmatrix}1 \mid 6;$.

Next, we proceed by subtracting the two numbers just as we would with whole numbers:
$6\begin{vmatrix}10 \\ 4\end{vmatrix}.\begin{vmatrix}7 & 0 \\ - & 3\end{vmatrix}.\begin{vmatrix}1 & 6 \\ 1 &.\end{vmatrix}5 \mid 4$

Therefore,

$$
4.7-3.16 = 1.54\,
$$

## Subtracting Numbers With a Difference of One Decimal Place

**Example:** What is the value of $5.98-0.432$?

**Explanation**

There are $\color{blue}2$ decimal places in $5.98$ and there are $\color{blue}3$ decimal places in $0.432$.

To subtract the numbers, they both need the same number of decimals. Therefore, we rewrite the first number so that it has $\color{blue}3$ decimal places:

$$
5.98=5.98{\color{red}0}
$$

Now, we line up the decimal points:
$5\begin{vmatrix}. & 9 & 8 & 0 \\ - & 0 &. & 4\end{vmatrix}3 \mid 2;$.

Next, we proceed by subtracting the two numbers just as we would with whole numbers:
$7\begin{vmatrix}10 \\ 5\end{vmatrix}.\begin{vmatrix}9 & 8 & 0 \\ - & 0 &.\end{vmatrix}4\begin{vmatrix}3 & 2 \\ 5 &.\end{vmatrix}5 \mid 4 \mid 8$

Therefore,

$$
5.98-0.432= 5.548\,
$$

## Subtracting Numbers With a Difference of More Than One Decimal Place

**Example:** Find the value of $9.4-0.254$.

**Explanation**

There are is $\color{blue}1$ decimal place in $9.4$ and there are $\color{blue}3$ decimal places in $0.254$.

To subtract the numbers, they both need the same number of decimals. Therefore, we rewrite the first number so that it has $\color{blue}3$ decimal places:

$$
9.4 = 9.4{\color{red}00}
$$

Now, we line up the decimal points:
$9\begin{vmatrix}. & 4 & 0 & 0 \\ - & 0 &. & 2\end{vmatrix}5 \mid 4;$.

Next, we proceed by subtracting the two numbers just as we would with whole numbers:
$3\begin{vmatrix}9 & 10 \\ 9 &.\end{vmatrix}4\begin{vmatrix}0 & 0 \\ - & 0\end{vmatrix}.\begin{vmatrix}2 & 5 & 4 \\ 9 &. & 1\end{vmatrix}4 \mid 6$

Therefore,

$$
9.4-0.254=9.146\,
$$

## Subtracting a Decimal From a Whole Number

To subtract a decimal from a whole number, we convert the whole number to an equivalent decimal so that both numbers have the same number of decimal places.

For example, to calculate $4-0.4$, first notice that $4$ has no decimal places and there is $\color{blue}1$ decimal place in $0.4$.

To subtract the two numbers, we need them to have the same number of decimal places. So, we rewrite the first number so that it has $\color{blue}1$ decimal place:

$$
1
$$

Now, we subtract as normal, lining up the decimal points:

$$
4\begin{vmatrix}. & 0 \\ - & 0\end{vmatrix}. \mid 4;
$$

Next, we proceed by subtracting the two numbers just as we would with whole numbers:

$$
3\begin{vmatrix}10 \\ 4\end{vmatrix}.\begin{vmatrix}0 \\ -\end{vmatrix}0\begin{vmatrix}. & 4 \\ 3 &.\end{vmatrix}6
$$

Therefore,

$$
4-0.4 = 3.6\,
$$

<a id="subtracting-a-decimal-from-a-whole-number"></a>
## Subtracting a Decimal From a Whole Number

**Example:** What is $1-0.56$?

**Explanation**

There are no decimal places in $1$ and there are $\color{blue}2$ decimal places in $0.56$.

To subtract the numbers, they both need the same number of decimals. Therefore, we rewrite the first number so that it has $\color{blue}2$ decimal places.
$2$

Now, we line up the decimal points:
$1\begin{vmatrix}. & 0 & 0 \\ - & 0 &.\end{vmatrix}5 \mid 6;$.

Next, we proceed by subtracting the two numbers just as we would with whole numbers:
$0\begin{vmatrix}9 & 10 \\ 1 &.\end{vmatrix}0\begin{vmatrix}0 \\ -\end{vmatrix}0\begin{vmatrix}. & 5 & 6 \\ 0 &. & 4\end{vmatrix}4$

Therefore,

$$
1-0.56 = 0.44\,
$$

## Subtracting a Decimal From a Whole Number: Word Problems

**Example:** Carol bought $3$ yards of polyester fabric to make two shirts. She cut the fabric into two pieces and found that one piece was $1.4$ yards long. How long was the other piece of fabric?

**Explanation**

To find the length of the second piece of fabric, we need to calculate the difference $3 - 1.4$.

There are $\color{blue}0$ decimal places in $3$ and there is $\color{blue}1$ decimal place in $1.4$.

To subtract the numbers, both need to have the same number of decimals. Hence, we rewrite the first number so that it has $\color{blue}1$ decimal place:
$1$

First, we line up the decimal points:
$3\begin{vmatrix}. & 0 \\ - & 1\end{vmatrix}. \mid 4;$.

Next, we proceed by subtracting the two numbers just as we would with whole numbers:
$2\begin{vmatrix}10 \\ 3\end{vmatrix}.\begin{vmatrix}0 \\ -\end{vmatrix}1\begin{vmatrix}. & 4 \\ 1 &.\end{vmatrix}6$

Therefore,

$$
3 - 1.4 = 1.6
$$

and we conclude that the second piece was $1.6$ yards long.
