## Introduction

We can use the standard algorithm to multiply a three-digit number by a one-digit number. In this case, we might need to carry a digit from the multiplication of the ones *and* tens.

For instance, let's find the value of

$$
143 \times 7
$$

We start by writing the problem out as follows:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}1 & 4 & 3 \\ \cdot & 7 & 10and2 + 10 = 1200000\end{bmatrix}
$$

First, we multiply the ones. We have

$$
3\times 7 = {\color{blue}2}{\color{red}1}
$$

We write $\color{red}1$ in the ones place and carry the ${\color{blue}2}$.

$$
_(2); 1 \mid 4\begin{vmatrix}3 \\ \cdot\end{vmatrix}7; 1 \mid 3 \cdot 7 = 2100000000000000000; Carry:2; Write:1
$$

Next, we multiply the tens as follows:

- We have $4\times 7= 28$.
- We add the $2$ carried from the previous multiplication to get $2+28 = {\color{blue}3}{\color{red}0}$.
- We write down $\color{red}0$ and carry ${\color{blue}3}$.

$$
_(3)\begin{vmatrix}_(2) \\ 1\end{vmatrix}4\begin{vmatrix}3 \\ \cdot\end{vmatrix}7; 0 \mid 1 \mid 4 \cdot 7 = 28and2 + 28 = 30; Carry:3; Write:0
$$

Finally, we multiply the hundreds as follows:

- We have $1\times 7 = 7$.
- We add the $3$ carried from the previous multiplication to get $3+7= 10$.
- Since this is the final multiplication, we write the entire result, ${\color{red}10}$.

$$
_(3)\begin{vmatrix}_(2) \\ 1\end{vmatrix}4\begin{vmatrix}3 \\ \cdot\end{vmatrix}7; 1\begin{vmatrix}0 & 0 & 1\end{vmatrix}1 \cdot 7 = 7and3 + 7 = 10; Carry:-; Write:10
$$

Therefore,

$$
143 \times 7 = 1,001
$$

## Multiplying a Three-Digit Number By a One-Digit Number: Finding Missing Digits

**Example:** From left to right, what are the missing digits in the following multiplication problem?

$4\begin{vmatrix}7 & 9 \\ \cdot & 6 \\ 2 & [math]\phantom{i}[/math]\end{vmatrix}[math]\phantom{i}[/math] \mid 4$

**Explanation**

First, we multiply the ones:

$$
_(5); 4 \mid 7\begin{vmatrix}9 \\ \cdot\end{vmatrix}6; 4 \mid 9 \cdot 6 = 54; Carry:5; Write:4
$$

Next, we multiply the tens:

$$
_(4)\begin{vmatrix}_(5) \\ 4\end{vmatrix}7\begin{vmatrix}9 \\ \cdot\end{vmatrix}6; 7 \mid 4 \mid 7 \cdot 6 = 42and5 + 42 = 47; Carry:4; Write:7
$$

Finally, we multiply the hundreds:

$$
_(4)\begin{vmatrix}_(5) \\ 4\end{vmatrix}7\begin{vmatrix}9 \\ \cdot\end{vmatrix}6; 2\begin{vmatrix}[math]\color{red}8[/math] & 7 & 4\end{vmatrix}4 \cdot 6 = 24and4 + 24 = 28; Carry:-; Write:28
$$

Therefore, the missing digits, from left-to-right, are $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}8}$ and $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}7}$.

## Multiplying a Three-Digit Number By a One-Digit Number

**Example:** Find the value of $727 \times 6$.

**Explanation**

We write it this way:

$$
7 \mid 2\begin{vmatrix}7 \\ \cdot\end{vmatrix}6
$$

First, we multiply the ones:

$$
_(4); 7 \mid 2\begin{vmatrix}7 \\ \cdot\end{vmatrix}6; 2 \mid 7 \cdot 6 = 42; Carry:4; Write:2
$$

Next, we multiply the tens:

$$
_(1)\begin{vmatrix}_(4) \\ 7\end{vmatrix}2\begin{vmatrix}7 \\ \cdot\end{vmatrix}6; 6 \mid 2 \mid 2 \cdot 6 = 12and4 + 12 = 16; Carry:1; Write:6
$$

Finally, we multiply the hundreds:

$$
_(1)\begin{vmatrix}_(4) \\ 7\end{vmatrix}2\begin{vmatrix}7 \\ \cdot\end{vmatrix}6; 4\begin{vmatrix}3 & 6 & 2\end{vmatrix}7 \cdot 6 = 42and1 + 42 = 43; Carry:-; Write:43
$$

Therefore,

$$
727 \times 6 = 4 \, 362
$$

## Multiplying a Four-Digit Number By a One-Digit Number: Finding Missing Digits

**Example:** From left to right, what are the missing digits in the following multiplication problem?

$1\begin{vmatrix}8 & 5\end{vmatrix}2; \cdot\begin{vmatrix}7 \\ 1\end{vmatrix}[math]\phantom{i}[/math]\begin{vmatrix}9 & [math]\phantom{i}[/math]\end{vmatrix}4$

**Explanation**

First, we multiply the ones:

$$
_(1); 1\begin{vmatrix}8 & 5\end{vmatrix}2; \cdot\begin{vmatrix}7 \\ 4\end{vmatrix}2 \cdot 7 = 14; Carry:1; Write:4
$$

Next, we multiply the tens:

$$
_(3)\begin{vmatrix}_(1) \\ 1\end{vmatrix}8\begin{vmatrix}5 & 2 \\ \cdot & 7 \\ 6 & 4\end{vmatrix}5 \cdot 7 = 35and1 + 35 = 36; Carry:3; Write:6
$$

Next, we multiply the hundreds:

$$
_(5)\begin{vmatrix}_(3) & _(1) \\ 1 & 8\end{vmatrix}5\begin{vmatrix}2 \\ \cdot\end{vmatrix}7; 9\begin{vmatrix}6 & 4\end{vmatrix}8 \cdot 7 = 56and3 + 56 = 59; Carry:5; Write:9
$$

Finally, we multiply the thousands:

$$
_(5)\begin{vmatrix}_(3) & _(1) \\ 1 & 8\end{vmatrix}5\begin{vmatrix}2 \\ \cdot\end{vmatrix}7; 1\begin{vmatrix}[math]\color{red}2[/math] & 9 & 6 & 4\end{vmatrix}1 \cdot 7 = 7and5 + 7 = 12; Carry:-; Write:12
$$

Therefore, the missing digits, from left-to-right, are $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}2}$ and $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}6}$.

## Multiplying a Four-Digit Number By a One-Digit Number

**Example:** A bag of acorn seeds contains $2,162$ seeds in total. Mary purchases $4$ bags. How many seeds does she have in total?

**Explanation**

To calculate the total number of seeds, we need to multiply $2,162$ by $4$:

$$
2\begin{vmatrix}1 & 6\end{vmatrix}2; \cdot \mid 4 \mid 24and1 + 24 = 250000
$$

First, we multiply the ones. Notice that there is no digit to carry.

$$
_(); 2\begin{vmatrix}1 & 6\end{vmatrix}2; \cdot\begin{vmatrix}4 \\ 8\end{vmatrix}2 \cdot 4 = 80000000000000000; Carry: -; Write:8
$$

Next, we multiply the tens. This time there is a digit to carry, which we should place above the hundreds column.

$$
_(2)\begin{vmatrix}_() \\ 2\end{vmatrix}1\begin{vmatrix}6 & 2 \\ \cdot & 4 \\ 4 & 8\end{vmatrix}6 \cdot 4 = 240000000000000000; Carry:2; Write:4
$$

Next, we multiply the hundreds:

$$
_(2); 2\begin{vmatrix}1 & 6\end{vmatrix}2; \cdot\begin{vmatrix}4 \\ 6\end{vmatrix}4 \mid 8 \mid 1 \cdot 4 = 4and2 + 4 = 6; Carry: -; Write:6
$$

Finally, we multiply the thousands:

$$
_(2); 2\begin{vmatrix}1 & 6\end{vmatrix}2; \cdot\begin{vmatrix}4 \\ 8\end{vmatrix}6\begin{vmatrix}4 & 8\end{vmatrix}2 \cdot 4 = 80000000000000000; Carry:-; Write:8
$$

Therefore,

$$
2,162 \times 4 = 8, 648
$$
