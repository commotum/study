## Introduction

We can use the standard algorithm when we want to multiply a three-digit number by a two-digit number.

As an example, let's find $706 \times 25$. As always, we write the problem down in the following way:

$$
7\begin{vmatrix}0 & 6 \\ \cdot & 0\end{vmatrix}0\begin{vmatrix}0 & 2 & 5\end{vmatrix}3, 530 + 14, 120 = 17, 650
$$

First, we multiply the top number ($706$) by the ones digit of the second ($\bbox[2px, lightgray]{5}$).

$$
[math]\color{blue}\phantom{0}[/math]\begin{vmatrix}[math]\color{blue}3[/math] \\ 7\end{vmatrix}0\begin{vmatrix}6 \\ \cdot\end{vmatrix}0\begin{vmatrix}0 & 0 & 2 & 5 \\ 3 & 5 & 3 & 0\end{vmatrix}706 \cdot 5 = 3, 530000000
$$

Next, we multiply top number ($706$) by $\bbox[2px, lightgray]{2}0$. Let's do this in steps:

**Step 1**: We add a zero underneath the result of the first calculation, in the ones place:

$$
[math]\color{blue}\phantom{0}[/math]\begin{vmatrix}[math]\color{blue}3[/math] \\ 7\end{vmatrix}0\begin{vmatrix}6 \\ \cdot\end{vmatrix}0\begin{vmatrix}0 & 0 & 2 & 5 \\ 3 & 5 & 3 & 0 \\ 1 & 4 & 1 & 2\end{vmatrix}0 \mid 706 \cdot 20 = 14, 1200000
$$

**Step 2**: We continue to work out $706\times \bbox[2px, lightgray]{2}0$, proceeding as usual:

$$
[math]\color{blue}\phantom{0}[/math]; [math]\color{blue}\phantom{0}[/math]\begin{vmatrix}[math]\color{blue}1[/math] \\ [math]\color{blue}3[/math] \\ 7\end{vmatrix}0\begin{vmatrix}6 \\ \cdot\end{vmatrix}0\begin{vmatrix}0 & 0 & 2 & 5 \\ 3 & 5 & 3 & 0 \\ 1 & 4 & 1 & 2\end{vmatrix}0 \mid 706 \cdot 20 = 14, 1200000
$$

Finally, we add the results:

$$
[math]\color{blue}\phantom{0}[/math]; [math]\color{blue}\phantom{0}[/math]\begin{vmatrix}[math]\color{blue}1[/math] \\ [math]\color{blue}3[/math] \\ 7\end{vmatrix}0\begin{vmatrix}6 \\ \cdot\end{vmatrix}0\begin{vmatrix}0 & 0 & 2 & 5 \\ 3 & 5 & 3 & 0 \\ + & 1 & 4 & 1\end{vmatrix}2\begin{vmatrix}0 \\ 1\end{vmatrix}7\begin{vmatrix}6 & 5 & 0\end{vmatrix}3, 530 + 14, 120 = 17, 650
$$

Therefore,

$$
706 \times 25 = 17, 650
$$

## Multiplying Three-Digit Numbers by Two-Digit Numbers

**Example:** What is $268$ multiplied by $54$?

**Explanation**

We write it this way:

$$
2\begin{vmatrix}6 & 8 \\ \cdot & 0\end{vmatrix}0\begin{vmatrix}0 & 5 & 4\end{vmatrix}1, 072 + 13, 400 = 14, 472
$$

First, we multiply the ones:

$$
[math]\color{blue}2[/math]\begin{vmatrix}[math]\color{blue}3[/math] \\ 2\end{vmatrix}6\begin{vmatrix}8 \\ \cdot\end{vmatrix}0\begin{vmatrix}0 & 0 & 5 & 4 \\ 1 & 0 & 7 & 2\end{vmatrix}268 \cdot 4 = 1, 0720000000
$$

Next, we multiply the tens:

$$
[math]\color{blue}3[/math]; [math]\color{blue}2[/math]\begin{vmatrix}[math]\color{blue}4[/math] \\ [math]\color{blue}3[/math] \\ 2\end{vmatrix}6\begin{vmatrix}8 \\ \cdot\end{vmatrix}0\begin{vmatrix}0 & 0 & 5 & 4 \\ 1 & 0 & 7 & 2 \\ 1 & 3 & 4 & 0\end{vmatrix}0 \mid 268 \cdot 50 = 13, 4000000
$$

Finally, we add the results:

$$
[math]\color{blue}3[/math]; [math]\color{blue}2[/math]\begin{vmatrix}[math]\color{blue}4[/math] \\ [math]\color{blue}3[/math] \\ 2\end{vmatrix}6\begin{vmatrix}8 \\ \cdot\end{vmatrix}0\begin{vmatrix}0 & 0 & 5 & 4 \\ 1 & 0 & 7 & 2 \\ + & 1 & 3 & 4\end{vmatrix}0\begin{vmatrix}0 \\ 1\end{vmatrix}4\begin{vmatrix}4 & 7 & 2\end{vmatrix}1, 072 + 13, 400 = 14, 472
$$

Therefore,

$$
268 \times 54 = 14, 472
$$

## Multiplying Larger Numbers by Two-Digit Numbers

There is no limit to the size of the numbers that we can multiply using the standard algorithm.

For example, to calculate $6,132 \times 54$, we write it this way:

$$
6\begin{vmatrix}1 & 3 & 2 \\ \cdot & 0 & 0\end{vmatrix}0\begin{vmatrix}0 & 5 & 4\end{vmatrix}4, 859 + 97, 180 = 102, 039
$$

First, we multiply the ones:

$$
[math]\color{blue}\phantom{0}[/math]\begin{vmatrix}[math]\color{blue}1[/math] & [math]\color{blue}\phantom{0}[/math] \\ 6 & 1\end{vmatrix}3\begin{vmatrix}2 \\ \cdot\end{vmatrix}0\begin{vmatrix}0 & 0 & 0 & 5 & 4 \\ 2 & 4 & 5 & 2 & 8\end{vmatrix}6, 132 \cdot 4 = 24, 5280000000
$$

Next, we multiply the tens:

$$
[math]\color{blue}\phantom{0}[/math]; [math]\color{blue}\phantom{0}[/math]\begin{vmatrix}[math]\color{blue}1[/math] \\ [math]\color{blue}1[/math]\end{vmatrix}[math]\color{blue}1[/math]; [math]\color{blue}\phantom{0}[/math]; 6\begin{vmatrix}1 & 3 & 2 \\ \cdot & 0 & 0\end{vmatrix}0\begin{vmatrix}0 & 5 & 4 \\ 2 & 4 & 5\end{vmatrix}2\begin{vmatrix}8 \\ 3\end{vmatrix}0\begin{vmatrix}6 & 6 & 0 & 0\end{vmatrix}6, 132 \cdot 50 = 306, 60000000
$$

Finally, we add the results:

$$
[math]\color{blue}\phantom{0}[/math]; [math]\color{blue}\phantom{0}[/math]\begin{vmatrix}[math]\color{blue}1[/math] \\ [math]\color{blue}1[/math]\end{vmatrix}[math]\color{blue}1[/math]; [math]\color{blue}\phantom{0}[/math]; 6\begin{vmatrix}1 & 3 & 2 \\ \cdot & 0 & 0\end{vmatrix}0\begin{vmatrix}0 & 5 & 4 \\ 2 & 4 & 5\end{vmatrix}2\begin{vmatrix}8 \\ 3\end{vmatrix}0\begin{vmatrix}6 & 6 & 0 & 0 \\ 3 & 3 & 1 & 1\end{vmatrix}2 \mid 8 \mid 24, 528 + 306, 600 = 331, 128
$$

Therefore,

$$
6,132 \times 54 = 331, 128
$$

## Multiplying Four-Digit Numbers by Two-Digit Numbers

**Example:** Find the value of $2,324\times 46$.

**Explanation**

We write it this way:

$$
2\begin{vmatrix}3 & 2 & 4 \\ \cdot & 0 & 0\end{vmatrix}0\begin{vmatrix}0 & 4\end{vmatrix}6
$$

First, we multiply the ones:

$$
[math]\color{blue}1[/math]\begin{vmatrix}[math]\color{blue}1[/math] & [math]\color{blue}2[/math] \\ 2 & 3\end{vmatrix}2\begin{vmatrix}4 \\ \cdot\end{vmatrix}0\begin{vmatrix}0 & 0 & 0 & 4 & 6 \\ 1 & 3 & 9 & 4 & 4\end{vmatrix}2, 324 \cdot 6 = 13, 944
$$

Next, we multiply the tens:

$$
[math]\color{blue}1[/math]; [math]\color{blue}1[/math]\begin{vmatrix}[math]\color{blue}\phantom{0}[/math] \\ [math]\color{blue}1[/math]\end{vmatrix}[math]\color{blue}1[/math]; [math]\color{blue}2[/math]; 2\begin{vmatrix}3 & 2 & 4 \\ \cdot & 0 & 0\end{vmatrix}0\begin{vmatrix}0 & 4 & 6 \\ 1 & 3 & 9\end{vmatrix}4\begin{vmatrix}4 \\ 9\end{vmatrix}2\begin{vmatrix}9 & 6 & 0\end{vmatrix}2, 324 \cdot 40 = 92, 960
$$

Finally, we add the results:

$$
[math]\color{blue}1[/math]; [math]\color{blue}1[/math]\begin{vmatrix}[math]\color{blue}\phantom{0}[/math] \\ [math]\color{blue}1[/math]\end{vmatrix}[math]\color{blue}1[/math]; [math]\color{blue}2[/math]; 2\begin{vmatrix}3 & 2 & 4 \\ \cdot & 0 & 0\end{vmatrix}0\begin{vmatrix}0 & 4 & 6 \\ 1 & 3 & 9\end{vmatrix}4\begin{vmatrix}4 \\ +\end{vmatrix}9\begin{vmatrix}2 & 9 & 6 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 & 6 & 9\end{vmatrix}0 \mid 4 \mid 13, 944 + 92, 960 = 106, 904
$$

Therefore,

$$
2,324 \times 46 = 106, 904
$$

## Multiplying Four-Digit Numbers by Two-Digit Numbers: Word Problems

**Example:** A hospital orders $21$ shipments of syringes. Each shipment contains $4,859$ syringes. How many syringes has the hospital ordered in total?

**Explanation**

To calculate the total number of syringes the hospital ordered, we need to multiply $4,859$ by $21$.

We write it this way:

$$
4\begin{vmatrix}8 & 5 & 9 \\ \cdot & 0 & 0\end{vmatrix}0\begin{vmatrix}0 & 2 & 1\end{vmatrix}4, 859 + 97, 180 = 102, 039
$$

First, we multiply the ones:

$$
[math]\color{blue}\phantom{0}[/math]\begin{vmatrix}[math]\color{blue}\phantom{0}[/math] & [math]\color{blue}\phantom{0}[/math] \\ 4 & 8\end{vmatrix}5\begin{vmatrix}9 \\ \cdot\end{vmatrix}0\begin{vmatrix}0 & 0 & 0 & 2\end{vmatrix}1; 4\begin{vmatrix}8 & 5 & 9\end{vmatrix}4, 859 \cdot 1 = 4, 8590000000
$$

Next, we multiply the tens:

$$
[math]\color{blue}1[/math]; [math]\color{blue}\phantom{0}[/math]\begin{vmatrix}[math]\color{blue}1[/math] \\ [math]\color{blue}\phantom{0}[/math]\end{vmatrix}[math]\color{blue}1[/math]; [math]\color{blue}\phantom{0}[/math]; 4\begin{vmatrix}8 & 5 & 9 \\ \cdot & 0 & 0\end{vmatrix}0\begin{vmatrix}0 & 2 & 1 \\ 4 & 8 & 5\end{vmatrix}9; 9\begin{vmatrix}7 & 1 & 8 & 0\end{vmatrix}4, 859 \cdot 20 = 97, 18000000
$$

Finally, we add the results:

$$
[math]\color{blue}1[/math]; [math]\color{blue}\phantom{0}[/math]\begin{vmatrix}[math]\color{blue}1[/math] \\ [math]\color{blue}\phantom{0}[/math]\end{vmatrix}[math]\color{blue}1[/math]; [math]\color{blue}\phantom{0}[/math]; 4\begin{vmatrix}8 & 5 & 9 \\ \cdot & 0 & 0\end{vmatrix}0\begin{vmatrix}0 & 2 & 1 \\ 4 & 8 & 5\end{vmatrix}9; +\begin{vmatrix}9 & 7 & 1 & 8 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 & 2 & 0 & 3\end{vmatrix}9 \mid 4, 859 + 97, 180 = 102, 039
$$

So,

$$
4,859 \times 21 = 102, 039
$$

Therefore, the hospital ordered $102,039$ syringes in total.

## Multiplying Five-Digit Numbers by Two-Digit Numbers

**Example:** From left to right, what are the missing digits in the following multiplication problem?
$4\begin{vmatrix}2 & 6 & 8 & 9 \\ \cdot & 0 & 0 & 0\end{vmatrix}0\begin{vmatrix}0 & 4 & 7 \\ 2 & [math]\phantom{i}[/math] & 0\end{vmatrix}[math]\phantom{i}[/math]\begin{vmatrix}3 & [math]\phantom{i}[/math]\end{vmatrix}3$

**Explanation**

We write it this way:

$$
4\begin{vmatrix}2 & 6 & 8 & 9 \\ \cdot & 0 & 0 & 0\end{vmatrix}0\begin{vmatrix}0 & 4 & 7\end{vmatrix}42, 689 \cdot 40 = 1, 707, 560
$$

First, we multiply the ones:

$$
[math]\color{blue}1[/math]\begin{vmatrix}[math]\color{blue}4[/math] & [math]\color{blue}6[/math] & [math]\color{blue}6[/math] \\ 4 & 2 & 6\end{vmatrix}8\begin{vmatrix}9 \\ \cdot\end{vmatrix}0\begin{vmatrix}0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 4 & 7 \\ 2 & 9 & 8 & 8 & 2 & 3\end{vmatrix}42, 689 \cdot 7 = 298, 82300
$$

Next, we multiply the tens:

$$
[math]\color{blue}1[/math]; [math]\color{blue}1[/math]\begin{vmatrix}[math]\color{blue}2[/math] \\ [math]\color{blue}4[/math]\end{vmatrix}[math]\color{blue}3[/math]; [math]\color{blue}6[/math]\begin{vmatrix}[math]\color{blue}3[/math] \\ [math]\color{blue}6[/math] \\ 4\end{vmatrix}2\begin{vmatrix}6 & 8 & 9 \\ \cdot & 0 & 0\end{vmatrix}0\begin{vmatrix}0 & 0 & 4 & 7 \\ 2 & 9 & 8 & 8\end{vmatrix}2\begin{vmatrix}3 \\ 1\end{vmatrix}7\begin{vmatrix}0 & 7 & 5 & 6 & 0\end{vmatrix}42, 689 \cdot 40 = 1, 707, 560
$$

Finally, we add the results:

$$
[math]\color{blue}1[/math]; [math]\color{blue}1[/math]\begin{vmatrix}[math]\color{blue}2[/math] \\ [math]\color{blue}4[/math]\end{vmatrix}[math]\color{blue}3[/math]; [math]\color{blue}6[/math]\begin{vmatrix}[math]\color{blue}3[/math] \\ [math]\color{blue}6[/math] \\ 4\end{vmatrix}2\begin{vmatrix}6 & 8 & 9 \\ \cdot & 0 & 0\end{vmatrix}0\begin{vmatrix}0 & 0 & 4 & 7 \\ 2 & 9 & 8 & 8\end{vmatrix}2\begin{vmatrix}3 \\ +\end{vmatrix}1\begin{vmatrix}7 & 0 & 7 & 5 & 6 & 0 \\ 2 & [math]0[/math] & 0 & [math]6[/math] & 3 & [math]8[/math]\end{vmatrix}3 \mid 298, 823 + 1, 707, 560 = 0,,; = 2, 006, 383
$$

Therefore, the missing digits are $0,6$ and $8$.
