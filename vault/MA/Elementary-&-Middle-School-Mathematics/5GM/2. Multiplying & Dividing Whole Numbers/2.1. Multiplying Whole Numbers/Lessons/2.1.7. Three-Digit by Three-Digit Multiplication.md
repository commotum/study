## Introduction

The standard algorithm for multiplication works in the same way when multiplying three-digit numbers by three-digit numbers.

To demonstrate, let's compute $237 \times 142$. We start by writing it in the usual way:

$$
2\begin{vmatrix}3 & 7 \\ \cdot & 0\end{vmatrix}0\begin{vmatrix}0 & 1 & 4 & 2\end{vmatrix}474 + 9, 480 + 23, 700 =
$$

First, we multiply the ones:

$$
[math]\color{blue}\phantom{0}[/math]\begin{vmatrix}[math]\color{blue}1[/math] \\ 2\end{vmatrix}3\begin{vmatrix}7 \\ \cdot\end{vmatrix}0\begin{vmatrix}0 & 0 & 1 & 4\end{vmatrix}2; 4\begin{vmatrix}7 & 4\end{vmatrix}237 \cdot 2 = 4740000000
$$

Next, we multiply the tens. Remember that when multiplying the tens, the result will always end in a zero.

$$
[math]\color{blue}1[/math]; [math]\color{blue}\phantom{0}[/math]\begin{vmatrix}[math]\color{blue}2[/math] \\ [math]\color{blue}1[/math] \\ 2\end{vmatrix}3\begin{vmatrix}7 \\ \cdot\end{vmatrix}0\begin{vmatrix}0 & 0 & 1 & 4\end{vmatrix}2; 4\begin{vmatrix}7 & 4 \\ 9 & 4\end{vmatrix}8 \mid 0 \mid 237 \cdot 40 = 9, 48000000
$$

Next, we multiply the hundreds. Since a hundred ($100$) has two zeros at the end, the result will end in *two* zeros! So, we write these first:

$$
[math]\color{blue}1[/math]; [math]\color{blue}\phantom{0}[/math]\begin{vmatrix}[math]\color{blue}2[/math] \\ [math]\color{blue}1[/math] \\ 2\end{vmatrix}3\begin{vmatrix}7 \\ \cdot\end{vmatrix}0\begin{vmatrix}0 & 0 & 1 & 4\end{vmatrix}2; 4\begin{vmatrix}7 & 4 \\ 9 & 4\end{vmatrix}8\begin{vmatrix}0 \\ 2\end{vmatrix}3\begin{vmatrix}7 & 0 & 0\end{vmatrix}237 \cdot 100 = 23, 700000
$$

Then, we proceed to multiply the rest of the hundreds as usual.

$$
[math]\color{blue}\phantom{0}[/math]; [math]\color{blue}1[/math]; [math]\color{blue}\phantom{0}[/math]\begin{vmatrix}[math]\color{blue}\phantom{0}[/math] \\ [math]\color{blue}2[/math] \\ [math]\color{blue}1[/math] \\ 2\end{vmatrix}3\begin{vmatrix}7 \\ \cdot\end{vmatrix}0\begin{vmatrix}0 & 0 & 1 & 4\end{vmatrix}2; 4\begin{vmatrix}7 & 4 \\ 9 & 4\end{vmatrix}8\begin{vmatrix}0 \\ 2\end{vmatrix}3\begin{vmatrix}7 & 0 & 0\end{vmatrix}237 \cdot 100 = 23, 700000
$$

Finally, we add the results:

$$
[math]\color{blue}\phantom{0}[/math]; [math]\color{blue}1[/math]; [math]\color{blue}\phantom{0}[/math]\begin{vmatrix}[math]\color{blue}\phantom{0}[/math] \\ [math]\color{blue}2[/math] \\ [math]\color{blue}1[/math] \\ 2\end{vmatrix}3\begin{vmatrix}7 \\ \cdot\end{vmatrix}0\begin{vmatrix}0 & 0 & 1 & 4\end{vmatrix}2; 4\begin{vmatrix}7 & 4 \\ + & 9\end{vmatrix}4\begin{vmatrix}8 & 0 \\ + & 2\end{vmatrix}3\begin{vmatrix}7 & 0 & 0 \\ 3 & 3 & 6\end{vmatrix}5 \mid 4 \mid 474 + 9, 480 + 23, 700 =; = 33, 654
$$

Therefore,

$$
237 \times 142 = 33, 654
$$

## Three-Digit by Three-Digit Multiplication: Find the Missing Digits

**Example:** What are the missing digits in the following multiplication problem?

$1\begin{vmatrix}8 & 0 \\ \cdot & 0\end{vmatrix}0\begin{vmatrix}0 & 5 & 1\end{vmatrix}1; 1\begin{vmatrix}8 & 0 \\ + & 1\end{vmatrix}8\begin{vmatrix}0 & 0 \\ + & 9\end{vmatrix}0\begin{vmatrix}0 & 0 & 0 \\ 9 & [math]\phantom{i}[/math] & [math]\phantom{i}[/math]\end{vmatrix}[math]\phantom{i}[/math] \mid 0$

**Explanation**

We write it this way:

$$
1\begin{vmatrix}8 & 0 \\ \cdot & 0\end{vmatrix}0\begin{vmatrix}0 & 5 & 1 & 1\end{vmatrix}180 + 1, 800 + 90, 000 =
$$

First, we multiply the ones:

$$
[math]\color{blue}\phantom{0}[/math]\begin{vmatrix}[math]\color{blue}\phantom{0}[/math] \\ 1\end{vmatrix}8\begin{vmatrix}0 \\ \cdot\end{vmatrix}0\begin{vmatrix}0 & 0 & 5 & 1\end{vmatrix}1; 1\begin{vmatrix}8 & 0\end{vmatrix}180 \cdot 1 = 18000000,
$$

Next, we multiply the tens:

$$
[math]\color{blue}\phantom{0}[/math]; [math]\color{blue}\phantom{0}[/math]\begin{vmatrix}[math]\color{blue}\phantom{0}[/math] \\ [math]\color{blue}\phantom{0}[/math] \\ 1\end{vmatrix}8\begin{vmatrix}0 \\ \cdot\end{vmatrix}0\begin{vmatrix}0 & 0 & 5 & 1\end{vmatrix}1; 1\begin{vmatrix}8 & 0 \\ 1 & 8\end{vmatrix}0 \mid 0 \mid 180 \cdot 10 = 1, 800000,
$$

Next, we multiply the hundreds:

$$
[math]\color{blue}4[/math]; [math]\color{blue}\phantom{0}[/math]; [math]\color{blue}\phantom{0}[/math]\begin{vmatrix}[math]\color{blue}\phantom{0}[/math] \\ [math]\color{blue}\phantom{0}[/math] \\ [math]\color{blue}\phantom{0}[/math] \\ 1\end{vmatrix}8\begin{vmatrix}0 \\ \cdot\end{vmatrix}0\begin{vmatrix}0 & 0 & 5 & 1\end{vmatrix}1; 1\begin{vmatrix}8 & 0 \\ 1 & 8\end{vmatrix}0\begin{vmatrix}0 \\ 9\end{vmatrix}0\begin{vmatrix}0 & 0 & 0\end{vmatrix}180 \cdot 500 = 90, 0000,
$$

Finally, we add the results:

$$
[math]\color{blue}4[/math]; [math]\color{blue}\phantom{0}[/math]; [math]\color{blue}\phantom{0}[/math]\begin{vmatrix}[math]\color{blue}\phantom{0}[/math] \\ [math]\color{blue}\phantom{0}[/math] \\ [math]\color{blue}\phantom{0}[/math] \\ 1\end{vmatrix}8\begin{vmatrix}0 \\ \cdot\end{vmatrix}0\begin{vmatrix}0 & 0 & 5 & 1\end{vmatrix}1; 1\begin{vmatrix}8 & 0 \\ + & 1\end{vmatrix}8\begin{vmatrix}0 & 0 \\ + & 9\end{vmatrix}0\begin{vmatrix}0 & 0 & 0 \\ 9 & 1 & 9\end{vmatrix}8 \mid 0 \mid 180 + 1, 800 + 90, 000 =; = 91, 980
$$

Therefore, the missing digits are $1$, $9$ and $8$.

## Three-Digit by Three-Digit Multiplication

**Example:** Find the value of $435 \times 435$.

**Explanation**

We write it this way:

$$
4\begin{vmatrix}3 & 5 \\ \cdot & 0\end{vmatrix}0\begin{vmatrix}0 & 4 & 3 & 5\end{vmatrix}2, 175 + 13, 050 + 174, 000 =
$$

First, we multiply the ones:

$$
[math]\color{blue}1[/math]\begin{vmatrix}[math]\color{blue}2[/math] \\ 4\end{vmatrix}3\begin{vmatrix}5 \\ \cdot\end{vmatrix}0\begin{vmatrix}0 & 0 & 4 & 3\end{vmatrix}5; 2\begin{vmatrix}1 & 7 & 5\end{vmatrix}435 \cdot 5 = 2, 175000000000
$$

Next, we multiply the tens:

$$
[math]\color{blue}1[/math]; [math]\color{blue}1[/math]\begin{vmatrix}[math]\color{blue}1[/math] \\ [math]\color{blue}2[/math] \\ 4\end{vmatrix}3\begin{vmatrix}5 \\ \cdot\end{vmatrix}0\begin{vmatrix}0 & 0 & 4 & 3\end{vmatrix}5; 2\begin{vmatrix}1 & 7 & 5 \\ 1 & 3 & 0\end{vmatrix}5 \mid 0 \mid 435 \cdot 30 = 13, 05000000000
$$

Next, we multiply the hundreds:

$$
[math]\color{blue}1[/math]; [math]\color{blue}1[/math]; [math]\color{blue}1[/math]\begin{vmatrix}[math]\color{blue}2[/math] \\ [math]\color{blue}1[/math] \\ [math]\color{blue}2[/math] \\ 4\end{vmatrix}3\begin{vmatrix}5 \\ \cdot\end{vmatrix}0\begin{vmatrix}0 & 0 & 4 & 3\end{vmatrix}5; 2\begin{vmatrix}1 & 7 & 5 \\ 1 & 3 & 0\end{vmatrix}5\begin{vmatrix}0 \\ 1\end{vmatrix}7\begin{vmatrix}4 & 0 & 0 & 0\end{vmatrix}435 \cdot 400 = 174, 000000000
$$

Finally, we add the results:

$$
[math]\color{blue}1[/math]; [math]\color{blue}1[/math]; [math]\color{blue}1[/math]\begin{vmatrix}[math]\color{blue}2[/math] \\ [math]\color{blue}1[/math] \\ [math]\color{blue}2[/math] \\ 4\end{vmatrix}3\begin{vmatrix}5 \\ \cdot\end{vmatrix}0\begin{vmatrix}0 & 0 & 4 & 3\end{vmatrix}5; 2\begin{vmatrix}1 & 7 & 5 \\ + & 1 & 3\end{vmatrix}0\begin{vmatrix}5 & 0 \\ + & 1\end{vmatrix}7\begin{vmatrix}4 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 1 & 8 & 9 & 2\end{vmatrix}2 \mid 5 \mid 2, 175 + 13, 050 + 174, 000 =; = 189, 225
$$

Therefore,

$$
435 \times 435 = 189, 225
$$

## Three-Digit by Three-Digit Multiplication: Word Problem

**Example:** Richard has $504$ boxes stored in a warehouse. Each box contains $240$ plates. How many plates in total are there in the warehouse?

**Explanation**

To find the total number of plates stored in the warehouse, we need to calculate $504 \times 240$.

We write it this way:

$$
5\begin{vmatrix}0 & 4 \\ \cdot & 0\end{vmatrix}0\begin{vmatrix}0 & 2 & 4 & 0\end{vmatrix}0 + 20, 160 + 100, 800 =
$$

First, we multiply the ones:

$$
[math]\color{blue}\phantom{0}[/math]\begin{vmatrix}[math]\color{blue}\phantom{0}[/math] \\ 5\end{vmatrix}0\begin{vmatrix}4 \\ \cdot\end{vmatrix}0\begin{vmatrix}0 & 0 & 2 & 4\end{vmatrix}0; 0\begin{vmatrix}0 & 0\end{vmatrix}504 \cdot 0 = 00000000000
$$

Next, we multiply the tens:

$$
[math]\color{blue}\phantom{0}[/math]; [math]\color{blue}\phantom{0}[/math]\begin{vmatrix}[math]\color{blue}1[/math] \\ [math]\color{blue}\phantom{0}[/math] \\ 5\end{vmatrix}0\begin{vmatrix}4 \\ \cdot\end{vmatrix}0\begin{vmatrix}0 & 0 & 2 & 4\end{vmatrix}0; 0\begin{vmatrix}0 & 0 \\ 2 & 0\end{vmatrix}1\begin{vmatrix}6 & 0\end{vmatrix}504 \cdot 40 = 20, 1600000
$$

Next, we multiply the hundreds:

$$
[math]\color{blue}\phantom{0}[/math]; [math]\color{blue}\phantom{0}[/math]; [math]\color{blue}\phantom{0}[/math]\begin{vmatrix}[math]\color{blue}\phantom{0}[/math] \\ [math]\color{blue}1[/math] \\ [math]\color{blue}\phantom{0}[/math] \\ 5\end{vmatrix}0\begin{vmatrix}4 \\ \cdot\end{vmatrix}0\begin{vmatrix}0 & 0 & 2 & 4\end{vmatrix}0; 0\begin{vmatrix}0 & 0 \\ 2 & 0\end{vmatrix}1\begin{vmatrix}6 & 0 \\ 1 & 0\end{vmatrix}0\begin{vmatrix}8 & 0 & 0\end{vmatrix}504 \cdot 200 = 100, 80000
$$

Finally, we add the results:

$$
[math]\color{blue}\phantom{0}[/math]; [math]\color{blue}\phantom{0}[/math]; [math]\color{blue}\phantom{0}[/math]\begin{vmatrix}[math]\color{blue}\phantom{0}[/math] \\ [math]\color{blue}1[/math] \\ [math]\color{blue}\phantom{0}[/math] \\ 5\end{vmatrix}0\begin{vmatrix}4 \\ \cdot\end{vmatrix}0\begin{vmatrix}0 & 0 & 2 & 4\end{vmatrix}0; 0\begin{vmatrix}0 & 0 \\ + & 2\end{vmatrix}0\begin{vmatrix}1 & 6 & 0 \\ + & 1 & 0\end{vmatrix}0\begin{vmatrix}8 & 0 & 0 \\ 1 & 2 & 0\end{vmatrix}9\begin{vmatrix}6 & 0\end{vmatrix}0 + 20, 160 + 100, 800 =; = 120, 960
$$

Hence,

$$
504 \times 240 = 120, 960
$$

Therefore, Richard has $120,960$ plates in the warehouse.
