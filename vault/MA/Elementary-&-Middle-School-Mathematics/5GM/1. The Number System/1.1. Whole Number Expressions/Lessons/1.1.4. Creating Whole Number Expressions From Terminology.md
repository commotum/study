## Introduction

We can express operations in a variety of different ways.

For example, $4+2$ can be written as:

- Add $4$ and $2$
- The **sum** of $4$ and $2$
- The **total** of $4$ and $2$

Likewise, the expression $4-2$ can be written as:

- $4$ minus $2$
- Subtract $2$ from $4$
- The **difference** of $4$ and $2$

---

<a id="using-special-words-to-represent-addition-or-subtraction"></a>
## Using Special Words to Represent Addition or Subtraction

**Example:** Write the following statements as expressions:

1. "The sum of $5$ and $13$."
2. "The difference of $17$ and $9$."

**Explanation**

Remember that the word **sum** refers to addition, and the word **difference** refers to subtraction.

1. To find the **sum** of two numbers, we should insert "$+$" between them. Therefore, "the sum of $5$ and $13$" can be expressed as: $5+13$
2. To find the **difference** of two numbers, we should insert "$-$" between them without changing the order of the numbers. Therefore, "the difference of $17$ and $9$" can be expressed as: $17-9$

## Using Special Words to Represent Multiplication and Division

We also have some special words that represent multiplication and division.

The expression $4\times 2$ can be written as:

- $2$ multiplied by $4$ (or $4$ multiplied by $2$)
- The **product** of $2$ and $4$ (or the product of $4$ and $2$)

Likewise, the expression $4\div 2$ can be written as:

- The **quotient** of $4$ and $2$
- Divide $4$ by $2$
- $4$ divided by $2$

---

<a id="using-special-words-to-represent-multiplication-or-division"></a>
## Using Special Words to Represent Multiplication or Division

**Example:** Write the following statements as expressions:

1. "The product of $7$ and $11$."
2. "The quotient of $28$ and $4$."

**Explanation**

Remember that the word **product** refers to multiplication, and the word **quotient** refers to division.

1. To find the product of two numbers, we should insert "$\times$" between them. Therefore, "the product of $7$ and $11$" can be expressed as: $7\times11$
2. To find the quotient of two numbers, we should insert "$\div$" between them without changing the order of the numbers. Therefore, "the quotient of $28$ and $4$" can be expressed as: $28 \div 4$

<a id="using-parentheses"></a>
## Using Parentheses

When we have two operations within an expression, we can use parentheses to indicate which operation comes first.

For example, consider the statement "*the sum of $3$ and $4$, multiplied by $5$.*"

Let's break it down:

**Step 1.** "*The sum of $3$ and $4$*" means:
$3 + 4$

**Step 2.** Since this operation comes first, we use parentheses:
$(3+4)$

**Step 3.** The "*... multiplied by $5$*" means that we should multiply the above expression by $5$. So we get:
$(3+4) \times 5$

---

<a id="using-special-words-with-two-operations"></a>
## Using Special Words with Two Operations

**Example:** What expression represents "*$3$ more than the quotient of $12$ and $6$*"?

**Explanation**

"*The quotient of $12$ and $6$*" means:

$$
12 \div 6
$$

Since this operation comes first, we use parentheses:

$$
(12 \div 6)
$$

"*$3$ more than...*" means that we should add $3$ to the above expression. So we get:

$$
(12 \div 6) + 3
$$
