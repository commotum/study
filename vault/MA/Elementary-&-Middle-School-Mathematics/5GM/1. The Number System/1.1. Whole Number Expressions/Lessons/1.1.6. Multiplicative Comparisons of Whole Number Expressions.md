## Introduction

Let's take a look at the following expression:

$$
5 + (10\div 2)
$$

How do we create an expression that is $8$ times larger than this expression without evaluating the expression first?

First, let's note the language. We want an expression that is "$8$ *times larger*" than the one above, which means that we should *multiply* by $8$.

First, since the expression already has parentheses, we add square brackets.

$$
[5 + (10\div 2)]
$$

We add brackets to ensure that we're modifying the entire expression, not just part of it.

Now, we multiply the given expression by $8$. This gives:

$$
8\times[5 + (10\div 2)]
$$

And that's our answer!

## Modifying an Expression With Multiplication

**Example:** What expression is $7$ times larger than $5 + (3\times 2)$?

**Explanation**

First, let's note the language. We want an expression that is "$7$ times *larger*" than the one above, which means that we should multiply the *entire* expression by $7$.

First, since the expression already has parentheses, we add square brackets.

$$
[5 + (3\times 2)]
$$

Now, we *multiply* the given expression by $7$. This gives:

$$
7\times[5 + (3\times 2)]
$$

## Modifying an Expression With Division

Let's take a look at the following expression:

$$
6\times(4+2)
$$

How do we create an expression that is $3$ times smaller than this expression?

First, we once again pay careful attention to the language. We want an expression that is "$3$ *times smaller*" than the one above. This means that we should *divide* by $3$.

First, since the expression already has parentheses, we add square brackets:

$$
[6\times(4+2)]
$$

Now, we divide the expression by $3$. This gives:

$$
[6\times(4+2)]\div 3
$$

---

<a id="modifying-an-expression-with-division"></a>
## Modifying an Expression With Division

**Example:** Find an expression that is $8$ times smaller than the expression $5 + (2\times 4)$.

**Explanation**

First, we note that "${\color{blue}8}$ *times smaller* than the expression" means that we should *divide* the given expression by $8$.

First, we add some brackets:

$$
\left[5 + (2\times 4)\right]
$$

Next, we divide the expression by $\color{blue}8$:

$$
\left[5 + (2\times 4)\right]\div {\color{blue}8}
$$
