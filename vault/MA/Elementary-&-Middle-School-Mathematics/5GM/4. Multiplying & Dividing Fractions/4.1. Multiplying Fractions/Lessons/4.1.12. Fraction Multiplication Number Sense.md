## Introduction

When multiplying a whole number by a fraction, we can determine whether the result is larger or smaller than the original whole number by looking at the size of the fraction.

For example, consider the following product:

$$
{\color{blue}{2}}\times {\color{red}{\dfrac38}}
$$

Is this product (a) smaller than ${\color{blue}{2}}$, (b) larger than ${\color{blue}{2}}$, or (c) equal to ${\color{blue}{2}}$?

To answer this question *without* carrying out the multiplication, we need to look at the fractional factor:

$$
{\color{red}{\dfrac38}}
$$

Note the following:

- The numerator $({\color{red}{3}})$ is *less than* the denominator $({\color{red}{8}})$. Therefore, this fraction is *less than* $1$. We can write this using a "less than" symbol as follows:
${\color{red}{\dfrac38}} < 1$
- When we multiply a number by a fraction that is *less than* $1$, the number is scaled *down.* In other words, it becomes *smaller*.
- Therefore, our product is *less than* $2$. We can write this using a "less than" symbol as follows:
${\color{blue}{2}}\times {\color{red}{\dfrac38}} < {\color{blue}{2}}$

So, the correct answer is (a).

---

<a id="scaling-up"></a>
## Scaling Up

Now, let's consider the following product:

$$
{\color{blue}{3}}\times {\color{red}{\dfrac65}}
$$

Is this product (a) smaller than ${\color{blue}{3}}$, (b) larger than ${\color{blue}{3}}$, or (c) equal to ${\color{blue}{3}}$?

To answer this question, we need to look at the fractional factor:

$$
{\color{red}{\dfrac65}}
$$

Note the following:

- The numerator $({\color{red}{6}})$ is *greater than* the denominator $({\color{red}{5}})$. Therefore, this fraction is *greater than* $1$. We can write this using a "greater than" symbol as follows:
${\color{red}{\dfrac65}} > 1$
- When we multiply a number by a fraction that is *greater than* $1$, the number is scaled *up.* In other words, it becomes *larger*.
- Therefore, our product is *greater than* $3$.
${\color{blue}{3}}\times {\color{red}{\dfrac65}} > {\color{blue}{3}}$

So, the correct answer is (b).

---

<a id="equality"></a>
## Equality

Now, let's consider the following product:

$$
{\color{blue}{7}}\times {\color{red}{\dfrac44}}
$$

In this case, notice that the fraction

$$
{\color{red}{\dfrac44}}
$$

is *equivalent to* $1$ because the numerator and the denominator are *equal.* Therefore, we conclude that

$$
{\color{blue}{7}}\times {\color{red}{\dfrac44}} = {\color{blue}{7}}
$$

## Comparing a Product to a Whole Number

**Example:** Which of the options below makes the following statement true?

> $7 \times \dfrac{4}{5}$ is $\text{___________}$

1. less than $7$
2. equal to $7$
3. greater than $7$

**Explanation**

We must compare the size of the following product to the number $7{:}$

$$
7 \times {\color{red}\dfrac{4}{5}}
$$

Since

$$
\color{red}\dfrac{4}{5}
$$

is *less than* $1$, the number $7$ is *scaled down* (i.e., becomes *smaller*) when it's multiplied by

$$
{\color{red}\dfrac{4}{5}}
$$

Therefore, the correct statement is as follows:

> *$7 \times\dfrac{4}{5}$ is $7$*

## Identifying Products Greater Than or Less Than a Whole Number

**Example:** Which of the following products is greater than $6$?

1. $6 \times \dfrac{9}{5}$
2. $1\,\dfrac{1}{5} \times 6$
3. $\dfrac{5}{8} \times 6$

**Explanation**

Let's examine each product in turn.

- Since $\color{blue}\dfrac{9}{5}$ is *greater than* $1$, the number $6$ is *scaled up* (i.e., becomes *larger*) when it's multiplied by ${\color{blue}\dfrac{9}{5}}$. Therefore, this product is *greater than* $6.\quad{\color{green}{\checkmark}}$
- Since $\color{blue}1\dfrac{1}{5}$ is *greater than* $1$, the number $6$ is *scaled up* (i.e., becomes *larger*) when it's multiplied by ${\color{blue}1\dfrac{1}{5}}$. Therefore, this product is *greater than* $6.\quad{\color{green}{\checkmark}}$
- Since $\color{blue}\dfrac{5}{8}$ is *less than* $1$, the number $6$ is *scaled down* (i.e., becomes *smaller*) when it's multiplied by ${\color{blue}\dfrac{5}{8}}$. Therefore, this product is *less than* $6.\quad{\color{red}{\times}}$

So, the correct answer is "I and II only."

## Comparing Products to Fractions

We can apply the same reasoning to compare products to fractions.

For example, consider the following product:

$$
{\color{blue}{\dfrac35}}\times {\color{red}{\dfrac18}}
$$

Is this product (a) smaller than

$$
{\color{blue}{\dfrac35}}
$$

(b) larger than

$$
{\color{blue}{\dfrac35}}
$$

or (c) equal to

$$
{\color{blue}{\dfrac35}}
$$

To answer this, we need to consider the size of the second factor:

$$
\color{red}\dfrac{1}{8}
$$

Since

$$
\color{red}\dfrac{1}{8}
$$

is *less than* $1$, the number

$$
\color{blue}{\dfrac35}
$$

is *scaled down* (i.e., becomes *smaller*) when it's multiplied by

$$
{\color{red}\dfrac18}
$$

Therefore, this product is *less than*

$$
{\color{blue}{\dfrac35}}
$$

We can write this result using a "less than" symbol as follows:

$$
{\color{blue}{\dfrac35}}\times {\color{red}{\dfrac18}} < {\color{blue}{\dfrac35}}
$$

So, the correct answer is (a).

> $\dfrac{4}{9} \times \dfrac{7}{2}$ is $\text{____________}$

1. equal to $\dfrac{4}{9}$
2. less than $\dfrac{4}{9}$
3. greater than $\dfrac{4}{9}$

**Explanation**

We must compare the size of the following product to the number

$$
\dfrac49{:}
$$

$$
\dfrac49 \times {\color{red}\dfrac{7}{2}}
$$

Since

$$
\color{red}\dfrac{7}{2}
$$

is *larger than* $1$, the number

$$
\dfrac{4}{9}
$$

is *scaled up* (i.e., becomes *larger*) when it's multiplied by

$$
{\color{red}\dfrac{7}{2}}
$$

Therefore, the correct statement is as follows:

> *$\dfrac{4}{9} \times \dfrac{7}{2}$ is $\frac{4}{9}$*

## Identifying Products Greater Than or Less Than a Fraction

**Example:** Which of the following products is greater than $\dfrac{3}{2}$?

1. $\dfrac{3}{2} \times \dfrac{2}{2}$
2. $\dfrac{3}{2} \times \dfrac{3}{2}$
3. $\dfrac{3}{2} \times \dfrac{2}{3}$

**Explanation**

Let's examine each product in turn.

- Since $\color{blue}\dfrac{2}{2}$ is *equal to* $1$, the number $\dfrac32$ is neither scaled down nor scaled up when it's multiplied by ${\color{blue}\dfrac{2}{2}}$. Therefore, this product *equals* $\dfrac32.\quad{\color{red}{\times}}$
- Since $\color{blue}\dfrac{3}{2}$ is *greater than* $1$, the number $\dfrac{3}{2}$ is *scaled up* (i.e., becomes *larger*) when it's multiplied by ${\color{blue}\dfrac{3}{2}}$. Therefore, this product is *greater than* $\dfrac{3}{2}.\quad{\color{green}{\checkmark}}$
- Since $\color{blue}\dfrac23$ is *less than* $1$, the number $\dfrac32$ is *scaled down* (i.e., becomes *smaller*) when it's multiplied by ${\color{blue}\dfrac{2}{3}}$. Therefore, this product is *less than* $\dfrac32.\quad{\color{red}{\times}}$

So, the correct answer is "II only."
