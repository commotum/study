## Introduction

If the sides of a rectangle have fractional lengths, we find the rectangle's area by multiplying the fractions.

Look at the rectangle below. What is its area?

![](<../Source/4.1.11. Finding Areas Using Fraction Multiplication/Images/da449d706ab14cbbd94de68afd25e76d.png>)

We find the area of the rectangle by multiplying the lengths of the sides:

$$
\dfrac{6}{5} \times \dfrac{3}{5} = \dfrac{6 \times 3}{5 \times 5} = \dfrac{18}{25}
$$

So, the area of the rectangle is

$$
\dfrac{18}{25}
$$

square units.

## Finding a Missing Number in an Area Model

**Example:** What number is missing from the area model below?

![](<../Source/4.1.11. Finding Areas Using Fraction Multiplication/Images/b3c48914eec2d408b956cc71df070a67.png>)

**Explanation**

The area of the rectangle can be found by multiplying the lengths of the sides:

$$
\dfrac{2}{5} \times \dfrac{1}{4}
$$

We can simplify this product by swapping the denominators:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{2}{5} \cdot \frac{1}{4} &= \frac{2}{4} \cdot \frac{1}{5} \\
&= \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{5} \\
&= \frac{1 \cdot 1}{2 \cdot 5} \\
&= \frac{1}{[math]\color{blue}10[/math]}
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the missing number is $10$.

## Cases When Some Sides Are Improper Fractions

**Example:** What number is missing from the area model below?

![](<../Source/4.1.11. Finding Areas Using Fraction Multiplication/Images/20a8faae89b43a6c7336735ee0f767c9.png>)

**Explanation**

The area of the rectangle can be found by multiplying the lengths of the sides:

$$
\dfrac{5}{7} \times \dfrac{9}{5}
$$

We can simplify this product by swapping the denominators:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{5}{7} \cdot \frac{9}{5} &= \frac{5}{5} \cdot \frac{9}{7} \\
&= 1 \cdot \frac{9}{7} \\
&= \frac{1 \cdot 9}{7} \\
&= \frac{[math]\color{blue}9[/math]}{7}
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the missing number is $9$.

## Cases When Some Sides Are Mixed Numbers

**Example:** What number is missing from the area model below?

![](<../Source/4.1.11. Finding Areas Using Fraction Multiplication/Images/457720cdfddea82042c78fbbd4499d1c.png>)

**Explanation**

First, we write

$$
2\,\dfrac{1}{2}
$$

as an improper fraction:

$$
2\,\dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{(2\times 2)+1}{2} = \dfrac{5}{2}
$$

The area of the rectangle can be found by multiplying the lengths of the sides:

$$
\dfrac{4}{7} \times \dfrac{5}{2}
$$

We can simplify this product by swapping the denominators:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{4}{7} \cdot \frac{5}{2} &= \frac{4}{2} \cdot \frac{5}{7} \\
&= 2 \cdot \frac{5}{7} \\
&= \frac{2 \cdot 5}{7} \\
&= \frac{10}{[math]\color{blue}\,7\,[/math]}
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the missing number is $7$.

## Finding the Area of a Rectangle

**Example:** The breadth of a rectangular park is $\dfrac 3 4\, \text{km}$ and its length is $1\, \dfrac 12 \, \text{km}$. Using the model below, find the area of the park, expressed as an improper fraction.

![](<../Source/4.1.11. Finding Areas Using Fraction Multiplication/Images/faf024f3123fd5a8ee39fdffc6ec365b.png>)

**Explanation**

To find the area, we need to multiply the lengths of the sides.

First we write

$$
1\,\dfrac{1}{2}
$$

as an improper fraction:

$$
1\,\dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{(1\times2)+1}{2} = \dfrac{3}{2}
$$

Now, we find the area of the park by multiplying the lengths of its sides:

$$
\dfrac{3}{4} \times \dfrac{3}{2} = \dfrac{3 \times 3}{4 \times 2} = \dfrac{9}{8}
$$

So, the area of the park is

$$
\dfrac{9}{8}
$$

square kilometers.
