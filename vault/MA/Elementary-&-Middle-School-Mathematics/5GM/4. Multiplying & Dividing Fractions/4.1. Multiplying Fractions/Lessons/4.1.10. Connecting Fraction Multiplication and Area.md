## Introduction

Fraction multiplication can often be interpreted as the area of a part of a unit square.

Let's consider the unit square below. It has sides of length $1$ unit, divided into lots of little tiles.

![](<../Source/4.1.10. Connecting Fraction Multiplication and Area/Images/7b1a8f2f48c909e8319f2319fd5f6344.png>)

Each side of the unit square is divided into $\color{blue}4$ equal pieces. So, each side of a tile is

$$
\dfrac{1}{\color{blue}4}
$$

units long.

![](<../Source/4.1.10. Connecting Fraction Multiplication and Area/Images/36e3503a5a0e8c93a90ecf313a2c2a43.png>)

The area of each tile is the product of the two side lengths. Therefore, the area of each tile is

$$
\dfrac{1}{4} \times \dfrac{1}{4}\;\textrm{square units}
$$

## Another Method for Finding the Area of a Tile

Another way of computing the area of a part of a unit square is to realize that we can find the area of a single tile using the following formula:

$$
\textrm{area of a single tile} = \dfrac{1}{\textrm{number of tiles}}
$$

Knowing this, what is the area of the shaded region below, in square units?

![](<../Source/4.1.10. Connecting Fraction Multiplication and Area/Images/8df1dffcd02ba30d75540506ef65101c.png>)

First, note that the region is $7$ tiles long and $7$ tiles high.

![](<../Source/4.1.10. Connecting Fraction Multiplication and Area/Images/e95db5ab9215bc444f8f355e3cfac7f1.png>)

So there are a total of

$$
7 \times 7 = 49
$$

small square tiles. Therefore, the area of each tile is

$$
\dfrac{1}{49}
$$

square units.

Since $10$ tiles are shaded, the total shaded area is:

$$
10\times\dfrac{1}{49} = \dfrac{10}{49} \,\textrm{square units}
$$

## Finding the Area of Multiple Shaded Tiles by Counting the Number of Tiles

**Example:** The area of each small square tile in the unit square below is $\dfrac{1}{36}$ square units. In square units, what is the area of the shaded part?

![](<../Source/4.1.10. Connecting Fraction Multiplication and Area/Images/85422311b9cc5b09e0d6a455252912b0.png>)

**Explanation**

The shaded region is $5$ tiles long and $5$ tiles high. Therefore, the shaded part consists of

$$
5 \times 5 = 25
$$

small square tiles.

![](<../Source/4.1.10. Connecting Fraction Multiplication and Area/Images/877dc57475d0ed9e0e104034146e2866.png>)

Since each tile has an area of

$$
\dfrac{1}{36}
$$

square units, the area of the shaded part is

$$
25\times\dfrac{1}{36} = \dfrac{25}{36}\;\textrm{square units}
$$

<a id="finding-the-area-of-rectangles-with-the-length-of-the-sides"></a>
## Finding the Area of Rectangles With the Length of the Sides

Instead of counting the number of tiles in a shaded region, we can find the length of its sides and multiply them together.
So, what is the area of the shaded part below?

![](<../Source/4.1.10. Connecting Fraction Multiplication and Area/Images/e7931d0a2ac282ff74a125538f71c09e.png>)

First, notice that each side of the unit square is divided into $\color{blue}7$ equal pieces. So, each of the smaller tiles has sides that are

$$
\dfrac{1}{\color{blue}7}
$$

units long.

The shaded region is $4$ tiles long and $6$ tiles high, which means that it has dimensions

$$
\dfrac{4}{7}
$$

units by

$$
\dfrac{6}{7}
$$

units.

![](<../Source/4.1.10. Connecting Fraction Multiplication and Area/Images/51ea0164cf29bc28d15b64af8f21d908.png>)

Finally, we find the area of the shaded rectangle by multiplying the lengths of the sides. So, the area is:

$$
\dfrac{4}{7} \times \dfrac{6}{7}\,\,\textrm{square units}
$$
