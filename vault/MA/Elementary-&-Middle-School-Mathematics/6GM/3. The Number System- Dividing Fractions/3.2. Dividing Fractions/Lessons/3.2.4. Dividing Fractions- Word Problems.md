## Introduction

Many word problems ask how many equal-sized pieces, groups, or containers can be made from a given amount. In these situations, we compare the *total amount available* to the *size of one group*, which can lead to dividing one fraction by another fraction.

The key idea is that division helps us answer the question: *How many of this size fit into that amount?*

For example, consider the following question: How many

$$
\dfrac25
$$

-liter bottles can be filled with

$$
\dfrac56
$$

liters of juice?

This question asks how many groups of size

$$
\dfrac25
$$

fit into a total amount of

$$
\dfrac56
$$

. To answer it, we divide the total amount by the amount in one group:

$$
\dfrac56 \div \dfrac25
$$

Dividing a fraction by another fraction is equivalent to multiplying the first fraction by the **reciprocal** of the second fraction.

The reciprocal of

$$
\dfrac25
$$

is

$$
\dfrac52
$$

. So we rewrite the division as multiplication:

$$
\dfrac56 \div \dfrac25 = \dfrac56 \times \dfrac52
$$

Now we multiply fractions by multiplying the numerators and multiplying the denominators:

$$
\dfrac56 \times \dfrac52 = \dfrac{5 \times 5}{6 \times 2} = \dfrac{25}{12}
$$

Finally, we write the resulting improper fraction as a mixed number:

$$
\dfrac{25}{12} = 2\,\textrm{1} = 2\,\dfrac1{12}
$$

Therefore,

$$
2\,\dfrac{1}{12}
$$

bottles can be filled with

$$
\dfrac56
$$

liters of juice.

This same process applies to all fraction word problems in this lesson: identify what is being divided, rewrite the division using a reciprocal, and then multiply.

## Dividing Proper Fractions When the Result is a Whole Number

**Example:** How many $\dfrac{5}{12}\,\textrm{m}$ long boards can be cut from a $\dfrac{5}{3}\,\textrm{m}$ plank of wood?

**Explanation**

To determine the number of boards, we need to divide the length of the plank by the length of one board:

$$
\dfrac53 \div \dfrac{5}{12}
$$

Dividing a fraction by another fraction is equivalent to multiplying the first fraction by the reciprocal of the second fraction.

The reciprocal of

$$
\dfrac{5}{12}
$$

is

$$
\dfrac{12}{5}
$$

So, we have

$$
\dfrac53 \div \dfrac{5}{12} = \dfrac{\color{blue}5}{\color{red}3} \times \dfrac{\color{red}12}{\color{blue}5}
$$

Notice that the first fraction’s denominator ($\color{red}3$) divides the second fraction’s numerator ($\color{red}12$), and the second fraction’s denominator ($\color{blue}5$) divides the first fraction’s numerator ($\color{blue}5$).

So, we can simplify our problem by first swapping the denominators:

$$
\dfrac{\color{blue}5}{\color{blue}5} \times \dfrac{\color{red}12}{\color{red}3}
$$

Next, we simplify each fraction:

$$
\dfrac55 = 5 \div 5 = 1 \qquad\text{and}\qquad \dfrac{12}{3} = 12 \div 3 = 4
$$

Finally, we conclude that

$$
\dfrac55 \times \dfrac{12}{3} = 1 \times 4 = 4
$$

Therefore, the plank can be cut into $4$ boards.

## Dividing Proper Fractions When the Result is an Improper Fraction

**Example:** A container holds $\dfrac{5}{18}\,\textrm{kg}$ of clay. Each small sculpture requires $\dfrac{2}{27}\,\textrm{kg}$ of clay. How many sculptures can be made with the clay in the container? Give your answer as an **improper fraction** in its simplest form.

**Explanation**

To determine how many sculptures can be made, we need to divide the total amount of clay by the amount used for each sculpture:

$$
\dfrac{5}{18} \div \dfrac{2}{27}
$$

Dividing a fraction by another fraction is equivalent to multiplying the first fraction by the reciprocal of the second fraction.

The reciprocal of

$$
\dfrac{2}{27}
$$

is

$$
\dfrac{27}{2}
$$

So, we have

$$
\dfrac{5}{18} \div \dfrac{2}{27} = \dfrac{5}{\color{red}18} \times \dfrac{\color{blue}27}{2}
$$

Notice that the first fraction’s denominator ($\color{red}18$) and the second fraction’s numerator ($\color{blue}27$) have a common factor of $9$.

So, we can simplify our problem by first swapping the denominators:

$$
\dfrac{5}{2} \times \dfrac{\color{blue}27}{\color{red}18}
$$

Next, we reduce the second fraction to its lowest terms by dividing the numerator and denominator by $9{:}$

$$
\dfrac{5}{2} \times \dfrac{27 \div 9}{18 \div 9} = \dfrac{5}{2} \times \dfrac{3}{2}
$$

Finally, to multiply two fractions, we multiply the numerators, and we multiply the denominators:

$$
\dfrac{5}{2} \times \dfrac{3}{2} = \dfrac{15}{4}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\dfrac{15}{4}
$$

sculptures can be made.

## Dividing Proper Fractions and Expressing the Solution as a Mixed Number

**Example:** How many $\dfrac34\,\textrm{kg}$ bags of flour can be filled using $\dfrac{7}{6}\,\textrm{kg}$ of flour? Give your answer as a **mixed number** in its simplest form.

**Explanation**

To determine the number of bags that can be filled, we need to divide the total amount of flour by the amount in one bag:

$$
\dfrac76 \div \dfrac34
$$

Dividing a fraction by another fraction is equivalent to multiplying the first fraction by the reciprocal of the second fraction.

The reciprocal of

$$
\dfrac34
$$

is

$$
\dfrac43
$$

So, we have

$$
\dfrac76 \div \dfrac34 = \dfrac76 \times \dfrac43
$$

Now, we multiply fractions. We multiply the numerators, and we multiply the denominators:

$$
\dfrac76 \times \dfrac43 = \dfrac{7 \times 4}{6 \times 3} = \dfrac{28}{18}
$$

Next, we simplify the improper fraction:

$$
\dfrac{28}{18} = \dfrac{14}{9}
$$

Finally, we write the resulting improper fraction as a mixed number:

$$
\dfrac{14}{9} = 1\,\textrm{R}5 = 1\,\dfrac59
$$

Therefore,

$$
1\,\dfrac59
$$

bags of flour can be filled.
