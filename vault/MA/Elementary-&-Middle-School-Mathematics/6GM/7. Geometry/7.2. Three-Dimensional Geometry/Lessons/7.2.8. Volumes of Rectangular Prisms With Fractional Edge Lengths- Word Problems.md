## Introduction

When a real-world object is shaped like a rectangular prism, we use the volume formula:

$$
V = \text{length} \times \text{width} \times \text{height}
$$

If there are several identical objects, first find the volume of one object. Then multiply by the number of objects.

For example, suppose a storage box is

$$
\dfrac{5}{2}\,\textrm{ft}
$$

long,

$$
\dfrac{2}{3}\,\textrm{ft}
$$

wide, and $3\,\textrm{ft}$ high. Let's find the total volume of $3$ identical boxes.

![](<../Source/7.2.8. Volumes of Rectangular Prisms With Fractional Edge Lengths- Word Problems/Images/48e85ffc0f02f0e2dfc2fc26ea886908.png>)

First, find the volume of one box:

$$
\begin{aligned}
V &= \frac{5}{2} \cdot \frac{2}{3} \cdot 3 \\
&= \frac{5 \cdot 2 \cdot 3}{2 \cdot 3 \cdot 1} \\
&= \frac{30}{6} \\
&= 5
\end{aligned}
$$

So, one box has a volume of $5\,\textrm{ft}^3$.

Since there are $3$ boxes,

$$
3 \times 5 = 15
$$

The total volume of the boxes is $15\,\textrm{ft}^3$. Remember to include cubic units when giving a volume.

## Calculating the Volume of a Rectangular Prism

**Example:** Alessandro wants to build a concrete tank to store water. If he decides that the inner dimensions of this tank should be $2 \, \textrm{m}$ long, $\dfrac{4}{3} \, \textrm{m}$ wide, and $\dfrac{3}{2} \, \textrm{m}$ high, then how much water can this tank store?

**Explanation**

First, we make a drawing of the tank.

![](<../Source/7.2.8. Volumes of Rectangular Prisms With Fractional Edge Lengths- Word Problems/Images/10d5948bd646cdd3e768397c206daec7.png>)

The volume of the tank is:

$$
\begin{aligned}
V &= length \cdot width \cdot height \\
&= 2 \cdot \frac{4}{3} \cdot \frac{3}{2} \\
&= \frac{2}{1} \cdot \frac{4}{3} \cdot \frac{3}{2} \\
&= \frac{2 \cdot 4 \cdot 3}{1 \cdot 3 \cdot 2} \\
&= \frac{24}{6} \\
&= 4
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the tank will be able to store $4 \, \textrm{m}^3$ of water.

<a id="unit-costs-for-volumes"></a>
## Unit Costs for Volumes

Sometimes we need to find the total cost of a rectangular prism when we know the cost per cubic unit.

To solve these problems:

1. Find the volume.
2. Multiply the volume by the cost per cubic unit.

Be sure the units match. Volume is measured in cubic units (such as $\text{ft}^3$ or $\text{in}^3$), and the rate is given "per cubic unit."

For example, suppose a block of soap is shaped like a rectangular prism that is $2\,\textrm{in}$ long,

$$
\dfrac{3}{2}\,\textrm{in}
$$

wide, and

$$
\dfrac{4}{3}\,\textrm{in}
$$

high, and each cubic inch of soap costs $\[math]4$.

![](<../Source/7.2.8. Volumes of Rectangular Prisms With Fractional Edge Lengths- Word Problems/Images/bbbd3d32d06c710ef792b6766cbfbb22.png>)

To find the cost of the soap block, we first find the volume:

$$
\begin{aligned}
V &= 2 \cdot \frac{3}{2} \cdot \frac{4}{3} \\
&= \frac{2 \cdot 3 \cdot 4}{1 \cdot 2 \cdot 3} \\
&= \frac{24}{6} \\
&= 4
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the block has a volume of $4\,\text{in}^3$. Since each cubic inch costs $\[/math]4$, we get

$$
4 \times \[math]4 = \[/math]16
$$

Therefore, the total cost of the soap block is $\[math]16$. Always find the volume first, then multiply by the unit cost.

<a id="calculating-cost"></a>
## Calculating Cost

**Example:** A planter box is made from a rectangular concrete block that is $\dfrac{9}{2} \, \textrm{ft}$ long, $\dfrac{5}{3} \, \textrm{ft}$ wide, and $2 \, \textrm{ft}$ high. If each cubic foot of concrete requires $8\,\textrm{lb}$ of sand, how much sand was used to make the planter box?

**Explanation**

First, we make a sketch of the planter box.

![](<../Source/7.2.8. Volumes of Rectangular Prisms With Fractional Edge Lengths- Word Problems/Images/e7ff7d3e6dfa44e9b74b7963062495a4.png>)

The volume of the planter box is

$$
\begin{aligned}
V &= length \cdot width \cdot height \\
&= \frac{9}{2} \cdot \frac{5}{3} \cdot 2 \\
&= \frac{9}{2} \cdot \frac{5}{3} \cdot \frac{2}{1} \\
&= \frac{9 \cdot 5 \cdot 2}{2 \cdot 3 \cdot 1} \\
&= \frac{90}{6}
\end{aligned}
$$

We can simplify the above fraction by dividing the numerator and denominator by $6$:

$$
\dfrac{90 \div 6}{6 \div 6} = 15 \, \textrm{ft}^3
$$

Since each cubic foot of concrete requires $8\,\textrm{lb}$ of sand, we get

$$
8 \times 15 = 120
$$

Therefore, $120\,\textrm{lb}$ of sand were used to make the planter box.
