## Introduction

How do we calculate the area of a parallelogram, like the one below?

![](<../Source/7.1.5. Areas and Perimeters of Parallelograms/Images/eac342d313b626c1fb529ec16a38e32d.png>)

We can do this with the following formula:

$$
\textrm{Area} = \textrm{base} \times \textrm{height}
$$

Note that the height needs to be at a right angle to the base. This is very important.

Let's use this formula to determine the area of the parallelogram below.

![](<../Source/7.1.5. Areas and Perimeters of Parallelograms/Images/f61401f60b37be645cec927ff8be7c3f.png>)

First, we write down the base and height of the parallelogram:

$$
\begin{aligned}
base &= 8 \\
height &= 4
\end{aligned}
$$

Substituting the values of the base and height into the formula, we get:

$$
\begin{aligned}
Area &= base \cdot height \\
&= 8 \cdot 4 \\
&= 32
\end{aligned}
$$

We'll show how to derive the formula at the end of the lesson.

## Finding the Area of a Parallelogram with a Horizontal Base

**Example:** Determine the area of the parallelogram.

![](<../Source/7.1.5. Areas and Perimeters of Parallelograms/Images/0377741e647fc575b9bfe892cd3fc72b.png>)

**Explanation**

First, we find the base and the height of the parallelogram:

$$
\begin{aligned}
base &= 6 \\
height &= 3
\end{aligned}
$$

The area of the parallelogram can be found by the following formula:

$$
\textrm{Area} = \textrm{base} \times \textrm{height}
$$

Substituting the values of the base and height, we get:

$$
\begin{aligned}
Area &= 6 \cdot 3 = 18
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, let's find the units. Since base and height are measured in centimeters, the area is measured in square centimeters. Therefore:

$$
\textrm{Area} = 18 \, \textrm{cm}^2
$$

## Finding the Perimeter of a Parallelogram Given the Area

**Example:** What the perimeter of the parallelogram given that its area is $48$?

![](<../Source/7.1.5. Areas and Perimeters of Parallelograms/Images/df4b29f34d3be81c318386eb5e2b0414.png>)

**Explanation**

Let's write down what we know:

$$
Area = 48; base =?; height = 4; side = 5
$$

The area of the parallelogram can be found by the following formula:

$$
\textrm{Area} = \textrm{base} \times \textrm{height}
$$

Substituting the values of the area and height, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
48 &= base \cdot 4
\end{aligned}
$$

Using the relationship between multiplication and division, we can solve for the base:

$$
\textrm{base} = 48 \div 4 = 12
$$

Finally, the perimeter of the parallelogram is

$$
\begin{aligned}
Perimeter &= (base + side) \cdot 2 \\
&= (12 + 5) \cdot 2 \\
&= 17 \cdot 2 \\
&= 34
\end{aligned}
$$

## Deriving the Area Formula

![](<../Source/7.1.5. Areas and Perimeters of Parallelograms/Images/584e35166517f9dfbb7cf8a1dbe7d2d2.png>)

We'll now derive the formula for the area of a parallelogram. Consider a parallelogram shown above.

Let's now

- cut the small triangle along the height on the right-hand side, and
- place it on the other side of the original figure.

![](<../Source/7.1.5. Areas and Perimeters of Parallelograms/Images/429a575dd981971c5d8b0b9ba878922f.png>)

We now have a rectangle with the same base and height as our parallelogram. The area of this rectangle equals the area of our parallelogram:

$$
\textrm{Area of Parallelogram} = \textrm{Area of Rectangle} = \textrm{base} \times \textrm{height}
$$
