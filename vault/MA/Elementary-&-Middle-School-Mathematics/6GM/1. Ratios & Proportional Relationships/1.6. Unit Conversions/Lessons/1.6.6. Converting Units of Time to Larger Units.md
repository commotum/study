## Introduction

In previous lessons, we explored converting larger units of time to smaller ones. In this lesson, we will learn how to convert smaller units of time to larger ones.

To convert between units of time, we use the following unit conversions:

- $1 \textrm{week} = 7 \,\textrm{days}$
- $1 \,\textrm{day} = 24 \,\textrm{hours}$
- $1 \,\textrm{hour} = 60 \,\textrm{minutes}$
- $1 \,\textrm{minute} = 60 \,\textrm{seconds}$

Time is typically measured using clocks, and we can use clocks to visualize equivalent units of time.

For example, the image below shows one complete revolution of the *seconds* hand on a stopwatch. It takes precisely one minute (or $60$ seconds) for this hand to complete one full rotation.

![](<../Source/1.6.6. Converting Units of Time to Larger Units/Images/4181e273b49efb25a011f8e57ff24317.png>)

Let's get some practice at converting between different units of time.

<a id="a-worked-example"></a>
## A Worked Example

Suppose we want to determine how many weeks are in $35$ days.

We start with the unit conversion between weeks and days:

$$
1\, \textrm{week} = 7 \, \textrm{days}
$$

Since we wish to convert *from* days *to* weeks, we divide both sides by $7$ and obtain the following:

$$
\dfrac{1}{7} \, \textrm{weeks} = 1 \, \textrm{day}
$$

This equation tells us how many weeks are in $1$ day. To figure out how many weeks are in $35$ days, we multiply both sides of the above equation by ${\color{blue}{35}}{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
35 \cdot \frac{1}{7}weeks &= 35 \cdot 1day \\
\frac{35}{7}weeks &= 35days \\
(35 \div 7)weeks &= 35days \\
5weeks &= 35days
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, $35$ days equals $5$ weeks.

## Converting From Days to Weeks

**Example:** How many weeks are in $40$ days?

**Explanation**

We start with the unit conversion between weeks and days:

$$
1\, \textrm{week} = 7 \, \textrm{days}
$$

Now, we divide both sides by $7$ and obtain the following:

$$
\dfrac{1}{7} \, \textrm{weeks} = 1 \, \textrm{day}
$$

We want to figure out how many weeks are in $40$ days. So, we multiply both sides of the above equation by $40{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
40 \cdot \frac{1}{7}weeks &= 40 \cdot 1day \\
\frac{40}{7}weeks &= 40days \\
5\frac{5}{7}weeks &= 40days
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, $40$ days equals

$$
5\,\dfrac{5}{7}
$$

weeks.

## Converting From Hours to Days

**Example:** How many days are in $408$ hours?

**Explanation**

We start with the unit conversion between days and hours:

$$
1\, \textrm{day} = 24 \, \textrm{hours}
$$

Now, we divide both sides by $24$ and obtain the following:

$$
\dfrac{1}{24} \, \textrm{days} = 1 \, \textrm{hour}
$$

We want to figure out how many days are in $408$ hours. So, we multiply both sides of the above equation by $408{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
408 \cdot \frac{1}{24}days &= 408 \cdot 1hour \\
\frac{408}{24}days &= 408hours
\end{aligned}
$$

We can compute

$$
\dfrac{408}{24}
$$

using long division.

|  |  | $1$ | $7$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $24$ | $\!\!\require{enclose}\enclose{longdiv}{4 {\:\phantom{\mid}} 0 {\:\phantom{\mid}} 8}$ |  |  |
| $-\!\!\!\!$ | $2$ | $4$ |  |
|  | $1$ | $6$ | $8$ |
| $-\!\!\!\!$ | $1$ | $6$ | $8$ |
|  |  | $\phantom{0}$ | $0$ |

Therefore, $408$ hours equals $17$ days.

## Converting From Minutes to Hours

**Example:** How many hours are in $110$ minutes?

**Explanation**

We start with the unit conversion between hours and minutes:

$$
1\, \textrm{hour} = 60 \, \textrm{minutes}
$$

Now, we divide both sides by $60$ and obtain the following:

$$
\dfrac{1}{60} \, \textrm{hours} = 1 \, \textrm{minute}
$$

We want to figure out how many hours are in $110$ minutes. So, we multiply both sides of the above equation by $110{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
110 \cdot \frac{1}{60}hours &= 110 \cdot 1minute \\
\frac{110}{60}hours &= 110minutes \\
\frac{110 \div 10}{60 \div 10}hours &= 110minutes \\
\frac{11}{6}hours &= 110minutes \\
1\frac{5}{6}hours &= 110minutes
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, $110$ minutes equals

$$
1\,\dfrac{5}{6}
$$

hours.

## Converting From Seconds to Minutes

**Example:** How many minutes are in $270$ seconds?

**Explanation**

We start with the unit conversion between minutes and seconds:

$$
1\, \textrm{minute} = 60 \, \textrm{seconds}
$$

Now, we divide both sides by $60$ and obtain the following:

$$
\dfrac{1}{60} \, \textrm{minutes} = 1 \, \textrm{second}
$$

We want to figure out how many minutes are in $270$ seconds. So, we multiply both sides of the above equation by $270{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
270 \cdot \frac{1}{60}minutes &= 270 \cdot 1second \\
\frac{270}{60}minutes &= 270seconds \\
\frac{270 \div 30}{60 \div 30}minutes &= 270seconds \\
\frac{9}{2}minutes &= 270seconds \\
4\frac{1}{2}minutes &= 270seconds
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, $270$ seconds equals

$$
4\,\dfrac{1}{2}
$$

minutes.
