## Introduction

When working with fractions, percents, and decimals, we often need to decide which number is greater or smaller. Since these numbers are written in different forms, it can be difficult to compare them directly.

To make comparisons easier, we can *convert them to a common form*.

For example, let's compare the percent $48\%$ and the decimal $0.52$.

One way to do that is to convert the percent to a decimal. So, we divide the percent value by $100{:}$

$$
48 \div 100 = 0.48
$$

Thus, $48\%$ is equivalent to $0.48$.

Now, we can compare the two decimals:
$0.48 < 0.52$

Therefore, we can conclude that $48\%$ is smaller than $0.52$.

Let's see more examples.

**Explanation**

$$

$$

Therefore, $0.35$ is equivalent to $\bbox[4pt, border: solid 1pt gainsboro]{35\%}$,
which is $\bbox[4pt, border: solid 1pt gainsboro]{\text{greater than}}$ $30\%$.

<a id="comparing-a-percent-to-a-fraction"></a>
## Comparing a Percent to a Fraction

**Example:** Compare $60\%$ and $\dfrac{11}{15}$ by filling in the missing parts in the statement below.

$\qquad$
$60\%$ is equivalent to $AA$ which is $AAAAAA$ $\dfrac{11}{15}$.

**Explanation**

To convert the percent to a fraction, we first write $60\%$ as a fraction with a denominator $100{:}$

$$
60\% = \dfrac{60}{100}
$$

We can simplify the fraction by dividing the numerator and denominator by $10{:}$

$$
\dfrac{60}{100} = \dfrac{60 \div 10}{100 \div 10} = \dfrac{6}{10}
$$

Then, we divide the numerator and denominator by $2{:}$

$$
\dfrac{6}{10} = \dfrac{6 \div 2}{10 \div 2} = \dfrac{3}{5}
$$

We can write this with a denominator of $15$ by multiplying the numerator and denominator by $3{:}$

$$
\dfrac{3}{5} = \dfrac{3 \times 3}{5 \times 3} = \dfrac{9}{15}
$$

Now, notice that

$$
\dfrac{9}{15} \lt \dfrac{11}{15}
$$

Therefore, $60\%$ equivalent to

$$
\bbox[4pt, border: solid 1pt gray]{\dfrac{9}{15}}
$$

which is $\bbox[4pt, border: solid 1pt gray]{\text{less than}}$

$$
\dfrac{11}{15}
$$

## Comparing Decimals and Percents in Context

Suppose Ethan and Ryan are baseball players. In the previous season, $24\%$ of Ethan’s hits were home runs, while the decimal proportion of Ryan’s hits that were home runs was $0.3$.

Let’s determine whether either Ethan or Ryan hit home runs on more than half of their total hits.

- *For Ethan*, we compare his statistic to one-half written as a percentage, $50\%$. Since
$24\% < 50\%$,
less than half of Ethan’s hits were home runs.
- *For Ryan*, we compare his statistic to one-half written as a decimal, $0.5$. Since
$0.3 < 0.5$,
less than half of Ryan’s hits were home runs.

Therefore, neither player hit home runs on more than half of their hits.

## Identifying True Statements in Context

**Example:** Emma and Sofia are art students. In the previous semester, $18 \%$ of the paintings created by Emma were landscapes, while the decimal proportion of Sofia’s paintings that were landscapes was
$0.2$.

Which of the following statements are true?

1. Less than half of Emma's paintings were landscapes.
2. More than half of Sofia's paintings were landscapes.
3. Emma had a greater percentage of landscape paintings compared to Sofia.

**Explanation**

Let's examine our statements in turn.

- Statement I is true. Indeed, since
$18\% < 50\%$,
less than half of Emma's paintings were landscapes.
- Statement II is false. Notice that
$0.2 < 0.5$.
So, less than half of Sofia's paintings were landscapes.
- Statement III is false. We can compare $18\%$ and $\dfrac{1}{5}$ by converting the decimal into a percent. To do this, we multiply it by $100{:}$
$0.2 \times 100 = 20\%$
Since $18\% < 20 \%$, Emma had a **smaller** percentage of landscape paintings compared to Sofia.

Therefore, the correct answer is "I only".

## Comparing Fractions and Percents in Context

Now, suppose Carlos and Henry have the same number of trading cards. A total of $32 \%$ of Carlos's cards are rare cards, while

$$
\dfrac{1}{4}
$$

of Henry's cards are rare cards.

Who has more rare cards?

To answer this, we need to *compare the fraction and the percentage*:

$$
\dfrac{1}{4} \qquad\text{and}\qquad 32 \%
$$

A common method is to convert the fraction to a percentage.

First, we write

$$
\dfrac{1}{4}
$$

as an equivalent fraction with $100$ as the denominator:

$$
\dfrac{1}{4} = \dfrac{1 \times 25}{4 \times 25} = \dfrac{25}{100}
$$

Now, we can write this fraction as a percentage:

$$
\dfrac{25}{100} = 25 \%
$$

Since $25 \% < 32 \%$, we can conclude that Carlos has *more* rare cards than Carlos.

## Comparing Fractions and Percents: Word Problem

**Example:** Noah and Lucas have the same number of books. A total of $68 \%$ of Noah's books are fiction, while $\dfrac{7}{10}$ of Lucas's books are fiction.

Compare the number of books Noah and Lucas have by filling in the missing parts in the statement below.

$\qquad$
A total of $AA$ of Lucas's books are fiction.

$\qquad$
Therefore, he has $AAAAAA$ fiction books compared to Noah.

**Explanation**

We need to compare

$$
\dfrac{7}{10}
$$

with $68 \%$. We can do this by writing

$$
\dfrac{7}{10}
$$

as a percent.

First, we write

$$
\dfrac{7}{10}
$$

as an equivalent fraction with $100$ as the denominator:

$$
\dfrac{7}{10} = \dfrac{7 \times 10}{10 \times 10} = \dfrac{70}{100}
$$

Now, we can write this fraction as a percentage:

$$
\dfrac{70}{100} = 70 \%
$$

Since $70 \% > 68 \%$, we have the following:

- A total of $\bbox[4pt, border: solid 1pt gainsboro]{70\%}$ of Lucas's books are fiction.
- Therefore, he has $\bbox[4pt, border: solid 1pt gainsboro]{\text{more}}$ fiction books compared to Noah.
