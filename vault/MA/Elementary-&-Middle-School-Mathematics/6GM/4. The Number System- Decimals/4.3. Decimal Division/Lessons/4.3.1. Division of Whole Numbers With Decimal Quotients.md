## Introduction

When we divide two whole numbers using long division, we often get a remainder. In this lesson, we will learn how to use long division to express the quotient and remainder as a decimal.

To illustrate, let's compute $4 \div 5$. We start by writing down $4\div 5$ using long division notation:

| $\,\,0$$5$$\!\require{enclose}\enclose{longdiv}{\color{red}4}$ |
| --- |

Notice that $\color{red}4$ is less than the divisor $5$. But we can proceed further by adding a $\color{blue}0$ after the decimal point in the dividend. We also add a decimal point in the quotient, as shown below:

| $\,0 {\bbox[2pt, lightgray]{\color{blue}.}}$$5$$\!\!\require{enclose}\enclose{longdiv}{{\color{red}4} {\bbox[2pt, lightgray]{\color{blue}.}} {\color{blue}0}}$ |
| --- |

Now we complete the division, as usual, stopping once we obtain a remainder of $0$:

| $\,0 {\,.\,} 8$$5$$\!\!\require{enclose}\enclose{longdiv}{4 {\,. \,} 0}$$-\!\!\!\!\!$$4$$0$$\fbox{0}$ |
| --- |

Therefore,

$$
4 \div 5 = 0.8\,
$$

## Dividing Two Numbers When the Quotient Has One Decimal Place

**Example:** Calculate the value of $46 \div 5$.

**Explanation**

We start by going through the long division procedure as usual:

| $\,\,\phantom{0} {\:\phantom{\mid}} 9$$5$$\!\require{enclose}\enclose{longdiv}{4 {\:\phantom{\mid}} 6}$$-\!\!\!\!$$4$$5$$\color{red}1$ |
| --- |

Notice that the remainder $\color{red}1$ is less than the divisor $5$. But we can proceed further by adding a $\color{blue}0$ after the decimal point in the dividend. We also add a decimal point in the quotient, as shown below:

| $\,\,\phantom{0} {\:\phantom{\mid}} 9 {\bbox[2px, lightgray]{\color{blue}.}}$$5$$\!\require{enclose}\enclose{longdiv}{4 {\:\phantom{\mid}} 6 {\bbox[2px, lightgray]{\color{blue}.}} {\color{blue}0}}$$-\!\!\!\!$$4$$5$$\color{lightgray}\downarrow$$\color{red}1$$\color{lightgray}0$ |
| --- |

Now we complete the division, as usual, stopping once we obtain a remainder of $0$:

| $\,\,\phantom{0} {\:\phantom{\mid}} 9 {\,. \,} 2$$5$$\!\require{enclose}\enclose{longdiv}{4 {\:\phantom{\mid}} 6 {\,. \,} 0}$$-\!\!\!\!$$4$$5$$1$$0$$-\!\!\!\!\!$$1$$0$$\fbox{0}$ |
| --- |

Therefore,

$$
46 \div 5 = 9.2\,
$$

## Dividing Two Numbers When the Quotient Has Two Decimal Places

**Example:** Find $51 \div 4$.

**Explanation**

We start by going through the long division procedure as usual:

| $\,\,1 {\:\phantom{\mid}} 2$$4$$\!\require{enclose}\enclose{longdiv}{5 {\:\phantom{\mid}} 1}$$-\!\!\!\!$$4$$1$$1$$-\!\!\!\!$$8$$\color{red}3$ |
| --- |

Notice that the remainder $\color{red}3$ is less than the divisor $4$, but we can proceed further by adding a $\color{blue}0$ after the decimal point in the dividend. We also add a decimal point in the quotient, as shown below:

| $\,\,1 {\:\phantom{\mid}} 2 {\bbox[2px, lightgray]{\color{blue}.}}$$4$$\!\require{enclose}\enclose{longdiv}{5 {\:\phantom{\mid}} 1 {\bbox[2px, lightgray]{\color{blue}.}} {\color{blue}0}}$$-\!\!\!\!$$4$$\color{lightgray}\downarrow$$1$$1$$\color{lightgray}\downarrow$$-\!\!\!\!$$8$$\color{lightgray}\downarrow$$\color{red}3$$\color{lightgray}0$ |
| --- |

Now we complete the division, as usual, adding more zeros as needed but stopping once we obtain a remainder of $0$:

| $\,\,1 {\:\phantom{\mid}} 2 {\,. \,} 7 {\:\phantom{\mid}} 5$$4$$\!\require{enclose}\enclose{longdiv}{5 {\:\phantom{\mid}} 1 {\,. \,} 0 {\:\phantom{\mid}} 0}$$-\!\!\!\!$$4$$1$$1$$-\!\!\!\!\!\!\!$$8$$3$$0$$-\!\!\!\!$$2$$8$$2$$0$$-\!\!\!\!$$2$$0$$\fbox{0}$ |
| --- |

Therefore,

$$
51 \div 4 = 12.75\,
$$

## Dividing Two Numbers When the Quotient Has Three Decimal Places

**Example:** What is $79 \div 8$?

**Explanation**

We start by going through the long division procedure as usual:

| $\,\phantom{0}{\:\phantom{\mid}} 9$$8$$\!\!\require{enclose}\enclose{longdiv}{7 {\:\phantom{\mid}} 9}$$-\!\!\!\!\!$$7$$2$${\color{red}7}$ |
| --- |

Notice that the remainder $\color{red}7$ is less than the divisor $8$, but we can proceed further by adding a $\color{blue}0$ after the decimal point in the dividend. We also add a decimal point in the quotient, as shown below:

| $\,\,\phantom{0} {\:\phantom{\mid}} 9 {\bbox[2px, lightgray]{\color{blue}.}}$$8$$\!\require{enclose}\enclose{longdiv}{7 {\:\phantom{\mid}} 9 {\bbox[2px, lightgray]{\color{blue}.}} {\color{blue}0}}$$-\!\!\!\!\!$$7$$2$$\color{lightgray}\downarrow$$\color{red}7$$\color{lightgray}0$ |
| --- |

Now we complete the division, as usual, adding more zeros as needed but stopping once we obtain a remainder of $0$:

| $\,\,{\phantom{0}} {\:\phantom{\mid}} 9 {\,. \,} 8 {\:\phantom{\mid}} 7 {\:\phantom{\mid}} 5$$8$$\!\require{enclose}\enclose{longdiv}{7 {\:\phantom{\mid}} 9 {\,. \,} 0 {\:\phantom{\mid}} 0 {\:\phantom{\mid}} 0}$$-\!\!\!\!\!$$7$$2$$7$$0$$-\!\!\!\!$$6$$4$$6$$0$$-\!\!\!$$5$$6$$4$$0$$-\!\!\!$$4$$0$$\fbox{0}$ |
| --- |

Therefore,

$$
79 \div 8 = 9.875\,
$$

## Dividing Two Numbers When the Quotient is a Decimal: Word Problems

**Example:** Cameron spent $\[math]607$ in five days. If she spent an equal amount each day, how much did Cameron spend per day?

**Explanation**

To determine the amount Cameron spent each day, we need to divide $607$ by $5$.

We start by going through the long division procedure as usual:

| $\,\,1 {\:\phantom{\mid}} 2 {\:\phantom{\mid}} 1$$5$$\!\require{enclose}\enclose{longdiv}{6 {\:\phantom{\mid}} 0 {\:\phantom{\mid}} 7}$$-\!\!\!\!$$5$$1$$0$$-\!\!\!\!$$1$$0$$0$$7$$-\!\!\!\!$$5$$\color{red}2$ |
| --- |

Notice that the remainder $\color{red}2$ is less than the divisor $5$. But we can proceed further by adding a $\color{blue}0$ after the decimal point in the dividend. We also add a decimal point in the quotient, as shown below:

| $\,\,1 {\:\phantom{\mid}} 2 {\:\phantom{\mid}} 1 {\bbox[2px, lightgray]{\color{blue}.}}$$5$$\!\require{enclose}\enclose{longdiv}{6 {\:\phantom{\mid}} 0 {\:\phantom{\mid}} 7{\bbox[2px, lightgray]{\color{blue}.}}\color{blue}0}$$-\!\!\!\!$$5$$\color{lightgray}\downarrow$$1$$0$$\color{lightgray}\downarrow$$-\!\!\!\!$$1$$0$$\color{lightgray}\downarrow$$0$$7$$\color{lightgray}\downarrow$$-\!\!\!\!$$5$$\color{lightgray}\downarrow$$\color{red}2$$\color{lightgray}0$ |
| --- |

Now we complete the division, as usual, stopping once we obtain a remainder of $0$:

| $\,\,1 {\:\phantom{\mid}} 2 {\:\phantom{\mid}} 1 {\,.\,} 4$$5$$\!\require{enclose}\enclose{longdiv}{6 {\:\phantom{\mid}} 0 {\:\phantom{\mid}} 7 {\,.\,} 0}$$-\!\!\!\!$$5$$1$$0$$-\!\!\!\!$$1$$0$$0$$7$$-\!\!\!\!$$5$$2$$0$$-\!\!\!\!$$2$$0$$\fbox{0}$ |
| --- |

Therefore,

$$
607 \div 5 = 121.4 \,
$$

We conclude that Cameron spent $\[math]121.40$ each day.
