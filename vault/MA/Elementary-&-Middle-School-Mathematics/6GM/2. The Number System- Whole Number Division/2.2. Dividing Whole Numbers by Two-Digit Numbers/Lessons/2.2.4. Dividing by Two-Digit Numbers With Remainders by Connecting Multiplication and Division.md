## Introduction

In this lesson, we will learn how to divide larger numbers by two-digit numbers *with remainders* using the relationship between multiplication and division.

To begin with, let's figure out how to compute $47\div 13$.

We will start by computing products of $13$ and other whole numbers, starting from $1$, until we get to a number that is greater than $47$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
13 \cdot 1 &= 13 \\
13 \cdot 2 &= 13 + 13 = 26 \\
13 \cdot 3 &= 26 + 13 = 39✓ \\
13 \cdot 4 &= 39 + 13 = 52\cdot([math]> {47}[/math], so too big)
\end{aligned}
$$

So, $13 \times {\color{blue}3}$ is *smaller than* $47$, but $13 \times 4$ is *greater than* $47$. This means that ${47}\div {13}$ has a whole number part (or quotient) of ${\color{blue}3}$.

To find the remainder, we subtract the product from the dividend:

$$
{47} - {39} = {\color{red}8}
$$

Therefore,

$$
{47}\div {13} = {\color{blue}3}\,\textrm{R}\,{\color{red}8}
$$

## Dividing a Two-Digit Number by a Two-Digit Number

**Example:** Calculate $99 \div 14$ using the relationship between multiplication and division.

**Explanation**

Using our knowledge of multiplication:

$$
\begin{aligned}
14 \cdot 5 &= 70 \\
14 \cdot 6 &= 70 + 14 = 84 \\
14 \cdot 7 &= 84 + 14 = 98✓ \\
14 \cdot 8 &= 98 + 14 = 112\cdot(too big)
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, $99 \div 14$ has a whole number part of ${\color{blue}{7}}$.

We now find the remainder:

$$
99 - 98 = {\color{red}{1}}
$$

Therefore,

$$
99 \div 14 = {\color{blue}{7}}\,\textrm{R}\,{\color{red}{1}}
$$

## Dividing a Three-Digit Number by a Two-Digit Number

**Example:** Calculate the quotient of $681\div 32$ using the relationship between multiplication and division.

**Explanation**

Using our knowledge of multiplication:

$$
\begin{aligned}
32 \cdot 20 &= 640 \\
32 \cdot 21 &= 640 + 32 = 672✓ \\
32 \cdot 22 &= 672 + 32 = 704\cdot(too big)
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, $681\div 32$ has a quotient of ${\color{blue}{21}}$.

## Solving a Word Problem Involving Division

**Example:** A toy factory can assemble $460$ toy cars per day. If these cars are stored in boxes containing up to $15$ cars each, how many full boxes per day does the factory produce?

**Explanation**

To find how many boxes of $15$ the factory can fill, we need to find the quotient of $460 \div 15$.

Using our knowledge of multiplication:

$$
\begin{aligned}
15 \cdot 30 &= 450✓ \\
15 \cdot 31 &= 450 + 15 = 465\cdot(too big)
\end{aligned}
$$

Since $465$ is greater than $460$, the factory can't fill $31$ boxes.

Therefore, the factory can produce $\color{blue}30$ full boxes of toy cars each day.

## Solving Word Problems Involving Addition and Division

**Example:** Sean and Martin brought cupcakes for a party. Sean brought $35$ cupcakes, and Martin brought $46$. If each plate can hold up to $11$ cupcakes, how many plates can be filled completely? And how many cupcakes will remain?

**Explanation**

The total number of cupcakes to be distributed is

$$
35+46=81
$$

To calculate the number of plates that can be filled, we need to figure out $81\div 11$.

Using our knowledge of multiplication:

$$
\begin{aligned}
11 \cdot 7 &= 77✓ \\
11 \cdot 8 &= 77 + 11 = 88\cdot(too big)
\end{aligned}
$$

Since $88$ is greater than $81$, Sean and Martin can't fill $8$ plates. Therefore, $7$ plates can be completely filled with cupcakes.

Since $\color{blue}7$ plates will carry $77$ cupcakes in total, the number of cupcakes that will remain is

$$
81 - 77 ={\color{red}4}
$$

So we conclude that $7$ plates can be filled completely, and $4$ cupcakes will remain.
