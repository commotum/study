## Introduction

A **linear expression** is an algebraic expression that contains a variable, but the variable is only multiplied by a number or divided by a number.

Some examples of linear expressions are $3n+5$,

$$
x-\dfrac12
$$

and

$$
\dfrac{y}{5}+2
$$

To **evaluate** an algebraic expression, we replace the variable with the value that it represents and then simplify.

For example, suppose we are given the expression $3n+5$ and we know that $n=6$.
Then $n$ represents the number $6$, so we replace $n$ with $6{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
3n + 5 &=  \\
3 \cdot n + 5 &=  \\
3 \cdot 6 + 5 &=  \\
18 + 5 &=  \\
&= 23 \mid
\end{aligned}
$$

So, when $n=6$, the value of the expression $3n+5$ is $23$.

$$
\begin{aligned}

\end{aligned}

## Combining Different Operations in Expressions

We can combine several arithmetic operations in linear expressions.

For example, given that $m = 6$, let's compute $2m + 5$.

Replacing $m$ with $6$ in the expression, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
2m + 5 &=  \\
2 \cdot 6 + 5 &=  \\
12 + 5 &=  \\
&= 17 \mid
\end{aligned}
$$

## Evaluating Linear Expressions in Context

Finally, let's see how we can evaluate linear expressions in context.

Suppose the temperature (in degrees Celsius) after $t$ hours of a liquid mixture as it cools is given by the expression
$38 - 4t$.
What is the temperature of the mixture after

$$
\dfrac52
$$

hours?

To determine the temperature after

$$
\dfrac{5}{2}
$$

hours, we replace $t$ with

$$
\dfrac52
$$

and evaluate the resulting expression:

$$
\begin{aligned}
38 - 4t &=  \\
38 - 4 \cdot t &=  \\
38 - \frac{4}{1} \cdot \frac{5}{2} &=  \\
38 - \frac{4}{2} \cdot \frac{5}{1} &=  \\
38 - 2 \cdot 5 &=  \\
38 - 10 &=  \\
&= 28
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the temperature after

$$
\dfrac52
$$

hours is $28\,^\circ\textrm{C}$.

## Evaluating Linear Expressions: Word Problems

**Example:** The distance (in kilometers) that a delivery drone travels after $t$ hours is given by the expression
$14 - 3t$.
What distance does the drone travel after $\dfrac{5}{2}$ hours?

**Explanation**

To determine the distance after

$$
\dfrac{5}{2}
$$

hours, we replace $t$ with

$$
\dfrac52
$$

and evaluate the resulting expression:

$$
\begin{aligned}
14 - 3t &=  \\
14 - 3 \cdot t &=  \\
14 - \frac{3}{1} \cdot \frac{5}{2} &=  \\
14 - \frac{15}{2} &=  \\
\frac{28}{2} - \frac{15}{2} &=  \\
&= \frac{13}{2} \mid
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the distance the drone travels after

$$
\dfrac{5}{2}
$$

hours is

$$
\bbox[4pt,border: 1px solid lightgray]{\dfrac{13}{2}}
$$

kilometers.
