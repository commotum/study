## Introduction

Two expressions are **equivalent** if they always have the same value, even if they look different.

A useful way to check whether two expressions are equivalent is to simplify each one. If they can be written in the same form, they are equivalent. This often involves using techniques like *distributing*, *collecting like terms*, or *factoring*.

For example, let's check if $2(3x+4)$ and $8x+8-2x$ are equivalent.

The first expression, $2(3x+4)$, is already simplified in a factored form. We will now simplify the second expression, $8x+8-2x$, to see if we can get it into the same form.

First, we simplify the second expression by collecting like terms:

$$
\begin{aligned}
8x + 8 - 2x &=  \\
8x - 2x + 8 &=  \\
(8 - 2)x + 8 &=  \\
&= 6x + 8
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we factor out the greatest common factor. Since

$$
\text{GCF}(6x,8)=2
$$

we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
6x + 8 &=  \\
(2 \cdot 3x) + (2 \cdot 4) &=  \\
&= 2(3x + 4) \mid.✓
\end{aligned}
$$

The simplified form, $2(3x+4)$, matches the first expression.

Since the expressions can be written in the same form, they are equivalent.

## Identifying Equivalent Expressions by Collecting Like Terms and Factoring

**Example:** Which of the following expressions is equivalent to $4(3x-7)$?

1. $12x-28$
2. $5x-7-2x$
3. $6x+6x-32$

**Explanation**

To determine which of the expressions are equivalent to $4(3x-7)$, we first collect like terms and then factor each result:

- We factor out the greatest common factor in expression I. Since $\text{GCF}(12x,28)=4$, we get
$12x - 28|=; (4 \cdot 3x) - (4 \cdot 7)|=; 4 \cdot (3x - 7) \mid.✓$
So, expression I *is* equivalent to $4(3x-7)$.
- There are two like terms in expression II, $5x$ and $-2x$. Combining these terms, we have
$5x - 7 - 2x|=; 5x - 2x - 7|=; (5 - 2)x - 7|=; 3x - 7$.
Now, we factor out the greatest common factor. Since $\text{GCF}(3x,7)=1$, there is nothing to factor out.
$3x - 7 \qquad{\color{red}\times}$
So, expression II *is not* equivalent to $4(3x-7)$.
- There are two like terms in expression III, $6x$ and $6x$. Combining these terms, we have
$6x + 6x - 32|=; (6 + 6)x - 32|=; 12x - 32$.
Now, we factor out the greatest common factor. Since $\text{GCF}(12x,32)=4$, we get
$12x - 32|=; (4 \cdot 3x) - (4 \cdot 8)|=; 4 \cdot (3x - 8) \mid.\cdot$
So, expression III *is not* equivalent to $4(3x-7)$.

Therefore, the correct answer is "I only".

## Identifying Equivalent Expressions by Collecting Like Terms and Distributing

**Example:** Which of the following expressions is equivalent to $30x+25$?

1. $5(9x+5-3x)$
2. $3(8x+4+2x)$
3. $10(4x-2x+5)$

**Explanation**

To determine which of the expressions are equivalent to $30x+25$, we first collect like terms and then distribute the number outside each pair of parentheses:

- There are two like terms inside the parentheses of expression I, $9x$ and $-3x$. Combining these terms, we have
$9x + 5 - 3x|=; 9x - 3x + 5|=; (9 - 3)x + 5|=; 6x + 5$.
So, expression I is equivalent to
$5(9x + 5 - 3x)|=; 5(6x + 5)$.
Now, using the distributive law, we multiply each term inside the parentheses by the number $5{:}$
$5(6x + 5)|=; (5 \cdot 6x) + (5 \cdot 5)|=; 30x + 25 \mid ✓$
So, expression I *is* equivalent to $30x+25$.
- There are two like terms inside the parentheses of expression II, $8x$ and $2x$. Combining these terms, we have
$8x + 4 + 2x|=; 8x + 2x + 4|=; (8 + 2)x + 4|=; 10x + 4$.
So, expression II is equivalent to
$3(8x + 4 + 2x)|=; 3(10x + 4)$.
Now, using the distributive law, we multiply each term inside the parentheses by the number $3{:}$
$3(10x + 4)|=; (3 \cdot 10x) + (3 \cdot 4)|=; 30x + 12 \mid \cdot$
So, expression II *is not* equivalent to $30x+25$.
- There are two like terms inside the parentheses of expression III, $4x$ and $-2x$. Combining these terms, we have
$4x - 2x + 5|=; (4 - 2)x + 5|=; 2x + 5$.
So, expression III is equivalent to
$10(4x - 2x + 5)|=; 10(2x + 5)$.
Now, using the distributive law, we multiply each term inside the parentheses by the number $10{:}$
$10(2x + 5)|=; (10 \cdot 2x) + (10 \cdot 5)|=; 20x + 50 \mid \cdot$
So, expression III *is not* equivalent to $30x+25$.

Therefore, the correct answer is "I only".

## Identifying Equivalent Expressions by Distributing, Collecting Like Terms, and Factoring

**Example:** $8b+5+3(4b-6)=$

**Explanation**

First, using the distributive law, we multiply each term inside the parentheses by $3{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
8b + 5 + 3(4b - 6) &=  \\
8b + 5 + 3 \cdot 4b - 3 \cdot 6 &=  \\
&= 8b + 5 + 12b - 18
\end{aligned}
$$

The resulting expression has two groups like terms, $8b$ and $12b$, and $5$ and $-18$. Combining the terms in each group, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
8b + 5 + 12b - 18 &=  \\
8b + 12b + 5 - 18 &=  \\
(8 + 12)b + (5 - 18) &=  \\
&= 20b - 13 \mid
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we factor out the greatest common factor. Since

$$
\text{GCF}(20b,13)=1
$$

there is nothing to factor out.

$$
20b - 13
$$

Therefore, the expression is equivalent to $20b-13$.
