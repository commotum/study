## Introduction

When we **factor** a linear expression, we rewrite it as a product.

A common situation is when two terms share a greatest common factor (GCF).
To factor, we do two steps:

1. Write each term as a product using the GCF.
2. Pull the GCF out in front of parentheses.

To demonstrate, let's factor the expression $32x + 12$.

1. First, find the greatest common factor: $\textrm{GCF}(32x,12)=4$. Since $32x = 4 \cdot 8x$ and $12 = 4\cdot 3$, we separate that factor from each term as follows:
$32x + 12|=; (4 \cdot 8x) + (4 \cdot 3)$
2. Now the factor $4$ is common to both terms, so we place it in front of the parentheses:
$(4 \cdot 8x) + (4 \cdot 3)|=; 4_(⏟)_(GCF)(8x + 3)$

After factoring, we can check our work by using the distributive law on $4(8x+3)$ to get back $32x+12$.

## Factoring A Sum of Two Linear Expressions

**Example:** $32x+12=$

**Explanation**

We factor in two steps:

1. Separate the greatest common factor from each of the terms. In this case, $\textrm{GCF}(32x, 12) = 4$, so
$32x + 12|=; (4 \cdot 8x) + (4 \cdot 3) \mid$.
2. Place the common factor in front of the parentheses, leaving whatever remains inside the parentheses:
$(4 \cdot 8x) + (4 \cdot 3)|=; 4(8x + 3)$

Thus, the factored form of the expression is $4(8x+3)$.

## Factoring a Difference of Two Linear Expressions

**Example:** $63x-36=$

**Explanation**

We factor in two steps:

1. Separate the greatest common factor from each of the terms (ignoring any negative signs). In this case, $\textrm{GCF}(63x,36) = 9$, so:
$63x - 36|=; (9 \cdot 7x) - (9 \cdot 4)$
2. Place the common factor in front of the parentheses, leaving whatever remains inside the parentheses:
$(9 \cdot 7x) - (9 \cdot 4)|=; 9(7x - 4)$

Thus, the factored form of the expression is $9(7x-4)$.

## Factoring Expressions With More Than Two Terms

**Example:** $4x+20y-12=$

**Explanation**

We factor in two steps:

1. Separate the greatest common factor from each of the terms (ignoring any negative signs). In this case, $\textrm{GCF}(4x,20y,12) = 4$, so
$4x + 20y - 12|=; (4 \cdot x) + (4 \cdot 5y) - (4 \cdot 3)$.
2. Place the common factor in front of the parentheses, leaving whatever remains inside the parentheses:
$(4 \cdot x) + (4 \cdot 5y) - (4 \cdot 3)|=; 4(x + 5y - 3)$

Thus, the factored form of the expression is $4(x + 5y - 3)$.
