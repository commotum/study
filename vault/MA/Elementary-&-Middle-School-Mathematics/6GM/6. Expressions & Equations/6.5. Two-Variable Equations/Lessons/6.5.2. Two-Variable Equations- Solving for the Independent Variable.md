## Introduction

Sometimes, after substituting the value of a variable into another equation, we may still need to use algebra to solve that equation.

For example, suppose that

$$
b = a + 2
$$

and $b = 5$, and we want to solve for $a$.

To combine these two equations, we can substitute the value

$$
\color{blue}b=5
$$

into the equation

$$
b=a+2
$$

as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
b &= a + 2 \\
5 &= a + 2
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we need to solve the resulting equation for $a$.

To isolate $a$, we need to perform the *opposite of adding $2$* to $a$, which is *subtracting* $2$ from $a$. So we subtract $2$ from both sides of the equation and simplify:

$$
\begin{aligned}
5 - 2 &= a + 2 - 2 \\
3 &= a + 0 \\
3 &= a
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the solution is $a=3$.

## Solving for the Independent Variable Using Addition or Subtraction

**Example:** Find the value of $q$ given that $p = q + 6$ and $p = 19$.

**Explanation**

To find the value of $q$, we first substitute $p = 19$ into this equation:

$$
19 = q + 6
$$

Now, we solve for $q$ by subtracting $6$ from both sides of this equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
19 &= q + 6 \\
19 - 6 &= q + 6 - 6 \\
13 &= q + 0 \\
13 &= q \\
q &= 13
\end{aligned}
$$

## Solving for the Independent Variable Using Multiplication or Division

**Example:** Find the value of $w$ given that $z = \dfrac{w}{4}$ and $z = 7$.

**Explanation**

To find the value of $w$, we first substitute $z = 7$ into this equation:

$$
7 = \dfrac{w}{4}
$$

Now, we solve for $w$ by multiplying both sides of this equation by $4$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
7 &= \frac{w}{4} \\
7 \cdot 4 &= \frac{w}{4} \cdot 4 \\
28 &= \frac{4w}{4} \\
28 &= w \\
w &= 28
\end{aligned}
$$

## Solving Contextual Two-Variable Equations

**Example:** At the beginning of the year, there were $n$ students in the sixth-grade class. There are currently $m$ students in this class. The relationship between $n$ and $m$ is given by the equation $m = n + 7$. If there are currently $38$ students in the sixth-grade class, how many students did this class have at the beginning of the year?

**Explanation**

If there are currently $38$ students in the sixth-grade class, we have that $m = 38$.
So, we substitute $m = 38$ into the equation

$$
m = n + 7
$$

and get:

$$
38 = n + 7
$$

We solve for $n$ by subtracting $7$ from both sides of this equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
38 &= n + 7 \\
38 - 7 &= n + 7 - 7 \\
31 &= n + 0 \\
31 &= n
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, there were $31$ students in the sixth-grade class at the beginning of the year.

## Equations Requiring Two-Step Solutions

Next, suppose we would like to solve for $x$ given that

$$
y+2=\dfrac{x}{8}
$$

and $y=4$.

To find the value of $x$, we first substitute $y = 4$ into this equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
4 + 2 &= \frac{x}{8} \\
6 &= \frac{x}{8}
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we solve for $x$ by multiplying both sides of this equation by $8{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
6 &= \frac{x}{8} \\
6 \cdot 8 &= \frac{x}{8} \cdot 8 \\
48 &= \frac{8x}{8} \\
48 &= x \\
x &= 48
\end{aligned}
$$

## Solving for the Independent Variable Using Two Steps

**Example:** Solve for $a$ if $3a = b + 5$ and $b = 10$.

**Explanation**

To find the value of $a$, we first substitute $b = 10$ into this equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
3a &= 10 + 5 \\
3a &= 15
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we solve for $a$ by dividing both sides of this equation by $3{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
3a &= 15 \\
\frac{3a}{3} &= \frac{15}{3} \\
\frac{3a}{3} &= 5 \\
a &= 5
\end{aligned}
$$
