## Introduction

We can find which values satisfy an inequality by substituting a number for the variable and checking whether the result is true.

For example, consider the following inequality

$$
y - 1 > 3
$$

Which of the following values of $y$ satisfies the inequality?

- $y=6$
- $y=8$
- $y=3$

Let's go through the possibilities one by one.

- Substituting the value ${\color{blue}y}={\color{blue}6}$ into the given inequality, we get:
$\begin{bmatrix}y - 1 & > 3 \\ 6 - 1 & > 3 \\ 5 & > 3✓\end{bmatrix}$
This is a *true* statement, so $y=6$ *satisfies* this inequality.
- Substituting the value ${\color{blue}y}={\color{blue}8}$ into the given inequality, we get:
$\begin{bmatrix}y - 1 & > 3 \\ 8 - 1 & > 3 \\ 7 & > 3✓\end{bmatrix}$
This is a *true* statement, so $y=8$ *satisfies* this inequality.

- Substituting the value ${\color{blue}y}={\color{blue}3}$ into the given inequality, we get:
$\begin{bmatrix}y - 1 & > 3 \\ 3 - 1 & > 3 \\ 2 & > 3\cdot\end{bmatrix}$
This is a *false* statement, so $y=3$ *does not satisfy* this inequality.

Therefore, the values that satisfy the inequality are $y = 6$ and $y = 8$.

## Identifying Values Satisfying a Whole Number Inequality

**Example:** Which of the following values of $x$ satisfy the inequality $2x < 10\,$?

1. $x=0$
2. $x=2$
3. $x=5$

**Explanation**

Let's go through the possibilities one at a time.

- Substituting the value ${\color{blue}x}={\color{blue}0}$ into the given inequality, we get:
$\begin{bmatrix}2x & < 10 \\ 2 \cdot x & < 10 \\ 2 \cdot 0 & < 10 \\ 0 & < 10✓\end{bmatrix}$
This is a *true* statement, so $x=0$ *satisfies* this inequality.
- Substituting the value ${\color{blue}x}={\color{blue}2}$ into the given inequality, we get:
$\begin{bmatrix}2x & < 10 \\ 2 \cdot x & < 10 \\ 2 \cdot 2 & < 10 \\ 4 & < 10✓\end{bmatrix}$
This is a *true* statement, so $x=2$ *satisfies* this inequality.
- Substituting the value ${\color{blue}x}={\color{blue}5}$ into the given inequality, we get:
$\begin{bmatrix}2x & < 10 \\ 2 \cdot x & < 10 \\ 2 \cdot 5 & < 10 \\ 10 & < 10\cdot\end{bmatrix}$
This is a *false* statement, so $x=5$ *does not satisfy* this inequality.

Therefore, only I and II satisfy the inequality.

## Identifying Values Satisfying an Inequality Containing Fractions

**Example:** Which of the following values of $t$ satisfy the inequality $\dfrac{2t}{3} < 8\,$?

1. $t=9$
2. $t=6$
3. $t=3$

**Explanation**

Let's go through the possibilities one at a time.

- Substituting the value ${\color{blue}t}={\color{blue}9}$ into the given inequality, we get:
$\begin{bmatrix}\frac{2t}{3} & < 8 \\ \frac{2 \cdot t}{3} & < 8 \\ \frac{2 \cdot 9}{3} & < 8 \\ \frac{18}{3} & < 8 \\ 6 & < 8✓\end{bmatrix}$
This is a *true* statement, so $t=9$ *satisfies* this inequality.
- Substituting the value ${\color{blue}t}={\color{blue}6}$ into the given inequality, we get:
$\begin{bmatrix}\frac{2t}{3} & < 8 \\ \frac{2 \cdot t}{3} & < 8 \\ \frac{2 \cdot 6}{3} & < 8 \\ \frac{12}{3} & < 8 \\ 4 & < 8✓\end{bmatrix}$
This is a *true* statement, so $t=6$ *satisfies* this inequality.
- Substituting the value ${\color{blue}t}={\color{blue}3}$ into the given inequality, we get:
$\begin{bmatrix}\frac{2t}{3} & < 8 \\ \frac{2 \cdot t}{3} & < 8 \\ \frac{2 \cdot 3}{3} & < 8 \\ \frac{6}{3} & < 8 \\ 2 & < 8✓\end{bmatrix}$
This is a *true* statement, so $t=3$ *satisfies* this inequality.

Therefore, I, II, and III all satisfy the inequality.

## Identifying Whole Number Inequalities Satisfied by a Value

**Example:** The value $a = 2$ satisfies which of the following inequalities?

1. $a + 2 > 5$
2. $2a < 6$
3. $3a - 1 < 10$

**Explanation**

Let's go through the possibilities one at a time.

- Substituting the value ${\color{blue}a} = {\color{blue}2}$ into the inequality I, we get:
$\begin{bmatrix}a + 2 & > 5 \\ 2 + 2 & > 5 \\ 4 & > 5\cdot\end{bmatrix}$
This is a *false* statement, so $a = 2$ *does not satisfy* this inequality.
- Substituting the value ${\color{blue}a}={\color{blue}2}$ into the inequality II, we get:
$\begin{bmatrix}2a & < 6 \\ 2 \cdot a & < 6 \\ 2 \cdot 2 & < 6 \\ 4 & < 6✓\end{bmatrix}$
This is a *true* statement, so $a = 2$ *satisfies* this inequality.
- Substituting the value ${\color{blue}a}={\color{blue}2}$ into the inequality III, we get:
$\begin{bmatrix}3a - 1 & < 10 \\ 3 \cdot a - 1 & < 10 \\ 3 \cdot 2 - 1 & < 10 \\ 5 & < 10✓\end{bmatrix}$
This is a *true* statement, so $a = 2$ *satisfies* this inequality.

Therefore, the value $a=2$ only satisfies inequalities II and III.

## Identifying Fraction Inequalities Satisfied by a Value

**Example:** The value $z = 1$ satisfies which of the following inequalities?

1. $5z + 4 < 8$
2. $z + \dfrac{1}{3} < \dfrac{2}{3}$
3. $\dfrac{z}{2} < 1$

**Explanation**

Let's go through the possibilities one at a time.

- Substituting the value ${\color{blue}z}={\color{blue}1}$ into inequality I, we get:
$\begin{bmatrix}5z + 4 & < 8 \\ 5 \cdot z + 4 & < 8 \\ 5 \cdot 1 + 4 & < 8 \\ 9 & < 8\cdot\end{bmatrix}$
This is a *false* statement, so $z = 1$ *does not satisfy* this inequality.
- Substituting the value ${\color{blue}z}={\color{blue}1}$ into inequality II, we get:
$\begin{bmatrix}z + \frac{1}{3} & < \frac{2}{3} \\ 1 + \frac{1}{3} & < \frac{2}{3} \\ \frac{1 \cdot 3 + 1}{3} & < \frac{2}{3} \\ \frac{4}{3} & < \frac{2}{3}\cdot\end{bmatrix}$
This is a *false* statement, so $z = 1$ *does not satisfy* this inequality.
- Substituting the value ${\color{blue}z}={\color{blue}1}$ into inequality III, we get:
$\begin{bmatrix}\frac{z}{2} & < 1 \\ \frac{1}{2} & < 1✓\end{bmatrix}$
This is a *true* statement, so $z = 1$ *satisfies* this inequality.

Therefore, the value $z=1$ only satisfies inequality III.
