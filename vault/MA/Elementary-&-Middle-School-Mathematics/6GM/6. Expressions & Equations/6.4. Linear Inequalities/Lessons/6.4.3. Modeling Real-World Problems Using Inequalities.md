## Introduction

In many real-world situations, we want to describe quantities that must stay *below*, *above*, or *at least* some value.

For example:

- A budget must stay *below* a certain amount.
- A factory must produce *at least* a certain number of items.
- A container must hold *no more than* a certain weight.

Situations like these are modeled using *inequalities*.

For example, suppose Liam originally had $s$ stickers. He gave $4$ of his stickers to a friend, and now he has fewer than $20$ stickers. Which inequality represents this situation?

We are told that Liam originally had $s$ stickers.

Since he gave $4$ stickers to a friend, the number of stickers he has now is
$s - 4$.

Because Liam now has *fewer than* $20$ stickers, this quantity must satisfy the following inequality:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}Liam's stickers after giving some away & < 20 \\ s - 4 & < 20\end{bmatrix}
$$

Therefore, the correct inequality is
$s - 4 < 20$

## Modeling Using Strict Inequalities

**Example:** Each notebook costs $4$ dollars. A student buys $x$ notebooks, and the total cost is less than $30$ dollars. Which inequality represents this situation?

**Explanation**

If each notebook costs $4$ dollars and a student buys $x$ notebooks, then the total cost can be written as

$$
4x
$$

Because the total cost is *less than* $30$ dollars, this quantity must satisfy the following inequality:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}Total cost & < 30 \\ 4x & < 30\end{bmatrix}
$$

Therefore, the correct inequality is

$$
4x < 30
$$

## Modeling Using Non-Strict Inequalities

**Example:** A bakery makes $x$ trays of muffins each morning. Each tray contains $6$ muffins. The bakery must bake at least $72$ muffins in the morning. Which inequality represents this situation?

**Explanation**

We are told that the bakery makes $x$ trays each morning.

Since each tray contains $6$ muffins, the total number of muffins baked can be written as

$$
6x
$$

Because the bakery must bake *at least* $72$ muffins, this quantity must satisfy the following inequality:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}Total muffins baked & \ge 72 \\ 6x & \ge 72\end{bmatrix}
$$

Therefore, the correct inequality is

$$
6x \geq 72
$$

## Modeling Using One-Step Inequalities With Fractions

**Example:** Each batch of paint uses $\dfrac{3}{4}$ liter of blue dye. If an artist mixes $x$ batches, the total amount of blue dye used is less than $6$ liters. Which inequality represents this situation?

**Explanation**

Each batch requires

$$
\dfrac{3}{4}
$$

liter of blue dye. If the artist mixes $x$ batches, then the total amount of blue dye used can be written as

$$
\dfrac{3}{4}x
$$

Because the total amount of blue dye used is *less than* $6$ liters, this quantity must satisfy the following inequality:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}Total dye used & < 6 \\ \frac{3}{4}x & < 6\end{bmatrix}
$$

Therefore, the correct inequality is

$$
\dfrac{3}{4}x < 6
$$
