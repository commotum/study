## Introduction

Many real-world situations involve an unknown quantity that we want to determine. By translating the description of the situation into an equation, we can represent the relationship between the quantities.

For example, a concert ticket costs a base fee of $\[math]15$ plus a service charge of $s$ dollars. If the total price of the ticket is $\[/math]42$, which equation can be used to find $s$?

The total cost is made up of two parts:

- the base ticket fee of $\[math]15$, and
- the service charge of $s$ dollars.

So, the total cost can be written as

$$
s + 15
$$

Since the total ticket price is $\[/math]42$, we equate this expression to $42$.

Therefore, the correct equation is

$$
s + 15 = 42
$$

Let's see more examples.

## Modeling Using One-Step Addition and Subtraction Equations

**Example:** A water tank contained $w$ liters of water at the start of the day. After $7.4$ liters were drained, $13.6$ liters remained. Which equation can be used to find $w$?

**Explanation**

The tank started with $w$ liters of water. Then, $7.4$ liters were drained, so the amount of water remaining can be written as

$$
w - 7.4
$$

Since $13.6$ liters remained, we equate this expression to $13.6$.

Therefore, the correct equation is

$$
w - 7.4 = 13.6
$$

## Modeling Using One-Step Multiplication Equations

Now, suppose a delivery truck carries $8$ boxes on each trip. If the truck delivers a total of $56$ boxes, which equation can be used to find the number of trips, $t$?

Each trip carries $8$ boxes. If the truck makes $t$ trips, then the total number of boxes delivered can be written as

$$
8t
$$

Since the truck delivered a total of $56$ boxes, we equate this expression to $56$.

Therefore, the correct equation is

$$
8t = 56
$$

## Modeling Using One-Step Multiplication and Division Equations

**Example:** A total of $p$ pencils are packed equally into $6$ boxes. If each box contains $9$ pencils, which equation can be used to find $p$?

**Explanation**

If $p$ pencils are packed equally into $6$ boxes, then the number of pencils in each box can be written as

$$
\dfrac{p}{6}
$$

Since each box contains $9$ pencils, we equate this expression to $9$.

Therefore, the correct equation is

$$
\dfrac{p}{6} = 9
$$

## Modeling Using One-Step Equations With Fractions

**Example:** A recipe uses $\dfrac{2}{5}$ of a cup of oil for each batch of cookies. If a baker used a total of $4$ cups of oil, which equation can be used to find the number of batches, $b$?

**Explanation**

Each batch requires

$$
\dfrac{2}{5}
$$

of a cup of oil. If the baker makes $b$ batches, then the total amount of oil used can be written as

$$
\dfrac{2}{5}b
$$

Since the baker used a total of $4$ cups of oil, we equate this expression to $4$.

Therefore, the correct equation is

$$
\dfrac{2}{5}b = 4
$$

---

- [ ] E. $\frac{2}{3} - x = \frac{11}{6}$
