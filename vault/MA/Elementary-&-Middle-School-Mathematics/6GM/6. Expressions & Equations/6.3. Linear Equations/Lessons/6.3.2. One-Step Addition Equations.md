## Introduction

To **solve** a linear equation, we isolate the variable by reversing the operations applied to it.

If a number is **added** to the variable, we use the **inverse operation**, which is subtraction.

Whatever operation we perform on one side of the equation, we must perform the same operation on the other side to keep the equation balanced.

For example, consider the equation

$$
x + 8 = 15
$$

Since $8$ is added to $x$, we subtract $8$ from both sides:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x + 8 &= 15 \\
x + 8 - 8 &= 15 - 8
\end{aligned}
$$

Now simplify each side:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x + (8 - 8) &= 15 - 8 \\
x + 0 &= 7 \\
x &= 7
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the solution is $x = 7$.

## Solving Addition Equations

**Example:** If $5 + x = 12$, then $x=$

**Explanation**

To solve a linear equation, we isolate the variable by reversing the operations applied to it, performing each inverse operation on both sides of the equation step by step.

In this case, to isolate $x$, we need to perform the *opposite of adding* $5$ to $x$. So, we *subtract* $5$ from $x$, remembering to perform the same operation on the right-hand side:

$$
\begin{aligned}
5 + x &= 12 \\
5 + x - 5 &= 12 - 5
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, simplifying each side, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
x + 5 - 5 &= 12 - 5 \\
x + (5 - 5) &= 12 - 5 \\
x + 0 &= 7 \\
x &= 7
\end{aligned}
$$

## Equations With the Variable on the Right-Hand Side

Sometimes the variable is on the right-hand side of the equation, such as

$$
14 = 5 + u
$$

We can make the equation easier to solve by swapping the left and right-hand sides:

$$
5 + u = 14
$$

Now we solve using inverse operations, as before. Since $5$ is added to $u$, we subtract $5$ from both sides and simplify:

$$
\begin{aligned}
5 + u &= 14 \\
5 + u - 5 &= 14 - 5 \\
u + (5 - 5) &= 14 - 5 \\
u + 0 &= 9 \\
u &= 9
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the solution is $u = 9$.

## Solving Addition Equations With the Variable on the Right-Hand Side

**Example:** If $16 = r + 9$, then $r=$

**Explanation**

To solve a linear equation, we isolate the variable by reversing the operations applied to it, performing each inverse operation on both sides of the equation step by step.

First, let's swap the left and right-hand sides so that the variable is on the left-hand side.

$$
r + 9 = 16
$$

In this case, to isolate $r$, we need to perform the *opposite of adding* $9$ to $r$. So, we *subtract* $9$ from $r$, remembering to perform the same operation on the right-hand side:

$$
\begin{aligned}
r + 9 &= 16 \\
r + 9 - 9 &= 16 - 9
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, simplifying each side, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
r + (9 - 9) &= 16 - 9 \\
r + 0 &= 7 \\
r &= 7
\end{aligned}
$$

## Solving Addition Equations: Rational Solutions

**Example:** If $\dfrac{5}{6} + y = \dfrac{11}{6}$, then $y=$

**Explanation**

To solve a linear equation, we isolate the variable by reversing the operations applied to it, performing each inverse operation on both sides of the equation step by step.

In this case, to isolate $y$, we need to perform the *opposite of adding*

$$
\dfrac{5}{6}
$$

to $y$. So, we *subtract*

$$
\dfrac{5}{6}
$$

from $y$, remembering to perform the same operation on the right-hand side:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{5}{6} + y &= \frac{11}{6} \\
\frac{5}{6} + y - \frac{5}{6} &= \frac{11}{6} - \frac{5}{6}
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, simplifying each side, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
y + \frac{5}{6} - \frac{5}{6} &= \frac{11}{6} - \frac{5}{6} \\
y + (\frac{5}{6} - \frac{5}{6}) &= \frac{11}{6} - \frac{5}{6} \\
y + 0 &= \frac{6}{6} \\
y &= 6 6
\end{aligned}
$$
