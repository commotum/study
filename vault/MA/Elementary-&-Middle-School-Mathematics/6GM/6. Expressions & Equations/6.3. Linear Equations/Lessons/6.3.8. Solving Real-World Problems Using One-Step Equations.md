## Introduction

Many real-world situations involve an unknown quantity that we can determine using an equation. By translating a real-world description into an equation, we can solve for the unknown value.

For example, a concert ticket costs a base fee of $\[math]18$ plus a service charge of $s$ dollars. If the total price of the ticket is $\[/math]45$, what is the value of $s$?

The total cost is made up of two parts:

- the base ticket fee of $\[math]18$, and
- the service charge of $s$ dollars.

So, the total cost can be written as

$$
s + 18
$$

Since the total ticket price is $\[/math]45$, we equate this expression to $45$.

Therefore, the equation is

$$
s + 18 = 45
$$

To isolate $s$, we need to perform the opposite of adding $18$, which is subtracting $18$.

So, we subtract $18$ from both sides of the equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
s + 18 &= 45 \\
s + 18 - 18 &= 45 - 18
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we simplify:

$$
\begin{aligned}
s + 0 &= 27 \\
s &= 27
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the service charge is $\[math]27$.

## Modeling Using One-Step Addition and Subtraction Equations

**Example:** A water tank contained $w$ liters of water at the beginning of the day. After $9.6$ liters were drained, $14.7$ liters remained. What is the value of $w$?

**Explanation**

The tank started with $w$ liters of water. Then, $9.6$ liters were drained, so the amount of water remaining can be written as

$$
w - 9.6
$$

Since $14.7$ liters remained, we equate this expression to $14.7$.

Therefore, the equation is

$$
w - 9.6 = 14.7
$$

To isolate $w$, we need to perform the opposite of subtracting $9.6$ from $w$, which is adding $9.6$ to $w$.

So, we add $9.6$ to both sides of the equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
w - 9.6 &= 14.7 \\
w - 9.6 + 9.6 &= 14.7 + 9.6
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we simplify:

$$
\begin{aligned}
w + 0 &= 24.3 \\
w &= 24.3
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the tank originally contained $24.3$ liters of water.

## Modeling Using One-Step Multiplication and Division Equations

**Example:** A bakery places $6$ muffins into each box. If the bakery packs a total of $48$ muffins, how many boxes did the bakery fill?

**Explanation**

Each box holds $6$ muffins. If the bakery fills $b$ boxes, then the total number of muffins packed can be written as

$$
6b
$$

Since the bakery packed a total of $48$ muffins, we equate this expression to $48$.

Therefore, the equation is

$$
6b = 48
$$

To isolate $b$, we divide both sides of the equation by $6{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
6b &= 48 \\
\frac{6b}{6} &= \frac{48}{6}
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we simplify:

$$
\begin{aligned}
b &= 8
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the bakery filled $8$ boxes.

## Modeling Using One-Step Equations With Fractions

**Example:** A ribbon of length $L$ meters is cut into $6$ equal pieces. If each piece is $\dfrac{2}{3}$ of a meter long, what is the length of the ribbon?

**Explanation**

If a ribbon of length $L$ meters is cut into $6$ equal pieces, then the length of each piece can be written as

$$
\dfrac{L}{6}
$$

Since each piece is

$$
\dfrac{2}{3}
$$

of a meter long, we equate this expression to

$$
\dfrac{2}{3}
$$

Therefore, the equation is

$$
\dfrac{L}{6} = \dfrac{2}{3}
$$

To isolate $L$, we multiply both sides of the equation by $6{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{L}{6} &= \frac{2}{3} \\
\frac{L}{6} \cdot 6 &= \frac{2}{3} \cdot 6
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we simplify:

$$
\begin{aligned}
L &= 4
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the length of the ribbon is $4$ meters.
