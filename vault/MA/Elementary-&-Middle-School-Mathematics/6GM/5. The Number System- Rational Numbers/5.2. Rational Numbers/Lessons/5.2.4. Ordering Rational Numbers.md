## Introduction

With the help of a number line, we can place a set of positive and negative whole numbers in order.
Let's place the following set of numbers from *least* to *greatest*:
$-3, \; 1, \; 2, \; -1, \; 4$

First, we plot all of these numbers on a number line:

![](<../Source/5.2.4. Ordering Rational Numbers/Images/64d62c41391e07949ce43c26c01c1e76.png>)

Recall that numbers to the left of a value on the line are less than the value.
So, going from left to right, we have:
$-3< -1 < 1 < 2 < 4$

Finally, we can write the numbers in order, from *least* to *greatest*:

$$
-3, \; -1, \; 1, \; 2, \; 4
$$

## Ordering Rational Numbers

We can use our knowledge of ordering whole numbers to place rational numbers in order.
To do this, we write the numbers with a common denominator, and then compare the numerators.

For example, let's place the following set of numbers in order, from *least* to *greatest*:

$$
\dfrac{5}{2}, \;-3, \; 1, \;4
$$

First, we write all of the numbers as fractions with a common denominator of ${\color{blue}2}$, as follows:

$$
\dfrac{5}{\color{blue}2}, \; -\dfrac{6}{\color{blue}2}, \; \dfrac{2}{\color{blue}2}, \; \dfrac {8}{\color{blue}2}
$$

Next, we bring any negative signs inside the numerator:

$$
\dfrac{5}{2}, \; \dfrac{(-6)}{2}, \; \dfrac{2}{2}, \; \dfrac 8 2
$$

Now, we order the numerators *least* to *greatest*:

$$
\dfrac{(-6)}{2} < \dfrac{2}{2} < \dfrac 5 2 < \dfrac{8}{2}
$$

Then, we change each fraction back to its original form:

$$
-3 < 1 < \dfrac{5}{2} < 4
$$

Finally, we can write the numbers in order, from *least* to *greatest*:

$$
-3, \; 1, \; \dfrac{5}{2}, \; 4
$$

## Ordering a Set of Rational Numbers

**Example:** Order the following set of numbers from *least* to *greatest*:

$-\dfrac{7}{6},\;3,\;-2, \; \dfrac{3}{2}$

**Explanation**

The lowest common denominator of all fractions in the given list is $6$.

To begin with, we write all of the numbers as fractions with a denominator of $6$, as follows:

$$
-\dfrac{7}{6}, \; \dfrac{18}{6}, \; -\dfrac{12}{6}, \; \dfrac{9}{6}
$$

For the negative fractions, we can bring the negative sign inside the numerator:

$$
\dfrac{(-7)}{6}, \; \dfrac{18}{6},\; \dfrac{(-12)}{6}, \; \dfrac{9}{6}
$$

Now, to order the given list from *least* to *greatest*, we order the numerators.

$$
\dfrac{(-12)}{6} < \dfrac{(-7)}{6} < \dfrac{9}{6} < \dfrac{18}{6}
$$

Then, we change each fraction back to its original form:

$$
-2 < -\dfrac{7}{6} < \dfrac{3}{2} < 3
$$

Finally, the required ordered list is:

$$
-2, \; -\dfrac{7}{6}, \; \dfrac{3}{2}, \; 3
$$

## Ordering a Set of Rational Numbers Containing Decimals

**Example:** Order the following set of numbers from *greatest* to *least*:

$4,\; -\dfrac 6 5,\; 3,\;-0.8,\; -1$

**Explanation**

First, we write the decimal $(-0.8)$ as a fraction:

$$
4,\; -\dfrac 6 5,\; 3,\;-\dfrac 8 {10},\; -1
$$

Next, we write all of the numbers as fractions with a denominator of $10$, as follows:

$$
\dfrac{40}{10},\;-\dfrac{12}{10}, \; \dfrac{30}{10},\;-\dfrac{8}{10}, \; -\dfrac{10}{10}
$$

For the negative fractions, we can bring the negative sign inside the numerator:

$$
\dfrac{40}{10}, \; \dfrac{(-12)}{10},\;\dfrac{30}{10}, \; \dfrac{(-8)}{10}, \; \dfrac{(-10)}{10}
$$

Now, to order the given list from *greatest* to *least*, we order the numerators.

$$
\dfrac{40}{10} > \dfrac{30}{10} > \dfrac{(-8)}{10} > \dfrac{(-10)}{10} > \dfrac{(-12)}{10}
$$

Then, we change each fraction back to its original form:

$$
4 > 3 > -0.8 > -1 > -\dfrac{6}{5}
$$

Finally, the required ordered list is:

$$
4,\; 3,\; -0.8,\; -1,\; -\dfrac 6 5
$$
