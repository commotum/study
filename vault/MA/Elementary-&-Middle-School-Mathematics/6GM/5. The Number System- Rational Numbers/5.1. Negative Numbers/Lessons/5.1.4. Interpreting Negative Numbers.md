## Introduction

When we place a negative sign in front of a number, we're finding the **opposite** number.
This number has the same distance from zero, but is on the opposite side of zero.

To demonstrate, let's $-(5)$, the opposite of $5$.

The number $5$ is a distance of $5$ from zero, as shown on the number line.

![](<../Source/5.1.4. Interpreting Negative Numbers/Images/0e3e719b709fdd02a126c497e05b55a7.png>)

We want to find $-(5)$, which is the *opposite* of the number $5$. The opposite number has the same distance from zero, but is on the opposite side:

![](<../Source/5.1.4. Interpreting Negative Numbers/Images/ba8568642b5caa3f8fb1e48a4772003f.png>)

Therefore, $-(5) = -5$.

## Using Negative Numbers to Measure Temperature

Negative numbers appear often in real life.

For instance, we can use *negative numbers* to measure temperature. Water freezes at $0^\circ\textrm{C}$, but the scale can go even lower.
For example, the temperatures at the North Pole can reach $-40^\circ\textrm{C}\,$!

To read a thermometer, find where the fluid level aligns with the scale.

![](<../Source/5.1.4. Interpreting Negative Numbers/Images/64d14d7df9fb23548159c7363fc0ef4e.png>)

On the thermometer above, the fluid is at the same level as $-3$ on the scale, and the temperature is measured in degrees Celsius. Therefore, the thermometer shows $-3^\circ\textrm{C}$.

<a id="increasing-the-temperature-on-a-thermometer"></a>
## Increasing the Temperature on a Thermometer

To increase the temperature in a thermometer, we *raise* the fluid level by the desired number of units.
So, what temperature is *$3^\circ\textrm{C}$ warmer* than $-6^\circ\textrm{C}$?

Let's locate $-6^\circ\textrm{C}$ on a thermometer.

![](<../Source/5.1.4. Interpreting Negative Numbers/Images/cf1042c64b2762a2698838d26a8a9dad.png>)

To find a temperature that is $3^\circ\textrm{C}$ *warmer*, the fluid needs to *move up* by $3$ units. This gives:

![](<../Source/5.1.4. Interpreting Negative Numbers/Images/82e0f3144033a4c5a2d31a7f1c635764.png>)

The new fluid level is at $-3^\circ\textrm{C}$.

Therefore, $-3^\circ\textrm{C}$ is $3^\circ\textrm{C}$ warmer than $-6^\circ\textrm{C}$.

## Finding a Temperature Warmer than a Negative Temperature

**Example:** Which temperature is $5^\circ\textrm{C}$ warmer than $-3^\circ\textrm{C}$?

**Explanation**

Let's imagine $-3^\circ\,\textrm{C}$ on a thermometer.

![](<../Source/5.1.4. Interpreting Negative Numbers/Images/5f18cdf0074634fb3bd2486501f2edd6.png>)

To find a temperature that is $5^\circ\,\textrm{C}$ warmer, the fluid needs to move up by $5$ units. This gives:

![](<../Source/5.1.4. Interpreting Negative Numbers/Images/dc441d5de5b28f94c0702a5a4b98ff41.png>)

Therefore, $2^\circ\textrm{C}$ is $5^\circ\textrm{C}$ warmer than $-3^\circ\textrm{C}$.

<a id="understanding-positive-and-negative-values-and-zero"></a>
## Understanding Positive and Negative Values, and Zero

**Example:** In a bank account, deposits are presented by positive numbers and withdrawals by negative numbers. Which of the following statements are true?

- A transaction of $-\[math]4$ means that $\[/math]4$ were withdrawn.
- If the balance changes from $-\[math]3$ to $\[/math]2$, it increases by $\[math]5$.
- A balance cannot be less than $\[/math]0$.

**Explanation**

Let's examine our statements in turn.

- A negative transaction means the number is on the left side of zero on the number line. The number $-4$ is $4$ units to the left of zero, which represents removing $4$ dollars from the account.![](<../Source/5.1.4. Interpreting Negative Numbers/Images/e3fc2b3649323a2993a06048050dc9cf.png>)
Therefore, this statement is *True.*

- To find the increase, we count how many units the balance moves upward on the number line. From $-3$ to $0$ is an increase of $3$ dollars. From $0$ to $2$ is another increase of $2$ dollars.![](<../Source/5.1.4. Interpreting Negative Numbers/Images/ef09493067f727c6ed81e565d5a8d661.png>)
So, the total increase is $3 + 2 = 5$ dollars.Therefore, this statement is *True.*
- The number $0$ is on the number line exactly between positive and negative numbers. It represents a balance that is not below zero and not above zero. Since there could be negative balances, a balance could be less than $\[math]0$.![](<../Source/5.1.4. Interpreting Negative Numbers/Images/fef5f5116a3b535627cdd9150bcd90ee.png>)Therefore, this statement is *False.*
