## Introduction

When a coordinate plane represents a map, we can determine the distance between two locations by calculating the *length of the line segment* that connects the points.

Let's find the distance between the school and the candy store in the diagram below.

![](<../Source/5.3.4. Solving Problems in the Coordinate Plane/Images/38437a50f64b018ef581cd039344f41c.png>)

By counting the grid units between the School and the Candy Store, we find that the length of the connecting segment is $6$ units.

![](<../Source/5.3.4. Solving Problems in the Coordinate Plane/Images/ed84e6c9e4159af50c16b23349b69ce0.png>)

According to the scale, one unit represents $1$ kilometer. Therefore, the total distance is:

$$
6 \times 1 \,\textrm{km} = 6 \,\textrm{km}
$$

---

<a id="an-alternative-method-of-finding-distances"></a>
## An Alternative Method of Finding Distances

Now, let's consider an alternative method.

![](<../Source/5.3.4. Solving Problems in the Coordinate Plane/Images/09883645261c0e2231935fa41d393095.png>)

From the diagram, we can see that the school has coordinates $(-2,2)$ and the candy store has coordinates $(4,2)$.

We can find the distance between the two points in this case by finding the distance of each point from the $y$-axis and then adding.

![](<../Source/5.3.4. Solving Problems in the Coordinate Plane/Images/53b2affc17076246de356a7a9500e08d.png>)

The point $({\color{blue}{-2}},2)$ is at a distance of $\color{blue}{2}$ from the $y$-axis, and the point $({\color{red}{4}},2)$ is at a distance of $\color{red}{4}$ from the $y$-axis.

Therefore, the distance between the two points is

$$
{\color{blue}{2}} + {\color{red}{4}} = 6
$$

One unit is $1$ kilometer, so the total distance is

$$
6 \times 1 \,\textrm{km} = 6 \,\textrm{km}
$$

<a id="finding-the-distance-between-two-points-on-a-map"></a>
## Finding the Distance Between Two Points On a Map

**Example:** The diagram shows a map of a district. What is the distance between the church and the TV station?

![](<../Source/5.3.4. Solving Problems in the Coordinate Plane/Images/0ff329e66eef6c43c3fc39d17366c857.png>)

**Explanation**

**Method 1**

The distance between two points is the length of the line segment that connects them.

From the diagram, we see that the length of the segment connecting the church and the TV station is $8$ units.

![](<../Source/5.3.4. Solving Problems in the Coordinate Plane/Images/ad5fcc80611bba233845f39e28bf5443.png>)

One unit is $1$ kilometer, so the distance is

$$
8 \times 1 \,\textrm{km} = 8 \,\textrm{km}
$$

**Method 2**

From the diagram, we can see that the church has coordinates $(-3,-3)$ and the TV station has coordinates $(-3,5)$.

We can find the distance between the two points in this case by finding the distance of each point from the $x$-axis and then adding.

![](<../Source/5.3.4. Solving Problems in the Coordinate Plane/Images/5771d97e591d928f756c2f5223275f3b.png>)

The point $(-3,{\color{red}{-3}})$ is at a distance of $\color{red}{3}$ from the $x$-axis, and the point $(-3,{\color{blue}{5}})$ is at a distance of $\color{blue}{5}$ from the $x$-axis.

Therefore, the distance between the two points is

$$
{\color{red}{3}} + {\color{blue}{5}} = 8
$$

One unit is $1$ kilometer, so the total distance is

$$
8 \times 1 \,\textrm{km} = 8 \,\textrm{km}
$$

## Identifying the Quadrant Containing Some Desired Points

**Example:** The graph shows the average daily temperature $T$ (measured in $^{\circ}\text{C}$) and the deviation from the normal pressure $P$ (measured in $\text{mbar}$) in Anchorage for several days. Which quadrant contains data for the days that are with a negative deviation from the normal pressure but with a positive average daily temperature?

![](<../Source/5.3.4. Solving Problems in the Coordinate Plane/Images/2510ce3c85e97bbbcd7494a5452e5f08.png>)

**Explanation**

In the given coordinate plane,

- the horizontal $x$-axis corresponds to the deviation from the normal pressure, and
- the vertical $y$-axis corresponds to the average daily temperature.

So, we need to find the quadrant containing points that have negative $x$-coordinates and positive $y$-coordinates.

![](<../Source/5.3.4. Solving Problems in the Coordinate Plane/Images/4d8a69aaa60424e0574817e08168689d.png>)

From the graph, we see that this data is represented by points in the second quadrant.

## Identifying Parts of a Journey

**Example:** A plane took off from JFK airport (New York, USA) bound for Madrid, Spain. Every $90$ minutes the GPS navigator recorded the coordinates of the plane's position on a map. The data that it collects is shown in the table below. The coordinate system is set up so that the positive horizontal axis is aligned with the East, and the positive vertical axis is aligned with the North.

| New York local time | Coordinates |
| --- | ---: |
| $12\!:\!00$ am | $(-5,3)$ |
| $1\!:\!30$ am | $(-2,3)$ |
| $3\!:\!00$ am | $(1,2)$ |
| $4\!:\!30$ am | $(3,2)$ |
| $6\!:\!00$ am | $(4,-3)$ |
| $7\!:\!30$ am | $(5,-4)$ |

During what period of time did the plane travel strictly East?

**Explanation**

Plotting the coordinates from the table on the map, we get:

![](<../Source/5.3.4. Solving Problems in the Coordinate Plane/Images/a4ac4363148fe0d0f8f6c345491f7f6e.png>)

From the graph, we can see that the plane traveled East from $12\!\mathbin:\!00$ to $1\!\mathbin:\!30$ and from $3\!\mathbin:\!00$ to $4\!\mathbin:\!30$.

<a id="finding-distance-and-speed-from-plots"></a>
## Finding Distance and Speed from Plots

**Example:** Orlando works delivering newspapers every morning. Every minute he recorded his coordinates on a map using a GPS navigator. The results are given in the table below. The coordinates that he records are set up so that a difference of one unit corresponds to $1$ meter.

| Time | Coordinates |
| --- | ---: |
| $7\!:\!00$ am | $(10,-30)$ |
| $7\!:\!01$ am | $(30,-30)$ |
| $7\!:\!02$ am | $(30,40)$ |
| $7\!:\!03$ am | $(-30,40)$ |
| $7\!:\!04$ am | $(-40,-50)$ |

Assuming that Orlando traveled at a constant speed from $7\!:\!02$ am to $7\!:\!03$ am, what was his speed during this time interval?

**Explanation**

Plotting the coordinates from the table on the map, we get:

![](<../Source/5.3.4. Solving Problems in the Coordinate Plane/Images/6994290691459d111b134db9e399d510.png>)

In the interval from $7\!:\!02$ am to $7\!:\!03$ am, Orlando traveled $60$ units. Since one unit corresponds to $1$ meter, then Orlando traveled $60 \, \textrm{m}$.

Therefore, Orlando traveled at a speed of $60$ meters per minute during this time period.
