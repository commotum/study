## Introduction

To compare two exponents, we must calculate each exponent and compare the resulting values.

Let's determine if $3^2$ is less than, greater than, or equal to $2^4$.

- First, we calculate $3^2$. We have
$2$
- Then, we calculate $2^4$. We have
$2^{4} = 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2_(⏟)_([math]{\color{red}4} [/math] times) = 16$.

Therefore, since $9 <16$, the correct statement is
$3^2 < 2^4$.

**Example:** Which symbols could replace the empty box below to make the statement true?

**Explanation**

First, we calculate $4^2$. We have
$2$

Then, for $1^7$, we have

$$
1^{7} = 1 \cdot 1 \cdot 1 \cdot 1 \cdot 1 \cdot 1 \cdot 1_(⏟)_([math]{\color{red}7} [/math] times) = 1
$$

Because $16 > 1$, the correct statement must be
$4^2 > 1^7 \,$.

Therefore, the correct answer is "I only."

## Identifying the Correct Symbol When Comparing Fractional Exponents

**Example:** Which symbols could replace the empty box below to make the statement true?
$\left(\dfrac{1}{3}\right)^2 \,\,\square\,\, \left(\dfrac{1}{4}\right)^1$

1. $>$
2. $<$
3. $=$

**Explanation**

First, we calculate

$$
\left(\dfrac{1}{3}\right)^2
$$

We have

$$
\begin{aligned}
(\frac{1}{3})^{2} &= \frac{1}{3} \cdot \frac{1}{3}_(⏟)_([math]{\color{blue}2} [/math] times) \\
&= \frac{1 \cdot 1}{3 \cdot 3} \\
&= \frac{1}{9}
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, for

$$
\left(\dfrac{1}{4}\right)^1
$$

we have

$$
(\frac{1}{4})^{1} = \frac{1}{4}
$$

Because

$$
\dfrac{1}{9} < \dfrac{1}{4}
$$

the correct statement must be

$$
\left(\dfrac{1}{3}\right)^2 < \left(\dfrac{1}{4}\right)^1 \,
$$

Therefore, the correct answer is "II only."

<a id="a-note-on-exponent-convention"></a>
## A Note on Exponent Convention

Let's work through the details of how to compute the following exponent:

$$
-5^2
$$

It's important to realize that this is shorthand for

$$
-(5)^2
$$

and *not* $(-5)^2$.

We start by calculating the positive exponent first, ignoring the negative sign:

$$
2
$$

Finally, we replace the negative sign. Therefore, we conclude that

$$
-5^2 = -25
$$

---

<a id="comparing-negative-exponents"></a>
## Comparing Negative Exponents

Now, let's find out if $-5^2$ is less than, greater than, or equal to $-1^4$.

- Earlier, we found that $-5^2 = -25$.
- To compute $-1^4$, we ignore the negative sign and calculate the positive exponent first:
$1^{4} = 1 \cdot 1 \cdot 1 \cdot 1_(⏟)_([math]{\color{red}4} [/math] times) = 1$
Then, we replace the negative sign. Therefore,
$-1^4 = -1$.

To compare the two numbers, we draw a number line.

![](<../Source/5.4.6. Comparing Exponents/Images/1c5774e03ace502ee3b7ab8d87336591.png>)

Notice that $-25$ is to the left of $-1$. Therefore,

$$
-25 < -1
$$

Writing this statement using the numbers expressed in component form, we have

$$
-5^2 < -1^4
$$

<a id="identifying-the-correct-symbol-when-comparing-negative-exponents"></a>
## Identifying the Correct Symbol When Comparing Negative Exponents

**Example:** Which symbols could replace the empty box below to make the statement true?
$-2^3 \,\,\,\square\,-3^2$

1. $>$
2. $<$
3. $=$

**Explanation**

**Watch Out!** Remember that,

- $-2^3$ is shorthand for $-\!\left(2^3\right)$,
- similarly, $-3^2$ is shorthand for $-\!\left(3^2\right)$.

First, we calculate $-2^3$. We ignore the negative sign and calculate the positive exponent first:
$3$

Therefore, $-2^3 = -8$.

Now, to compute $-3^2$, we again ignore the negative sign and calculate the positive exponent first:

$$
3^{2} = 3 \cdot 3_(⏟)_([math]{\color{red}2} [/math] times) = 9
$$

Therefore, $-3^2 = -9$.

Finally, let's look at the number line to compare $-8$ and $-9$.

![](<../Source/5.4.6. Comparing Exponents/Images/efeee8ac69b78a77d149f8c20d7e626d.png>)

Since $-8 > -9$, the correct statement must be
$-2^3 > -3^2 \,$.

Therefore, the correct answer is "I only."
