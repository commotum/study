## Introduction

Let's think about how to simplify the following:

$$
\dfrac{\left(\dfrac{2}{3}\right)}{\left(\dfrac{3}{4}\right)}
$$

The expression above is a fraction, but there are additional fractions in the numerator and denominator.
Such expressions are called **fractions of fractions**.
We use parentheses to make it easier to read.

The trick to working with fractions of fractions is to remember that a fraction represents a division. So, a fraction of fractions is a division of two fractions:

$$
\dfrac{\left(\dfrac{2}{3}\right)}{\left(\dfrac{\color{blue}{3}}{\color{red}{4}}\right)} = \left(\dfrac{2}{3}\right) \boldsymbol{\div} \left(\dfrac{\color{blue}{3}}{\color{red}{4}}\right)
$$

Now, recall that dividing by

$$
\dfrac{\color{blue}{3}}{\color{red}{4}}
$$

is the same as multiplying by its reciprocal

$$
\dfrac{\color{red}{4}}{\color{blue}{3}}
$$

We can use this fact to simplify the fraction above:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(\frac{2}{3}) \div (\frac{3}{4}) &=  \\
(\frac{2}{3}) \cdot (\frac{4}{3}) &=  \\
\frac{2 \cdot 4}{3 \cdot 3} &=  \\
&= \frac{8}{9}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\dfrac{\left(\dfrac{2}{3}\right)}{\left(\dfrac{3}{4}\right)} = \dfrac{8}{9}
$$

<a id="simplifying-a-fraction-of-fractions"></a>
## Simplifying a Fraction of Fractions

**Example:** What is $\dfrac {\left(\dfrac 1 4\right)} {\left(-\dfrac 3 7\right)}$ expressed as a single fraction?

**Explanation**

First, remember that a fraction represents a division. So, a fraction of fractions is a division of two fractions:

$$
\begin{aligned}
((\frac{1}{4}))/((-\frac{3}{7})) &= \frac{1}{4} \div (-\frac{3}{7})
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, recall that dividing by

$$
-\dfrac{3}{7}
$$

is the same as multiplying by its reciprocal

$$
-\dfrac{7}{3}
$$

We can use this fact to simplify the fraction above:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{1}{4} \div (-\frac{3}{7}) &=  \\
\frac{1}{4} \cdot (-\frac{7}{3}) &=  \\
- \frac{1 \cdot 7}{4 \cdot 3} &=  \\
&= - \frac{7}{12}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
((\frac{1}{4}))/((-\frac{3}{7})) &= -\frac{7}{12}
\end{aligned}
$$

Notice that we used the fact that a positive number times a negative number gives a negative number.

## Dividing a Counting Number by a Fraction

How do we simplify

$$
\dfrac{5}{\left(\dfrac{2}{3}\right)}
$$

Notice that, this time, we have a counting number divided by a fraction.

The trick is to write

$$
5 = \dfrac{\color{blue}{5}}{\color{red}{1}}
$$

and perform the same process as before:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(5)/((\frac{2}{3})) &=  \\
5 \div (\frac{2}{3}) &=  \\
(\frac{5}{1}) \div (\frac{2}{3}) &=  \\
(\frac{5}{1}) \cdot (\frac{3}{2}) &=  \\
\frac{5 \cdot 3}{1 \cdot 2} &=  \\
&= \frac{15}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

---

<a id="dividing-a-counting-number-by-a-fraction"></a>
## Dividing a Counting Number by a Fraction

**Example:** Simplify $\dfrac{\left(-3\right)} {\left(-\dfrac 3 5\right)}$.

**Explanation**

First, remember that a fraction represents a division:

$$
((-3))/((-\frac{3}{5})) =-3 \div (-\frac{3}{5})
$$

Now, recall that

$$
-3 =-\dfrac{3}{1}
$$

and dividing by

$$
-\dfrac{3}{5}
$$

is the same as multiplying by its reciprocal

$$
-\dfrac{5}{3}
$$

We can use these facts to simplify the fraction above:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(-3) \div (-\frac{3}{5}) &=  \\
(-\frac{3}{1}) \div (-\frac{3}{5}) &=  \\
(-\frac{3}{1}) \cdot (-\frac{5}{3}) &=  \\
\frac{3 \cdot 5}{1 \cdot 3} &=  \\
\frac{15}{3} &=  \\
&= 5
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\dfrac{\left(-3\right)} {\left(-\dfrac 3 5\right)} = 5
$$

Notice that we used the fact that a negative number times a negative number gives a positive number.

<a id="dividing-a-fraction-by-a-counting-number"></a>
## Dividing a Fraction by a Counting Number

Suppose we want to simplify a fraction of fractions like

$$
\dfrac{\left(\dfrac{5}{3}\right)}{4}
$$

In this case, we have a fraction divided by a counting number. We have two methods available.

The long way is to write

$$
4 = \dfrac 4 1
$$

and then calculate in the same way as we did earlier:

$$
\begin{aligned}
((\frac{5}{3}))/(4) &=  \\
(\frac{5}{3}) \div 4 &=  \\
(\frac{5}{3}) \div (\frac{4}{1}) &=  \\
(\frac{5}{3}) \cdot (\frac{1}{4}) &=  \\
\frac{5 \cdot 1}{3 \cdot 4} &=  \\
&= \frac{5}{12}
\end{aligned}
$$

A quicker way is to recognize that the result will have the same numerator as the top fraction and that the denominator is just the product of the two denominators:

$$
((\frac{5}{3}))/(4) = \frac{5}{3 \cdot 4} = \frac{5}{12}
$$

<a id="dividing-a-fraction-by-a-counting-number"></a>
## Dividing a Fraction by a Counting Number

**Example:** Calculate $\dfrac{\left(-\dfrac 3 2\right)}{6}$.

**Explanation**

We can use either of the following two methods.

**Method 1 - The Long Way**

First, remember that a fraction represents a division:

$$
\dfrac{\left(-\dfrac 3 2\right)}{6} = \left(-\dfrac 3 2\right) \div 6
$$

Now, recall that dividing by $6$ is the same as multiplying by its reciprocal

$$
\dfrac{1}{6}
$$

We can use this fact to simplify the fraction above:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(-\frac{3}{2}) \div 6 &=  \\
(-\frac{3}{2}) \cdot (\frac{1}{6}) &=  \\
- \frac{3 \cdot 1}{2 \cdot 6} &=  \\
&= - \frac{3}{12}
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we simplify:

$$
-\dfrac{3}{12} =-\dfrac{3 \div 3}{12 \div 3} = -\dfrac{1}{4}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\dfrac{\left(-\dfrac 3 2\right)}{6}= -\dfrac14
$$

**Method 2 - The Quick Way**

The result will have the same numerator as the top fraction, and the denominator is just the product of the two denominators:

$$
\begin{aligned}
((-\frac{3}{2}))/(6) &=  \\
\frac{-3}{2 \cdot 6} &=  \\
- \frac{3}{12} &=  \\
- \frac{3 \div 3}{12 \div 3} &=  \\
&= - \frac{1}{4}
\end{aligned}
$$
