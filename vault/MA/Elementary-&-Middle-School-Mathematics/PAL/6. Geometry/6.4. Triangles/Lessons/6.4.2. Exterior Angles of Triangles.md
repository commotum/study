## Introduction

Consider the following triangle, where the side $\overline{AC}$ has been extended beyond the vertex $C$.

![](<../Source/6.4.2. Exterior Angles of Triangles/Images/e21b11060648d11559ac96439357581a.png>)

The resulting angle $\angle{BCD}$ is called the **exterior angle** at vertex $C$.

Exterior angles have the following properties:

- An exterior angle and its corresponding interior angle form a linear pair and are therefore supplementary. Here, we have
$\underbrace{\color{YellowOrange}m\angle{BCD}}_{\large\text{Exterior}} \:+\: \underbrace{\color{blue}m\angle{ACB}}_{\large\text{Interior}} = 180^\circ$
- The measure of an exterior angle is equal to the sum of the measures of the two non-adjacent interior angles. This is called the **exterior angles theorem**. To see why it is true, notice that since the internal angles of a triangle sum to $180^\circ$, we have
$m∠A + m∠B + m∠C|= 180^{∘}; m∠A + m∠C|= 180^{∘} - m∠B_(⏟)_(Exterior angle at [math]B[/math])$.
- At each vertex of a triangle, there are two exterior angles. They are vertical angles, and consequently they have the same measure.![](<../Source/6.4.2. Exterior Angles of Triangles/Images/ee9f22d561df355ea42e6a0de9b5c2c7.png>)

## Finding the Value of a Variable Given an Exterior Angle and Two Internal Angles

**Example:** Find the value of $x$ in the following figure.

![](<../Source/6.4.2. Exterior Angles of Triangles/Images/7edbfe344b31c121decf034ae0afc001.png>)

**Explanation**

*Method 1*

Let's start by finding the value of the missing interior angle at $B$. We use the fact that the interior and exterior angles are supplementary:

$$
\begin{aligned}
73^{∘} + m∠B &= 180^{∘} \\
m∠B &= 180^{∘} - 73^{∘} \\
m∠B &= 107^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we calculate $x$ using the fact that the interior angles of a triangle sum to $180^\circ$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
m∠A + m∠B + m∠C &= 180^{∘} \\
41^{∘} + 107^{∘} + 2x &= 180^{∘} \\
148^{∘} + 2x &= 180^{∘} \\
2x &= 32^{∘} \\
x &= 16^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

*Method 2*

We could rewrite the equation

$$
m\angle{A} +m\angle{B} +m\angle{C} = 180^\circ
$$

as

$$
180^{∘} - m∠B_(⏟)_(Exterior angle at [math]B[/math]) = m∠A + m∠C
$$

Substituting, we can solve for $x$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
180^{∘} - m∠B_(⏟)_(Exterior angle at [math]B[/math]) &= m∠A + m∠C \\
73^{∘} &= 41^{∘} + 2x \\
32^{∘} &= 2x \\
16^{∘} &= x
\end{aligned}
$$

## The Sum of Exterior Angles

Yet another important fact about exterior angles is:

> The sum of the measures of the exterior angles of a triangle is always equal to $360^\circ$.

So, for the triangle below, we have

$$
x+y+z = 360^\circ
$$

![](<../Source/6.4.2. Exterior Angles of Triangles/Images/7727af2d26a86834f57c9e35d83a6a2a.png>)

To see why, first recall that the measure of an exterior angle is equal to the sum of the measures of the two non-adjacent interior angles. So, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
x &= m∠B + m∠C \\
y &= m∠A + m∠C \\
z &= m∠A + m∠B
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, remember that the internal angles of a triangle sum to $180^\circ$. Then we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
x + y + z &= (m∠B + m∠C) + (m∠A + m∠C) + (m∠A + m∠B) \\
&= 2m∠A + 2m∠B + 2m∠C \\
&= 2(m∠A + m∠B + m∠C) \\
&= 2(180^{∘}) \\
&= 360^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding the Value of a Variable Given the Exterior Angles

**Example:** Find the value of $x$ in the figure below.

![](<../Source/6.4.2. Exterior Angles of Triangles/Images/c9db0730fecf062fc5ff44d66d13a6a8.png>)

**Explanation**

Since the exterior angles of a triangle sum to $360^{\circ}$, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
145^{∘} + x + (2x - 25^{∘}) &= 360^{∘} \\
120^{∘} + 3x &= 360^{∘} \\
3x &= 240^{∘} \\
x &= 80^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Calculating the Measure of an Interior Angle Given the Exterior Angles

**Example:** Given a triangle $\triangle ABC$, let the exterior angles at ${A}$ and ${B}$ be equal $120^\circ$ and $100^\circ$, respectively. What is the measure of the angle $\angle C$?

**Explanation**

Let's start by drawing the corresponding triangle.

![](<../Source/6.4.2. Exterior Angles of Triangles/Images/0539ef9c5e054ed4682a99883a5206a6.png>)

Let $z$ be the measure of the exterior angle at ${C}$ pictured above. Since the sum of the exterior angles of a triangle is $360^{\circ}$, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
120^{∘} + 100^{∘} + z &= 360^{∘} \\
220^{∘} + z &= 360^{∘} \\
z &= 140^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we can find the value of the missing interior angle at $C$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
m∠C &= 180^{∘} - z \\
&= 180^{∘} - 140^{∘} \\
&= 40^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
m\angle C = 40^\circ
$$
