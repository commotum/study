## Introduction

When two lines meet at a point, they create two pairs of opposite angles. The angles in each pair are called **vertical angles**.

Vertical angles are *always* congruent.

![](<../Source/6.2.8. Vertical Angles/Images/12f25b222b83885944cf2c3253eb192e.png>)

In the diagram above, $\angle 1$ and $\angle 3$ are vertical angles and are therefore congruent.

Likewise, $\angle 2$ and $\angle 4$ are vertical angles and are therefore congruent.

So, we have $\angle 1 \cong \angle 3$ and $\angle 2 \cong \angle 4$.

## Calculating an Unknown Quantity Using Vertical Angles

**Example:** Given the figure below, find the value of $x$.

![](<../Source/6.2.8. Vertical Angles/Images/487d4187fdd5d438bc0ac14bb2856493.png>)

**Explanation**

The two angles marked in the diagram are vertical.
Since vertical angles are congruent, we have:

$$
\begin{aligned}
2x + 12^{∘} &= 54^{∘} \\
2x &= 42^{∘} \\
x &= 21^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Calculating the Measure of Vertical Angles Given in Terms of a Variable

**Example:** Compute the measures of the shaded angles in the figure below.

![](<../Source/6.2.8. Vertical Angles/Images/f0ca7aa35a3bba7f2848cd05e423c24e.png>)

**Explanation**

The two angles marked in the diagram are vertical. Since vertical angles are congruent, we have:

$$
\begin{aligned}
3x - 15^{∘} &= 2x + 10^{∘} \\
x - 15^{∘} &= 10^{∘} \\
x &= 25^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

Now that we know $x=25^\circ$, we can substitute into one of the expressions to calculate the measure of the angles. Let's substitute into the expression $2x + 10^\circ$:

$$
2x + 10^\circ = 2 \cdot 25^\circ + 10^\circ = 60^\circ
$$

Therefore, both angles have a measure of $60^\circ$.

## Finding an Unknown Angle by Combining Properties of Straight and Vertical Angles

**Example:** In the figure below, $\angle AOB$ is a straight angle. Determine $m \angle AOD$.

![](<../Source/6.2.8. Vertical Angles/Images/a355c7549268cafebc07a5fd707c0ad7.png>)

**Explanation**

Since $\angle AOB$ is straight,

$$
m\angle AOB = 180^\circ
$$

So by the angle addition postulate, we have

$$
m\angle AOC + m\angle BOC = 180^\circ
$$

Substituting the given expressions into the above, we can solve for $x$ as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(5x + 100^{∘}) + 11x &= 180^{∘} \\
16x + 100^{∘} &= 180^{∘} \\
16x &= 80^{∘} \\
x &= 5^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

Now that we know $x=5^\circ$, we can calculate $m \angle COB$ as follows:

$$
m \angle COB = 11(5^\circ) = 55^\circ
$$

Finally, since the angles $\angle AOD$ and $\angle COB$ are vertical angles, they have the same measure:

$$
m \angle AOD = m \angle COB = 55^\circ
$$

## Using Vertical Angles in a Situation With Three Lines

**Example:** In the diagram shown below, it is known that $m \angle 1 = 60^\circ$ and $m \angle 3 = 120^\circ$. Determine the value of $m \angle 2 + m \angle 4$.

![](<../Source/6.2.8. Vertical Angles/Images/39cfd505e80cb61a0517cbb26fff5997.png>)

**Explanation**

We have two pairs of vertical angles, and we know that vertical angles are congruent and therefore have the same measure. So, we have

$$
m∠1 = m∠2,; m∠3 = m∠4
$$

We are told that

$$
m \angle 1 = 60^\circ
$$

and

$$
m \angle 3 = 120^\circ
$$

so we have

$$
60^{∘} = m∠2,; 120^{∘} = m∠4
$$

Therefore, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
m∠2 + m∠4 &= 60^{∘} + 120^{∘} \\
&= 180^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$
