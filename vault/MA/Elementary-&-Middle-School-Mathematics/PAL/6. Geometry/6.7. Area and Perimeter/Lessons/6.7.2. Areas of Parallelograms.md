## Introduction

The **area of a parallelogram** is given by the formula

$$
\mathcal{A} = b \cdot h
$$

where $b$ is the **base** and $h$ is the **height** of the parallelogram. The height of a parallelogram is the length of a perpendicular segment drawn from the base to the opposite side.

To demonstrate, let's use the above formula to compute the area of the parallelogram $ABCD$ shown below.

![](<../Source/6.7.2. Areas of Parallelograms/Images/ac2831ef2590492f083c173c5556a742.png>)

From the picture above, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= b \cdot h \\
&= 7 \cdot 5 \\
&= 35ft^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Calculating the Area of a Parallelogram

**Example:** The length of a side of a parallelogram is $15\,\textrm{in}$ and the shortest distance from this side to the opposite side is $20\,\textrm{in}$. What is the area of this parallelogram?

**Explanation**

From the given information, we have that

- the base of our parallelogram is $b=15\,\textrm{in}$, and
- the corresponding height is $h=20\,\textrm{in}$.

![](<../Source/6.7.2. Areas of Parallelograms/Images/5437f506ca8cc948c45c95faa5747e64.png>)

Therefore, the area of the parallelogram is

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= b \cdot h \\
&= 15 \cdot 20 \\
&= 300in^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Calculating the Area of a Parallelogram Using a Key

**Example:** What is the area of the field shown below?

![](<../Source/6.7.2. Areas of Parallelograms/Images/6ae2c8bfa6cc65d633fdf1b1c9149af8.png>)

**Explanation**

Note that the length of a side of a cell is

$$
\dfrac{1}{2} \cdot 1\,\textrm{km}=0.5\,\textrm{km}
$$

Let the bottom side be our base. It is $6$ cells long, which means that

$$
b = 6 \cdot 0.5 = 3\,\textrm{km}
$$

Next, we notice that there are $8$ cells from the chosen base to the opposite side, which means that the height is

$$
h= 8 \cdot 0.5 = 4\,\textrm{km}
$$

![](<../Source/6.7.2. Areas of Parallelograms/Images/13dd20b74b873129351906a91db9d45b.png>)

Therefore, the area of the parallelogram is

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= b \cdot h = 3 \cdot 4 = 12km^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Determining the Height of a Parallelogram Given Its Area and Its Base or Height

**Example:** Find the height $DH$ of the parallelogram $ABCD$ given that its area is $75$ square units.

![](<../Source/6.7.2. Areas of Parallelograms/Images/1ba9a4a7f19623f9539d458fcbfb7d10.png>)

**Explanation**

In the parallelogram $ABCD$, we are told that the base is

$$
b = AB = CD=10
$$

the corresponding height is

$$
h = DH
$$

and the area is

$$
\mathcal{A} = 75
$$

So, we can calculate the measure of $\overline{DH}$ using the formula for the area of a parallelogram, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= b \cdot h \\
A &= AB \cdot DH \\
75 &= 10 \cdot DH \\
DH &= \frac{75}{10} \\
DH &= 7.5
\end{aligned}
$$

## Connecting the Area of a Parallelogram to Other Attributes

**Example:** Find the height $h$ of the parallelogram $ABCD$ given that its perimeter is $176$ units and its area is $\mathcal{A} = 1\,536$ square units.

![](<../Source/6.7.2. Areas of Parallelograms/Images/2683cbcfdec0c727d51b5cd97cd4b80a.png>)

**Explanation**

From the figure, we have ${BC} = 40$. Let's also denote $AB=x$.

Since the perimeter $p$ of the parallelogram $ABCD$ is $p = 176$, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
p &= AB + BC + CD + DA \\
p &= 2AB + 2BC \\
176 &= 2x + 2(40) \\
176 &= 2x + 80 \\
2x &= 96 \\
x &= 48
\end{aligned}
$$

So, we have ${AB} = 48$.

The area of a parallelogram is given by

$$
\mathcal{A} = b \cdot h
$$

where $b$ is the base and $h$ is the height. We'll choose $AB$ as the base, so

$$
b = AB = 48
$$

We also know that the area is

$$
\mathcal{A} = 1\,536
$$

Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
1536 &= 48 \cdot h \\
h &= \frac{1536}{48} \\
h &= 32
\end{aligned}
$$

## Deriving the Area Formula

![](<../Source/6.7.2. Areas of Parallelograms/Images/5cfc17a1b7b188656d7f97ecb4e7a1e6.png>)

We'll now derive the formula for the area of a parallelogram. Consider a parallelogram with base $b$ and height $h$, as shown above.

Let's now cut the small triangle along the height on the right-hand side and place it on the other side of the original figure.

![](<../Source/6.7.2. Areas of Parallelograms/Images/9a09bf078fe5c24d8d01ef2d94eb3406.png>)

We now have a rectangle with a base of length $b$ and a height of $h$. The area of this rectangle equals the area of our parallelogram:

$$
\textrm{Area of Parallelogram} = \textrm{Area of Rectangle} = \textrm{base} \times \textrm{height} = b \times h
$$
