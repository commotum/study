## Introduction

Two angles are **supplementary** if their measures add up to $180^\circ$.

![](<../Source/6.3.3. Supplementary Angles/Images/eaea03cf3947c10705418e831791ff7f.png>)

These angles are supplementary because the sum of their measures is $180^\circ{:}$

$$
105^\circ +75^\circ = 180^\circ
$$

A **linear pair** is a pair of adjacent angles formed when two lines intersect. The angles shown above form a linear pair.

The **linear pair postulate** states the following:

> If two angles form a linear pair, then they are supplementary.

The converse is *not* true. That is, if two angles are supplementary, they do not necessarily form a linear pair.

To see why, consider the diagram shown below:

![](<../Source/6.3.3. Supplementary Angles/Images/690283fc338c98fc275550b19e00d5ce.png>)

Although the angles are supplementary (the sum of their measures is

$$
120^\circ + 60^\circ = 180^\circ
$$

), they do not form a linear pair of angles since they are not adjacent.

## Calculating Supplementary Angles

**Example:** Given that $\overline{AD}$ is a segment, what is the measure of $\angle FOD$?

![](<../Source/6.3.3. Supplementary Angles/Images/220698f9973865a966472aeec9b224b1.png>)

**Explanation**

![](<../Source/6.3.3. Supplementary Angles/Images/f140550fae5d882b327b18ea58161d43.png>)

Two angles are supplementary if their measures add up to $180^\circ$, a straight angle.

Since $\overline{AD}$ is a segment, we have that $\angle AOF$ and $\angle FOD$ are supplementary.

$$
m \angle AOF + m \angle FOD = 180^\circ
$$

Therefore, the measure of $\angle FOD$ is given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
m∠FOD &= 180^{∘} - m∠AOF \\
&= 180^{∘} - 45^{∘} \\
&= 135^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Calculating Supplementary Angles From a Description

**Example:** Find the measure of an angle that's supplementary to an angle of $(7x -63)^\circ$.

**Explanation**

Two angles are supplementary if their measures add up to $180^\circ$.

So, the measure of an angle that's supplementary to an angle of $(7x -63)^\circ$ must be

$$
\begin{aligned}
180^{∘} - (7x - 63)^{∘} &= (180 - 7x + 63)^{∘} \\
&= (180 + 63 - 7x)^{∘} \\
&= (243 - 7x)^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding an Unknown Quantity Using Supplementary Angles

**Example:** Solve for $y$ given the figure above.

![](<../Source/6.3.3. Supplementary Angles/Images/db992e99b94788c4daaae82b7abe4fa0.png>)

**Explanation**

Since the two angles are supplementary, the sum of their measures is equal to $180^\circ$. Therefore:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(3y - 17^{∘}) + (y + 37^{∘}) &= 180^{∘} \\
4y + 20^{∘} &= 180^{∘} \\
4y &= 160^{∘} \\
y &= 40^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$
