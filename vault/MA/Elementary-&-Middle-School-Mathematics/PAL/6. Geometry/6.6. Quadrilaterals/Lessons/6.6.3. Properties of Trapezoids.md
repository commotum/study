## Introduction

Let's consider the trapezoid $ABCD$, shown below.

![](<../Source/6.6.3. Properties of Trapezoids/Images/248bf51edfa7b5b20d2e0c4424b9f8ec.png>)

Notice that the angles $\angle A$ and $\angle D$ are on the same leg. Two angles of a trapezoid that are on the same leg are called **adjacent angles.** Adjacent angles of a trapezoid are always supplementary.

Now, since $\angle A$ and $\angle D$ are supplementary, we can solve for $\angle D$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
m∠A + m∠D &= 180^{∘} \\
65^{∘} + m∠D &= 180^{∘} \\
m∠D &= 115^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

To understand *why* $\angle A$ and $\angle D$ are supplementary, notice that we have two parallel lines $\overline{AB}$ and $\overline{CD}$ intersected by the transversal $\overline{AD}$. This means that the angles $\angle{A}$ and $\angle{D}$ are consecutive interior angles, and we know that when a transversal intersects two parallel lines, the consecutive interior angles are supplementary.

Finally, notice that we can make the same conclusion about $\angle B$ and $\angle C$. So in this trapezoid, we have two pairs of supplementary angles:

$$
m∠A + m∠D = 180^{∘}, m∠B + m∠C = 180^{∘}
$$

## Finding the Measures of the Interior Angles of a Trapezoid

**Example:** In the trapezoid $ABCD$ above, determine $m \angle ADC + m \angle ABC$.

![](<../Source/6.6.3. Properties of Trapezoids/Images/fed8c61199bf5bf5f0125f7d9b363b6c.png>)

**Explanation**

Note that

$$
m \angle{BCD} = 180^{\circ} - 55^{\circ} = 125^{\circ}
$$

Because the angles adjacent to the legs of the trapezoid are supplementary, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
m∠ADC &= 180^{∘} - m∠BAD \\
&= 180^{∘} - 70^{∘} \\
&= 110^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

and

$$
\begin{aligned}
m∠ABC &= 180^{∘} - m∠BCD \\
&= 180^{∘} - 125^{∘} \\
&= 55^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally,

$$
m∠ADC + m∠ABC = 110^{∘} + 55^{∘} = 165^{∘}
$$

## Finding the Value of an Unknown Using Properties of Interior Angles

**Example:** What is the value of $x$ given the trapezoid $PQRS$ above?

![](<../Source/6.6.3. Properties of Trapezoids/Images/6b146016228e57b4582ea6100ccedd9a.png>)

**Explanation**

The angles $\angle{P}$ and $\angle{S}$ are supplementary. Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
m∠P + m∠S &= 180^{∘} \\
(x + 25^{∘}) + (3x - 17^{∘}) &= 180^{∘} \\
4x + 8^{∘} &= 180^{∘} \\
4x &= 172^{∘} \\
x &= 43^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

## The Relationship Between the Bases and Legs of a Trapezoid

The length of the largest base of a trapezoid is the sum of the smaller base and the projections of the legs onto the largest base.

![](<../Source/6.6.3. Properties of Trapezoids/Images/d97d9d8cceb5b6fdf2a5a88df67b7747.png>)

From the diagram above, we note the following:

- The smaller base is $\overline{CD}$.
- The projection of the leg $\overline{AD}$ onto the largest base is $\overline{AH}$.
- The projection of the leg $\overline{BC}$ onto the largest base is $\overline{BF}$.

So, we have

$$
AB = AH + CD + BF
$$

## Finding Measures of Unknown Line Segments

**Example:** Given the trapezoid below, find $CD$ if $AH = 2$, $FB = 8$, and $AB = 15$.

![](<../Source/6.6.3. Properties of Trapezoids/Images/56f698800b31b643906d59a4c325ffd4.png>)

**Explanation**

Notice that $\overline{AB}$ is the largest base of a trapezoid and

$$
AB=AH+HF+FB
$$

Also, $CD = HF$.

So, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
AB &= AH + HF + FB \\
15 &= 2 + HF + 8 \\
15 &= HF + 10 \\
HF &= 15 - 10 \\
HF &= 5
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
CD = HF = 5
$$
