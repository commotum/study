## Introduction

Suppose we need to evaluate an expression like
$1+\sqrt{4}$,
where we have a square root sign. What would be the order of operations? It turns out that the root signs ($\sqrt{\phantom{x}}$ or $\sqrt[3]{\phantom{x}}$) can be thought of as parentheses.
In other words, we find the value under the square root first:

$$
\begin{aligned}
1 + \sqrt{4} &=  \\
1 + 2 &=  \\
&= 3
\end{aligned}
$$

Similarly, let's evaluate $\big(1-\sqrt[3]{-27}\big) \div 2$.

To evaluate expressions with radicals, we use PEMDAS as before, but with the following additional rules:

- expressions underneath radicals are treated the same as parentheses
- radicals themselves are treated as exponents

Following the order of operations, we obtain:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(1 - \sqrt[3]{-27}) \div 2 &=  \\
(1 - \sqrt[3]{(-3)^{3}}) \div 2 &=  \\
(1 - (-3)) \div 2 &=  \\
4 \div 2 &=  \\
&= 2
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="evaluating-expressions-containing-radicals-and-one-or-two-additional-operations"></a>
## Evaluating Expressions Containing Radicals and One or Two Additional Operations

**Example:** What is the value of $7\times \sqrt[3]{-8}$?

**Explanation**

To evaluate the given expression, we use PEMDAS. In terms of the order of operations:

- expressions underneath radicals are treated the same as parentheses
- radicals themselves are treated as exponents

Therefore, we first evaluate the value of the radical, and we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
7 \cdot \sqrt[3]{-8} &=  \\
7 \cdot \sqrt[3]{(-2) \cdot (-2) \cdot (-2)} &=  \\
7 \cdot \sqrt[3]{(-2)^{3}} &=  \\
7 \cdot (-2) &=  \\
&= - 14
\end{aligned}
$$

## Evaluating Expressions Containing Radicals and Multiple Operations

**Example:** Find the value of the expression $\sqrt{(3^4+7^2-2) \div 2}$.

**Explanation**

To evaluate the given expression, we use PEMDAS. In terms of the order of operations:

- expressions underneath radicals are treated the same as parentheses
- radicals themselves are treated as exponents

Therefore, following the order of operations, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{(3^{4} + 7^{2} - 2) \div 2} &=  \\
\sqrt{(81 + 49 - 2) \div 2} &=  \\
\sqrt{(130 - 2) \div 2} &=  \\
\sqrt{128 \div 2} &=  \\
\sqrt{64} &=  \\
\sqrt{8 \cdot 8} &=  \\
\sqrt{8^{2}} &=  \\
&= 8 \mid
\end{aligned}
$$

## Expressions Resulting in Radicals

Suppose we want to simplify the following expression:

$$
-3\sqrt{\left(2^4-1\right) \div 3}\,
$$

The first thing to realize is that there is an invisible multiplication symbol here. The above expression is short for

$$
-3\,{\color{red}{\times}}\,\sqrt{\left(2^4-1\right) \div 3}\,
$$

When multiplying a radical by a number, we usually omit the additional multiplication symbol.

Now, to simplify the given expression, we use PEMDAS. In terms of the order of operations:

- Expressions underneath radicals are treated as if they are contained within parentheses (the **P** in **P**EMDAS), so we always deal with this first.
- Radicals themselves are treated as exponents (the **E** in P**E**MDAS).

Therefore, following PEMDAS, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
-3\sqrt{(2^{4} - 1) \div 3} &=  \\
- 3\sqrt{(16 - 1) \div 3} &=  \\
- 3\sqrt{15 \div 3} &=  \\
&= - 3\sqrt{5} \mid
\end{aligned}
$$

And that's our answer!

To get a numerical approximation, we need to use a calculator. In this case, we get

$$
-3 \sqrt{5} = -3\times \sqrt{5} \approx -6.708\,204
$$

rounded to six decimal places.

<a id="simplifying-a-radical-expression"></a>
## Simplifying a Radical Expression

**Example:** Simplify the expression $\sqrt{((5^2-4) \div 3)-2}$.

**Explanation**

To simplify the given expression, we use PEMDAS. In terms of the order of operations,

- expressions underneath radicals are treated the same as parentheses, and
- radicals themselves are treated as exponents.

Therefore, following the order of operations, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{((5^{2} - 4) \div 3) - 2} &=  \\
\sqrt{((25 - 4) \div 3) - 2} &=  \\
\sqrt{(21 \div 3) - 2} &=  \\
\sqrt{7 - 2} &=  \\
&= \sqrt{5} \mid
\end{aligned}
$$

## Approximating Radical Expressions

**Example:** What is the value of $2\sqrt{13\times 2 - 5} + 1$ rounded to $3$ decimal places?

**Explanation**

To simplify the given expression, we use PEMDAS. In terms of the order of operations,

- expressions underneath radicals are treated the same as parentheses, and
- radicals themselves are treated as exponents.

Therefore, following the order of operations, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
2\sqrt{13 \cdot 2 - 5} + 1 &=  \\
2\sqrt{26 - 5} + 1 &=  \\
&= 2\sqrt{21} + 1
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we approximate the value of the radical using a calculator:

$$
\sqrt{21} \approx 4.582\,576
$$

rounded to $6$ decimal places.

Finally, we approximate the value of the expression:

$$
\begin{aligned}
2\sqrt{21} + 1\begin{vmatrix}\approx \\ ‭‭‭2(4.582576) + 1 &= \\ 9.165152 + 1|= \\ 10.165152\end{vmatrix}\approx \\
&= 10.165
\end{aligned}
$$

rounded to $3$ decimal places.
