## Introduction

Suppose that we have two numbers in scientific notation, like $2 \times 10^2$ and $3.5 \times 10^4$. How can we multiply them together?

To calculate $(2 \times 10^2)\times(3.5 \times 10^4)$, we follow three steps:

- **Step 1:** Group the decimal parts and the powers of $10$ separately.
$(2 \cdot 10^{2}) \cdot (3.5 \cdot 10^{4})|= (2 \cdot 3.5) \cdot (10^{2} \cdot 10^{4})$
- **Step 2:** Multiply the decimal parts.
$(2 \cdot 3.5) \cdot (10^{2} \cdot 10^{4})|= 7 \cdot (10^{2} \cdot 10^{4})$
- **Step 3:** Multiply the powers of $10$ using the product rule for exponents.
$7 \cdot (10^{2} \cdot 10^{4})|= 7 \cdot (10^{2 + 4}); = 7 \cdot 10^{6}$

Therefore, our final answer is $7\times 10^6$.

**Note**: The decimal part of a number in scientific notation is sometimes called the **mantissa**.

<a id="multiplying-two-numbers-with-integer-mantissas"></a>
## Multiplying Two Numbers With Integer Mantissas

**Example:** Simplify the product $\left(-2 \times 10^{-7} \right) \left(3 \times 10^{12} \right)$.

**Explanation**

To multiply two numbers in scientific notation, we separate the decimal parts from the powers of $10$ and multiply them separately.

We also recall that a positive number times a negative number gives a negative number.

So, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
(-2 \cdot 10^{-7})(3 \cdot 10^{12}) &= (-2 \cdot 3) \cdot (10^{-7} \cdot 10^{12}) \\
&=-6 \cdot (10^{(-7) + 12}) \\
&=-6 \cdot 10^{5}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Multiplying Two Numbers Where One Has an Integer Mantissa

**Example:** Simplify the product $\left(-3 \times 10^{6} \right) \left(-3.3 \times 10^{2} \right)$.

**Explanation**

To multiply two numbers in scientific notation, we separate the decimal parts from the powers of $10$ and multiply them separately.

We also recall that a negative number times a negative number gives a positive number.

So, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
(-3 \cdot 10^{6})(-3.3 \cdot 10^{2}) &= ((-3) \cdot (-3.3)) \cdot (10^{6} \cdot 10^{2}) \\
&= (3 \cdot 3.3) \cdot (10^{6} \cdot 10^{2}) \\
&= 9.9 \cdot (10^{6 + 2}) \\
&= 9.9 \cdot 10^{8}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Multiplying Two Numbers With Decimal Mantissas

**Example:** Write $\left(-1.2 \times 10^{3}\right)\left(2.4 \times 10^{2}\right)$ in scientific notation.

**Explanation**

To multiply two numbers in scientific notation, we separate the decimal parts from the powers of $10$ and multiply them separately.

We also recall that a negative number times a positive number gives a negative number.

So, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
(-1.2 \cdot 10^{3}) \cdot (2.4 \cdot 10^{2}) &= ((-1.2) \cdot 2.4) \cdot (10^{3} \cdot 10^{2}) \\
&= - (1.2 \cdot 2.4) \cdot (10^{3} \cdot 10^{2}) \\
&=-2.88 \cdot (10^{3 + 2}) \\
&=-2.88 \cdot 10^{5}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Multiplying Numbers in Scientific Notation: Advanced Cases

**Example:** Express $\left(5.8 \times 10^{-2}\right) \left(4.5 \times 10^{5}\right)$ in scientific notation.

**Explanation**

To multiply two numbers in scientific notation, we separate the decimal parts from the powers of $10$ and multiply them separately:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(5.8 \cdot 10^{-2})(4.5 \cdot 10^{5}) &= (5.8 \cdot 4.5) \cdot (10^{-2} \cdot 10^{5}) \\
&= 26.1 \cdot (10^{(-2) + 5}) \\
&= 26.1 \cdot 10^{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

This is not in scientific notation since $26.1 \gt 10$. Writing the decimal part in scientific notation, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
26.1 \cdot 10^{3} &= (2.61 \cdot 10^{1}) \cdot 10^{3} \\
&= 2.61 \cdot (10^{1} \cdot 10^{3}) \\
&= 2.61 \cdot (10^{1 + 3}) \\
&= 2.61 \cdot 10^{4}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the final answer is $2.61 \times 10^{4}$.
