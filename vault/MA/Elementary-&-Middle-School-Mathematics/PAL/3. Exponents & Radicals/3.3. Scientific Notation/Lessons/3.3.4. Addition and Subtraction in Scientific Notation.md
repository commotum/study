## Introduction

To add two numbers given in scientific notation with the same power of ten, we simply add the decimal parts.

To demonstrate, let's add the numbers $7.7\times 10^4$ and $1.2 \times 10^4$.

Since both numbers are multiplied by $10^4$, we add the decimal parts and then multiply the result by $10^4$. By doing this, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
(7.7 \cdot 10^{4}) + (1.2 \cdot 10^{4}) &= (7.7 + 1.2) \cdot 10^{4} \\
&= 8.9 \cdot 10^{4}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Rewriting the Result When Adding and Subtracting Numbers in Scientific Notation

Sometimes, we'll have to adjust the final result after adding (or subtracting) numbers in scientific notation.

Suppose we wish to add $8.1 \times 10^5$ and $6.2 \times 10^5$.

Since the numbers have the same power of $10$, we add the decimal parts and then multiply the result by $10^{5}$. By doing this, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
(8.1 \cdot 10^{5}) + (6.2 \cdot 10^{5}) &= (8.1 + 6.2) \cdot 10^{5} \\
&= 14.3 \cdot 10^{5}
\end{aligned}
$$

However, the above number is *not* in scientific notation because $14.3 \gt 10$.

So, we need to write $14.3$ in scientific notation as follows:

$$
14.3 = 1.43 \times 10^1
$$

Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
14.3 \cdot 10^{5} &= (1.43 \cdot 10^{1}) \cdot 10^{5} \\
&= 1.43 \cdot (10^{1} \cdot 10^{5}) \\
&= 1.43 \cdot 10^{1 + 5} \\
&= 1.43 \cdot 10^{6}
\end{aligned}
$$

So, our final answer is $1.43 \times 10^6$.

## Adding Numbers in Scientific Notation and Rewriting the Result

**Example:** Express $\left(7.8 \times 10^{-12} \right) + \left(5 \times 10^{-12} \right)$ in scientific notation.

**Explanation**

Since the numbers have the same power of $10$, we add the decimal parts and then multiply the result by $10^{-12}$. By doing this, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
(7.8 \cdot 10^{-12}) + (5 \cdot 10^{-12}) &= (7.8 + 5) \cdot 10^{-12} \\
&= 12.8 \cdot 10^{-12}
\end{aligned}
$$

The above number is not in scientific notation because $12.8 \gt 10$. So, we need to write $12.8$ in scientific notation, as follows:

$$
12.8 = 1.28\times 10^1
$$

Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
12.8 \cdot 10^{-12} &= (1.28 \cdot 10^{1}) \cdot 10^{-12} \\
&= 1.28 \cdot (10^{1} \cdot 10^{-12}) \\
&= 1.28 \cdot 10^{1 - 12} \\
&= 1.28 \cdot 10^{-11}
\end{aligned}
$$

So, our final answer is $1.28 \times 10^{-11}$.

## Subtracting Numbers in Scientific Notation and Rewriting the Result

**Example:** Express $\left(2.7 \times 10^{-9} \right) - \left(3 \times 10^{-9} \right)$ in scientific notation.

**Explanation**

Since the numbers have the same power of $10$, we subtract the decimal parts and then multiply the result by $10^{-9}$. By doing this, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
(2.7 \cdot 10^{-9}) - (3 \cdot 10^{-9}) &= (2.7 - 3) \cdot 10^{-9} \\
&=-0.3 \cdot 10^{-9}
\end{aligned}
$$

The above number is not in scientific notation because $0.3 \lt 1$. So, we need to write $0.3$ in scientific notation, as follows:

$$
0.3 = 3 \times 10^{-1}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
-0.3 \cdot 10^{-9} &= - (3 \cdot 10^{-1}) \cdot 10^{-9} \\
&=-3 \cdot (10^{-1} \cdot 10^{-9}) \\
&=-3 \cdot 10^{-1 - 9} \\
&=-3 \cdot 10^{-10}
\end{aligned}
$$

So, our final answer is $-3 \times 10^{-10}$.
