## Introduction

In this lesson, we'll learn how to add numbers in scientific notation with different powers of ten.

For example, let's compute the following sum:

$$
(4\times 10^{\color{blue}{5}}) + (2 \times 10^{\color{red}{4}})
$$

Notice that the two numbers in our sum have different powers of ten (${\color{blue}{5}}$ and ${\color{red}{4}}$, respectively).

To add these two numbers, we use the following steps:

- **Step 1**: Identify the smaller number in the sum.
- **Step 2**: Rewrite the smaller number to have the same power of ten as the larger number.
- **Step 3**: Add the two numbers as usual.

In our sum, the *second* number $(2\times 10^{\color{red}{4}})$ is the smaller of the two because it has the smallest power of ten.

So, we rewrite this number so that its power of ten is ${\color{blue}{5}}$, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
2 \cdot 10^{4} &=  \\
2 \cdot 10^{5 - 1} &=  \\
2 \cdot 10^{-1 + 5} &=  \\
2 \cdot 10^{-1} \cdot 10^{5} &=  \\
&= 0.2 \cdot 10^{5}
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, carrying out the addition, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
(4 \cdot 10^{5}) + (2 \cdot 10^{4}) &=  \\
(4 \cdot 10^{5}) + (0.2 \cdot 10^{5}) &=  \\
(4 + 0.2) \cdot 10^{5} &=  \\
&= 4.2 \cdot 10^{5}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Adding Numbers With Different Positive Powers of Ten

**Example:** Express $\left(2.4 \times 10^{7}\right) + \left(3.7 \times 10^{8} \right)$ in scientific notation.

**Explanation**

The first number is the smaller of the two, so we rewrite it to have the same power of $10$ as the first number, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
2.4 \cdot 10^{7} &=  \\
2.4 \cdot 10^{8 - 1} &=  \\
2.4 \cdot 10^{-1 + 8} &=  \\
2.4 \cdot 10^{-1} \cdot 10^{8} &=  \\
&= 0.24 \cdot 10^{8}
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, carrying out the addition, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
(2.4 \cdot 10^{7}) + (3.7 \cdot 10^{8}) &=  \\
(0.24 \cdot 10^{8}) + (3.7 \cdot 10^{8}) &=  \\
(0.24 + 3.7) \cdot 10^{8} &=  \\
&= 3.94 \cdot 10^{8}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Adding Numbers With Different Negative Powers of Ten

**Example:** Express $\left(3 \times 10^{-4}\right) + \left(5 \times 10^{-3} \right)$ in scientific notation.

**Explanation**

The first number is the smaller of the two, so we rewrite it to have the same power of $10$ as the second number, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
3 \cdot 10^{-4} &=  \\
3 \cdot 10^{-3 - 1} &=  \\
3 \cdot 10^{-1 - 3} &=  \\
3 \cdot 10^{-1} \cdot 10^{-3} &=  \\
&= 0.3 \cdot 10^{-3}
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, carrying out the addition, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
(3 \cdot 10^{-4}) + (5 \cdot 10^{-3}) &=  \\
(0.3 \cdot 10^{-3}) + (5 \cdot 10^{-3}) &=  \\
(0.3 + 5) \cdot 10^{-3} &=  \\
&= 5.3 \cdot 10^{-3}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Adding Numbers Where the Powers Differ by More Than One

**Example:** Express $\left(7 \times 10^{10} \right) + \left(2\times 10^{12} \right)$ in scientific notation.

**Explanation**

The first number is the smaller of the two, so we rewrite it to have the same power of $10$ as the second number, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
7 \cdot 10^{10} &=  \\
7 \cdot 10^{12 - 2} &=  \\
7 \cdot 10^{-2 + 12} &=  \\
7 \cdot 10^{-2} \cdot 10^{12} &=  \\
&= 0.07 \cdot 10^{12}
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, carrying out the addition, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
(7 \cdot 10^{10}) + (2 \cdot 10^{12}) &=  \\
(0.07 \cdot 10^{12}) + (2 \cdot 10^{12}) &=  \\
&= (0.07 + 2) \cdot 10^{12} \\
&= 2.07 \cdot 10^{12}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Word Problems

**Example:** Astronomers observe the motion of Mars' two moons, Phobos and Deimos, and use these observations to approximate their masses. The mass of Phobos is approximately $1.07\times 10^{16}\,\textrm{kg}$, while the mass of Deimos is approximately $1.5 \times 10^{15}\,\textrm{kg}$. What is the total mass of Mars' two moons?

**Explanation**

We need to write the following expression in scientific notation:
$1.07 \times 10^{16} + 1.5 \times 10^{15}$

The second number is the smaller of the two, so we rewrite it to have the same power of $10$ as the first number, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
1.5 \cdot 10^{15} &=  \\
1.5 \cdot 10^{16 - 1} &=  \\
1.5 \cdot 10^{-1 + 16} &=  \\
1.5 \cdot 10^{-1} \cdot 10^{16} &=  \\
&= 0.15 \cdot 10^{16}
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, carrying out the addition, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
(1.07 \cdot 10^{16}) + (1.5 \cdot 10^{15}) &=  \\
(1.07 \cdot 10^{16}) + (0.15 \cdot 10^{16}) &=  \\
(1.07 + 0.15) \cdot 10^{16} &=  \\
&= 1.22 \cdot 10^{16}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the total mass of the two moons is $1.22 \times 10^{16} \,\textrm{kg}$.
