## Introduction

**Scientific notation** is a special way of writing numbers. It is used by scientists who often deal with very small or very large numbers and need a convenient way to write them.

To write a number in scientific notation, we need to express it in the form

$$
a\times 10^n
$$

where $a$ is a number between $1$ and $10$ (but not including $10$) and $n$ is an integer.

As an example, let's learn how to express $7\,200$ in scientific notation.

To find $a$ in this case, we place a decimal point directly after the first non-zero digit. This gives the following:

$$
a = 7.200 = {\color{blue}{7.2}}
$$

To find $n$, we take the number $a$ that we just found and count the number of spaces we need to move the decimal point to retrieve the original number.

$$
7.2 \rightarrow 7\,200
$$

We can see that the decimal point must move $3$ spaces to the *right*, so we have

$$
n={\color{red}{3}}
$$

Finally, our number can be expressed in scientific notation as

$$
7\,200 = {\color{blue}{7.2}} \times 10^{\color{red}{3}}
$$

## Expressing a Number Greater Than Ten in Scientific Notation

**Example:** What is the number $50\,700$ expressed in scientific notation?

**Explanation**

To write a number in scientific notation, we need to express it in the form

$$
a\times 10^n
$$

where

$$
1 \leq a < 10
$$

and $n$ is an integer.

To find $a$, we place the decimal point directly after the first non-zero digit. In this case, we have the following:

$$
a = 5.0700=5.07
$$

To find $n$, we count the number of spaces we need to move the decimal point to retrieve the original number.

$$
5.07 \rightarrow 50\,700
$$

We can see that the decimal point must move $4$ spaces to the right, so $n=4$.

Finally, our number can be expressed in scientific notation as

$$
50\,700 = 5.07 \times 10^4
$$

## Writing a Number Times an Exponent In Scientific Notation

**Example:** Write $7\,800 \times 10^{3}$ in scientific notation.

**Explanation**

To write a number in scientific notation, we need to express it in the form

$$
a\times 10^n
$$

where

$$
1 \leq a < 10
$$

and $n$ is an integer.

Let's start by writing $7\,800$ in scientific notation.

To find $a$, we place the decimal point directly after the first non-zero digit. In this case, we have the following:
$a = 7.8$

To find $n$, we take the number $a$ that we just found and count the number of spaces we need to move the decimal point to retrieve the original number.

$$
7.8 \rightarrow 7\,800
$$

We can see that the decimal point must move $3$ spaces to the *right*, so $n=3$.

Therefore,

$$
7\,800 = 7.8 \times 10^{3}
$$

Finally, we can express our original number in scientific notation using the laws of exponents, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
7800 \cdot 10^{3} &= 7.8 \cdot 10^{3}_(⏟)_(7800) \cdot 10^{3} \\
&= 7.8 \cdot 10^{3} \cdot 10^{3} \\
&= 7.8 \cdot 10^{3 + 3} \\
&= 7.8 \cdot 10^{6}
\end{aligned}
$$
