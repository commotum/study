## Introduction

To **cube** a number means to multiply that number by itself and then multiply the result by the original number once more.

For example, to "cube $2$", we need to multiply $2$ by $2$ to make $4$, and then multiply this result by $2$ once more. We write the "cube of $2$" as $2^3$, so we have

$$
2^3 = \underbrace{2\cdot 2}_{\large 4} \cdot 2 = 4 \cdot 2 = 8
$$

We would then say that "the cube of $2$ is $8$". We could also say "$2$ cubed equals $8$".

The number ${\color{blue}3}$ in the expression $2^{\color{blue}3}$ is called the power (or exponent).

The act of cubing a number can be represented geometrically. For example, let's start by representing the number $2$ with the following collection of two items:

![](<../Source/3.1.2. Cubing Rational Numbers/Images/2ae3b94e67962495af25d24889d45acb.png>)

Then we can picture $2^3$ by creating a bigger shape (called a **cube**) with length, width and height all equal to $2$:

![](<../Source/3.1.2. Cubing Rational Numbers/Images/944caba9decf5904bfe4d663b13aef09.png>)

There are $8$ items in total in our big cube (one little cube is hiding behind the others), indicating that $2^3=8$.

Clearly,

$$
1^3=1\cdot1\cdot1={\color{blue}1}
$$

The next few first basic cubes are shown below:

| $2^3=2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2={\color{blue}8}$ | $3^3=3 \cdot 3 \cdot 3={\color{blue}27}$ | $4^3=4 \cdot 4 \cdot 4={\color{blue}64}$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| ![](<../Source/3.1.2. Cubing Rational Numbers/Images/ef2941c0838332b0fa9f12953ef86004.png>) | ![](<../Source/3.1.2. Cubing Rational Numbers/Images/ce17cd5891a8dcedfb65ff0ad694c905.png>) | ![](<../Source/3.1.2. Cubing Rational Numbers/Images/bdcaf9471d87b6b98cd2b59cc25b7d5b.png>) |

The cubes of our usual counting numbers are called **cube numbers** or **perfect cubes**. We can write them as follows:
$1^3, 2^3, 3^3, 4^3, 5^3, 6^3, \ldots \qquad \text{or} \qquad {\color{blue}1}, {\color{blue}8}, {\color{blue}27}, {\color{blue}64}, {\color{blue}125}, {\color{blue}216}, \ldots$

## Calculating the Cube of a Number

**Example:** What is the value of $70^3$?

**Explanation**

To work out $70^3$, we multiply $70$ by itself and then multiply the result by $70$ once more:

$$
\begin{aligned}
70^{3} &= 70 \cdot 70 \cdot 70 \\
&= 4900 \cdot 70 \\
&= 343000
\end{aligned}
$$

## The Cube of a Negative Number

Cubing a negative number *always* results in another negative number.

This is because a negative times a negative gives a positive value, and then a positive times a negative is a negative again:

$$
(-)\cdot(-)\cdot(-) = (-)\cdot(-)_(⏟)_((+))\cdot(-) = (+)\cdot(-) = (-)
$$

For example,

$$
({\color{red}-5})^3 = \underbrace{({\color{red}-5}) \cdot ({\color{red}-5})}_{\large {\color{blue}25}} \cdot ({\color{red}-5}) = {\color{blue}25} \cdot ({\color{red}-5}) = {\color{red}-125}
$$

---

<a id="calculating-the-cube-of-a-negative-number"></a>
## Calculating the Cube of a Negative Number

**Example:** Calculate the value of $\left(-13\right)^3$.

**Explanation**

To work out

$$
\left(-13\right)^3
$$

we multiply $-13$ by itself and then multiply the result by $-13$ again:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(-13)^{3} &= (-13) \cdot (-13)^(⏞)^(169) \cdot (-13) \\
&= 169 \cdot (-13) \\
&=-2197
\end{aligned}
$$

## Calculating the Cube of a Fraction

**Example:** Find the value of $\dfrac{3}{5}$ cubed.

**Explanation**

To work out

$$
\left(\dfrac{3}{5}\right)^3
$$

we multiply

$$
\dfrac{3}{5}
$$

by itself and then multiply the result by

$$
\dfrac{3}{5}
$$

again:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(\frac{3}{5})^{3} &= \frac{3}{5} \cdot \frac{3}{5} \cdot \frac{3}{5} \\
&= \frac{3 \cdot 3 \cdot 3}{5 \cdot 5 \cdot 5} \\
&= \frac{27}{125}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Calculating the Cube of a Decimal

**Example:** What is the cube of $(-0.5)$?

**Explanation**

To work out

$$
\left(-0.5\right)^3
$$

we multiply $-0.5$ by itself and then multiply the result by $-0.5$ once more:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(-0.5)^{3} &= (-0.5) \cdot (-0.5)^(⏞)^(0.25) \cdot (-0.5) \\
&= 0.25 \cdot (-0.5) \\
&=-0.125
\end{aligned}
$$
