## Introduction

A **rational expression** is, roughly speaking, an algebraic expression that's in the form of a fraction, or contains fractions.

To evaluate a rational expression like

$$
\dfrac 6 x
$$

for $x=2$, all we need to do is replace $x$ with $2$ in the above expression and simplify as much as possible.

$$
\frac{6}{x} = \frac{6}{2} = 3
$$

---

<a id="evaluating-a-single-fraction"></a>
## Evaluating a Single Fraction

**Example:** Evaluate $\dfrac{x-2}{x+1}$ for $x=5$.

**Explanation**

We replace $x$ with $5$ in the expression and simplify as much as possible.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{x - 2}{x + 1} &=  \\
\frac{5 - 2}{5 + 1} &=  \\
\frac{3}{6} &=  \\
&= \frac{1}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Evaluating Expressions With Parentheses

**Example:** Evaluate $\dfrac{3(2a-1)}{2(3a+1)}$ for $a=1$.

**Explanation**

We replace $a$ with $1$ in the expression and simplify as much as possible.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(3(2a - 1))/(2(3a + 1)) &=  \\
(3(2(1) - 1))/(2(3(1) + 1)) &=  \\
(3(2 - 1))/(2(3 + 1)) &=  \\
(3(1))/(2(4)) &=  \\
&= \frac{3}{8}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Evaluating a Sum or Difference

**Example:** Evaluate $\dfrac{1}{p} + \dfrac{1}{2p}$ for $p=4$.

**Explanation**

We replace $p$ with $4$ in the expression and simplify as much as possible.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{1}{p} + \frac{1}{2p} &=  \\
\frac{1}{4} + \frac{1}{2 \cdot 4} &=  \\
\frac{1}{4} + \frac{1}{8} &=  \\
\frac{2}{8} + \frac{1}{8} &=  \\
\frac{2 + 1}{8} &=  \\
&= \frac{3}{8}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Evaluating Expressions With Multiple Variables

**Example:** Evaluate $\dfrac{n-2}{m-1} + \dfrac{2(m+1)}{n}$ for $m=4$ and $n=6$.

**Explanation**

We replace $m$ with $4$ and $n$ with $6$ in the expression and simplify as much as possible.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{n - 2}{m - 1} + (2(m + 1))/(n) &=  \\
\frac{6 - 2}{4 - 1} + (2(4 + 1))/(6) &=  \\
\frac{4}{3} + (2(5))/(6) &=  \\
\frac{4}{3} + \frac{10}{6} &=  \\
\frac{4}{3} + \frac{5}{3} &=  \\
\frac{4 + 5}{3} &=  \\
\frac{9}{3} &=  \\
&= 3
\end{aligned}
$$
