## Introduction

Suppose that we want to simplify the following expression:

$$
2+\dfrac{5}{2}x -5 + \dfrac{7}{2}x
$$

We begin by collecting like terms.

$$
(\frac{5}{2}x + \frac{7}{2}x) + (2 - 5)
$$

Then, we combine the like terms, simplifying the coefficients as much as possible.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(\frac{5}{2}x + \frac{7}{2}x) + (2 - 5) &=  \\
(\frac{5 + 7}{2})x + (-3) &=  \\
\frac{12}{2}x - 3 &=  \\
&= 6x - 3 \mid
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus, the simplified expression is $6x-3$.

<a id="simplifying-fractions-with-like-denominators"></a>
## Simplifying Fractions with Like Denominators

**Example:** Simplify $\dfrac{1}{3}k +1 + \dfrac{4}{3}k - 3$.

**Explanation**

We collect and combine the like terms, simplifying the coefficients as much as possible.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{1}{3}k + 1 + \frac{4}{3}k - 3 &=  \\
(\frac{1}{3}k + \frac{4}{3}k) + (1 - 3) &=  \\
(\frac{1 + 4}{3})k + (-2) &=  \\
&= \frac{5}{3}k - 2
\end{aligned}
$$

## Simplifying Fractions with Unlike Denominators

**Example:** Simplify the expression $-\dfrac{1}{2}u + \dfrac{7}{8}u$.

**Explanation**

We combine the like terms by adding the coefficients.

$$
\begin{aligned}
-\frac{1}{2}u + \frac{7}{8}u &=  \\
&= (-\frac{1}{2} + \frac{7}{8})u
\end{aligned}
$$

In order to add the coefficients, we have to convert them to have the same denominator.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(-\frac{1}{2} + \frac{7}{8})u &=  \\
(-\frac{4}{8} + \frac{7}{8})u &=  \\
(\frac{-4 + 7}{8})u &=  \\
&= \frac{3}{8}u
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus, the expression simplifies to

$$
\dfrac{3}{8}u
$$

## Using the Distributive Law With Fractions

**Example:** Simplify $\dfrac{1}{2}(p-4q)$.

**Explanation**

Applying the distributive law and simplifying, we have:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{1}{2}(p - 4q) &=  \\
(\frac{1}{2}) \cdot p - (\frac{1}{2}) \cdot 4q &=  \\
\frac{1}{2}p - \frac{4q}{2} &=  \\
&= \frac{1}{2}p - 2q
\end{aligned}
$$

## Simplifying Expressions Using the Distributive Law

**Example:** Simplify $\dfrac{1}{3}(3a+6b)+2a$.

**Explanation**

First, we apply the distributive law.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{1}{3}(3a + 6b) + 2a &=  \\
(\frac{1}{3}) \cdot 3a + (\frac{1}{3}) \cdot 6b + 2a &=  \\
\frac{3a}{3} + \frac{6b}{3} + 2a &=  \\
&= a + 2b + 2a
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we collect and combine the like terms, simplifying the coefficients as much as possible.

$$
\begin{aligned}
a + 2b + 2a &=  \\
a + 2a + 2b &=  \\
&= 3a + 2b
\end{aligned}
$$
