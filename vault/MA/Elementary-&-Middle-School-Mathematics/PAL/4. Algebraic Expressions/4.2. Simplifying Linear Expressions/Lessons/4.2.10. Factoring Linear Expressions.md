## Introduction

**Factoring** is the opposite of distributing. To illustrate, consider the expression $4(a-2b)$. If you consider distributing as going forward,

$$
\begin{aligned}
4(a - 2b) &=  \\
4 \cdot a - 4 \cdot 2b &=  \\
&= 4a - 8b
\end{aligned}
$$

then factoring would be going in reverse:

$$
\begin{aligned}
4a - 8b &=  \\
(4 \cdot a)-(4 \cdot 2b) &=  \\
&= 4(a - 2b)
\end{aligned}
$$

In this case, you would say that $\color{red}4$ was **factored out** of $4a-8b$.

To factor any expression, we just need to follow these two steps:

1. Separate the greatest common factor from each of the terms (ignoring any negative signs). In this case, $\textrm{GCF}(4a,8b) = {\color{red}{4}}$, so:
$4a - 8b|=; (4 \cdot a) - (4 \cdot 2b)$
2. Place the common factor in front of the parentheses, leaving whatever remains inside the parentheses:
$(4 \cdot a) - (4 \cdot 2b)|=; 4(a - 2b)$

<a id="factoring-out-a-positive-number"></a>
## Factoring Out a Positive Number

**Example:** Factor the following expression: $6a+30$.

**Explanation**

We factor in two steps:

1. Separate the greatest common factor from each of the terms. In this case, $\textrm{GCF}(6a,30) = 6$, so:
$6a + 30|=; (6 \cdot a) + (6 \cdot 5)$
2. Place the common factor in front of the parentheses, leaving whatever remains inside the parentheses:
$(6 \cdot a) + (6 \cdot 5)|=; 6(a + 5)$

Thus, the factored form of the expression is $6(a+5)$.

## Factoring Out a Negative Sign

When the terms of an expression are negative, we can also factor out a negative sign. For example, the expression $-x-1$ can be factored into $-(x+1)$.

By the same token, the expression $-2x-2$ can be factored by first factoring out a negative sign, and then factoring out the greatest common factor of $2{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
-2x - 2 &=  \\
- (2x + 2) &=  \\
&= - 2(x + 1)
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="factoring-out-a-negative-number"></a>
## Factoring Out a Negative Number

**Example:** Factor the following expression: $-5a-35$.

**Explanation**

Both terms are negative, so we begin by factoring out a negative sign:

$$
\begin{aligned}
-5a - 35 &=  \\
&= - (5a + 35)
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we continue factoring in two steps:

1. Separate the greatest common factor from each of the terms. In this case, $\textrm{GCF}(5a,35) = 5$, so
$- (5a + 35)|=; - (5 \cdot a + 5 \cdot 7)$
2. Place the common factor in front of the parentheses, leaving whatever remains inside the parentheses:
$- (5 \cdot a + 5 \cdot 7)|=; - 5(a + 7)$

Thus, the factored form of the expression is $-5(a+7)$.

## Factoring Expressions With More Than Two Terms

**Example:** Factor the expression $27a+33b-30$.

**Explanation**

We factor in two steps:

1. Separate the greatest common factor from each of the terms (ignoring any negative signs). In this case, $\textrm{GCF}(27a, 33b, 30) = 3$, so:
$27a + 33b - 30|=; 3 \cdot 9a + 3 \cdot 11b - 3 \cdot 10$
2. Place the common factor in front of the parentheses, leaving whatever remains inside the parentheses:
$3 \cdot 9a + 3 \cdot 11b - 3 \cdot 10|=; 3(9a + 11b - 10)$

Thus, the factored form of the expression is $3(9a+11b-10)$.
