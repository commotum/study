## Introduction

Suppose Linda prepares some fruit juice for a hot summer day. The recipe tells her to mix

$$
2\, \dfrac{1}{5}\,\textrm{L}
$$

of apple juice with

$$
1\, \dfrac{2}{5}\,\textrm{L}
$$

of cherry juice. If Linda follows the recipe exactly, how many liters of fruit juice will she get?

To find the volume of the fruit juice she will get, we need to calculate the following sum:

$$
2\, \dfrac{1}{5} +1\, \dfrac{2}{5}
$$

To add two mixed numbers, we add

- whole numbers to whole numbers, and
- fractions to fractions.

With that in mind, let's add our mixed numbers.

- Adding the whole numbers, we get
$2 + 1 = {\color{red}3}$.
- Adding the fractions, we get
$\dfrac{1}{5} + \dfrac{2}{5} = \dfrac{1+2}{5} = {\color{blue}{\dfrac{3}{5}}}$.

Finally, we combine our results:

$$
2\, \dfrac{1}{5} +1\, \dfrac{2}{5} = {\color{red}3} + {\color{blue}\dfrac{3}{5}} = 3\,\dfrac{3}{5}
$$

Therefore, Linda will get

$$
3\,\dfrac{3}{5}\,\textrm{L}
$$

of fruit juice.

## Adding Mixed Numbers

**Example:** Tom spent $3\,\dfrac{1}{8}$ hours studying math and $2\,\dfrac{3}{8}$ hours studying science. How many hours did Tom spend studying?

**Explanation**

To find the total number of hours Tom spent studying, we need to calculate the sum

$$
3\,\dfrac{1}{8} + 2\,\dfrac{3}{8}
$$

To add two mixed numbers, we add

- whole numbers to whole numbers, and
- fractions to fractions.

With that in mind, let's add our mixed numbers.

- Adding the whole numbers, we get
$3 + 2 = {\color{red}5}$.
- Adding the fractions, we get
$\dfrac{1}{8} + \dfrac{3}{8} = \dfrac{1+3}{8} = \dfrac{4}{8}$.
We can simplify this fraction by dividing the numerator and denominator by $4{:}$
$\dfrac{4}{8} = \dfrac{4 \div 4}{8 \div 4} = {\color{blue}\dfrac{1}{2}}$.

Finally, we combine our results:

$$
3\,\dfrac{1}{8} + 2\,\dfrac{3}{8} = {\color{red}5} + {\color{blue}\dfrac{1}{2}} = 5\,\dfrac{1}{2}
$$

Therefore, Tom spent

$$
5\,\dfrac{1}{2}
$$

hours studying.

## Adding Mixed Numbers When the Fractional Parts Sum to Greater Than a Whole

**Example:** On August $15$, Natalie collected $10\,\dfrac{7}{8}\,\textrm{kg}$ of tomatoes from her garden. On August $18$, she picked $3\,\dfrac{5}{8}\,\textrm{kg}$ more tomatoes than on August $15$. How many kilograms of tomatoes did she pick on August $18$?

**Explanation**

To find the weight of the tomatoes Natalie picked on August $18$, we need to calculate the sum

$$
10\,\dfrac{7}{8} + 3\,\dfrac{5}{8}
$$

To add two mixed numbers, we add

- whole numbers to whole numbers, and
- fractions to fractions.

With that in mind, let's add our mixed numbers.

- Adding the whole numbers, we get
$10 + 3 = {\color{red}13}$.
- Adding the fractions, we get
$\dfrac{7}{8} + \dfrac{5}{8} = \dfrac{7+5}{8} = {\color{blue}\dfrac{12}{8}}$.
We can simplify this fraction by dividing the numerator and denominator by $4{:}$
$\dfrac{12}{8} = \dfrac{12\div 4}{8 \div 4} = \dfrac{3}{2}$.
Then, we convert the fraction $\dfrac{3}{2}$ to a mixed number, as follows:
$\dfrac{3}{2} = 1\,\textrm{R}1 = {\color{blue}1\,\dfrac{1}{2}}$

Finally, we combine our results:

$$
10\,\dfrac{7}{8} + 3\,\dfrac{5}{8} = {\color{red}13} + {\color{blue}1\,\dfrac{1}{2}} =14\,\dfrac{1}{2}
$$

Therefore, on August $18$, Natalie collected

$$
14\,\dfrac{1}{2}\,\textrm{kg}
$$

of tomatoes from her garden.
