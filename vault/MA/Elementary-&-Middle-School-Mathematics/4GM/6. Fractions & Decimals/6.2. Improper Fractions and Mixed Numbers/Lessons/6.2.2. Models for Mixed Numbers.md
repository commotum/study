## Introduction

Another way to represent an improper fraction is to write it as a **mixed number**.

Let's look at the improper fraction

$$
\dfrac{7}{5}
$$

shown below.

![](<../Source/6.2.2. Models for Mixed Numbers/Images/98f0536e88c8bbe3156583c11cc7e7f9.png>)

We can separate this improper fraction into "wholes" and "fractions" like so:

![](<../Source/6.2.2. Models for Mixed Numbers/Images/989eab0af94696e36f16292186e14589.png>)

We see that we have $\color{red}1$ whole and a fraction of

$$
\color{blue}\dfrac 2 5
$$

Therefore, this picture represents the mixed number

$$
{\color{red}{1}}\,{\color{blue}{\dfrac 2 5}}\,
$$

It's important to realize that

$$
\dfrac 7 5
$$

and

$$
1\,\dfrac 2 5
$$

represent the *same* number!

## Representing Mixed Numbers Using Area Models

**Example:** What mixed number is shown in the picture below?

![](<../Source/6.2.2. Models for Mixed Numbers/Images/8f78004099bf7d9c3d62ab311eebbf36.png>)

**Explanation**

There are $\color{red}2$ shapes completely shaded:

![](<../Source/6.2.2. Models for Mixed Numbers/Images/7252df900b72aa955ff2f2ed510be795.png>)

So, there are $\color{red}2$ wholes.

Additionally, one shape is partially shaded:

![](<../Source/6.2.2. Models for Mixed Numbers/Images/fc2afe76471c654c25f14c4c2d3c2cf8.png>)

This shape has $\color{blue}3$ parts, and $\color{blue}2$ parts are shaded. So,

$$
\color{blue}\dfrac{2}{3}
$$

of this shape is shaded.

Therefore, the mixed number is

$$
{\color{red}2}\, {\color{blue}\dfrac{2}{3}}
$$

<a id="number-line-models-for-mixed-numbers"></a>
## Number Line Models for Mixed Numbers

We can represent mixed numbers with number lines.

For example, let's write the number shown on the number line below as a mixed number.

![](<../Source/6.2.2. Models for Mixed Numbers/Images/fb88c92d4c5779868600d0189699d514.png>)

First, notice that the line segment from $0$ to $1$ is split into $\color{blue}3$ equal parts.

![](<../Source/6.2.2. Models for Mixed Numbers/Images/b5df9d555e3e5d2465966022cfc4426a.png>)

We can land on our point by making $\color{red}1$ whole step plus $\color{blue}2$ part steps (out of a possible ${\color{blue}{3}})$.

![](<../Source/6.2.2. Models for Mixed Numbers/Images/8e350f825fa881f5181f90439808a8dd.png>)

Therefore, the mixed number is

$$
{\color{red}1}{\color{blue}\dfrac{2}{3}}
$$

<a id="representing-mixed-numbers-using-number-lines"></a>
## Representing Mixed Numbers Using Number Lines

**Example:** What mixed number is shown on the number line?

![](<../Source/6.2.2. Models for Mixed Numbers/Images/c5d5506c4844efccf3a73c2f6fa59722.png>)

**Explanation**

The line segment from $0$ to $1$ is split into $\color{blue}6$ equal parts. Each part represents

$$
\dfrac{1}{\color{blue}6}
$$

of a whole.

![](<../Source/6.2.2. Models for Mixed Numbers/Images/1cf32f123e3f1f58047b7226300ad5f1.png>)

Starting from zero, we land on our point by making $\color{red}2$ whole steps plus $\color{blue}1$ part step:

![](<../Source/6.2.2. Models for Mixed Numbers/Images/94e3752ec43416082cc2e879baa027fc.png>)

Therefore, the mixed number is

$$
{\color{red}2}{\color{blue}\dfrac{1}{6}}
$$
