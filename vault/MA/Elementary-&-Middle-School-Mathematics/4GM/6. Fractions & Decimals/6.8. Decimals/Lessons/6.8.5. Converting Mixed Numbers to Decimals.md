## Introduction

Whenever we write a mixed number as a decimal, our decimal will have

- a **whole number part** to the *left* of the decimal point, corresponding to the whole number part of the mixed number, and
- a **decimal part** to the *right* of the decimal point, corresponding to the fractional part of the mixed number.

For example, let's write the following mixed number as a decimal:

$$
7 \,\dfrac{9}{10}
$$

To express this mixed number as a decimal, we look at the whole number and fractional parts:

- The whole number part is ${\color{blue}{7}}$.
- The fractional part can be written as a decimal in the following way:
$\dfrac{\color{red}9}{10} = 0.{\color{red}{9}}$

Therefore, we can express our mixed number as a decimal as follows:

$$
{\color{blue}{7.}}{\color{red}{9}}
$$

## Converting Mixed Numbers With Tenths to Decimals

**Example:** Which decimal number is equivalent to $3 \,\dfrac{5}{10}$?

**Explanation**

Whenever we write a mixed number as a decimal, our decimal will have

- a *whole number part* to the left of the decimal point, corresponding to the whole number, and
- a *decimal part* to the right of the decimal point, corresponding to the fraction.

With that in mind, let's write

$$
{\color{blue}3} \,{\color{red}\dfrac{5}{10}}
$$

as a decimal.

- The whole number part is ${\color{blue}3}$.
- The fractional part can be written as a decimal in the following way:
$\dfrac{\color{red}5}{10} = 0.{\color{red}5}$

Therefore, we can write the given mixed number as a decimal number with $1$ decimal place:

$$
{\color{blue}{3}} \,{\color{red}{\dfrac{5}{10}}} = {\color{blue}{3.}}{\color{red}{5}}
$$

## Converting Fractions to Decimals Using Equivalent Fractions

We sometimes need to find an equivalent fraction for the fractional part when expressing a mixed number as a decimal.

For example, let's consider the following mixed number:

$$
{\color{blue}2}\,{\color{red}\dfrac{1}{5}}
$$

Notice the denominator of the fractional part $({\color{red}{5}})$ is not $10,100$, or $1,000$. However, we can still express this fraction as a decimal, as follows:

- The whole number part is ${\color{blue}2}$.
- The fractional part is $\dfrac{1}{5}$. We can make a denominator of $10$ by multiplying the numerator and denominator by $2{:}$
$\dfrac{1}{5} = \dfrac{1\times 2}{5 \times 2} = \dfrac{2}{10}$
So, the fractional part can be represented as a decimal with $1$ decimal place:
$\dfrac{\color{red}2}{10} =0.{\color{red}2}$

Therefore, we can express our mixed number as a decimal as follows:

$$
{\color{blue}{2}}\,{\color{red}{\dfrac{1}{5}}} = {\color{blue}{2.}}{\color{red}{2}}
$$

## Converting Mixed Numbers to Decimals

**Example:** Which decimal number is equivalent to $53 \,\dfrac{3}{50}$?

**Explanation**

Whenever we write a mixed number as a decimal, our decimal will have

- a *whole number part* to the left of the decimal point, corresponding to the whole number, and
- a *decimal part* to the right of the decimal point, corresponding to the fraction.

With that in mind, let's write

$$
{\color{blue}53} \,{\color{red}\dfrac{3}{50}}
$$

as a decimal.

- The whole number part is ${\color{blue}53}$.
- For the fractional part $\dfrac{3}{50}$, we can make a denominator of $100$ by multiplying the numerator and denominator by $2{:}$
$\dfrac{3}{50} = \dfrac{3\times 2}{50 \times 2} = \dfrac{6}{100}$
So, the fractional part can be represented as a decimal with $2$ decimal places:
$\dfrac{\color{red}6}{100} =0.{\color{red}06}$

Therefore, we can express our mixed number as a decimal as follows:

$$
{\color{blue}53} \,{\color{red}\dfrac{3}{50}} = {\color{blue}{53.}}{\color{red}{06}}
$$
