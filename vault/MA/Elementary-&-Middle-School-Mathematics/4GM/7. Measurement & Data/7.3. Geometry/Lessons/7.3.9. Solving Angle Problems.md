## Introduction

In this lesson, we'll extend our understanding of angles to real-world and mathematical problems.

For example, let's find the angle formed by the minute and hour hands of a clock at $5$:$00$, as shown below.

![](<../Source/7.3.9. Solving Angle Problems/Images/fe07e8e3ab7529a0885f9a1431142ad4.png>)

If we connect the center of the clock face and the hour marks, we get $12$ identical angles. And, since a full angle has a measure of $360^\circ$, each of these angles has a measure of

$$
\dfrac{360^\circ}{12}
$$

Now, let's compute the value of this fraction:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{360}{12} &=  \\
360 \div 12 &=  \\
36 \cdot 10 \div 12 &=  \\
(36 \div 12) \cdot 10 &=  \\
3 \cdot 10 &=  \\
&= 30
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\dfrac{360^\circ}{12} = 30^\circ
$$

The angle formed by the minute and hour hands of a clock at $5$:$00$ contains $5$ such angles, so its measure is

$$
5 \times 30^\circ = 150^\circ
$$

## Calculating Angles From Full Angles Given in Context

**Example:** A wheel has $10$ spokes, as shown below. The angles between the adjacent spokes are identical. Find the measure of the angle formed by adjacent spokes.

![](<../Source/7.3.9. Solving Angle Problems/Images/87d9bc46b300541633136adda1ce9f04.png>)

**Explanation**

The spokes of a wheel form $10$ identical central angles. These $10$ angles form a full angle of $360^\circ$, so the measure of each angle is

$$
\dfrac{360^\circ}{10}
$$

Now, let's compute the value of the fraction:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{360}{10} &=  \\
360 \div 10 &=  \\
360 &=  \\
&= 36
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\dfrac{360^\circ}{10} = 36^\circ
$$

## Finding Fractions of a Full Angle Given in Context

**Example:** A wheel has $18$ spokes. The angles between the adjacent spokes are identical. Find the measure of the shaded angle.

![](<../Source/7.3.9. Solving Angle Problems/Images/7275414902082449f577d8aea8c8d7ad.png>)

**Explanation**

The spokes of the wheel form $18$ identical angles. Since a full angle has a measure of $360^\circ$, the measure of one division is

$$
\dfrac{360^\circ}{18}
$$

Now, let's compute the value of the fraction:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{360}{18} &=  \\
360 \div 18 &=  \\
(36 \div 18) \cdot 10 &=  \\
2 \cdot 10 &=  \\
&= 20
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\dfrac{360^\circ}{18} = 20^\circ
$$

The shaded angle consists of $4$ divisions, so its measure is

$$
4 \times 20^\circ = 80^\circ
$$
