## Introduction

Consider a rectangle shown below.

![](<../Source/7.3.11. Modeling With Rectangles/Images/a23b18cbc294ee4018cde16c0468d9db.png>)

From the diagram, we note the following:

- The *width* of the rectangle is $8 \, \textrm{m}$.
- The *area* of the rectangle is $48 \, \textrm{m}^2$. The area units $(\textrm{m}^2)$ are called **square meters.**

How do we use this information to determine the *length* of this rectangle?

First, let's denote the unknown length using the letter $x$.

![](<../Source/7.3.11. Modeling With Rectangles/Images/10909feecdd2e21ecbdce1c15fe7ac49.png>)

Now, remember that the area of a rectangle is given by the formula

$$
{\color{red}{\textrm{Area}}} = \textrm{Length} \times {\color{blue}{\textrm{Width}}}
$$

In our example, the area is ${\color{red}{48}} \, \textrm{m}^2$, the length is $x\,\textrm{m}$, and the width is ${\color{blue}{8}} \, \textrm{m}$.
Substituting these values into the equation above, we obtain

$$
{\color{red}{48}} = x \times {\color{blue}{8}}
$$

Now, the number $x$ whose product with $8$ equals $48$ is given by

$$
48 \div 8 = 6
$$

Therefore, the rectangle's length is $6 \, \textrm{m}$.

## Finding an Equation for a Rectangle’s Area With an Unknown Factor

**Example:** The area of a rectangle is $28 \, \textrm{cm}^2$ and its length is $7 \, \textrm{cm}$. If $x$ denotes the unknown width of the rectangle, what equation can be used to find that width?

![](<../Source/7.3.11. Modeling With Rectangles/Images/67520f61087537b4181ac5b13a983b38.png>)

*Note: The picture is not drawn to scale.*

**Explanation**

Remember that the area of a rectangle is given by

$$
\textrm{Area} = \textrm{Length} \times \textrm{Width}
$$

In our example, the area is $28 \, \textrm{cm}^2$, the length is $7 \, \textrm{cm}$, and the width is $x\, \textrm{cm}$.

![](<../Source/7.3.11. Modeling With Rectangles/Images/c88ba3761958cf83270667568a93fd1a.png>)

Substituting these into the equation above, we obtain

$$
28 = 7 \times x
$$

Notice that we can swap the factors on the right-hand side. Therefore, there are two possible equations:

$$
28 = 7 \times x \qquad\textrm{or}\qquad 28 = x \times 7
$$

## Finding the Length or Width of a Rectangle Given Its Area

**Example:** The area of a rectangle is $30 \, \textrm{m}^2$ and its width is $6 \, \textrm{m}$. What is the length of the rectangle?

![](<../Source/7.3.11. Modeling With Rectangles/Images/1673a6c7095b89d3a64ff2af946901c0.png>)

*Note: The picture is not drawn to scale.*

**Explanation**

Remember that the area of a rectangle is given by

$$
\textrm{Area} = \textrm{Length} \times \textrm{Width}
$$

In our example, the area is $30 \, \textrm{m}^2$ and the width is $6 \, \textrm{m}$. Let's denote the unknown length as $x$.

![](<../Source/7.3.11. Modeling With Rectangles/Images/7e11520b7ef495d65ea789dcdbf06cf3.png>)

Substituting the known values into the equation above, we obtain

$$
30 = x \times 6
$$

Now, the number $x$ whose product with $6$ equals $30$ is

$$
30 \div 6 = 5
$$

Therefore, the rectangle's length is $5 \, \textrm{m}$.

## Perimeters of Rectangles

The **perimeter** of a rectangle is the combined length of the rectangle's sides.

Let's compute the perimeter of the rectangle shown below.

![](<../Source/7.3.11. Modeling With Rectangles/Images/8283aeb99c012fcc3e47be2d1d673e8d.png>)

Note the following:

- The rectangle's *length* is ${\color{red}{6}}\,\textrm{m}$.
- The rectangle's *width* is ${\color{blue}{8}}\,\textrm{m}$.

Now, notice that the rectangle has $2$ sides of length ${\color{red}{6}}\,\textrm{m}$, and $2$ sides of length ${\color{blue}{8}}\,\textrm{m}$. Therefore, the perimeter of the rectangle, in meters, is given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
Perimeter &= 2 \cdot Length + 2 \cdot Width \\
&= 2 \cdot 6 + 2 \cdot 8 \\
&= 12 + 16 \\
&= 28
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the perimeter of the rectangle is $28\,\textrm{m}$.

Sometimes, we might know the perimeter of a rectangle, and we need to use this to determine the rectangle's length or width. Let's see an example.

<a id="finding-the-length-or-width-of-a-rectangle-given-its-perimeter"></a>
## Finding the Length or Width of a Rectangle Given Its Perimeter

**Example:** The perimeter of a rectangle is $18 \, \textrm{cm}$ and its width is $3 \, \textrm{cm}$. Which of the following options gives the length of the rectangle?

1. $4 \, \textrm{cm}$
2. $5 \, \textrm{cm}$
3. $6 \, \textrm{cm}$

**Explanation**

Remember that the perimeter of a rectangle is given by

$$
\textrm{Perimeter} = 2 \times \textrm{Length} + 2 \times \textrm{Width}
$$

In our example, the perimeter is $18 \, \textrm{cm}$ and the width is $3 \, \textrm{cm}$. Let's denote the unknown length as $x$.

![](<../Source/7.3.11. Modeling With Rectangles/Images/98feda7ff5ac6e1abacba036ea49a218.png>)

Substituting the known values into the equation above, we obtain

$$
18 = 2 \times x+2 \times 3
$$

Since

$$
{\color{red}2 \times 3} = {\color{red}6}
$$

we have

$$
18 = 2 \cdot x + 6
$$

Now, to find $x$, we try each of the given values, starting from the smallest:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\begin{bmatrix}2 \cdot 4 + 6 &= 14 \ne 18 & \cdot \\ 2 \cdot 5 + 6|= 16 \ne 18 & \cdot \\ 2 \cdot 6 + 6|= 18 & ✓\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the rectangle's length is $6 \, \textrm{cm}$.

**Note**: The symbol

$$
\neq
$$

means "is not equal to."

## Modeling With Rectangles

**Example:** The perimeter of a rectangular laptop screen is $42 \, \textrm{in}$, and its length is $12 \, \textrm{in}$. Which of the following gives the width of the laptop screen?

1. $7\, \textrm{in}$
2. $8 \, \textrm{in}$
3. $9 \, \textrm{in}$

**Explanation**

Remember that the perimeter of a rectangle is given by

$$
\textrm{Perimeter} = 2 \times \textrm{Length} + 2 \times \textrm{Width}
$$

In our example, the perimeter is $42 \, \textrm{in}$ and the length is $12 \, \textrm{in}$. Let's denote the unknown width as $x$.

Substituting the known values into the equation above, we obtain

$$
42 = 2 \times 12 + 2 \times x
$$

Since

$$
{\color{red}2 \times 12} = {\color{red}24}
$$

we have

$$
42 = 24 + 2 \cdot x
$$

Now, to find $x$, we try each of the given values, starting from the smallest:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\begin{bmatrix}24 + 2 \cdot 7 &= 38 \ne 42 & \cdot \\ 24 + 2 \cdot 8|= 40 \ne 42 & \cdot \\ 24 + 2 \cdot 9|= 42 & ✓\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the laptop screen's width is $9 \, \textrm{in}$.
