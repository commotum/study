## Introduction

Comparison statements frequently occur in real-life situations. In this lesson, we will learn how to turn problems involving comparisons into mathematical equations and solve them.

For example, let's take a look at the following situation:

$\qquad$ *Clara is $9$ years old. If Clara's father is $4$ times older than she is, how old is Clara's father?*

We usually begin by assigning a letter to the unknown value we want to find. So, we let ${\color{blue}{A}}$ represent Clara's father's age.

Now, let's write down the important parts of the given information:

- Clara is $9$ years old.
- Clara's father is $4$ times older than Clara.

So, Clara's father's age, ${\color{blue}{A}}$, is $4$ times larger than Clara's age, $9$. This can be represented by the following multiplication problem:

$$
4 \times 9 = {\color{blue}{A}}
$$

Calculating the product, we get

$$
4 \times 9 = {\color{blue}{36}}
$$

Therefore,

$$
{\color{blue}{A}} = {\color{blue}{36}}
$$

This means that Clara's father is $36$ years old.

## Representing an Additive Comparison as an Equation

**Example:** There were $B$ boys and $36$ girls at a summer camp. If there were $9$ more girls than boys at the camp, which equation could be used to find how many boys there were?

**Explanation**

Let's start by writing down the important parts of the given information:

- There were $B$ boys at a summer camp.
- There were also $36$ girls at the camp, and this was $9$ more than the number of boys.

So, the number of girls, $36$, is $9$ **larger** than the number of boys, $B$. This can be represented by the following addition problems:

$$
B+9 = 36 \qquad \text{or} \qquad 9 + B = 36
$$

## Representing a Multiplicative Comparison as an Equation: Unknown Product

**Example:** Four employees are asked to hand out flyers in a shopping mall to promote a pizzeria. If they each received $40$ flyers, what equation could we use to find how many flyers they were given in total? Use $F$ to represent the total number of flyers.

**Explanation**

Let's start by writing down the important parts of the given information:

- Each employee received $40$ flyers.
- The task of handing out the flyers was given to $4$ employees.

So, the total number of flyers, $F$, is $4$ **times larger** than the number of flyers given to each employee, $40$. This can be represented by the following multiplication problems:

$$
40 \times 4 = F \qquad \text{or} \qquad 4 \times 40 = F
$$

## Representing a Multiplicative Comparison as an Equation: Unknown Factor

**Example:** Eve and Rosie went shopping. If Eve spent $5$ times as much as Rosie and, in total, she spent $\[math]105$, what equation could we use to determine how much Rosie spent? Use $R$ to represent the amount of money Rosie spent.

**Explanation**

Let's start by writing down the important parts of the given information:

- Eve spent $\[math]105$.
- Eve spent $5$ times as much as Rosie.

So, the amount spent by Eve, $\[/math]105$, is $5$ **times larger** than the amount spent by Rosie, $R$. This can be represented by the following multiplication problems:

$$
R \times 5 = 105 \qquad \text{or} \qquad 5 \times R = 105
$$

## Solving a Word Problem

**Example:** Hank has $36$ marbles. If Hank has $4$ times as many marbles as Ned, how many marbles does Ned have?

**Explanation**

Let ${\color{blue}{N}}$ be the number of marbles Ned has. Now, let's list the given information:

- Hank has $36$ marbles.
- Hank has $4$ times as many marbles as Ned.

So, the number of marbles Hank has, $36$, is $4$ times larger than the number of marbles Ned has, ${\color{blue}{N}}$. This can be represented by the following multiplication problem:

$$
4 \times {\color{blue}{N}} = 36
$$

Therefore,

$$
{\color{blue}{N}} = 9
$$

and we have

$$
4 \times {\color{blue}{9}} = 36
$$

So, Ned has $9$ marbles.
