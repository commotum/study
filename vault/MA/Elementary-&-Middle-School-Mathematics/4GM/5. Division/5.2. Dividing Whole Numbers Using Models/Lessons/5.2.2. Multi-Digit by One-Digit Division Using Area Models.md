## Introduction

We can use area models to represent division problems involving three-digit numbers.

Let's consider the area model below.

![](<../Source/5.2.2. Multi-Digit by One-Digit Division Using Area Models/Images/588a30c846eb03562d5d4edf2273d337.png>)

To determine the missing length, we consider the area of the yellow rectangle (the one on the left-hand side).

The area of the yellow rectangle is $320$, and its width is $4$. Hence, the length of this rectangle must be
$320\div 4$.

To carry out this division, we write $320$ as a product with $10$ as one of the factors and then swap the order of multiplication and division:

$$
\begin{aligned}
320 \div 4 &=  \\
32 \cdot 10 \div 4 &=  \\
32 \div 4 \cdot 10 &=  \\
(32 \div 4) \cdot 10 &=  \\
8 \cdot 10 &=  \\
&= 80
\end{aligned}
$$

We can now add this length to the area model.

![](<../Source/5.2.2. Multi-Digit by One-Digit Division Using Area Models/Images/6eda76779dd8fd772054b8e573b06b75.png>)

## Completing an Area Model

**Example:** From left to right, find the missing numbers in the area model below.

![](<../Source/5.2.2. Multi-Digit by One-Digit Division Using Area Models/Images/85cc3b59b3f65bfe1cdb0781519dd527.png>)

**Explanation**

We need to look at the blue and pink rectangles:

- Let's first look at the blue rectangle (the one on the left-hand side). Its area is $350$, and its width is $5$. Therefore, the length of the rectangle must be
$350 \div 5$.
To carry out this division, we write $350$ as a product with $10$ as one of the factors and then swap the order of multiplication and division:
$350 \div 5|=; 35 \cdot 10 \div 5|=; 35 \div 5 \cdot 10|=; (35 \div 5) \cdot 10|=; 7 \cdot 10|=; 70$
Let's add this to our model:![](<../Source/5.2.2. Multi-Digit by One-Digit Division Using Area Models/Images/d20061e2d8665d21e7b9223eb88df1ff.png>)
- Now, let's look at the pink rectangle (the one on the right-hand side). Its area is $30$, and its width is $5$. Therefore, the length of the rectangle must be
$30 \div 5 = {\color{blue}6}$.
Let's add this to our model:![](<../Source/5.2.2. Multi-Digit by One-Digit Division Using Area Models/Images/bcd26e0b0c2e08c0c4c1f1aa952e059b.png>)

Therefore, from left to right, the missing numbers are $70$ and $6$.

## Interpreting an Area Model

**Example:** The area model above can be used to represent the following division problem

![](<../Source/5.2.2. Multi-Digit by One-Digit Division Using Area Models/Images/cfb097302ab4fb08293414b4c4dd8f2e.png>)

$00$

What is the missing number?

**Explanation**

Let's look at the big rectangle:

- its length is $40+7=47$
- its width is $9$
- its area is $360 + 63 = 423$

Therefore, this area model can be used to represent the following multiplication problem:

$$
423 = 47 \times 9
$$

Writing this as a division problem, we get

$$
423 \div 9 = 47
$$

Therefore, the missing number is $423$.

<a id="completing-an-area-model-and-stating-a-result"></a>
## Completing an Area Model and Stating a Result

**Example:** Leonard packed $812$ apples into bags. One bag holds $4$ apples. Using the area model below, find how many bags of apples Leonard packed.

![](<../Source/5.2.2. Multi-Digit by One-Digit Division Using Area Models/Images/f4ea3e3bb86a2bd008a6e86f1306256f.png>)

**Explanation**

To determine the number of bags Leonard packed, we need to calculate $812\div 4$.

- Let's first look at the blue rectangle (the one on the left-hand side). Its area is $800$, and its width is $4$. Hence, the length of this rectangle must be
$800 \div 4$.
To carry out this division, we write $800$ as a product with $100$ as one of the factors and then swap the order of multiplication and division:
$800 \div 4|=; 8 \cdot 100 \div 4|=; 8 \div 4 \cdot 100|=; (8 \div 4) \cdot 100|=; 2 \cdot 100|=; 200$
Let's add this to our model:![](<../Source/5.2.2. Multi-Digit by One-Digit Division Using Area Models/Images/0ebbaa6d6d76cb05216fb33858f75201.png>)
- Now, let's look at the pink rectangle (the one on the right-hand side). Its area is $12$, and its width is $4$. Hence, the length of this rectangle must be
$12 \div 4 = 3$.
Let's add this to our model:![](<../Source/5.2.2. Multi-Digit by One-Digit Division Using Area Models/Images/df647f393874de4f49e0c5c37c647a65.png>)

Now, notice the following regarding the big rectangle:

- its length is $200+3=203$
- its width is $4$
- its area is $800+12=812$

Therefore, this area model can be used to represent the following multiplication problem:

$$
812 = 203 \times 4
$$

Writing this as a division problem, we get

$$
812 \div 4 = 203
$$

Therefore, Leonard packed $203$ bags of apples.
