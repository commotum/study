## Introduction

We can use place value strategies to divide some large numbers quickly and efficiently.

To demonstrate, let's discuss how to use place value strategies to solve the following division problem:

$$
{\color{red}{20}} \div 2
$$

First, we write the number we're dividing $({\color{red}{20}})$ in expanded form:

$$
2 \cdot 10 \div 2
$$

Then, we swap the order of the multiplication and division and evaluate the expression:

$$
\begin{aligned}
2 \cdot 10 \div 2 &=  \\
2 \div 2 \cdot 10 &=  \\
(2 \div 2) \cdot 10 &=  \\
1 \cdot 10 &=  \\
&= 10
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
20 \div 2 = 10
$$

## Dividing Two-Digit Numbers by One-Digit Numbers

**Example:** Sarah will use $90$ beads to make $3$ identical necklaces. How many beads will each necklace have?

**Explanation**

To determine the number of beads each necklace will have, we need to calculate $90\div 3$.

Remember that $90$ in expanded form is

$$
9 \times 10
$$

Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
90 \div 3 &=  \\
&= 9 \cdot 10 \div 3 \mid
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we swap the order of the multiplication and division:

$$
\begin{aligned}
9 \cdot 10 \div 3 &=  \\
9 \div 3 \cdot 10 &=  \\
(9 \div 3) \cdot 10 &=  \\
3 \cdot 10 &=  \\
&= 30
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, each necklace will have $30$ beads.

## Dividing Three-Digit Numbers by One-Digit Numbers

**Example:** What is $280 \div 7$?

**Explanation**

Remember that $280$ is the same as $28$ tens. So, we have

$$
280 = 28 \times {10}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
280 \div 7 &=  \\
&= 28 \cdot 10 \div 7 \mid
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we swap the order of the multiplication and division:

$$
\begin{aligned}
28 \cdot 10 \div 7 &=  \\
28 \div 7 \cdot 10 &=  \\
(28 \div 7) \cdot 10 &=  \\
4 \cdot 10 &=  \\
&= 40
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
280\div 7 = 40
$$

## Dividing Four-Digit Numbers by One-Digit Numbers

**Example:** Compute the value of $3,200 \div 4$.

**Explanation**

Remember that $3,200$ is the same as $32$ hundreds. So, we have

$$
3,200 = 32 \times {100}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
3, 200 \div 4 &=  \\
&= 32 \cdot 100 \div 4 \mid
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we swap the order of the multiplication and division:

$$
\begin{aligned}
32 \cdot 100 \div 4 &=  \\
32 \div 4 \cdot 100 &=  \\
(32 \div 4) \cdot 100 &=  \\
8 \cdot 100 &=  \\
&= 800
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
3,200\div 4 = 800
$$
