## Introduction

We can use rounding to *estimate* the result of a division problem.

For example, let's estimate the quotient of

$$
478 \div 10
$$

By rounding the dividend to the nearest ten, we obtain the following:

$$
478\begin{vmatrix}\div & 10 \\ ↓ & ↓ \\ 480 & \div\end{vmatrix}10
$$

So, to estimate the quotient of our original division problem, we need to find the value of

$$
480 \div 10
$$

We can solve this simplified problem as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
480 \div 10 &=  \\
480 &=  \\
&= 48
\end{aligned}
$$

So, we conclude that

$$
478 \div 10 \approx 48
$$

Remember that the symbol "

$$
\approx
$$

" means "is approximately equal to."

## Estimating a Division by Rounding to the Nearest Ten

**Example:** By rounding the dividend to the nearest ten, estimate the quotient of $448\div 5$.

**Explanation**

We round the dividend to the nearest ten:

$$
448\begin{vmatrix}\div & 5 \\ ↓ & ↓ \\ 450 & \div\end{vmatrix}5
$$

Hence, we need to find the value of $450 \div 5$.

Remember that $450$ is the same as

$$
450 = 45 \times 10
$$

So, we can solve this division problem using place value strategies as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
450 \div 5 &=  \\
45 \cdot 10 \div 5 &=  \\
45 \div 5 \cdot 10 &=  \\
(45 \div 5) \cdot 10 &=  \\
9 \cdot 10 &=  \\
&= 90
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
448 \div 5 \approx 90
$$

## Estimating a Division by Rounding to the Nearest Hundred or Thousand

**Example:** By rounding the dividend to the nearest hundred, estimate the quotient of $7,971 \div 10$.

**Explanation**

We round the dividend to the nearest hundred:

$$
7, 971\begin{vmatrix}\div & 10 \\ ↓ & ↓ \\ 8, 000 & \div\end{vmatrix}10
$$

Hence, we need to find the value of $8,000 \div 10$.

Since the divisor equals $10$, we solve this simplified division problem as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
8, 000 \div 10 &=  \\
&= 8, 000 \\
&= 800
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
7,971 \div 10 \approx 800
$$

## Estimating Division: Word Problems

**Example:** A farmer needs to pack $27,825\,\textrm{kg}$ of potatoes into bags. Each bag fits exactly $7\,\textrm{kg}$ of potatoes. By rounding the total weight of potatoes to the nearest thousand kilograms, estimate the number of bags needed to pack all the potatoes.

**Explanation**

To find the number of bags needed to pack the potatoes, we need to divide $27,825$ by $7$.

First, we round the dividend to the nearest thousand:

$$
27, 825\begin{vmatrix}\div & 7 \\ ↓ & ↓ \\ 28, 000 & \div\end{vmatrix}7
$$

Hence, we need to find the value of $28,000 \div 7$.

Remember that $28,000$ is the same as

$$
28,000 = 28 \times 1,000
$$

So, we can solve this division problem using place value strategies as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
28, 000 \div 7 &=  \\
28 \cdot 1, 000 \div 7 &=  \\
28 \div 7 \cdot 1, 000 &=  \\
(28 \div 7) \cdot 1, 000 &=  \\
4 \cdot 1, 000 &=  \\
&= 4, 000
\end{aligned}
$$

So,

$$
27,825 \div 7 \approx 4,000
$$

Therefore, the farmer needs about $4,000$ bags.
