## Introduction

We've seen how to use area models to multiply two-digit numbers. Let's now discuss a similar method that does not require models.

For example, let's consider the following multiplication problem:

$$
13 \times 24
$$

We begin by expressing both factors in expanded form:

$$
\begin{aligned}
13 &= 10 + 3, 24 = 20 + 4
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we multiply *both* numbers in the first factor's expanded form by *both* numbers in the second factor's expanded form:

- First, we multiply ${\color{blue}{10}}$ by ${\color{red}{20}}$ and ${\color{red}{4}}$:
$10 \cdot 20|= 200; 10 \cdot 4|= 40$
- Then, we multiply ${\color{blue}{3}}$ by ${\color{red}{20}}$ and ${\color{red}{4}}$:
$3 \cdot 20|= 60; 3 \cdot 4|= 12$

Let's now add all our results:

$$
1; 2\begin{vmatrix}0 & 0 \\ + & 4\end{vmatrix}0; +\begin{vmatrix}6 & 0 \\ + & 1\end{vmatrix}2; 3 \mid 1 \mid 2
$$

Therefore,

$$
13 \times 24 = 312
$$

It's worth taking a moment to compare this computation to the corresponding area model.

![](<../Source/4.3.6. Two-Digit by Two-Digit Multiplication Using Place Value/Images/91b9ab30944beefea6ea435d62986c24.png>)

Notice the following:

- The intermediate products $(200,40,60$, and $12)$ we found during our calculation all correspond to the areas of small rectangles in the area model.
- Summing these intermediate products corresponds to finding the area of the large rectangle by adding the areas of the small rectangles.

## Multiplying Two-Digit Numbers Given a Decomposition

**Example:** Ann wishes to find the value of $36\times 19$. She starts by writing both factors in expanded form.

$36 = {\color{blue}{30}}+{\color{blue}{6}},\qquad 19 = {\color{red}{10}}+{\color{red}{9}}$

Then, she proceeds as follows:

$30 \cdot 10|= [math]\phantom{000}[/math]; 30 \cdot 9|= [math]\phantom{000}[/math]; 6 \cdot 10|= [math]\phantom{10}[/math]; 6 \cdot 9|= [math]\phantom{10}[/math]$

Find the missing numbers, and use your answers to find the value of $36\times 19$.

**Explanation**

We can compute $36\times 19$ by filling in the missing numbers and adding the results. So, let's find the missing values:

$$
\begin{aligned}
30 \cdot 10 &= [math]\,300\,[/math] \\
30 \cdot 9 &= [math]\,270\,[/math] \\
6 \cdot 10 &= [math]\,60\,[/math] \\
6 \cdot 9 &= [math]\,54\,[/math]
\end{aligned}
$$

Let's now add these results:

$$
1; 3\begin{vmatrix}0 & 0 \\ + & 2\end{vmatrix}7\begin{vmatrix}0 \\ +\end{vmatrix}6\begin{vmatrix}0 \\ +\end{vmatrix}5\begin{vmatrix}4 \\ 6\end{vmatrix}8 \mid 4
$$

Therefore,

$$
36\times 19 = 684
$$

## Multiplying Two-Digit Numbers

**Example:** What is the value of $83 \times 47$?

**Explanation**

First, let's write our factors in expanded form:

$$
\begin{aligned}
83 &= 80 + 3, 47 = 40 + 7
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we multiply *both* numbers in the first factor's expanded form by *both* numbers in the second factor's expanded form:

- First, we multiply ${\color{blue}{80}}$ by ${\color{red}{40}}$ and ${\color{red}{7}}$:
$80 \cdot 40|= 3, 200; 80 \cdot 7|= 560$
- Then, we multiply ${\color{blue}{3}}$ by ${\color{red}{40}}$ and ${\color{red}{7}}$:
$3 \cdot 40|= 120; 3 \cdot 7|= 21$

Let's now add all our results:

$$
1; 3\begin{vmatrix}2 & 0 & 0 \\ + & 5 & 6\end{vmatrix}0; +\begin{vmatrix}1 & 2 & 0 \\ + & 2 & 1 \\ 3 & 9 & 0\end{vmatrix}1
$$

Therefore,

$$
83 \times 47 = 3,901
$$

## Multiplying Two-Digit Numbers: Word Problems

**Example:** A maximum of $68$ people can enter a particular gym every hour. What is the maximum number of people that can enter the gym over a $36$-hour period?

**Explanation**

To determine the maximum number of people, we need to compute the value of

$$
68 \times 36
$$

First, let's write our factors in expanded form:

$$
\begin{aligned}
68 &= 60 + 8, 36 = 30 + 6
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we multiply *both* numbers in the first factor's expanded form by *both* numbers in the second factor's expanded form:

- First, we multiply ${\color{blue}{60}}$ by ${\color{red}{30}}$ and ${\color{red}{6}}$:
$60 \cdot 30|= 1, 800; 60 \cdot 6|= 360$
- Then, we multiply ${\color{blue}{8}}$ by ${\color{red}{30}}$ and ${\color{red}{6}}$:
$8 \cdot 30|= 240; 8 \cdot 6|= 48$

Let's now add all our results:

$$
1\begin{vmatrix}1 \\ 1\end{vmatrix}8\begin{vmatrix}0 & 0 \\ + & 3\end{vmatrix}6\begin{vmatrix}0 \\ +\end{vmatrix}2\begin{vmatrix}4 & 0 \\ + & 4\end{vmatrix}8; 2\begin{vmatrix}4 & 4\end{vmatrix}8
$$

Therefore, the maximum number of people that can enter the gym is $2,448$.
