## Introduction

Let's use an area model to solve the following multiplication problem:

$$
{43} \times 4
$$

The number $43$ in expanded form is

$$
{\color{blue}{43}} = {\color{blue}40}+{\color{blue}3}
$$

So, we draw an area model, place ${\color{blue}40}$ and ${\color{blue}3}$ above the left and right rectangles, and ${\color{red}4}$ to the left of the big rectangle.

![](<../Source/4.3.3. Two-Digit by One-Digit Multiplication Using Place Value/Images/0129055de11a9bfb08d5e765487bfbed.png>)

Next, we compute the areas of the blue and pink rectangles. This gives the following picture:

![](<../Source/4.3.3. Two-Digit by One-Digit Multiplication Using Place Value/Images/7ddceea16a36eeb6c914fdf59992d3d8.png>)

The area of the big rectangle is

$$
160+12 = 172
$$

Therefore, we conclude that

$$
{43} \times 4 = 172
$$

We'll now describe a similar process that allows us to carry out this multiplication *without* using models.

<a id="multiplication-without-models"></a>
## Multiplication Without Models

Let's once again consider the following multiplication problem:

$$
{43} \times {\color{red}{4}}
$$

To evaluate this product without models, we follow a three-step process:

- First, we write our two-digit number $(43)$ in expanded form:
$43 = {\color{blue}{40}} + {\color{blue}{3}}$
- Then, we multiply each of the numbers from the expanded form by ${\color{red}{4}}$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
40 \cdot 4 &= 160 \\
3 \cdot 4 &= 12
\end{aligned}
$$

- Finally, we add the results:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}1 & 6 & 0 \\ + & 1 & 2 \\ 1 & 7 & 2\end{bmatrix}
$$

So, we conclude that

$$
43 \times 4 = 172
$$

## Multiplying a Two-Digit Number by a One-Digit Number Given a Decomposition

**Example:** Kyle wishes to find the value of $8\times 62$. He proceeds as follows:

$8 \cdot 60|= [math]\phantom{000}[/math]; 8 \cdot 2|= [math]\phantom{00}[/math]$

Find the missing numbers, and use your answers to find the value of $8\times 62$.

**Explanation**

Notice that $62$ in expanded form is

$$
62 = 60+2
$$

So, we can compute $8\times 62$ by filling in the missing numbers and adding the results.

First, let's find the missing values:

$$
\begin{aligned}
8 \cdot 60 &= [math]\,480\,[/math] \\
8 \cdot 2 &= [math]\,16\,[/math]
\end{aligned}
$$

Let's now add these results:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}4 & 8 & 0 \\ + & 1 & 6 \\ 4 & 9 & 6\end{bmatrix}
$$

Therefore,

$$
8 \times 62 = 496
$$

## Multiplying a Two-Digit Number by a One-Digit Number

**Example:** What is the value of $59 \times 5$?

**Explanation**

First, let's write the two-digit number $(59)$ in expanded form:

$$
59 = {\color{blue}{50}} + {\color{blue}{9}}
$$

We now multiply both numbers in our expanded form by ${\color{red}{5}}$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
50 \cdot 5 &= 250 \\
9 \cdot 5 &= 45
\end{aligned}
$$

Let's now add these results:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}2 & 5 & 0 \\ + & 4 & 5 \\ 2 & 9 & 5\end{bmatrix}
$$

Therefore,

$$
59 \times 5 = 295
$$

## Multiplying a Two-Digit Number by a One-Digit Number: Word Problems

**Example:** A hospital has $5$ floors. On each floor, there are $96$ beds. How many beds does the hospital have in total?

**Explanation**

To determine the total number of beds in the hospital, we need to compute the value of

$$
{\color{red}{5}} \times 96
$$

First, let's write the two-digit number $(96)$ in expanded form:

$$
96 = {\color{blue}{90}} + {\color{blue}{6}}
$$

We now multiply both numbers in our expanded form by ${\color{red}{5}}$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
5 \cdot 90 &= 450 \\
5 \cdot 6 &= 30
\end{aligned}
$$

Let's now add these results:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}4 & 5 & 0 \\ + & 3 & 0 \\ 4 & 8 & 0\end{bmatrix}
$$

Therefore, there are $480$ beds in the hospital.
