## Introduction

The result of a multiplication is called a **product**.

For example, when we multiply the numbers $2$ and $3$, the result is $6$. We say that the number $6$ is the **product** of $2$ and $3$. The numbers $2$ and $3$ are called **factors**.

![](<../Source/4.1.1. Products and Factors/Images/5c582316f0c5b12e483eec971ef95b4b.png>)

In general, a product is a result we get when we multiply two or more factors.

<a id="recognizing-products-and-factors"></a>
## Recognizing Products and Factors

**Example:** Identify the product and the factors of the equation $42 = 2 \times 3 \times 7$.

**Explanation**

![](<../Source/4.1.1. Products and Factors/Images/8d0decf176d9b72170a1ee962b7bfc0f.png>)

The result of a multiplication problem is called the *product*, while the numbers being multiplied are called the *factors*.

In this equation,

- $42$ is the result (the product), and
- $2$, $3$, and $7$ are the numbers being multiplied (the factors).

Therefore, "*the product is $42$, while $2, 3$, and $7$ are the factors.*"

<a id="identifying-missing-factors-two-factor-equations"></a>
## Identifying Missing Factors: Two-Factor Equations

**Example:** What factor is represented by the circle in the following equation?

$\bigcirc \times 7=28$

**Explanation**

We know that

$$
{\color{blue}{4}}\times 7 =28
$$

Therefore, the missing factor is ${\color{blue}{4}}$.

## Identifying Missing Factors: Three-Factor Equations

**Example:** What factor is represented by the empty box in the following equation?
$A$

**Explanation**

We know that

$$
2 \times 3 = 6
$$

Therefore, our equation can be simplified as
$A$

Furthermore, we know that

$$
24 = 6 \times {\color{blue}{4}}
$$

Therefore, the missing factor is ${\color{blue}{4}}$.
