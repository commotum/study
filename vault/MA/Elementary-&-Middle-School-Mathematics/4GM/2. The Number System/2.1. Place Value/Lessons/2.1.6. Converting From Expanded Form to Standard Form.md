## Introduction

A number is in **expanded form** when it's written as a sum of place values.

For example, the number
${\color{blue}{2}}00+{\color{red}{7}}0+{\color{magenta}{5}}$
is in expanded form because it is the same as
${\color{blue}{2}}\:\textrm{hundreds} + {\color{red}{7}}\:\textrm{tens} + {\color{magenta}{5}}\:\textrm{ones}$.

Similarly, the number
${\color{purple}{6}},000+{\color{red}{7}}0 + {\color{magenta}{6}}$
is also in expanded form because it is the same as
${\color{purple}{6}}\:\textrm{thousands} + {\color{blue}{0}}\:\textrm{hundreds} + {\color{red}{7}}\:\textrm{tens} + {\color{magenta}{6}}\:\textrm{ones}$.

However, the number

$$
600 + 21 + 2
$$

is *not* in expanded form because the second number $(21)$ contains *both* tens *and* ones.

<a id="identifying-numbers-written-in-expanded-form"></a>
## Identifying Numbers Written in Expanded Form

**Example:** Which of the following is the expanded form of a four-digit number?

1. $3,010 + 100 + 8$
2. $3,000 + 110 + 8$
3. $3,000 + 100 + 10 + 8$

**Explanation**

A four-digit whole number is in expanded form when it is written as follows:

$$
\textrm{thousands} +\textrm{hundreds} + \textrm{tens} + \textrm{ones}
$$

With that in mind, let's examine our numbers in turn.

- The number $3,010 + 100 + 8$ is *not* written in expanded form because the first term ($3,010$) is a mix of "thousands" and "tens."
- The number $3,000 + 110 + 8$ is *not* written in expanded form because the second term ($110$) is a mix of "hundreds" and "tens."

- The number $3,000 + 100 + 10 + 8$ is written in expanded form because
${\color{purple}{3}}000+{\color{blue}{1}}00+{\color{red}{1}}0+{\color{magenta}{8}} = {\color{purple}{3}}\:\textrm{thousands} + {\color{blue}{1}}\:\textrm{hundred} + {\color{red}{1}}\:\textrm{ten} + {\color{magenta}{8}}\:\textrm{ones}$.

Therefore, the correct answer is "III only."

## Identifying Numbers Containing Zeros Written in Expanded Form

**Example:** Which of the following is the expanded form of a three-digit number?

1. $100+1$
2. $110+1$
3. $100+10$

**Explanation**

A three-digit whole number is in expanded form when it is written as a sum of exactly three terms:

$$
\textrm{hundreds} + \textrm{tens} + \textrm{ones}
$$

With that in mind, let's examine our numbers in turn.

- The number $100+1$ is written in expanded form because
${\color{blue}{1}}00+{\color{red}{0}}0+{\color{magenta}{1}} = {\color{blue}{1}}\:\textrm{hundred} + {\color{red}{0}}\:\textrm{tens} + {\color{magenta}{1}}\:\textrm{one}$.
- The number $110+1$ is *not* written in expanded form because the first term ($110$) is a mix of "hundreds" and "tens."
- The number $100+10$ is written in expanded form because
${\color{blue}{1}}00+{\color{red}{1}}0+{\color{magenta}{0}} = {\color{blue}{1}}\:\textrm{hundred} + {\color{red}{1}}\:\textrm{ten} + {\color{magenta}{0}}\:\textrm{ones}$.

Therefore, the correct answer is "I and III only."

## Converting From Expanded Form to Standard Form

We've seen how to convert numbers from standard form to expanded form. We'll now learn how to reverse the process.

Let's convert the following expanded form to standard form:

$$
500 +40 +3
$$

First, we write each number as a place value:

- $500$ is $\color{blue}5$ hundreds
- $40$ is $\color{red}4$ tens
- $3$ is $\color{magenta}3$ ones

Putting these into a place value chart, we get the following:

| **hundreds** | **tens** | **ones** |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| $\color{blue}5$ | $\color{red}4$ | $\color{magenta}3$ |

Therefore, the given number (in standard form) is $543$.

<a id="converting-from-expanded-form-to-standard-form"></a>
## Converting From Expanded Form to Standard Form

**Example:** Which number is equivalent to the expanded form below?

$50,000 + 400 + 6$

**Explanation**

We need to convert the given number from expanded form to standard form. Notice that:

- $50,000$ is $5$ ten-thousands
- $400$ is $4$ hundreds
- $6$ is $6$ ones

Watch out! There are no thousands or tens, which means that we have

- $0$ thousands, and
- $0$ tens.

Putting this into the place value chart, we get the following:

| **Ten-Thousands** | **Thousands** | **Hundreds** | **Tens** | **Ones** |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $5$ | $0$ | $4$ | $0$ | $6$ |

Therefore, the resulting number is $50,406$.

## Determining Missing Place Values in a Given Expanded Form

**Example:** From left to right, what are the missing digits in the following equality?

$0$

**Explanation**

We need to convert the given number from expanded form to standard form. Notice that:

- $500,000$ is $5$ hundred-thousands
- $30,000$ is $3$ ten-thousands
- $6,000$ is $6$ thousands
- $400$ is $4$ hundreds
- $20$ is $2$ tens
- $6$ is $6$ ones

Putting this into a place value chart, we get the following:

| **Hundred-Thousands** | **Ten-Thousands** | **Thousands** | **Hundreds** | **Tens** | **Ones** |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $5$ | $3$ | $\color{blue}6$ | $4$ | $\color{blue}2$ | $6$ |

Therefore, the resulting number is

$$
6
$$

and the missing digits are $6$ and $2$.
