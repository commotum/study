## Introduction

A **dilation** of scale factor $k$ with the center at the origin is a linear transformation that multiplies the standard basis vectors by $k$. It is represented by the matrix
$\begin{bmatrix}k & 0 \\ 0 & k\end{bmatrix}$

For example, let $\begin{bmatrix}2 & 0 \\ 0 & 2\end{bmatrix}$ be the matrix of a linear transformation $\mathbf{T}$. This transformation is a dilation of scale factor $2$.

To visualize the dilation $\mathbf{T}$, consider the standard basis $\{\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j} \}$ of the Euclidean plane $\mathbb{R}^2$ and the unit square $\mathrm{R}$ spanned by the vectors, as shown below.

![](<../Source/14.5.5. Dilations and Reflections as Linear Transformations/Images/5b0f6bfa4dae673897b259120e1c15ca.png>)

We can find the images of $\{\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j} \}$ under $T$, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
i^{′} &= Ti = \begin{bmatrix}2 & 0 \\ 0 & 2\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 0\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}2 \\ 0\end{bmatrix} = 2i, \\
j^{′} &= Tj = \begin{bmatrix}2 & 0 \\ 0 & 2\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}0 \\ 1\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}0 \\ 2\end{bmatrix} = 2j
\end{aligned}
$$

The transformation $\mathbf{T}$ multiplies vectors $\mathbf{i}$ and $\mathbf{j}$ by $2$. The resulting vectors span a larger region, shown below.

![](<../Source/14.5.5. Dilations and Reflections as Linear Transformations/Images/bfd820a131398a4fd763b3f59745fa19.png>)

## Finding the Standard Matrix of a Dilation Given a Vector and its Image

**Example:** A linear transformation $\mathbf{T}$ represents a dilation. Given that $\begin{bmatrix}24 \\ 16\end{bmatrix}$ and $\begin{bmatrix}6 \\ 4\end{bmatrix}$ what is the standard matrix of $\mathbf{T}$?

**Explanation**

We can write $\mathbf{T}(\mathbf{v})$ as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
T(v) &= \begin{bmatrix}6 \\ 4\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \frac{1}{4} \cdot \begin{bmatrix}24 \\ 16\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \frac{1}{4}v
\end{aligned}
$$

Since we have

$$
\mathbf{T}(\mathbf{v}) = \dfrac{1}{4}\mathbf{v}
$$

we conclude that the linear transformation $\mathbf T$ is a dilation with scale factor

$$
k = \dfrac{1}{4}
$$

Remember that the matrix representing a dilation of scale factor $k$ is given by
$\begin{bmatrix}k & 0 \\ 0 & k\end{bmatrix}$

Therefore, the standard matrix of $\mathbf{T}$ is

$$
\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{4} & 0 \\ 0 & \frac{1}{4}\end{bmatrix}
$$

## Reflection in the X and Y Axes

A **reflection in the $x$-axis** is represented by the matrix
$\begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1\end{bmatrix}$

To visualize a reflection in the $x$-axis, consider the standard basis $\{\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j} \}$ of the Euclidean plane $\mathbb{R}^2$ and the unit square $\mathrm{R}$ spanned by the vectors, as shown below.

![](<../Source/14.5.5. Dilations and Reflections as Linear Transformations/Images/127980a6f2bafea3cac09f1dd2b27826.png>)

We can find the images of $\{\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j} \}$ under $T$, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
i^{′} &= T(i) = \begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 0\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 0\end{bmatrix} = i \\
j^{′} &= T(j) = \begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}0 \\ 1\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}0 \\ -1\end{bmatrix} =-j
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we obtain the following image:

![](<../Source/14.5.5. Dilations and Reflections as Linear Transformations/Images/dba5538cd9c0e4c8d1610753931726b2.png>)

Similarly, a **reflection in the $y$-axis** is represented by the matrix
$\begin{bmatrix}-1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1\end{bmatrix}$

Again, we can find the images of $\{\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j} \}$ under $T$, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
i^{′} &= T(i) = \begin{bmatrix}-1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 0\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}-1 \\ 0\end{bmatrix} =-i \\
j^{′} &= T(j) = \begin{bmatrix}-1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}0 \\ 1\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}0 \\ 1\end{bmatrix} = j
\end{aligned}
$$

We obtain the following image:

![](<../Source/14.5.5. Dilations and Reflections as Linear Transformations/Images/e12c26abe02ac39773cc624fc17d8d23.png>)

## Reflection in the Lines y=x and y=-x

A **reflection in line $y=x$** is represented by the matrix
$\begin{bmatrix}0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0\end{bmatrix}$

We can find the images of $\{\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j} \}$ under $T$, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
i^{′} &= T(i) = \begin{bmatrix}0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 0\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}0 \\ 1\end{bmatrix} = j \\
j^{′} &= T(j) = \begin{bmatrix}0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}0 \\ 1\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 0\end{bmatrix} = i
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we obtain the following image:

![](<../Source/14.5.5. Dilations and Reflections as Linear Transformations/Images/6e63aa4a66fa04484294a88d744c0c31.png>)

Similarly, a **reflection in line $y=-x$** is represented by the matrix
$\begin{bmatrix}0 & -1 \\ -1 & 0\end{bmatrix}$

We can find the images of $\{\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j} \}$ under $T$, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
i^{′} &= T(i) = \begin{bmatrix}0 & -1 \\ -1 & 0\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 0\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}0 \\ -1\end{bmatrix} =-j \\
j^{′} &= T(j) = \begin{bmatrix}0 & -1 \\ -1 & 0\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}0 \\ 1\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}-1 \\ 0\end{bmatrix} =-i

$$

Then, we obtain the following image:

## Identifying the Linear Transformation Represented by a Matrix

**Example:** What linear transformation is represented by the matrix
$\begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1\end{bmatrix}$

**Explanation**

Consider the standard basis $\{\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j} \}$ of the Euclidean plane $\mathbb{R}^2$ and the unit square spanned by the vectors, as depicted below.

![](<../Source/14.5.5. Dilations and Reflections as Linear Transformations/Images/a075449f9d98c2f5b4e14b915f2365f2.png>)

We can find the images of $\{\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j} \}$ under $T$, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
i^{′} &= T(i) = \begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 0\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 0\end{bmatrix} = i \\
j^{′} &= T(j) = \begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 \\ 0 & -1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}0 \\ 1\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}0 \\ -1\end{bmatrix} =-j
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we obtain the following image:

![](<../Source/14.5.5. Dilations and Reflections as Linear Transformations/Images/45b1b054a2d140d9a815f9b4816138e7.png>)

Therefore, the matrix represents the reflection across the $x$-axis.
