## Introduction

Consider the linear transformation $\mathbf{T}$ whose matrix $T$ is given by
$\begin{bmatrix}2 & 4 \\ 4 & 10\end{bmatrix}$

Suppose we know that some vector $\mathbf{v}$ in the plane is mapped to the vector $\mathbf w$ under the action of $\mathbf T$, where the vector $\mathbf w$ is given by

$$
\begin{bmatrix}2 \\ 6\end{bmatrix}
$$

The mapping

$$
\mathbf T(\mathbf v) = \mathbf w
$$

is shown schematically in the diagram below.

![](<../Source/14.5.10. Inverting Linear Transformations/Images/f24a77539fba8b74974971cb346eb81b.png>)

How can we find the original vector $\mathbf v$?

To find the vector $\mathbf v$, we compute the inverse of the matrix $T$ and then use it to reverse the action of $\mathbf T$. Let's see how this works.

From the given information, we have

$$
\begin{bmatrix}2 \\ 6\end{bmatrix}
$$

If we pre-multiply the above equation by $T^{-1}$, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
T^{-1} \cdot T \cdot v &= T^{-1} \cdot \begin{bmatrix}2 \\ 6\end{bmatrix} \\
I_{2} \cdot v &= T^{-1} \cdot \begin{bmatrix}2 \\ 6\end{bmatrix} \\
v &= T^{-1} \cdot \begin{bmatrix}2 \\ 6\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

This equation tells that to find the vector $\mathbf v$, we need to pre-multiply the vector $\mathbf w$ by the inverse of $T$.

So, we now calculate the inverse of $T$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
T^{-1} &= \frac{1}{20 - 16}\begin{bmatrix}10 & -4 \\ -4 & 2\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \frac{1}{4}\begin{bmatrix}10 & -4 \\ -4 & 2\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, we can calculate the original vector $\mathbf v$ as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
v &= T^{-1} \cdot \begin{bmatrix}2 \\ 6\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \frac{1}{4}\begin{bmatrix}10 & -4 \\ -4 & 2\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}2 \\ 6\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \frac{1}{4}\begin{bmatrix}-4 \\ 4\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}-1 \\ 1\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

We can easily check that this is the correct answer by computing $T\cdot \mathbf v$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
T \cdot v &= \begin{bmatrix}2 & 4 \\ 4 & 10\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}-1 \\ 1\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}2 \\ 6\end{bmatrix}✓
\end{aligned}
$$

We summarize the process we just completed in the following diagram.

![](<../Source/14.5.10. Inverting Linear Transformations/Images/d073e5286a6fd1e3c465a7adfe77f1b4.png>)

In general, if $T$ is a $2\times 2$ matrix that represents a linear transformation, then $T^{-1}$ is the inverse linear transformation and, if it exists, it reverses the action of $T$. So for any vector $\mathbf{v}$ on the plane, we have

$$
T^{-1}\cdot T\cdot \mathbf{v} = I_2 \cdot \mathbf{v} = \mathbf{v}
$$

## Calculating a Vector Given Its Image Under a Linear Transformation

**Example:** The vector $\mathbf{v}$ in the plane is mapped to the vector $\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 4\end{bmatrix}$ under the action of the linear transformation $\mathbf{T}$ whose matrix $T$ is given by
$\begin{bmatrix}5 & -2 \\ 10 & -3\end{bmatrix}$
What is the original vector $\mathbf{v}$?

**Explanation**

From the given information, we have

$$
\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 4\end{bmatrix}
$$

We need to reverse the action of the matrix $T$. So, we multiply the above equation by $T^{-1}$, and we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
T^{-1} \cdot T \cdot v &= T^{-1} \cdot \begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 4\end{bmatrix} \\
v &= T^{-1} \cdot \begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 4\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we need to calculate the inverse of $T$, which is

$$
\begin{aligned}
T^{-1} &= (1)/(-15 - (-20))\begin{bmatrix}-3 & 2 \\ -10 & 5\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \frac{1}{5}\begin{bmatrix}-3 & 2 \\ -10 & 5\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the original vector is

$$
\begin{aligned}
v &= T^{-1} \cdot \begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 4\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \frac{1}{5}\begin{bmatrix}-3 & 2 \\ -10 & 5\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 4\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \frac{1}{5}\begin{bmatrix}5 \\ 10\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}1 \\ 2\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Calculating the Vertices of a Polygon Given Its Image Under a Linear Transformation

**Example:** Consider the linear transformation $\mathbf T$ with matrix representation $T$, given by
$\begin{bmatrix}4 & -1 \\ 3 & 1\end{bmatrix}$

The triangle $S$ is mapped to the triangle $S'$ under the action of $\mathbf T$. The triangle $S'$ has vertices $(4,3),(4,10)$, and $(-4,-3)$. Which of the following are vertices of $S$?

1. $(1,0)$
2. $(4,-3)$
3. $(-1,0)$

**Explanation**

First, we create a matrix $Y$ containing all of the vertices of $S'$:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}4 & 4 & -4 \\ 3 & 10 & -3\end{bmatrix}
$$

Now, if $X$ denotes the matrix that contains the corresponding vertices of $S$, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
T \cdot X &= Y \\
T \cdot X &= \begin{bmatrix}4 & 4 & -4 \\ 3 & 10 & -3\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

We need to reverse the action of the matrix $T$. So, we multiply the above equation by $T^{-1}$, and we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
T^{-1} \cdot T \cdot X &= T^{-1} \cdot \begin{bmatrix}4 & 4 & -4 \\ 3 & 10 & -3\end{bmatrix} \\
X &= T^{-1} \cdot \begin{bmatrix}4 & 4 & -4 \\ 3 & 10 & -3\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

The inverse of $T$ is

$$
\begin{aligned}
T^{-1} &= (1)/(4 - (-3))\begin{bmatrix}1 & 1 \\ -3 & 4\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \frac{1}{7}\begin{bmatrix}1 & 1 \\ -3 & 4\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
X &= T^{-1} \cdot \begin{bmatrix}4 & 4 & -4 \\ 3 & 10 & -3\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \frac{1}{7}\begin{bmatrix}1 & 1 \\ -3 & 4\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}4 & 4 & -4 \\ 3 & 10 & -3\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \frac{1}{7}\begin{bmatrix}7 & 14 & -7 \\ 0 & 28 & 0\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}1 & 2 & -1 \\ 0 & 4 & 0\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

As a result, the coordinates of the vertices of the original triangle are $(1,0),(2,4),(-1,0)$.

Therefore, the correct answer is "I and III only."
