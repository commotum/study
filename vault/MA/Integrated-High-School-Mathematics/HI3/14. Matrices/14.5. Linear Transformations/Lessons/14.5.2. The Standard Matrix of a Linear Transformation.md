## Introduction

Every linear transformation $\mathbf{T}$ that acts on 2D-vectors has a $2 \times 2$ matrix $T$ associated with it. The matrix is called the **standard matrix** of the linear transformation.
For any vector $\mathbf{v}$, the standard matrix $T$ of a linear transformation $\mathbf{T}$ must have the property

$$
\mathbf{T}(\mathbf{v}) = T \cdot \mathbf{v}
$$

where $T \cdot \mathbf{v}$ is the matrix product of the standard matrix $T$ and the vector $\mathbf{v}$.

To illustrate, consider the following linear transformation:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}x \\ y\end{bmatrix}
$$

The standard matrix for the linear transformation $\mathbf{S}$ is $\begin{bmatrix}-3 & 7 \\ 1 & -5\end{bmatrix}$ since it has the following property:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\begin{bmatrix}-3 & 7 \\ 1 & -5\end{bmatrix}^(⏞)^(S) \cdot \begin{bmatrix}x \\ y\end{bmatrix} &= \begin{bmatrix}-3 \cdot x + 7 \cdot y \\ 1 \cdot x + (-5) \cdot y\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}-3x + 7y \\ x - 5y\end{bmatrix} \\
&= S\begin{bmatrix}x \\ y\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Notice here that $\mathbf{S}$ (using a **bold** letter) denotes the transformation (function) itself, while $S$ (big *italic* letter) is a $2 \times 2$ matrix that corresponds to the linear transformation.

In general, the standard matrix of the linear transformation $\begin{bmatrix}x \\ y\end{bmatrix}$ is
$\begin{bmatrix}a & b \\ c & d\end{bmatrix}$

We can learn a lot about linear transformations by looking at their standard matrices.

## Identifying the Standard Matrix of a Linear Transformation

**Example:** Find the standard matrix that represents the linear transformation $\mathbf{T}$, where
$\begin{bmatrix}x \\ y\end{bmatrix}$

**Explanation**

We require a $2\times 2$ matrix ${T}$ such that
$\begin{bmatrix}x \\ y\end{bmatrix}$
Notice that
$\begin{bmatrix}1 & 3 \\ 2 & -1\end{bmatrix}$

Therefore, the matrix which represents the linear transformation $\mathbf{T}$ is
$\begin{bmatrix}1 & 3 \\ 2 & -1\end{bmatrix}$

## Determining a Linear Transformation Given its Standard Matrix

**Example:** What is the linear transformation $\mathbf{T}$ whose standard matrix is $\begin{bmatrix}8 & -1 \\ -5 & -2\end{bmatrix}$

**Explanation**

Consider a general vector $\begin{bmatrix}x \\ y\end{bmatrix}$ Multiplying by the matrix $T$, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
T \cdot \begin{bmatrix}x \\ y\end{bmatrix} &= \begin{bmatrix}8 & -1 \\ -5 & -2\end{bmatrix} \cdot \begin{bmatrix}x \\ y\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}8x - y \\ -5x - 2y\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the linear transformation $\,\mathbf{T}\,$ is given by

$$
\begin{bmatrix}x \\ y\end{bmatrix}
$$

## Finding the Image of a Vector Under a Linear Transformation Given its Standard Matrix

**Example:** Consider the matrix $T$ and the vector $\mathbf{v}$ given below. If $T$ is the standard matrix of the linear transformation $\mathbf{T}$, find $\mathbf{T}(\mathbf{v})$.

$\begin{bmatrix}4 & 7 \\ -6 & -2\end{bmatrix}$

**Explanation**

Using the fact that

$$
\,\mathbf{T}(\mathbf{v})=T\cdot \mathbf{v}
$$

we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
T(v) &= T \cdot v \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}4 & 7 \\ -6 & -2\end{bmatrix} \cdot \begin{bmatrix}-2 \\ 5\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}27 \\ 2\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Identifying the Standard Matrix of a Linear Transformation With Rearranging

**Example:** Find the standard matrix that represents the linear transformation $\mathbf{T}$, where
$\begin{bmatrix}x \\ y\end{bmatrix}$

**Explanation**

First, rewrite the order of the variables in the usual way:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}x \\ y\end{bmatrix}
$$

We require a $2\times 2$ matrix ${T}$ such that
$\begin{bmatrix}x \\ y\end{bmatrix}$

Notice that
$\begin{bmatrix}-5 & 4 \\ 3 & 1\end{bmatrix}$

Therefore, the matrix which represents the linear transformation $\mathbf{T}$ is
$\begin{bmatrix}-5 & 4 \\ 3 & 1\end{bmatrix}$
