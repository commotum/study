## Introduction

Consider the vector

$$
\mathbf{a}=\langle 2,-1,3 \rangle
$$

shown below.

![](<../Source/13.3.3. Calculating the Magnitude of Cartesian Vectors in 3D/Images/3309f41e2b21210b444cb4192ea7233c.png>)

To compute the magnitude (length) of $\mathbf{a}$, we can use the Pythagorean theorem in three dimensions:

$$
\begin{aligned}
a &= \sqrt{(2)^{2} + (-1)^{2} + (3)^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{4 + 1 + 9} \\
&= \sqrt{14}
\end{aligned}
$$

In general, given a vector
$\begin{bmatrix}a_{x} \\ a_{y} \\ a_{z}\end{bmatrix}$

the magnitude of the vector can always be computed as

$$
\mid \,\mathbf{a}\, \mid = \sqrt{a_x^2+a_y^2+a_z^2}
$$

**Note**

We sometimes use the notation $\mid \mid \mathbf a \mid \mid$ to denote the magnitude of a vector.

<a id="calculating-the-magnitude-of-a-vector-given-in-component-form"></a>
## Calculating the Magnitude of a Vector Given in Component Form

**Example:** Let $\mathbf{a}=\langle 2, -3, -7 \rangle$. Find $\mid \mathbf{a} \mid$.

**Explanation**

Using the formula, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mid a \mid &= \sqrt{a_{x}^{2} + a_{y}^{2} + a_{z}^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{2^{2} + (-3)^{2} + (-7)^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{4 + 9 + 49} \\
&= \sqrt{62}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Calculating a Unit Vector Parallel to a Given Vector

**Example:** Let $\begin{bmatrix}4 \\ -5 \\ 1\end{bmatrix}$ Find a unit vector that is parallel to $\mathbf{a}$.

**Explanation**

Remember that to find a unit vector that is parallel to a given vector, we need to divide the given vector by its length:

$$
\mathbf{u} = \dfrac{\mathbf{a}}{\mid \,\mathbf{a}\, \mid}
$$

Using the formula for the magnitude of a vector, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mid a \mid &= \sqrt{a_{x}^{2} + a_{y}^{2} + a_{z}^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{4^{2} + (-5)^{2} + 1^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{16 + 25 + 1} \\
&= \sqrt{42}
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we divide $\mathbf{a}$ by its length and get the following unit vector:

$$
\begin{aligned}
u &= \frac{a}{\mid a \mid} \\
&= \frac{1}{\sqrt{42}}a \\
&= \frac{1}{\sqrt{42}} \cdot \begin{bmatrix}4 \\ -5 \\ 1\end{bmatrix} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}4/\sqrt{42} \\ -5/\sqrt{42} \\ 1/\sqrt{42}\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Calculating the Magnitude of a Vector Given the Coordinates of its Endpoints

**Example:** Let $A(1,1,2)$ and $B(2,-2,3)$. Find $\mid \overrightarrow{AB} \mid$.

**Explanation**

We have

$$
OA^(→) = a = i + j + 2k,; OB^(→) = b = 2i - 2j + 3k
$$

Expressing $\overrightarrow{AB}$ as the difference of position vectors, we obtain:

$$
\begin{aligned}
AB^(→) &= OB^(→) - OA^(→) \\
&= b - a \\
&= (2i - 2j + 3k) - (i + j + 2k) \\
&= i - 3j + k
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, using the formula for the magnitude of a vector, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mid AB^(→) \mid &= \sqrt{1^{2} + (-3)^{2} + 1^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{1 + 9 + 1} \\
&= \sqrt{11}
\end{aligned}
$$
