## Introduction

Let $\mathbf{a}$ be the vector shown below. Suppose we know that it makes the angle

$$
\theta=30^\circ
$$

with the positive $x$-axis and has magnitude

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mid \mathbf{a} &= 4
\end{aligned}
$$

How can we find the components of $\mathbf{a}$?

![](<../Source/13.2.5. Calculating the Components of Cartesian Vectors in 2D/Images/114f88c9cf6645f6f84944cde533b313.png>)

The trick is to use trigonometry to solve for the legs of the right triangle $\triangle OXA$. In this triangle, the hypotenuse is given by

$$
\mid \mathbf{a} \mid = OA
$$

and if

$$
\mathbf{a} = a_x \mathbf{i} + a_y \mathbf{j}
$$

then the legs of the triangle are

$$
a_x = OX, \qquad a_y = XA
$$

First, we compute for $a_x$ using cosine:

$$
\begin{aligned}
a_{x} &= OX \\
&= OA\cos θ \\
&= \mid a \mid \cos θ \\
&= 4 \cdot \cos 30^{∘} \\
&= 4 \cdot \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \\
&= 2\sqrt{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we compute for $a_y$ using sine:

$$
\begin{aligned}
a_{y} &= XA \\
&= OA\sin θ \\
&= \mid a \mid \sin θ \\
&= 4 \cdot \sin 30^{∘} \\
&= 4 \cdot \frac{1}{2} \\
&= 2
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
a &= a_{x}i + a_{y}j \\
&= 2\sqrt{3}i + 2j
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding the Magnitude of the Components of a Vector When Given Graphically

**Example:** The vector $\mathbf{a}$ makes the angle $\theta=147^\circ$ with the positive $x$-axis, as shown below. Find the length of $XO$ given that $\mid \mathbf{a}|=3$. Round your answer to $1$ decimal place.

![](<../Source/13.2.5. Calculating the Components of Cartesian Vectors in 2D/Images/1ec3503443c42ab54710e39907ab3e3f.png>)

**Explanation**

In $\bigtriangleup OXA$, the measure of $\angle{XOA}$ can be found as

$$
\begin{aligned}
m∠XOA &= 180^{∘} - θ \\
&= 180^{∘} - 147^{∘} \\
&= 33^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
XO &= OA\cos (m∠XOA) \\
&= \mid a \mid \cos (33^{∘}) \\
&\approx 3 \cdot 0.839 \\
&\approx 2.5
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="general-formula"></a>
## General Formula

If a vector $\mathbf{a}$ makes the angle $\theta$ with the positive $x$-axis then

$$
\begin{bmatrix}a_{x} = & a & \cos θ \\ a_{y} = & a & \sin θ\end{bmatrix}
$$

and

$$
\mathbf{a} = \langle a_x, a_y \rangle = \langle \begin{vmatrix}\mathbf{a} & \cos\theta, & \mathbf{a}\end{vmatrix} \sin\theta \rangle
$$

<a id="finding-the-components-of-a-vector-given-its-magnitude-and-direction"></a>
## Finding the Components of a Vector Given Its Magnitude and Direction

**Example:** Find the components of $\mathbf{a}$ if it makes an angle $\theta=-31^\circ$ with the positive $x$-axis and $\mid \mathbf{a}|=12$. Round your answer to $1$ decimal place where appropriate.

**Explanation**

Note that our vector lies in the $4$th quadrant. Using basic trigonometry, we obtain:

$$
\begin{aligned}
a_{x} &= \mid a\begin{vmatrix}\cos θ \\ = 12\cos (-31^{∘}) \\ \approx 12 \cdot 0.857 \\ \approx 10.3 \\ a_{y}|=\end{vmatrix}a \mid \sin θ \\
&= 12\sin (-31^{∘}) \\
&\approx 12 \cdot (-0.515) \\
&\approx - 6.2
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, we get
$\begin{bmatrix}10.3 \\ -6.2\end{bmatrix}$

## Finding the Components of a Vector Given Its Magnitude and Angle Made With the y-Axis

**Example:** Find the components of $\mathbf{a}$ if it makes an angle $\theta=\dfrac{\pi}{3}$ with the *positive $y$-axis* and $\mid \mathbf{a}|=60$.

**Explanation**

Note that our vector lies in the $2$nd quadrant.

First, to find the angle that out vector makes with the positive $x$-axis, we add

$$
\dfrac{\pi}{2}
$$

$$
\theta = \dfrac{\pi}{3} + \dfrac{\pi}{2} = \dfrac{5\pi}{6}
$$

Using our formulas, we obtain:

$$
\begin{aligned}
a_{x} &= \mid a\begin{vmatrix}\cos θ \\ = 60\cos (\frac{5π}{6}) \\ = 60 \cdot (-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}) \\ =-30\sqrt{3} \\ a_{y}|=\end{vmatrix}a \mid \sin θ \\
&= 60\sin (\frac{5π}{6}) \\
&= 60 \cdot \frac{1}{2} \\
&= 30
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, we get
$\begin{bmatrix}-30\sqrt{3} \\ 30\end{bmatrix}$
