## Introduction

Consider the two vectors

$$
\mathbf{a}=\langle 1,-2, 2 \rangle
$$

and

$$
\mathbf{b}=\langle 3,0,-1 \rangle
$$

shown in the picture below.

![](<../Source/13.4.3. The Angle Between Two Vectors/Images/05a9116a219fa5c7e3a9cffa70876d2d.png>)

To find the angle $\theta$ between the vectors, we can rearrange the dot product formula:

$$
\begin{aligned}
a \cdot b &= \begin{vmatrix}a & \cdot & b\end{vmatrix}\cdot \cos θ \\
\cos θ &= \frac{a \cdot b}{\mid a \mid \mid b\begin{vmatrix}} \\ θ|= \arccos (\frac{a \cdot b}{\mid a \mid \mid b\end{vmatrix}})
\end{aligned}
$$

The dot product is given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
a \cdot b &= a_{x}b_{x} + a_{y}b_{y} + a_{z}b_{z} \\
&= 1 \cdot 3 + (-2) \cdot 0 + 2 \cdot (-1) \\
&= 1
\end{aligned}
$$

and the magnitudes of $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$ are

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mid a \mid &= \sqrt{a_{x}^{2} + a_{y}^{2} + a_{z}^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{1^{2} + (-2)^{2} + 2^{2}} \\
&= 3, \\
\mid b \mid &= \sqrt{b_{x}^{2} + b_{y}^{2} + b_{z}^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{3^{2} + 0^{2} + (-1)^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{10}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\theta = \arccos \Bigg(\dfrac{1}{3\sqrt{10}} \Bigg) \approx 83.9^\circ
$$

## Finding the Angle Between Two Vectors Given Their Components

**Example:** Find the angle between $\mathbf{a}=\langle 2,-1,1 \rangle$ and $\mathbf{b}=\langle 1,5,-2 \rangle$. Round your answer to $1$ decimal place.

**Explanation**

Rearranging the dot product formula, we have:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\cos θ &= \frac{a \cdot b}{\mid a \mid \mid b \mid} \\
&= \frac{a_{x}b_{x} + a_{y}b_{y} + a_{z}b_{z}}{\sqrt{a_{x}^{2} + a_{y}^{2} + a_{z}^{2}}\sqrt{b_{x}^{2} + b_{y}^{2} + b_{z}^{2}}} \\
&= (2 \cdot 1 + (-1) \cdot 5 + 1 \cdot (-2))/(\sqrt{2^{2} + (-1)^{2} + 1^{2}}\sqrt{1^{2} + 5^{2} + (-2)^{2}}) \\
&= \frac{-5}{\sqrt{6} \cdot \sqrt{30}} \\
&= \frac{-5}{\sqrt{6} \cdot \sqrt{6} \cdot \sqrt{5}} \\
&=-\frac{\sqrt{5}}{6}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\theta = \arccos \left(-\dfrac{\sqrt{5}}{6} \right) \approx 111.9^\circ
$$

## Finding the Angle Between Two Vectors Given Three Points

**Example:** Find $\angle BAC$ given the points $A(2,-1,-5)$, $B(-1,0,-4)$, and $C(5,1,1)$. Round your answer to $1$ decimal place.

**Explanation**

Note that the position vectors of the points $A$, $B$, $C$ are

$$
a = ⟨2,-1,-5⟩, b = ⟨ - 1, 0,-4⟩, c = ⟨5, 1, 1⟩
$$

The triangle $\triangle ABC$ is shown (schematically) in the diagram below, where we denote

$$
\angle BAC = \theta
$$

![](<../Source/13.4.3. The Angle Between Two Vectors/Images/3326328c02627ffa40dbcdf7cb16cc96.png>)

First, we find the components of $\overrightarrow{AB}$ and $\overrightarrow{AC}$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
AB^(→) &= b - a \\
&= ⟨ - 1, 0,-4⟩ - ⟨2,-1,-5⟩ \\
&= ⟨ - 3, 1, 1⟩ \\
AC^(→) &= c - a \\
&= ⟨5, 1, 1⟩ - ⟨2,-1,-5⟩ \\
&= ⟨3, 2, 6⟩
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, the cosine of the angle between $\overrightarrow{AB}$ and $\overrightarrow{AC}$ is

$$
\begin{aligned}
\cos θ &= (AB^(→) \cdot AC^(→))/(\mid AB^(→) \mid \mid AC^(→) \mid) \\
&= (-3 \cdot 3 + 1 \cdot 2 + 1 \cdot 6)/(\sqrt{(-3)^{2} + 1^{2} + 1^{2}}\sqrt{3^{2} + 2^{2} + 6^{2}}) \\
&= \frac{-1}{\sqrt{11}\sqrt{49}} \\
&=-\frac{1}{7\sqrt{11}}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\theta = \arccos \left(-\dfrac{1}{7\sqrt{11}} \right) \approx 92.5^\circ
$$

<a id="finding-the-angle-between-two-vectors-given-the-value-of-their-dot-product"></a>
## Finding the Angle Between Two Vectors Given the Value of Their Dot Product

**Example:** Given that $\mathbf{a}\cdot \mathbf{b}=-4$, find the angle between $\mathbf{a}=\langle t, \, -2 \rangle$ and $\mathbf{b}=\langle 6, \, t \rangle$. Round your answer to $1$ decimal place.

**Explanation**

First, since

$$
\mathbf{a}\cdot \mathbf{b}=-4
$$

, we can solve for $t$, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
a \cdot b &= -4 \\
t \cdot 6 + (-2) \cdot t &= -4 \\
6t - 2t &= -4 \\
4t &= -4 \\
t &= -1
\end{aligned}
$$

So,

$$
a = ⟨ - 1,-2⟩, b = ⟨6,-1⟩
$$

Now, the cosine of the angle between $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$ is

$$
\begin{aligned}
\cos θ &= \frac{a \cdot b}{\mid a \mid \mid b \mid} \\
&= \frac{a_{x}b_{x} + a_{y}b_{y}}{\sqrt{a_{x}^{2} + a_{y}^{2}}\sqrt{b_{x}^{2} + b_{y}^{2}}} \\
&= (-4)/(\sqrt{(-1)^{2} + (-2)^{2}} \cdot \sqrt{6^{2} + (-1)^{2}}) \\
&=-\frac{4}{\sqrt{5} \cdot \sqrt{37}} \\
&=-\frac{4}{\sqrt{185}}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\theta = \arccos \left(-\dfrac{4}{\sqrt{185}} \right) \approx 107.1^\circ
$$
