## Introduction

Remember that to find the volume of the parallelepiped spanned by the vectors $\mathbf{a}$, $\mathbf{b}$, and $\mathbf{c}$, we take the absolute value of the triple product:

$$
V = \mid \mathbf{a}\cdot (\mathbf{b} \times \mathbf{c}) \mid
$$

However, sometimes we want to compute the volume of the *tetrahedron* spanned by the same vectors, as shown in the diagram below.

![](<../Source/13.5.7. Finding Volumes of Tetrahedrons and Pyramids Using Vector Products/Images/116fa812c3e7d4af3c4b7f6e12224e13.png>)

To compute the volume of the tetrahedron, we can just take

$$
\dfrac{1}{6}
$$

of the volume of the corresponding parallelepiped. So, denoting the volume of the tetrahedron by $V_T$, we get

$$
V_T = \dfrac{1}{6} V = \dfrac{1}{6} \mid \mathbf{a}\cdot (\mathbf{b} \times \mathbf{c}) \mid
$$

<a id="calculating-the-volume-of-a-tetrahedron-spanned-by-three-vectors"></a>
## Calculating the Volume of a Tetrahedron Spanned by Three Vectors

**Example:** Calculate the volume of the tetrahedron spanned by $\mathbf{a}=\langle -1,-7,0 \rangle$, $\mathbf{b}=\langle -8,2,4 \rangle$, and $\mathbf{c}=\langle 2,-3,1 \rangle$.

**Explanation**

Remember that the volume of a tetrahedron is

$$
\dfrac{1}{6}
$$

the volume of the corresponding parallelepiped:

$$
V_{T}=\dfrac{1}{6}V = \dfrac{1}{6}\left|\mathbf{a}\cdot (\mathbf{b}\times\mathbf{c})\right|
$$

First, let's compute the triple product:

$$
\begin{aligned}
a \cdot (b \cdot c) &= \begin{vmatrix}-1 & -7 & 0 \\ -8 & 2 & 4 \\ 2 & -3 & 1\end{vmatrix} \\
&= (-1) \cdot\begin{vmatrix}2 & 4 \\ -3 & 1\end{vmatrix}- (-7) \cdot\begin{vmatrix}-8 & 4 \\ 2 & 1\end{vmatrix}+ 0 \cdot\begin{vmatrix}-8 & 2 \\ 2 & -3\end{vmatrix} \\
&= - (2 + 12) + 7(-8 - 8) + 0 \\
&=-14 - 112 \\
&=-126
\end{aligned}
$$

So the volume of the corresponding parallelepiped is

$$
V = \mid -126 \mid = 126
$$

Therefore, the volume of the tetrahedron is

$$
V_{T} = \dfrac{1}{6}V = \dfrac{1}{6} \cdot 126 = 21
$$

## The Volume of a Pyramid

Other times, we might want to compute the volume of the *pyramid* spanned by the vectors $\mathbf{a}$, $\mathbf{b}$, and $\mathbf{c}$, as shown in the diagram below (notice that vectors $\mathbf{b}$ and $\mathbf{c}$ span a parallelogram which is the base of our pyramid).

![](<../Source/13.5.7. Finding Volumes of Tetrahedrons and Pyramids Using Vector Products/Images/855be6b272a12398782ded8bb6761c1d.png>)

To compute the volume of the pyramid, we can just take

$$
\dfrac{1}{3}
$$

of the volume of the corresponding parallelepiped. So, denoting the volume of the pyramid by $V_P$, we get

$$
V_P = \dfrac{1}{3} V = \dfrac{1}{3} \mid \mathbf{a}\cdot (\mathbf{b} \times \mathbf{c}) \mid
$$

---

<a id="calculating-the-volume-of-a-pyramid-spanned-by-three-vectors"></a>
## Calculating the Volume of a Pyramid Spanned by Three Vectors

**Example:** The parallelogram $ABCD$ is the base of the pyramid $SABCD$. Calculate the volume of the pyramid if

$\overrightarrow{AS} = \langle 6, 1, 1 \rangle, \qquad \overrightarrow{AB} = \langle 0, 2, 0 \rangle, \qquad \overrightarrow{AD} = \langle 0, 0, 2 \rangle$.

**Explanation**

Remember that the volume of a pyramid is

$$
\dfrac{1}{3}
$$

the volume of the corresponding parallelepiped:

$$
V_{P}=\dfrac{1}{3}V = \dfrac{1}{3}\left| \overrightarrow{AS} \cdot (\overrightarrow{AB} \times \overrightarrow{AD})\right|
$$

First, let's compute the triple product:

$$
\begin{aligned}
AS^(→) \cdot (AB^(→) \cdot AD^(→)) &= \begin{vmatrix}6 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 2\end{vmatrix} \\
&= 6 \cdot\begin{vmatrix}2 & 0 \\ 0 & 2\end{vmatrix}- 1 \cdot\begin{vmatrix}0 & 0 \\ 0 & 2\end{vmatrix}+ 1 \cdot\begin{vmatrix}0 & 2 \\ 0 & 0\end{vmatrix} \\
&= 6(4 - 0) - (0 - 0) + (0 - 0) \\
&= 24 - 0 + 0 \\
&= 24
\end{aligned}
$$

So the volume of the corresponding parallelepiped is

$$
\begin{aligned}
V= \mid 24 &= 24
\end{aligned}
$$

and therefore, the volume of the pyramid is

$$
V_{P}=\dfrac{1}{3}V=\dfrac{1}{3}\cdot 24=8
$$

<a id="calculating-the-volume-of-a-tetrahedron-given-the-coordinates-of-the-vertices"></a>
## Calculating the Volume of a Tetrahedron Given the Coordinates of the Vertices

**Example:** Calculate the volume of the tetrahedron $ABCD$ given the points $A(-1,1,-2)$, $B(2,1,1)$, $C(2,2,1)$, and $D(1,0,-1)$.

**Explanation**

First, we find the vectors $\overrightarrow{AB}$, $\overrightarrow{AC}$, and $\overrightarrow{AD}$ that span the tetrahedron:

$$
\begin{aligned}
AB^(→) &= b - a \\
&= ⟨2, 1, 1⟩ - ⟨ - 1, 1,-2⟩ \\
&= ⟨3, 0, 3⟩ \\
AC^(→) &= c - a \\
&= ⟨2, 2, 1⟩ - ⟨ - 1, 1,-2⟩ \\
&= ⟨3, 1, 3⟩ \\
AD^(→) &= d - a \\
&= ⟨1, 0,-1⟩ - ⟨ - 1, 1,-2⟩ \\
&= ⟨2,-1, 1⟩
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, remember that the volume of a tetrahedron is

$$
\dfrac{1}{6}
$$

the volume of the corresponding parallelepiped:

$$
V_T = \dfrac{1}{6}V = \dfrac{1}{6} \mid \overrightarrow{AB} \cdot (\overrightarrow{AC} \times \overrightarrow{AD}) \mid
$$

Let's compute the triple product:

$$
\begin{aligned}
AB^(→) \cdot (AC^(→) \cdot AD^(→)) &= \begin{vmatrix}3 & 0 & 3 \\ 3 & 1 & 3 \\ 2 & -1 & 1\end{vmatrix} \\
&= 3 \cdot\begin{vmatrix}1 & 3 \\ -1 & 1\end{vmatrix}- 0 \cdot\begin{vmatrix}3 & 3 \\ 2 & 1\end{vmatrix}+ 3 \cdot\begin{vmatrix}3 & 1 \\ 2 & -1\end{vmatrix} \\
&= 3(1 + 3) - 0 + 3(-3 - 2) \\
&= 12 - 0 - 15 \\
&=-3
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the volume of the tetrahedron is

$$
V_T = \dfrac{1}{6} \cdot \mid {-3} \mid = \dfrac{1}{6} \cdot 3 = \dfrac{1}{2}
$$
