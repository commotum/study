## Introduction

A **quadratic inequality** is an inequality that involves a quadratic expression. We can often solve quadratic inequalities using a graphical method.

For example, suppose we want to find the solution to the quadratic inequality

$$
x^2+2x-3 \geq 0
$$

Let's consider the graph of the corresponding parabola

$$
y=x^2+2x-3
$$

shown below.

![](<../Source/5.1.2. Solving Quadratic Inequalities From Graphs/Images/347570866a30daee9b3485d6aa75860b.png>)

Our inequality is a "*greater than or equal to*" inequality. Therefore, we need to find the values of $x$ for which the parabola is *above* the $x$-axis or *touches* this axis

Using the graph above, we notice that our parabola has roots at $x=-3$ and $x=1$. The parabola is above the $x$-axis to the left of $x=-3$ and the right of $x=1$, as shown below.

![](<../Source/5.1.2. Solving Quadratic Inequalities From Graphs/Images/1d89c18e5862331348baf7a4b8c1ed16.png>)

Therefore

$$
y = x^2+2x-3 \geq 0
$$

when

$$
x \leq -3 \quad \text{or} \quad x \geq 1
$$

which is our solution.

**Important:** Since we have a "greater than *or equal to*" inequality, the roots of the parabola are *included* in the solution set.

## Solving a "Greater Than or Equal To" Inequality Given the Graph of the Corresponding Parabola

**Example:** Given the graph below, solve the inequality $(x+2)(1-x) \geq 0$.

![](<../Source/5.1.2. Solving Quadratic Inequalities From Graphs/Images/7f86e2d3365b5b2d8c7d4529af5e4b74.png>)

**Explanation**

Notice that our inequality

$$
(x+2)(1-x) \geq 0
$$

is a "*greater than or equal to*" inequality. Therefore, we need to find the values of $x$ for which the parabola

$$
y = (x+2)(1-x)
$$

is *above* the $x$-axis or *touches* this axis. We mark these values of $x$ in blue in the diagram below.

![](<../Source/5.1.2. Solving Quadratic Inequalities From Graphs/Images/210002d3cf31f38ff05cfb4d2c8facd8.png>)

So, the solution to the inequality is

$$
-2 \leq x \leq 1
$$

## Strict Inequalities

Suppose we want to find the solution to the quadratic inequality
$x^2+x-6 < 0$.

We first plot the graph of the corresponding parabola

$$
y=x^2+x-6
$$

shown below.

![](<../Source/5.1.2. Solving Quadratic Inequalities From Graphs/Images/13e601f52ad1c4eb14d1c0b194284441.png>)

Notice that our inequality is a "*less than*" ($<$) inequality. Therefore, we need to find the values of $x$ for which the parabola is *below* the $x$-axis.

The graph above shows that our parabola has roots at $x=-3$ and $x=2$. Moreover, the parabola lies below the $x$-axis for all values of $x$ that lie between $x=-3$ and $x=2$.

![](<../Source/5.1.2. Solving Quadratic Inequalities From Graphs/Images/70ea9357f1832f7d643f8f714ddff677.png>)

Therefore, $x^2+x-6 <0$ when
$-3 < x < 2$,

which is our solution.

**Important:** Since our inequality is a "*less than*" inequality, the roots of the parabola are *not* included in the solution set.
