## Introduction

The following identities are called the **cofunction identities**. We can split them into three main groups.

- Sine and **co**sine:
$\sin x|= \cos (\frac{π}{2} - x); \cos x|= \sin (\frac{π}{2} - x)$
- Tangent and **co**tangent:
$\tan x|= \cot (\frac{π}{2} - x); \cot x|= \tan (\frac{π}{2} - x)$

- Secant and **co**secant:
$\sec x|= \csc (\frac{π}{2} - x); \csc x|= \sec (\frac{π}{2} - x)$

---

<a id="rewriting-trigonometric-ratios-using-cofunction-identities"></a>
## Rewriting Trigonometric Ratios Using Cofunction Identities

**Example:** Express $\cos{\left(\dfrac \pi 5\right)}$ using the corresponding cofunction.

**Explanation**

First, notice that the cofunction of $\cos x$ (**co**sine) is $\sin x$ (sine). The corresponding identity is given by

$$
\cos x = \sin \left(\dfrac{\pi}{2} - x \right)
$$

Substituting

$$
x=\dfrac{\pi}{5}
$$

into the above, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
\cos(\frac{π}{5}) &= \sin(\frac{π}{2} - \frac{π}{5}) \\
&= \sin (\frac{3π}{10})
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, we conclude that

$$
\cos{\left(\dfrac \pi 5\right)} = \sin\left(\dfrac {3 \pi} {10}\right)
$$

## Rewriting an Expression With a Nonstandard Argument Using Cofunction Identities

**Example:** Express $\sin \left(30^\circ- \theta \right)$ using the corresponding cofunction.

**Explanation**

First, we recall the cofunction identity (in degrees) for sine and cosine:

$$
\cos x = \sin \left(30^\circ - x \right)
$$

Substituting

$$
x=30^\circ - \theta
$$

into the above, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sin(30^{∘} - θ) &= \cos(90^{∘} - (30^{∘} - θ)) \\
&= \cos ((90^{∘} - 30^{∘}) + θ) \\
&= \cos (60^{∘} + θ)
\end{aligned}
$$

## Simplifying Trigonometric Expressions Using Cofunction Identities

**Example:** $1 - \cos \left(\dfrac \pi 2 - y \right) \sin y=$

**Explanation**

First, we recall the cofunction identity for sine and cosine:

$$
\cos \left(\dfrac{\pi}{2} - y \right) = \sin y
$$

Substituting this into our expression, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
1 - \cos(\frac{π}{2} - y)\sin y &= 1 - \siny\sin y \\
&= 1 - \sin^{2} y \\
&= \cos^{2} y
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="simplifying-expressions-using-multiple-cofunction-identities"></a>
## Simplifying Expressions Using Multiple Cofunction Identities

**Example:** $\tan \left(90^\circ - x \right) \cos x- \sec\left(90^\circ - x \right)=$

**Explanation**

First, we recall the cofunction identity for tangent and contangent:

$$
\tan\left(90^\circ - x \right) = \cot x
$$

We also recall the cofunction identity for secant and cosecant:

$$
\sec(90^\circ - x) = \csc x
$$

Substituting these identities into our expression and using the fact that

$$
\cot x = \dfrac{\cos x}{\sin x} \qquad \text{and} \qquad \csc x = \dfrac{1}{\sin x}
$$

we get the following:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\tan(90^{∘} - x)\cos x - \sec(90^{∘} - x) &= \cotx\cos x - \cscx \\
&= \frac{\cosx}{\sinx} \cdot \cos x - \frac{1}{\sinx} \\
&= \frac{\cos^{2} x - 1}{\sin x} \\
&= \frac{-\sin^{2} x}{\sin x} \\
&= \frac{-\sin x\sin x}{\sin x} \\
&=-\sin x
\end{aligned}
$$
