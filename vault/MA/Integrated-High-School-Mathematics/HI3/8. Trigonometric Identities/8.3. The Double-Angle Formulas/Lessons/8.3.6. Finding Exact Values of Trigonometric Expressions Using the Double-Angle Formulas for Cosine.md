## Introduction

If we are given that

$$
\cos{\theta} = \dfrac{4}{5}
$$

and

$$
\sin{\theta}=\dfrac{3}{5}
$$

then we can find the value of $\cos 2\theta$ using the double-angle formula for cosine:

$$
\cos{2\theta} = \cos^2\theta - \sin^2{\theta}
$$

Substituting

$$
\cos \theta= \dfrac 4 5
$$

and

$$
\sin \theta = \dfrac{3}{5}
$$

into the above, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
\cos 2θ &= (\frac{4}{5})^{2} - (\frac{3}{5})^{2} \\
&= \frac{16}{25} - \frac{9}{25} \\
&= \frac{7}{25}
\end{aligned}
$$

In fact, given only $\sin\theta$ or $\cos\theta$, we can always work out $\cos2\theta$ using an alternative form of the double-angle formula for cosine:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\cos 2θ &= 2\cos^{2} θ - 1 \\
\cos 2θ &= 1 - 2\sin^{2} θ
\end{aligned}
$$

Let's take a look at some more examples.

## Finding the Exact Value for the Cosine of a Double Angle Given the Cosine of the Angle

**Example:** Given $\cos{x}=-\dfrac{1}{5}$, find the exact value of $\cos{2x}$.

**Explanation**

We're given the value of $\cos{x}$, so we'll use the following double-angle formula for cosine to compute $\cos2x$:

$$
\cos{2x} = 2 \cos^2{x}-1
$$

Substituting

$$
\cos{x} = -\dfrac{1}{5}
$$

into the above, we get

$$
\eqalign {\cos 2x &= 2\cos^2 x-1 \[3pt] &= 2\left(-\dfrac{1}{5}\right)^2-1\[3pt] &=2 \cdot \dfrac{1}{25}-1 \[3pt] &=\dfrac{2}{25}-1 \[3pt] &=-\dfrac{23}{25}.}
$$

## Finding the Exact Value for the Cosine of a Double Angle Given the Sine of the Angle

**Example:** If $\sin x = -\dfrac{2}{\sqrt{6}}$, find the exact value of $\cos{2x}$.

**Explanation**

We're given the value of $\sin{x}$, so we'll use the following double-angle formula for cosine to compute $\cos2x$:

$$
\cos{2x} = 1 - 2 \sin^2{x}
$$

Substituting

$$
\sin{x} = -\dfrac{2}{\sqrt{6}}
$$

into the above, we get

$$
\eqalign {\cos 2{x} &= 1-2\sin^2{x} \[5pt] &= 1-2\left(-\dfrac{2}{\sqrt{6}}\right)^2\[5pt] &= 1-2\cdot\dfrac{4}{6}\[5pt] &= 1-\dfrac{4}{3}\[5pt] &= -\dfrac{1}{3}.}
$$

## Finding the Exact Value for the Cosine of a Double Angle Given the Secant or Cosecant of the Angle

**Example:** Given that $\sec{x} = \dfrac{\sqrt{6}}{2}$, find the exact value of $\cos{2x}$.

**Explanation**

First, notice that we can compute $\cos{x}$, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\cos x &= \frac{1}{\sec x} \\
&= (1)/((\frac{\sqrt{6}}{2})) \\
&= \frac{2}{\sqrt{6}} \\
&= \frac{\sqrt{6}}{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, since we know $\cos{x}$, we use the following double-angle formula for cosine to compute $\cos2x$:

$$
\cos{2x}= 2\cos^2{x}-1
$$

Substituting

$$
\cos x = \dfrac{\sqrt{6}}{3}
$$

into the above, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
\cos 2x &= 2\cos^{2} x - 1 \\
&= 2(\frac{\sqrt{6}}{3})^{2} - 1 \\
&= \frac{4}{3} - 1 \\
&= \frac{1}{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding the Exact Value for Cosine of a Double Angle Given a System of Equations

**Example:** If $\cos x + \sin x = \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{3}$ and $\cos x - \sin x = -\dfrac{4}{3}$, find the value of $\cos{2x}$.

**Explanation**

First, let's recall the double-angle formula for cosine:

$$
\cos{2x} = \cos^2{x} - \sin^2{x}
$$

We are given the following system of equations:

$$
{\cos x + \sin x = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{3}; \cos x - \sin x =-\frac{4}{3}
$$

Multiplying the two equations and using the double-angle formula for cosine, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
(\cos x + \sin x)(\cos x - \sin x) &= (\frac{\sqrt{2}}{3}) \cdot (-\frac{4}{3}) \\
\cos^{2} x - \sin^{2} x &= -\frac{4\sqrt{2}}{9} \\
\cos 2x &= -\frac{4\sqrt{2}}{9}
\end{aligned}
$$
