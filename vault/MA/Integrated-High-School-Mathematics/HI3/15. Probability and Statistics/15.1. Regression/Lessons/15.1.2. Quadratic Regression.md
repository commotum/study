## Introduction

Another type of regression sometimes encountered in statistics is **quadratic regression**. When a set of paired observations $(x,y)$ in a scatter plot appears to follow a parabola, the data can be approximated by a quadratic function.

The scatter plot below shows a relationship between two data sets of paired observations.

![](<../Source/15.1.2. Quadratic Regression/Images/7f26cbd223830310b5bc91fb8ea14cad.png>)

In this case, the paired observations seem to follow a parabola. Let's sketch our best fit quadratic regression curve:

![](<../Source/15.1.2. Quadratic Regression/Images/9f1daffaa491696daf6c32b5ab8776f2.png>)

Since the curve of best fit is parabolic, it must be of the form

$$
y = ax^2 + bx + c
$$

Let's now deduce some information about the parameters $a, b$, and $c$ from the graph:

- Since the parabola opens downward, we must have $a < 0$.
- The $y$-intercept of our parabola is negative. So, we must have $c \lt 0$.

## Identifying a Possible Quadratic Curve of Best Fit

**Example:** The scatter plot above shows a relationship between two data sets of paired observations. Which of the following could be the corresponding best fit quadratic regression curve?

![](<../Source/15.1.2. Quadratic Regression/Images/70d8f4c0351cac6874709b13158a576f.png>)

1. $y = -2.1x^2 + 0.7x - 1$
2. $y = -2.1x^2 + 0.7x + 1$
3. $y = 2.1x^2 + 0.7x + 1$
4. $y = 2.1x^2 - 0.7x - 1$

**Explanation**

Let's sketch our best fit quadratic regression curve:

![](<../Source/15.1.2. Quadratic Regression/Images/f0273f3b279bdb190809c6589f65a1a6.png>)

Recall that a general parabola has the equation

$$
y = ax^2 + bx + c
$$

Let's now examine our options in turn.

- We have a downward parabola. So, $a < 0$. As a result, we can reject the following options:
$y = 2.1x^2 + 0.7x + 1, \qquad y = 2.1x^2 - 0.7x - 1$
- The $y$-intercept of our parabola is positive. So, we must have $c \gt 0$. As a result, we also reject the following options:
$y = -2.1x^2 + 0.7x - 1$.

Therefore, the correct answer is

$$
y = -2.1x^2 + 0.7x + 1
$$

## Identifying a Possible Quadratic Curve of Best Fit Using the Vertex Formula

**Example:** The scatterplot shows the relationship between two variables, $x$ (time) and $y$ (revenue). Of the following equations, which best models the data in the scatterplot?

![](<../Source/15.1.2. Quadratic Regression/Images/7fa3e7a53cc0cef43da1ac854b9840a7.png>)

1. $y=-2.28x^{2}+20.2x+652.76$
2. $y=2.28x^{2}+20.2x+652.76$
3. $y=-2.28x^{2}-20.2x+652.76$
4. $y=-2.28x^{2}+20.2x-652.76$

**Explanation**

We want to find the best model for the scatterplot data shown, in the quadratic form

$$
y = ax^2+bx+c
$$

where $a$, $b$, and $c$ are constants, with

$$
a \neq 0
$$

- The data in the scatterplot appear to form a downward-opening parabola. Therefore, the leading coefficient $a$ must be negative.
- The points look symmetric about the line $x=4$. Using the axis of symmetry formula $x=-\dfrac{b}{2a}$ and solving for $b$, we get
$x|=-\frac{b}{2a}; 4|=-\frac{b}{2a}; b|=-8a$.
Since $a$ is negative, the coefficient of the linear term should be positive.
- The $y$-intercept of the quadratic model is near $(0,650)$.

Therefore, of the given choices, only choice

$$
y = -2.28x^{2} + 20.2x+652.76
$$

is the best quadratic model for the data in the scatterplot.

<a id="using-quadratic-regression-to-predict-the-output-for-a-given-observation"></a>
## Using Quadratic Regression To Predict the Output for a Given Observation

**Example:** The scatter plot above shows a relationship between the number of people in a particular cafeteria $(x)$ and the waiting time $(y)$, measured in minutes. The corresponding best fit quadratic model is given by
$y = \dfrac{1}{27} x^2+2$.
Estimate the waiting time if there are $9$ people in the cafeteria.

![](<../Source/15.1.2. Quadratic Regression/Images/686d06052703764f3fbb7ca62eeabd2a.png>)

**Explanation**

To predict the waiting time when $9$ people are in the cafeteria, we need to substitute $x=9$ into the equation of the trend curve:

$$
\begin{aligned}
y &= \frac{1}{27}x^{2} + 2 \\
&= \frac{1}{27}(9)^{2} + 2 \\
&= 3 + 2 \\
&= 5
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the model predicts that the waiting time equals $5$ minutes when $9$ people are in the cafeteria.

## Computing the Residual for a Data Point

**Example:** The scatter plot above shows a relationship between the concentration of a water pollutant ($x$, measured in $\mathrm{mg/mL}$) and the number of animal species $(y)$ that live in some ponds in a particular region. The corresponding quadratic regression curve is also shown.

![](<../Source/15.1.2. Quadratic Regression/Images/fd06c4fe1562287828520f3bcc1d162d.png>)

According to the regression model, what is the residual for the highlighted data point?

**Explanation**

The residual equals the difference between the actual value of the dependent variable and the value estimated using the regression model:

$$
\textrm{Residual} = \text{Actual} - \textrm{Estimated}
$$

The highlighted datapoint has coordinates $(80,60)$. So, our actual value is $y=60$.

On the other hand, the point on the regression curve that corresponds to $x=80$ has the $y$-coordinate $50$. So, our estimated value is

$$
\widehat{y}=50
$$

![](<../Source/15.1.2. Quadratic Regression/Images/72772881727d71723d66048747d78e3f.png>)

As a result,

$$
\begin{aligned}
Residual &= Actual - Estimated \\
&= y - y^(^) \\
&= 60 - 50 \\
&= 10
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the residual is $10$ animal species.
