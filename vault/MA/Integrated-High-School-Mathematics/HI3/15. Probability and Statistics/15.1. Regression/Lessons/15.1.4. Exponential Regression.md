## Introduction

Suppose there is an exponential relationship of the form

$$
y \approx a \cdot b^x
$$

between the variables $x$ and $y$, shown in the semi-log scatter plot below.

![](<../Source/15.1.4. Exponential Regression/Images/f98a3751586612016aa8f6b627f5144c.png>)

Suppose further that the semi-log scatter plot has the trend line

$$
Y = 4 + 0.3x
$$

where $Y = \log y$. Using only this information, it's possible to figure out the values of $a$ and $b$ in the exponential relationship

$$
y \approx a \cdot b^x
$$

The first step is to transform the exponential relationship into a linear form. We can do this by taking the logarithm of both sides of the equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
y &= a \cdot b^{x} \\
\log y &= \log (a \cdot b^{x}) \\
\log y &= \log a + \log b^{x} \\
\log y &= \log a + x \cdot \log b \\
Y &= A + Bx
\end{aligned}
$$

where

$$
Y=\log y,\qquad A=\log a,\qquad B=\log b
$$

Now, comparing

$$
Y = A + Bx
$$

with the equation

$$
Y = 4 + 0.3x
$$

we obtain

$$
A = 4, \qquad B = 0.3
$$

Finally, all that's left is to substitute the values above in the equations relating $A$ to $a$ and $B$ to $b$.

- Substituting $A=4$ into the equation relating $A$ to $a$, we get
$\log a|= A; \log a|= 4; a|= 10^{4}; a|= 10000$.
- Substituting $B=0.3$ into the equation relating $B$ to $b$, we get
$\log b|= B; \log b|= 0.3; b|= 10^{0.3}; b \mid \approx 2.00$,
rounded to two decimal places.

Therefore, we conclude that the approximate relationship between $x$ and $y$ is given by

$$
y\approx 10\,000 \cdot 2^x
$$

## Calculating Exponential Parameters from Semi-Log Scatter Plots and Trend Line Equations

**Example:** There is an exponential relationship of the form $y \approx a \cdot b^x$ between the variables $x$ and $y$. The corresponding semi-log scatter plot with the trend line $Y = 3 - 0.2x$ is shown above. Estimate the values of $a$ and $b$. Round your answers to $2$ decimal places where appropriate.

![](<../Source/15.1.4. Exponential Regression/Images/99e25b92d4b305584ced795c1c73d787.png>)

**Explanation**

Applying $\log$ to both sides of

$$
y = a \cdot b^x
$$

we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
y &= a \cdot b^{x} \\
\log y &= \log (a \cdot b^{x}) \\
\log y &= \log a + \log b^{x} \\
\log y &= \log a + x \cdot \log b \\
Y &= A + Bx
\end{aligned}
$$

where

$$
Y=\log y,\qquad A=\log a,\qquad B=\log b
$$

Comparing

$$
Y = A + Bx
$$

with the given equation

$$
Y = 3 - 0.2x
$$

we obtain

$$
A = 3, \qquad B = -0.2
$$

As a result, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= 3 \\
\log a &= 3 \\
a &= 10^{3} \\
a &= 1000
\end{aligned}
$$

Also,

$$
\begin{aligned}
B &= -0.2 \\
\log b &= -0.2 \\
b &= 10^{-0.2} \\
&\approx 0.63
\end{aligned}
$$

## Calculating Parameters by Finding the Slope or Y-Intercept of a Semi-Log Scatter Plot

**Example:** There is an exponential relationship of the form $y \approx a \cdot b^x$ between variables $x$ and $y$. The corresponding semi-log scatter plot with the associated trendline is shown above. Find the values of $a$ and $b$. Round your answer to $2$ decimal places where appropriate.

![](<../Source/15.1.4. Exponential Regression/Images/fa386c7595288c88852186bcc4b625fb.png>)

*Hint: The trend line passes close to the points $(3,4)$ and $(9,6)$.*

**Explanation**

To get the corresponding semi-log plot, we apply $\log$ to both sides of

$$
y = a \cdot b^x
$$

This gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
y &= a \cdot b^{x} \\
\log y &= \log (a \cdot b^{x}) \\
\log y &= \log a + \log b^{x} \\
\log y &= \log a + x \cdot \log b \\
Y &= A + Bx
\end{aligned}
$$

where

$$
Y=\log y, \qquad A=\log a, \qquad B=\log b
$$

Let's now consider an approximate trend line

$$
Y = A + Bx
$$

for our data:

![](<../Source/15.1.4. Exponential Regression/Images/32312fbdc16c10efeea613b7ef2f8f26.png>)

We now proceed to calculate the constants $a$ and $b$:

- Looking at the trendline, the $Y$-intercept is $Y \approx 3$. Therefore,
$A\begin{vmatrix}\approx 3 \\ \log_{10} a\end{vmatrix}\approx 3; a \mid \approx 10^{3}; a|= 1000$.
- The trendline passes close to the points $(3,4)$ and $(9,6)$. Therefore, the line's slope is
$B \approx \dfrac{6 - 4}{9 - 3} = \dfrac{2}{6} = \dfrac{1}{3}$
and we obtain
$B\begin{vmatrix}\approx \frac{1}{3} \\ \log b\end{vmatrix}\approx \frac{1}{3}; b \mid \approx 10^{1/3}; = \sqrt[3]{10}; \approx 2.15$
rounded to $2$ decimal places.

## Making Predictions using Semi-Log Scatter Plots

**Example:** The semi-log scatter plot above shows the monthly earnings $(y)$, in millions of dollars, of a company that manufactures computers and the number of years $(x)$ since $2010$. The equation of the trend line is $Y = 0.5 + 0.15x$.

![](<../Source/15.1.4. Exponential Regression/Images/8029ce3d52d4f3e23fdcd8a37ba061bd.png>)

Approximately how many years must pass before the company's monthly earnings equal $\[math]15$ million?

**Explanation**

Applying $\log$ to both sides of

$$
y = a \cdot b^x
$$

we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
y &= a \cdot b^{x} \\
\log y &= \log (a \cdot b^{x}) \\
\log y &= \log a + \log b^{x} \\
\log y &= \log a + x \cdot \log b \\
Y &= A + Bx
\end{aligned}
$$

where

$$
Y=\log y, \qquad A=\log a, \qquad B=\log b
$$

When $y = 15$ (corresponding to monthly earnings of $\[math]15$ million), we have

$$
Y = \log {y} = \log (15) \approx 1.1761
$$

rounded to $4$ decimal places.

To find when the monthly earnings will equal $\[/math]15$ million, we need to substitute $Y = 1.1761$ into the equation of the trend line and solve for $x{:}$

$$
\begin{aligned}
1.1761 &= 0.5 + 0.15x \\
0.6761 &= 0.15x \\
&= x \mid \approx 4.5
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the monthly earnings will equal $\[math]15$ million after approximately $4.5$ years.
