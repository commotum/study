## Introduction

Suppose we want to find all of the solutions to the equation

$$
0 \le x < 2π
$$

To calculate a solution, we can reduce the equation to a more convenient form by dividing through by $\cos x$, and then use the identity

$$
\tan x = \dfrac{\sin x}{\cos x}
$$

to simplify. This gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sin x - \sqrt{3}\cos x &= 0 \\
\frac{\sin x}{\cos x} - \frac{\sqrt{3}\cos x}{\cos x} &= 0 \\
\frac{\sin x}{\cos x} - \frac{\sqrt{3}\cos x}{\cos x} &= 0 \\
\frac{\sin x}{\cos x} - \sqrt{3} &= 0 \\
\tan x - \sqrt{3} &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we rearrange the equation and isolate $\tan x$:

$$
\tan x = \sqrt{3}
$$

We can immediately calculate the principal value $x$:

$$
x = \arctan(\sqrt{3}) = \dfrac{\pi}{3}
$$

Since

$$
\dfrac{\pi}{3}
$$

lies inside the given domain, our first solution is

$$
x_1 = \dfrac{\pi}{3}
$$

To find the other solution in the given interval, we add $\pi$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_{2} &= x_{1} + π \\
&= \frac{π}{3} + π \\
&= \frac{4π}{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

So the solutions are

$$
x = \dfrac{\pi}{3}, \dfrac{4\pi}{3}
$$

## Solving Trigonometric Equations Using the Sin-Cos-Tan Identity

**Example:** Calculate, in degrees to the nearest degree, the solutions to the equation $5\sin x = -7\cos x$ for $0^\circ \leq x < 360^\circ$.

**Explanation**

We divide each term of the equation by $\cos x$ and use the identity

$$
\tan x = \dfrac{\sin x}{\cos x}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
5\sin x &= -7\cos x \\
\frac{5\sin x}{\cos x} &= -\frac{7\cos x}{\cos x} \\
\frac{5\sin x}{\cos x} &= -\frac{7\cos x}{\cos x} \\
5 \cdot \frac{\sin x}{\cos x} &= -7 \\
5\tan x &= -7
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we rearrange the equation and isolate $\tan x$:

$$
\tan x = -\dfrac{7}{5}
$$

The length of the interval for $x$ is $2\pi$, so we will likely require $2$ solutions.

We can immediately calculate the principal value $x$:

$$
x = \arctan\left(-\dfrac{7}{5}\right) \approx -54.46
$$

This value is outside the domain

$$
0^\circ \leq x < 360^\circ
$$

To get a value that's inside the domain, we add $180^\circ$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_{1} &= \arctan (-\frac{7}{5}) + 180^{∘} \\
&\approx - 54.46^{∘} + 180^{∘} \\
&= 125.54^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

To find the other solution inside the given interval, we add $180^\circ$ to $x_1$. Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_{2} &= x_{1} + 180^{∘} \\
&\approx 125.54^{∘} + 180^{∘} \\
&= 305.54^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

So the solutions, rounded to the nearest degree, are

$$
x= 126^\circ, 306^\circ
$$

## Solving Trigonometric Equations Using the Sin-Cos-Tan Identity Under a Modified Domain

Let's find all the solutions to the equation

$$
- π < x \le π
$$

Notice that this time, the domain is

$$
-\pi < x \leq \pi
$$

First, we divide each term of the equation by $\cos x$ and use the identity

$$
\tan x = \dfrac{\sin x}{\cos x}
$$

to simplify. This gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sin x - \cos x &= 0 \\
\frac{\sin x}{\cos x} - \frac{\cos x}{\cos x} &= 0 \\
\frac{\sin x}{\cos x} - \frac{\cos x}{\cos x} &= 0 \\
\frac{\sin x}{\cos x} - 1 &= 0 \\
\tan x - 1 &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we rearrange the equation and isolate $\tan x$:
$\tan x = 1$

We can immediately calculate the principal value $x$:

$$
x = \arctan(1) = \dfrac{\pi}{4}
$$

Since

$$
\dfrac{\pi}{4}
$$

lies inside the given domain, our first solution is

$$
x_1 = \dfrac{\pi}{4}
$$

To find the other solution in the given interval, we *subtract* $\pi$. Hence:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_{2} &= x_{1} - π \\
&= \frac{π}{4} - π \\
&=-\frac{3π}{4}
\end{aligned}
$$

So the solutions are

$$
x = -\dfrac{3\pi}{4}, \dfrac{\pi}{4}
$$

## Solving Trigonometric Equations Using the Sin-Cos-Tan Identity Under a Modified Domain

**Example:** Calculate, in degrees to the nearest degree, the solutions to the equation $3\sin x + 12\cos x = 0$ for $-180^\circ < x \leq 180^\circ$.

**Explanation**

We divide each term of the equation by $\cos x$ and use the identity

$$
\tan x = \dfrac{\sin x}{\cos x}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
3\sin x + 12\cos x &= 0 \\
\frac{3\sin x}{\cos x} + \frac{12\cos x}{\cos x} &= 0 \\
\frac{3\sin x}{\cos x} + \frac{12\cos x}{\cos x} &= 0 \\
3 \cdot \frac{\sin x}{\cos x} + 12 &= 0 \\
3\tan x + 12 &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we rearrange the equation and isolate $\tan x$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
3\tan x &= -12 \\
\tan x &= -4
\end{aligned}
$$

We can immediately calculate the principal value $x$:

$$
x = \arctan(-4) \approx -75.96^\circ
$$

Since this value lies inside the given domain, our first solution is

$$
x_1 = -75.96^\circ
$$

To find the other solution in the given interval, we *add* $180^\circ$. Hence,

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_{2} &= x_{1} + 180^{∘} \\
&\approx - 75.96^{∘} + 180^{∘} \\
&= 104.04^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

So the solutions, rounded to the nearest degree, are

$$
x= -76^\circ, 104^\circ
$$
