## Introduction

Suppose we want to factor the polynomial

$$
8x^2 + 24x + 16
$$

At first glance, it looks tricky because the coefficients are rather large!

However, notice that all the terms have a common factor of $8$. If we first factor out an $8$ from each of the terms, then we can cut the factoring down to something more manageable:

$$
\begin{aligned} 8x^2 + 24x + 16 \\[5pt] &= 8(x^2 + 3x + 2) \end{aligned}
$$

Now, we need to factor the polynomial $x^2 + 3x + 2$ in the parentheses.

The constant term $(2)$ has a single factor pair, namely

$$
(1, 2)
$$

Notice that the numbers in this factor pair indeed sum to the linear term's coefficient

$$
(1+2=3)
$$

So, we can write the linear term as

$$
3x=x+2x
$$

and factor as usual:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^{2} + 3x + 2 &=  \\
x^{2} + x + 2x + 2 &=  \\
(x^{2} + x) + (2x + 2) &=  \\
x(x + 1) + 2(x + 1) &=  \\
&= (x + 2)(x + 1)
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, our original expression factors as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
8x^{2} + 24x + 16 &=  \\
8(x^{2} + 3x + 2) &=  \\
&= 8(x + 2)(x + 1)
\end{aligned}
$$

## Factoring a Trinomial Whose Terms Have a Common Factor

**Example:** Factor $3x^2 - 21x + 30$.

**Explanation**

First, notice that we can factor out a $3$ from each of the terms:

$$
\begin{aligned}
3x^{2} - 21x + 30 &=  \\
&= 3(x^{2} - 7x + 10)
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we just need to factor the polynomial $x^2-7x+10$ in parentheses.

We start by listing the factor pairs of the constant term $(10)$, but only the negative factor pairs since the coefficient of the linear term $(-7)$ is negative.

$$
(-1,-10), (-2,-5)
$$

Now we find which factor pair adds up to the coefficient of the linear term $(-7)$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\begin{bmatrix}(-1) + (-10) &= -11 & \cdot \\ (-2) + (-5)|=-7 & ✓\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Using the factor pair identified above, we write the linear term as $-7x=-2x-5x$, and factor:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^{2} - 7x + 10 &=  \\
x^{2} - 2x - 5x + 10 &=  \\
(x^{2} - 2x) + (-5x + 10) &=  \\
x(x - 2) - 5(x - 2) &=  \\
&= (x - 5)(x - 2)
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, our original expression factors as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned} 3\left(x^2-7x+10\right) \\[5pt] &= 3(x-5)(x-2) \end{aligned}
$$

## Factoring a Trinomial With a Negative Quadratic Term

**Example:** Factor the expression $-2x^2-22x-48$.

**Explanation**

First, notice that we can factor out a $-2$ from each of the terms:

$$
\begin{aligned}
-2x^{2} - 22x - 48 &=  \\
&= - 2(x^{2} + 11x + 24)
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we just need to factor the polynomial $x^2+11x+24$ in parentheses.

First, we find the factor pairs of the constant term $(24)$:

$$
(1, 24), (2, 12), (3, 8), (4, 6)
$$

Now, we find which factor pair adds up to the coefficient of the linear term $(11)$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\begin{bmatrix}1 + 24 &= 25 & \cdot \\ 2 + 12|= 14 & \cdot \\ 3 + 8|= 11 & ✓ \\ 4 + 6|= 10 & \cdot\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Using the factor pair identified in the previous step, write the linear term as

$$
11x=3x+8x
$$

and then factor the expression completely:

$$
\begin{aligned} -2\left(x^2+11x+24\right) \\[5pt] &= -2\left(x^2+3x+8x+24\right) \\[5pt] &= -2\left((x^2+3x)+(8x+24)\right) \\[5pt] &= -2\Bigl(x(x+3)+8(x+3)\Bigr) \\[5pt] &= -2(x+8)(x+3) \end{aligned}
$$
