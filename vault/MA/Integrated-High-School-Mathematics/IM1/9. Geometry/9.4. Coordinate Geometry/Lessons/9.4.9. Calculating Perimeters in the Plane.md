## Introduction

We can use our knowledge of calculating distances in the coordinate plane to find the perimeter of polygons.

For example, consider $\triangle ABC$ below with vertices $A(6,4)$, $B(3,4)$, $C(3,8)$.

![](<../Source/9.4.9. Calculating Perimeters in the Plane/Images/71fca453d370a534304931ac305655e5.png>)

Since $\overline{AB}$ is horizontal, we can calculate the length of this segment by computing the absolute value of the difference between the $x$-coordinates of the endpoints:

$$
\begin{aligned}
AB &= \mid x_{B} - x_{A} \mid \\
&= \mid 6 - 3 \mid \\
&= 3
\end{aligned}
$$

Similarly, since $\overline{BC}$ is vertical, we can calculate the length of this segment by computing the absolute value of the difference between the $y$-coordinates of the endpoints:

$$
\begin{aligned}
BC &= \mid y_{C} - y_{B} \mid \\
&= \mid 8 - 4 \mid \\
&= 4
\end{aligned}
$$

To compute the length of $\overline{AC}$, we use the distance formula, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
AC &= \sqrt{(x_{C} - x_{A})^{2} + (y_{C} - y_{A})^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{(3 - 6)^{2} + (8 - 4)^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{9 + 16} \\
&= 5
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the perimeter is of the triangle is

$$
\begin{aligned}
p &= AB + AC + BC \\
&= 3 + 5 + 4 \\
&= 12
\end{aligned}
$$

## Calculating the Perimeter of a Polygon With Horizontal and Vertical Sides Only

**Example:** What is the perimeter of the square $ABCD$ with vertices $A(0,0)$, $B(2,0)$, $C(2,2)$, and $D(0,2)$?

**Explanation**

Let's plot the square using the given coordinates.

![](<../Source/9.4.9. Calculating Perimeters in the Plane/Images/4340f58ca35ff6e3c29c36963f3e7bbe.png>)

Since our quadrilateral is a square,

$$
AB=BC=CD=AD
$$

From the diagram, we can calculate the length of $\overline{AB}$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
AB &= \mid x_{B} - x_{A} \mid \\
&= \mid 2 - 0 \mid \\
&= 2
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the perimeter of the polygon is

$$
\begin{aligned}
p &= AB + BC + CD + DA \\
&= 4AB \\
&= 4 \cdot 2 \\
&= 8
\end{aligned}
$$

## Calculating the Perimeter of a Polygon Using the Distance Formula

**Example:** What is the perimeter of the parallelogram $ABCD$ with vertices $A(-1,1)$, $B(4,1)$, $C(6,3)$, and $D(1,3)$?

**Explanation**

Let's plot the parallelogram using the given coordinates.

![](<../Source/9.4.9. Calculating Perimeters in the Plane/Images/d7014ec660b75a09a3c75a29e6603565.png>)

Since the segment $\overline{AB}$ is horizontal, we can easily compute its length:

$$
\begin{aligned}
AB &= \mid x_{B} - x_{A} \mid \\
&= \mid 4 - (-1) \mid \\
&= 5
\end{aligned}
$$

To compute the length of $\overline{BC}$, we use the distance formula, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
BC &= \sqrt{(x_{C} - x_{B})^{2} + (y_{C} - y_{B})^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{(6 - 4)^{2} + (3 - 1)^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{4 + 4} \\
&= 2\sqrt{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the perimeter of the parallelogram is

$$
\begin{aligned}
p &= AB + AC + BC + CD \\
&= 2AB + 2BC \\
&= 2 \cdot 5 + 2 \cdot 2\sqrt{2} \\
&= 10 + 4\sqrt{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding the Perimeter of a Square Given Two Opposite Coordinates

**Example:** Given that $A(2,2)$ and $C(5,5)$ are the coordinates of two opposite vertices of a square $ABCD$, what is the perimeter of the square?

**Explanation**

We need to calculate the measure of the sides of the square. To do that, we first find the measure of the diagonal $\overline{AC}$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
AC &= \sqrt{(x_{C} - x_{A})^{2} + (y_{C} - y_{A})^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{(5 - 2)^{2} + (5 - 2)^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{3^{2} + 3^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{9 + 9} \\
&= \sqrt{18} \\
&= 3\sqrt{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, if $a$ is the measure of a side of the square, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
a &= \frac{AC}{\sqrt{2}} \\
&= \frac{3\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{2}} \\
&= 3
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the perimeter of the square is

$$
\begin{aligned}
p &= 4a \\
&= 4 \cdot 3 \\
&= 12
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding the Coordinates of a Vertex of a Polygon Given Its Perimeter

**Example:** The points $A(-6,4)$, $B(0,4)$, and $C(p,q)$ are the coordinates of the vertices of an isosceles triangle $\triangle ABC$ with base $\overline{AB}$ and perimeter $16$. Given that $q>4$, find the coordinates of $C$.

**Explanation**

Let's sketch our triangle.

![](<../Source/9.4.9. Calculating Perimeters in the Plane/Images/536495683020f178ff3474d69a8461f1.png>)

Since the vertex $C$ is equidistant from $A$ and $B$, it lies on the perpendicular bisector of the segment $\overline{AB}$. In our case, this is the line $x=-3$. So the coordinates of $C$ are $(-3,q)$, where $q$ is an unknown parameter.

From the diagram, we can calculate the length of $\overline{AB}$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
AB &= \mid x_{B} - x_{A} \mid \\
&= \mid 0 - (-6) \mid \\
&= 6
\end{aligned}
$$

On the other hand, the length of $\overline{AC}$ can be found as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
AC &= \sqrt{(x_{C} - x_{A})^{2} + (y_{C} - y_{A})^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{(-3 - (-6))^{2} + (q - 4)^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{9 + (q - 4)^{2}}
\end{aligned}
$$

Since $AC=BC$ and the perimeter is $16$, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
16 &= AC + BC + AB \\
16 &= 2AC + AB \\
16 &= 2AC + 6 \\
2AC &= 10 \\
AC &= 5 \\
AC^{2} &= 5^{2} \\
9 + (q - 4)^{2} &= 25 \\
(q - 4)^{2} &= 16 \\
q - 4 &= ± 4 \\
q &= 0, 8
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, since we are told that $q > 4$, the coordinates of the point $C$ are $(-3,8)$.
