## Introduction

The area of the whole circle below is $24\,\textrm{cm}^2$, and the area of the shaded sector is $4\,\textrm{cm}^2$. What is the measure of $\angle{AOB}$?

![](<../Source/9.3.8. Further Calculating Areas of Sectors/Images/c2120206baad35af57fdc2f467b72161.png>)

First, note that the ratio of the smaller area to the larger area is

$$
\dfrac{4\,\textrm{cm}^2}{24\,\textrm{cm}^2} = \dfrac{1}{6}
$$

Therefore, the shaded area is precisely one-sixth of the total area.

![](<../Source/9.3.8. Further Calculating Areas of Sectors/Images/8462554ce7ea61a37378a8bdbe5316ad.png>)

Consequently, $\angle AOB$ represents

$$
\dfrac16
$$

of a complete turn. So, the measure of $\angle{AOB}$ is

$$
\begin{aligned}
m∠AOB &= \frac{1}{6} \cdot 360^{∘} = 60^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Calculating the Area of a Circular Sector From a Diagram

**Example:** What is the area of the shaded circular sector shown below?

![](<../Source/9.3.8. Further Calculating Areas of Sectors/Images/56fee0d0432e7a0896ca184ca0262b64.png>)

**Explanation**

From the diagram, we have that the radius $r$ of the circle is $4\,\textrm{cm}$.

Now, $\angle{AOB}$ is the central angle that intercepts the arc $\overset{\frown}{AB}$. So the measure of the major arc (that corresponds to our sector) is

$$
360^\circ - m\angle{AOB} = 360^\circ - 30^\circ = 330^\circ
$$

Note that the ratio of the measure of the major arc to a full circle turn is

$$
\dfrac{330^\circ}{360^\circ} = \dfrac{11}{12}
$$

Therefore, if the area of the circle is $\mathcal{A}$, then the area of the major sector will be

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{11}{12} \cdot A &= \frac{11}{12} \cdot πr^{2} \\
&= \frac{11}{12} \cdot π \cdot 4^{2} \\
&= \frac{44π}{3}cm^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding an Unknown Radius Given the Area of a Circular Sector

**Example:** The area of the circular sector $AOB$ is $9\pi\,\textrm{km}^2$ and $m\overset{\frown}{AB}=90^\circ$. Find the radius of the circle.

![](<../Source/9.3.8. Further Calculating Areas of Sectors/Images/61e241acd697a4dea9525e0636cbb286.png>)

**Explanation**

The ratio of the measure of the arc to a full circle turn is

$$
\dfrac{m\angle{AOB}}{360^\circ} = \dfrac{m\overset{\frown}{AB}}{360^\circ}= \dfrac{90^\circ}{360^\circ} = \dfrac{1}{4}
$$

Therefore, if the area of the circle is $\mathcal{A}$, then the area of the sector will be

$$
\begin{aligned}
A_{S} &= \frac{1}{4}A \\
&= \frac{1}{4} \cdot πr^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Substituting the value of the area of the sector, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
9π &= \frac{1}{4} \cdot πr^{2} \\
9π &= \frac{1}{4} \cdot πr^{2} \\
9 &= \frac{1}{4} \cdot r^{2} \\
r &= ± 6
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
r=6\,\textrm{km}
$$

, since a distance can't be negative.

## Word Problems

**Example:** A $12$-inch pendulum swings through an angle of $60^\circ$, as depicted below. Find the total area swept by the pendulum.

![](<../Source/9.3.8. Further Calculating Areas of Sectors/Images/64bbd7a08f9e77f5332e200f0385e436.png>)

**Explanation**

Let

$$
r= 12\,\textrm{in}
$$

Then, the area of the corresponding full circle is

$$
\pi r^2 = \pi \cdot 12^2 = 144\pi\,\textrm{in}^2
$$

The ratio of the central angle to $360^\circ$ is

$$
\dfrac{60^\circ}{360^\circ} = \dfrac{1}6
$$

Therefore, the area of the sector swept by the pendulum is

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= \frac{1}{6} \cdot 144π \\
&= 24πin^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$
