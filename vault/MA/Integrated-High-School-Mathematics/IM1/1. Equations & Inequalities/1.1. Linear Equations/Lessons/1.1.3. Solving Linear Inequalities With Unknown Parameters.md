## Introduction

Suppose we wish to solve the following inequality for the variable $x$:

$$
5x + 4 < 8k
$$

We solve this inequality using the addition and multiplication principles, treating $k$ like any other number.

First, we apply the addition principle, subtracting $4$ from both sides of the inequality:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}5x + 4 & < 8k \\ 5x + 4 - 4 & < 8k - 4 \\ 5x & < 8k - 4\end{bmatrix}
$$

Then, we apply the multiplication principle, dividing both sides of the inequality by $5{:}$

$$
\begin{bmatrix}5x & < 8k - 4 \\ \frac{5x}{5} & < \frac{8k - 4}{5} \\ x & < \frac{8k - 4}{5}\end{bmatrix}
$$

Therefore, the solution to our inequality is

$$
x < \dfrac{8k-4}{5}
$$

## Solving Linear Inequalities

**Example:** Solve the inequality $\dfrac{3x-2k}{5} \ge -1$ for the variable $x$.

**Explanation**

We solve the inequality using the addition and multiplication principles, treating $k$ like any other number.

First, we multiply both sides of the inequality by $5$ to eliminate the fraction:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}\frac{3x - 2k}{5} & \ge - 1 \\ 5 \cdot (\frac{3x - 2k}{5}) & \ge 5 \cdot (-1) \\ 3x - 2k & \ge - 5\end{bmatrix}
$$

Now, we apply the addition principle, adding $2k$ to both sides of the inequality:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}3x - 2k & \ge - 5 \\ 3x - 2k + 2k & \ge - 5 + 2k \\ 3x & \ge - 5 + 2k \\ 3x & \ge 2k - 5\end{bmatrix}
$$

Finally, we apply the multiplication principle, dividing both sides of the inequality by $3{:}$

$$
\begin{bmatrix}3x & \ge 2k - 5 \\ \frac{3x}{3} & \ge \frac{2k - 5}{3} \\ x & \ge \frac{2k - 5}{3}\end{bmatrix}
$$

## Solving Inequalities Where Flipping is Required

**Example:** Solve the inequality $1-3x \leq 13-6k$ for the variable $x$.

**Explanation**

We solve the inequality using the addition and multiplication principles, treating $k$ like any other number.

First, we apply the addition principle, subtracting $1$ from both sides of the inequality:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}1 - 3x & \le 13 - 6k \\ 1 - 3x - 1 & \le 13 - 6k - 1 \\ -3x & \le 12 - 6k\end{bmatrix}
$$

Then, we apply the multiplication principle, dividing both sides of the inequality by $-3$. Note that since we are dividing by a negative number, we need to flip the inequality.

$$
\begin{bmatrix}-3x & \le 12 - 6k \\ (-3x)/((-3)) & \ge (12 - 6k)/((-3)) \\ x & \ge (12 - 6k)/((-3))\end{bmatrix}
$$

Finally, we further simplify the right-hand side, as follows:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}x & \ge (12 - 6k)/((-3)) \\ x & \ge (3(4 - 2k))/((-3)) \\ x & \ge (3(4 - 2k))/((-3)) \\ x & \ge (4 - 2k)/((-1)) \\ x & \ge 2k - 4\end{bmatrix}
$$

## Solving Inequalities Containing Unknown Coefficients

**Example:** Given that $k< 0$, solve the inequality $\dfrac{kx +3}3 \geq 2k$ for the variable $x$.

**Explanation**

We solve the inequality using the addition and multiplication principles, treating $k$ like any other number.

First, we multiply both sides of the inequality by $3$ to eliminate the fraction:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}\frac{kx + 3}{3} & \ge 2k \\ 3 \cdot (\frac{kx + 3}{3}) & \ge 3 \cdot 2k \\ kx + 3 & \ge 6k\end{bmatrix}
$$

Now, we apply the addition principle, subtracting $3$ from both sides of the inequality:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}kx + 3 & \ge 6k \\ kx & \ge 6k - 3\end{bmatrix}
$$

Then, we divide by $k$. Since $k < 0$, we need to flip the inequality:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}kx & \ge 6k - 3 \\ \frac{kx}{k} & \le \frac{6k - 3}{k} \\ x & \le \frac{6k - 3}{k}\end{bmatrix}
$$
