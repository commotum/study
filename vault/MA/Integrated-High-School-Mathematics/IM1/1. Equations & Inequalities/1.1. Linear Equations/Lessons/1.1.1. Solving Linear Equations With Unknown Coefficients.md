## Introduction

Consider the following equation:

$$
2{\color{red}m}{\color{blue}t} = 1
$$

Notice that this equation has two variables, ${\color{red}m}$ and ${\color{blue}t}$. Suppose we want to solve the equation for the variable ${\color{blue}t}$.

To isolate ${\color{blue}t}$, we apply the multiplication principle and divide both sides of the equation by $2{\color{red}m}$, treating ${\color{red}m}$ as though it were a constant:

$$
\begin{aligned}
2mt &= 1 \\
\frac{2mt}{2m} &= \frac{1}{2m} \\
\frac{2mt}{2m} &= \frac{1}{2m} \\
t &= \frac{1}{2m}
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we'll take a look at an example where we need to apply both the addition and multiplication principles.

## Solving Equations With Unknown Coefficients

**Example:** Solve the equation $5ky-13=7$ for the variable $y$.

**Explanation**

We need to isolate $y$. To do this, we first add $13$ to both sides of the equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
5ky - 13 &= 7 \\
5ky - 13 + 13 &= 7 + 13 \\
5ky &= 20
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we divide both sides of the equation by $5k$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
5ky &= 20 \\
\frac{5ky}{5k} &= \frac{20}{5k} \\
\frac{5ky}{5k} &= \frac{20}{5k} \\
y &= \frac{20}{5k}
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we can further simplify the right-hand side:

$$
\begin{aligned}
y &= \frac{5 \cdot 4}{5k} \\
y &= \frac{5 \cdot 4}{5k} \\
y &= \frac{4}{k}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Solving Equations Containing Fractions

**Example:** Solve the equation $\dfrac 34 kx + 9 = 2$ for the variable $x$.

**Explanation**

First, we multiply both sides of the equation by $4$ to get rid of the fraction:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{3}{4}kx + 9 &= 2 \\
4 \cdot (\frac{3}{4}kx + 9) &= 4 \cdot 2 \\
3kx + 36 &= 8
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we need to isolate $x$. To do this, we first subtract $36$ from both sides of the equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
3kx + 36 &= 8 \\
3kx + 36 - 36 &= 8 - 36 \\
3kx &= -28
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we divide both sides of the equation by $3k$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
3kx &= -28 \\
\frac{3kx}{3k} &= \frac{-28}{3k} \\
\frac{3kx}{3k} &= \frac{-28}{3k} \\
x &= -\frac{28}{3k}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Solving Equations Using Cross Multiplication

**Example:** Solve the equation $\dfrac{1}{3} = -\dfrac{ax}{5}$ for the variable $x$.

**Explanation**

We need to isolate $x$. To do this, we first cross-multiply:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{1}{3} &= -\frac{ax}{5} \\
\frac{1}{3} &= \frac{-ax}{5} \\
5 \cdot 1 &= 3 \cdot (-ax) \\
5 &= -3ax
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we divide both sides of the equation by $-3a$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
5 &= -3ax \\
\frac{5}{-3a} &= \frac{-3ax}{-3a} \\
\frac{5}{-3a} &= \frac{-3ax}{-3a} \\
- \frac{5}{3a} &= x \\
x &= -\frac{5}{3a}
\end{aligned}
$$
