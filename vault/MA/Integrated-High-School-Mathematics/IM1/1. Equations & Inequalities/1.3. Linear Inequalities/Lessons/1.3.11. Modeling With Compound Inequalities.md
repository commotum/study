## Introduction

In many real-world scenarios, quantities can have multiple constraints at once. We can often model such situations using *compound inequalities*.

For example, suppose the cost of a movie ticket is $\[math]12$ per person, and during the weekend, between $100$ and $150$ people attend the movie screenings each day. We can use this information to find a compound inequality that must be satisfied by the cinema's daily earnings.

Since the number of attendees ranges from $100$ to $150$ per day, we can calculate the range of possible earnings:

- If $100$ people attend per day, and the ticket price is $\[/math]12$, then the minimum earnings are given by
$\[math]12 \cdot 100 = \[/math]1\,200$.
- If $150$ people attend per day, and the ticket price is $\[math]12$, then the maximum earnings are given by
$\[/math]12 \cdot 150 = \[math]1\,800$.

Therefore, if $P$ represents the daily earnings of the cinema, in dollars, we have

$$
1\,200 \leq P \leq 1\,800
$$

Let's see another example.

## Constructing Simple Compound Inequalities

**Example:** A clothing store offers a $15\%$ discount on all jackets. The price of a jacket varies between $\[math]80$ and $\[/math]140$. If $S$ represents the saving that a customer can receive on a single jacket purchase, find an inequality that must be satisfied by $S$.

**Explanation**

The price of the jackets varies between $\[math]80$ and $\[/math]140$.

If the $15\%$ discount is applied, then:

- The minimum saving a customer can receive is given by
$15\% \cdot \[math]80 = \[/math]12$.
- The maximum saving a customer can receive is given by
$15\% \cdot \[math]140 = \[/math]21$.

Therefore, if $S$ represents the total saving a customer can receive, we have

$$
12 \leq S \leq 21
$$

## Constructing Compound Inequalities

**Example:** The formula
$B = 30x + 40$
gives the brightness level $B$ (in lumens) of a display screen as a function of the slider position $x$, where $x = 0$ represents the lowest setting and $x = 1$ represents the maximum setting. For recommended viewing conditions, the slider should be set between $0.4$ and $0.9$. What inequality describes the acceptable brightness range during use?

**Explanation**

We are asked to find the brightness range based on the formula and a recommended slider range.

The formula is

$$
B = 30x + 40
$$

The slider should be set between $0.4$ and $0.9$. Thus, we compute:

- At the minimum recommended slider level $x = 0.4$, the brightness is
$B|= 30(0.4) + 40; = 12 + 40; = 52lumens$.
- At the maximum recommended slider level $x = 0.9$, the brightness is
$B|= 30(0.9) + 40; = 27 + 40; = 67lumens$.

Therefore, the acceptable brightness range during use is

$$
52\leq B\leq 67
$$

## Constructing and Solving Compound Inequalities With Multiple Variables

**Example:** A branding consultant charges a base fee of $b$ dollars and receives a royalty equal to $10\%$ of the product revenue $x$ generated from a marketing campaign. For a particular contract, the consultant wants total compensation to be at least $1.4$ times and at most $1.8$ times the base fee. What inequality shows all possible values of $x$ that meet this goal?

**Explanation**

The consultant earns $10\%$ of the revenue. So, if the total revenue is $x$ dollars, they earn $0.10x$ dollars.

Additionally, there is a base fee of $b$ dollars. Therefore, the total earned, in dollars, is

$$
b + 0.10x
$$

Since the consultant wants total earnings to be at least $1.4$ times and at most $1.8$ times the base fee, we must have

$$
1.4b \leq b + 0.10x \leq 1.8b
$$

Subtracting $b$ throughout, we get

$$
0.4b \le 0.10x \mid \le 0.8b
$$

Then, we divide by $0.10{:}$

$$
\begin{bmatrix}\frac{0.4b}{0.10} \le x & \le \frac{0.8b}{0.10} \\ 4b \le x & \le 8b\end{bmatrix}
$$

Therefore, the correct inequality is

$$
4b \leq x \leq 8b
$$
