## Introduction

In this lesson, we will learn how to construct and solve inequalities in context.

Suppose that Carol earns $\[math]20$ per hour and needs at least $\[/math] 360$ each week to cover her living expenses. What's the minimum number of hours Carol needs to work each week to cover her expenses?

Suppose Carol works $h$ hours. Then

- she will earn $20h$ dollars in total, and
- this amount of money should be *greater or equal to* $\[math]360$ because she needs to earn *at least* this amount.

So, the number of hours that Carol must work must satisfy the following inequality:

$$
20h \geq 360
$$

To solve this inequality, we must divide both sides by $20$. This gives

$$
\begin{bmatrix}20h & \ge 360 \\ \frac{20h}{20} & \ge \frac{360}{20} \\ \frac{20h}{20} & \ge 18 \\ h & \ge 18\end{bmatrix}
$$

Therefore, Carol needs to work for *at least* $18$ hours per week to cover her expenses.

## Modeling With Non-Strict Inequalites

**Example:** Jenny has a hot dog stand in a park, where she makes a profit of $\[math]3$ per hot dog. She needs to make a profit of at least $\[/math]540$ per week to continue operating. How many hot dogs does Jenny need to sell weekly to continue working?

**Explanation**

Jenny charges $\[math] 3$ per hot dog. So, if she sells $H$ hot dogs, she makes a total of $3H$ dollars.

Since Jenny must make *at least* $\[/math] 540$, the following inequality must be satisfied:

$$
3H \geq 540
$$

To solve for $H$, we divide both sides by $3$:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}3H & \ge 540 \\ \frac{3H}{3} & \ge \frac{540}{3} \\ \frac{3H}{3} & \ge 180 \\ H & \ge 180\end{bmatrix}
$$

So, to continue operating, Jenny must sell *at least* $180$ hot dogs.

## Modeling With Strict Inequalites

**Example:** A box of nails costs $\[math] 8$. How many boxes of nails a person can buy for less than $\[/math] 440$?

**Explanation**

To buy $B$ boxes of nails, the person needs to spend $8B$ dollars.

Since the person has *less than* $\[math] 440$, the following inequality must be satisfied:
$8B \lt 440$

To solve for $B$, we divide both sides of the inequality by $8{:}$

$$
\begin{bmatrix}8B & < 440 \\ \frac{8B}{8} & < \frac{440}{8} \\ \frac{8B}{8} & < 55 \\ B & < 55\end{bmatrix}
$$

Thus, the person can buy *less than* $55$ boxes of nails.

## Interpreting Solutions With Fractions

**Example:** Erika wants to spend at least $\[math] 115$ on a pair of sports shoes. If Erika has a job where she earns $\[/math]14$ for each full hour worked, how many full hours must Erika work to buy a pair of sports shoes?

**Explanation**

Erika earns $\[math]14$ for each full hour worked. So if she works $H$ full hours, she makes $14H$ dollars.

Since she wants to spend *at least* $\[/math] 115$ on the shoes, the following inequality must be satisfied:

$$
14H \ge 115
$$

To solve for $H$, we divide both sides of the inequality by $14{:}$

$$
\begin{bmatrix}14H & \ge 115 \\ \frac{14H}{14} & \ge \frac{115}{14} \\ \frac{14H}{14} & \ge \frac{115}{14} \\ H & \ge \frac{115}{14} \\ H & \ge 8\frac{3}{14}\end{bmatrix}
$$

Now, $H$ must be an integer. Therefore, we conclude that Erika must work at least $9$ full hours.
