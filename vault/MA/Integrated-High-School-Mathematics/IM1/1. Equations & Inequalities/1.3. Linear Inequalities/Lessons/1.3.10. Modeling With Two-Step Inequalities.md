## Introduction

In this lesson, we will learn how to construct and solve two-step inequalities in context.

Suppose a cab driver charges her customers $\[math]10$ to drive them from the station to the hotel. Her total expenses amount to $\[/math]20$ per day. What is the smallest number of rides she has to offer to earn at least $\[math]100$ per day?

The price per ride is $\[/math]10$. So, if the driver makes $n$ rides, she receives $10n$ dollars.

However, she spends $\[math]20$ every day on expenses. So her daily profit (in dollars) equals

$$
10n - 20
$$

Since the driver wants to make *at least* $\[/math]100$ per day, the following inequality must be satisfied:

$$
10n - 20 \geq 100
$$

Solving for $n$, we obtain the following:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}10n - 20 & \ge 100 \\ 10n & \ge 100 + 20 \\ 10n & \ge 120 \\ n & \ge \frac{120}{10} \\ n & \ge 12\end{bmatrix}
$$

Therefore, the driver must offer *at least* $12$ rides per day.

<a id="modeling-with-non-strict-inequalities"></a>
## Modeling With Non-Strict Inequalities

**Example:** Anna wants to spend at most $\[math]16$ at the store. Each candy bar costs $\[/math]2$, and she also decides to buy one bottle of water for $\[math]6$. Which of the following statements is correct?

1. Anna can buy at most $3$ candy bars.
2. Anna can buy at most $4$ candy bars.
3. Anna can buy at most $5$ candy bars.

**Explanation**

Each candy bar costs $\[math]2$. So, if Anna buys $c$ candy bars, she will pay a total of $2c$ dollars.

However, she also decides to buy one bottle of water for $\[/math]6$. So, the total amount (in dollars) Anna must pay equals

$$
2c+6
$$

Now, since Anna wants to spend *at most* $\[math]16$, the following inequality must be satisfied:

$$
2c+6 \leq 16
$$

Solving for $c$, we obtain the following:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}2c + 6 & \le 16 \\ 2c & \le 16 - 6 \\ 2c & \le 10 \\ c & \le \frac{10}{2} \\ c & \le 5\end{bmatrix}
$$

Therefore, Anna can buy *at most* $5$ candy bars.

So, the correct answer is "III only."

## Modeling With Strict Inequalities

**Example:** Peter wants to spend less than $\[math]320$ to rent a van to go on a trip with his family. The rental company charges a flat rate of $\[/math]70$ plus $\[math]50$ per day. Which of the following statements is correct?

1. Peter can rent the van for less than $6$ days.
2. Peter can rent the van for less than $5$ days.
3. Peter can rent the van for up to $6$ days.

**Explanation**

The price per day is $\[math]50$. So, for $d$ days, Peter will pay $50d$ dollars.

However, there is also a flat fee of $\[/math]70$. So, the total (in dollars) Peter must pay equals

$$
50d+70
$$

Since Peter wants to spend *less than* $\[math]320$, the following inequality must be satisfied:

$$
50d+70 < 320
$$

Solving for $d$, we obtain the following:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}50d + 70 & < 320 \\ 50d & < 320 - 70 \\ 50d & < 250 \\ d & < \frac{250}{50} \\ d & < 5\end{bmatrix}
$$

Therefore, Peter can rent the van for *less than* $5$ days. So, the correct answer is "II only."

<a id="interpreting-solutions-with-fractions"></a>
## Interpreting Solutions With Fractions

**Example:** Vincent wants to watch some movies and spend at most $\[math]8$. Each movie costs $\[/math]1.40$, and Vincent plans to use a coupon that gives him $\[math]4$ off the final purchase. Which of the following statements is correct?

1. Vincent can watch at most $8$ movies.
2. Vincent can watch at most $9$ movies.
3. Vincent can watch up to $10$ movies.

**Explanation**

Each movie costs $\[math]1.40$. So, if Vincent buys $n$ movies, he would normally pay $1.4n$ dollars.

However, Vincent will use a coupon that takes $\[/math]4$ off the purchase price. So, Vincent will have to pay (in dollars) a total of

$$
1.4n-4
$$

Since Vincent wants to spend *at most* $\[math]8.00$, the following inequality must be satisfied:

$$
1.4n-4 \leq8
$$

Solving for $n$, we obtain the following:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}1.4n - 4 & \le 8 \\ 1.4n & \le 8 + 4 \\ 1.4n & \le 12 \\ n & \le \frac{12}{1.4} \\ n & \le \frac{120}{14} \\ n & \le 8\frac{4}{7}\end{bmatrix}
$$

Now, $n$ must be an integer. Therefore, we conclude that Vincent can watch *at most* $8$ movies.

Therefore, the correct answer is "I only. "
