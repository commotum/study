## Introduction

Suppose that we wish to evaluate the expression $\sqrt x + 3$ for the value $x=9$. This type of expression is called a **radical expression** because it contains the radical symbol $\sqrt{\phantom 0}$. Remember that $\sqrt x$ means "the positive square root of the number $x$."

To evaluate this expression at the given value, we replace $x$ with $9$ in the given expression and simplify.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{x} + 3 &=  \\
\sqrt{9} + 3 &=  \\
\sqrt{3^{2}} + 3 &=  \\
3 + 3 &=  \\
&= 6
\end{aligned}
$$

**Important:** You may have heard somewhere that

$$
\sqrt{9} = \pm 3
$$

because $(-3)^2=9$ and $(3)^2 = 9$. However, when we write down $\sqrt{9}$, we *always* mean "the positive square root of $9$".

If we want the negative square root of $9$, we write

$$
-\sqrt{9} = -3
$$

Sometimes, we want both, in which case we would write

$$
\pm\sqrt{9}=\pm 3
$$

## Evaluating a Radical Expression that Simplifies to a Whole Number

**Example:** Evaluate $\sqrt{x} + 2x -1$ when $x=16$.

**Explanation**

We replace $x$ with $16$ in the given expression, and simplify:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{x} + 2x - 1 &=  \\
\sqrt{16} + 2 \cdot 16 - 1 &=  \\
\sqrt{4^{2}} + 32 - 1 &=  \\
4 + 32 - 1 &=  \\
&= 35
\end{aligned}
$$

## Evaluating a Radical Expression that Does Not Simplify to a Whole Number

**Example:** Evaluate $\sqrt{3x-1}$ for $x=3$.

**Explanation**

We replace $x$ with $3$ in the given expression, and simplify:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{3x - 1} &=  \\
\sqrt{3 \cdot 3 - 1} &=  \\
\sqrt{9 - 1} &=  \\
&= \sqrt{8}
\end{aligned}
$$

We can simplify even further using the product rule:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{8} &=  \\
\sqrt{4 \cdot 2} &=  \\
\sqrt{2^{2} \cdot 2} &=  \\
\sqrt{2^{2}} \cdot \sqrt{2} &=  \\
&= 2\sqrt{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Evaluating a Radical Expression Containing Fractions

**Example:** Given that $f(x) = \dfrac{\sqrt{9x+3}}{\sqrt{x-2}}$, find $f(5)$.

**Explanation**

We replace $x$ with $5$ in the given expression, and simplify:

$$
\begin{aligned}
f(5) &= (\sqrt{9(5) + 3})/(\sqrt{(5) - 2}) \\
&= \frac{\sqrt{48}}{\sqrt{3}} \\
&= \sqrt{\frac{48}{3}} \\
&= \sqrt{16} \\
&= \sqrt{4^{2}} \\
&= 4
\end{aligned}
$$

## Evaluating a Radical Expression Containing a Cube Root

**Example:** Evaluate $\sqrt[3]{x+6}$ at $x=2$.

**Explanation**

We replace $x$ with $2$ in the given expression, and simplify as much as possible:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt[3]{x + 6} &=  \\
\sqrt[3]{2 + 6} &=  \\
\sqrt[3]{8} &=  \\
\sqrt[3]{2^{3}} &=  \\
&= 2
\end{aligned}
$$
