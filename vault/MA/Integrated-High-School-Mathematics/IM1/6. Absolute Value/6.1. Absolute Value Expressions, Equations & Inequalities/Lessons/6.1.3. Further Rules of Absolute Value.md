## Introduction

In this lesson, we'll extend our knowledge of absolute value.

Let's first remind ourselves of the rules of absolute value:

- $\begin{vmatrix}{-a} & = & a\end{vmatrix}$
- $\begin{vmatrix}a \cdot b & = & a & \cdot & b\end{vmatrix}$
- $\left\begin{vmatrix}\dfrac{a}{b} \right & = \dfrac{\begin{vmatrix}a &}{\end{vmatrix}b\end{vmatrix}}$

Recall that the last two rules are known as the **distributive properties.**

---

<a id="simplifying-absolute-value-expressions"></a>
## Simplifying Absolute Value Expressions

**Example:** Simplify the expression $\begin{vmatrix}{-3z} & -5\cdot & 4z\end{vmatrix}$.

**Explanation**

Firstly, since

$$
\begin{vmatrix}{-a} & = & a\end{vmatrix}
$$

we can write our expression as

$$
\begin{vmatrix}3z & -5\cdot & 4z\end{vmatrix}
$$

Then, we distribute the absolute value over the multiplication, and simplify:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\begin{vmatrix}3z & -5 \cdot & 4z\end{vmatrix} &=  \\
\begin{vmatrix}3 & \cdot & z & -5 \cdot & 4 & \cdot & z\end{vmatrix} &=  \\
3 \cdot\begin{vmatrix}z & -5 \cdot 4 \cdot & z & &= \\ 3 \cdot & z & -20 \cdot & z\end{vmatrix}|= \\
3\begin{vmatrix}z & -20 & z\end{vmatrix} &=  \\
&= - 17 \mid z \mid
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="the-absolute-value-of-a-square"></a>
## The Absolute Value of a Square

We know that the absolute value operation does nothing to a number if it is non-negative. For example:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mid 0 \mid &= 0 \\
\mid 1 \mid &= 1 \\
\mid 2 \mid &= 2 \\
\mid 3 \mid &= 3 \\
&= ⋮
\end{aligned}
$$

We can summarize this fact as follows:

$$
\mid x \mid = x \quad \text{if} \quad x \geq 0
$$

We also know that $x^2$ is non-negative for any value of $x$. Therefore,

$$
\left|x^2\right| = x^2
$$

where $x$ is *any* real number.

---

<a id="simplifying-the-absolute-value-of-a-square"></a>
## Simplifying the Absolute Value of a Square

**Example:** Simplify the expression $\left| - \dfrac{x^2}{10} \right|$.

**Explanation**

Firstly, since

$$
\begin{vmatrix}{-a} & = & a\end{vmatrix}
$$

we can write our expression as

$$
\left| \dfrac{x^2}{10} \right|
$$

Then, we distribute the absolute value over the multiplication and division:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mid \frac{x^{2}}{10} \mid &=  \\
\frac{\begin{vmatrix}x^{2} &}{\mid 10\end{vmatrix}} &=  \\
&= \frac{\mid x^{2} \mid}{10}
\end{aligned}
$$

Since

$$
x^2 \geq 0
$$

for any real $x$, we have that

$$
\begin{aligned}
\left|x^2\right &= x^2
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\mid x^{2} \mid}{10} &= \frac{x^{2}}{10}
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="the-absolute-value-of-a-negative-variable"></a>
## The Absolute Value of a Negative Variable

We know that

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mid x &= x
\end{aligned}
$$

if

$$
x \geq 0
$$

But what if $x < 0$?

If a variable is known to be negative, then the absolute value changes it to positive. To turn a negative number into a positive number, we multiply by $-1$. Therefore, we have

$$
\mid x \mid = -x \quad\textrm{for}\quad x < 0
$$

To convince ourselves that this is indeed true, let's verify it in the case when $x=-2$. Substituting this value into the above, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mid - 2 \mid &= - (-2) = 2.✓
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="evaluating-an-absolute-value-expression-with-a-domain-restriction"></a>
## Evaluating an Absolute Value Expression with a Domain Restriction

**Example:** Given that $x\lt 0$, simplify $\mid {-6x} \mid +2x$.

**Explanation**

Firstly, since

$$
\begin{vmatrix}{-a} & = & a\end{vmatrix}
$$

we can write our expression as

$$
\mid 6x \mid +2x
$$

Then, we distribute the absolute value over the multiplication. This gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mid 6x \mid + 2x &=  \\
\begin{vmatrix}6 & \cdot & x\end{vmatrix}+ 2x &=  \\
6 \cdot\begin{vmatrix}x & + 2x &= \\ 6 & x\end{vmatrix}+ 2x \mid
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, since $x \lt 0$, we have that

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mid x &= -x
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
6 \mid x\begin{vmatrix}+ 2x &= \\ 6(-x) + 2x|= \\ -6x + 2x|= \\ -4x\end{vmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$
