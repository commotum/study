## Introduction

Suppose we have surveyed $100$ students regarding their sports preferences, asking them whether they like baseball or volleyball. The results of our survey are shown in the Venn diagram below.

![](<../Source/12.3.1. Conditional Probabilities From Venn Diagrams/Images/0eb5cc19648067442db072c7288d1aa5.png>)

Now, suppose that we wish to calculate the probability that a randomly selected student who likes volleyball also likes baseball. We represent this probability symbolically as
$P(\textrm{student likes baseball} \mid \textrm{student likes volleyball})$.

Said in words, we would read the above as "the probability that a student likes baseball **given that** they like volleyball." This type of probability is called a **conditional probability.** In this case, we can think of this probability as the proportion of volleyball-liking students who also like baseball.

Since we assume that the student likes volleyball, we only need to look at the area of the Venn diagram that is inside the right-hand circle.

![](<../Source/12.3.1. Conditional Probabilities From Venn Diagrams/Images/978b4807bfe532261b148db61801dc83.png>)

In total, the number of students who like volleyball is

$$
30+20=50
$$

Of these $50$ students, only $30$ of them also like baseball. This is represented by the area where the circles $A$ and $B$ overlap.

![](<../Source/12.3.1. Conditional Probabilities From Venn Diagrams/Images/028dbfd49f11014adcc150011ab850d2.png>)

Therefore, the probability that a randomly selected student likes baseball *given that* they like volleyball, is

$$
P(\textrm{student likes baseball} \mid \textrm{student likes volleyball}) = \dfrac{30}{50} = \dfrac35
$$

## Computing a Conditional Probability Using a Venn Diagram

**Example:** The Venn diagram below represents two events $A$ and $B$ and the probabilities of each event. Calculate $P(B \mid A)$.

![](<../Source/12.3.1. Conditional Probabilities From Venn Diagrams/Images/ab7cfc6a306f5dc667492e1b49a16c2c.png>)

**Explanation**

To compute $P(B \mid A)$, we need to ask, "what is the probability of $B$ occurring given that $A$ has occurred."

Since we are assuming $A$ has occurred, we only consider the probabilities inside $A$.

![](<../Source/12.3.1. Conditional Probabilities From Venn Diagrams/Images/b912f69b6391ca31e2d3233861d2e986.png>)

The probability of event $A$ occurring is

$$
P(A)=0.3+0.1=0.4
$$

Now, $P(B \mid A)$ can be interpreted as the proportion of $B$ that lies inside $A$, relative to the size of $A$. So, we have

$$
P(B \mid A) = \dfrac{0.1}{0.4} = 0.25
$$

## Computing a Conditional Probability Using a Venn Diagram: Word Problems

**Example:** Consider the Venn diagram below. The set $T$ represents all of the customers of a cafe who like tea, and $C$ represents all of the customers who like coffee. Find the probability that a randomly selected customer who likes coffee also likes tea.

![](<../Source/12.3.1. Conditional Probabilities From Venn Diagrams/Images/36c6c4bbd81ea1fcb61c6518278b375d.png>)

**Explanation**

We wish to compute the probability that a customer likes tea *given that* they like coffee. Therefore, we need to compute $P(T \mid C)$.

To compute $P(T \mid C)$, we need to ask, "what is the probability of $T$ occurring given that $C$ has occurred?"

Since we are assuming that $C$ has occurred, we only consider the customers inside $C$.

![](<../Source/12.3.1. Conditional Probabilities From Venn Diagrams/Images/8045fce47a29f8929c2421bf0a267cdd.png>)

In total, the number of customers that lie in the set $C$ is

$$
21+42=63
$$

Of these $63$ customers, $21$ lie inside $T$.

![](<../Source/12.3.1. Conditional Probabilities From Venn Diagrams/Images/bc0dcdddd07e242e3c08fe63350fe7bf.png>)

Since each outcome is equally likely, $P(T \mid C)$ can be interpreted as the proportion of $T$ that lies inside $C$, relative to the size of $C$. Therefore, we have

$$
P(T \mid C) = \dfrac{21}{63} = \dfrac13
$$

## Computing a Conditional Probability Involving the Complement of an Event Using a Venn Diagram

**Example:** Consider the Venn diagram below. The set $A$ represents all of the students in a school that play sports, and $G$ represents all of the students in the same school that play computer games. Find the probability that a randomly selected student who doesn't play computer games plays sports.

![](<../Source/12.3.1. Conditional Probabilities From Venn Diagrams/Images/33262231f5da59a44182d702a624f999.png>)

**Explanation**

We wish to compute the probability that a student plays sports *given that* they don't play computer games. Therefore, we need to compute $P(A \mid G')$.

To compute $P(A \mid G')$, we need to ask, "what is the probability of $A$ occurring given that $G'$ has occurred?"

Since we are assuming that $G'$ has occurred, we only consider the students inside $G'$.

![](<../Source/12.3.1. Conditional Probabilities From Venn Diagrams/Images/e30f25f267a4beb66f53ce50244a04dd.png>)

In total, the number of students in the set $G'$ is

$$
80+20=100
$$

Of these $100$ students, $80$ lie inside $A$.

![](<../Source/12.3.1. Conditional Probabilities From Venn Diagrams/Images/b47cd9a3fe0a691dd19f446e1237b0e2.png>)

Since each outcome is equally likely, $P(A \mid G')$ can be interpreted as the proportion of $A$ that lies inside $G'$, relative to the size of $G.'$ Therefore, we have

$$
P(A \mid G') = \dfrac{80}{100} = \dfrac45
$$
