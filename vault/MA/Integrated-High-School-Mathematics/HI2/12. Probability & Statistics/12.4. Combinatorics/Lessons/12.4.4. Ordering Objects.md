## Introduction

Given a collection of $n$ objects, there are $n$! ways to arrange those objects in a sequence.

- For example, suppose we have $n=2$ objects: $A$ and $B$. Then there are $2!=2 \cdot 1 = 2$ arrangements:
$\underbrace{A,B}_{1\text{st way}}, \quad \underbrace{B,A}_{2\text{nd way}}$
- Likewise, suppose we have $n=3$ objects: $A$, $B$, and $C$. Then there are $3!=3 \cdot 2 \cdot 1 = 6$ arrangements:
$\underbrace{A,B,C}_{1\text{st way}},\quad \underbrace{A,C,B}_{2\text{nd way}}, \quad \underbrace{B,A,C}_{3\text{rd way}}, \quad \underbrace{B,C,A}_{4\text{th way}}, \quad \underbrace{C,A,B}_{5\text{th way}},\quad \underbrace{C,B,A}_{6\text{th way}}$

This pattern extends to any number of objects.

---

<a id="calculating-the-number-of-ways-that-n-objects-can-be-arranged"></a>
## Calculating the Number of Ways That N Objects Can Be Arranged

**Example:** Suppose there are five books on a bookshelf. How many possible ways can we arrange them?

**Explanation**

There are $n=5$ books, so the number of possible arrangements is

$$
\begin{aligned}
5! &= 5 \cdot 4 \cdot 3 \cdot 2 \cdot 1 \\
&= 120
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, there are $120$ ways of arranging the $5$ books.

## Permutations

The mathematical name for an arrangement is a **permutation**. With a permutation, *the order matters*. This means that for the letters $A,B,C,D,E$, the permutation
$A,B,C,D,E$
is different from
$E,D,C,B,A$.

---

<a id="calculating-permutations"></a>
## Calculating Permutations

**Example:** How many permutations are there of the letters in the word "MUSICAL"?

**Explanation**

In the word "MUSICAL" there are a total of $7$ letters with no repeat letters, and so there are $7$! ways of arranging them:

$$
\begin{aligned}
7! &= 7 \cdot 6 \cdot 5 \cdot 4 \cdot 3 \cdot 2 \cdot 1 \\
&= 5040
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, there are $5\,040$ possible arrangements of the letters of the word "MUSICAL."
