## Introduction

We can combine vertical reflections, stretches, and shifts to get new transformed trigonometric curves. For example, using transformations, let's draw the graph of the curve

$$
y = -2 \cos x - 1
$$

To plot this curve, we follow these steps:

- Start with the graph of $y=\cos{x}$.

![](<../Source/11.7.10. Graphing Reflections of Trigonometric Functions- Three or More Transformations/Images/7e15db7c2b5c8e8a2179230683288c2c.png>)

- Reflect it in the $x$-axis to give $y=-\cos x$.

![](<../Source/11.7.10. Graphing Reflections of Trigonometric Functions- Three or More Transformations/Images/e31ec2c9b17c18407b111e4f7c9c261e.png>)

- Vertically stretch the result by a scale factor of $2$ to give $y=-2\cos x$.

![](<../Source/11.7.10. Graphing Reflections of Trigonometric Functions- Three or More Transformations/Images/f679bd83fd396d8f01f00fa5b71ee7aa.png>)

- Finally, shift the result down by $1$ unit to give $y=-2\cos x - 1$.

![](<../Source/11.7.10. Graphing Reflections of Trigonometric Functions- Three or More Transformations/Images/4a02c574740f070bc834ef07986fc37e.png>)

## Identifying the Reflection, Amplification, and Shifting of a Sine Curve

**Example:** Identify the equation that generated the graph above.

![](<../Source/11.7.10. Graphing Reflections of Trigonometric Functions- Three or More Transformations/Images/bd5f1fe03559ca7f2dcdb446c1cc0ce7.png>)

**Explanation**

![](<../Source/11.7.10. Graphing Reflections of Trigonometric Functions- Three or More Transformations/Images/e70fdea433583a8dbfbb471289358de3.png>)

From the graph, we see that the maximum value is

$$
y_{\textrm{max}} = 4
$$

and the minimum value is

$$
y_{\textrm{min}} =-2
$$

Therefore, the amplitude is

$$
A = \dfrac{y_{\textrm{max}} - y_{\textrm{min}}}{2} = \dfrac{4-(-2)}{2} = 3
$$

Therefore, to plot the given curve, we follow these steps:

- Start with the graph of $y=\sin x$.
- Reflect it in the $x$-axis to give $y=-\sin x$.
- Vertically stretch it by a scale factor of $3$ to give $y=-3\sin x$.
- Finally, translate it by $1$ unit **up** to give $y=-3\sin x+1$.

Therefore, the given curve is the graph of

$$
y=-3\sin x+1
$$

## Identifying the Reflection, Amplification, and Shifting of a Cosine Curve

**Example:** Identify the equation that generated the graph above.

![](<../Source/11.7.10. Graphing Reflections of Trigonometric Functions- Three or More Transformations/Images/1ab3802210d9f5ad0b1b521a577e8ddc.png>)

**Explanation**

![](<../Source/11.7.10. Graphing Reflections of Trigonometric Functions- Three or More Transformations/Images/ed08af494bb4673c1d1475579cf0bdbb.png>)

From the graph, we see that the maximum value is

$$
y_{\textrm{max}} = 4
$$

and the minimum value is

$$
y_{\textrm{min}} = 0
$$

Therefore, the amplitude is

$$
A = \dfrac{y_{\textrm{max}} - y_{\textrm{min}}}{2} = \dfrac{4-0}{2} = 2
$$

Therefore, to plot the given curve, we follow these steps:

- Start with the graph of $y=\cos x$.
- Reflect it in the $x$-axis to give $y=-\cos x$.
- Vertically stretch it by a scale factor of $2$ to give $y=-2\cos x$
- Finally, translate it by $2$ units **up** to give $y=-2\cos x+2$

Therefore, the given curve is the graph of

$$
y=-2\cos x+2
$$
