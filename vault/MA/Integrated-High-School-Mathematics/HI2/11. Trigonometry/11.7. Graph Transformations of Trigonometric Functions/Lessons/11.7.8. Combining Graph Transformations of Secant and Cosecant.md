## Introduction

We can combine vertical and horizontal shifts and stretches to get new transformed secant and cosecant curves. For example, let's draw the graph of the curve

$$
y = 2 \sec\left(x- \dfrac{\pi}{8}\right) + 1
$$

To plot the given curve, we follow these steps:

- Start with the graph of $y=\sec{x}$.

![](<../Source/11.7.8. Combining Graph Transformations of Secant and Cosecant/Images/ec9b2015645b95348d99d8e9941661ba.png>)

- Then, stretch it parallel to the $y$-axis by a stretch factor of $2$ to give $y=2\sec{x}$.

![](<../Source/11.7.8. Combining Graph Transformations of Secant and Cosecant/Images/33f3f0e47172000728c81f072ae0e780.png>)

- Next, shift to the **right** by $\dfrac{\pi}{8}$ units to give $y=2\sec\left(x - \dfrac{\pi}{8}\right)$.

![](<../Source/11.7.8. Combining Graph Transformations of Secant and Cosecant/Images/00ceda514df4f31d3d5a827b5d40f3b9.png>)

- Finally, shift it **up** by $1$ unit to give $y=2\sec\left(x - \dfrac{\pi}{8}\right) + 1$.

![](<../Source/11.7.8. Combining Graph Transformations of Secant and Cosecant/Images/4a82346b110fb600bb6d9336222e2c69.png>)

## Identifying the Vertical Shift of a Transformed Secant or Cosecant Curve

**Example:** The graph below shows the function $y=\csc{\left(2x\right)}+D$, where $D$ is a constant. What is the value of $D$?

![](<../Source/11.7.8. Combining Graph Transformations of Secant and Cosecant/Images/933477a9e82e9ce55df6624d5d3f7581.png>)

**Explanation**

The constant $D$ represents a vertical shift of the function

$$
y=\csc\left(2x\right)
$$

Since the local minimum of cosecant is $y=1$, from the graph we see that the given curve is shifted **down** by $1$ unit. So, the equation of the curve is

$$
y=\csc\left(2x\right)-1
$$

Therefore, $D =-1$.

## Identifying the Horizontal Shift of a Transformed Secant or Cosecant Curve

**Example:** The graph above shows a section of the function $y=\dfrac 1 2\sec{(x+C)}$, where $C$ is a constant. Given that $-\pi < C < \pi$, what is the value of $C$?

![](<../Source/11.7.8. Combining Graph Transformations of Secant and Cosecant/Images/72cd0a91625e1b6278119c19a59c36b5.png>)

**Explanation**

The constant $C$ represents a horizontal shift of the function

$$
y=\dfrac 1 2\sec x
$$

From the graph, we see that the given curve is a secant graph that's been shifted **right** by $1$ unit. So, the equation of the curve is

$$
y=\dfrac 1 2\sec\left(x-1\right)
$$

Therefore, $C =-1$.

## Identifying the Horizontal Stretch of a Transformed Secant or Cosecant Curve

**Example:** The graph above shows a section of the function $y=\csc{\left(Bx-\dfrac{\pi}{4}\right)}$, where $B$ is a constant. What is the value of $B$?

![](<../Source/11.7.8. Combining Graph Transformations of Secant and Cosecant/Images/d4215af119e817864f364427ce8c3381.png>)

**Explanation**

The period of the function is

$$
T = \frac{27\pi}{4} -\frac{3\pi}{4} = \frac{24\pi}{4} = 6\pi
$$

The formula for the period of the function

$$
y=A\csc(Bx + C) + D
$$

is given by

$$
T = \dfrac{2\pi}{B}
$$

Applying the formula for $T$ gives

$$
6\pi = \dfrac{2\pi}{B} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad B = \dfrac{2\pi}{6\pi} = \dfrac 1 3
$$

So

$$
B=\dfrac 1 3
$$

and therefore, the equation of the function is

$$
y=\csc\left(\dfrac x 3-\dfrac\pi 4\right)
$$

## Identifying the Horizontal Stretch and Vertical Shift of a Transformed Secant or Cosecant Curve

**Example:** The graph above shows the function $y=\sec{(Bx)}+D$, where $B$ and $D$ are constants. What is $B+D$?

![](<../Source/11.7.8. Combining Graph Transformations of Secant and Cosecant/Images/8b8bf5f8b27be8a4a42104790c0e1ad6.png>)

**Explanation**

The period of the function is

$$
T = \dfrac{3\pi}4 - \left(-\dfrac \pi 4\right) = \pi
$$

The formula for the period of the function

$$
y=A\sec(Bx + C) + D
$$

is given by

$$
T = \dfrac{2\pi}{B}
$$

Applying the formula of the period, we get

$$
\pi = \dfrac{2\pi}{B}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad B = \dfrac{2\pi}{\pi} = 2
$$

The parameter $D$ represents the vertical shift of the secant function. Since the local minimum of secant is $y=1$, we see that the function has been translated by $2$ units **up**. Therefore, $D=2$.

So, we have

$$
B+D = 2+2 = 4
$$

Finally, the equation of the curve is

$$
y=\sec\left(2x\right)+2
$$

## Identifying the Horizontal Shift of a Horizontally Stretched Secant or Cosecant Curve

**Example:** The graph above shows the function $y=\sec{\left(2x+C\right)}$, where $C$ is a constant. Given that $-\pi < C < \pi$, what is the value of $C$?

![](<../Source/11.7.8. Combining Graph Transformations of Secant and Cosecant/Images/b9701f3385d0607674c049539ef38542.png>)

**Explanation**

The parameter $C$ represents a horizontal shift of the function.

First, we rewrite the function by factoring out the horizontal stretch factor, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
y &= \sec (2x + C) \\
&= \sec (2(x + \frac{C}{2}))
\end{aligned}
$$

From the graph, we see that the graph of secant has been shifted by

$$
\dfrac 1 2
$$

units to the left. Therefore,

$$
\dfrac C 2 = \dfrac{1}{2}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad C = 1
$$
