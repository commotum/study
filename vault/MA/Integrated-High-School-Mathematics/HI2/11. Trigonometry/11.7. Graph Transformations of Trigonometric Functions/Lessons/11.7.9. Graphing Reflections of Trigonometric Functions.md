## Introduction

We can combine vertical reflections, stretches, and shifts to get new transformed trigonometric curves. For example, let's draw the graph of the curve

$$
y = -2 \cos x - 1
$$

To plot the given curve, we follow these steps:

- Start with the graph of $y=\cos{x}$.

![](<../Source/11.7.9. Graphing Reflections of Trigonometric Functions/Images/df2dad9435c330d21d7ddd5426746625.png>)

- Then, stretch it parallel to the $y$-axis by a stretch factor of $2$ to give $y=2\cos{x}$.

![](<../Source/11.7.9. Graphing Reflections of Trigonometric Functions/Images/5d09d7aea2d115dc6ae561284ed0d3f0.png>)

- Next, reflect it over the $x$-axis to give $y=-2\cos x$.

![](<../Source/11.7.9. Graphing Reflections of Trigonometric Functions/Images/e0774be58eb5149df999ff3f77402837.png>)

- Finally, shift it **down** by $1$ unit to give $y=-2\cos x - 1$.

![](<../Source/11.7.9. Graphing Reflections of Trigonometric Functions/Images/efa7758f61d1bb770c17a9c1375146ed.png>)

## Identifying the Reflection of a Sine Curve Over the Horizontal Axis

**Example:** The graph above represents a transformed sine curve. What is the equation of the graph?

![](<../Source/11.7.9. Graphing Reflections of Trigonometric Functions/Images/89614702470515495bff6e9a7d2ee710.png>)

**Explanation**

![](<../Source/11.7.9. Graphing Reflections of Trigonometric Functions/Images/da96b4325b5cb99127a208386e5d33a5.png>)

To plot the given curve, we follow these steps:

- Start with the graph of $y=\sin x$.
- Then, reflect it in the $x$-axis to give $y=-\sin x$.
- Finally, stretch it parallel to the $y$-axis by a stretch factor of $8$ to give $y=-8\sin x$.

Therefore, the given curve is the graph of $y=-8\sin x$.

## Identifying the Reflection of a Sine or Cosine Curve Over the Vertical Axis

**Example:** The graph above shows the function $y=2\sin(kx)$. What is the value of $k$?

![](<../Source/11.7.9. Graphing Reflections of Trigonometric Functions/Images/643d638672fe9059c6151e7469aa87c1.png>)

**Explanation**

![](<../Source/11.7.9. Graphing Reflections of Trigonometric Functions/Images/eb76dc56d2a6336bf24e289a588ed2db.png>)

To plot the given curve, we follow these steps:

- Start with the graph of $y=\sin x$.
- Then, reflect it across the $y$-axis.
- Finally, stretch it parallel to the $y$-axis by a stretch factor of $2$.

Therefore, the given curve is the graph of

$$
y=2\sin(-x)
$$

Hence, $k=-1$.

Notice that, because the sine function is odd, the $y$-axis reflection has the same effect as the $x$-axis reflection, i.e.,

$$
2\sin(-x) = -2\sin x
$$

So the graph could also be viewed as $y=-2\sin x$.

**Example:** The graph above represents a transformed cosine curve. What is the equation of the graph?

![](<../Source/11.7.9. Graphing Reflections of Trigonometric Functions/Images/8aaec5b66acc1171f90f49727710df5e.png>)

**Explanation**

![](<../Source/11.7.9. Graphing Reflections of Trigonometric Functions/Images/7f231c9d8d6e80fb1a38e120cf2a63bc.png>)

To plot the given curve, we follow these steps:

- Start with the graph of $y=\cos x$.
- Then, reflect it in the $x$-axis to give $y=-\cos x$.
- Finally, translate it by $2$ units **up** to give $y=-\cos x+2$.

Therefore, the given curve is the graph of

$$
y=-\cos x+2
$$
