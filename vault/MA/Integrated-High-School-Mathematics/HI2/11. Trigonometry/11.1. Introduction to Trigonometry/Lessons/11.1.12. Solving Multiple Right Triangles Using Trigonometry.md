## Introduction

Suppose that we are given a non-right triangle, like the one in the figure below. How do we calculate $HB$?

![](<../Source/11.1.12. Solving Multiple Right Triangles Using Trigonometry/Images/fe411854bac91a0f2a076f3f9727da1f.png>)

We start by noting that the height $\overline{CH}$ divides the triangle into two right triangles with a common side. To solve the triangle on the right, we can use information about the triangle on the left.

First, we find the height $CH$. The sine ratio for the left triangle gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sin (50^{∘}) &= \frac{CH}{7} \\
CH &= 7\sin (50^{∘}) \\
CH &= 7 \cdot (0.767044) \\
CH &= 5.362308
\end{aligned}
$$

We write the intermediate steps in the calculation to $6$ decimal places, to avoid rounding error.

Now, we can calculate $HB$ by applying the tangent ratio to the triangle on the right:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\tan (57^{∘}) &= \frac{HB}{CH} \\
\tan (57^{∘}) &= \frac{HB}{5.362308} \\
HB &= \tan (57^{∘}) \cdot (5.362308) \\
HB &= (1.539864) \cdot (5.362308) \\
HB &= 8.257230 \\
HB &= 8.26
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding the Measure of a Angle in a Triangle by Splitting into Two Right Triangles

**Example:** Find the value of $\theta$ rounded to the nearest tenth.

![](<../Source/11.1.12. Solving Multiple Right Triangles Using Trigonometry/Images/3d7e8726f4ac0c027425c537e7a16753.png>)

**Explanation**

First, consider the triangle $\triangle LKH$. Using the sine ratio, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{KH}{KL} &= \sin L \\
\frac{KH}{8} &= \sin 35^{∘} \\
KH &= 8 \cdot \sin 35^{∘} \\
KH &= 4.5886
\end{aligned}
$$

rounded to four decimal places.

Now, we consider $\triangle KMH$. We have

$$
\begin{aligned}
\tan θ &= \frac{KH}{HM} \\
\tan θ &= \frac{4.5886}{10} \\
\tan θ &= 0.4589 \\
θ &= \arctan (0.4589) \\
θ &= 24.6504^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\theta\approx 24.7^\circ
$$

rounded to the nearest tenth.

## Determining the Length of a Line Segment Using Trigonometry

**Example:** Given that $ABCD$ is a rectangle, what is the measure of $\overline{AD}$ to the nearest integer?

![](<../Source/11.1.12. Solving Multiple Right Triangles Using Trigonometry/Images/ce095f59b44c2058cad5044757f697d4.png>)

**Explanation**

Since $ABCD$ is the rectangle, $BA=CD=10$ and

$$
m\angle CDE=m\angle BAE=90^\circ
$$

![](<../Source/11.1.12. Solving Multiple Right Triangles Using Trigonometry/Images/cb09d2f994ae95c647faf1b3e0151541.png>)

First, consider the triangle $\triangle ABE$. Using the tangent ratio, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{AE}{BA} &= \tan B \\
\frac{AE}{10} &= \tan 60^{∘} \\
AE &= 10\tan 60^{∘} \\
AE &= 17.321
\end{aligned}
$$

rounded to three decimal places.

Considering $\triangle CDE$, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{ED}{CD} &= \tan C \\
\frac{ED}{10} &= \tan 43^{∘} \\
ED &= 10\tan 43^{∘} \\
ED &= 9.325
\end{aligned}
$$

rounded to three decimal places.

Finally.

$$
\begin{aligned}
AD &= AE - ED \\
&= 17.321 - 9.325 \\
&= 7.996
\end{aligned}
$$

Hence,

$$
AD \approx 8
$$

rounded to the nearest integer.

## Finding the Area of a Triangle by Splitting it into Two Right Triangles

**Example:** Find the area of the triangle $\triangle PQR$. Round the answer to the nearest integer.

![](<../Source/11.1.12. Solving Multiple Right Triangles Using Trigonometry/Images/6e3d0d2439fbdb9af771ecfeda46a834.png>)

**Explanation**

First, consider the triangle $\triangle PQH$. Using the tangent ratio, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{PH}{QH} &= \tan ∠Q \\
\frac{PH}{8} &= \tan (40^{∘}) \\
PH &= 8\tan (40^{∘}) \\
&= PH \mid \approx 6.713
\end{aligned}
$$

rounded to three decimal places.

Now, consider $\triangle QHR$. We have

$$
\begin{aligned}
\tan R &= \frac{QH}{HR} \\
\tan (47^{∘}) &= \frac{8}{HR} \\
\tan (47^{∘}) \cdot HR &= 8 \\
HR &= (8)/(\tan (47^{∘})) \\
&= HR \mid \approx 7.460
\end{aligned}
$$

rounded to three decimal places.

Finally,

$$
\begin{aligned}
PR &= PH + HR \\
&\approx 6.713 + 7.460 \\
&= 14.173
\end{aligned}
$$

We now know the base ($PR$) and height ($QH$) of the triangle $\triangle{PRQ}$. Hence, the area of $\triangle{PRQ}$ is

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= \frac{PR \cdot QH}{2} \\
&\approx \frac{14.173 \cdot 8}{2} \\
&\approx 56.692
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\mathcal A \approx 57
$$

rounded to the nearest integer.
