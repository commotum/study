## Introduction

Consider an absolute value graph that has undergone the following transformations:

- vertical stretch with scale factor $a$
- translation of $h$ units right and $k$ units up

In general, we can express the equation of this graph as

$$
y= a \mid x-h \mid +k
$$

Note that if $a$ is negative, the graph is reflected in the $x$-axis.

For example, to plot the graph of

$$
y=-3 \mid x-2 \mid +1
$$

we proceed as follows:

- Take the graph of $y=-\mid x \mid$.
- Stretch it vertically by a scale factor of $3$ to get the graph of $y=-3 \mid x \mid$.

![](<../Source/5.1.4. Combining Transformations of Absolute Value Graphs/Images/b085fc180ba191ec3821e79436954e29.png>)

- Translate the result by $2$ units to the *right* and $1$ unit *up* to get the graph of $y=-3 \mid x-2 \mid +1$.

![](<../Source/5.1.4. Combining Transformations of Absolute Value Graphs/Images/ff9df1e92ae5290b062260d409289b48.png>)

## Graphing a Scaled Absolute Value Function With a Translation

**Example:** Sketch the graph of $y = \mid 2x-3 \mid$.

**Explanation**

First, let's re-write the equation using the properties of the absolute value function:

$$
\begin{aligned}
y &= \mid 2x - 3 \mid \\
&= \mid 2(x - \frac{3}{2}) \mid \\
&=\begin{vmatrix}2 & \cdot & x - \frac{3}{2}\end{vmatrix} \\
&= 2 \mid x - \frac{3}{2} \mid
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, to get the graph of

$$
y=2\left|x-\dfrac 3 2\right|
$$

we proceed as follows:

- Take the graph of $y= \mid x \mid$.
- Stretch it vertically by a scale factor of $2$ to get the graph of $y=2 \mid x \mid$.

![](<../Source/5.1.4. Combining Transformations of Absolute Value Graphs/Images/059b5ba1ac191c81c61ef717b1776bb5.png>)

- Translate the result by $\dfrac 3 2$ units to the right to get the graph of $y=2\left|x-\dfrac 3 2\right|$.

![](<../Source/5.1.4. Combining Transformations of Absolute Value Graphs/Images/fa543848987726204a12ce3d9e8f292e.png>)

## Finding the Equation of a Scaled Absolute Value Function With a Translation

**Example:** Given the graph of $y=a \mid x \mid +b$ below, find the value of $a+b$.

![](<../Source/5.1.4. Combining Transformations of Absolute Value Graphs/Images/a46365496382c7bdff2e9a81d3f40079.png>)

**Explanation**

Remember that

$$
y= a \mid x \mid +b
$$

is a scaled absolute value graph

$$
y=a \mid x \mid
$$

that is translated $b$ units up.

Here, the graph is moved down by $1$ unit. This corresponds to $b = -1$. So, we have

$$
y=a \mid x \mid -1
$$

To find the value of $a$, we can pick a point on the curve and substitute it into the equation. Notice that the curve passes through the point $(1,2)$, so we can substitute $x=1$ and $y=2$ into the equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
y &= a\begin{vmatrix}x & -1 \\ 2|= a \cdot & 1\end{vmatrix}- 1 \\
2 &= a \cdot 1 - 1 \\
2 &= a - 1 \\
a &= 3
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the equation of the curve is

$$
y=3 \mid x \mid -1
$$

Finally, we have

$$
a + b = 3 + (-1) = 2
$$
