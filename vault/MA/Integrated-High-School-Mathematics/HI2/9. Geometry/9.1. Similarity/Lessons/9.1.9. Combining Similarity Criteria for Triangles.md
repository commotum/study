## Introduction

The three similarity criteria for triangles are often needed when proving geometric results. Therefore, it's essential to identify which criterion is being applied in a particular situation.

Let's start by briefly reviewing the three criteria that we have learned.

- The **angle-angle (AA)** criterion: If two angles of one triangle are congruent to two angles of another triangle, then the two triangles are similar.

![](<../Source/9.1.9. Combining Similarity Criteria for Triangles/Images/cfdee203433a16e2fdba0711f3bc356b.png>)

- The **side-side-side (SSS)** criterion: Two triangles are similar if the ratios of the lengths of their corresponding sides are equal.

![](<../Source/9.1.9. Combining Similarity Criteria for Triangles/Images/63a4e59ebbdafb4f13436870da47bdd4.png>)

- The **side-angle-side (SAS)** criterion: Two triangles are similar if the ratio of the lengths of two pairs of corresponding sides is the same, and the angles included by these sides are congruent.

![](<../Source/9.1.9. Combining Similarity Criteria for Triangles/Images/f08c8596427da61e4017a44356aa703f.png>)

Let's now get some practice at correctly identifying these criteria.

## Identifying Similar Triangles

**Example:** By which similarity criterion does it follow that $\triangle BOA\sim \triangle MON$?

![](<../Source/9.1.9. Combining Similarity Criteria for Triangles/Images/3a77979a6132ebe059b68eef9698eb3f.png>)

*(Note: Figures not drawn to scale)*

**Explanation**

First, let's simplify our diagram, keeping only the relevant information:

![](<../Source/9.1.9. Combining Similarity Criteria for Triangles/Images/e2f4e4cb60095f009601125754f567ec.png>)

Now, let's recall the SAS similarity criterion:

> *Two triangles are similar if the ratio of the lengths of two pairs of corresponding sides is the same and the angles included by these sides are congruent.*

Notice that $\triangle BOA$ and $\triangle MON$ have a pair of congruent angles:

$$
\angle BOA \cong \angle MON
$$

Let's list the sides that enclose the congruent angle for each triangle, from smallest to largest:

$$
\underbrace{({\color{blue}{9}},{\color{blue}{12}})}_{\triangle BOA}, \qquad \underbrace{({\color{red}{12}},{\color{red}{16}})}_{\triangle MON}
$$

Notice that the ratios of these lengths are in proportion:

$$
\dfrac{\color{blue}9}{\color{red}12} = \dfrac{\color{blue}12}{\color{red}16} = \dfrac34
$$

Therefore, $\triangle BOA \sim \triangle MON$ by the SAS criterion.

## Similar Triangles With Common Sides and Angles

It's important that we can identify similar triangles that share common sides and angles.

Consider the following diagram:

![](<../Source/9.1.9. Combining Similarity Criteria for Triangles/Images/5011676e096bd8456219e584895d3a46.png>)

We can show that $\triangle{ABD}$ and $\triangle{ACE}$ are similar as follows:

- First, notice that $\angle{A}$ is common to both triangles.
- Second, note that the ratios of the sides containing the congruent angles are equal:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{AE}{AD} &= \frac{12 + 8}{12} = \frac{5}{3} \\
\frac{AC}{AB} &= \frac{6 + 4}{6} = \frac{5}{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, $\triangle{ABD} \sim \triangle{ACE}$ by the SAS (side-angle-side) criterion.

Let's see another example.

<a id="identifying-similar-triangles-with-common-sides-and-angles"></a>
## Identifying Similar Triangles With Common Sides and Angles

**Example:** By which similarity criterion does it follow that $\triangle PNM \sim \triangle PMD$?

![](<../Source/9.1.9. Combining Similarity Criteria for Triangles/Images/111d0efb81ebe1f2e1990f661143a4d1.png>)

**Explanation**

First, let's recall the AA similarity criterion:

> *If two angles of one triangle are congruent to two angles of another triangle, then these triangles are similar.*

Notice that the triangles $\triangle PNM$ and $\triangle PMD$ have a common angle $\angle P$.
Also,

$$
m\angle PNM= m\angle PMD
$$

![](<../Source/9.1.9. Combining Similarity Criteria for Triangles/Images/1e7a0923478c0cc4becb7b44d82c6bab.png>)

Hence,
$\triangle PNM \sim \triangle PMD$ by the AA criterion.

<a id="diagrams-containing-many-triangles"></a>
## Diagrams Containing Many Triangles

**Example:** Which of the following statements must be true:

![](<../Source/9.1.9. Combining Similarity Criteria for Triangles/Images/e2145525f1469164a97e0776b6f16ca8.png>)

1. $\triangle ABC \sim \triangle AKL$
2. $\triangle ABC \sim \triangle BLC$
3. $\triangle ABC \sim \triangle BKL$

**Explanation**

Let's compare each of the given triangles to $\triangle{ABC}{:}$

- First, let's analyze whether $\triangle{ABC} \sim \triangle{AKL}$ is true:![](<../Source/9.1.9. Combining Similarity Criteria for Triangles/Images/f7909f9488c8dcb1d48711e77d6886f5.png>)
There is no similarity criterion we can apply to these triangles. Therefore, we **cannot** conclude that $\triangle ABC \sim \triangle AKL$.

- Next, let's analyze whether $\triangle{ABC} \sim \triangle{BLC}$ is true:![](<../Source/9.1.9. Combining Similarity Criteria for Triangles/Images/e50c45461b2d1926685f781a5077c261.png>)
Note that
$\angle{C} \cong \angle{C}, \qquad \dfrac{6}{4} = \dfrac{9}{6} = \dfrac{3}{2}$.
Therefore, $\triangle{ABC} \sim \triangle{BLC}$ must be true by the SAS criterion.

- Finally, let's analyze whether $\triangle{ABC} \sim \triangle{BKL}{:}$![](<../Source/9.1.9. Combining Similarity Criteria for Triangles/Images/728c5ebdb054108347f386fa5adffa7e.png>)
Note that
$\angle{A} \cong \angle{LBK}, \qquad \angle{C} \cong \angle{KLB}$.
Therefore, $\triangle{ABC} \sim \triangle{BKL}$ must be true by the AA criterion.

Therefore, the correct answer is "II and III only."
