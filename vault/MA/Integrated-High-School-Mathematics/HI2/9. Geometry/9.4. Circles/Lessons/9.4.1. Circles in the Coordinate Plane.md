## Introduction

Suppose that the points $A$ and $B$ have coordinates $(-1,3)$ and $(3,0)$, and are the endpoints of the diameter of a circle, shown below.

![](<../Source/9.4.1. Circles in the Coordinate Plane/Images/5dcc0fa626ce28c1e3478e1cb0e92987.png>)

Since $\overline{AB}$ is a diameter of the circle, the center of the circle is the midpoint of $\overline{AB}$. So, we can find the coordinates of the center using the midpoint formula:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(h, k) &= (\frac{x_{1} + x_{2}}{2}, \frac{y_{1} + y_{2}}{2}) \\
&= (\frac{3 - 1}{2}, \frac{0 + 3}{2}) \\
&= (\frac{2}{2}, \frac{3}{2}) \\
&= (1, 1.5)
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the coordinates of the center of the circle are $(1,1.5)$.

## The Radius of a Circle

Suppose that the points $(-2,1)$ and $(4,1)$ lie on a diameter of a circle. What is the radius of the circle?

![](<../Source/9.4.1. Circles in the Coordinate Plane/Images/a212ed6cde29d4934093974411400c80.png>)

We can find the length of the diameter using the distance formula:

$$
\begin{aligned}
d &= \sqrt{(x_{1} - x_{2})^{2} + (y_{1} - y_{2})^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{(-2 - 4)^{2} + (1 - 1)^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{(-6)^{2}} \\
&= 6
\end{aligned}
$$

The radius is half the diameter:

$$
r = \dfrac{d}{2} = \dfrac{6}{2}=3
$$

---

<a id="finding-the-radius-of-a-circle-on-the-coordinate-plane-using-the-distance-formula"></a>
## Finding the Radius of a Circle on the Coordinate Plane Using the Distance Formula

**Example:** The points $A$ and $B$ have coordinates $(-5,3)$ and $(-3,7)$, respectively, and the segment $\overline{AB}$ is a diameter of a circle. Find the radius of the circle.

**Explanation**

The diameter can be calculated using the distance formula:

$$
\begin{aligned}
d &= \sqrt{(x_{1} - x_{2})^{2} + (y_{1} - y_{2})^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{(-5 - (-3))^{2} + (3 - 7)^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{(-2)^{2} + (-4)^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{4 + 16} \\
&= \sqrt{20} \\
&= 2\sqrt{5}
\end{aligned}
$$

The radius is half the diameter:

$$
r = \dfrac{d}{2} = \dfrac{2\sqrt{5}}{2} = \sqrt{5}
$$

## Determining the Center and Radius of a Circle on the Coordinate Plane

**Example:** The segment $\overline{CD}$ is the diameter of a circle where the points $C$ and $D$ have coordinates $(7a,6a)$ and $(a,-2a)$, respectively, with $a>0$. Find the coordinates of the center and the radius of the circle.

**Explanation**

The center of the circle is located at the midpoint of the diameter. So, we can calculate the coordinates of the center using the midpoint formula:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(h, k) &= (\frac{x_{1} + x_{2}}{2}, \frac{y_{1} + y_{2}}{2}) \\
&= (\frac{7a + a}{2}, (6a + (-2a))/(2)) \\
&= (\frac{8a}{2}, \frac{4a}{2}) \\
&= (4a, 2a)
\end{aligned}
$$

The radius can be found by calculating the distance between the center $(4a, 2a)$ and a point on the circle, such as $C(7a,6a)$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
r &= \sqrt{(x_{1} - x_{2})^{2} + (y_{1} - y_{2})^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{(4a - 7a)^{2} + (2a - 6a)^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{(-3a)^{2} + (-4a)^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{9a^{2} + 16a^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{25a^{2}} \\
&= 5 \mid a \mid
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, since $a>0$, we have that

$$
r = 5\vert a \vert = 5a
$$

Therefore, the center of the circle is at $(4a, 2a)$ and the radius is $5a$.
