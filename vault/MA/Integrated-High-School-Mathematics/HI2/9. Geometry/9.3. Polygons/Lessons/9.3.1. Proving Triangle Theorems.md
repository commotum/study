## Introduction

In this lesson, we'll learn how to prove some elementary triangle theorems.

Consider the triangle $\triangle{ABC}$ below.

![](<../Source/9.3.1. Proving Triangle Theorems/Images/f49e1e7e670b6a39a6edb6165f02d30f.png>)

Suppose we wish to prove the following statement:

> *The sum of the measures of the interior angles of $\triangle{ABC}$ equals $180^\circ$.*

In addition to the definitions, postulates, and theorems we've used previously, we'll need the following definition of a **straight angle**.

> *An angle is straight if its measure equals $180^\circ$.*

Let's now prove this result.

---

<a id="proving-the-sum-of-interior-angles-theorem"></a>
## Proving the Sum of Interior Angles Theorem

![](<../Source/9.3.1. Proving Triangle Theorems/Images/b223c038701adb5ad33350c981ae9a40.png>)

We wish to prove the following statement:

> *The sum of the measures of the interior angles of $\triangle{ABC}$ equals $180^\circ$.*

The key to unlocking this proof is to *construct* a line parallel to $\overline{AB}$ passing through $C$, and then apply the alternate interior angles theorem twice.

We'll now go through the step-by-step proof of this statement.

- **Step 1**: We start by constructing a line $\overset{\longleftrightarrow}{DE}$ that passes through the point $C$ and is parallel to $\overline{AB}$.

![](<../Source/9.3.1. Proving Triangle Theorems/Images/d921810aad252588f3bf3cc1cb5ad2dc.png>)

- **Step 2**: Since $\overline{AB}$ and $\overset{\longleftrightarrow}{DE}$ are parallel, $\angle{ACD}$ and $\angle{A}$ are congruent by the *alternate interior angles theorem*.

![](<../Source/9.3.1. Proving Triangle Theorems/Images/c90763883d2832485486552f013034da.png>)

- **Step 3**: Since $\angle{ACD}\cong \angle{A}$, we have
$m\angle{ACD} = m\angle{A}$
by the *definition of congruent angles.*
- **Step 4**: Since $\overline{AB}$ and $\overset{\longleftrightarrow}{DE}$ are parallel, $\angle{BCE}$ and $\angle{B}$ are congruent by the *alternate interior angles theorem*.

![](<../Source/9.3.1. Proving Triangle Theorems/Images/45c004a66a989585a917f3420d413be6.png>)

- **Step 5**: Since $\angle{BCE} \cong \angle{B}$, we have
$m\angle{BCE} = m\angle{B}$
by the *definition of congruent angles*.
- **Step 6**: The segments $\overline{AC}$ and $\overline{BC}$ divide the straight angle $\angle{DCE}$ into three consecutive angles: $\angle{ACD}$, $\angle{ACB}$, and $\angle{BCE}$. So, by the *angle addition postulate*, we must have
$m\angle{ACD} + m\angle{ACB} + m\angle{BCE} = m\angle{DCE}$.
- **Step 7**: In steps 3 and 5, we showed that $m\angle{ACD} = m\angle{A}$ and $m\angle{BCE} = m\angle{B}$, respectively. So, by *substituting* these values into the equality in step 6, we obtain
$m\angle{A} + m\angle{ACB} + m\angle{B} = m\angle{DCE}$.
- **Step 8**: Since $\overset{\longleftrightarrow}{DE}$ is a line, $m\angle{DCE} = 180^\circ$ by the *definition of a straight angle*.
- **Step 9**: In step 8, we showed that $m\angle{DCE} = 180^\circ$, and in step 7, we showed that
$m\angle{A} + m\angle{ACB} + m\angle{B} = m\angle{DCE}$.
Therefore, by the *transitive property of equality*, we have
$m\angle{A} + m\angle{ACB} + m\angle{B} = 180^\circ$,
as required.

It's **highly recommended** that you practice reconstructing this proof. Try to do this *without* referring to these notes.

## The Sum of Interior Angles Theorem

**Example:** In the diagram above, $\overset{\longleftrightarrow}{KL} \parallel \overset{\longleftrightarrow}{MN}$.

![](<../Source/9.3.1. Proving Triangle Theorems/Images/2520df4380bc210e59b270d662a449fb.png>)

Consider the following statement:

> $m\angle{\alpha} + m\angle{\beta} + m\angle{\gamma} = 180^\circ$

Complete the proof below by finding the missing entries.

|  | Statement | Reason |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | $\overset{\longleftrightarrow}{KL} \parallel \overset{\longleftrightarrow}{MN}$ | Given |
| 2 | $\angle{MOQ} \cong \angle{\alpha}$ |  |
| 3 | $m\angle{MOQ} = m\angle{\alpha}$ | Definition of congruent angles |
| 4 | $\angle{QOP} \cong \angle{\beta}$ |  |
| 5 | $m\angle{QOP} = m\angle{\beta}$ | Definition of congruent angles |
| 6 | $\angle{PON} \cong \angle{\gamma}$ | Corresponding angles postulate |
| 7 | $m\angle{PON} = m\angle{\gamma}$ | Definition of congruent angles |
| 8 | $m\angle{MON}=180^\circ$ |  |
| 9 | $m\angle{MOQ} + m\angle{QOP} + m\angle{PON}$<br>$\qquad\qquad = m\angle{MON}$ | Angle addition postulate |
| 10 | $m\angle{\alpha} + m\angle{\beta} + m\angle{\gamma} = m\angle{MON}$ | Substitution |
| 11 | $m\angle{\alpha} + m\angle{\beta} + m\angle{\gamma} = 180^\circ$ |  |

**Explanation**

![](<../Source/9.3.1. Proving Triangle Theorems/Images/76b270de307909800d9faa152dd4492f.png>)

The correct proof is below.

|  | Statement | Reason |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | $\overset{\longleftrightarrow}{KL} \parallel \overset{\longleftrightarrow}{MN}$ | Given |
| 2 | $\angle{MOQ} \cong \angle{\alpha}$ | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm{Corresponding angles postulate}}$ |
| 3 | $m\angle{MOQ} = m\angle{\alpha}$ | Definition of congruent angles |
| 4 | $\angle{QOP} \cong \angle{\beta}$ | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm{Vertical angles theorem}}$ |
| 5 | $m\angle{QOP} = m\angle{\beta}$ | Definition of congruent angles |
| 6 | $\angle{PON} \cong \angle{\gamma}$ | Corresponding angles postulate |
| 7 | $m\angle{PON} = m\angle{\gamma}$ | Definition of congruent angles |
| 8 | $m\angle{MON}=180^\circ$ | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm{Definition of a straight angle}}$ |
| 9 | $m\angle{MOQ} + m\angle{QOP} + m\angle{PON}$<br>$\qquad\qquad = m\angle{MON}$ | Angle addition postulate |
| 10 | $m\angle{\alpha} + m\angle{\beta} + m\angle{\gamma} = m\angle{MON}$ | Substitution |
| 11 | $m\angle{\alpha} + m\angle{\beta} + m\angle{\gamma} = 180^\circ$ | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm{Transitive property of equality}}$ |

Let's examine the rows of the table with missing parts in turn.

- Consider row 2. From row 1, we have that $\overset{\longleftrightarrow}{KL}$ and $\overset{\longleftrightarrow}{MN}$ are parallel. Therefore, by the corresponding angles postulate, the corresponding angles $\angle {MOQ}$ and $\angle{\alpha}$ are congruent.
- Consider row 4. Since the lines $\overset{\longleftrightarrow}{KP}$ and $\overset{\longleftrightarrow}{LQ}$ meet at the point $O$, $\angle{QOP}$ and $\beta$ are vertical angles, so they are congruent by the vertical angles theorem.
- Consider row 8. Since $\overset{\longleftrightarrow}{MN}$ is a line, $m \angle MON = 180^\circ$ by the definition of a straight angle.
- Consider row 11. From row 8, we have $m \angle MON = 180^\circ$, and from row 10, we have
$m\angle{\alpha} + m\angle{\beta} + m\angle{\gamma} = m\angle{MON}$.
Therefore, by the transitive property of equality, we have
$m\angle{\alpha} + m\angle{\beta} + m\angle{\gamma} = m\angle{MON} = 180^\circ$.

## The Exterior Angle Theorem

We'll now discuss how to prove the exterior angle theorem. Let's begin by stating this:

> *An exterior angle in a triangle equals the sum of the measures of the two non-adjacent interior angles.*

Consider the diagram below, where the points $F$, $H$, and $I$ are colinear.

![](<../Source/9.3.1. Proving Triangle Theorems/Images/a3c6459c6f09167eaf37eae40143f85e.png>)

We wish to prove the following statement:

> $m\angle{IHG} = m\angle{F} + m\angle{G}$

Several different proofs of this result are available, and we'll study two of them.

---

<a id="proving-the-exterior-angle-theorem"></a>
## Proving the Exterior Angle Theorem

![](<../Source/9.3.1. Proving Triangle Theorems/Images/fb9fafb35d57eb3fcb7552dc8f308fcd.png>)

Our goal is to prove the following:

> $m\angle{IHG} = m\angle{F} + m\angle{G}$

To prove this theorem, we construct a ray from the point $H$ that's parallel to $\overline{FG}$, and then apply the corresponding angles postulate and alternate interior angles theorem.

We complete the proof as follows:

- **Step 1**: We start by constructing a line $\overset{\longleftrightarrow}{HJ}$ through the point $H$ and parallel to $\overset{\longleftrightarrow}{FG}$.![](<../Source/9.3.1. Proving Triangle Theorems/Images/ce4cb7b881f3e1a165bc9b1d2ad137d5.png>)
- **Step 2**: Since $\overset{\longleftrightarrow}{FG} \parallel \overset{\longleftrightarrow}{HJ}$, we have that the corresponding angles $\angle{IHJ}$ and $\angle{F}$ are congruent by the *corresponding angles postulate*.Let's add this to our diagram.![](<../Source/9.3.1. Proving Triangle Theorems/Images/7016f71f920e385db7f9dbcbb053c2e6.png>)
- **Step 3**: Since $\angle{IHJ} \cong \angle{F}$ are congruent, we have
$m\angle{IHJ} = m\angle{F}$
by the *definition of congruent angles*.
- **Step 4**: Since $\overset{\longleftrightarrow}{FG}$ and $\overset{\longleftrightarrow}{HJ}$ are parallel, we have that the alternate interior angles $\angle{JHG}$ and $\angle{G}$ are congruent by the *alternate interior angles theorem*.Let's add this to our diagram.![](<../Source/9.3.1. Proving Triangle Theorems/Images/223efd745bba18aad2c60f48ccf14d4b.png>)
- **Step 5**: Since $\angle{JHG}$ and $\angle{G}$ are congruent, we have
$m\angle{JHG} = m\angle{G}$
by the *definition of congruent angles*.
- **Step 6**: In steps 3 and 5, we showed that $m\angle{IHJ}=m\angle{F}$ and $m\angle{JHG}=m\angle{G}$, respectively. So, by the *addition principle*, we get
$m∠IHJ|= m∠F; m∠IHJ + m∠JHG|= m∠F + m∠JHG; m∠IHJ + m∠JHG|= m∠F + m∠G$.
- **Step 7**: Notice that $\overline{HJ}$ is the common side of the angles $\angle{IHJ}$ and $\angle{JHG}$. Therefore, by the *angle addition postulate*, we have that
$m\angle{IHG} = m\angle{IHJ} + m\angle{JHG}$.
- **Step 8**: In steps 7 and 6, respectively, we showed that
${\color{blue}m\angle{IHG}} = m\angle{IHJ} + m\angle{JHG}$,
and
$m\angle{IHJ} + m\angle{JHG} = {\color{blue}m\angle{F} + m\angle{G}}$.
Therefore, by the *transitive property of equality*, we get
${\color{blue}m\angle{IHG}} = {\color{blue}m\angle{F} + m\angle{G}}$,
as required.

Again, it's **highly recommended** that you practice reconstructing this proof.

## Proving the Exterior Angle Theorem

**Example:** In the diagram above, the points $B$, $C$, and $D$ are colinear.

![](<../Source/9.3.1. Proving Triangle Theorems/Images/c5eea16783d1250ce771036a419cbaa5.png>)

Consider the following statement:

> $m\angle{ACD} = m\angle{A} + m\angle{B}$

The table below provides proof of the statement. Complete the proof by filling in the empty boxes.

|  | Statement | Reason |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | $\angle{ACB}$ and $\angle{ACD}$ are supplementary |  |
| 2 | $m\angle{ACB} + m\angle{ACD} = 180^\circ$ |  |
| 3 | $m\angle{A} + m\angle{B} + m\angle{ACB} = 180^\circ$ | Interior angles of a triangle theorem |
| 4 | $180^\circ = m\angle{A} + m\angle{B} + m\angle{ACB}$ |  |
| 5 | $m\angle{ACB} + m\angle{ACD} = m\angle{A} + m\angle{B} + m\angle{ACB}$ | Transitive property of equality |
| 6 | $m\angle{ACD} = m\angle{A} + m\angle{B}$ | Addition principle |

**Explanation**

The complete proof is below.

|  | Statement | Reason |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | $\angle{ACB}$ and $\angle{ACD}$ are supplementary | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm{Linear pair postulate}}$ |
| 2 | $m\angle{ACB} + m\angle{ACD} = 180^\circ$ | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm{Definition of supplementary angles}}$ |
| 3 | $m\angle{A} + m\angle{B} + m\angle{ACB} = 180^\circ$ | Interior angles of a triangle theorem |
| 4 | $180^\circ = m\angle{A} + m\angle{B} + m\angle{ACB}$ | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm{Symmetric property of equality}}$ |
| 5 | $m\angle{ACB} + m\angle{ACD} = m\angle{A} + m\angle{B} + m\angle{ACB}$ | Transitive property of equality |
| 6 | $m\angle{ACD} = m\angle{A} + m\angle{B}$ | Addition principle |

Let's examine the rows of the table with missing parts in turn.

- Consider row 1. According to the linear pair postulate, $\angle{ACB}$ and $\angle{ACD}$ are supplementary angles.
- Consider row 2. From row 1, $\angle{ACB}$ and $\angle{ACD}$ are supplementary angles. Therefore, by definition of supplementary angles,
$m\angle{ACB} + m\angle{ACD} = 180^\circ$.
- Consider row 4. From row 3, we have
$m\angle{A} + m\angle{B} + m\angle{ACB} = 180^\circ$.
Now, using the symmetric property of equality, we get
$180^\circ = m\angle{A} + m\angle{B} + m\angle{ACB}$.

## Constructing Complete Proofs of Triangle Theorems

**Example:** In the diagram above, the points $A$, $C$, and $D$ are colinear, and the lines $\overset{\longleftrightarrow}{EF}$ and $\overset{\longleftrightarrow}{AD}$ are parallel.

![](<../Source/9.3.1. Proving Triangle Theorems/Images/009dc1c7838d55be1cfc855fa4c23f9e.png>)

Consider the following statement:

> $m\angle{BCD} = m\angle{A} + m\angle{ABC}$

The table below gives the proof of the statement. Complete the proof by filling in the empty boxes.

|  | Statement | Reason |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | $\overset{\longleftrightarrow}{EF} \parallel \overset{\longleftrightarrow}{AD}$ |  |
| 2 | $\angle{BCD} \cong \angle{CBE}$ |  |
| 3 | $m\angle{BCD} = m\angle{CBE}$ |  |
| 4 | $m\angle{CBE} = m\angle{ABE} + m\angle{ABC}$ |  |
| 5 | $\angle{ABE} \cong \angle{A}$ |  |
| 6 | $m\angle{ABE} = m\angle{A}$ |  |
| 7 | $m\angle{CBE} = m\angle{A} + m\angle{ABC}$ |  |
| 8 | $m\angle{BCD} = m\angle{A} + m\angle{ABC}$ |  |

**Explanation**

![](<../Source/9.3.1. Proving Triangle Theorems/Images/818cc1154a726110652e6a07377b3d52.png>)

The correct proof is below.

|  | Statement | Reason |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | $\overset{\longleftrightarrow}{EF} \parallel \overset{\longleftrightarrow}{AD}$ | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm{Given}}$ |
| 2 | $\angle{BCD} \cong \angle{CBE}$ | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm{Alternate interior angles theorem}}$ |
| 3 | $m\angle{BCD} = m\angle{CBE}$ | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm{Definition of congruent angles}}$ |
| 4 | $m\angle{CBE} = m\angle{ABE} + m\angle{ABC}$ | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm{Angle addition postulate}}$ |
| 5 | $\angle{ABE} \cong \angle{A}$ | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm{Alternate interior angles theorem}}$ |
| 6 | $m\angle{ABE} = m\angle{A}$ | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm{Definition of congruent angles}}$ |
| 7 | $m\angle{CBE} = m\angle{A} + m\angle{ABC}$ | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm{Substitution}}$ |
| 8 | $m\angle{BCD} = m\angle{A} + m\angle{ABC}$ | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm{Transitive property of equality}}$ |

Let's examine each row in turn.

1. Note that we're given $\overset{\longleftrightarrow}{EF} \parallel \overset{\longleftrightarrow}{AD}$.
2. From row 1, we have that $\overset{\longleftrightarrow}{EF}$ and $\overset{\longleftrightarrow}{AD}$ are parallel. Therefore, $\angle{BCD}$ and $\angle{CBE}$ are congruent by the alternate interior angles theorem.
3. From row 2, we have that $\angle{BCD}$ and $\angle{CBE}$ are congruent. Therefore, by the definition of congruent angles, $m\angle{BCD} = m\angle{CBE}$.
4. Note that the segment $\overline{AB}$ divides the angle $\angle{CBE}$ into consecutive angles $\angle{ABE}$ and $\angle{ABC}$. Therefore, by the angle addition postulate, we must have
$m\angle{CBE} = m\angle{ABE} + m\angle{ABC}$.
5. From row 1, we have that $\overset{\longleftrightarrow}{EF}$ and $\overset{\longleftrightarrow}{AD}$ are parallel. Therefore, $\angle{ABE}$ and $\angle{A}$ are congruent by the alternate interior angles theorem.
6. From row 5, $\angle{ABE}$ and $\angle{A}$ are congruent. Therefore, by the definition of congruent angles, $m\angle{ABE} = m\angle{A}$.
7. From row 6, $m\angle{ABE} = m\angle{A}$. Substituting this into the equation in row 4, we have
$m\angle{CBE} = m\angle{A} + m\angle{ABC}$.
8. From rows 3 and 7, we have
$m\angle{BCD} = m\angle{CBE}, \qquad m\angle{CBE} = m\angle{A} + m\angle{ABC}$.
Therefore, by the transitive property of equality, $m\angle{BCD} = m\angle{A} + m\angle{ABC}$.
