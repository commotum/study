## Introduction

In this lesson, we'll learn how to prove theorems relating parallelograms and rectangles.

Let's begin by restating the definition of a parallelogram.

> *A parallelogram is a quadrilateral where the opposite sides are parallel.*

A rectangle is simply a special type of parallelogram.

> *A rectangle is a parallelogram containing four right angles.*

Let's now consider our first theorem.

---

<a id="proving-that-parallelograms-with-congruent-diagonals-are-rectangles"></a>
## Proving That Parallelograms With Congruent Diagonals are Rectangles

The first theorem we'll consider is the following:

> *If a parallelogram has congruent diagonals, then it's a rectangle.*

To prove this theorem, consider the diagram below, where $PQRS$ is a parallelogram and $\overline{QS} \cong \overline{PR}$.

![](<../Source/9.3.7. Proving Criteria for Parallelograms and Rectangles/Images/177c93861490208dbeb40086d5ef5a65.png>)

We wish to prove the following statement:

> $\angle{QPS}$ is a right angle

As we'll see shortly, this is enough to conclude that $PQRS$ is a rectangle.

First, let's outline our strategy for this proof:

- First, we want to show that $\triangle QPS$ and $\triangle RSP$ are congruent using the SSS congruence criterion.
- Next, we'll CPCTC to deduce that $\angle QPS \cong \angle RSP$.
- Finally, $\angle QPS$ and $\angle RSP$ are also supplementary since $PQRS$ is a parallelogram. This gives us enough information to deduce that $\angle QPS$ must be a right angle.

Following this strategy, we complete our proof as follows:

- **Step 1**: We are *given* that $PQRS$ is a parallelogram.
- **Step 2**: Since $PQRS$ is a parallelogram, *its opposite sides are congruent*. Therefore, we have
$\overline{PQ} \cong \overline{RS}$.

![](<../Source/9.3.7. Proving Criteria for Parallelograms and Rectangles/Images/43a1b72ba874decf82840d3c312c44bb.png>)

- **Step 3**: We are *given* that $\overline{QS} \cong \overline{PR}$.

![](<../Source/9.3.7. Proving Criteria for Parallelograms and Rectangles/Images/984bc6525d53fa9d96e85bc3e3df2330.png>)

- **Step 4**: The *reflexive property of congruence* states that every segment is congruent to itself. Therefore, $\overline{PS} \cong \overline{PS}$.

![](<../Source/9.3.7. Proving Criteria for Parallelograms and Rectangles/Images/30a1b2d05a951b3962e5181b7ef71708.png>)

- **Step 5**: In steps 2, 3, and 4, we showed that all three sides of $\triangle{QPS}$ are congruent to the corresponding sides of $\triangle{RSP}$. Therefore,
$\triangle{QPS} \cong \triangle{RSP}$
by the *SSS criterion*.
- **Step 6**: Since $\triangle{QPS} \cong \triangle{RSP}$, we have
$\angle{QPS} \cong \angle{RSP}$
by *CPCTC*.

![](<../Source/9.3.7. Proving Criteria for Parallelograms and Rectangles/Images/e8f637cb41e8883580e1cc67bb9c0fa4.png>)

- **Step 7**: By *the definition of congruent angles,* we obtain
$m\angle{QPS} = m\angle{RSP}$.
- **Step 8**: Since *adjacent angles of a parallelogram are supplementary,* we have that $\angle{QPS}$ and $\angle{RSP}$ are supplementary.
- **Step 9**: Since $\angle{QPS}$ and $\angle{RSP}$ are supplementary, by the *definition of supplementary angles,* we have
$m\angle{QPS} + m\angle{RSP} = 180^\circ$.

- **Step 10**: *Substituting* the expression from step 7 into the expression from step 9, we get
$m\angle{QPS} + m\angle{QPS} = 180^\circ$.
- **Step 11**: We can solve for $m\angle{QPS}$ using *algebra* as follows:
$m∠QPS + m∠QPS|= 180^{∘}.; 2m∠QPS|= 180^{∘}.; m∠QPS|= 90^{∘}$.
- **Step 12**: Finally, by the *definition of a right angle*, we conclude that $\angle{QPS}$ is a right angle, as required.

Let's now state our proof using a two-column format.

## A Parallelogram Containing a Right Angle Is a Rectangle

The last proof showed that a parallelogram with congruent diagonals must contain a right angle. As it turns out, this is enough to conclude that the parallelogram is a rectangle:

> *If a parallelogram contains a right angle, then it's a rectangle.*

Let's discuss a strategy for proving this result.

- **Step 1**: We're given a parallelogram with a right angle.![](<../Source/9.3.7. Proving Criteria for Parallelograms and Rectangles/Images/0cdd7fa1753a237db222d4ef2fa035e2.png>)
- **Step 2**: Since our quadrilateral is a parallelogram, the opposite angles are congruent, which means our parallelogram must contain two right angles:![](<../Source/9.3.7. Proving Criteria for Parallelograms and Rectangles/Images/fbf13804b983a4b1019008de2eafc030.png>)
- **Step 3**: Since adjacent angles in a parallelogram are supplementary, we can use some algebra to conclude that the remaining two angles must be right, too.![](<../Source/9.3.7. Proving Criteria for Parallelograms and Rectangles/Images/23717a7b0ed119d28daf27bf54a6230c.png>)

Let's now look at a formal proof of this result.

## Constructing Complete Proofs of Parallelogram and Rectangle Criteria

**Example:** In the diagram above, $PQRS$ is a parallelogram and $\overline{PR} \cong \overline{QS}$.

![](<../Source/9.3.7. Proving Criteria for Parallelograms and Rectangles/Images/550f8717f1aa75644ab19c8066f7a07b.png>)

Consider the following statement:

> *$\angle{RSP}$ is a right angle*

The table below gives the proof of the statement. Fill in the blanks and thus complete the proof.

|  | Statement | Reason |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | $PQRS$ is a parallelogram |  |
| 2 | $\overline{PR} \cong \overline{QS}$ |  |
| 4 | $\overline{PS} \cong \overline{PS}$ |  |
| 3 | $\overline{PQ} \cong \overline{RS}$ |  |
| 5 | $\triangle{PQS} \cong \triangle{SRP}$ |  |
| 6 | $\angle{QPS} \cong \angle{RSP}$ |  |
| 7 | $m\angle{QPS} = m\angle{RSP}$ |  |
| 8 | $\angle{QPS}$ and $\angle{RSP}$ are supplementary |  |
| 9 | $m\angle{QPS} + m\angle{RSP} = 180^\circ$ |  |
| 10 | $m\angle{RSP} + m\angle{RSP} = 180^\circ$ |  |
| 11 | $m\angle{RSP} = 90^\circ$ |  |
| 12 | $\angle{RSP}$ is a right angle |  |

**Explanation**

![](<../Source/9.3.7. Proving Criteria for Parallelograms and Rectangles/Images/91415f8a4f5fbaca2b3137c7a9d6312e.png>)

The correct proof is shown below.

|  | Statement | Reason |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | $PQRS$ is a parallelogram | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm{Given}}$ |
| 2 | $\overline{PR} \cong \overline{QS}$ | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm{Given}}$ |
| 4 | $\overline{PS} \cong \overline{PS}$ | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm{Reflexive property of congruence}}$ |
| 3 | $\overline{PQ} \cong \overline{RS}$ | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm{Opposite sides are congruent}}$ |
| 5 | $\triangle{PQS} \cong \triangle{SRP}$ | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm{SSS congruence criterion}}$ |
| 6 | $\angle{QPS} \cong \angle{RSP}$ | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm{CPCTC}}$ |
| 7 | $m\angle{QPS} = m\angle{RSP}$ | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm{Definition of congruent angles}}$ |
| 8 | $\angle{QPS}$ and $\angle{RSP}$ are supplementary | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm{Adjacent angles are supplementary}}$ |
| 9 | $m\angle{QPS} + m\angle{RSP} = 180^\circ$ | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm{Definition of supplementary angles}}$ |
| 10 | $m\angle{RSP} + m\angle{RSP} = 180^\circ$ | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm{Substitution}}$ |
| 11 | $m\angle{RSP} = 90^\circ$ | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm{Algebra}}$ |
| 12 | $\angle{RSP}$ is a right angle | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm{Definition of a right angle}}$ |

Let's examine each row in turn.

1. We are *given* that $PQRS$ is a parallelogram.
2. We are *given* $\overline{PR} \cong \overline{QS}$.
3. The *reflexive property of congruence* states that every segment is congruent to itself. Therefore, $\overline{PS} \cong \overline{PS}$.
4. Notice that $\overline{PQ}$ and $\overline{RS}$ are opposite sides of a parallelogram. In any parallelogram, the *opposite sides are congruent*. Therefore,
$\overline{PQ} \cong \overline{RS}$.
5. From rows 2, 3, and 4, we have
$\overline{PR} \cong \overline{QS}, \qquad \overline{PS} \cong \overline{PS}, \qquad \overline{PQ} \cong \overline{RS}$.
Therefore, by the SSS congruence criterion,
$\triangle{PQS} \cong \triangle{SRP}$.
6. Therefore, by CPCTC,
$\angle{QPS} \cong \angle{RSP}$.
7. Therefore, by the definition of congruent angles, we get
$m\angle{QPS} = m\angle{RSP}$.
8. Notice that $\angle{QPS}$ and $\angle{RSP}$ are adjacent angles of a parallelogram. In any parallelogram, the adjacent angles are supplementary. Therefore, $\angle{QPS}$ and $\angle{RSP}$ are supplementary.
9. Therefore, by the definition of supplementary angles, we have
$m\angle{QPS} + m\angle{RSP} = 180^\circ$.
10. Substituting the expression from row 7 into the expression in row 9, we get
$m\angle{RSP} + m\angle{RSP} = 180^\circ$.
11. Using algebra (solving for $m\angle{RSP}$), we obtain
$m\angle{RSP} = 90^\circ$.
12. Therefore, by the definition of a right angle, we get that $\angle{RSP}$ is a right angle.
