## Introduction

Suppose we are given the parabola

$$
x^2 - 4x - 8y - 28 = 0
$$

whose graph is shown below. How can we find the vertex of the parabola?

![](<../Source/9.5.4. Calculating the Vertex of a Parabola by Completing the Square/Images/a37e99d5033ba8818d790b66baceff3e.png>)

Recall that the standard equation of a *vertical* parabola that has its vertex at $(h,k)$ is

$$
(x-h)^2 = 4p(y-k)
$$

where $p$ is a constant.

To write our equation in standard form, we group the $x$-terms on the left-hand side and complete the square, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x^{2} - 4x - 8y - 28 &= 0 \\
x^{2} - 4x &= 8y + 28 \\
(x - 2)^{2} - 4 &= 8y + 28 \\
(x - 2)^{2} &= 8y + 32 \\
(x - 2)^{2} &= 8(y + 4) \\
(x - 2)^{2} &= 4(2)(y - (-4))
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the coordinates of the vertex are

$$
(h,k) = ({\color{blue}2},{\color{red}-4})
$$

## Calculating the Vertex of a Vertical Parabola by Completing the Square

**Example:** Calculate the coordinates of the vertex of the parabola $5x^2 -10x - 4y - 7 = 0$.

**Explanation**

The standard equation of an upward- or downward-opening parabola that has its vertex at $(h,k)$ is

$$
(x-h)^2 = 4p(y-k)
$$

where $p$ is a constant.

To write the given equation in this form, we need to group the $x$-terms on the left-hand side and complete the square, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
5x^{2} - 10x - 4y - 7 &= 0 \\
5x^{2} - 10x &= 4y + 7 \\
5[x^{2} - 2x] &= 4y + 7 \\
5[(x - 1)^{2} - 1] &= 4y + 7 \\
5(x - 1)^{2} - 5 &= 4y + 7 \\
5(x - 1)^{2} &= 4y + 7 + 5 \\
5(x - 1)^{2} &= 4y + 12 \\
5(x - 1)^{2} &= 4(y + 3) \\
(x - 1)^{2} &= \frac{4}{5}(y + 3) \\
(x - 1)^{2} &= 4(\frac{1}{5})(y + 3)
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the coordinates of the vertex are

$$
(h,k) = (1,-3)
$$

## Calculating the Vertex of a Horizontal Parabola by Completing the Square

**Example:** Calculate the coordinates of the vertex of the parabola $y^2 - 8x - 6y + 33 = 0$.

**Explanation**

The standard equation of a left- or right-opening parabola that has its vertex at $(h,k)$ is

$$
(y-k)^2 = 4p(x-h)
$$

where $p$ is a constant.

To write the given equation in this form, we need to group the $y$-terms on the left-hand side and complete the square, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
y^{2} - 8x - 6y + 33 &= 0 \\
y^{2} - 6y &= 8x - 33 \\
(y - 3)^{2} - 9 &= 8x - 33 \\
(y - 3)^{2} &= 8x - 33 + 9 \\
(y - 3)^{2} &= 8x - 24 \\
(y - 3)^{2} &= 8(x - 3) \\
(y - 3)^{2} &= 4(2)(x - 3)
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the coordinates of the vertex are

$$
(h,k) = (3,3)
$$
