## Introduction

In the diagram below, $\triangle ABC$ is **inscribed** in a circle with center $O$. This means that all three of the triangle's vertices lie on the circle's circumference.

![](<../Source/9.2.3. Thales' Theorem/Images/6211fa4c51ee009df2576ac4510d3488.png>)

From the diagram, we can see that $\overline{AB}$ passes through the point $O$. So, $\overline{AB}$ is a diameter of the circle.

Based on this information, **Thales' theorem** tells us that the triangle $\triangle ABC$ *must* be a right triangle. More precisely:

> *If $A, B$, and $C$ are distinct points on a circle where $\overline{AB}$ is a diameter, then $m\angle C = 90^\circ$.*

![](<../Source/9.2.3. Thales' Theorem/Images/cfe2fee8c6bb56587e64a9bb83246a08.png>)

Now that we know $\triangle ABC$ is a right triangle, we can apply the Pythagorean theorem to find the length of the hypotenuse:

$$
\begin{aligned}
AB &= \sqrt{AC^{2} + BC^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{8^{2} + 6^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{64 + 36} \\
&= \sqrt{100} \\
&= 10cm
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding the Radius of a Circle Given an Inscribed Right Triangle

**Example:** Consider the figure below, where $AC=7\,\textrm{cm}$ and $BC=24\,\textrm{cm}$. What is the radius of the circle?

![](<../Source/9.2.3. Thales' Theorem/Images/fe89aca96133297aa49b60959d0feab8.png>)

**Explanation**

Notice that $\triangle{ABC}$ is inscribed in the circle, where the side $\overline{AB}$ is a diameter. Therefore,

$$
m\angle{ACB}=90^\circ
$$

So, by the Pythagorean theorem, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
AB &= \sqrt{AC^{2} + BC^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{7^{2} + 24^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{625} \\
&= 25cm
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, the radius of the circle is

$$
OA = \dfrac{AB}{2} = \dfrac{25}{2} = 12.5 \,\textrm{cm}
$$

## Finding the Length of a Leg in an Inscribed Right Triangle

**Example:** The radius of the above circle is $10\,\textrm{m}$ and $AC=12\,\textrm{m}$. What is the length of the chord $\overline{BC}$?

![](<../Source/9.2.3. Thales' Theorem/Images/25f162e7c1a97d042d0c18d2b8a41be1.png>)

**Explanation**

Notice that $\triangle{ACB}$ is inscribed in the circle, where $\overline{AB}$ is a diameter. Therefore, by Thales' theorem, we have that

$$
m\angle{C}=90^\circ
$$

as shown below.

![](<../Source/9.2.3. Thales' Theorem/Images/d1a7fb042332f7771243df163d3505b3.png>)

Now,

$$
AB=2AO=2 \cdot 10 = 20 \, \textrm{m}
$$

Finally, by the Pythagorean theorem,

$$
\begin{aligned}
BC^{2} &= AB^{2} - AC^{2} \\
&= 20^{2} - 12^{2} \\
&= 400 - 144 \\
&= 256
\end{aligned}
$$

So,

$$
BC = \sqrt{256} = 16\,\textrm{cm}
$$
