## Introduction

The **zero product rule** says that if we have the equation

$$
a\cdot b = 0
$$

then $a=0$ or $b=0$.

In other words, if we know that the product of two numbers is zero, then at least one of them must be zero because any number multiplied by zero is zero.

The zero product rule is useful for solving quadratic equations. For example, let's take a look at the quadratic equation

$$
{\color{red}(x-1)}{\color{blue}(x+2)} = 0
$$

Whenever a quadratic equation is factored on the left-hand side and has zero on the right-hand side, we can apply the zero product rule to solve the equation. So for our equation, we have

$$
{\color{red}x-1=0} \qquad\qquad\text{or}\qquad\qquad {\color{blue}x+2 =0}
$$

We solve each of the equations separately, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\begin{bmatrix}x - 1 &= 0 & x + 2|= 0 \\ x|= 1 & x|=-2\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the solutions are $x = -2,1$.

As usual, we can check our solutions by substituting them back into the original equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\begin{bmatrix}x &= -2 & x|= 1 \\ ⇓ & ⇓ \\ (x - 1)(x + 2)|= 0 & (x - 1)(x + 2)|= 0 \\ (-2 - 1)(-2 + 2)|= 0 & (1 - 1)(1 + 2)|= 0 \\ (-3)(-0)|= 0 & (0)(3)|= 0 \\ 0|= 0✓ & 0|= 0✓\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Solving a Factored Quadratic Equation When Both Factors are Binomials

**Example:** Calculate the values of $x$ that satisfy $(2x-1)(5x+3)=0$.

**Explanation**

Applying the zero product rule, we get

$$
2x-1=0\qquad \textrm{or}\qquad 5x+3=0
$$

We solve each of the equations separately, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\begin{bmatrix}2x - 1 &= 0 & 5x + 3|= 0 \\ 2x|= 1 & 5x|=-3 \\ x|= \frac{1}{2} & x|=-\frac{3}{5}\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the solutions are

$$
x=-\dfrac{3}{5},\dfrac{1}{2}
$$

## Solving a Factored Quadratic Equation When One Factor is a Binomial

**Example:** Solve the equation $x(x-16) = 0$.

**Explanation**

Applying the zero product rule, we get

$$
x = 0\qquad \textrm{or}\qquad x-16=0
$$

Solving each of these equations separately, we find that the solutions are $x=0$ and $x=16$.

## Constructing a Quadratic Equation With Specified Roots

**Example:** Find a quadratic equation that has roots $x=5$ and $x=7$.

**Explanation**

The given roots $x=5$ and $x=7$ are the solutions to the equations $x-5=0$ and $x-7=0$, respectively.

So, according to the zero product rule, they are also the solutions of the product

$$
(x-5)(x-7)=0
$$

We multiply out the product and collect like terms to produce a quadratic equation in standard form:

$$
\begin{aligned} (x-5)(x-7) &=0 \\ x(x-7) - 5(x-7) &=0 \\ x^2 - 7x - 5x + 35 &= 0 \\ x^2 -12x+35 &=0 \end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, a quadratic equation that has roots $x=5$ and $x=7$ is

$$
x^2 -12x+35=0
$$
