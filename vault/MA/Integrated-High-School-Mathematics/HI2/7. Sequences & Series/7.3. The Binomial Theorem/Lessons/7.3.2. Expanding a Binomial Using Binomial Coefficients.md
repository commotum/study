## Introduction

The **binomial theorem** states that for any real numbers $a$ and $b$ and any positive integer $n$,

$$
{(a + b)^n} = {n \choose 0}{a^n}{b^0} + {n \choose 1}{a^{n - 1}}{b^1} + {n \choose 2}{a^{n - 2}}{b^2} + \cdots + {n \choose n}{a^0}{b^n}
$$

where we recall that

$$
{n \choose k} = \dfrac{n!}{k!\, (n-k)!}
$$

Let's use the binomial theorem to find the first few terms in the expansion of

$$
(2+x)^6
$$

In this case, we have $a = 2$, $b= x$, and $n=6$. Therefore, the first three terms of our expansion are given by

$$
{\left({2 + x} \right)^{6}} = {6\choose0} {\left(2 \right)^{6}}{\left(x \right)^0} + {6\choose1} {\left(2 \right)^{6 - 1}}{\left(x \right)^1} + {6\choose2} {\left(2 \right)^{6 - 2}}{\left(x \right)^2} + \cdots
$$

Using a calculator, we find that

$$
{6\choose0} = {\color{blue}{1}}, \qquad {6\choose1} = {\color{blue}{6}}, \qquad {6\choose2} = {\color{blue}{15}}
$$

Therefore, our expansion can be simplified as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(2 + x)^{6} &= (\frac{6}{0})(2)^{6}(x)^{0} + (\frac{6}{1})(2)^{6 - 1}(x)^{1} + (\frac{6}{2})(2)^{6 - 2}(x)^{2} + ⋯ \\
&= (1)(2^{6})(1) + (6)(2^{5})(x) + (15)(2^{4})(x^{2}) + ⋯ \\
&= (1)(64)(1) + (6)(32)(x) + (15)(16)(x^{2}) + ⋯ \\
&= 64 + 192x + 240x^{2} + ⋯
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding the First Few Terms of a Binomial Expansion

**Example:** Expand ${(3x + y)}^3$ using the binomial theorem.

**Explanation**

The binomial theorem states that for any positive integer $n$, we have

$$
{(a + b)^n} = {n \choose 0}{a^n}{b^0} + {n \choose 1}{a^{n - 1}}{b^1} + {n \choose 2}{a^{n - 2}}{b^2} + \cdots + {n \choose n}{a^0}{b^n}
$$

In the case of ${(3x +y)}^3$, we have $a=3x$, $b=y$, and $n=3$.

Therefore, we can expand ${(3x + y)}^3$ as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(3x + y)^{3} &= (\frac{3}{0})(3x)^{3}(y)^{0} + (\frac{3}{1})(3x)^{3 - 1}(y)^{1} + (\frac{3}{2})(3x)^{3 - 2}(y)^{2} + (\frac{3}{3})(3x)^{3 - 3}(y)^{3} \\
&= (\frac{3}{0})(3x)^{3}(1) + (\frac{3}{1})(3x)^{2}(y)^{1} + (\frac{3}{2})(3x)^{1}(y)^{2} + (\frac{3}{3})(3x)^{0}(y)^{3} \\
&= 1 \cdot (3x)^{3} + 3 \cdot (3x)^{2}(y) + 3 \cdot (3x)(y)^{2} + 1 \cdot 1 \cdot (y^{3}) \\
&= 27x^{3} + 27x^{2}y + 9xy^{2} + y^{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding the First Few Terms of an Expansion With Negative Coefficients

**Example:** Find the first three terms of the binomial expansion of ${(x - 3y)}^ 5$.

**Explanation**

The binomial theorem states that for any positive integer $n$, we have

$$
{(a + b)^n} = {n \choose 0}{a^n}{b^0} + {n \choose 1}{a^{n - 1}}{b^1} + {n \choose 2}{a^{n - 2}}{b^2} + \cdots + {n \choose n}{a^0}{b^n}
$$

In the case of ${(x - 3y)}^ 5$, we have $a=x$, $b=-3y$, and $n=5$.

Therefore, the first three terms of ${(x - 3y)}^ 5$ are as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x - 3y)^{5} &= (\frac{5}{0})(x)^{5}(-3y)^{0} + (\frac{5}{1})(x)^{5 - 1}(-3y)^{1} + (\frac{5}{2})(x)^{5 - 2}(-3y)^{2} + ⋯ \\
&= 1(x)^{5} + 5(x)^{4}(-3y) + 10(x)^{3}(9y^{2}) + ⋯ \\
&= x^{5} - 15x^{4}y + 90x^{3}y^{2} + ⋯
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding the Coefficient of a Particular Term

**Example:** Find the coefficient of $x^{4}$ in the expansion of ${\left({3 + x} \right)^{7}}$.

**Explanation**

The binomial theorem states that for any positive integer $n$, we have

$$
{(a + b)^n} = {n \choose 0}{a^n}{b^0} + {n \choose 1}{a^{n - 1}}{b^1} + {n \choose 2}{a^{n - 2}}{b^2} + \cdots + {n \choose n}{a^0}{b^n}
$$

In the case of

$$
{\left({3 +x} \right)^{7}}
$$

we have $a=3$, $b=x$, and $n=7$. Therefore,

$$
(3 + x)^{7} = (\frac{7}{0})3^{7}x^{0} + (\frac{7}{1})3^{7 - 1}x^{1} + (\frac{7}{2})3^{7 - 2}x^{2} + ⋯ + (\frac{7}{7})3^{7 - 7}x^{7}
$$

From here, we see that the $x^4$ term must be

$$
{7 \choose 4}{3^{7 - 4}}{x^4}
$$

Evaluating the coefficient, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
(\frac{7}{4})3^{7 - 4} &= 35 \cdot 3^{3} \\
&= 35 \cdot 27 \\
&= 945
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the coefficient of $x^{4}$ is $945$.
