## Introduction

Sometimes we can simplify a radical expression by factoring the expression inside the radical.

For instance, let's try to simplify the expression

$$
\sqrt{a^2+2a+1}
$$

We can begin by factoring the expression under the radical, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{a^{2} + 2a + 1} &=  \\
\sqrt{a^{2} + 2 \cdot 1 \cdot a + 1} &=  \\
&= \sqrt{(a + 1)^{2}}
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, using the fact that

$$
\sqrt{a^2} = \mid a \mid
$$

we can simplify our expression even further. This gives

$$
\sqrt{(a+1)^2} = \mid a+1 \mid
$$

## Simplifying the Square Root of a Perfect Square by Factoring

**Example:** Given that $x\geq1$, simplify $\sqrt{4x^2 - 8x + 4}$.

**Explanation**

To simplify the radical expression, we factor the expression inside the radical and then simplify using the identity

$$
\sqrt{a^2} = \mid a \mid
$$

as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{4x^{2} - 8x + 4} &= \sqrt{4 \cdot (x^{2} - 2x + 1)} \\
&= \sqrt{4} \cdot \sqrt{x^{2} - 2x + 1} \\
&= 2 \cdot \sqrt{x^{2} - 2x + 1} \\
&= 2 \cdot \sqrt{x^{2} - 2 \cdot 1 \cdot x + 1^{2}} \\
&= 2 \cdot \sqrt{(x - 1)^{2}} \\
&= 2 \mid x - 1 \mid
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, since

$$
x \geq 1
$$

we have that

$$
x-1 \geq 0 \quad \Longrightarrow\quad \mid x-1 \mid = x-1
$$

Therefore, our expression simplifies to $2(x-1)$.

## Simplifying a Square Root by Factoring a Higher Order Polynomial

**Example:** Simplify $\sqrt{4x^4y +12x^4}$.

**Explanation**

To simplify the radical expression, we factor the expression inside the radical and then simplify using the identity

$$
\sqrt{a^2} = \mid a \mid
$$

as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned} \sqrt{4x^4y +12x^4} &= \sqrt{4x^4(y+3)} \\[3pt] &= \sqrt{4x^4} \cdot \sqrt{y+3} \\[3pt] &= \sqrt{\left(2x^2\right)^2} \cdot \sqrt{y+3} \\[3pt] &= \mid 2x^2 \mid \sqrt{y+3} \end{aligned}
$$

Now, since

$$
x^2 \geq0
$$

we have that

$$
2x^2 \geq 0 \quad \Longrightarrow\quad \mid 2x^2 \mid = 2x^2
$$

Therefore, our expression simplifies to $2x^2\sqrt{y + 3}$.

<a id="simplifying-the-square-root-of-a-rational-expression"></a>
## Simplifying the Square Root of a Rational Expression

**Example:** Simplify $\sqrt{\dfrac{x^2-4x+4}{x^2+6x+9}}$.

**Explanation**

To simplify the expression, we first factor the numerator and denominator under the radical:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{\frac{x^{2} - 4x + 4}{x^{2} + 6x + 9}} &= \sqrt{(x^{2} + 2 \cdot x \cdot (-2) + (-2)^{2})/(x^{2} + 2 \cdot x \cdot 3 + 3^{2})} \\
&= \sqrt{((x - 2)^{2})/((x + 3)^{2})}
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, by applying the quotient rule for radicals and the rules of absolute value, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sqrt{((x - 2)^{2})/((x + 3)^{2})} &= (\sqrt{(x - 2)^{2}})/(\sqrt{(x + 3)^{2}}) \\
&= \frac{\begin{vmatrix}x - 2 &}{\mid x + 3 \mid} \\ = & \frac{x - 2}{x + 3}\end{vmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$
