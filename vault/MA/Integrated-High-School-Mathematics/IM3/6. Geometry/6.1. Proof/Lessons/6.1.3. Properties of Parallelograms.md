## Introduction

We'll explore some properties of parallelograms in this lesson. Let's begin by stating the definition of a parallelogram.

> *A parallelogram is a quadrilateral where the opposite sides are parallel.*

![](<../Source/6.1.3. Properties of Parallelograms/Images/ac3c2d89635dbc0d71c0bf368986bbd6.png>)

It's possible to prove the following property.

> *In a parallelogram, the opposite sides are congruent.*

![](<../Source/6.1.3. Properties of Parallelograms/Images/a12cf95048a7f12ad650f9b2bf7bb8fb.png>)

The fact that the opposite sides are congruent is a *consequence* of the definition of a parallelogram, not part of the definition itself. We'll learn how to prove this in future lessons.

<a id="finding-side-lengths-of-parallelograms"></a>
## Finding Side Lengths of Parallelograms

**Example:** For the parallelogram shown above, find the measure of the side $\overline{BC}$.

![](<../Source/6.1.3. Properties of Parallelograms/Images/6ce2a79bcfe8786c5bf68fce7ff58cca.png>)

**Explanation**

In any parallelogram, the opposite sides are congruent. So, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
AB &= CD \\
2x + 5 &= 15 \\
2x &= 10 \\
x &= 5
\end{aligned}
$$

and therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
AD &= x + 5 \\
&= 5 + 5 \\
&= 10
\end{aligned}
$$

Using again the fact that the opposite sides of a parallelogram are congruent, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
BC &= AD = 10
\end{aligned}
$$

## Angle Properties

![](<../Source/6.1.3. Properties of Parallelograms/Images/1b3a96e6a05ebbcb66d5a7c0fa10df4b.png>)

Given any parallelogram, we have the following property.

> *The opposite angles of a parallelogram are congruent, whereas adjacent (or consecutive) angles are supplementary.*

We'll learn how to prove these properties in future lessons.

<a id="finding-angles-measures-of-parallelograms"></a>
## Finding Angles Measures of Parallelograms

**Example:** For the parallelogram $ABCD$, you're given that $m\angle{A} = 80^{\circ}$. What is the measure of $\angle{D}$?

**Explanation**

![](<../Source/6.1.3. Properties of Parallelograms/Images/f658437d7de110e951a41487415c0801.png>)

In any parallelogram, the opposite angles are equal, while the adjacent (or consecutive) angles are supplementary.

Since $\angle{A}$ and $\angle{D}$ are adjacent angles of the parallelogram $ABCD$, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
m∠D &= 180^{∘} - m∠A \\
&= 180^{∘} - 80^{∘} \\
&= 100^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Diagonal Lengths

All parallelograms have the following property.

> *The diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other.*

In other words, the first diagonal of a parallelogram bisects the second, and the second diagonal bisects the first, as shown below.

![](<../Source/6.1.3. Properties of Parallelograms/Images/5f9f0b2dac02f10fedb154a4377511fe.png>)

We'll prove this property in a future lesson.

---

<a id="finding-diagonal-lengths-of-parallelograms"></a>
## Finding Diagonal Lengths of Parallelograms

**Example:** For the parallelogram $ABCD$ shown above, find the length of the diagonal $\overline{BD}$.

![](<../Source/6.1.3. Properties of Parallelograms/Images/4dc5c48ad9b487a9b5f520726b084ee4.png>)

**Explanation**

The diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other. That is, they intersect at their respective midpoints. In our case, this means that

$$
\qquad BO = DO \quad
$$

and

$$
\quad CO = AO
$$

Now, since $CO = AO$, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
AO &= CO \\
3x + 9 &= 27 \\
3x &= 27 - 9 \\
3x &= 18 \\
x &= 6
\end{aligned}
$$

As a result,

$$
\begin{aligned}
BO &= 7x - 5 \\
&= 7 \cdot 6 - 5 \\
&= 42 - 5 \\
&= 37
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, since $BO = DO$, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
BD &= BO + DO \\
&= BO + BO \\
&= 2BO \\
&= 2 \cdot 37 \\
&= 74
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="the-diagonal-criterion"></a>
## The Diagonal Criterion

We've already seen that if a quadrilateral is a parallelogram, its diagonals bisect each other.

The **converse** of this statement is also true.

> *If the diagonals of a quadrilateral bisect each other, then it is a parallelogram.*

For example, consider the *quadrilateral* shown below.

![](<../Source/6.1.3. Properties of Parallelograms/Images/a74638c70edfbc2620d696bada2df202.png>)

Note that

- the diagonal $\overline{BD}$ bisects the diagonal $\overline{AC}$, and
- the diagonal $\overline{AC}$ bisects the diagonal $\overline{BD}$.

Therefore, this quadrilateral *must* be a parallelogram!

---

<a id="using-the-diagonal-criterion"></a>
## Using the Diagonal Criterion

**Example:** The diagonals $\overline{AC}$ and $\overline{BD}$ of the quadrilateral $ABCD$ above intersect at the point $O$. If $AO = 4x+3$, $BO = 5-x$, $CO = y+2$, and $DO=y-1$, find the values ​​of $x$ and $y$ such that $ABCD$ is a parallelogram.

![](<../Source/6.1.3. Properties of Parallelograms/Images/faa4ea19a876aaa518bf090f6dc0f933.png>)

**Explanation**

If the diagonals of a quadrilateral bisect each other, then the quadrilateral is a parallelogram. Thus, $ABCD$ is a parallelogram if $AO=CO$ and $BO=DO$.

- Using the first equality, we have
$AO|= CO; 4x + 3|= y + 2; y|= 4x + 1$.
- Using the second equality, we get
$BO|= DO; 5 - x|= y - 1; x|= 6 - y$.

Substituting

$$
y = 4x+1
$$

from the first equation into the above, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
x &= 6 - y \\
x &= 6 - (4x + 1) \\
x &= 5 - 4x \\
5x &= 5 \\
x &= 1
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, substituting $x$ back into the first equation, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
y &= 4x + 1 \\
&= 4 \cdot 1 + 1 \\
&= 4 + 1 \\
&= 5
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, $x=1$ and $y=5$.
