## Introduction

In this lesson, we'll learn how to prove statements about isosceles triangles.

We begin by stating and proving the *isosceles triangle theorem*:

> *If a triangle is isosceles, then the base angles are congruent.*

To prove this theorem, consider the isosceles $\triangle{ABC}$ below, where $\overline{AB} \cong \overline{AC}$.

![](<../Source/6.1.1. Proofs Involving Isosceles Triangles/Images/da6e15ba3347e64261e4275246cef0b2.png>)

Our goal is to prove the following:

> $\angle B \cong \angle C$

Our strategy for proving this theorem is to split this isosceles triangle into two congruent triangles and then use CPCTC to show that the base angles are congruent.

We complete the proof as follows:

- **Step 1**: We start by *constructing* the median $\overline{AM}$ joining the vertex $A$ with the midpoint $M$ of the side $\overline{BC}$.![](<../Source/6.1.1. Proofs Involving Isosceles Triangles/Images/c5f66da090be0263495e75da836ef318.png>)
- **Step 2**: We're *given* that the segments $\overline{AB}$ and $\overline{AC}$ are congruent.
$\overline{AB} \cong \overline{AC}$
- **Step 3**: By the *definition of a median,* we have that $M$ is the midpoint of $\overline{BC}$.
- **Step 4**: By the *definition of a midpoint*, we have
$BM = CM$.
- **Step 5**: By the *definition of congruent segments*, it follows that
$\overline{BM} \cong \overline{CM}$.

- **Step 6**: By the *reflexive property of congruence*, $\overline{AM}$ is congruent to itself.
$\overline{AM} \cong \overline{AM}$
- **Step 7**: From steps 2 and 5, we have
$\overline{AB} \cong\overline{AC}, \qquad \overline{BM} \cong \overline{CM}$.
Also, the segment $\overline{AM}$ is a common side of $\triangle{ABM}$ and $\triangle{ACM}$, and $\overline{AM} \cong \overline{AM}$ from step 6. So, we have three pairs of congruent sides. Therefore, by the *SSS criterion*, we obtain that
$\triangle{ABM} \cong \triangle{ACM}$.
- **Step 8**: Finally, we conclude that
$\angle{B} \cong \angle{C}$
by *CPCTC*, as required.![](<../Source/6.1.1. Proofs Involving Isosceles Triangles/Images/48b6d8ce9ecebf8e2c63442060129550.png>)

## Stating the Full Proof Using the Two-Column Format

![](<../Source/6.1.1. Proofs Involving Isosceles Triangles/Images/40d50e971dbc561648e578dd129e6f4b.png>)

The table below shows the proof of the isosceles triangle theorem in a two-column format.

|  | Statement | Reason |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | $\overline{AM}$ is a median of $\triangle ABC$ | Geometric construction |
| 2 | $\overline{AB} \cong \overline{AC}$ | Given |
| 3 | $M$ is the midpoint of $\overline{BC}$ | Definition of median |
| 4 | $BM = CM$ | Definition of a midpoint |
| 5 | $\overline{BM} \cong \overline{CM}$ | Definition of congruent segments |
| 6 | $\overline{AM} \cong \overline{AM}$ | Reflexive property of congruence |
| 7 | $\triangle ABM \cong \triangle ACM$ | SSS criterion |
| 8 | $\angle {B} \cong \angle {C}$ | CPCTC |

## Partial Proofs Involving Isosceles Triangles

**Example:** In the diagram above, $\overline{CD} \perp \overline{AB}$ and $\angle{ACD} \cong \angle BCD$.

![](<../Source/6.1.1. Proofs Involving Isosceles Triangles/Images/b45c8bbcdf0a1acca984fdc2ecc18988.png>)

Consider the following statement:

> $AC=CB$

The table below gives the proof of the statement. Fill in the empty rows of the table with the correct reasons and thus complete the proof.

|  | Statement | Reason |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | $\overline{CD}\perp \overline{AB}$ | Given |
| 2 | $\angle{CDA}$ and $\angle{CDB}$ are right angles | Definition of perpendicular lines |
| 3 | $\angle CDA \cong \angle CDB$ |  |
| 4 | $\overline{CD} \cong \overline{CD}$ | Reflexive property of congruence |
| 5 | $\angle ACD \cong \angle BCD$ | Given |
| 6 | $\triangle ACD \cong \triangle BCD$ |  |
| 7 | $\overline{AC} \cong \overline{BC}$ | CPCTC |
| 8 | $AC = BC$ |  |

**Explanation**

The correct proof is shown below.

|  | Statement | Reason |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | $\overline{CD}\perp \overline{AB}$ | Given |
| 2 | $\angle{CDA}$ and $\angle{CDB}$ are right angles | Definition of perpendicular lines |
| 3 | $\angle CDA \cong \angle CDB$ | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm{Right angle postulate}}$ |
| 4 | $\overline{CD} \cong \overline{CD}$ | Reflexive property of congruence |
| 5 | $\angle ACD \cong \angle BCD$ | Given |
| 6 | $\triangle ACD \cong \triangle BCD$ | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm{ASA criterion}}$ |
| 7 | $\overline{AC} \cong \overline{BC}$ | CPCTC |
| 8 | $AC = BC$ | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm{Definition of congruent segments}}$ |

Let's examine the rows of the table with missing parts in turn.

- Consider row 3. From row 2, we have that $\angle{CDA}$ and $\angle{CDB}$ are right angles. Therefore, $\angle{CDA}\cong \angle{CDB}$ by the right angle postulate.
- Consider row 6. From rows 3, 4, and 5, we have
$\angle{CDA} \cong \angle{CDB}, \qquad \overline{CD} \cong \overline{CD}, \qquad \angle ACD \cong \angle BCD$.
Therefore, $\triangle ACD \cong \triangle BCD$ by the ASA criterion.
- Consider row 8. From row 7, the segments $\overline{AC}$ and $\overline{BC}$ are congruent. Therefore, by definition of congruent segments, these segments have the same measure.

## Partial Proofs Involving Isosceles Triangles, Midpoints and Bisectors

**Example:** In the diagram above, $M$ is the midpoint of $\overline{BC}$.

![](<../Source/6.1.1. Proofs Involving Isosceles Triangles/Images/910a9b6ebb99231469e88155ff1ab2b7.png>)

Consider the following statement:

> *The angles $\angle {BAM}$ and $\angle {CAM}$ are congruent.*

The table below gives the proof of the statement. Select the correct options in the table and thus complete the proof.

|  | Statement | Reason |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | $BM=CM$ | Definition of a midpoint |
| 2 | $\overline{BM} \cong \overline{CM}$ |  |
| 3 | $AB=6\,\textrm{cm}$ | Given |
| 4 | $AC=6\,\textrm{cm}$ | Given |
| 5 | $AB=AC$ |  |
| 6 | $\overline{AB} \cong \overline{AC}$ | Definition of congruent segments |
| 7 | $\overline{AM} \cong \overline{AM}$ |  |
| 8 | $\triangle ABM \cong \triangle ACM$ |  |
| 9 | $\angle {BAM}\cong \angle {CAM}$ | CPCTC |

**Explanation**

|  | Statement | Reason |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | $BM=CM$ | Definition of a midpoint |
| 2 | $\overline{BM} \cong \overline{CM}$ | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm{Definition of congruent segments}}$ |
| 3 | $AB=6\,\textrm{cm}$ | Given |
| 4 | $AC=6\,\textrm{cm}$ | Given |
| 5 | $AB=AC$ | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm{Transitive property of equality}}$ |
| 6 | $\overline{AB} \cong \overline{AC}$ | Definition of congruent segments |
| 7 | $\overline{AM} \cong \overline{AM}$ | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm{Reflexive property of congruence}}$ |
| 8 | $\triangle ABM \cong \triangle ACM$ | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm{SSS criterion}}$ |
| 9 | $\angle {BAM}\cong \angle {CAM}$ | CPCTC |

Let's examine the rows of the table with missing parts in turn.

- Consider row 2. From row 1, we have that $BM=CM$ Therefore, $\overline{BM} \cong \overline{CM}$ by the definition of congruent segments.
- Consider row 5. From rows 3 and 4, we have that $AB=6\,\textrm{cm}=AC$. Therefore, $AB=AC$ by the transitive property of equality.
- Consider row 7. Since every segment is congruent with itself, by the reflexive property of congruence, $\overline{AM} \cong \overline{AM}$.
- Consider row 8. From rows 2, 6, and 7, we have
$\overline{BM} \cong \overline{CM}, \qquad\overline{AB} \cong \overline{AC}, \qquad \overline{AM} \cong \overline{AM}$.
Therefore, $\triangle ABM \cong \triangle ACM$ by the SSS criterion.

## Constructing Complete Proofs of Statements Involving Isosceles Triangles

**Example:** In the diagram above, $\overline{AH} \perp \overline{BC}$, and $\overline{AB} \cong \overline{AC}$.

![](<../Source/6.1.1. Proofs Involving Isosceles Triangles/Images/0336b7da1021a11495b6f14cdc76bc31.png>)

Consider the following statement:

> *The angles $\angle {B}$ and $\angle {C}$ are congruent.*

The table below gives the proof of the statement. Select the correct options in the table and thus complete the proof.

|  | Statement | Reason |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | $\angle{AHB}$ and $\angle{AHC}$ are right angles |  |
| 2 | $\triangle{AHB}$ and $\triangle{AHC}$ are right triangles |  |
| 3 | $\overline{AB} \cong \overline{AC}$ |  |
| 4 | $\overline{AH} \cong \overline{AH}$ |  |
| 5 | $\triangle{AHB} \cong \triangle{AHC}$ |  |
| 6 | $\angle B \cong \angle C$ |  |

**Explanation**

|  | Statement | Reason |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | $\angle{AHB}$ and $\angle{AHC}$ are right angles | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\overline{AH} \perp \overline{BC}}$ |
| 2 | $\triangle{AHB}$ and $\triangle{AHC}$ are right triangles | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm{Definition of a right triangle}}$ |
| 3 | $\overline{AB} \cong \overline{AC}$ | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm{Given}}$ |
| 4 | $\overline{AH} \cong \overline{AH}$ | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm{Reflexive property of congruence}}$ |
| 5 | $\triangle{AHB} \cong \triangle{AHC}$ | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm{HL criterion}}$ |
| 6 | $\angle B \cong \angle C$ | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm{CPCTC}}$ |

Let's examine each row in turn.

1. We are given that $\overline{AH} \perp \overline{BC}$. Therefore, $\angle{AHB}$ and $\angle{AHC}$ are right angles by the definition of perpendicular lines.
2. By the definition of a right triangle, we have from row 1 that $\triangle{AHB}$ and $\triangle{AHC}$ are right triangles.
3. We are given that $\overline{AB} \cong \overline{AC}$ in the question stem.
4. The reflexive property of congruence states that every segment is congruent to itself. Therefore, $\overline{AH} \cong \overline{AH}$.
5. From rows 2, 3, and 4, we have that $\triangle{ABH}$ and $\triangle{ACH}$ are right triangles, and
$\overline{AB} \cong \overline{AC}, \qquad \overline{AH} \cong \overline{AH}$.
Therefore, $\triangle{AHB} \cong \triangle{AHC}$ by the HL criterion.
6. From row 5, we have $\triangle AH{\color{blue}B} \cong \triangle AH{\color{blue}C}$. Therefore, $\angle {\color{blue}B} \cong \angle {\color{blue}C}$ by CPCTC.
