## Introduction

Sometimes, we may be given a sequence in terms of an unknown variable. For example, suppose we're given the arithmetic sequence
$x,\quad x+3,\quad 8,\quad \ldots$,

where the value of $x$ is unknown. How can we use our knowledge of arithmetic sequences to solve for $x$?

To solve for $x$, we can calculate the common difference $d$ in two different ways and then set the results equal to each other.

We begin by calculating the common difference using the first and second terms:

$$
\begin{aligned}
d &= a_{2} - a_{1} \\
&= (x + 3) - (x) \\
&= x + 3 - x \\
&= 3
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we calculate the common difference using the second and third terms:

$$
\begin{aligned}
d &= a_{3} - a_{2} \\
&= (8) - (x + 3) \\
&= 8 - x - 3 \\
&= 5 - x
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we have two different expressions for the common difference: $d=3$ and $d=5-x$. These expressions must be equal, so we can set them equal to each other and then solve for $x$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
3 &= 5 - x \\
x &= 2
\end{aligned}
$$

## Solving for a Variable Given the Common Difference

**Example:** The first term of an arithmetic sequence is $x$, the fourth term is $3x-2$, and the common difference is $12$. What is the value of $x$?

**Explanation**

We are given the following information:

$$
a_{1} = x, a_{4} = 3x - 2, d = 12
$$

Using the general formula for the common difference, we construct an equation that we can solve for $x$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
d &= \frac{a_{n} - a_{m}}{n - m} \\
12 &= \frac{a_{4} - a_{1}}{4 - 1} \\
12 &= ((3x - 2) - x)/(4 - 1) \\
12 &= \frac{2x - 2}{3} \\
36 &= 2x - 2 \\
38 &= 2x \\
19 &= x
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, $x=19$.

## Solving for a Variable Given Three Consecutive Terms

**Example:** Given that
$\dfrac{1}{4}x,\quad x,\quad 2x-2$
are three consecutive terms of an arithmetic sequence, find the value of $x$.

**Explanation**

To solve for $x$, we can calculate the common difference $d$ in two different ways and then set the results equal to each other.

We begin by calculating the common difference using the first and second terms:

$$
\begin{aligned}
d &= a_{2} - a_{1} \\
&= x - \frac{1}{4}x \\
&= \frac{3}{4}x
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we calculate the common difference using the second and third terms:

$$
\begin{aligned}
d &= a_{3} - a_{2} \\
&= (2x - 2) - (x) \\
&= x - 2
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we have two different expressions for the common difference:

$$
d=\dfrac{3}{4}x
$$

and $d=x-2$. These expressions must be equal, so we can set them equal to each other and then solve for $x$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{3}{4}x &= x - 2 \\
2 + \frac{3}{4}x &= x \\
2 &= x - \frac{3}{4}x \\
2 &= \frac{1}{4}x \\
8 &= x
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, $x=8$.

## Solving for a Variable Given Three Nonconsecutive Terms

**Example:** Given that the $4$th term of an arithmetic sequence is $-x$, the $6$th term is $x-1$, and $9$th term is $x+1$, what is $x$?

**Explanation**

We are given the following information:

$$
a_{4} =-x, a_{6} = x - 1, a_{9} = x + 1
$$

To solve for $x$, we can calculate the common difference $d$ in two different ways and then set the results equal to each other.

We begin by calculating the common difference using $a_4$ and $a_6$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
d &= \frac{a_{6} - a_{4}}{6 - 4} \\
&= (x - 1 - (-x))/(6 - 4) \\
&= \frac{x - 1 + x}{2} \\
&= \frac{2x - 1}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we calculate the common difference using $a_6$ and $a_9$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
d &= \frac{a_{9} - a_{6}}{9 - 6} \\
&= ((x + 1) - (x - 1))/(9 - 6) \\
&= \frac{x + 1 - x + 1}{3} \\
&= \frac{2}{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we have two different expressions for the common difference:

$$
d=\dfrac{2x-1}{2}
$$

and

$$
d=\dfrac{2}{3}
$$

These expressions must be equal, so we can set them equal to each other and then solve for $x$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{2x - 1}{2} &= \frac{2}{3} \\
3 \cdot (2x - 1) &= 2 \cdot 2 \\
6x - 3 &= 4 \\
6x &= 7 \\
x &= \frac{7}{6}
\end{aligned}
$$
