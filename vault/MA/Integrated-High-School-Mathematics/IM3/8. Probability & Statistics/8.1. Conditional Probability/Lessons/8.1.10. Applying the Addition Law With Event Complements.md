## Introduction

![](<../Source/8.1.10. Applying the Addition Law With Event Complements/Images/a68f6e9ff78b40c20e464808a746302c.png>)

Recall that the addition law for probability states that

$$
P(A\cup B)= P(A)+P(B)-P(A\cap B)
$$

We can use the addition law with Venn diagrams to calculate probabilities involving event complements.

For example, suppose we wish to find $P(A' \cap B)$, where

$$
P(A) = \dfrac{1}{2}, \qquad P(B) = \dfrac{1}{4}, \qquad P(A \cap B) = \dfrac{3}{16}
$$

To solve this problem, we proceed as follows:

- First, we calculate $P(A\cup B)$ using the addition law.
- Next, we draw a Venn diagram.
- Finally, we shade the region corresponding to $A' \cap B$, deduce the required probability.

So, we start by calculating $P(A \cup B)$ using the addition law:

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(A∪B) &= P(A) + P(B) - P(A∩B) \\
&= \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{4} - \frac{3}{16} \\
&= \frac{8}{16} + \frac{4}{16} - \frac{3}{16} \\
&= \frac{9}{16}
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we construct our Venn diagram as usual. This gives the following:

![](<../Source/8.1.10. Applying the Addition Law With Event Complements/Images/46f06fbaec093759f57e44d96da1cb05.png>)

Then, we shade the region corresponding to the event $A' \cap B$. Remember that this region represents the overlap between all events *not* in $A$ with $B$.

![](<../Source/8.1.10. Applying the Addition Law With Event Complements/Images/c1c395c2c72a00f87b525bc9f0d8f636.png>)

Finally, we have

$$
P(A' \cap B) = \dfrac{1}{16}
$$

## Finding the Probability of an Intersection

**Example:** Given $P(A)=0.3$, $P(B)=0.5$, and $P(A\cup B)=0.7$, find $P(A'\cap B)$.

**Explanation**

First, we calculate $P(A\cap B)$ using the addition law:

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(A∪B) &= P(A) + P(B) - P(A∩B) \\
0.7 &= 0.3 + 0.5 - P(A∩B) \\
P(A∩B) &= 0.3 + 0.5 - 0.7 \\
P(A∩B) &= 0.1
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we construct the corresponding Venn diagram and shade the region $A'\cap B$.

![](<../Source/8.1.10. Applying the Addition Law With Event Complements/Images/cb945eebe175b9cf96e1be4d308d32d0.png>)

Finally, we have

$$
P(A'\cap B) = 0.4
$$

## Finding the Probability of a Union

**Example:** Given that $P(A)=\dfrac12$, $P(A \cap B)=\dfrac16$, and $P(A \cup B)=\dfrac23$, find $P(A'\cup B')$.

**Explanation**

First, we calculate $P(B)$ using the addition law:

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(A∪B) &= P(A) + P(B) - P(A∩B) \\
\frac{2}{3} &= \frac{1}{2} + P(B) - \frac{1}{6} \\
P(B) &= \frac{2}{3} + \frac{1}{6} - \frac{1}{2} \\
P(B) &= \frac{1}{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we construct the corresponding Venn diagram and shade the region $A'\cup B'$.

![](<../Source/8.1.10. Applying the Addition Law With Event Complements/Images/dfa6d853388443970cef0fa6c14c69db.png>)

Finally, we have

$$
P(A'\cup B') = \dfrac1{3} + \dfrac{1}{6} + \dfrac{1}{3} = \dfrac56
$$

## Finding the Probability of a Complement

**Example:** Given that $P(B)=0.6$, $P(A\cap B)=0.4$, and $P(A\cup B)=0.9$, find $P(A')$.

**Explanation**

First, we calculate $P(A)$ using the addition law:

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(A∪B) &= P(A) + P(B) - P(A∩B) \\
0.9 &= P(A) + 0.6 - 0.4 \\
P(A) &= 0.9 + 0.4 - 0.6 \\
P(A) &= 0.7
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(A^{′}) &= 1 - P(A) \\
&= 1 - 0.7 \\
&= 0.3
\end{aligned}
$$
