## Introduction

Suppose $A$ and $B$ are two events in a random experiment.
The conditional probability $P(A \mid B)$ is the probability that the event $A$ occurs *given that $B$ has already occurred*.

We've already seen how to compute conditional probabilities using Venn diagrams. But is there a formula that we can use?

By definition, we say that $P(A \mid B)$ is given by the formula

$$
P(A \mid B) = \dfrac{P(A\cap B)}{P(B)}
$$

For instance, let's suppose we have different shaped and colored candies in a bag, and we take one out at random. Let's assume that the probability of picking a green circle candy is $0.3$, while the probability of picking a green candy is $0.5$. Then, what is the probability that the candy we have picked is a circle, given that it is green?

Let's call $A$ the event "the candy is a green circle" and $B$ the event "the candy is green." Then we need to find $P(A \mid B)$.

- The probability of picking a green circle candy is $0.3$, so $P(A\cap B) = 0.3$.
- The probability of picking a green candy is $0.5$, therefore, $P(B) = 0.5$.

So, using the definition of a conditional probability, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(A \mid B) &= (P(A∩B))/(P(B)) \\
&= \frac{0.3}{0.5} \\
&= \frac{3}{5} \\
&= 0.6
\end{aligned}
$$

## Computing a Conditional Probability Given a Joint Probability

**Example:** Suppose that $A$ and $B$ are two events such that $P(B) = \dfrac23$ and $P(A\cap B) = \dfrac13$. What is $P(A \mid B)$?

**Explanation**

We use the definition of the conditional probability $P(A \mid B){:}$

$$
P(A \mid B) = \dfrac{P(A\cap B)}{P(B)}
$$

Substituting the given values, we get

$$
\begin{aligned} P(A \mid B)&=\dfrac{P(A\cap B)}{P(B)} \\[5pt] &=\dfrac{\left(\dfrac{1}{3}\right)}{\left(\dfrac{2}{3}\right)} \\[5pt] &=\dfrac{1}{2}. \end{aligned}
$$

## Computing a Conditional Probability: Word Problems

**Example:** A number is selected randomly from the set of integers between $1$ to $10$ inclusive. If the selected number is odd, then what is the probability that the number is less than $4$?

**Explanation**

Let us call $O$ the event "the number is odd" and $L$ the event " the number is less than $4$".

The required probability is $P(L \mid O)$, which is given by

$$
P(L \mid O)= \dfrac{P(O\cap L)}{P(O)}
$$

Let's find $P(O)$ and $P(O\cap L)$.

- There are $5$ odd numbers among the $10$ integers from $1$ to $10$, so we have
$P(O) = \dfrac{5}{10} = \dfrac{1}{2}$.
- There are only two numbers, $1$ and $3$, that are both odd and less than $4$. So, we have
$P(O\cap L) = \dfrac{2}{10} = \dfrac{1}{5}$.

Therefore, substituting the above into our formula for $P(L \mid O)$, we get

$$
P(L \mid O) = \dfrac{\left(\dfrac{1}{5}\right)}{\left(\dfrac12\right)} = \dfrac{2}{5}
$$

## The Multiplication Law for Conditional Probability

Given two events $A$ and $B$ in a random experiment, the definition of the conditional probability $P(A \mid B)$ is

$$
P(A \mid B) = \dfrac{P(A\cap B)}{P(B)}
$$

By multiplying both sides of the formula by $P(B)$, we get the **multiplication law for probability**, which states that

$$
P(A\cap B) = P(B) \cdot P(A \mid B)
$$

Also, since

$$
P(A\cap B) = P(B\cap A)
$$

we can also write the multiplication law in the following form:

$$
P(A\cap B) = P(A) \cdot P(B \mid A)
$$

---

<a id="computing-a-probability-using-the-multiplication-law-for-conditional-probability"></a>
## Computing a Probability Using the Multiplication Law for Conditional Probability

**Example:** Suppose that $A$ and $B$ are two events such that $P(A \mid B)=0.3$, $P(A)=0.5$, and $P(B)=0.6$. Find $P(B \mid A)$.

**Explanation**

Using the multiplication law for probability, we have that

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(A∩B) &= P(B) \cdot P(A \mid B) \\
&= 0.6 \cdot 0.3 \\
&= 0.18
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, substituting

$$
P(A\cap B) = 0.18
$$

and

$$
P(A)=0.5
$$

into the definition of $P(B \mid A)$, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
P(B \mid A) &= (P(B∩A))/(P(A)) \\
&= (P(A∩B))/(P(A)) \\
&= \frac{0.18}{0.5} \\
&= 0.36
\end{aligned}
$$

Notice that we used the fact that

$$
P(A\cap B) = P(B\cap A)
$$

## Computing a Probability Using the Multiplication Law for Conditional Probability: Word Problems

**Example:** A box contains $3$ red, $1$ blue, and $2$ green marbles. Two marbles are randomly drawn one by one without replacement. What is the probability that both marbles are red?

**Explanation**

Let us call $R_1$ the event "the first marble is red" and $R_2$ the event "the second marble is red."

The required probability is $P(R_1\cap R_2)$, which is given by

$$
P(R_1\cap R_2) = P(R_1) \cdot P(R_2 \mid R_1)
$$

Let's find $P(R_1)$ and $P(R_2 \mid R_1)$.

- There are $3$ red marbles among the $3+1+2=6$ marbles, so we have
$P(R_1) = \dfrac3{6}=\dfrac{1}{2}$.
- If a red marble is drawn, there are $2$ red marbles left in the box among the remaining $5$ marbles. So, we have
$P(R_2 \mid R_1) = \dfrac2{5}$.

Substituting the above data into the multiplication law for conditional probability, we get

$$
P(R_1\cap R_2)=\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot\dfrac{2}{5}=\dfrac{1}{5}
$$
