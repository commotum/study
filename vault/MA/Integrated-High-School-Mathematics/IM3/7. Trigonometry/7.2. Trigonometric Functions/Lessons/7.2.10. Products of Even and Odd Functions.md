## Introduction

Let $f(x)$ and $g(x)$ be the non-zero functions.
We have the following results concerning their products.

- If $f(x)$ and $g(x)$ are *both* even, then $f(x)\cdot g(x)$ is even.
- If $f(x)$ and $g(x)$ are *both* odd, then $f(x)\cdot g(x)$ is even.
- If $f(x)$ is even and $g(x)$ is odd, then $f(x)\cdot g(x)$ is odd.
- If $f(x)$ is odd and $g(x)$ is even, then $f(x)\cdot g(x)$ is odd.

We will prove these results at the end of the lesson. However, notice the similarity with the rules for multiplying positive and negative numbers:

- If $a$ and $b$ are *both* positive, then $a\cdot b$ is positive.
- If $a$ and $b$ are *both* negative, then $a\cdot b$ is positive.
- If $a$ is positive and $b$ is negative, then $a\cdot b$ is negative.
- If $a$ is negative and $b$ is positive, then $a\cdot b$ is negative.

## Classifying Products of Functions As Even or Odd

**Example:** Let $f(x)$ be a non-zero odd function, $g(x)$ be a non-zero even function, and $h(x)$ be a non-zero odd function. Is the function $f(x) \cdot g(x) \cdot h(x)$ even, odd, or neither?

**Explanation**

First, let's recall the following rules regarding the products of even and odd functions:

$$
even \cdot even = even; odd \cdot odd = even; even \cdot odd = odd; odd \cdot even = odd
$$

In our case, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
f(x) \cdot g(x) \cdot h(x) &= odd \cdot even \cdot odd \\
&= (odd \cdot even) \cdot odd \\
&= odd \cdot odd \\
&= even
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, $f(x) \cdot g(x) \cdot h(x)$ is an even function.

## Classifying a Product of Containing Particular Even and Odd Functions

**Example:** Which of the following statements are true?

1. $x^3-x^5$ is an even function
2. $\sin^2 x$ is an even function
3. $(x^3-x^5) \sin^2 x$ is an odd function

**Explanation**

First, let's recall the following rules regarding the products of even and odd functions:

$$
even \cdot even = even; odd \cdot odd = even; even \cdot odd = odd; odd \cdot even = odd
$$

With that in mind, let's examine our functions in turn.

- Statement I is false. Let $f(x) = x^3-x^5$. Then, $f(x)$ is odd since
$f(-x)|= (-x)^{3} - (-x)^{5}; =-x^{3} + x^{5}; = - (x^{3} - x^{5}); =-f(x)$
for all $x \in (-\infty,\infty)$.
- Statement II is true. Let $g(x) = \sin^2 x$. Then, $g(x)$ is even since
$g(-x)|= \sin^{2} (-x); = (-\sin x)^{2}; = \sin^{2} x; = g(x)$
for all $x \in (-\infty,\infty)$.
- Statement III is true. Notice that
$(x^{3} - x^{5}) \cdot \sin^{2} x|= f(x) \cdot g(x); = odd \cdot even; = odd$.

Therefore, the correct answer is "II and III only."

## Classifying Functions in a Product as Even or Odd

**Example:** Let $f(x) \cdot x^3$ be an odd function. Is the non-zero function $f(x)$ even, odd, or neither?

**Explanation**

First, let's recall the following rules regarding the products of the non-zero even and odd functions:

$$
even \cdot even = even; odd \cdot odd = even; even \cdot odd = odd; odd \cdot even = odd
$$

Notice that $x^3$ is an odd function since

$$
(-x)^3 = -x^3
$$

for all $x \in (-\infty,\infty)$.

Now, since $f(x) \cdot x^3$ and $x^3$ are odd, we have that $f(x)$ must be an even function:

$$
\underbrace{\color{blue}\text{even}}_{f(x)} \quad \cdot \quad \underbrace{\text{odd}}_{{\large x^3}} \quad = \quad \underbrace{\text{odd}}_{f(x) \,\cdot \,{\large x^3}}
$$

## Proof of the Results

Let's prove the results using this lesson by considering all possible cases for products of even and odd functions:

- Case I: Let $f(x)$ and $g(x)$ be non-zero even functions. Then, we have that
$f(-x) = f(x),\qquad g(-x) = g(x)$.
Now define $h(x) = f(x)\cdot g(x)$. Therefore,
$h(-x)|= f(-x) \cdot g(-x); = f(x) \cdot g(x); = h(x)$.
Since $h(x)$ is even, we conclude that $f(x)\cdot g(x)$ is even.
- Case II: Let $f(x)$ and $g(x)$ be non-zero odd functions. Then, we have that
$f(-x) = -f(x),\qquad g(-x) = -g(x)$.
Now define $h(x) = f(x)\cdot g(x)$. Therefore,
$h(-x)|= f(-x) \cdot g(-x); = (-f(x)) \cdot (-g(x)); = f(x) \cdot g(x); = h(x)$.
Since $h(x)$ is even, we conclude that $f(x)\cdot g(x)$ is even.
- Case III: Let $f(x)$ be non-zero even and $g(x)$ be non-zero odd. Then, we have that
$f(-x) = f(x),\qquad g(-x) = -g(x)$.
Now define $h(x) = f(x)\cdot g(x)$. Therefore,
$h(-x)|= f(-x) \cdot g(-x); = f(x) \cdot (-g(x)); = - (f(x) \cdot g(x)); =-h(x)$.
Since $h(x)$ is odd, we conclude that $f(x)\cdot g(x)$ is odd.
- Case IV: Let $f(x)$ be non-zero odd and $g(x)$ be non-zero even. Then, we have that
$f(-x) = -f(x),\qquad g(-x) = g(x)$.
Now define $h(x) = f(x)\cdot g(x)$. Therefore,
$h(-x)|= f(-x) \cdot g(-x); =-f(x) \cdot g(x); = - (f(x) \cdot g(x)); =-h(x)$.
Since $h(x)$ is odd, we conclude that $f(x)\cdot g(x)$ is odd.
