## Introduction

Suppose that Bobby and his dad took a $2$-hour car trip from Boston to Rochester. The car was traveling at a constant speed, and they traveled $70$ miles.

We can represent this information visually using a **distance-time** graph, where distance is plotted as a function of time. To construct the distance-time graph, we use the given information to find two points on the graph.

- At time $t=0$, the distance they had traveled was $0$ miles. So, our graph will start at the point $(0,0)$.
- We also know that it took them $2$ hours to travel $70$ miles, so our graph will pass through the point $(2,70)$.

Now, we plot these two points and connect them to form a line.

![](<../Source/2.2.5. Distance-Time Graphs/Images/e4e7ce520045b45917d2dc284e1c0b12.png>)

This is our distance-time graph! Its slope represents the **speed** of the car:

$$
m = \dfrac{70 - 0}{2- 0} = 35
$$

The units of speed are units of distance (miles) divided by units of time (hours). Therefore, the speed of the car is $35\, \textrm{miles/hour}$.

We can also use the slope to write the equation of the line. In slope-intercept form, the line can be written as

$$
s = mt + b
$$

We know that the $y$-intercept is $b=0$ and the slope is $m=35$. So, the equation of the line is

$$
s = 35t+0
$$

or more simply,
$s = 35t$.

## Calculating the Speed of an Object

**Example:** The graph above shows the distance traveled by a cyclist on a road over a period of $4$ seconds. What was the speed of the cyclist?

![](<../Source/2.2.5. Distance-Time Graphs/Images/e35b05c450c1ec67a6b94b639642b984.png>)

**Explanation**

The slope of the distance-time graph gives the speed. So, to compute the speed of the cyclist, we need to find its slope.

This line begins at $(0,1)$ and also contains the point $(2,7)$. So, we can find the speed by computing the slope as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
m &= \frac{y_{2} - y_{1}}{x_{2} - x_{1}} \\
&= \frac{7meters - 1meter}{2seconds - 0seconds} \\
&= 3meters/second
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the speed of the cyclist was $3\,\textrm{m/s}$.

## Cases When the Speed Varies

**Example:** The graph above shows the displacement of an eagle over a period of time. What was the speed of the eagle at time $t=4$?

![](<../Source/2.2.5. Distance-Time Graphs/Images/36d18435aceab0cb341706e70d89db32.png>)

**Explanation**

The slope of the distance-time graph gives the speed. However, the distance-time graph is composed of two lines with different slopes. The speed of the eagle changed at $t=1$ seconds.

We are interested in the eagle's speed at $t=4$ seconds. This corresponds to the second line. This line begins at $(1,3)$ and also contains the point $(5,5)$. So, we can find the speed by computing the slope as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
m &= \frac{y_{2} - y_{1}}{x_{2} - x_{1}} \\
&= \frac{5meters - 3meters}{5seconds - 1second} \\
&= \frac{2meters}{4seconds} \\
&= \frac{1}{2}meters/second
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, at $t=4$, the speed of the eagle was

$$
\dfrac12\,\textrm{m/s}
$$

<a id="sketching-a-distance-time-graph"></a>
## Sketching a Distance-Time Graph

**Example:** A moving object travels at a speed of $0.25 \, \textrm{m/s}$ during the first four seconds and at $1.75 \, \textrm{m/s}$ after that. Draw the corresponding distance-time graph.

**Explanation**

We start the first line from the point $(0,0)$. Since the speed of the object during the first four seconds was $0.25 \, \textrm{m/s}$, the object covered a distance of

$$
d=0.25\cdot 4 = 1 \, \textrm{m}
$$

So, the first line goes from $(0,0)$ to $(4,1)$.

![](<../Source/2.2.5. Distance-Time Graphs/Images/20e2aeaec9f79af78e144c0445d43898.png>)

We start the second line where the first line left off, at $(4,1)$. The speed of the object is now $1.75 \, \textrm{m/s}$, so if the object travels for one additional second, then it covers an additional distance of $1.75 \textrm{m}$.

The object has now traveled for a total time of

$$
4\textrm{s}+1\textrm{s}=5\textrm{s}
$$

and covered a total distance of

$$
1\textrm{m}+1.75\textrm{m}=2.75\textrm{m}
$$

So, we have another point $(5,2.75)$.

Finally, we draw the line from the point $(4,1)$ through the point $(5,2.75)$.

![](<../Source/2.2.5. Distance-Time Graphs/Images/35b0043c4e887c23e2662bca98562674.png>)
