## Introduction

Suppose that we want to solve, *without using graphs*, the following system of linear equations:

$$
{x + y = 1; 5x - y = 11
$$

To solve the system, we must combine the equations into a single equation that can be solved using algebraic manipulation. One way to combine the equations is to use the **elimination method.**

Notice that the coefficient of $y$ in the first equation is $1$, and the coefficient of $y$ in the second equation is $-1$. Since these numbers add to zero, we can *add* the two equations, *eliminate* the variable $y$, and solve the resulting equation for $x$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x + y &= 1 \\
5x - y &= 11 \\
(x + 5x) &= (1 + 11) \\
6x &= 12 \\
x &= 2
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the solution must have $x=2$. However, we still need to find $y$. To do this, we substitute $x=2$ back into one of the equations. Let's choose the first equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x + y &= 1 \\
(2) + y &= 1 \\
y &= -1
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the solution to the system of equations is $x=2$ and $y=-1$, which can be expressed as $(2, -1)$.

## Eliminating the Second Variable

**Example:** Find the solution $(a,b)$ to the following system of equations using the elimination method:

${a + b = 3; 4a - b = 7$

**Explanation**

Notice that the coefficient of $b$ in the first equation is $1$, and the coefficient of $b$ in the second equation is $-1$.

Since these numbers add to zero, we can add the two equations, *eliminate* the variable $b$, and solve the resulting equation for $a$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
a + b &= 3 \\
4a - b &= 7 \\
(a + 4a) &= (3 + 7) \\
5a &= 10 \\
a &= 2
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the solution must have $a=2$. However, we still need to find $b$. To do this, we substitute $a=2$ back into one of the equations. Let's choose the first equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
a + b &= 3 \\
(2) + b &= 3 \\
b &= 1
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the solution to the system of equations is $a=2, \,b=1$, which can be expressed as $(2, 1)$.

## Eliminating the First Variable

**Example:** Find the solution $(a,b)$ to the following system of equations using the elimination method:
${3a - b = 2; - 3a + 2b =-1$

**Explanation**

Notice that the coefficient of $a$ in the first equation is $3$, and the coefficient of $a$ in the second equation is $-3$.

Since these numbers add to zero, we can add the two equations, *eliminate* the variable $a$, and solve the resulting equation for $b$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
3a - b &= 2 \\
- 3a + 2b &= -1 \\
(-b + 2b) &= (2 + (-1)) \\
b &= 1
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the solution must have $b=1$. However, we still need to find $a$. To do this, we substitute $b=1$ back into one of the equations. Let's choose the first equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
3a - b &= 2 \\
3a - (1) &= 2 \\
3a &= 3 \\
a &= 1
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the solution to the system of equations is $a=1, \,b=1$, which can be expressed as

$$
\left(1,1\right)
$$

## Eliminating Variables From Systems of Linear Equations

Now, suppose that we want to solve the following system of linear equations using elimination:

$$
{x + 2y = 1; x - y =-2
$$

As it stands, adding the two equations will not eliminate any of the variables.

However, suppose we want to eliminate the variable $x$. We begin by multiplying the second equation by $\color{blue}-1$, which gives the following equivalent system:

$$
{(x + 2y = 1; (x - y =-2)\cdot (-1)
$$

Then, we add the two resulting equations together. This eliminates the variable $x$, making it possible to solve for $y$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x + 2y &= 1 \\
- x + y &= 2 \\
(2y + y) &= (1 + 2) \\
3y &= 3 \\
y &= 1
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the solution must have $y=1$. However, we still need to find $x$. To do this, we substitute $y=1$ back into one of the equations. Let's choose the second equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x - y &= -2 \\
x - (1) &= -2 \\
x &= -1
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the solution to the system of equations is $x=-1$ and $y=1$, which can be expressed as $(-1, 1)$.

## Determining the Result of a Transformation

**Example:** Consider the following system of equations.

${x + y = 3; 6x - 2y =-2$

The variable $y$ can be eliminated from the system using the following two steps:

1. Multiply the first equation by $2$ and obtain
$2x + 2y|= 6$.
2. Add the multiplied equation to the second equation to get
$⋯?⋯ = 4$.

What's the missing expression?

**Explanation**

Let's follow the steps provided:

**Step 1**: Multiplying the first equation by ${\color{blue}{2}}$ yields the following equivalent system:

$$
{(x + y = 3) \cdot (2); 6x - 2y =-2
$$

**Step 2**: Next, we add these two equations together:

$$
\require{cancel} \begin{array}{rl} 2x + \cancel{2y} & = 6 \\[5pt] 6x - \cancel{2y} & = -2 \\[5pt] \hline (2x + 6x) &= (6 - 2) \\[5pt] {\color{red}8x} &= 4 \end{array}
$$

Therefore, the missing expression is ${\color{red}8x}$.

## Solving a System of Equations

**Example:** Consider the following system of equations.
${3x - 2y = 9; x - 3y =-4$

The variable $x$ can be eliminated from the system using the following two steps:

1. Multiply the second equation by $-3$ and obtain
$-3x + 9y = 12$.
2. Add the multiplied equation to the first equation to get
$⋯?⋯ = 21$.

Find the missing expression, and hence find the solution $(x,y)$ of the system.

**Explanation**

Let's follow the steps provided:

**Step 1**: Multiplying the second equation by ${\color{blue}{-3}}$ yields the following equivalent system:

$$
{(3x - 2y = 9; (x - 3y =-4) \cdot (-3)
$$

**Step 2**: Next, we add these two equations together, *eliminate* the variable $x$, and solve the resulting equation for $y{:}$

$$
\require{cancel} \begin{array}{rl} \cancel{3x} - 2y & = 9 \\[5pt] -\cancel{3x} + 9y & = 12 \\[5pt] \hline (9y - 2y) & = (9 + 12) \\[5pt] {\color{red}7y} & = 21 \\[5pt] y & = 3 \end{array}
$$

So, the solution must have $y = 3$. However, we still need to find $x$. To do this, we substitute $y = 3$ back into one of the equations. Let's choose the second equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
x - 3y &= -4 \\
x - 3(3) &= -4 \\
x - 9 &= -4 \\
x &= 5
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the solution to the system of equations is

$$
x=5, \,y = 3
$$

which can be expressed as $(5,3)$.
