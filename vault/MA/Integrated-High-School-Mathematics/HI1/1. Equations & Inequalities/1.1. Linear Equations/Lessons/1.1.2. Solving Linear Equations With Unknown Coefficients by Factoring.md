## Introduction

When solving a multivariable equation for a particular variable, we sometimes have to factor some terms in the equation to reach the solution.

For example, let's solve the following equation for the variable $d$:

$$
2cd - 3d = 5
$$

First, we factor out $d$ in the expression on the left-hand side. This gives

$$
d(2c-3) = 5
$$

Then, we divide both sides of the equation by $(2c-3)$ to isolate $d$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
d(2c - 3) &= 5 \\
(d(2c - 3))/(2c - 3) &= \frac{5}{2c - 3} \\
(d(2c - 3))/(2c - 3) &= \frac{5}{2c - 3} \\
d &= \frac{5}{2c - 3}
\end{aligned}
$$

**Note:** This result assumes that

$$
2c-3\neq 0
$$

<a id="solving-linear-equations-by-factoring"></a>
## Solving Linear Equations by Factoring

**Example:** Solve the equation $2 = wz - 2w +1$ for the variable $w$.

**Explanation**

We need to isolate all terms with $w$ on one side of the equation. To do this, we subtract $1$ from both sides:

$$
\begin{aligned}
2 &= wz - 2w + 1 \\
2 - 1 &= wz - 2w + 1 - 1 \\
1 &= wz - 2w
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we factor out $w$ in the expression on the right-hand side.

$$
\begin{aligned}
1 &= wz - 2w \\
1 &= w(z - 2)
\end{aligned}
$$

Then, we divide both sides by $(z-2)$ to isolate $w$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
1 &= w(z - 2) \\
\frac{1}{z - 2} &= (w(z - 2))/(z - 2) \\
\frac{1}{z - 2} &= (w(z - 2))/(z - 2) \\
\frac{1}{z - 2} &= w \\
w &= \frac{1}{z - 2}
\end{aligned}
$$

**Note:** This result assumes that

$$
z-2\neq 0
$$

<a id="solving-linear-equations-with-variables-on-both-sides"></a>
## Solving Linear Equations With Variables on Both Sides

**Example:** Solve the equation $4ky=5y + 1$ for the variable $y$.

**Explanation**

We need to isolate all terms with $y$ on one side of the equation. To do this, we subtract $5y$ from both sides:

$$
\begin{aligned}
4ky &= 5y + 1 \\
4ky - 5y &= 5y + 1 - 5y \\
4ky - 5y &= 1
\end{aligned}
$$

Notice that all terms containing $y$ are on the left-hand side, and all terms independent of $y$ are on the right-hand side.

Next, we factor out $y$ in the expression on the left-hand side.

$$
\begin{aligned}
4ky - 5y &= 1 \\
y(4k - 5) &= 1
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we divide both sides by $(4k -5)$ to isolate $y$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
y(4k - 5) &= 1 \\
(y(4k - 5))/((4k - 5)) &= (1)/((4k - 5)) \\
(y(4k - 5))/((4k - 5)) &= \frac{1}{4k - 5} \\
y &= \frac{1}{4k - 5}
\end{aligned}
$$

**Note:** This result assumes that

$$
4k-5\neq 0
$$

<a id="solving-linear-equations-using-cross-multiplication"></a>
## Solving Linear Equations Using Cross Multiplication

**Example:** Solve the equation $\dfrac{xy}{3} = \dfrac{y - 1}{2}$ for the variable $y$.

**Explanation**

We need to isolate all terms with $y$ on one side of the equation. To do this, we first cross-multiply:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{xy}{3} &= \frac{y - 1}{2} \\
2 \cdot xy &= 3 \cdot (y - 1) \\
2xy &= 3y - 3
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we subtract $3y$ from both sides of the equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
2xy &= 3y - 3 \\
2xy - 3y &= 3y - 3 - 3y \\
2xy - 3y &= -3
\end{aligned}
$$

Notice that all terms containing $y$ are on the left-hand side, and all terms independent of $y$ are on the right-hand side.

Then, we factor out $y$ from the left-hand side:

$$
\begin{aligned}
2xy - 3y &= -3 \\
y(2x - 3) &= -3
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we divide both sides by $(2x - 3)$ to isolate $y$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
y(2x - 3) &= -3 \\
(y(2x - 3))/(2x - 3) &= \frac{-3}{2x - 3} \\
(y(2x - 3))/(2x - 3) &= \frac{-3}{2x - 3} \\
y &= -\frac{3}{2x - 3}
\end{aligned}
$$

**Note:** This result assumes that

$$
2x-3\neq 0
$$
