## Introduction

Integers are said to be **consecutive** if they follow each other in order. For example, the integers $7$, $8$, and $9$ are consecutive, as are the integers $-3$, $-2$, and $-1$.

If $n$ is an integer, then the next integer is $n+1$. This is followed by $n+2$, $n+3$, and so on. Thus, the integers

$$
n, \, n+1, \, n+2, \, n+3, \, \ldots
$$

are consecutive.

---

<a id="finding-consecutive-integers-with-a-given-sum"></a>
## Finding Consecutive Integers with a Given Sum

**Example:** Find three consecutive integers whose sum is $57$.

**Explanation**

Let $n$ be the first integer. Then the second integer is $n+1$, and the third is $n+2$.

Since the sum of these three integers is $57$, we have the equation

$$
n + (n+1) + (n+2) = 57
$$

We solve the equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
n + (n + 1) + (n + 2) &= 57 \\
3n + 3 &= 57 \\
3n + 3 - 3 &= 57 - 3 \\
3n &= 54 \\
\frac{3n}{3} &= \frac{54}{3} \\
n &= 18
\end{aligned}
$$

Since $n=18$, the first integer is $18$. Thus, the three consecutive integers are $18$, $19$, and $20$.

## Finding Consecutive Integers Given a Ratio of Largest to Smallest

**Example:** The largest of three consecutive integers is twice the smallest. What are the integers?

**Explanation**

Let $n$ be the first integer. Then the second integer is $n+1$ and the third is $n+2$.

The largest of the integers is $n+2$, and the smallest is $n$. Since the largest is twice the smallest, we have

$$
n+2 = 2n
$$

We solve the equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
n + 2 &= 2n \\
n + 2 - n &= 2n - n \\
2 &= n
\end{aligned}
$$

Since $n=2$, the first integer is $2$. Thus, the three consecutive integers are $2$, $3$, and $4$.

## Consecutive Even and Odd Integers

If the integer $n$ is even, then $n+1$ is odd, $n+2$ is even, $n+3$ is odd, $n+4$ is even, and so on. So,

$$
n, \, n+2, \, n+4, \, \ldots
$$

are consecutive *even* integers. For example $2,4,6,8$ are consecutive even integers.

Similarly, if $n$ is odd, then

$$
n, \, n+2, \, n+4, \, \ldots
$$

are consecutive *odd* integers. For example $5,7,9,11$ are consecutive odd integers.

<a id="finding-consecutive-even-or-odd-integers"></a>
## Finding Consecutive Even or Odd Integers

**Example:** Find four consecutive odd integers such that the largest is three times the smallest.

**Explanation**

Let $n$ be the first odd integer. Then the second odd integer is $n+2$, the third is $n+4$, and the fourth is $n+6$.

The largest of the integers is $n+6$, and the smallest is $n$. Since the largest is three times the smallest, we have

$$
n+6 = 3n
$$

We solve the equation:

$$
\begin{aligned}
n + 6 &= 3n \\
n + 6 - n &= 3n - n \\
6 &= 2n \\
\frac{6}{2} &= \frac{2n}{2} \\
3 &= n
\end{aligned}
$$

Since $n=3$, the first integer is $3$. Thus, the four consecutive odd integers are $3$, $5$, $7$, and $9$.
