## Introduction

Remember that in a $45^\circ$-$45^\circ$-$90^\circ$ triangle, both legs have the same length. Let $s$ denote the length of the legs, as shown below.

![](<../Source/10.4.4. The Area of an Isosceles Right Triangle/Images/b5180d6a230e52b0bee06364227935b8.png>)

Therefore, if we know the length of a leg of a $45^\circ$-$45^\circ$-$90^\circ$ triangle, we can compute its area. The base is of length $s$ and the height is of length $s$, so the area is given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= \frac{1}{2} \cdot s \cdot s \\
&= \frac{1}{2}s^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

There is some nice intuition behind the above formula: as shown below, the $45^\circ$-$45^\circ$-$90^\circ$ triangle covers half the area of a square with side length $s$.

![](<../Source/10.4.4. The Area of an Isosceles Right Triangle/Images/58b6ceeb915dc2756b21f7297583fd43.png>)

## Finding the Area of a 45-45-90 Triangle Given the Length of a Leg

**Example:** What is the area of the triangle shown below?

![](<../Source/10.4.4. The Area of an Isosceles Right Triangle/Images/f28a91b37ad4273ce37b58da24f2d20f.png>)

**Explanation**

Since the given triangle is a right triangle where the non-right angles are congruent, it must be a $45^\circ$-$45^\circ$-$90^\circ$ triangle.

![](<../Source/10.4.4. The Area of an Isosceles Right Triangle/Images/737a11223cdbe41075b58fcdf91bacd2.png>)

The area of a $45^\circ$-$45^\circ$-$90^\circ$ triangle with legs of length $s$ is given by

$$
\mathcal A = \dfrac{1}{2} s^2
$$

In our case, the legs have length

$$
s=2 \sqrt{7}
$$

so the area is given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= \frac{1}{2} \cdot (2\sqrt{7})^{2} \\
&= \frac{1}{2} \cdot 4 \cdot 7 \\
&= 14
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding a Side Length of a 45-45-90 Triangle Given the Area

**Example:** If the area of a $45^\circ$-$45^\circ$-$90^\circ$ triangle is $16$, then what is the length of one of its legs?

**Explanation**

The area of a $45^\circ$-$45^\circ$-$90^\circ$ triangle with legs of length $s$ is given by

$$
\mathcal A = \dfrac{1}{2} s^2
$$

We can solve for $s$ in terms of $\mathcal A$ as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{1}{2}s^{2} &= A \\
s^{2} &= 2A \\
s &= \sqrt{2A}
\end{aligned}
$$

In our case, the area is

$$
\mathcal A=16
$$

so the length of a leg is given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
s &= \sqrt{2 \cdot 16} \\
&= \sqrt{2} \cdot \sqrt{16} \\
&= 4\sqrt{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

## The Area of a 45-45-90 Triangle in Terms of the Hypotenuse

We can also find the area of a $45^\circ$-$45^\circ$-$90^\circ$ triangle if we're given the length of the hypotenuse.

First, recall that in a $45^\circ$-$45^\circ$-$90^\circ$ triangle, the length of the hypotenuse is $\sqrt{2}$ times the length of a leg. Therefore, the length of a leg is the length of the hypotenuse *divided* by $\sqrt{2}$.

So, if the hypotenuse has length $d$, then the legs have length

$$
\dfrac{d}{\sqrt 2}
$$

![](<../Source/10.4.4. The Area of an Isosceles Right Triangle/Images/70653956d4b8670bb59fb9a12b731e52.png>)

So, the area of the $45^\circ$-$45^\circ$-$90^\circ$ triangle shown above is given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= \frac{1}{2}(\frac{d}{\sqrt{2}})^{2} \\
&= \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{d^{2}}{2} \\
&= \frac{1}{4}d^{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

There is some nice intuition behind the above formula: as shown below, the $45^\circ$-$45^\circ$-$90^\circ$ triangle covers a quarter of a square with sides of length $d$.

![](<../Source/10.4.4. The Area of an Isosceles Right Triangle/Images/5968df3cfd0cc5c3b7834b14e0fa4225.png>)

## Finding the Area of a 45-45-90 Triangle Given the Length of the Hypotenuse

**Example:** What is the area of the triangle below?

![](<../Source/10.4.4. The Area of an Isosceles Right Triangle/Images/c21a00528418f5d2b9930f1ec87a00b1.png>)

**Explanation**

Since the given triangle is a right triangle where the non-right angles are congruent, it must be a $45^\circ$-$45^\circ$-$90^\circ$ triangle.

![](<../Source/10.4.4. The Area of an Isosceles Right Triangle/Images/c57cfb671e643802ba466ef4938dc7ea.png>)

The area of a $45^\circ$-$45^\circ$-$90^\circ$ triangle with a hypotenuse of length $d$ is given by

$$
\mathcal A = \dfrac{1}{4} d^2
$$

In our case, the hypotenuse has length

$$
d=\sqrt{3}
$$

so the area is given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= \frac{1}{4} \cdot (\sqrt{3})^{2} \\
&= \frac{1}{4} \cdot 3 \\
&= \frac{3}{4}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding a Hypotenuse of a 45-45-90 Triangle Given the Area

**Example:** If the area of a $45^\circ$-$45^\circ$-$90^\circ$ triangle is $16$, then what is the length of its hypotenuse?

**Explanation**

The area of a $45^\circ$-$45^\circ$-$90^\circ$ triangle with a hypotenuse of length $d$ is given by

$$
\mathcal A = \dfrac{1}{4} d^2
$$

We can solve for $d$ in terms of $\mathcal A$ as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{1}{4}d^{2} &= A \\
d^{2} &= 4A \\
d &= \sqrt{4A} \\
d &= 2\sqrt{A}
\end{aligned}
$$

In our case, the area is

$$
\mathcal A=16
$$

so the length of the hypotenuse is given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
d &= 2\sqrt{16} \\
&= 2 \cdot 4 \\
&= 8
\end{aligned}
$$
