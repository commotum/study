## Introduction

In this lesson, we'll learn how to prove the converse consecutive angle theorems and consolidate our knowledge of proving all consecutive angle theorems and their converses.

Consider the diagram below, where the lines $\overset{\longleftrightarrow}{AB}$ and $\overset{\longleftrightarrow}{CD}$ are crossed by a transversal $\overset{\longleftrightarrow}{PT}$,

$$
m\angle AQR=\alpha
$$

and

$$
m\angle QRC=180^\circ - \alpha
$$

![](<../Source/10.1.6. Further Proving Consecutive Angle Theorems/Images/c1d9abc43d74cdb360d40be228aa6c0e.png>)

Suppose we wish to *prove* the following statement:

> *$\overset{\longleftrightarrow}{AB}$ and $\overset{\longleftrightarrow}{CD}$ are parallel.*

Before we begin, note that in addition to the tools we've used previously, we'll need to make use of the *converse corresponding angles postulate,* which we state below:

> *If a transversal cuts two lines and the corresponding angles are congruent, then the lines are parallel.*

<a id="proving-the-converse-consecutive-interior-angle-theorems"></a>
## Proving the Converse Consecutive Interior Angle Theorems

![](<../Source/10.1.6. Further Proving Consecutive Angle Theorems/Images/ae17d0bc6c23e13e51bd82b8e853ccc7.png>)

We wish to prove the following statement:

> *$\overset{\longleftrightarrow}{AB}$ and $\overset{\longleftrightarrow}{CD}$ are parallel.*

Notice that, according to the given information, the angles $\angle{AQR}$ and $\angle{QRC}$ are supplementary. Thus, the statement we want to prove is the converse consecutive interior angles theorem!

Our strategy is to use the fact that $\angle AQR$ and $\angle AQP$ form a linear pair (and are therefore supplementary) and show this implies that the corresponding angles $\angle AQP$ and $\angle QRC$ must be congruent. By the converse corresponding angles postulate, this is enough to prove that the lines are parallel.

We can divide the proof into steps as follows:

- **Step 1**: We are *given* that $m\angle{AQR=\alpha}$ and $m\angle{QRC}= 180^\circ - \alpha$.
- **Step 2**: By the *linear pair postulate*, $\angle{AQR}$ and $\angle{AQP}$ are supplementary. Let's add this to our diagram.

![](<../Source/10.1.6. Further Proving Consecutive Angle Theorems/Images/e1cd1dc5ee270e6f371b4052f41611a1.png>)

- **Step 3**: Since $\angle{AQR}$ and $\angle{AQP}$ are supplementary, we have
$m\angle{AQR} + m\angle{AQP} = 180^\circ$
by the *definition of supplemetary angles*.
- **Step 4**: Using the *addition principle*, we can subtract $m\angle AQR$ from both sides of the equality from step 3. This gives
$m∠AQR + m∠AQP|= 180^{∘}; m∠AQR + m∠AQP - m∠AQR|= 180^{∘} - m∠AQR; m∠AQP|= 180^{∘} - m∠AQR$
- **Step 5**: From step 1, we have
$m\angle{AQR} = \alpha$.
Therefore, by *substituting* this into the result from step 4, we get
$m\angle AQP = 180^\circ -\alpha$.

- **Step 6**: From step 1, we have
$m\angle QRC = 180^\circ -\alpha$,
and in step 5, we showed that
$m\angle AQP = 180^\circ -\alpha$.
Therefore, by the *transitive property of equality*, we have
$m\angle AQP = m\angle{QRC}$.
- **Step 7**: Using the *definition of congruent angles*, we get
$\angle AQP \cong \angle{QRC}$.Let's add this to our diagram.

![](<../Source/10.1.6. Further Proving Consecutive Angle Theorems/Images/31dd692d4accb6f55ac3cab0bb22eb47.png>)

- **Step 8**: Finally, note that $\angle AQP$ and $\angle{QRC}$ are corresponding angles, and they are congruent from step 7. Consequently, by the *converse corresponding angles postulate,* *$\overset{\longleftrightarrow}{AB}$ and $\overset{\longleftrightarrow}{CD}$ are parallel,* as required.

It's **highly recommended** that you practice reconstructing this proof. Try to do this *without* referring to these notes.

## Stating the Full Proof Using the Two-Column Format

![](<../Source/10.1.6. Further Proving Consecutive Angle Theorems/Images/77fea13549554ac9d6654a40a765384d.png>)

The table below shows the proof of the converse of the consecutive interior angles theorem in a two-column format.

|  | Statement | Reason |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | $m\angle AQR = \alpha$ and $m\angle QRC = 180^\circ - \alpha$ | Given |
| 2 | $\angle AQR$ and $\angle AQP$ are supplementary angles | Linear pair postulate |
| 3 | $m\angle AQR + m\angle AQP = 180^\circ$ | Definition of supplementary angles |
| 4 | $m\angle AQP = 180^\circ - m\angle AQR$ | Addition principle |
| 5 | $m\angle AQP = 180^\circ - \alpha$ | Substitution |
| 6 | $m\angle AQP = m\angle QRC$ | Transitive property of equality |
| 7 | $\angle AQP \cong \angle QRC$ | Definition of congruent angles |
| 8 | $\overset{\longleftrightarrow}{AB} \parallel \overset{\longleftrightarrow}{CD}$ | Converse corresponding angles postulate |

Now, let's consider a proof of the converse consecutive *exterior* angles theorem.

## Proving Converse Consecutive Angle Theorems

**Example:** In the diagram above, $\angle 1$ and $\angle 8$ are supplementary.

![](<../Source/10.1.6. Further Proving Consecutive Angle Theorems/Images/530c065a59db34032830c3a96b36381d.png>)

Consider the following statement:

> *The lines $l$ and $m$ are parallel.*

The table below provides proof of the statement. Please complete the proof by filling in the missing entries.

|  | Statement | Reason |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | $\angle 1\cong \angle 3$ | Vertical angles theorem |
| 2 | $\angle 7$ and $\angle 8$ are supplementary angles |  |
| 3 | $m\angle 7 + m\angle 8 = 180^\circ$ | Definition of supplementary angles |
| 4 | $\angle 1$ and $\angle 8$ are supplementary angles | Given |
| 5 | $m\angle 1 + m\angle 8 = 180^\circ$ | Definition of supplementary angles |
| 6 | $180^\circ = m\angle 1 + m\angle 8$ | Symmetric property of congruence |
| 7 | $m\angle 7 + m\angle 8 = m\angle 1 + m\angle 8$ |  |
| 8 | $m\angle 7 = m\angle 1$ | Addition principle |
| 9 | $\angle 7 \cong \angle 1$ |  |
| 10 | $\angle 7 \cong \angle 3$ | Transitive property of congruence |
| 11 | $l$ and $m$ are parallel lines |  |

**Explanation**

The correct proof is shown below.

|  | Statement | Reason |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | $\angle 1\cong \angle 3$ | Vertical angles theorem |
| 2 | $\angle 7$ and $\angle 8$ are supplementary angles | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm{Linear pair postulate}}$ |
| 3 | $m\angle 7 + m\angle 8 = 180^\circ$ | Definition of supplementary angles |
| 4 | $\angle 1$ and $\angle 8$ are supplementary angles | Given |
| 5 | $m\angle 1 + m\angle 8 = 180^\circ$ | Definition of supplementary angles |
| 6 | $180^\circ = m\angle 1 + m\angle 8$ | Symmetric property of congruence |
| 7 | $m\angle 7 + m\angle 8 = m\angle 1 + m\angle 8$ | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm{Transitive property of equality}}$ |
| 8 | $m\angle 7 = m\angle 1$ | Addition principle |
| 9 | $\angle 7 \cong \angle 1$ | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm{Definition of congruence}}$ |
| 10 | $\angle 7 \cong \angle 3$ | Transitive property of congruence |
| 11 | $l$ and $m$ are parallel lines | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm{Converse corresponding angles postulate}}$ |

Let's examine the rows of the table with missing parts in turn.

- Consider row 2. Notice from the diagram that $\angle 7$ and $\angle 8$ form a linear pair. The linear pair postulate states that such angles must be supplementary. Therefore, $\angle{7}$ and $\angle{8}$ are supplementary angles.
- Consider row 7. From rows 3 and 6, we have
${\color{blue}{m\angle 7 + m\angle 8}} = 180^\circ \quad\textrm{and}\quad 180^\circ = {\color{blue}{m\angle 1 + m\angle 8}}$.
Therefore, by the transitive property of equality, we have
${\color{blue}{m\angle 7 + m\angle 8}} = {\color{blue}{m\angle 1 + m\angle 8}}$.
- Consider row 9. From row 8, we have that $m\angle 7 = m\angle 1$. Therefore, by the definition of congruent angles, these angles are congruent:
$\angle 7 \cong \angle 1$
- Consider row $11$. From row $10$, we have $\angle 7 \cong \angle 3$. Since these are corresponding angles, by the converse corresponding angles postulate, the lines $l$ and $m$ are parallel.

## Constructing Complete Proofs of the Consecutive Angle Theorems

**Example:** In the diagram above, the parallel lines $\overset{\longleftrightarrow}{AB}$ and $\overset{\longleftrightarrow}{CD}$ are crossed by the transversal $\overset{\longleftrightarrow}{EF}$.

![](<../Source/10.1.6. Further Proving Consecutive Angle Theorems/Images/a7a63da974eeafb3f999596cc845c904.png>)

Consider the following statement:

> $\angle{BJI}$ and $\angle{DIJ}$ are supplementary.

The table below provides proof of the statement. Complete the proof by filling in the missing entries.

|  | Statement | Reason |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | $\angle{AJF} \cong \angle{BJI}$ |  |
| 2 | $\angle{CIJ} \cong \angle{AJF}$ |  |
| 3 | $\angle{CIJ} \cong \angle{BJI}$ |  |
| 4 | $m\angle{CIJ} = m\angle{BJI}$ |  |
| 5 | $\angle{CIJ}$ and $\angle{DIJ}$ are supplementary |  |
| 6 | $m\angle{CIJ} + m\angle{DIJ} = 180^\circ$ |  |
| 7 | $m\angle{BJI} + m\angle{DIJ} = 180^\circ$ |  |
| 8 | $\angle{BJI}$ and $\angle{DIJ}$ are supplementary |  |

**Explanation**

The correct proof is shown below.

|  | Statement | Reason |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | $\angle{AJF} \cong \angle{BJI}$ | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm{Vertical angles theorem}}$ |
| 2 | $\angle{CIJ} \cong \angle{AJF}$ | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm{Corresponding angles postulate}}$ |
| 3 | $\angle{CIJ} \cong \angle{BJI}$ | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm{Transitive property of congruence}}$ |
| 4 | $m\angle{CIJ} = m\angle{BJI}$ | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm{Definition of congruent angles}}$ |
| 5 | $\angle{CIJ}$ and $\angle{DIJ}$ are supplementary | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm{Linear pair postulate}}$ |
| 6 | $m\angle{CIJ} + m\angle{DIJ} = 180^\circ$ | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm{Definition of supplementary angles}}$ |
| 7 | $m\angle{BJI} + m\angle{DIJ} = 180^\circ$ | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm{Substitution}}$ |
| 8 | $\angle{BJI}$ and $\angle{DIJ}$ are supplementary | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm{Definition of supplementary angles}}$ |

Let's examine each row in turn.

1. Note that $\angle AJF$ and $\angle BJI$ are vertical angles. Therefore, these angles are congruent by the vertical angles theorem.
2. Note that $\angle {CIJ}$ and $\angle{AJF}$ are corresponding angles. Therefore, these angles are congruent by the corresponding angles postulate.
3. From rows 2 and 1, we have
${\color{blue}{\angle{CIJ}}} \cong \angle{AJF}\quad\textrm{and}\quad\angle{AJF} \cong {\color{blue}{\angle{BJI}}}$.
Therefore, by the transitive property of congruence, we have
${\color{blue}{\angle{CIJ}}} \cong {\color{blue}{\angle{BJI}}}$.
4. From row 3, we have $\angle{CIJ} \cong \angle{BJI}$. Therefore, we have that $m\angle{CIJ} = m\angle{BJI}$ by the definition of congruent angles.
5. Note that $\angle {CIJ}$ and $\angle {DIJ}$ form a linear pair. Therefore, these angles are supplementary by the linear pair postulate.
6. From row 5, we have that $\angle {CIJ}$ and $\angle {BJI}$ are supplementary angles. Therefore, by the definition of supplementary angles, $m\angle{CIJ} + m\angle{DIJ} = 180^\circ$.
7. From row 6, we have
${\color{blue}{m\angle{CIJ}}} + m\angle{DIJ} = 180^\circ$.
From row 4, we have ${\color{blue}{m\angle{CIJ}}} = m\angle{BJI}$. Substituting this into the above, we have
$m\angle{BJI} + m\angle{DIJ} = 180^\circ$.
8. From row 7, we have that $m\angle{BJI} + m\angle{DIJ} = 180^\circ$. Therefore, these angles are supplementary by the definition of supplementary angles.
