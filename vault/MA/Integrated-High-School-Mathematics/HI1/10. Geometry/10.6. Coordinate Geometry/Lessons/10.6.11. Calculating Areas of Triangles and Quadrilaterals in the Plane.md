## Introduction

Suppose we would like to find the area of $\triangle ABC$, where $A(0,0)$, $B(6,0)$, $C(6,3)$. First, let's plot the triangle using the given coordinates.

![](<../Source/10.6.11. Calculating Areas of Triangles and Quadrilaterals in the Plane/Images/6803f5ff3aaaee7297fd9b21139e0ffb.png>)

Notice that $\triangle ABC$ is a right triangle with legs $\overline{AB}$ and $\overline{BC}$.

Since $\overline{AB}$ is horizontal, we can calculate the length of this segment by computing the absolute value of the difference between the $x$-coordinates of the endpoints:

$$
\begin{aligned}
AB &= \mid x_{B} - x_{A} \mid \\
&= \mid 6 - 0 \mid \\
&= 6
\end{aligned}
$$

Similarly, since $\overline{BC}$ is vertical, we can calculate the length of this segment by computing the absolute value of the difference between the $y$-coordinates of the endpoints:

$$
\begin{aligned}
BC &= \mid y_{C} - y_{B} \mid \\
&= \mid 3 - 0 \mid \\
&= 3
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, using the formula for the area of the triangle, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= \frac{AB \cdot BC}{2} \\
&= \frac{6 \cdot 3}{2} \\
&= 9
\end{aligned}
$$

## Calculating the Area of a Triangle in the Coordinate Plane

**Example:** Calculate the area of $\triangle ABC$ given that $A(1,1)$, $B(6,1)$, $C(4,3)$.

**Explanation**

Let's plot the triangle using the given coordinates.

![](<../Source/10.6.11. Calculating Areas of Triangles and Quadrilaterals in the Plane/Images/a32adb7c6febb8ee4267d37cf2bf0167.png>)

Since the $y$-coordinates of $A$ and $B$ are the same, the side $\overline{AB}$ must be parallel to the $x$-axis. Let's use this side as a base of the triangle.

From the diagram, we can calculate the length of the base, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
AB &= \mid x_{B} - x_{A} \mid \\
&= \mid 6 - 1 \mid \\
&= 5
\end{aligned}
$$

Here, $H$ is the point on $\overleftrightarrow{AB}$ such that $\overline{CH}\perp \overline{AB}$. Since $H$ lies on $\overleftrightarrow{AB}$, the $y$-coordinate of $H$ must be equal to $1$ too.

From the diagram, we can now calculate the length of the height:

$$
\begin{aligned}
CH &= \mid y_{C} - y_{H} \mid \\
&= \mid 3 - 1 \mid \\
&= 2
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the area of the triangle is

$$
\begin{aligned}
A_{ABC} &= \frac{AB \cdot CH}{2} \\
&= \frac{5 \cdot 2}{2} \\
&= 5
\end{aligned}
$$

## Calculating the Area of a Triangle Formed by a Line and the Coordinate Axes

**Example:** The line $l$ with slope $m=-2$ passes through the point $P(2,2)$. Calculate the area of the triangle bounded by $l$ and the coordinate axes.

**Explanation**

First, we find the equation of $l$.

Since the slope is $m=-2$, the equation of $l$ is

$$
y=-2x + k
$$

where $k$ is a constant. Next, since $P$ lies on $l$, substituting the coordinates of the point into the equation, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
2 &= -2 \cdot 2 + k \\
k &= 6
\end{aligned}
$$

So, the equation of $l$ is

$$
y=-2x+6
$$

Let $A(x_A,0)$ be the intersection point between $l$ and the $x$-axis. Substituting the coordinates into the equation of the line, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
0 &= -2x_{A} + 6 \\
x_{A} &= 3
\end{aligned}
$$

Let $B(0,y_B)$ be the intersection point between $l$ and the $y$-axis. Substituting the coordinates into the equation of the line, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
y_{B} &= -2 \cdot 0 + 6 \\
y_{B} &= 6
\end{aligned}
$$

As a result, we obtain the following right triangle.

![](<../Source/10.6.11. Calculating Areas of Triangles and Quadrilaterals in the Plane/Images/c8dfbdfb570412f575d3b539fc9e0fdc.png>)

From the diagram, we can calculate the length of the legs:

$$
\begin{aligned}
AB &= \mid x_{B} - x_{A} \mid \\
&= \mid 3 - 0 \mid \\
&= 3 \\
AC &= \mid y_{C} - y_{A} \mid \\
&= \mid 6 - 0 \mid \\
&= 6
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the area of the triangle is

$$
\begin{aligned}
A_{△ABC} &= \frac{AB \cdot AC}{2} \\
&= \frac{3 \cdot 6}{2} \\
&= 9
\end{aligned}
$$

## Calculating the Area of a Parallelogram in the Coordinate Plane

**Example:** Calculate the area of the parallelogram shown below.

![](<../Source/10.6.11. Calculating Areas of Triangles and Quadrilaterals in the Plane/Images/2921ef032cfd6432cfd00b74f4a85829.png>)

**Explanation**

The area of a parallelogram is given by

$$
\mathcal{A} = b \cdot h
$$

where $b$ is the base and $h$ is the height.

In our case, we have $b=AB=CD$ and $h=BD$.

From the diagram, we can calculate the lengths of $\overline{AB}$ and $\overline{BD}$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
AB &= \sqrt{(x_{B} - x_{A})^{2} + (y_{B} - y_{A})^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{(0 - (-3))^{2} + (0 - (2))^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{9 + 4} \\
&= \sqrt{13} \\
BD &= \sqrt{(x_{D} - x_{B})^{2} + (y_{D} - y_{B})^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{(2 - 0)^{2} + (3 - 0)^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{4 + 9} \\
&= \sqrt{13}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, the area of the parallelogram is

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= AB \cdot BD \\
&= \sqrt{13} \cdot \sqrt{13} \\
&= 13
\end{aligned}
$$
