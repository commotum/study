## Introduction

When we take the absolute value of a *function*, the positive values of the function remain unchanged, but the absolute value operation removes the minus sign from any negative values.

As a consequence, to get the graph of

$$
y = \mid f(x) \mid
$$

from the graph of $y=f(x)$,

- any parts that lie on or above the $x$-axis will remain unchanged, and
- any parts that lie *below* the $x$-axis will be reflected in the $x$-axis.

For example, consider the graph of $y=f(x)$, shown below. What is the graph of

$$
y= \mid f(x) \mid
$$

![](<../Source/2.2.13. Absolute Value Graph Transformations/Images/b2d581691b2cc9d953d4b13bef77a98b.png>)

To get the graph of

$$
y= \mid f(x) \mid
$$

from the graph of $y=f(x)$, we take the part of $y=f(x)$ that lies below the $x$-axis and reflect it across the $x$-axis.

![](<../Source/2.2.13. Absolute Value Graph Transformations/Images/0e830c977ee1129bb31e78ca976fecd3.png>)

## Plotting the Absolute Value of the Argument of a Function

Suppose we have the graph $y=f(x)$. How do we plot the graph of

$$
y = f(\mid x \mid)
$$

First, notice that since

$$
\left\begin{vmatrix}-x\right & = & x\end{vmatrix}
$$

we have that

$$
f(\left\begin{vmatrix}x\right &)=f(\left|-x\right\end{vmatrix})
$$

Therefore, the values of $f(\mid x \mid)$ will be equal for corresponding positive and negative values of $x$. Moreover, every input is non-negative. Thus, to plot the graph of

$$
y = f(\mid x \mid)
$$

from the graph of $y=f(x)$, we reflect the *right* half of the graph across the $y$-axis.

For example, the graph of $y=f(x)$ is shown below. What is the graph of

$$
y=f(\mid x \mid)
$$

![](<../Source/2.2.13. Absolute Value Graph Transformations/Images/10d7435547fc87616da3b965d495ff7f.png>)

To get the graph of

$$
y=f(\mid x \mid)
$$

from the graph of $y=f(x)$, we reflect the part of $y=f(x)$ that lies to the right of the $y$-axis across the $y$-axis.

![](<../Source/2.2.13. Absolute Value Graph Transformations/Images/5d0f87efc93caf76469c773812e2a66d.png>)

**Watch out:** Pay attention to the notation. Although they look similar,

$$
f(\begin{vmatrix}x &) \neq & f(x)\end{vmatrix}
$$

These two functions are defined differently and have different graphs!

![](<../Source/2.2.13. Absolute Value Graph Transformations/Images/e0a374215db0038a45958ee3e78331ae.png>)

## Plotting the Absolute Value of a Function and Its Argument

**Example:** The graph of $y=f(x)$ is shown below. What is the graph of $y= \mid f(\begin{vmatrix}x &)\end{vmatrix}$?

![](<../Source/2.2.13. Absolute Value Graph Transformations/Images/5b8f510bfce1cb99512d07559a962b9a.png>)

**Explanation**

We find the graph of

$$
y= \mid f(\begin{vmatrix}x &)\end{vmatrix}
$$

in two steps.

- To get the graph of $y=f(\mid x \mid)$ from the graph of $y=f(x)$, we reflect the part of $y=f(x)$ that lies to the right of the $y$-axis across the $y$-axis.

![](<../Source/2.2.13. Absolute Value Graph Transformations/Images/29ca301901fbb3e60bf761c43a5a786b.png>)

- Then, to get the graph of $y= \mid f(\begin{vmatrix}x &)\end{vmatrix}$ from the graph of $y=f(\mid x \mid)$, we reflect the part of $y=f(\mid x \mid)$ that lies below the $x$-axis across the $x$-axis.

![](<../Source/2.2.13. Absolute Value Graph Transformations/Images/3c0847cae222e3b7961fd72b7b20eafa.png>)
