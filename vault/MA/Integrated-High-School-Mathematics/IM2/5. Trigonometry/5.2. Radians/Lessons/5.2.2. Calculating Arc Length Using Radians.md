## Introduction

Suppose that we are given a sector of a circle of radius $4\,\textrm{cm}$, like the one in the diagram below, where

$$
m\angle AOB = \dfrac{\pi}4
$$

radians. How can we find the length of the arc $\overset{\frown}{AB}$?

![](<../Source/5.2.2. Calculating Arc Length Using Radians/Images/a0ab710433a62119878410343df1ceee.png>)

Let the length of the arc $\overset{\frown}{AB}$ be denoted by $s$. To find $s$, we can use the **arc length formula** stated below:

$$
s = r \cdot \theta
$$

where $r$ is the radius of the circle and $\theta$ is the central angle. Note that for this formula to work, $\theta$ *must* be in radians.

In our example, we have $r = 4$ and

$$
\theta = \dfrac{\pi}{4}
$$

So, the arc length is given by

$$
\begin{aligned}
s &= 4 \cdot \frac{π}{4} = πcm
\end{aligned}
$$

**Note:** To see where the arc length formula comes from, the trick is to set up a proportion.

The ratio of the arc length $s$ to the circumference $C$ must be equal to the ratio of the angle $\theta$ to a full revolution of $2\pi$ radians. So, we have

$$
\dfrac{s}{C} = \dfrac{\theta}{2\pi}
$$

Substituting $C = 2\pi r$ and making $s$ the subject of the equation, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{s}{2πr} &= \frac{θ}{2π} \\
2π \cdot \frac{s}{2πr} &= 2π \cdot \frac{θ}{2π} \\
\frac{s}{r} &= θ \\
s &= rθ
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding an Arc Length Using the Arc Length Formula

**Example:** The diagram below shows a sector of a circle of radius $10 \textrm{cm}$. Given that the measure of its central angle is $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$ radians, find the length of the arc $\overset{\frown}{AB}$.

![](<../Source/5.2.2. Calculating Arc Length Using Radians/Images/0e6292ed7336c1fd0202dab502d492c6.png>)

**Explanation**

The central angle is

$$
\theta = \dfrac{\pi}{4}
$$

radians and the radius is

$$
r = 10\,\textrm{cm}
$$

We find the length of $\overset{\frown}{AB}$ using the arc length formula:

$$
s = r \cdot θ = 10 \cdot \frac{π}{4} = \frac{5π}{2}cm
$$

## Finding the Measure of an Angle Using the Arc Length Formula

**Example:** Given that the length of the arc $\overset{\frown}{AB}$ in the circular sector below is $6\,\textrm{cm}$, find the value of $\theta$ in radians.

![](<../Source/5.2.2. Calculating Arc Length Using Radians/Images/30ee31a03cc91f299361f03cdc595467.png>)

**Explanation**

Here, the length of the arc is

$$
s = 6\,\textrm{cm}
$$

and the radius is

$$
r = 5\,\textrm{cm}
$$

Rearranging the arc length formula, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
s &= r \cdot θ \\
θ &= \frac{s}{r}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\theta = \dfrac{s}{r} = \dfrac{6}{5} \, \text{rad}
$$

## Determining the Radius of a Circular Sector Using the Arc Length Formula

**Example:** Given that the length of the arc $\overset{\frown}{AB}$ in the circle below is $4\,\textrm{cm}$, and that $\theta =2$ radians, what is the radius of the circle?

![](<../Source/5.2.2. Calculating Arc Length Using Radians/Images/94ead8bac2734d1d362dc4792172a18b.png>)

**Explanation**

Here, the length of the arc is

$$
s=4 \, \textrm{cm}
$$

and the central angle is $\theta=2$.

Rearranging the arc length formula, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
s &= r \cdot θ \\
r &= \frac{s}{θ}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
r = \dfrac{s}{\theta} = \dfrac{4}{2} = 2 \, \textrm{cm}
$$
