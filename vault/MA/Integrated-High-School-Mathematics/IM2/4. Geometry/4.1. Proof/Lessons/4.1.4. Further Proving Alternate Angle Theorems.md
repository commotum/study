## Introduction

In this lesson, we'll learn how to prove the converse alternate angle theorems and consolidate our knowledge of proving all alternate angle theorems and their converses.

Consider the diagram below, where the lines $l$ and $m$ are crossed by a transversal $t$, and $\angle TYZ \cong \angle UZY$.

![](<../Source/4.1.4. Further Proving Alternate Angle Theorems/Images/b807ecfc5c0b4023a95ecbbd0e553b62.png>)

Suppose we wish to prove the following statement:

> *$l$ and $m$ are parallel.*

In other words, we want to prove the converse alternate interior angles theorem!

To prove this theorem, we start with $\angle TYZ \cong \angle UZY$ and construct a sequence of statements to show that the lines $l$ and $m$ must be parallel. To do this, we'll need the *converse corresponding angles postulate*, which we restate here:

> *If a transversal cuts two lines and the corresponding angles are congruent, then the lines are parallel.*

We now proceed to proving that $l$ and $m$ are parallel.

<a id="proving-the-converse-alternate-angle-theorems"></a>
## Proving the Converse Alternate Angle Theorems

![](<../Source/4.1.4. Further Proving Alternate Angle Theorems/Images/1ed41148b3ffc5249091550a64fac66a.png>)

We wish to prove the following statement:

> *$l$ and $m$ are parallel.*

We start by considering the angle $\angle TYZ$.

![](<../Source/4.1.4. Further Proving Alternate Angle Theorems/Images/ff8f8e9c8f77fee9441419feb051c83e.png>)

Our strategy is to construct a sequence of congruent angles, two of which are corresponding angles, and then use the corresponding angles postulate to conclude that the lines must be parallel.

We complete the proof as follows:

- **Step 1**: By the *vertical angles theorem*, $\angle TYZ$ and $\angle WYS$ are congruent. Let's add this to our diagram.

![](<../Source/4.1.4. Further Proving Alternate Angle Theorems/Images/1191ffe266354f07ab4b97a98942de03.png>)

- **Step 2**:
In step 1, we showed that
$\angle WYS \cong \angle TYZ$.
We are also given that
$\angle TYZ \cong \angle UZY$.
Therefore, by the *transitive property of congruence*, we have
$\angle WYS \cong \angle UZY$
as shown.

![](<../Source/4.1.4. Further Proving Alternate Angle Theorems/Images/9c2bcbd4ef589157fb6ea5d4f7039422.png>)

- **Step 3**: In step 2, we showed that the corresponding angles $\angle WYS$ and $\angle UZY$ are congruent. Therefore, by the *converse corresponding angles postulate,* $l$ and $m$ must be parallel, as required.

## Stating the Full Proof Using the Two-Column Format

![](<../Source/4.1.4. Further Proving Alternate Angle Theorems/Images/f36dd2e34f965aadd3e3f3b7d09f10c2.png>)

The table below shows our proof of the converse alternate interior angles theorem in a two-column format.

|  | Statement | Reason |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | $\angle WYS \cong \angle TYZ$ | Vertical angles theorem |
| 2 | $\angle TYZ \cong \angle UZY$ | Given |
| 3 | $\angle WYS \cong \angle UZY$ | Transitive property of congruence |
| 4 | $l$ and $m$ are parallel | Converse corresponding angles postulate |

Let's now look at a proof of the converse alternate *exterior* angles theorem.

<a id="proving-the-converse-alternate-angles-theorems"></a>
## Proving the Converse Alternate Angles Theorems

**Example:** In the diagram above, the lines $\overleftrightarrow{AB}$ and $\overleftrightarrow{CD}$ are crossed by the transversal $\overleftrightarrow{EF}$ and $\angle EGB \cong \angle CHF$.

![](<../Source/4.1.4. Further Proving Alternate Angle Theorems/Images/00637f76cc9c527ef0795b8772f32eb6.png>)

Consider the following statement:

> *$\overleftrightarrow{AB}$ and $\overleftrightarrow{CD}$ are parallel.*

The table below gives the proof of the statement. Fill in the missing entries in the table and thus complete the proof.

|  | Statement | Reason |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | $\angle CHF$ and $\angle FHD$ are supplementary |  |
| 2 | $m\angle CHF + m\angle FHD = 180^\circ$ | Definition of supplementary angles |
| 3 | $m\angle CHF = 180^\circ - m\angle FHD$ |  |
| 4 | $\angle EGB$ and $\angle BGH$ are supplementary | Linear pair postulate |
| 5 | $m\angle EGB + m\angle BGH = 180^\circ$ | Definition of supplementary angles |
| 6 | $\angle EGB \cong \angle CHF$ | Given |
| 7 | $m\angle EGB = 180^\circ - m\angle FHD$ |  |
| 8 | $(180^\circ - m\angle FHD) + m\angle BGH = 180^\circ$ | Substitution |
| 9 | $m\angle BGH = m\angle FHD$ | Addition principle |
| 10 | $\angle BGH \cong \angle FHD$ | Definition of congruent angles |
| 11 | $\overleftrightarrow{AB}$ and $\overleftrightarrow{CD}$ are parallel |  |

**Explanation**

The correct proof is shown below.

|  | Statement | Reason |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | $\angle CHF$ and $\angle FHD$ are supplementary | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm{Linear pair postulate}}$ |
| 2 | $m\angle CHF + m\angle FHD = 180^\circ$ | Definition of supplementary angles |
| 3 | $m\angle CHF = 180^\circ - m\angle FHD$ | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm{Addition principle}}$ |
| 4 | $\angle EGB$ and $\angle BGH$ are supplementary | Linear pair postulate |
| 5 | $m\angle EGB + m\angle BGH = 180^\circ$ | Definition of supplementary angles |
| 6 | $\angle EGB \cong \angle CHF$ | Given |
| 7 | $m\angle EGB = 180^\circ - m\angle FHD$ | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm{Definition of congruent angles}}$ |
| 8 | $(180^\circ - m\angle FHD) + m\angle BGH = 180^\circ$ | Substitution |
| 9 | $m\angle BGH = m\angle FHD$ | Addition principle |
| 10 | $\angle BGH \cong \angle FHD$ | Definition of congruent angles |
| 11 | $\overleftrightarrow{AB}$ and $\overleftrightarrow{CD}$ are parallel | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm{Converse corresponding angles postulate}}$ |

Let's examine the rows of the table with missing parts in turn.

- Consider row 1. Notice from the diagram that $\angle CHF$ and $\angle FHD$ form a linear pair. The linear pair postulate states that such angles must be supplementary. Therefore, $\angle CHF$ and $\angle FHD$ are supplementary angles.
- Consider row 3. From row 2, we have $m\angle CHF + m\angle FHD = 180^\circ$. By the addition principle, we can subtract $m\angle FHD$ from both sides
$m∠CHF + m∠FHD|= 180^{∘}; m∠CHF + m∠FHD - m∠FHD|= 180^{∘} - m∠FHD; m∠CHF + m∠FHD - m∠FHD|= 180^{∘} - m∠FHD; m∠CHF|= 180^{∘} - m∠FHD$.
- Consider row 7. From row 3, we have that $m\angle CHF = 180^\circ - m\angle FHD$, and from row 6, we have that $\angle EGB \cong \angle CHF$. Therefore, by the definition of congruent angles, these angles have equal measure:
$m \angle EGB= 180^\circ - m\angle FHD$
- Consider row 11. From row 10, we have that $\angle BGH\cong \angle FHD$. By the converse corresponding angles postulate, if two lines and a transversal form corresponding angles that are congruent, then the lines are parallel. Notice that $\angle BGH$ and $\angle FHD$ are corresponding angles formed by the lines $\overleftrightarrow{AB}$ and $\overleftrightarrow{CD}$ with the transversal $\overleftrightarrow{EF}$. Therefore, $\overleftrightarrow{AB}$ and $\overleftrightarrow{CD}$ are parallel.

## Constructing Complete Proofs of the Alternate Angle Theorems

**Example:** In the diagram above, the parallel lines $l,m$, and $n$ are crossed by the transversal $t$.

![](<../Source/4.1.4. Further Proving Alternate Angle Theorems/Images/e94d8582a0132e085640f860a571a442.png>)

Consider the following statement:

> $m\angle AIJ = \gamma$

The table below provides proof of the statement. Fill in the missing reasons to complete the proof.

|  | Statement | Reason |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | $\angle ABC \cong \angle DBE$ |  |
| 2 | $\angle AIJ \cong \angle ABC$ |  |
| 3 | $\angle AIJ \cong \angle DBE$ |  |
| 4 | $m\angle AIJ = \gamma$ |  |

**Explanation**

The correct proof is shown below.

|  | Statement | Reason |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | $\angle ABC \cong \angle DBE$ | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm{Vertical angles theorem}}$ |
| 2 | $\angle AIJ \cong \angle ABC$ | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm{Corresponding angles postulate}}$ |
| 3 | $\angle AIJ \cong \angle DBE$ | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm{Transitive property of congruence}}$ |
| 4 | $m\angle AIJ = \gamma$ | $\bbox[3pt,Gainsboro]{\color{blue}\textrm{Definition of congruent angles}}$ |

Let's examine each row in turn.

1. Notice from the diagram that $\angle ABC$ and $\angle DBE$ are vertical angles. The vertical angles theorem states that such angles must be congruent:
$\angle ABC \cong \angle DBE$
2. Notice from the diagram that $\angle AIJ$ and $\angle ABC$ are corresponding angles. The corresponding angles postulate states that such angles must be congruent:
$\angle AIJ \cong \angle ABC$
3. From rows 1 and 2, we have
${\color{blue}\angle AIJ} \cong \angle ABC \quad\text{and}\quad \angle ABC \cong {\color{blue}\angle DBE}$.
Therefore, by the transitive property of congruence, we have
${\color{blue}\angle AIJ} \cong {\color{blue}\angle DBE}$.
4. From row 3, we have that $\angle AIJ \cong \angle DBE$. Therefore, by the definition of congruent angles, these angles have equal measure:
$m\angle AIJ = m\angle DBE$
Finally, since $m\angle DBE = \gamma$, we have
$m\angle AIJ = \gamma$.
