## Introduction

Suppose we are given the right cone shown below.

![](<../Source/4.7.4. Slant Heights of Right Cones/Images/cc4770c367e656b2179543f0eaf44bc3.png>)

How do we calculate the slant height $l$ of this cone?

First, notice that the base radius, height, and slant height form a right triangle, as shown below.

![](<../Source/4.7.4. Slant Heights of Right Cones/Images/43f50181b79982d91e3b1df0b8aadbcc.png>)

Therefore, we can find the slant height using the Pythagorean theorem, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
l &= \sqrt{(35)^{2} + (12)^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{1225 + 144} \\
&= \sqrt{1369} \\
&= 37cm
\end{aligned}
$$

## The General Formula

![](<../Source/4.7.4. Slant Heights of Right Cones/Images/a4ed79eb518176e3dd094d9fb5ea6f14.png>)

In general, if $r,h$, and $l$ denote the base radius, height, and slant height of a right cone, respectively, then by the Pythagorean theorem,

$$
l^2 = h^2+r^2
$$

## Finding the Slant Height of a Right Cone

**Example:** Calculate the slant height of a right cone whose base radius and height are $8 \: \textrm{in}$ and $15 \: \textrm{in}$, respectively.

**Explanation**

Let $r$, $h$, and $l$ denote the cone's base radius, height, and slant height, respectively.

![](<../Source/4.7.4. Slant Heights of Right Cones/Images/9f9b1a875e968464f3dceb50d02f280d.png>)

In our case,

$$
r=8\,\textrm{in}, h = 15\,\textrm{in}
$$

and $l$ is to be found.

We can find the slant height $l$ of the cone using the Pythagorean theorem on the right triangle shown above, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
l &= \sqrt{h^{2} + r^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{(15)^{2} + (8)^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{225 + 64} \\
&= \sqrt{289} \\
&= 17in
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding a Dimension of a Right Cone Using the Base Circumference and Slant Height Formula

**Example:** The circumference of the base of the right cone shown below is $28 \pi \text{cm}$. What is the height of the cone?

![](<../Source/4.7.4. Slant Heights of Right Cones/Images/fb0a79e779803ff54294352cdb9e93e2.png>)

**Explanation**

Let $r$, $h$, and $l$ denote the cone's base radius, height, and slant height, respectively.

![](<../Source/4.7.4. Slant Heights of Right Cones/Images/842e3574d71f4382ae6373aaf741fcab.png>)

In our case,

$$
l = 50\,\textrm{cm}
$$

and $r$ and $h$ are to be found. Additionally, we're given that

$$
C=28 \pi\, \text{cm}
$$

where $C$ is the circumference of the base.

The circumference of the base is given by the formula

$$
C = 2 \pi r
$$

Substitutuing

$$
C = 28 \pi \: \textrm{cm}
$$

into the above formula, we can solve for $r$, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
2πr &= 28π \\
r &= \frac{28π}{2π} \\
r &= 14cm
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we can find the height $h$ of the cone using the Pythagorean theorem in the right triangle shown in the diagram, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
h &= \sqrt{l^{2} - r^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{(50)^{2} - (14)^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{2500 - 196} \\
&= \sqrt{2304} \\
&= 48cm
\end{aligned}
$$

<a id="relating-the-dimensions-of-a-right-cone-to-its-volume"></a>
## Relating the Dimensions of a Right Cone to Its Volume

**Example:** The height of a particular right cone is $\dfrac 43$ times the radius of its base. If the volume of the cone is $12 \pi \: \textrm{in}^3$, what is the slant height of the cone?

**Explanation**

First, we are given that

$$
h=\dfrac 43r
$$

where $r$ is the cone's base radius, and $h$ is its height.

We can find the volume of the cone using the formula

$$
V = \dfrac{1}{3} \pi r^2 h
$$

Substituting

$$
V = 12 \pi \: \textrm{in}^3
$$

and

$$
h=\dfrac 43 r
$$

into the formula, we can solve for $r$, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
V &= \frac{1}{3}πr^{2}h \\
12π &= \frac{1}{3}πr^{2}(\frac{4}{3}r) \\
12π &= \frac{4}{9}πr^{3} \\
r^{3} &= \frac{12π \cdot 9}{4π} \\
r^{3} &= 27 \\
r &= 3in
\end{aligned}
$$

Hence,

$$
h = \dfrac 43r = \dfrac 43 \cdot 3 = 4 \: \textrm{in}
$$

![](<../Source/4.7.4. Slant Heights of Right Cones/Images/5752427e1cd41698393d5e8ae08f04a9.png>)

Now, we can find the slant height $l$ of the cone using the Pythagorean theorem in the right triangle shown above, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
l &= \sqrt{h^{2} + r^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{4^{2} + 3^{2}} \\
&= \sqrt{16 + 9} \\
&= \sqrt{25} \\
&= 5in
\end{aligned}
$$
