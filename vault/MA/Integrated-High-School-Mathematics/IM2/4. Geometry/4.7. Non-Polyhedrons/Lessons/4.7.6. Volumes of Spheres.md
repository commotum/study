## Introduction

Given a sphere with radius

$$
r= 3\:\textrm{cm}
$$

how do we calculate its volume?

![](<../Source/4.7.6. Volumes of Spheres/Images/f54b4469f4f650b1fdc27e6a06f7f02f.png>)

In general, we can find the volume of a sphere using the formula

$$
V= \dfrac 4 3 \pi r^3
$$

where $r$ is the radius of the sphere.

Substituting

$$
r=3\:\textrm{cm}
$$

into the formula, we obtain

$$
V = \dfrac 4 3 \pi \cdot (3)^3 =36 \pi \:\textrm{cm}^3
$$

We often express the volume of a sphere as a multiple of $\pi$. However, we can also use a calculator to get a numerical approximation. In this case, we get

$$
V \approx 113.10 \:\textrm{cm}^3
$$

rounded to two decimal places.

<a id="finding-the-volume-of-a-sphere-given-its-radius-or-diameter"></a>
## Finding the Volume of a Sphere Given Its Radius or Diameter

**Example:** What is the volume of a sphere whose diameter is $18 \:\textrm{cm}$?

**Explanation**

We can find the volume of a sphere using the formula

$$
V = \dfrac 4 3 \pi r^3
$$

where $r$ is the radius of the sphere.

Substituting

$$
r=\dfrac{18}{2}=9\:\textrm{cm}
$$

into the formula, we obtain

$$
V = \dfrac 4 3 \pi \left(9\right)^3 = 972\pi \:\textrm{cm}^3
$$

## Finding the Radius or Diameter of a Sphere Given Its Volume

**Example:** What is the radius of a sphere whose volume is $36\pi\:\textrm{in}^3$.

![](<../Source/4.7.6. Volumes of Spheres/Images/1b2dc38f65a084e486d1800dc77e806e.png>)

**Explanation**

We can find the volume of a sphere using the formula

$$
V = \dfrac 4 3 \pi r^3
$$

where $r$ is the radius of the sphere.

Substituting

$$
V=36\pi\:\textrm{in}^3
$$

into the formula, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
V &= \frac{4}{3}πr^{3} \\
36π &= \frac{4}{3}πr^{3} \\
36π \cdot \frac{3}{4π} &= r^{3} \\
r^{3} &= 27
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore,

$$
r = \sqrt[3]{27} = 3\:\textrm{in}
$$
