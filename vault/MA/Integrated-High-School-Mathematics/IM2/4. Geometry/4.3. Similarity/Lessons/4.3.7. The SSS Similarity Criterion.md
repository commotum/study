## Introduction

The **side-side-side (SSS) similarity criterion** can be used as a shortcut to prove that two triangles are similar. It states the following:

> Two triangles are similar if the ratios of the lengths of their corresponding sides are equal.

To demonstrate, consider the triangles below.

![](<../Source/4.3.7. The SSS Similarity Criterion/Images/9aef0cc1e1bcfb45976b1fed48b415a0.png>)

Let's check the ratios of the corresponding sides:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{AB}{EF} &= \frac{3}{6} = \frac{1}{2} \\
\frac{BC}{FG} &= \frac{4}{8} = \frac{1}{2} \\
\frac{CA}{GE} &= \frac{2}{4} = \frac{1}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

The ratios are all equal, so according to the SSS criterion, the two triangles are similar:

$$
\triangle ABC \sim \triangle EFG
$$

## Identifying Similar Triangles Using the SSS Criterion

**Example:** According to the SSS criterion only, which of the following triangles is similar to $\mathcal{T}$?

![](<../Source/4.3.7. The SSS Similarity Criterion/Images/ab64c8a3498de4456e7725ceb8348939.png>)

**Explanation**

The side-side-side (SSS) similarity criterion states the following:

> Two triangles are similar if the ratios of the lengths of their corresponding sides are equal.

With that in mind, let's examine each of the given triangles.

- $\mathcal{P}$ is similar to $\mathcal{T}$ by SSS since the ratios of the lengths of the corresponding sides are equal:
$\dfrac{16}{8}=\dfrac{12}{6} =\dfrac{8}{4}= 2$

- $\mathcal{Q}$ is *not* similar to $\mathcal{T}$ by SSS since the ratios of the lengths of the corresponding sides are not equal:
$\dfrac{12}{8}\neq\dfrac{10}{6}\neq\dfrac{8}{4}$.
- $\mathcal{R}$ is *not* simlar to $\mathcal{T}$ by SSS since the ratios of the lengths of the corresponding sides are not equal:
$\dfrac{12}{8}\neq\dfrac{10}{6}\neq\dfrac{9}{4}$.

Therefore, the correct answer is "$\mathcal{P}$ only."

## Solving for an Unknown Given Two Similar Triangles

**Example:** Find the value of $x$ that makes $\triangle ABC$ similar to $\triangle FDE$.

![](<../Source/4.3.7. The SSS Similarity Criterion/Images/1e51894f3c3bf5f9061d51d41a93fca1.png>)

**Explanation**

By the SSS similarity criterion, for $\triangle ABC$ to be similar to $\triangle FDE$, we require that the ratios of the corresponding sides are equal. In other words,

$$
\dfrac{AB}{FD}=\dfrac{BC}{DE}=\dfrac{AC}{EF}
$$

Therefore, to find $x$, we solve the following:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{AB}{FD} &= \frac{BC}{DE} \\
(3(2x + 1))/(9) &= \frac{12}{4} \\
(3(2x + 1))/(9) &= 3 \\
3(2x + 1) &= 27 \\
2x + 1 &= 9 \\
2x &= 8 \\
x &= 4
\end{aligned}
$$

## Calculating the Length of an Element of a Triangle Given a Similar Triangle

**Example:** For the triangles $\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle A'B'C'$ shown below, the dashed segments are the medians corresponding to the sides $\overline{BC}$ and $\overline{B'C'}$, respectively. Find the value of $\dfrac{x}{y}$.
*(Note: Figures not drawn to scale)*

![](<../Source/4.3.7. The SSS Similarity Criterion/Images/f7182bd0cd65f92ec7da26c6541aa9cd.png>)

**Explanation**

First, notice the following:

$$
\begin{aligned} \dfrac{{A'C'}}{{AC}} &= \dfrac{28}{7}=4 \\[5pt] \dfrac{{B'C'}}{{BC}} &= \dfrac{20}{5}=4 \\[5pt] \dfrac{{A'B'}}{{AB}} &= \dfrac{16}{4}=4 \end{aligned}
$$

Since the corresponding sides are proportional, the triangles are similar by the SSS similarity criterion with a scale factor of $4$.

The ratio of corresponding medians of similar triangles is equal to the scale factor. Therefore, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{x}{y} &= 4
\end{aligned}
$$

## Determining the Measure of an Angle Using the SSS Criterion

**Example:** Given the diagram below, find the measure of $\angle BCD$.

![](<../Source/4.3.7. The SSS Similarity Criterion/Images/cbfafc3c0c8ee84d61907d2feadae973.png>)

**Explanation**

Consider triangles $\triangle BAC$ and $\triangle ACD$. Let's calculate the ratios of corresponding sides:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{AB}{AC} &= \frac{8}{6} = \frac{4}{3} \\
\frac{BC}{AD} &= \frac{4}{3} \\
\frac{AC}{CD} &= \frac{6}{4.5} = \frac{4}{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

Since the ratios are equal, we conclude that $\triangle BAC\sim\triangle ACD$ by the SSS criterion. Therefore,
$∠ACD≅∠CAB$.

![](<../Source/4.3.7. The SSS Similarity Criterion/Images/6588d461eb9764d77efd9b9f4982f6f5.png>)

Now, since the angles $\angle ACD$ and $\angle CAB$ are congruent, they are alternate interior angles, and we have

$$
\overline{AB} \parallel \overline{CD}
$$

Therefore, the consecutive interior angles $\angle ABC$ and $\angle BCD$ are supplementary, and we obtain the following:

$$
\begin{aligned}
m∠BCD + m∠ABC &= 180^{∘} \\
m∠BCD + 47^{∘} &= 180^{∘} \\
m∠BCD &= 133^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$
