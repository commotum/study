## Introduction

Recall that the inscribed angle theorem tells us that the measure of an inscribed angle is half the measure of the central angle that intercepts the same arc.

![](<../Source/4.4.2. Problem-Solving With Inscribed Angles/Images/00a90207630535a5df1a4b6aa8246121.png>)

We can use this theorem to solve different types of problems involving circles.

For instance, how can we find $m\overset{\frown}{UV}$ in the diagram below?

![](<../Source/4.4.2. Problem-Solving With Inscribed Angles/Images/d37a294d713b44c1e80198ae0d8059d1.png>)

By considering the sum of the interior angles in $\triangle UVW$, we get the following:

$$
\begin{aligned}
m∠U + m∠V + m∠W &= 180^{∘} \\
55^{∘} + 40^{∘} + m∠W &= 180^{∘} \\
95^{∘} + m∠W &= 180^{∘} \\
m∠W &= 85^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, according to the inscribed angle theorem, the measure of an inscribed angle is half the measure of its intercepted arc.

![](<../Source/4.4.2. Problem-Solving With Inscribed Angles/Images/7c251fcd580e72631866f3c0432de381.png>)

Therefore, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
mUV^(⌢) &= 2m∠W \\
&= 2 \cdot 85^{∘} \\
&= 170^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Applying the Inscribed Angle Theorem to Inscribed Triangles

**Example:** Given that $m\overset{\frown}{AB} = 64^\circ$, calculate $m \angle A$.

![](<../Source/4.4.2. Problem-Solving With Inscribed Angles/Images/64e019d51dbcae7911d2b31c571d90ee.png>)

**Explanation**

![](<../Source/4.4.2. Problem-Solving With Inscribed Angles/Images/4d27fe76e3ceb4ee7cce060bb72f2309.png>)

According to the inscribed angle theorem, the measure of an inscribed angle is half the measure of its intercepted arc.

$$
\begin{aligned}
m∠P &= \frac{1}{2}mAB^(⌢) \\
&= \frac{1}{2} \cdot 64^{∘} \\
&= 32^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore, by considering the sum of the interior angles in $\triangle ABP$, we get the following:

$$
\begin{aligned}
m∠A + m∠B + m∠P &= 180^{∘} \\
m∠A + 73^{∘} + 32^{∘} &= 180^{∘} \\
m∠A + 105^{∘} &= 180^{∘} \\
m∠A &= 75^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding the Measure of an Arc Using the Inscribed Angle Theorem

**Example:** Given the picture below, find $m\overset{\frown}{BC}$.

![](<../Source/4.4.2. Problem-Solving With Inscribed Angles/Images/56b687c0c1f77922e9b21b67b8d34093.png>)

**Explanation**

First, we will find $m\overset{\frown}{CDA}$.

![](<../Source/4.4.2. Problem-Solving With Inscribed Angles/Images/128e4f5b8bdc94f3e7e63636b9bda285.png>)

The measure of an inscribed angle is half the measure of its intercepted arc. So,

$$
\begin{aligned}
mCDA^(⌢) &= 2m∠ABC \\
&= 2 \cdot 70^{∘} \\
&= 140^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

Now, we can find the measure of $\overset{\frown}{ABC}$ as

$$
\begin{aligned}
mABC^(⌢) &= 360^{∘} - mCDA^(⌢) \\
&= 360^{∘} - 140^{∘} \\
&= 220^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, we obtain

$$
\begin{aligned}
mBC^(⌢) &= mABC^(⌢) - mAB^(⌢) \\
&= 220^{∘} - 60^{∘} \\
&= 160^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

## Finding the Length of an Arc or Radius Using the Inscribed Angle Theorem

**Example:** In the diagram below, the length of the arc $\overset{\frown}{AB}$ equals $10\pi$ and $m\angle{BCA} = 50^\circ$. What is the radius of the circle?

![](<../Source/4.4.2. Problem-Solving With Inscribed Angles/Images/fbb58ffe13a047697d6a1f5947ea5b3b.png>)

**Explanation**

According to the inscribed angle theorem, the measure of an inscribed angle is half the measure of its intercepted arc. Therefore,

$$
\begin{aligned}
mAB^(⌢) &= 2m∠BCA \\
&= 2 \cdot 50^{∘} \\
&= 100^{∘}
\end{aligned}
$$

The corresponding central angle of the arc $\overset{\frown}{AB}$ is $\angle{AOB}$.

![](<../Source/4.4.2. Problem-Solving With Inscribed Angles/Images/db9cff82d924c861678196d11040ec3b.png>)

Notice that

$$
\dfrac{m\angle{AOB}}{360^\circ} = \dfrac{100^\circ}{360^\circ} = \dfrac{5}{18}
$$

Now, if the circumference of the circle is $C=2 \pi r$, then we can write an equation for the length of the arc $\overset{\frown}{AB}$ as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{5}{18}C &= 10π \\
\frac{5}{18} \cdot 2πr &= 10π \\
\frac{5}{9}πr &= 10π
\end{aligned}
$$

Solving for $r$, we get

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{5}{9}r &= 10 \\
r &= \frac{90}{5} \\
r &= 18
\end{aligned}
$$
