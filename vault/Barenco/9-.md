## Arbitrary Local Phases in an All-Zero Register

In standard quantum mechanics, all kets $e^{i\chi}|0\rangle$ represent the same ray, the same density operator, and therefore the same operational quantum state. However, the same operational state may correspond to infinitely many underlying physical realities.

Consider an $n$-qubit register. Assign an arbitrary phase $\theta_j$ to each initialized qubit:

$$
|\Psi(\boldsymbol{\theta})\rangle
=
\bigotimes_{j=1}^{n}
\left(e^{i\theta_j}|0\rangle\right),
\qquad
\theta_j\in\mathbb{R}.
$$

The phases can have any values. They do not have to be multiples of $\pi$, $\pi/2$, or any other fixed angle.

Scalar factors pull outside the tensor product:

$$
|\Psi(\boldsymbol{\theta})\rangle
=
\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n}e^{i\theta_j}\right)
\left(\bigotimes_{j=1}^{n}|0\rangle\right).
$$

Because

$$
\prod_{j=1}^{n}e^{i\theta_j}
=
e^{i\sum_{j=1}^{n}\theta_j},
$$

the register state is

$$
|\Psi(\boldsymbol{\theta})\rangle
=
e^{i\sum_{j=1}^{n}\theta_j}|00\ldots0\rangle.
$$

Thus, all independently assigned phases combine into one global phase. They do not create measurable phase differences between the qubits.

Therefore,

$$
\boxed{
\bigotimes_{j=1}^{n}
\left(e^{i\theta_j}|0\rangle\right)
\sim
|00\ldots0\rangle
\quad
\text{for all }
\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_n\in\mathbb{R}.
}
$$

Here, $\sim$ means that the kets represent the same operational quantum state.

No standard quantum measurement can distinguish one phase assignment from another. A computational-basis measurement gives

$$
P(00\ldots0)=1
$$

for every possible tuple

$$
(\theta_1,\theta_2,\ldots,\theta_n).
$$

Since the phases can vary continuously, infinitely many phase configurations produce the same operational state $|00\ldots0\rangle$.

The core argument is therefore the tensor-product identity

$$
\left(e^{i\theta_1}|0\rangle\right)
\otimes
\left(e^{i\theta_2}|0\rangle\right)
=
e^{i(\theta_1+\theta_2)}|00\rangle,
$$

and its extension to arbitrary $n$.


## Distinct Local Phases in an All-Zero Qubit Register

In standard quantum mechanics, all kets $e^{i\chi}|0\rangle$ represent the same ray, the same density operator, and therefore the same operational quantum state. However, the same operational state may correspond to infinitely many underlying physical realities.

For one qubit, let

$$
|\psi_\chi\rangle=e^{i\chi}|0\rangle.
$$

The density operator is

$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho_\chi
&=|\psi_\chi\rangle\langle\psi_\chi| \\
&=\left(e^{i\chi}|0\rangle\right)
\left(e^{-i\chi}\langle0|\right) \\
&=|0\rangle\langle0|.
\end{aligned}
$$

The phase cancels because

$$
e^{i\chi}e^{-i\chi}=1.
$$

Thus, the value of $\chi$ does not change the quantum state or any measurement result.

For two qubits, assign a different phase to each qubit:

$$
\left(e^{i\theta_1}|0\rangle\right)
\otimes
\left(e^{i\theta_2}|0\rangle\right).
$$

Scalars pull outside the tensor product:

$$
\left(e^{i\theta_1}|0\rangle\right)
\otimes
\left(e^{i\theta_2}|0\rangle\right)
=
e^{i\theta_1}e^{i\theta_2}
\left(|0\rangle\otimes|0\rangle\right).
$$

Because

$$
e^{i\theta_1}e^{i\theta_2}
=
e^{i(\theta_1+\theta_2)},
$$

the result is

$$
e^{i(\theta_1+\theta_2)}|00\rangle.
$$

The two different phases become one global phase.

The same result applies to $n$ qubits. Let

$$
|\Psi(\boldsymbol{\theta})\rangle
=
\bigotimes_{j=1}^{n}
\left(e^{i\theta_j}|0\rangle\right).
$$

Then

$$
\begin{aligned}
|\Psi(\boldsymbol{\theta})\rangle
&=
\left(\prod_{j=1}^{n}e^{i\theta_j}\right)
\left(\bigotimes_{j=1}^{n}|0\rangle\right) \\[4pt]
&=
e^{i\sum_{j=1}^{n}\theta_j}
|00\ldots0\rangle.
\end{aligned}
$$

Define the total phase as

$$
\Theta=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\theta_j.
$$

Then

$$
|\Psi(\boldsymbol{\theta})\rangle
=
e^{i\Theta}|00\ldots0\rangle.
$$

The density operator is

$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho(\boldsymbol{\theta})
&=
|\Psi(\boldsymbol{\theta})\rangle
\langle\Psi(\boldsymbol{\theta})| \\[4pt]
&=
e^{i\Theta}|00\ldots0\rangle
e^{-i\Theta}\langle00\ldots0| \\[4pt]
&=
|00\ldots0\rangle\langle00\ldots0|.
\end{aligned}
$$

The density operator does not contain any $\theta_j$.

Let $E_k$ represent measurement result $k$. The probability of that result is

$$
P(k\mid\boldsymbol{\theta})
=
\operatorname{Tr}\!\left(E_k\rho(\boldsymbol{\theta})\right).
$$

Therefore,

$$
P(k\mid\boldsymbol{\theta})
=
\operatorname{Tr}\!\left(
E_k|00\ldots0\rangle\langle00\ldots0|
\right).
$$

This probability does not depend on any individual phase. No standard quantum measurement can identify the phase values.

A computational-basis measurement gives

$$
P(00\ldots0\mid\boldsymbol{\theta})=1.
$$

This result also holds after any standard quantum circuit $U$:

$$
U|\Psi(\boldsymbol{\theta})\rangle
=
e^{i\Theta}U|00\ldots0\rangle.
$$

The phase remains global. It still has no effect on measurement probabilities.

In the code, each qubit has the phase

$$
\theta_j
=
\texttt{local\_angle}_j
+
\texttt{global\_angle}.
$$

Thus, the complete register is

$$
e^{i\left(
\sum_{j=1}^{n}\texttt{local\_angle}_j
+
n\,\texttt{global\_angle}
\right)}
|00\ldots0\rangle.
$$

Therefore, the code creates a valid representative of

$$
|00\ldots0\rangle
$$

for every number of qubits and every choice of phase angles. This statement is valid when `phase_state` acts only as a scalar phase.

In the continuous model, the phase angles can have infinitely many values. All phase assignments give the same density operator. They are physically indistinguishable under all standard quantum measurements.

$$
\boxed{
\text{Infinitely many phase configurations produce one operational quantum state.}
}
$$

If the model treats the stored phase configurations as different underlying physical states, then infinitely many underlying physical realizations produce the same observable state $|00\ldots0\rangle$.
