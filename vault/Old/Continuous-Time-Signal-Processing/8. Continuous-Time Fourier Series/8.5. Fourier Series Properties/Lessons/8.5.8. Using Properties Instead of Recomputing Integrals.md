## Introduction to Using Properties Instead of Recomputing Integrals

You already know the Fourier-series property formulas. In this lesson, the job is not to re-integrate; it is to inspect a transformed periodic signal with known $c_k$ and reuse the matching property or property chain to update the coefficients directly.

$$
x(t-t_0) \longrightarrow c_k e^{-jk\omega_0 t_0}
$$

Scan the signal statement for the cue: time shift, time scaling, time reversal, vertical offset, or real-signal symmetry. If the signal has more than one change, chain the matching properties in the same order as the transformations instead of starting over with the coefficient integral.

$$
x(t)+A \longrightarrow c_0 + A
$$

For a single shift, every harmonic coefficient changes by the corresponding phase rule; for a vertical shift, only the DC term changes. When the signal is real, use symmetry to fill the negative indices instead of recomputing them.

$$
d_{-k}=d_k^*
$$

Use the quick check: ask what changes in $c_k$, what changes only in $c_0$, and when negative coefficients come from symmetry. If you can answer those three questions, you can update the coefficients directly and avoid recomputing the coefficient integral.

## Selecting the Property from a Transformed Signal

**Example:** A periodic signal $x(t)$ has known coefficients $c_k$. The new signal is $y(t)=x(-t)$. Decide which Fourier-series property applies and state how the coefficient indices change.

**Explanation**

The cue here is the minus sign inside the argument: $y(t)=x(-t)$. That tells you the signal has been time-reversed, so the property to select is the time-reversal property rather than a shift or a scaling.

$$
y(t)=x(-t) \longrightarrow d_k = c_{-k}
$$

Read that rule as an index swap: the new coefficient at harmonic $k$ is the old coefficient at harmonic $-k$. Because this example contains only time reversal, you stop here and do not recompute the coefficient integral.

## Updating Known Coefficients with One Fourier Series Property

**Example:** A periodic signal $x(t)$ has coefficients $c_k$. Find the coefficients of $y(t)=x(t-t_0)$.

**Explanation**

The cue is the delay inside $y(t)=x(t-t_0)$. That tells you to use the time-shift property, so each Fourier-series coefficient keeps its magnitude and picks up a harmonic-dependent phase factor.

$$
d_k = c_k e^{-jk\omega_0 t_0}
$$

So the update is coefficient-by-coefficient: multiply every $c_k$ by $e^{-jk\omega_0 t_0}$ and leave the magnitudes unchanged. The $k=0$ term stays the same, and no coefficient integral is recomputed.

<a id="combining-a-time-shift-with-a-vertical-shift"></a>
## Combining a Time Shift with a Vertical Shift

**Example:** A periodic signal $x(t)$ has coefficients $c_k$. Find the coefficients of $y(t)=x(t-t_0)+A$.

**Explanation**

The delay inside $x(t-t_0)$ is the first cue. Apply the time-shift property to the known coefficients before you handle the constant offset.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\tilde c_k &= c_k e^{-jk\omega_0 t_0}
\end{aligned}
$$

Then add the vertical shift. The constant $A$ changes only the DC term, so $d_0=\tilde c_0+A=c_0+A$ while every $k\neq 0$ coefficient stays equal to the time-shifted value.

$$
\begin{aligned}
 d_0 &= \tilde c_0 + A = c_0 + A \\
 d_k &= \tilde c_k = c_k e^{-jk\omega_0 t_0}, \quad k\neq 0
\end{aligned}
$$

The update is ordered and local: first update every coefficient by the time-shift rule, then change only the DC term by the vertical offset. That keeps the combined transformation from turning into a recomputation.

## Combining Time Reversal with Time Scaling

**Example:** A periodic signal $x(t)$ has coefficients $c_k$ and period $T$. Determine the coefficients and new fundamental period of $y(t)=x(-2t)$.

**Explanation**

The cue is the negative scale in $x(-2t)$. Treat it as a time reversal together with a factor-2 compression of the time axis.

$$
\begin{aligned}
T_y &= \frac{T}{2}, \qquad \omega_{0,y} = 2\omega_0
\end{aligned}
$$

Once the new period is fixed, use the reversal rule on the coefficient sequence. The harmonic index order flips, so the coefficient at index $k$ comes from the old coefficient at $-k$.

$$
d_k = c_{-k}, \qquad k\in\mathbb{Z}
$$

So the update is complete: halve the period, double the fundamental angular frequency, and read the coefficient list backward. No coefficient integral is recomputed.

## Using Conjugate Symmetry to Complete Coefficient Updates

**Example:** A real periodic signal has updated positive-harmonic coefficients after a transformation. Use conjugate symmetry to determine the missing negative-index coefficients and complete the spectrum.

**Explanation**

The cue is that the signal is real after the transformation. That means the completed Fourier-series coefficients must satisfy conjugate symmetry.

$$
\begin{aligned}
d_{-k} &= d_k^* \\
d_0 &= d_0^*
\end{aligned}
$$

So after you update the positive harmonics, fill each missing negative coefficient by conjugating the matching positive one. Leave the DC term real, and do not recompute any integrals.

$$
d_0 \in \mathbb{R}
$$

That completes the spectrum directly from the known positive side: mirror the indices, apply conjugation, and keep the zero-frequency term real.
