## Introduction to Cosine Coefficients

A trigonometric Fourier series separates a periodic signal into a DC term, cosine harmonics, and sine harmonics. The cosine coefficient $a_k$ measures how much of the $k$th cosine harmonic, $\cos(k\omega_0 t)$, is present in $x(t)$ over one period.

$$
a_k = \frac{2}{T_0}\int_{t_0}^{t_0+T_0} x(t)\cos(k\omega_0 t)\,dt
$$

Read this as a projection: multiply $x(t)$ by the target cosine, integrate over one full period, and scale by $\frac{2}{T_0}$. Orthogonality makes every nonmatching cosine and every sine term disappear, so the integral isolates only the contribution at index $k$.

$$
\int_{t_0}^{t_0+T_0}\cos(m\omega_0 t)\cos(n\omega_0 t)\,dt = 0 \quad (m \ne n)
$$

In the matching case, the cosine survives. If $x(t)=A\cos(k\omega_0 t)$, then the coefficient formula should return $A$ because the product becomes $\cos^2(k\omega_0 t)$ over one period.

$$
\int_{t_0}^{t_0+T_0}\cos^2(k\omega_0 t)\,dt = \frac{T_0}{2}
$$

That normalization check confirms the meaning of $a_k$: it is the cosine-harmonic weight of $x(t)$ at harmonic index $k$, recovered by projecting onto $\cos(k\omega_0 t)$ over one period.

## Setting Up the Integral for a Cosine Coefficient

**Example:** Given $x(t)=4+3\cos(\omega_0 t)-2\sin(2\omega_0 t)$ with period $T_0$, write the integral that computes $a_1$ and substitute the signal into it.

**Explanation**

The cue is the requested coefficient index $1$, so the matching cosine factor is $\cos(\omega_0 t)$. To set up $a_1$, use one full period of the signal and keep the normalization factor $\frac{2}{T_0}$ in front.

$$
a_1 = \frac{2}{T_0}\int_{t_0}^{t_0+T_0} x(t)\cos(\omega_0 t)\,dt
$$

Now substitute the given periodic signal directly into the formula. This keeps the setup aligned with the requested harmonic while leaving the integral unevaluated.

$$
a_1 = \frac{2}{T_0}\int_{t_0}^{t_0+T_0} \bigl(4+3\cos(\omega_0 t)-2\sin(2\omega_0 t)\bigr)\cos(\omega_0 t)\,dt
$$

This is the full setup for the coefficient calculation: the limits cover one period, the integrand matches the $k=1$ cosine harmonic, and the signal has been inserted without evaluating the integral.

## Computing a Matching Cosine Harmonic Coefficient

**Example:** Given $x(t)=2+5\cos(3\omega_0 t)-\sin(\omega_0 t)$, compute $a_3$ by integrating over one period and using orthogonality to isolate the matching cosine harmonic.

**Explanation**

The cue is the requested coefficient index $3$, so the matching test function is $\cos(3\omega_0 t)$. To find $a_3$, project the signal onto that harmonic over one full period.

$$
a_3 = \frac{2}{T_0}\int_{t_0}^{t_0+T_0} x(t)\cos(3\omega_0 t)\,dt
$$

Substitute the signal and keep the three parts separate: the DC term, the matching cosine term, and the sine term. That makes it clear which contributions orthogonality will eliminate.

$$
a_3 = \frac{2}{T_0}\int_{t_0}^{t_0+T_0} \bigl(2+5\cos(3\omega_0 t)-\sin(\omega_0 t)\bigr)\cos(3\omega_0 t)\,dt
$$

Over one period, the DC term and the sine term integrate to zero against $\cos(3\omega_0 t)$, so only the matching cosine term remains.

$$
a_3 = \frac{2}{T_0}\int_{t_0}^{t_0+T_0} 5\cos^2(3\omega_0 t)\,dt = \frac{2}{T_0}\cdot 5 \cdot \frac{T_0}{2} = 5
$$

The coefficient is $5$, which matches the amplitude of the cosine term at the target harmonic. That is exactly what the projection is designed to recover.

## Computing a Zero Coefficient for a Nonmatching Harmonic

**Example:** Given $x(t)=1+4\cos(2\omega_0 t)+3\sin(\omega_0 t)$, compute $a_3$ and show why every term vanishes when the target cosine harmonic does not appear.

**Explanation**

The cue is the requested coefficient index $3$, so the projection uses $\cos(3\omega_0 t)$. As before, write the cosine coefficient over one full period with the standard normalization.

$$
a_3 = \frac{2}{T_0}\int_{t_0}^{t_0+T_0} x(t)\cos(3\omega_0 t)\,dt
$$

Substitute the signal and separate the DC, cosine, and sine parts so each contribution can be checked against the target harmonic.

$$
a_3 = \frac{2}{T_0}\int_{t_0}^{t_0+T_0} \bigl(1+4\cos(2\omega_0 t)+3\sin(\omega_0 t)\bigr)\cos(3\omega_0 t)\,dt
$$

No term in $x(t)$ matches $\cos(3\omega_0 t)$, so orthogonality kills every product over one period. The DC term is orthogonal to the target cosine, the $\cos(2\omega_0 t)$ term is a different cosine harmonic, and the sine term is orthogonal to the cosine test function as well.

$$
a_3 = \frac{2}{T_0}\left(0+0+0\right)=0
$$

The coefficient is zero because the signal has no matching cosine harmonic at index $3$. In projection form, the target harmonic is absent, so nothing survives the integral.

## Computing Cosine Coefficients of a Harmonic Sum

**Example:** Given $x(t)=2+3\cos(\omega_0 t)-4\cos(2\omega_0 t)+5\cos(4\omega_0 t)$, compute the ordered cosine coefficients $(a_1,a_2,a_3,a_4)$ by applying linearity and orthogonality term by term.

**Explanation**

The cue is the ordered tuple $(a_1,a_2,a_3,a_4)$, so write one projection integral for each requested harmonic. Each coefficient is found by testing $x(t)$ against $\cos(k\omega_0 t)$ over one period.

$$
\begin{aligned}
a_1 &= \frac{2}{T_0}\int_{t_0}^{t_0+T_0} x(t)\cos(\omega_0 t)\,dt \\
a_2 &= \frac{2}{T_0}\int_{t_0}^{t_0+T_0} x(t)\cos(2\omega_0 t)\,dt \\
a_3 &= \frac{2}{T_0}\int_{t_0}^{t_0+T_0} x(t)\cos(3\omega_0 t)\,dt \\
a_4 &= \frac{2}{T_0}\int_{t_0}^{t_0+T_0} x(t)\cos(4\omega_0 t)\,dt
\end{aligned}
$$

Now substitute $x(t)=2+3\cos(\omega_0 t)-4\cos(2\omega_0 t)+5\cos(4\omega_0 t)$ into each integral. Linearity lets the sum split term by term, so each coefficient can be checked against its own cosine test function.

$$
\begin{aligned}
a_1 &= \frac{2}{T_0}\int_{t_0}^{t_0+T_0} \bigl(2+3\cos(\omega_0 t)-4\cos(2\omega_0 t)+5\cos(4\omega_0 t)\bigr)\cos(\omega_0 t)\,dt \\
a_2 &= \frac{2}{T_0}\int_{t_0}^{t_0+T_0} \bigl(2+3\cos(\omega_0 t)-4\cos(2\omega_0 t)+5\cos(4\omega_0 t)\bigr)\cos(2\omega_0 t)\,dt \\
a_3 &= \frac{2}{T_0}\int_{t_0}^{t_0+T_0} \bigl(2+3\cos(\omega_0 t)-4\cos(2\omega_0 t)+5\cos(4\omega_0 t)\bigr)\cos(3\omega_0 t)\,dt \\
a_4 &= \frac{2}{T_0}\int_{t_0}^{t_0+T_0} \bigl(2+3\cos(\omega_0 t)-4\cos(2\omega_0 t)+5\cos(4\omega_0 t)\bigr)\cos(4\omega_0 t)\,dt
\end{aligned}
$$

Orthogonality now does the work. Over one period, the DC term is orthogonal to every cosine, mismatched cosine harmonics vanish, and the matching cosine survives with its amplitude.

$$
a_1=3,\qquad a_2=-4,\qquad a_3=0,\qquad a_4=5
$$

The missing $\cos(3\omega_0 t)$ term makes $a_3=0$, while the other requested coefficients are read directly from the matching amplitudes. The ordered tuple is the projection result for the whole harmonic sum.

$$
(a_1,a_2,a_3,a_4)=(3,-4,0,5)
$$
