## Introduction to Reconstructing a Periodic Signal from Harmonics

Reconstructing a periodic signal from harmonics means reading the coefficient list as a template for the signal, not trying to recover it from a sketch. The cue is always the same: one DC term, then cosine and sine harmonics at integer multiples of the fundamental angular frequency.

$$
x(t)=\frac{a_0}{2}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left[a_k\cos(k\omega_0 t)+b_k\sin(k\omega_0 t)\right]
$$

To build the signal, write the DC term once as $a_0/2$, then add each harmonic pair with its own index $k$: $a_k$ multiplies $\cos(k\omega_0 t)$ and $b_k$ multiplies $\sin(k\omega_0 t)$. Keep the coefficient-to-term mapping fixed, and keep the sign attached to the coefficient that produced it.

$$
x(t)=\frac{a_0}{2}+a_1\cos(\omega_0 t)+b_1\sin(\omega_0 t)
$$

In the simplest base case, the whole pattern is already visible: the DC term appears once, and the first harmonic contributes one cosine term and one sine term at $\omega_0$. Later examples will use the same assembly rule with more values of $k$, missing terms, and coefficient tables.

## Reconstructing a Signal from the DC Term and First Harmonic

**Example:** Reconstruct $x(t)$ when $a_0=8$, $a_1=-3$, $b_1=5$, and $\omega_0=\pi$.

**Explanation**

Use the reconstruction template from the previous section. The cue is the coefficient list, and the rule is to write the DC term once as $a_0/2$, then attach the first harmonic pair at $\omega_0$.

$$
x(t)=\frac{a_0}{2}+a_1\cos(\omega_0 t)+b_1\sin(\omega_0 t)=\frac{8}{2}+(-3)\cos(\pi t)+5\sin(\pi t)
$$

Halve the DC coefficient to get $4$, keep the negative sign on $a_1=-3$, and keep $b_1=5$ on the sine term. Because $\omega_0=\pi$, the first harmonic appears as $\cos(\pi t)$ and $\sin(\pi t)$.

$$
x(t)=4-3\cos(\pi t)+5\sin(\pi t)
$$

So the reconstructed signal is $x(t)=4-3\cos(\pi t)+5\sin(\pi t)$. This is the complete first-harmonic reconstruction: one DC term and one cosine-sine pair placed at the fundamental frequency.

## Reconstructing a Signal from Multiple Harmonics

**Example:** Reconstruct $x(t)$ when $a_0=6$, $a_1=1$, $b_1=-4$, $a_2=-2$, $b_2=3$, and $\omega_0=2$.

**Explanation**

Use the reconstruction template from the previous section. The cue is the coefficient list, and the rule is to write the DC term once as $a_0/2$, then add each harmonic pair with its own index $k$ and frequency $k\omega_0$.

$$
x(t)=\frac{a_0}{2}+a_1\cos(\omega_0 t)+b_1\sin(\omega_0 t)+a_2\cos(2\omega_0 t)+b_2\sin(2\omega_0 t)=\frac{6}{2}+1\cos(2t)+(-4)\sin(2t)+(-2)\cos(4t)+3\sin(4t)
$$

Write the DC term first and halve $a_0$ to get $3$. Then place the $k=1$ pair at $2t$ and the $k=2$ pair at $4t$, keeping each coefficient attached to its own harmonic index.

$$
x(t)=3+\cos(2t)-4\sin(2t)-2\cos(4t)+3\sin(4t)
$$

So the reconstructed signal is $x(t)=3+\cos(2t)-4\sin(2t)-2\cos(4t)+3\sin(4t)$. The only new step beyond the first example is adding a second harmonic pair without letting any coefficient cross to a different $k$.

## Reconstructing a Signal with Missing Harmonic Terms

**Example:** Reconstruct $x(t)$ when $a_0=10$, $a_1=-1$, $b_1=0$, $a_3=4$, $b_3=-2$, and the $k=2$ coefficients are missing.

**Explanation**

Use the reconstruction template from the previous section. The cue is the coefficient list, and the rule is to write the DC term once as $a_0/2$, then keep only the harmonic terms that are actually present. Missing coefficients contribute nothing.

$$
x(t)=\frac{a_0}{2}+a_1\cos(\omega_0 t)+b_1\sin(\omega_0 t)+a_3\cos(3\omega_0 t)+b_3\sin(3\omega_0 t)=\frac{10}{2}+(-1)\cos(\omega_0 t)+0\sin(\omega_0 t)+4\cos(3\omega_0 t)+(-2)\sin(3\omega_0 t)
$$

Halve $a_0$ to get $5$. The $k=1$ cosine term survives as $-\cos(\omega_0 t)$, but the $k=1$ sine term is $0\sin(\omega_0 t)$, so it contributes nothing. There is no $k=2$ pair to invent, so the next surviving terms are the $k=3$ cosine and sine terms.

$$
x(t)=5-\cos(\omega_0 t)+4\cos(3\omega_0 t)-2\sin(3\omega_0 t)
$$

So the reconstructed signal is $x(t)=5-\cos(\omega_0 t)+4\cos(3\omega_0 t)-2\sin(3\omega_0 t)$. The reusable rule is simple: write only the coefficients that are present, and treat blank or zero entries as no contribution.

## Reconstructing a Signal from a Coefficient Table

**Example:** Reconstruct $x(t)$ from the coefficient table with $a_0=12$, $a_1=2$, $b_1=-1$, $a_2=0$, $b_2=3$, $a_3=-4$, and $b_3=0$.

**Explanation**

Read the table row by row. The cue is the coefficient table, and the rule is to convert each listed $a_k$ and $b_k$ value into the matching cosine or sine term at $k\omega_0$, while writing $a_0/2$ once at the front and ignoring zero entries.

$$
x(t)=\frac{a_0}{2}+a_1\cos(\omega_0 t)+b_1\sin(\omega_0 t)+a_2\cos(2\omega_0 t)+b_2\sin(2\omega_0 t)+a_3\cos(3\omega_0 t)+b_3\sin(3\omega_0 t)=\frac{12}{2}+2\cos(\omega_0 t)+(-1)\sin(\omega_0 t)+0\cos(2\omega_0 t)+3\sin(2\omega_0 t)+(-4)\cos(3\omega_0 t)+0\sin(3\omega_0 t)
$$

Halve $a_0$ to get $6$. Then read the table row by row: $a_1=2$ goes on $\cos(\omega_0 t)$, $b_1=-1$ goes on $\sin(\omega_0 t)$, $b_2=3$ goes on $\sin(2\omega_0 t)$, and $a_3=-4$ goes on $\cos(3\omega_0 t)$. The zero entries $a_2=0$ and $b_3=0$ add nothing.

$$
x(t)=6+2\cos(\omega_0 t)-\sin(\omega_0 t)+3\sin(2\omega_0 t)-4\cos(3\omega_0 t)
$$

So the reconstructed signal is $x(t)=6+2\cos(\omega_0 t)-\sin(\omega_0 t)+3\sin(2\omega_0 t)-4\cos(3\omega_0 t)$. The reusable rule is to scan each row, keep the correct $k\omega_0$ argument, and drop any zero-valued coefficient before combining the terms.
