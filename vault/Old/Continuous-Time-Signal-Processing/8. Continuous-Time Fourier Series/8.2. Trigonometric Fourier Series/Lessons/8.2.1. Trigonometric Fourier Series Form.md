## Introduction to Trigonometric Fourier Series Form

A periodic continuous-time signal is written as a DC component plus sinusoidal harmonics at integer multiples of a fundamental angular frequency $\omega_0$. In this form, the template tells you where the constant level goes, where the cosine terms go, and where the sine terms go.

$$
x(t)=\frac{a_0}{2}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left[a_k\cos(k\omega_0 t)+b_k\sin(k\omega_0 t)\right]
$$

In the template, $\frac{a_0}{2}$ is the DC term, $a_k$ multiplies the cosine harmonic $\cos(k\omega_0 t)$, and $b_k$ multiplies the sine harmonic $\sin(k\omega_0 t)$. The index $k$ labels the harmonics, so each positive integer $k$ gives one cosine term and one sine term at frequency $k\omega_0$.

$$
x(t)=\frac{a_0}{2}+a_1\cos(\omega_0 t)+b_1\sin(\omega_0 t)+\cdots
$$

For the simplest base case, read the first harmonic as $a_1\cos(\omega_0 t)+b_1\sin(\omega_0 t)$ added to the DC term $\frac{a_0}{2}$. The next steps will fill this same template term by term, using the same notation and the same harmonic labels.

The key recognition cue is simple: when you see a periodic continuous-time signal written in trigonometric Fourier series form, look for the DC term first, then the cosine and sine harmonic pairs at $k\omega_0$.

## Writing the DC Term and First Harmonic Terms

**Example:** Write the DC term and first harmonic terms of the trigonometric Fourier series for a signal with $a_0=6$, $a_1=4$, and $b_1=-2$.

**Explanation**

Use the template from the previous step. The DC term comes from $a_0/2$, and the first harmonic uses $a_1\cos(\omega_0 t)$ and $b_1\sin(\omega_0 t)$.

$$
x(t)=\frac{a_0}{2}+a_1\cos(\omega_0 t)+b_1\sin(\omega_0 t)=\frac{6}{2}+4\cos(\omega_0 t)+(-2)\sin(\omega_0 t)
$$

The constant term is $\frac{a_0}{2}=\frac{6}{2}=3$. The first harmonic is $4\cos(\omega_0 t)-2\sin(\omega_0 t)$, with the minus sign staying attached to the sine coefficient.

$$
x(t)=3+4\cos(\omega_0 t)-2\sin(\omega_0 t)
$$

So the filled-in partial trigonometric Fourier series is $x(t)=3+4\cos(\omega_0 t)-2\sin(\omega_0 t)$. This is the first concrete fill-in after the template, with only the constant term and the $k=1$ pair used.

## Writing Sine and Cosine Terms at Higher Harmonic Frequencies

**Example:** Write the second- and third-harmonic terms of the trigonometric Fourier series for a signal with coefficients $a_2=-1$, $b_2=5$, $a_3=2$, and $b_3=-4$.

**Explanation**

The cue is that this step asks for higher-harmonic terms, so each coefficient pair stays attached to its own harmonic index. Use $k=2$ with $2\omega_0$ and $k=3$ with $3\omega_0$.

$$
(-1)\cos(2\omega_0 t)+5\sin(2\omega_0 t)+2\cos(3\omega_0 t)+(-4)\sin(3\omega_0 t)
$$

Now keep the signs attached to the coefficients and simplify each term. The second-harmonic pair is $-\cos(2\omega_0 t)+5\sin(2\omega_0 t)$, and the third-harmonic pair is $2\cos(3\omega_0 t)-4\sin(3\omega_0 t)$.

$$
-\cos(2\omega_0 t)+5\sin(2\omega_0 t)+2\cos(3\omega_0 t)-4\sin(3\omega_0 t)
$$

So the higher-harmonic contribution is $-\cos(2\omega_0 t)+5\sin(2\omega_0 t)+2\cos(3\omega_0 t)-4\sin(3\omega_0 t)$. The rule is the same as before: keep each cosine-sine pair with the same $k$ and only change the frequency label to $k\omega_0$.

## Writing the General Trigonometric Fourier Series Sum

**Example:** Write the full trigonometric Fourier series for a periodic signal with DC term $a_0/2$ and harmonic coefficients $a_1,b_1,a_2,b_2,a_3,b_3$.

**Explanation**

The cue is that this step is asking for the compact full series, so keep the DC term outside the harmonic sum and keep each cosine-sine pair together under the same harmonic index $k$.

$$
x(t)=\frac{a_0}{2}+a_1\cos(\omega_0 t)+b_1\sin(\omega_0 t)+a_2\cos(2\omega_0 t)+b_2\sin(2\omega_0 t)+a_3\cos(3\omega_0 t)+b_3\sin(3\omega_0 t)
$$

Now rewrite the repeated harmonic pattern with a summation. The index $k=1,2,3,\dots$ runs over all positive harmonics, and each value of $k$ contributes one cosine term and one sine term at frequency $k\omega_0$.

$$
x(t)=\frac{a_0}{2}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left[a_k\cos(k\omega_0 t)+b_k\sin(k\omega_0 t)\right]
$$

So the compact full trigonometric Fourier series is $x(t)=\frac{a_0}{2}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left[a_k\cos(k\omega_0 t)+b_k\sin(k\omega_0 t)\right]$. The explicit harmonic terms and the summation form describe the same structure, with the DC term outside and the harmonic pair inside each $k$.
