## Introduction to Meaning of Fourier Coefficients

A trigonometric Fourier series is a weighted sum of one DC term and infinitely many sinusoidal harmonics. Read the template as a coefficient map: the subscripts tell you which harmonic you are looking at, and the coefficient names tell you whether the term is a constant, a cosine weight, or a sine weight.

$$
x(t)=\frac{a_0}{2}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\big[a_k\cos(k\omega_0 t)+b_k\sin(k\omega_0 t)\big]
$$

The term $\frac{a_0}{2}$ is the DC level. It is the average value or vertical offset of the signal, so it appears once outside the harmonic sum rather than repeating at every frequency.

$$
\text{DC level}=\frac{a_0}{2}
$$

For each positive integer $k$, the pair $a_k\cos(k\omega_0 t)$ and $b_k\sin(k\omega_0 t)$ is the $k$th harmonic. The signs of $a_k$ and $b_k$ matter: they tell you whether that cosine or sine contribution is positive, negative, or absent.

$$
\text{harmonic }k\text{ lives at }k\omega_0
$$

Start with the simplest base case: $\frac{a_0}{2}$ plus the first harmonic pair $a_1\cos(\omega_0 t)+b_1\sin(\omega_0 t)$. That base case makes the coefficient roles visible before the next examples interpret one coefficient type at a time and then combine them.

## Interpreting the DC Level from $a_0$

**Example:** A periodic signal is written in trigonometric Fourier series form with $a_0=6$. Explain what the DC level is and how it affects the whole signal.

**Explanation**

The cue is the constant term $\frac{a_0}{2}$ in the Fourier series. For $a_0=6$, the DC level is the constant offset obtained by halving $a_0$.

$$
\frac{a_0}{2}=\frac{6}{2}=3
$$

So the signal is shifted upward by $3$ at every time instant. That constant term does not oscillate; it is the average value of the signal.

## Interpreting Signed Cosine Weights from $a_k$

**Example:** A signal has $a_1=3$ and $a_2=-2$ in its trigonometric Fourier series. Explain what the positive and negative cosine coefficients say about the harmonics at $\omega_0$ and $2\omega_0$.

**Explanation**

The cue is the cosine coefficient $a_k$. After reading $\frac{a_0}{2}$ as the DC level, read each $a_k$ as the signed weight multiplying the cosine harmonic at frequency $k\omega_0$.

$$
a_1\cos(\omega_0 t)+a_2\cos(2\omega_0 t)=3\cos(\omega_0 t)-2\cos(2\omega_0 t)
$$

So $a_1=3$ means a positive cosine contribution at $\omega_0$, while $a_2=-2$ means the $2\omega_0$ cosine is inverted and scaled by $2$. The magnitude tells the strength of the harmonic, and the sign tells its direction.

If $a_k=0$, that cosine harmonic is absent. Read $a_k$ as the weight on the cosine basis at harmonic $k$, not as the frequency itself.

<a id="interpreting-signed-sine-weights-from-bk"></a>
## Interpreting Signed Sine Weights from $b_k$

**Example:** A signal has $b_1=-4$ and $b_3=2$ in its trigonometric Fourier series. Explain what the signed sine coefficients say about the harmonics at $\omega_0$ and $3\omega_0$.

**Explanation**

The cue is the sine coefficient $b_k$. Read each $b_k$ as the signed weight multiplying the sine harmonic at frequency $k\omega_0$.

$$
b_1\sin(\omega_0 t)+b_3\sin(3\omega_0 t)=-4\sin(\omega_0 t)+2\sin(3\omega_0 t)
$$

So $b_1=-4$ means an inverted sine contribution at $\omega_0$, while $b_3=2$ means a positive sine contribution at $3\omega_0$. The magnitude tells the strength of the harmonic, and the sign tells its direction.

If $b_k=0$, that sine harmonic is absent. Read $b_k$ as the weight on the sine basis at harmonic $k$, not as the frequency itself.

<a id="identifying-present-and-missing-harmonics-from-coefficients"></a>
## Identifying Present and Missing Harmonics from Coefficients

**Example:** A coefficient table gives $a_0=4$, $a_1=0$, $a_2=-3$, $b_1=5$, and $b_2=0$. Identify which harmonic terms are present, which are missing, and what the DC term is.

**Explanation**

The cue is the mixed coefficient table. Check $a_0$ first, because it sets the DC level: $a_0=4$ means the signal has DC term $a_0/2=2$.

$$
x(t)=2+0\cos(\omega_0 t)-3\cos(2\omega_0 t)+5\sin(\omega_0 t)+0\sin(2\omega_0 t)
$$

Then scan each harmonic index $k$ and test $a_k$ and $b_k$ for zero versus nonzero. At $k=1$, $a_1=0$ means no cosine term at $\omega_0$, but $b_1=5$ means the sine term at $\omega_0$ is present. At $k=2$, $a_2=-3$ means the cosine term at $2\omega_0$ is present with negative weight, while $b_2=0$ means no sine term at $2\omega_0$.

So the signal has the DC level $2$, a sine harmonic at $\omega_0$, and a cosine harmonic at $2\omega_0$. The zero coefficients remove only their matching terms; they do not remove the whole signal.
