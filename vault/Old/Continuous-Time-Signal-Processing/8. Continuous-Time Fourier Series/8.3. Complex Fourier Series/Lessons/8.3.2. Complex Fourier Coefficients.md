## Introduction to Complex Fourier Coefficients

Start with the complex Fourier series $x(t)=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty} C_k e^{jk\omega_0 t}$. The coefficient on the $k$th exponential term is $C_k$, so the subscript tells you which harmonic the coefficient weights.

$$
x(t)=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty} C_k e^{jk\omega_0 t}
$$

Use the simplest base case first: $x(t)=C_0+C_1e^{j\omega_0 t}$. Here $C_1$ is the 1st-harmonic weight and $C_0$ is the DC weight, so one coefficient read already tells you which part of the signal each term controls.

$$
x(t)=C_0+C_1e^{j\omega_0 t}
$$

Read each coefficient in polar form as $C_k=|C_k|e^{j\angle C_k}$. The magnitude $|C_k|$ gives the harmonic strength, and the phase $\angle C_k$ gives its offset.

$$
C_k=|C_k|e^{j\angle C_k}
$$

A list of coefficients is therefore a compact description of the periodic signal: it tells you which harmonics are present and how strongly each one appears.

## Identifying the Harmonic Indexed by a Coefficient

**Example:** In the term $C_3e^{j3\omega_0 t}$, identify which harmonic $C_3$ weights and explain why the subscript, not the exponential alone, tells you the harmonic number.

**Explanation**

The cue is the coefficient-subscript pair. In $C_3e^{j3\omega_0 t}$, the subscript $3$ names the harmonic, so $C_3$ is the weight on the 3rd harmonic.

$$
C_3e^{j3\omega_0 t}
$$

Read the subscript as the harmonic index $k=3$. That is the number you use to name the harmonic in words: the 3rd harmonic.

The exponential term agrees with that same index because the factor $e^{j3\omega_0 t}$ also carries the number $3$. The coefficient and the exponential are matched to the same harmonic.

So the answer is that $C_3$ weights the 3rd harmonic. The coefficient's value is not needed yet; this step is only about reading the harmonic index from the subscript.

## Interpreting Coefficient Magnitude as Harmonic Weight

**Example:** If $C_2=4e^{j\pi/6}$, state the weight of the 2nd harmonic and separate the magnitude from the phase.

**Explanation**

The cue is the polar form of the coefficient. In $C_2=4e^{j\pi/6}$, the number in front of the exponential is the magnitude, so it is the weight of the 2nd harmonic.

$$
C_2=4e^{j\pi/6}
$$

Read the magnitude as $|C_2|=4$. That is the harmonic's strength or weight; the phase $\pi/6$ is separate and does not change that weight.

So the 2nd harmonic has weight 4, regardless of the phase. When the question asks for the harmonic's strength, report the magnitude and leave the angle out of the weight claim.

## Interpreting Coefficient Phase as Harmonic Phase

**Example:** If $C_{-1}=3e^{-j\pi/4}$, identify the phase of the $-1$st harmonic and keep it separate from the magnitude.

**Explanation**

The cue is the polar form attached to the harmonic index. In $C_{-1}=3e^{-j\pi/4}$, the subscript $-1$ tells you which harmonic it is, and the angle $-\pi/4$ tells you the phase.

$$
C_{-1}=3e^{-j\pi/4}
$$

Read the angle as $\angle C_{-1}=-\pi/4$. That is the phase carried by the $-1$st harmonic.

The magnitude $3$ is separate. It tells you the harmonic's weight, but it does not change the phase.

So the phase answer is $-\pi/4$. When the question asks for phase, report the angle and leave the magnitude out of the claim.

<a id="reading-nonzero-coefficients-as-present-harmonics"></a>
## Reading Nonzero Coefficients as Present Harmonics

**Example:** Given $C_{-2}=0$, $C_{-1}=2e^{j\pi/4}$, $C_0=3$, $C_1=0$, and $C_2=1e^{-j\pi/3}$, list the harmonics that are present in the signal and say which are absent.

**Explanation**

The cue is the coefficient list. A harmonic is present exactly when its coefficient is nonzero, so the job is to keep the nonzero coefficients and drop the zeros.

$$
C_{-2}=0,\quad C_{-1}=2e^{j\pi/4},\quad C_0=3,\quad C_1=0,\quad C_2=1e^{-j\pi/3}
$$

Here $C_{-2}=0$ and $C_1=0$, so the $-2$nd harmonic and the 1st harmonic are absent. The nonzero coefficients are $C_{-1}=2e^{j\pi/4}$, $C_0=3$, and $C_2=1e^{-j\pi/3}$.

So the present harmonics are the $-1$st harmonic, the DC term at $k=0$, and the 2nd harmonic. The zero coefficients do not contribute to the signal.

That is the rule to use in this kind of question: nonzero coefficients mark present harmonics, and zero coefficients mark absent harmonics.
