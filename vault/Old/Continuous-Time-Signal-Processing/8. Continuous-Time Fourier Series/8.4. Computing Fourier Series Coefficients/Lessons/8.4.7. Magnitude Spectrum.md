## Introduction to Magnitude Spectrum

The quantity to recognize here is the magnitude spectrum. It takes the complex Fourier coefficients $c_k$ from the previous step and replaces each one with its magnitude, so the spectrum height at harmonic index $k$ is always nonnegative.

$$
M_k = |c_k|
$$

![](<../Source/8.4.7. Magnitude Spectrum/Images/l007-s001-te-section-001.png>)

Read the centered stem at $k=0$ as the DC magnitude $M_0 = |c_0|$. Read each stem at $k=\pm 1$, $\pm 2$, and so on as the magnitude of that positive or negative harmonic, so the plot records harmonic strength at each index.

$$
M_0 = |c_0|
$$

$$
M_{\pm k} = |c_{\pm k}|
$$

This plot keeps strength and discards phase: two coefficients with different signs or angles can land at the same height if their absolute values match. That is the only idea needed here, and it is the basis for reading harmonic strength in the sections that follow.

<a id="computing-magnitudes-of-complex-fourier-coefficients"></a>
## Computing Magnitudes of Complex Fourier Coefficients

**Example:** Given $c_1=3-4j$ and $c_{-1}=3+4j$, compute $|c_1|$ and $|c_{-1}|$.

**Explanation**

The cue is a conjugate pair of complex Fourier coefficients. When you see $c_1$ and $c_{-1}$, the move is to convert each one into a magnitude using $|c_k|$ because the stem heights in a magnitude spectrum are nonnegative.

$$
\begin{aligned}
|c_1| &= |3-4j| = \sqrt{3^2+(-4)^2} = \sqrt{25} = 5 \\
|c_{-1}| &= |3+4j| = \sqrt{3^2+4^2} = \sqrt{25} = 5
\end{aligned}
$$

The result is the pair $(5, 5)$. Both harmonic stems have the same height, so this step shows how a conjugate pair can produce equal magnitude-spectrum values even though the complex coefficients differ in sign.

## Including the DC Coefficient in a Magnitude Spectrum

**Example:** Given $c_0=6$, $c_1=2-2j$, and $c_{-1}=2+2j$, include the DC coefficient when listing the magnitude spectrum values.

**Explanation**

The cue is that the spectrum includes a zero-frequency term as well as a conjugate harmonic pair. Use the same magnitude rule for every index: the DC stem is $|c_0|$, and the harmonic stems are $|c_1|$ and $|c_{-1}|$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
M_0 &= |c_0| = |6| = 6 \\
M_1 &= |c_1| = |2-2j| = \sqrt{2^2+(-2)^2} = \sqrt{8} = 2\sqrt{2} \\
M_{-1} &= |c_{-1}| = |2+2j| = \sqrt{2^2+2^2} = \sqrt{8} = 2\sqrt{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

In index order, the magnitude-spectrum values are $6$, $2\sqrt{2}$, and $2\sqrt{2}$. The DC term is included by the same absolute-value rule as the other coefficients; it is simply the $k=0$ stem height in the same nonnegative spectrum.

## Plotting Positive and Negative Harmonic Magnitudes

**Example:** Given $|c_0|=6$, $|c_{\pm1}|=2\sqrt{2}$, and $|c_{\pm2}|=1$, draw the centered magnitude-spectrum stem plot.

**Explanation**

The cue is that the magnitudes are already known, so the job is to place them on a centered spectrum axis. Use the same magnitude rule as before, and put one nonnegative stem at each harmonic index.

$$
\begin{aligned}
M_0 &= 6 \\
M_{\pm1} &= 2\sqrt{2} \\
M_{\pm2} &= 1
\end{aligned}
$$

![](<../Source/8.4.7. Magnitude Spectrum/Images/l007-s004-te-section-004.png>)

Place the stem at $k=0$ with height $6$, the stems at $k=\pm1$ with height $2\sqrt{2}$, and the stems at $k=\pm2$ with height $1$. The finished plot is centered, symmetric across matching harmonic indices, and every stem stays nonnegative.

<a id="interpreting-harmonic-strengths-from-a-magnitude-spectrum"></a>
## Interpreting Harmonic Strengths from a Magnitude Spectrum

**Example:** Given a magnitude spectrum with stems $|c_0|=5$, $|c_{\pm1}|=2$, and $|c_{\pm2}|=0$, interpret which harmonic is strongest and which harmonic is absent.

**Explanation**

Read the stems as a strength comparison, not as signed values. The tallest stem marks the dominant harmonic component, and any zero-height stem marks an absent harmonic.

$$
M_0 = 5,\quad M_{\pm1} = 2,\quad M_{\pm2} = 0
$$

![](<../Source/8.4.7. Magnitude Spectrum/Images/l007-s005-te-section-005.png>)

Here, the DC component is strongest because $|c_0|=5$ is the tallest stem. The $\pm1$ harmonic pair is present but weaker, and the $\pm2$ harmonic pair is absent because its stems have height $0$.
