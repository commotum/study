## Introduction to DC Component

The DC component of a periodic signal is the constant part of the waveform: the average value you get over one full period, written as `$x_{\mathrm{DC}}$`.

$$
x_{\mathrm{DC}} = \frac{1}{T_0}\int_{t_0}^{t_0+T_0} x(t)\,dt
$$

Because the signal is periodic, you can start that average at any time $t_0$ as long as the interval length is $T_0$. The average does not change when you slide the window by whole periods.

$$
x(t)=C \Rightarrow x_{\mathrm{DC}}=C
$$

For the base case $x(t)=C$, the average returns the same constant, so the definition matches the idea of a flat signal: its DC component is just the level it sits at.

In Fourier series language, that average is the zero-frequency term. Later examples will compute the same quantity from signed areas and vertical offsets, but the rule is already here: average one period, and you have the DC component.

<a id="computing-the-average-value-over-one-period"></a>
## Computing the Average Value Over One Period

**Example:** Let $x(t)=4$ for $0 \le t < T_0/4$ and $x(t)=0$ for $T_0/4 \le t < T_0$, repeated every $T_0$. Find the DC component.

**Explanation**

The cue is a periodic piecewise-constant signal, so the DC component is still the average value over one period. Use the same rule from the previous step: average the signal over any interval of length $T_0$.

$$
x_{\mathrm{DC}} = \frac{1}{T_0}\int_0^{T_0} x(t)\,dt
$$

Over one period, the signal is $4$ on the first quarter and $0$ on the remaining three quarters. That means we compute signed area by weighting each level by how long it lasts, not by averaging the heights alone.

$$
x_{\mathrm{DC}} = \frac{1}{T_0}\left(4 \cdot \frac{T_0}{4} + 0 \cdot \frac{3T_0}{4}\right) = 1
$$

So the DC component is $1$. In Fourier series language, that is the zero-frequency term: the constant level left after the waveform is averaged over one period.

<a id="recognizing-a-zero-dc-component-from-balanced-areas"></a>
## Recognizing a Zero DC Component from Balanced Areas

**Example:** Let $x(t)=2$ for $0 \le t < T_0/3$ and $x(t)=-1$ for $T_0/3 \le t < T_0$, repeated every $T_0$. Find the DC component.

**Explanation**

The cue is a periodic piecewise-constant signal with one positive level and one negative level, so the DC component comes from signed area over one period. Use the same average formula as before and check whether the positive and negative contributions cancel.

$$
x_{\mathrm{DC}} = \frac{1}{T_0}\left(2\cdot \frac{T_0}{3} + (-1)\cdot \frac{2T_0}{3}\right)
$$

The positive contribution is $\frac{2T_0}{3}$ and the negative contribution is $-\frac{2T_0}{3}$. They have equal magnitude, so the signed areas cancel before the division by $T_0$.

$$
x_{\mathrm{DC}} = \frac{1}{T_0}\left(\frac{2T_0}{3}-\frac{2T_0}{3}\right)=0
$$

So the DC component is $0$. The waveform is not zero everywhere, but its one-period average is zero, so the zero-frequency term in the Fourier series vanishes.

<a id="computing-a-nonzero-dc-component-from-vertical-offset"></a>
## Computing a Nonzero DC Component from Vertical Offset

**Example:** Let $q(t)=2$ for $0 \le t < T_0/3$ and $q(t)=-1$ for $T_0/3 \le t < T_0$, and define $x(t)=3+q(t)$. Find the DC component of $x(t)$.

**Explanation**

The cue is the decomposition $x(t)=3+q(t)$. Treat $3$ as the constant vertical offset and $q(t)$ as the balanced periodic variation. The DC component is the one-period average, so average the two parts separately.

$$
x_{\mathrm{DC}} = \frac{1}{T_0}\int_0^{T_0} \bigl(3+q(t)\bigr)\,dt = 3 + \frac{1}{T_0}\int_0^{T_0} q(t)\,dt
$$

From the previous step, the variation $q(t)$ has zero average because its positive and negative areas cancel over one period. That means only the constant baseline survives the averaging.

$$
x_{\mathrm{DC}} = 3 + \frac{1}{T_0}\left(2\cdot \frac{T_0}{3} + (-1)\cdot \frac{2T_0}{3}\right) = 3 + 0 = 3
$$

So the DC component is $3$. The vertical offset is exactly the zero-frequency term, while the balanced waveform contributes no additional DC component.

## Interpreting the DC Component as the Zero-Frequency Term

**Example:** Given the trigonometric Fourier series $x(t)=\frac{a_0}{2}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\big(a_n\cos(n\omega_0 t)+b_n\sin(n\omega_0 t)\big)$, identify the DC component and explain why it is the zero-frequency term.

**Explanation**

The cue is the standard trigonometric Fourier-series form. The DC component is the constant term, so look for the piece with no oscillation: $\frac{a_0}{2}$.

$$
x(t)=\frac{a_0}{2}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\big(a_n\cos(n\omega_0 t)+b_n\sin(n\omega_0 t)\big)
$$

Every term in the sum has $n\ge 1$, so each one is a harmonic at nonzero frequency. Those sine and cosine terms average to zero over one period, so they do not contribute to the DC component.

$$
x_{\mathrm{DC}}=\frac{a_0}{2}
$$

So the DC component is the zero-frequency term $\frac{a_0}{2}$. A frequency-$0$ term does not oscillate, so it matches the constant average-value part of the signal.
