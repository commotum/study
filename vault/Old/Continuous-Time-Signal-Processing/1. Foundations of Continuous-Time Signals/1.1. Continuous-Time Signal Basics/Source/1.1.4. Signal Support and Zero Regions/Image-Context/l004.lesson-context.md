Generated step/example content:
Tutorial blocks:
- paragraph:
  text: A continuous-time signal is a function of time. At each time $t$, the signal value $x(t)$ is either zero or nonzero. Support and zero regions come from marking those two possibilities over intervals of time.
- paragraph:
  text: The support of a signal is the time region where the signal is nonzero. To identify support, scan along the time axis and mark the intervals where $x(t)\ne 0$.
- display-math:
  latex: \operatorname{support of } x(t): \{t : x(t) \ne 0\}
- paragraph:
  text: A zero region is an interval where the signal is identically zero. This is stronger than saying the signal equals zero at one isolated time; every time in the interval must have value $0$.
- display-math:
  latex: \text{zero region: an interval on which } x(t)=0 \text{ for every } t
- image:
  image_slot_id: section-001
- paragraph:
  text: In the picture, the signal is nonzero only on the middle interval. That interval is the support. The flat parts on the time axis before and after the support are zero regions because $x(t)=0$ for every time in those intervals.
- paragraph:
  text: The repeatable procedure is: first find where $x(t)\ne 0$ and write those intervals as the support. Then write the intervals where $x(t)=0$ throughout as the zero regions. The same procedure applies whether the signal is shown by a graph, a piecewise definition, or a formula.
