Correct answer: $\frac{2}{3}$
Answer explanation blocks:
- paragraph:
  text: Read the support from the graph: the signal is nonzero only on $0 \le t \le 2$. On $0 \le t \le 1$, the line is $x(t)=t$; on $1 \le t \le 2$, the line is $x(t)=2-t$.
- display-math:
  latex: E=\int_0^1 t^2\,dt+\int_1^2 (2-t)^2\,dt
- paragraph:
  text: Each side contributes $\frac{1}{3}$, so the total energy is $\frac{2}{3}$. The area under the graph is not the answer; the signal must be squared before integrating.
