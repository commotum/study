## Introduction to Fundamental Period

A continuous-time signal is periodic when there is a positive time shift that makes the signal repeat itself for every $t$. The test is whether shifting by $T$ leaves the waveform unchanged.

$$
x(t+T)=x(t)
$$

The fundamental period, written $T_0$, is the smallest positive shift that works. If several positive shifts repeat the same signal, $T_0$ is the first one you would name.

$$
T_0 = \min\{T>0 : x(t+T)=x(t)\}
$$

Any larger integer multiple of $T_0$ is also a period, but it is not fundamental. So the question is not just whether the signal repeats, but what the smallest positive repeat shift is.

A simple repeating sinusoid or waveform makes the idea concrete: one visible cycle gives a picture of the smallest shift that returns the signal to the same shape. That is the meaning of $T_0$.

Use $T_0$ as the basic repeat time for any periodic signal. Later sections will use it to connect period with angular frequency and harmonic structure.

## Checking Whether a Time Shift Is a Period

**Example:** Determine whether $T=2$ is a period of $x(t)=\cos(\pi t)$.

**Explanation**

The cue is the proposed shift $T=2$. To test it, substitute $t+2$ into the signal and check whether the result equals $x(t)$ for every $t$.

$$
x(t+2)=\cos(\pi(t+2))=\cos(\pi t+2\pi)=\cos(\pi t)=x(t)
$$

The angle increases by $2\pi$, and cosine repeats after a full $2\pi$ turn. So the shifted signal matches the original signal for every $t$.

Therefore, $T=2$ is a period of $x(t)=\cos(\pi t)$.

<a id="finding-the-smallest-positive-repeating-time-shift"></a>
## Finding the Smallest Positive Repeating Time Shift

**Example:** Find the fundamental period of $x(t)=\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2}t\right)$.

**Explanation**

The cue is the coefficient of $t$ inside the cosine. To find the fundamental period, set the phase advance from $t$ to $t+T_0$ equal to $2\pi$ and solve for the smallest positive $T_0$.

$$
x(t+T_0)=\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2}(t+T_0)\right)=\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2}t+\frac{\pi}{2}T_0\right)
$$

$$
\frac{\pi}{2}T_0=2\pi \quad\Rightarrow\quad T_0=4
$$

That is the smallest positive shift that produces one full $2\pi$ phase wrap, so the fundamental period is $T_0=4$.

<a id="distinguishing-a-period-from-the-fundamental-period"></a>
## Distinguishing a Period from the Fundamental Period

**Example:** For $x(t)=\cos(2\pi t)$, determine whether $T=2$ is the fundamental period or just a period.

**Explanation**

The cue is the proposed shift $T=2$. First substitute it into the signal and check whether the waveform repeats exactly.

$$
x(t+2)=\cos(2\pi(t+2))=\cos(2\pi t+4\pi)=\cos(2\pi t)=x(t)
$$

So $T=2$ is a valid period. But to decide whether it is fundamental, find the smallest positive shift that produces the same repeat.

$$
2\pi T_0=2\pi \quad\Rightarrow\quad T_0=1
$$

Because the smallest positive repeating shift is $T_0=1$, the shift $T=2$ is only a period. It is a larger multiple of the fundamental period, not the fundamental period itself.

## Finding the Fundamental Period from One Complete Cycle

**Example:** A periodic signal completes one full cycle from $t=1$ to $t=7$. What is its fundamental period?

**Explanation**

The cue is the stated start and end of one complete cycle. The fundamental period is the length of that repeating interval.

$$
T_0=7-1=6
$$

So the signal repeats every $6$ time units. That interval length is the fundamental period.
