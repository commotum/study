## Introduction to Periodic Continuous-Time Signals

A periodic continuous-time signal repeats itself after a positive time shift. The cue is that the same waveform comes back at a later time, and the equality must hold for every $t$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x(t+T) &= x(t) \\
T &> 0
\end{aligned}
$$

Check the definition on a simple repeating sinusoid. The point is to see one full cycle match the next cycle after a positive shift.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x(t) &= \cos(2\pi t) \\
x(t+2) &= \cos(2\pi(t+2)) \\
&= \cos(2\pi t + 4\pi) \\
&= \cos(2\pi t) \\
&= x(t)
\end{aligned}
$$

A signal can have more than one valid positive shift, so this lesson only needs existence of some $T>0$, not the smallest one. If no positive shift works for every $t$, the signal is not periodic; later lessons will use this idea for the fundamental period, angular frequency, and harmonics.

## Checking a Proposed Period from the Definition

**Example:** Verify that $T=2$ is a period for $x(t)=\cos(2\pi t)$ by checking that $x(t+2)=x(t)$ for every $t$.

**Explanation**

The cue is a proposed positive shift. For $x(t)=\cos(2\pi t)$, test $T=2$ by substituting $t+2$ into the formula and checking whether the result matches the original signal for every $t$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x(t+2) &= \cos(2\pi(t+2)) \\
&= \cos(2\pi t + 4\pi) \\
&= \cos(2\pi t) \\
&= x(t)
\end{aligned}
$$

The substitution returns the original signal, so $T=2$ is a valid period. That is the definition in action: one positive shift works for every $t$.

## Finding a Valid Positive Shift for a Repeating Signal

**Example:** A signal takes the value $1$ on $0 \le t < 1$, $0$ on $1 \le t < 2$, and $-1$ on $2 \le t < 3$, then repeats that three-part block forever. Find one positive $T$ such that $x(t+T)=x(t)$.

**Explanation**

The cue is a repeating block description. Read one complete block, note that its length is $3$, and test that block length as the candidate positive shift.

$$
\begin{aligned}
[0,1) &: 1 \\
[1,2) &: 0 \\
[2,3) &: -1 \\
[3,4) &: 1 \\
[4,5) &: 0 \\
[5,6) &: -1
\end{aligned}
$$

The same sequence of values reappears after $3$ time units, and each subinterval in the next block matches the original block after that shift. Therefore $x(t+3)=x(t)$ for every $t$, so $T=3$ is a valid positive shift.

## Recognizing Periodicity from a Graph

**Example:** From the graph of $x(t)$, decide whether the waveform repeats after a positive shift and identify one visible shift that works.

**Explanation**

The cue is the graph itself. Find one obvious landmark on a cycle, compare it with the same landmark on the next cycle, and read the horizontal distance as a candidate positive shift.

![](<../Source/1.2.1. Periodic Continuous-Time Signals/Images/l001-s004-te-section-004.png>)

The same waveform shape reappears $4$ units later, so the landmark check matches the full cycle, not just one point. That gives one visible positive shift of $4$.

$$
x(t+4)=x(t)
$$

Because the whole graph matches after that shift, the signal is periodic. The graph is the cue, the repeated cycle is the rule, and $T=4$ is one visible positive shift.

<a id="showing-a-signal-is-not-periodic"></a>
## Showing a Signal Is Not Periodic

**Example:** Show that $x(t)=t$ is not periodic by assuming a positive shift $T$ and checking whether $x(t+T)=x(t)$ can hold for every $t$.

**Explanation**

The cue is a monotone trend. Test periodicity by assuming a positive shift $T$ and substituting it into $x(t)=t$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x(t+T) &= t+T \\
x(t) &= t \\
t+T &= t \\
T &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

That forces $T=0$, which contradicts the requirement that a period be positive. So no positive $T$ satisfies $x(t+T)=x(t)$ for every $t$, and the signal is not periodic.

**Question 7:**

```quiz

- id: b
  content: |-

- id: c
  content: |-

  correct: true
  feedback: |-
    A period must work for every $t$, not just one time value. Substituting gives $x(t+T)=2t+2T$, and matching that to $2t$ for every $t$ forces $T=0$, which is impossible for a period.

- id: d
  content: |-
    Because the signal is linear, it is periodic whenever the shift is positive.

- id: e
  content: |-
    A negative shift such as $T=-1$ shows the signal is periodic in reverse time.
```
