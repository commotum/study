## Introduction to Finding Output with $Y(s)=X(s)H(s)$

The core move of this section is to compute the output of a continuous-time LTI system by following the fixed flow: form $Y(s)=X(s)H(s)$, simplify the Laplace-domain product, and then invert once to get $y(t)$.

The required objects are the input transform $X(s)$, the transfer function $H(s)$, and the target time-domain output $y(t)$. They connect through $Y(s)=\mathcal{L}\{y(t)\}$, then $y(t)=\mathcal{L}^{-1}\{Y(s)\}$ at the end of the section.

Use a base case with first-order factors only: set $X(s)=\frac{1}{s+3}$ and $H(s)=\frac{2}{s+4}$. Then form $Y(s)=\frac{2}{(s+3)(s+4)}$ and identify this as the immediate inverse-transform target before moving to harder algebra.

Checkpoint before inversion: decide whether $Y(s)$ is already in a table match or whether it needs extra algebra such as expansion or partial-fraction setup. Keep this decision explicit so every section follows the same action sequence.

$$
Y(s)=X(s)H(s)
$$

$$
y(t)=\mathcal{L}^{-1}\{Y(s)\}
$$

## Forming $Y(s)$ from $X(s)$ and $H(s)$

**Example:** Compute $Y(s)$ for $X(s)=1/(s+3)$ and $H(s)=4/(s+1)$.

**Explanation**

The cue is the product rule for an LTI system in the Laplace domain: form $Y(s)=X(s)H(s)$ before doing any inverse-transform work.

$$
Y(s)=X(s)H(s)=\left(\frac{1}{s+3}\right)\left(\frac{4}{s+1}\right)
$$

Multiply the numerators together and multiply the denominator factors together. The numerator is $1\cdot 4=4$, and the denominator keeps both first-order factors, $(s+3)(s+1)$.

$$
Y(s)=\frac{4}{(s+3)(s+1)}=\frac{4}{(s+1)(s+3)}
$$

So the formed output transform is $Y(s)=\frac{4}{(s+1)(s+3)}$. Its pole factors are $s+1$ and $s+3$, giving poles at $s=-1$ and $s=-3$ for the later inverse-transform step.

This exact rational product is not yet a single basic table-match form. The important result for this step is the correctly formed product; any algebraic decomposition belongs to the next step.

## Simplifying the Laplace-Domain Product

**Example:** Find the simplified algebraic form of $Y(s)$ for $X(s)=\frac{2s+4}{s+1}$ and $H(s)=\frac{s+3}{s+2}$.

**Explanation**

The cue is still $Y(s)=X(s)H(s)$, but now each factor is more complex than the first example, so this step focuses on algebraic cleanup while preserving the one-notation flow.

$$
Y(s)=X(s)H(s)=\left(\frac{2s+4}{s+1}\right)\left(\frac{s+3}{s+2}\right)
$$

Multiply the two numerators to get $(2s+4)(s+3)$ and multiply the two denominators to get $(s+1)(s+2)$, keeping numerator and denominator structure explicit.

$$
(2s+4)(s+3)=2s^2+10s+12, \quad (s+1)(s+2)=s^2+3s+2
$$

$$
Y(s)=\frac{(2s+4)(s+3)}{(s+1)(s+2)}=\frac{2s^2+10s+12}{s^2+3s+2}
$$

The simplified result is $Y(s)=\frac{2s^2+10s+12}{s^2+3s+2}$, equivalent to the fully factorized ratio $Y(s)=\frac{(2s+4)(s+3)}{(s+1)(s+2)}$. This form is now clean and ready for the next checkpoint: decide whether this is a direct table match or whether partial fractions are needed before inversion.

## Finding $y(t)$ When $Y(s)$ Matches a Known Transform Pair

**Example:** From $Y(s)=\frac{5}{s+4}$, derive $y(t)$ directly.

**Explanation**

The cue is a single first-order transform factor: $Y(s)$ is already in a direct inversion shape, so the move is to identify the matching table entry and map constants.

$$
Y(s)=\frac{5}{s+4}
$$

Use the pair $\mathcal{L}\{e^{-at}u(t)\}=\frac{1}{s+a}$ and compare directly with $Y(s)=\frac{K}{s+a}$.

Here, the denominator has $a=4$ and the numerator gives $K=5$, so this is the exact form of a single-term decaying exponential with no extra decomposition.

$$
Y(s)=\frac{K}{s+a}\Longrightarrow y(t)=Ke^{-at}u(t),\quad a=4,\;K=5
$$

$$
y(t)=5e^{-4t}u(t)
$$

State result and checkpoint: if a later $Y(s)$ has multiple factors or repeated/complex poles, skip the direct table shortcut and route to the partial-fraction or decomposition route.

## Finding $y(t)$ When $Y(s)$ Requires Partial Fractions

**Example:** Find $y(t)$ for $Y(s)=\frac{2s+9}{(s+2)(s+5)}$ using partial fractions.

**Explanation**

The cue is a rational $Y(s)$ with two distinct poles, so direct table inversion is not immediate and the controlled next move is partial fraction decomposition.

Set up the decomposition as $Y(s)=\frac{A}{s+2}+\frac{B}{s+5}$ and keep a stable sign convention with positive $a$ in terms of $s+ a$ factors.

$$
\frac{2s+9}{(s+2)(s+5)}=\frac{A}{s+2}+\frac{B}{s+5}=\frac{A(s+5)+B(s+2)}{(s+2)(s+5)}
$$

$$
2s+9=A(s+5)+B(s+2)= (A+B)s+(5A+2B)
$$

Match coefficients to get $A+B=2$ and $5A+2B=9$, so $A=\frac{5}{3}$ and $B=\frac{1}{3}$. The inverse-ready form is now ready.

$$
Y(s)=\frac{5/3}{s+2}+\frac{1/3}{s+5}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad y(t)=\frac{5}{3}e^{-2t}u(t)+\frac{1}{3}e^{-5t}u(t)
$$

The poles are at $s=-2$ and $s=-5$, both in the left half-plane, so this output is a stable sum of two decaying exponentials.
