## Introduction to Deriving Transfer Functions from Differential Equations

In this section, the target move is to turn one input-output differential equation into one transfer-function ratio, then reuse that algebraic template repeatedly. The goal is to isolate the map $H(s)=\frac{Y(s)}{X(s)}$ so the system can be analyzed by poles, stability, and frequency response in later topics.

Use one-sided Laplace notation and keep initial conditions explicit: $\mathcal{L}\{x(t)\}=X(s)$, $\mathcal{L}\{y(t)\}=Y(s)$, and $\mathcal{L}\{y'(t)\}=sY(s)-y(0^-)$, with the zero-initial-conditions rule setting $y(0^-)=0$ for this lesson. The same cue appears every time: map each term to the $s$ domain, then simplify.

$$
\mathcal{L}\{y'(t)\}=sY(s)-y(0^-)
$$

$$
\mathcal{L}\{y''(t)\}=s^2Y(s)-sy(0^-)-y'(0^-)
$$

Use the base case $y'(t)+3y(t)=x(t)$ and apply the map term by term: replace $y'(t)$ with $sY(s)-y(0^-)$, replace $y(t)$ with $Y(s)$, and replace $x(t)$ with $X(s)$. Under zero initial conditions this becomes $(s+3)Y(s)=X(s)$, which is already in transfer-function-ready form.

$$
\mathcal{L}\{y(t)\}=Y(s),\quad \mathcal{L}\{x(t)\}=X(s)
$$

Isolate the ratio using three moves: gather $Y(s)$ terms, divide by the full $X(s)$ side, and name the quotient $H(s)$. For the base case, $\frac{Y(s)}{X(s)}=\frac{1}{s+3}$. Every later section adds one controlled wrinkle, but the sequence stays the same.

## Applying Zero-Initial-Condition Laplace Derivative Rules

**Example:** Transform $y''(t)+4y'(t)+5y(t)=x(t)$ into the Laplace-domain algebraic equation using zero initial conditions.

**Explanation**

The cue is to keep the target move visible: map each time-domain derivative to an $s$-domain algebraic term, then remove initial-value terms using zero-initial-conditions. This keeps the same transfer-function workflow as the prior tutorial while adding one second-derivative wrinkle.

Start from the derivative identities used in this topic. For one-sided Laplace with $y(t)$, the transforms are

$$
\mathcal{L}\{y'(t)\}=sY(s)-y(0^-)\\\\n\mathcal{L}\{y''(t)\}=s^2Y(s)-sy(0^-)-y'(0^-)
$$

Apply the rules term-by-term to the differential equation: replace $y''(t)$ by $s^2Y(s)-sy(0^-)-y'(0^-)$, replace $y'(t)$ by $sY(s)-y(0^-)$, replace $y(t)$ by $Y(s)$, and replace $x(t)$ by $X(s)$.

$$
s^2Y(s)-sy(0^-)-y'(0^-)+4\,\left(sY(s)-y(0^- )\right)+5Y(s)=X(s)
$$

Set the zero-initial-condition terms to zero: $y(0^-)=0$ and $y'(0^-)=0$. This is the controlled novelty of this step, and it strips the equation to pure algebra in $s$:

$$
s^2Y(s)+4sY(s)+5Y(s)=X(s)
$$

Collect the coefficients of $Y(s)$ and keep the final relation exactly in $X(s),Y(s)$ form. Here the result is $s^2Y(s)+4sY(s)+5Y(s)=X(s)$, which is the clean baseline for later question variants with different derivative orders or coefficients.

## Transforming an Input-Output Differential Equation into an Algebraic Equation

**Example:** Transform $y'(t)+3y(t)=x''(t)-2x(t)$ into $X(s)$ and $Y(s)$ form, using zero initial conditions.

**Explanation**

The new cue in this step is a mixed-order equation: the input now has its own derivative, so the same rule set from the previous section must be applied on both sides. Convert each term to the Laplace domain and keep the same target algebraic form.

Apply the one-sided transform identities for each derivative term before collecting coefficients:

$$
\mathcal{L}\{y'(t)\}=sY(s)-y(0^-),\quad \mathcal{L}\{y(t)\}=Y(s),\quad \mathcal{L}\{x''(t)\}=s^2X(s)-sx(0^-)-x'(0^-),\quad \mathcal{L}\{x(t)\}=X(s)
$$

Substitute these rules into the differential equation: $(sY(s)-y(0^-))+3Y(s)=\left(s^2X(s)-sx(0^-)-x'(0^-)\right)-2X(s)$.

Use zero-initial-condition assumptions for this topic. Set $y(0^-)=0$, $x(0^-)=0$, and $x'(0^-)=0$. The transformed equation becomes:

$$
(s+3)Y(s)=(s^2-2)X(s)
$$

Final result: all $Y(s)$ terms are on the left and all $X(s)$ terms are on the right, exactly in the transfer-form algebraic template. This is the same pattern as earlier steps with one added wrinkle on the input side.

## Isolating $H(s)=Y(s)/X(s) from the Transformed Equation$

**Example:** From $(s+1)Y(s)+(2s+3)Y(s)=(4s+5)X(s)$, isolate $H(s)=\frac{Y(s)}{X(s)}$ as a single rational function.

**Explanation**

The cue for this step is a final algebraic cleanup: the transformed equation has multiple $Y(s)$ groups on one side, and the goal is to combine them into one coefficient before forming $H(s)=\frac{Y(s)}{X(s)}$.

First, combine like $Y(s)$ terms using the distributive rule on the left side.

$$
(s+1)Y(s)+(2s+3)Y(s)=\left((s+1)+(2s+3)\right)Y(s)=(3s+4)Y(s)
$$

Now divide both sides by $X(s)$ to form the transfer-function ratio directly.

$$
\frac{(3s+4)Y(s)}{X(s)}=4s+5 \;\Rightarrow\; \frac{Y(s)}{X(s)}=\frac{4s+5}{3s+4}
$$

Solve for $\frac{Y(s)}{X(s)}$ and express the transfer function in canonical rational form.

$$
H(s)=\frac{Y(s)}{X(s)}=\frac{4s+5}{3s+4}
$$

This isolated form is the reusable target: one numerator and one denominator in $s$, ready for pole/stability work in the next step.

## Writing $H(s)$ as a Rational Function for Pole and Stability Analysis

**Example:** Rewrite $\frac{5s+2}{(s+1)(s+3)}$ as a single rational form and state the characteristic denominator polynomial used for pole evaluation.

**Explanation**

The cue in this step is to represent the denominator in a form that directly exposes poles. The expression already has numerator and denominator pieces, but we now connect the factored denominator to equivalent expanded form so the pole locations can be read consistently.

Start from the rational function and expand the denominator only as a structural check.

$$
H(s)=\frac{5s+2}{(s+1)(s+3)}=\frac{5s+2}{s^2+4s+3}
$$

Next, identify and label the characteristic denominator polynomial for stability work: the denominator is $s^2+4s+3$, so the characteristic equation is $s^2+4s+3=0$.

$$
s^2+4s+3=(s+1)(s+3)
$$

Solve for the roots of the denominator polynomial to locate poles.

$$
s=-1\quad \text{and}\quad s=-3
$$

Both poles are in the left half-plane, so this transfer function is stable in the standard LTI sign convention used in this module. Preserve this result pattern because future questions reuse the same denominator-to-pole mapping in both expanded and factored forms.
