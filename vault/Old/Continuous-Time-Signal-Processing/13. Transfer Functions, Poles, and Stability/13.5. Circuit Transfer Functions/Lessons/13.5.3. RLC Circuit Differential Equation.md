## Introduction to RLC Circuit Differential Equation

For this lesson, treat the circuit as the target object: a **series** loop with source input $x(t)$, resistor $R$, inductor $L$, capacitor $C$, and output $y(t)=v_C(t)$. Use one loop current $i(t)$ for all three passive elements so each branch can be represented by the same current.

The branch equations are the action rules for this step. Write resistor voltage as $v_R(t)=R\,i(t)$, inductor voltage as $v_L(t)=L\,\frac{d i(t)}{dt}$, and capacitor voltage as $v_C(t)=y(t)$ with $i(t)=C\,\frac{d y(t)}{dt}$.

![](<../Source/13.5.3. RLC Circuit Differential Equation/Images/l003-s001-te-section-001.png>)

Now apply one-loop KVL in symbolic form: $x(t)=v_R(t)+v_L(t)+v_C(t)$. This is the direct substitution target that lets the student move from element-level relations to one input-output differential equation.

$$
x(t)=v_R(t)+v_L(t)+y(t)
$$

$$
v_R(t)=R\,i(t),\;v_L(t)=L\,\frac{d i(t)}{dt},\;i(t)=C\,\frac{d y(t)}{dt}
$$

Substitute these relations and replace $i(t)$ with $\frac{d y(t)}{dt}$ to reach the canonical form $L C\,\frac{d^2 y(t)}{dt^2}+R C\,\frac{d y(t)}{dt}+y(t)=x(t)$. In later steps, these coefficients $L C$, $R C$, and $1$ become the denominator structure that predicts pole locations in the transfer-function stage.

## Writing Kirchhoff's Law for a Series RLC Circuit

**Example:** Write the KVL equation for the shown series RLC circuit with input $x(t)$ and output $y(t)=v_C(t)$.

**Explanation**

Use the same loop direction used in the diagram: current $i(t)$ flows through $R$, $L$, and $C$ in series from the source toward the output node. The cue is that all three element voltages must sum algebraically to the source voltage in a single closed loop.

![](<../Source/13.5.3. RLC Circuit Differential Equation/Images/l003-s002-te-section-002.png>)

Apply Kirchhoff's voltage law to the one loop and write one raw sum equation before substitution:

$$
x(t)=v_R(t)+v_L(t)+v_C(t)
$$

Now label each term with its branch so sign conventions are fixed: $v_R(t)$ is the resistor drop, $v_L(t)$ is the inductor drop, and $v_C(t)$ is the capacitor drop across the output node. This is the canonical raw KVL identity to carry into the next section, where each term will be converted into $R$, $L$, and $C$ relations.

## Substituting Resistor, Inductor, and Capacitor Relations

**Example:** From the KVL identity in the previous step, substitute $v_R(t)$, $v_L(t)$, and $v_C(t)$ to write the differential expression in terms of $y(t)$ and its derivatives.

**Explanation**

The cue is the raw series loop identity from the previous section, so we keep that as the starting line and replace each branch voltage with its component relation without changing signs or topology.

$$
x(t)=v_R(t)+v_L(t)+v_C(t)
$$

Use the stable output choice $y(t)=v_C(t)$ from earlier sections, then apply the substitutions: $v_R(t)=R i(t)$, $v_L(t)=L\frac{d i(t)}{dt}$, and $i(t)=C\frac{d y(t)}{dt}$. Substitute $i(t)$ into both $v_R$ and $v_L$, and keep $v_C(t)=y(t)$.

$$
x(t)=R C\frac{d y(t)}{dt}+L C\frac{d^2 y(t)}{dt^2}+y(t)
$$

Now group terms so that the input is isolated on the left and the output derivatives stay on the right. This is the clean first-pass differential form that identifies the $L C$, $R C$, and leading-$1$ coefficients used in the next section.

## Eliminating Intermediate Variables to Relate Input and Output

**Example:** Simplify the substituted equation from Step 3 to remove any intermediate current variable and write the final input-output differential equation.

**Explanation**

The cue is that Step 3 left us with $x(t)=R C\frac{d y(t)}{dt}+L C\frac{d^2 y(t)}{dt^2}+y(t)$, so the goal now is pure algebra: eliminate no new physics, only intermediate form.

Choose the standard differential-order arrangement by collecting like terms in $y''(t)$, $y'(t)$, and $y(t)$. Reorder the same equation so the highest-order derivative appears first:

$$
L C\frac{d^2 y(t)}{dt^2}+R C\frac{d y(t)}{dt}+y(t)=x(t)
$$

Move everything to a single explicit ODE form and check for leftover current terms. No $i(t)$ remains, so the intermediate variable is eliminated and the input-output relation is ready for the next standardization step.

## Arranging the RLC Equation in Standard Second-Order Form

**Example:** Rewrite the derived ODE in standard form $y''(t)+a_1 y'(t)+a_0 y(t)=b_0 x(t)$ and compute $a_1$ and $a_0$.

**Explanation**

The cue is the unnormalized equation $L C\frac{d^2 y(t)}{dt^2}+R C\frac{d y(t)}{dt}+y(t)=x(t)$. The new move is a pure standardization pass: divide every term by $L C$ to match the canonical $y''+a_1 y'+a_0 y=b_0 x$ template.

$$
\frac{L C\frac{d^2 y(t)}{dt^2}+R C\frac{d y(t)}{dt}+y(t)}{L C}=\frac{x(t)}{L C}
$$

Collect the normalized coefficients term by term to identify the standard form constants: the second-derivative coefficient becomes 1, the first-derivative coefficient becomes $\frac{R}{L}$, and the zeroth-derivative coefficient becomes $\frac{1}{L C}$.

$$
y''(t)+\frac{R}{L}y'(t)+\frac{1}{L C}y(t)=\frac{1}{L C}x(t)
$$

Record the mapping for later handoff: $a_1=\frac{R}{L}$ and $a_0=\frac{1}{L C}$, with $b_0=\frac{1}{L C}$. This keeps coefficient interpretation aligned with later transfer-function denominator structure.

## Identifying Coefficients That Determine RLC Poles

**Example:** From $y''+\frac{R}{L}y'+\frac{1}{LC}y=\frac{1}{LC}x$, form the characteristic denominator and map coefficients to pole form.

**Explanation**

The cue is the normalized ODE, so to identify poles we now attach the standard homogeneous replacement $\frac{d}{dt}\to s$ to the zero-input part only and ignore the forcing coefficient for now.

$$
y''+\frac{R}{L}y'+\frac{1}{L C}y=\frac{1}{L C}x \quad\Rightarrow\quad s^2+\frac{R}{L}s+\frac{1}{L C}=0
$$

Convert back to the circuit-scaled denominator for transfer-function form by multiplying by $L C$: 

$$
D(s)=L C s^2+R C s+1.
$$

Keep the sign/placement rule explicit: positive $\frac{R}{L}$ and positive $\frac{1}{L C}$ keep both characteristic coefficients positive in passive circuits, which is the local stability trend signal expected in this topic. The mapping here is the carry-forward bridge to pole-interpretation work in the next section.
