## Introduction to Rectangular Form of a Phasor Sum

From the earlier phasor representation, keep the common $\omega$ fixed and rewrite each same-frequency phasor in rectangular form. A rectangular phasor has the form $a+jb$, where $a$ is the real part and $b$ is the imaginary part.

$$
X = A\cos\phi + jA\sin\phi
$$

So if $X_1 = a_1 + jb_1$ and $X_2 = a_2 + jb_2$, the phasor sum is formed componentwise: add the real parts together and add the imaginary parts together.

$$
X_1 + X_2 = (a_1 + a_2) + j(b_1 + b_2)
$$

The same bookkeeping works for three or more phasors. Collect every real part into one total and every imaginary part into another total, and keep the result rectangular for now.

$$
\sum_{k=1}^{n}(a_k + jb_k) = \left(\sum_{k=1}^{n} a_k\right) + j\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n} b_k\right)
$$

That is the rule the later examples will reuse: same $\omega$, rectangular components, and componentwise addition. The next step can turn this rectangular sum into polar form, but this section stops at the rectangular result.

## Adding Axis-Aligned Phasor Components

**Example:** Add the phasors $3 + j0$ and $0 + j5$ and write the result in rectangular form.

**Explanation**

These phasors are already axis-aligned, so the rectangular parts are visible immediately. Write them as $X_1 = 3 + j0$ and $X_2 = 0 + j5$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
X_1 &= 3 + j0 \\
X_2 &= 0 + j5
\end{aligned}
$$

Now add the real parts and the imaginary parts separately. The real-part total is $3 + 0$, and the imaginary-part total is $0 + 5$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
X_1 + X_2 &= (3 + 0) + j(0 + 5) \\
&= 3 + j5
\end{aligned}
$$

So the rectangular-form phasor sum is $3 + j5$.

## Resolving Two Cosine Phasors into Rectangular Components

**Example:** Find the rectangular-form phasor sum of $4\cos(\omega t + 60^\circ)$ and $6\cos(\omega t - 30^\circ)$.

**Explanation**

Because the two cosines share the same $\omega$, convert each one to a phasor first. Write $X_1 = 4\angle 60^\circ$ and $X_2 = 6\angle -30^\circ$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
X_1 &= 4\angle 60^\circ = 4\cos 60^\circ + j4\sin 60^\circ \\
X_2 &= 6\angle -30^\circ = 6\cos(-30^\circ) + j6\sin(-30^\circ)
\end{aligned}
$$

Now resolve the real and imaginary parts of each phasor. The first phasor becomes $X_1 = 2 + j2\sqrt{3}$, and the second becomes $X_2 = 3\sqrt{3} - j3$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
X_1 &= 4\cos 60^\circ + j4\sin 60^\circ = 2 + j2\sqrt{3} \\
X_2 &= 6\cos(-30^\circ) + j6\sin(-30^\circ) = 3\sqrt{3} - j3 \\
X_1 + X_2 &= (2 + 3\sqrt{3}) + j(2\sqrt{3} - 3)
\end{aligned}
$$

So the rectangular-form phasor sum is $(2 + 3\sqrt{3}) + j(2\sqrt{3} - 3)$.

## Adding Phasors with Negative Rectangular Components

**Example:** Add the phasors $(-4 + j3)$ and $(1 - j6)$ and write the result in rectangular form.

**Explanation**

These phasors are already in rectangular form, so preserve the signs and add the real parts and imaginary parts separately. Call them $X_1 = -4 + j3$ and $X_2 = 1 - j6$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
X_1 &= -4 + j3 \\
X_2 &= 1 - j6
\end{aligned}
$$

Now add the signed real parts and the signed imaginary parts. The real-part total is $-4 + 1 = -3$, and the imaginary-part total is $3 + (-6) = -3$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
X_1 + X_2 &= (-4 + 1) + j(3 - 6) \\
&= -3 - j3
\end{aligned}
$$

So the rectangular-form sum is $-3 - j3$.

## Adding More Than Two Same-Frequency Phasors in Rectangular Form

**Example:** Add $4 + j0$, $-1 + j3$, and $2 - j2$ and write the result in rectangular form.

**Explanation**

All three terms are already in rectangular form, so keep the signs and add the real parts and imaginary parts across the whole list. Label them $X_1 = 4 + j0$, $X_2 = -1 + j3$, and $X_3 = 2 - j2$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
X_1 &= 4 + j0 \\
X_2 &= -1 + j3 \\
X_3 &= 2 - j2
\end{aligned}
$$

Group the real parts as $4 + (-1) + 2$ and the imaginary parts as $0 + 3 + (-2)$. Then add each group separately.

$$
\begin{aligned}
X_1 + X_2 + X_3 &= (4 + (-1) + 2) + j(0 + 3 + (-2)) \\
&= 5 + j1
\end{aligned}
$$

So the rectangular-form sum is $5 + j1$.
