## Introduction to Damped Sinusoids

A damped sinusoid is a generalized exponential of the form $e^{(\sigma+j\omega)t}$ with $\sigma<0$. Read the real part $\sigma$ as the decay part and the imaginary part $\omega$ as the oscillation part.

$$
e^{(\sigma+j\omega)t}=e^{\sigma t}e^{j\omega t}
$$

The factor $e^{\sigma t}$ is the decaying envelope, and the factor $e^{j\omega t}$ is the oscillatory part. That split is the key move: one factor controls amplitude, and the other factor controls oscillation.

$$
\left|e^{(\sigma+j\omega)t}\right|=e^{\sigma t}
$$

Because $\sigma<0$, the envelope gets smaller as $t$ increases, so the amplitude decays at rate $-\sigma$. The more negative $\sigma$ is, the faster that decay happens.

$$
\sigma<0 \implies e^{\sigma t}\to 0 \text{ as } t\to\infty
$$

The oscillatory factor is still $e^{j\omega t}=\cos(\omega t)+j\sin(\omega t)$, so the angular frequency is $\omega$ rad/s. In short, $e^{(\sigma+j\omega)t}$ is a decaying oscillation whose amplitude falls like $e^{\sigma t}$.

$$
e^{j\omega t}=\cos(\omega t)+j\sin(\omega t)
$$

## Separating a Damped Complex Exponential into Envelope and Oscillation

**Example:** Work through $x(t)=e^{(-1.5+j4)t}$ by separating the envelope and oscillatory factor, then label the decay rate and angular frequency.

**Explanation**

The cue here is the complex exponent $s=-1.5+j4$, so split the signal into its real and imaginary parts. The real part will give the decaying envelope, and the imaginary part will give the oscillation.

$$
x(t)=e^{(-1.5+j4)t}=e^{-1.5t}e^{j4t}
$$

Now the first factor, $e^{-1.5t}$, is the envelope, and the second factor, $e^{j4t}$, is the oscillatory term. That is the only split you need for this step.

$$
\left|x(t)\right|=e^{-1.5t}
$$

From the envelope, read the decay rate as $1.5$ and note that the amplitude shrinks exponentially with $t$. From the oscillatory factor, read the angular frequency as $4$ rad/s.

$$
e^{j4t}=\cos(4t)+j\sin(4t)
$$

So $x(t)$ is a damped oscillation: it rotates at $4$ rad/s while its amplitude falls like $e^{-1.5t}$. The same split works whenever $s=\sigma+j\omega$ has a negative real part.

## Identifying the Decay Rate from Negative $\sigma$

**Example:** Work through $x(t)=e^{(-3+j2)t}$ by reading the decay rate from the negative real part and explaining how the envelope changes over time.

**Explanation**

The cue is the negative real part in $-3+j2$. Split the exponent so the decaying envelope and the oscillation are separate.

$$
x(t)=e^{(-3+j2)t}=e^{-3t}e^{j2t}
$$

Here $e^{-3t}$ is the envelope and $e^{j2t}$ is the oscillatory factor. The decay rate is the magnitude of the negative real part, so the amplitude decays at rate $3$ per unit time. The $j2$ term still gives angular frequency $2$ rad/s; it does not change the decay rate.

$$
|x(t)|=e^{-3t}
$$

Because $\sigma=-3$, the amplitude shrinks toward zero as $t$ increases. A more negative $\sigma$ would mean faster decay, while a less negative $\sigma$ would mean slower decay.

## Identifying the Angular Frequency from $\omega$

**Example:** Work through $x(t)=e^{(-1+j6)t}$ by isolating the oscillatory factor and reading the angular frequency from the imaginary part.

**Explanation**

The cue is the imaginary part in $-1+j6$. Keep the damping factor fixed and read the oscillation from the $j6$ term.

$$
x(t)=e^{(-1+j6)t}=e^{-t}e^{j6t}
$$

Here $e^{-t}$ is the envelope and $e^{j6t}$ is the oscillatory factor. The angular frequency is the coefficient of $t$ in the imaginary exponent, so $\omega=6$ rad/s.

$$
e^{j6t}=\cos(6t)+j\sin(6t)
$$

Do not read frequency from the real part. The decay rate is $1$ per unit time, but the angular frequency is $6$ rad/s, so the signal oscillates at $6$ rad/s while its amplitude decays.

## Interpreting Damped Sinusoid Behavior from $s=\sigma+j\omega$

**Example:** Work through $x(t)=e^{(-2+j4)t}$ by interpreting the full damped-sinusoid behavior directly from $s=\sigma+j\omega$.

**Explanation**

The cue is the full complex parameter $s=-2+j4$. Read the real and imaginary parts together: the real part controls decay, and the imaginary part controls oscillation.

$$
x(t)=e^{(-2+j4)t}=e^{-2t}e^{j4t}
$$

Here $\sigma=-2$ and $\omega=4$. From $e^{-2t}$, the envelope decays at rate $2$ per unit time. From $e^{j4t}$, the angular frequency is $4$ rad/s.

$$
|x(t)|=e^{-2t}
$$

So the signal is a damped sinusoid: it oscillates at $4$ rad/s while its amplitude shrinks toward zero. The real part sets damping, and the imaginary part sets frequency.
