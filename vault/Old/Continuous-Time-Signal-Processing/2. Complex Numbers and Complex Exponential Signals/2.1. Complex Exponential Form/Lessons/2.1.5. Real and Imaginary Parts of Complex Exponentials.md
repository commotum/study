## Introduction to Real and Imaginary Parts of Complex Exponentials

Start with the simplest fixed-angle complex exponential $e^{j\theta}$. Euler's formula rewrites it as a cosine term plus a sine term, so the real part is the term without $j$ and the imaginary part is the coefficient of $j$.

$$
e^{j\theta}=\cos\theta+j\sin\theta
$$

Read the parts directly from that form: the real part is $\cos\theta$, and the imaginary part is $\sin\theta$. In part notation, $\Re\{e^{j\theta}\}=\cos\theta$ and $\Im\{e^{j\theta}\}=\sin\theta$.

$$
\Re\{e^{j\theta}\}=\cos\theta,\quad \Im\{e^{j\theta}\}=\sin\theta
$$

This same read-off rule will be reused in the next examples; only the angle sign or the amplitude will change, while the Euler split into real and imaginary parts stays the same.

## Extracting the Real and Imaginary Parts of $e^{j\theta}$

**Example:** Find the real and imaginary parts of $e^{j\theta}$.

**Explanation**

For the canonical positive-angle exponential $e^{j\theta}$, apply Euler's formula and split the expression into its real term and its imaginary term. The cosine term is the real part, and the sine term is the imaginary part.

$$
e^{j\theta}=\cos\theta+j\sin\theta
$$

So the read-off is immediate: the term without $j$ is $\cos\theta$, and the coefficient of $j$ is $\sin\theta$. In part notation, $\Re\{e^{j\theta}\}=\cos\theta$ and $\Im\{e^{j\theta}\}=\sin\theta$.

$$
\Re\{e^{j\theta}\}=\cos\theta,\quad \Im\{e^{j\theta}\}=\sin\theta
$$

That is the whole move for this section: rewrite with Euler's formula, then identify the cosine term as the real part and the sine term as the imaginary part. The next examples keep the same read-off and change only the sign, the time variable, or the amplitude.

## Extracting the Real and Imaginary Parts of $e^{-j\theta}$

**Example:** Find the real and imaginary parts of $e^{-j\theta}$.

**Explanation**

For $e^{-j\theta}$, use the negative-angle form of Euler's formula. The only new cue is the minus sign in the exponent, so the cosine term stays in the real part while the sine term changes sign in the imaginary part.

$$
e^{-j\theta}=\cos\theta-j\sin\theta
$$

Read the parts directly from that form: the real part is still $\cos\theta$, and the imaginary part is now $-\sin\theta$. In part notation, $\Re\{e^{-j\theta}\}=\cos\theta$ and $\Im\{e^{-j\theta}\}=-\sin\theta$.

$$
\Re\{e^{-j\theta}\}=\cos\theta,\quad \Im\{e^{-j\theta}\}=-\sin\theta
$$

The cosine term did not change, and the sign flip only affected the sine term. That is the only new wrinkle here, and the next examples will keep the same read-off while changing the angle into $\omega t$ and then scaling by a real amplitude.

## Extracting Time-Varying Parts of $e^{j\omega t}$

**Example:** Find the real and imaginary parts of $e^{j\omega t}$ as functions of time.

**Explanation**

Use the same Euler split, but now the angle is time-varying: $\omega t$. The cue and the rule are unchanged, so the only thing that moves is the argument of the cosine and sine terms.

$$
e^{j\omega t}=\cos(\omega t)+j\sin(\omega t)
$$

Read the parts directly from that form: the real part is $\cos(\omega t)$, and the imaginary part is $\sin(\omega t)$. In part notation, $\Re\{e^{j\omega t}\}=\cos(\omega t)$ and $\Im\{e^{j\omega t}\}=\sin(\omega t)$.

$$
\Re\{e^{j\omega t}\}=\cos(\omega t),\quad \Im\{e^{j\omega t}\}=\sin(\omega t)
$$

The structure is the same as before: the cosine term is the real part, and the sine term is the imaginary part. The only new feature here is that both parts are now signals in $t$ instead of constants.

## Extracting Parts of $A e^{j\omega t}$ with Real $A$

**Example:** Find the real and imaginary parts of $A e^{j\omega t}$ when $A$ is real.

**Explanation**

Start with the real coefficient $A$ and apply Euler's formula to the exponential part. Because $A$ is real, it stays outside the split and multiplies both terms.

$$
A e^{j\omega t}=A\bigl(\cos(\omega t)+j\sin(\omega t)\bigr)=A\cos(\omega t)+jA\sin(\omega t)
$$

Now read the parts directly from that form. The real part is $A\cos(\omega t)$, and the imaginary part is $A\sin(\omega t)$. The coefficient scales both parts equally.

$$
\Re\{A e^{j\omega t}\}=A\cos(\omega t),\quad \Im\{A e^{j\omega t}\}=A\sin(\omega t)
$$

The new wrinkle is only the real amplitude factor. The Euler split stays the same; the real scalar just carries through both signal parts.
