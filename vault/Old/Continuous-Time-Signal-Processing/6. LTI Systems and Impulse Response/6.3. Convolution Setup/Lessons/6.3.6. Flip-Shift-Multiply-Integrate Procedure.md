## Introduction to Flip-Shift-Multiply-Integrate Procedure

The convolution output at time t is found by building one product on the $\tau$-axis and then integrating that product over $\tau$. In this setup, t is the output time you hold fixed, while $\tau$ is the variable that sweeps across the input timeline.

$$
y(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x(\tau)h(t-\tau)\,d\tau
$$

The flip-shift-multiply-integrate procedure is the concrete way to set up that integral. First flip the impulse response to form $h(-\tau)$. Next shift that flipped response by the fixed output time t to form $h(t-\tau)$. Then multiply it by $x(\tau)$. Finally, integrate the product over $\tau$.

![](<../Source/6.3.6. Flip-Shift-Multiply-Integrate Procedure/Images/l006-s001-te-section-001.png>)

The important point is that $h(t-\tau)$ is not drawn by moving $\tau$ itself. You choose one value of t, keep that value fixed, and view $h(t-\tau)$ as the flipped impulse response shifted along the $\tau$-axis.

$$
h(\tau)\rightarrow h(-\tau)\rightarrow h(t-\tau)\rightarrow x(\tau)h(t-\tau)\rightarrow \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x(\tau)h(t-\tau)\,d\tau
$$

This lesson uses the procedure to set up $y(t)$. Later work can focus on identifying overlap intervals and evaluating the integral, but the setup always follows the same order: flip, shift, multiply, integrate.

## Flipping the Impulse Response in $\tau$

**Example:** Given a plotted impulse response $h(\tau)$ with support from $\tau=1$ to $\tau=3$, sketch $h(-\tau)$.

**Explanation**

The cue is the negative sign inside $h(-\tau)$. That negative sign reflects the graph horizontally across $\tau=0$. The time locations change sign, but the heights of the graph stay the same.

![](<../Source/6.3.6. Flip-Shift-Multiply-Integrate Procedure/Images/l006-s002-te-section-002.png>)

Start by marking the original support endpoints and any shape landmarks. In this example, $h(\tau)$ is nonzero from $\tau=1$ to $\tau=3$, with a visible landmark at $\tau=2$.

$$
\tau=1\mapsto -1,\qquad \tau=2\mapsto -2,\qquad \tau=3\mapsto -3
$$

After the reflection, the support runs from $\tau=-3$ to $\tau=-1$. The part that was farther to the right moves farther to the left, and each amplitude is redrawn at its reflected $\tau$-location.

$$
\operatorname{support}(h(\tau))=[1,3]\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \operatorname{support}(h(-\tau))=[-3,-1]
$$

Label the reflected graph $h(-\tau)$. This is only the flip step of the convolution setup, so t has not been used yet and $h(t-\tau)$ has not been formed.

## Shifting the Flipped Impulse Response by Fixed $t$

**Example:** Starting from a plotted $h(-\tau)$, sketch $h(2-\tau)$.

**Explanation**

The cue is the fixed number in $h(2-\tau)$. Treat t as the fixed value 2, then move the already-flipped graph $h(-\tau)$ along the $\tau$-axis. Since $h(2-\tau)=h(-(\tau-2)$), the graph of $h(-\tau)$ shifts right by 2.

![](<../Source/6.3.6. Flip-Shift-Multiply-Integrate Procedure/Images/l006-s003-te-section-003.png>)

Start with the landmarks of the current $h(-\tau)$ graph. Here the support runs from $\tau=-3$ to $\tau=-1$, with a visible landmark at $\tau=-2$.

$$
h(2-\tau)=h\bigl(-(\tau-2)\bigr)
$$

Shift every $\tau$-location in the flipped graph by 2 units to the right. The landmarks move as follows, and the amplitudes do not change.

$$
-3\mapsto -1,\qquad -2\mapsto 0,\qquad -1\mapsto 1
$$

So the shifted support is $\tau=-1$ to $\tau=1$, with the same shape and the same heights as the starting $h(-\tau)$ graph.

$$
\operatorname{support}(h(-\tau))=[-3,-1]\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \operatorname{support}(h(2-\tau))=[-1,1]
$$

Label the result $h(2-\tau)$. This step only uses the fixed value $t=2$ to position the flipped response; $x(\tau)$, multiplication, and integration have not been introduced yet.

<a id="multiplying-xtau-by-ht-tau"></a>
## Multiplying $x(\tau)$ by $h(t-\tau)$

**Example:** For plotted rectangular $x(\tau)$ and plotted $h(2-\tau)$ on the same $\tau$-axis, sketch $x(\tau)h(2-\tau)$.

**Explanation**

The cue is that both factors are now drawn on the same $\tau$-axis. To form $x(\tau)$$h(2-\tau)$, keep only $\tau$-values where both signals are nonzero, then multiply the two heights point by point.

![](<../Source/6.3.6. Flip-Shift-Multiply-Integrate Procedure/Images/l006-s004-te-section-004.png>)

From the plots, $x(\tau)$ is nonzero on $0 \le \tau \le 2$. The shifted response $h(2-\tau)$ is nonzero on $-1 \le \tau \le 1$.

$$
[0,2]\cap[-1,1]=[0,1]
$$

So the product can only be nonzero on $0 \le \tau \le 1$. On that overlap, the plotted height of $x(\tau)$ is 2 and the plotted height of $h(2-\tau)$ is 1, so the product height is 2.

$$
2\cdot 1=2
$$

Outside the overlap, at least one factor is zero, so the product is zero. The product signal is therefore a rectangle of height 2 on $0 \le \tau \le 1$.

$$
x(\tau)h(2-\tau)=\begin{cases}2,&0\le \tau\le 1,\\0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}
$$

Label this product graph $x(\tau)$$h(2-\tau)$. This section forms the product only; the next step is where that product would be integrated over $\tau$.

<a id="integrating-the-product-over-tau"></a>
## Integrating the Product Over $\tau$

**Example:** For the plotted product $p(\tau)=x(\tau)h(2-\tau)$, compute $\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}p(\tau)\,d\tau$.

**Explanation**

The product signal has already been formed. At this stage, t is fixed at 2, and the remaining job is to integrate the product over $\tau$. For a plotted product, that means finding the signed area under $p(\tau)$.

![](<../Source/6.3.6. Flip-Shift-Multiply-Integrate Procedure/Images/l006-s005-te-section-005.png>)

From the plot, $p(\tau)$ is nonzero only on $0 \le \tau \le 1$. On that interval, the product height is 2.

$$
p(\tau)=\begin{cases}2,&0\le \tau\le 1,\\0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}
$$

Because $p(\tau)$ is zero outside that interval, the integral over all $\tau$ reduces to the area of one rectangle.

$$
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}p(\tau)\,d\tau=\int_0^1 2\,d\tau=2(1-0)=2
$$

So the product contributes 2 to the convolution output at this fixed value of t.

$$
y(2)=2
$$

<a id="setting-up-the-full-convolution-output"></a>
## Setting Up the Full Convolution Output

**Example:** Given $x(t)=1$ for $0\le t\le 2$ and $0$ otherwise, and $h(t)=e^{-t}$ for $t\ge 0$ and $0$ otherwise, write the convolution setup for $y(t)=(x*h)(t)$ as an integral over $\tau$ without evaluating it.

**Explanation**

The cue is that the prompt asks for the convolution setup, not the evaluated output. Use the flip-shift-multiply-integrate form by rewriting the input as $x(\tau)$, rewriting the impulse-response factor as $h(t-\tau)$, multiplying those two factors, and integrating over $\tau$.

$$
y(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x(\tau)h(t-\tau)\,d\tau
$$

First rewrite the input using $\tau$ as the variable. The original formula for $x(t)$ becomes the same rectangular rule on the $\tau$-axis.

$$
x(\tau)=\begin{cases}1,&0\le \tau\le 2,\\0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}
$$

Next form the shifted flipped impulse-response factor by substituting t-$\tau$ into h. Since $h(u)=e$^{-u} for u greater than or equal to 0, the factor $h(t-\tau)$ equals $e^{-(t-\tau)}$ when t-$\tau$ is nonnegative, and equals 0 otherwise.

$$
h(t-\tau)=\begin{cases}e^{-(t-\tau)},&t-\tau\ge 0,\\0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}
$$

Now multiply the two $\tau$-dependent factors and place the product inside the convolution integral. Keep the limits from negative infinity to infinity because this is only the setup step.

$$
y(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\begin{cases}1,&0\le \tau\le 2,\\0,&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}\begin{cases}e^{-(t-\tau)},&t-\tau\ge 0,\\0,&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}\,d\tau
$$

This is the full convolution setup. It identifies $x(\tau)$, forms $h(t-\tau)$, multiplies the factors, and integrates over $\tau$, but it does not analyze overlap intervals or evaluate the integral.
