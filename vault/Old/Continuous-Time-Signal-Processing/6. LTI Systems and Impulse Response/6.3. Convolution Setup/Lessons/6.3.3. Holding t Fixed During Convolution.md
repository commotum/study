## Introduction to Holding $t$ Fixed During Convolution

In the convolution integral, $t$ and $\tau$ do different jobs. The symbol $t$ names the output time, and $\tau$ is the integration variable that scans through input time.

$$
y(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x(\tau)h(t-\tau)\,d\tau
$$

To find one output value, choose one value of $t$ and hold it fixed. Then let $\tau$ run through the integral. During that one integral, $t$ is not another variable of integration; it is the chosen output time.

$$
y(t_0)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x(\tau)h(t_0-\tau)\,d\tau
$$

After fixing $t=t_0$, the input factor stays $x(\tau)$, and the impulse-response factor becomes $h(t_0-\tau)$. Both factors are now read as functions of $\tau$ for that chosen output time.

$$
\text{integrand at }t=t_0:\quad x(\tau)h(t_0-\tau)
$$

The result is one $\tau$-dependent integrand. Integrating it over $\tau$ produces the single output value $y(t_0)$. Choosing another output time means holding a new value of $t$ fixed and forming a new integrand.

## Substituting a Fixed Output Time into $h(t-\tau)$

**Example:** For $h(t)=e^{-t}u(t)$, rewrite $h(t-\tau)$ when the output time is fixed at $t=3$.

**Explanation**

The cue is the fixed output time $t=3$. In $h(t-\tau)$, substitute $3$ only for $t$ and leave $\tau$ as the variable.

$$
t-\tau \quad \longrightarrow \quad 3-\tau
$$

So the shifted impulse-response factor becomes $h(3-\tau)$. Now use the formula for $h$: whatever argument is inside $h(\cdot)$ replaces the argument in $e^{-(\cdot)}u(\cdot)$.

$$
h(3-\tau)=e^{-(3-\tau)}u(3-\tau)
$$

Thus, for the fixed output time $t=3$, $h(t-\tau)$ is rewritten as $e^{-(3-\tau)}u(3-\tau)$. The expression still depends on $\tau$, so it is ready to be used as a $\tau$-varying factor in the convolution setup.

<a id="forming-xtauht-tau-with-t-fixed"></a>
## Forming $x(\tau)h(t-\tau)$ with $t$ Fixed

**Example:** Given $x(\tau)=\tau u(\tau)$ and $h(t)=e^{-t}u(t)$, form $x(\tau)h(t-\tau)$ for fixed output time $t=2$.

**Explanation**

The cue is the fixed output time $t=2$. First rewrite the impulse-response factor by substituting $2$ only for $t$ in $h(t-\tau)$.

$$
h(t-\tau)\big|_{t=2}=h(2-\tau)
$$

Now use the formula $h(t)=e^{-t}u(t)$. The argument of $h$ is $2-\tau$, so replace the argument in the exponential and in the unit step with $2-\tau$.

$$
h(2-\tau)=e^{-(2-\tau)}u(2-\tau)
$$

The input factor is already written as a function of $\tau$, so it stays $x(\tau)=\tau u(\tau)$. Form the integrand by multiplying this factor by the rewritten impulse-response factor.

$$
x(\tau)h(2-\tau)=\tau u(\tau)e^{-(2-\tau)}u(2-\tau)
$$

The final integrand contains $\tau$ but no free $t$. That means $t$ has been fixed at $2$, while $\tau$ remains the variable for the convolution setup.

<a id="comparing-the-integrand-for-different-fixed-values-of-t"></a>
## Comparing the Integrand for Different Fixed Values of $t$

**Example:** For $x(\tau)=u(\tau)$ and $h(t)=e^{-t}u(t)$, form and compare $x(\tau)h(t-\tau)$ for fixed output times $t=1$ and $t=4$.

**Explanation**

The cue is that two different output times are being checked. Form one $\tau$-dependent integrand for $t=1$, then form a separate $\tau$-dependent integrand for $t=4$.

For $t=1$, substitute $1$ into the impulse-response factor and keep the input factor as $x(\tau)=u(\tau)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
h(1-\tau)&=e^{-(1-\tau)}u(1-\tau)\\
x(\tau)h(1-\tau)&=e^{-(1-\tau)}u(\tau)u(1-\tau)
\end{aligned}
$$

For $t=4$, repeat the same process with $4$ as the fixed output time. The input factor $u(\tau)$ does not change.

$$
\begin{aligned}
h(4-\tau)&=e^{-(4-\tau)}u(4-\tau)\\
x(\tau)h(4-\tau)&=e^{-(4-\tau)}u(\tau)u(4-\tau)
\end{aligned}
$$

The only change is the fixed output-time value inside the impulse-response factor: $1-\tau$ becomes $4-\tau$. In both integrands, $\tau$ is still the only variable left, and the input factor $u(\tau)$ stays the same.

<a id="recognizing-tau-as-the-only-variable-left-to-integrate"></a>
## Recognizing $\tau$ as the Only Variable Left to Integrate

**Example:** Decide whether $x(\tau)h(3-\tau)=\tau e^{-(3-\tau)}u(\tau)u(3-\tau)$ is ready to integrate over $\tau$, and explain what variable remains.

**Explanation**

The cue is that this is a readiness check, not an evaluation problem. Inspect the expression for any remaining free $t$.

$$
x(\tau)h(3-\tau)=\tau e^{-(3-\tau)}u(\tau)u(3-\tau)
$$

There is no free $t$ left in the expression. The appearance of $3-\tau$ shows that the output time has already been fixed at $t=3$.

$$
\text{fixed output time: }t=3\qquad \text{remaining variable: }\tau
$$

Every nonconstant part of the integrand depends on $\tau$: $\tau$, $u(\tau)$, $3-\tau$, and $u(3-\tau)$. So $\tau$ is the only variable left to scan during integration.

$$
y(3)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\tau e^{-(3-\tau)}u(\tau)u(3-\tau)\,d\tau
$$

Therefore, the expression is ready to integrate over $\tau$ to compute the one output value $y(3)$. The setup stops here; evaluating the integral is a separate step.
