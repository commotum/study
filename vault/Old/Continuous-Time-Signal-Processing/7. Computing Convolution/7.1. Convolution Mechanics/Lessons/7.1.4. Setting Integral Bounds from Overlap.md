## Introduction to Setting Integral Bounds from Overlap

At this point in the convolution setup, the supports of $x(\tau)$ and $h(t-\tau)$ have already been intersected. The result of that intersection is the overlap interval: the set of $\tau$ values where both factors can be nonzero.

$$
y(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x(\tau)h(t-\tau)\,d\tau
$$

The integral only accumulates product values where $x(\tau)h(t-\tau)$ can be nonzero. So once the overlap interval is known, the integral should be restricted to that interval.

The rule is direct: the left endpoint of the overlap interval becomes the lower limit of integration, and the right endpoint becomes the upper limit of integration.

$$
\text{overlap: } a \le \tau \le b \quad\Longrightarrow\quad y(t)=\int_a^b x(\tau)h(t-\tau)\,d\tau
$$

This lesson is only about setting up the bounds. After the overlap has been translated into lower and upper $\tau$ limits, evaluating the integral is a separate move.

<a id="setting-bounds-from-a-constant-overlap-interval"></a>
## Setting Bounds from a Constant Overlap Interval

**Example:** Given that the overlap interval is $1 \le \tau \le 3$, set the bounds for the convolution integral $y(t)=\int x(\tau)h(t-\tau)\,d\tau$ on that time region.

**Explanation**

The cue is the given overlap interval $1 \le \tau \le 3$. This already tells us where the product $x(\tau)h(t-\tau)$ can be nonzero, so the task is only to turn those endpoints into integration bounds.

$$
1 \le \tau \le 3
$$

The left endpoint is $1$, so $1$ becomes the lower limit. The right endpoint is $3$, so $3$ becomes the upper limit.

The integrand stays the same because this step only changes the limits of integration. We do not evaluate the integral here.

$$
y(t)=\int_1^3 x(\tau)h(t-\tau)\,d\tau
$$

So on this time region, the correctly bounded convolution setup is $y(t)=\int_1^3 x(\tau)h(t-\tau)\,d\tau$.

## Setting Bounds with a $t$-Dependent Lower Limit

**Example:** On a time region where the overlap interval is $t-2 \le \tau \le 1$, set the bounds for the convolution integral $y(t)=\int x(\tau)h(t-\tau)\,d\tau$.

**Explanation**

The cue is the overlap interval $t-2 \le \tau \le 1$. Read it from left to right in the $\tau$ variable: the expression on the left is the lower endpoint, and the expression on the right is the upper endpoint.

$$
t-2 \le \tau \le 1
$$

Even though the lower endpoint contains $t$, it still becomes the lower limit. During the convolution integral, $t$ is held fixed while integration happens with respect to $\tau$.

So $t-2$ goes below the integral sign, and the constant endpoint $1$ goes above the integral sign. The integrand stays $x(\tau)h(t-\tau)$.

$$
y(t)=\int_{t-2}^{1}x(\tau)h(t-\tau)\,d\tau
$$

This is the completed bound setup for this time region. The step stops after placing the overlap endpoints as limits; it does not evaluate the integral.

## Setting Bounds with a $t$-Dependent Upper Limit

**Example:** On a time region where the overlap interval is $0 \le \tau \le t+1$, set the bounds for the convolution integral $y(t)=\int x(\tau)h(t-\tau)\,d\tau$.

**Explanation**

The cue is the overlap interval $0 \le \tau \le t+1$. Read the interval in the order of the $\tau$ inequality: the left endpoint becomes the lower limit, and the right endpoint becomes the upper limit.

$$
0 \le \tau \le t+1
$$

Here the lower endpoint is the constant $0$. The upper endpoint is $t+1$, and it stays in that upper-limit position even though it contains $t$.

During the convolution integral, integration is with respect to $\tau$, so $t$ is held fixed. Place $0$ below the integral sign, place $t+1$ above it, and leave the integrand unchanged.

$$
y(t)=\int_0^{t+1}x(\tau)h(t-\tau)\,d\tau
$$

This is the completed bound setup for this time region. The example stops at the correctly bounded convolution integral, before any evaluation.

## Setting Bounds When Both Limits Depend on $t$

**Example:** On a time region where the overlap interval is $t-3 \le \tau \le t+1$, set the bounds for the convolution integral $y(t)=\int x(\tau)h(t-\tau)\,d\tau$.

**Explanation**

The cue is the overlap interval $t-3 \le \tau \le t+1$. Both endpoints contain $t$, but the endpoint rule is unchanged: read the interval in increasing $\tau$ order.

$$
t-3 \le \tau \le t+1
$$

The expression $t-3$ is the lower endpoint because it appears on the left side of the $\tau$ inequality. The expression $t+1$ is the upper endpoint because it appears on the right side.

Since the integral is with respect to $\tau$, $t$ is held fixed while integrating. Keep both endpoint expressions in their positions, place them as the limits, and leave the integrand unchanged.

$$
y(t)=\int_{t-3}^{t+1}x(\tau)h(t-\tau)\,d\tau
$$

This is the correctly bounded convolution setup for this time region. Do not simplify the limits or evaluate the integral in this step.

## Writing Region-Specific Integrals from Overlap Bounds

**Example:** Given the overlap intervals $0<t<1: 0\le \tau \le t$ and $1\le t<3: t-1\le \tau \le 1$, write the region-specific convolution integrals for $y(t)=\int x(\tau)h(t-\tau)\,d\tau$.

**Explanation**

The cue is that the overlap information is already split by time region. Treat each time region as a separate case, and apply the same endpoint-to-bounds rule inside each case.

$$
\begin{aligned}
0<t<1 &: 0\le \tau \le t \\
1\le t<3 &: t-1\le \tau \le 1
\end{aligned}
$$

For the first region, $0<t<1$, the overlap interval is $0\le \tau \le t$. The lower endpoint is $0$, and the upper endpoint is $t$.

For the second region, $1\le t<3$, the overlap interval is $t-1\le \tau \le 1$. The lower endpoint is $t-1$, and the upper endpoint is $1$.

Now write one convolution integral for each time region. Keep the integrand the same in both cases and change only the limits.

$$
\begin{aligned}
0<t<1 &: \quad y(t)=\int_0^t x(\tau)h(t-\tau)\,d\tau \\
1\le t<3 &: \quad y(t)=\int_{t-1}^{1}x(\tau)h(t-\tau)\,d\tau
\end{aligned}
$$

This is the region-specific setup. Do not merge the two regions or evaluate either integral in this step.
