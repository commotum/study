## Introduction to Recognizing No-Overlap Regions

In a convolution, the integral only accumulates values of $\tau$ where both factors are nonzero. If the support of $x(\tau)$ and the support of $h(t-\tau)$ do not overlap, then the product $x(\tau)h(t-\tau)$ is zero for every $\tau$, so the convolution output is zero for that value of $t$.

$$
(x*h)(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x(\tau)h(t-\tau)\,d\tau
$$

The two intervals to compare are the fixed support of $x(\tau)$ and the moving support of $h(t-\tau)$. The fixed support does not contain $t$. The moving support has endpoints that depend on $t$, so different values of $t$ can move it before, across, or after the fixed interval.

$$
\operatorname{supp}x=[a,b],\qquad \operatorname{supp}h(t-\tau)=[L(t),R(t)]
$$

There are two no-overlap cases. Before contact, the moving interval is entirely to the left of the fixed interval. After contact, it is entirely to the right of the fixed interval. Each case is decided by comparing one moving endpoint with one fixed endpoint.

$$
\text{no overlap when }R(t)<a\quad\text{or}\quad L(t)>b
$$

For a base case, suppose $x(\tau)$ is nonzero on $0\le \tau\le 2$ and $h(t-\tau)$ is nonzero on $t-1\le \tau\le t$. Before contact, the moving right endpoint $t$ is still less than $0$, so $t<0$. After contact, the moving left endpoint $t-1$ is greater than $2$, so $t>3$.

$$
[0,2]\cap[t-1,t]=\emptyset\quad\text{when}\quad t<0\quad\text{or}\quad t>3
$$

On those $t$-regions, there is no interval over which both factors are nonzero. Therefore the integrand is identically zero, and those regions become zero pieces in the final convolution output.

$$
(x*h)(t)=0\quad\text{for}\quad t<0\quad\text{or}\quad t>3
$$

## Recognizing No Overlap Before the Moving Support Reaches $x(\tau)$

**Example:** Suppose $x(\tau)$ is nonzero only on $1\le \tau\le 4$ and $h(t-\tau)$ is nonzero only on $t-2\le \tau\le t$. For what values of $t$ is there no overlap before the moving support reaches $x(\tau)$?

**Explanation**

The cue is the phrase "before the moving support reaches $x(\tau)$." That means the moving interval for $h(t-\tau)$ is entirely to the left of the fixed interval for $x(\tau)$. For this one-sided check, compare the right endpoint of the moving support with the left endpoint of the fixed support.

$$
\operatorname{supp}x=[1,4],\qquad \operatorname{supp}h(t-\tau)=[t-2,t]
$$

The fixed support begins at $\tau=1$. The moving support ends at $\tau=t$. Before contact, that moving right endpoint must still be less than $1$.

$$
t<1
$$

So for every $t<1$, the interval $[t-2,t]$ lies completely to the left of $[1,4]$. There is no interval of $\tau$ where both factors are nonzero, so the convolution output is zero on that before-contact region.

$$
(x*h)(t)=0\quad\text{for}\quad t<1
$$

## Recognizing No Overlap After the Moving Support Passes $x(\tau)$

**Example:** Suppose $x(\tau)$ is nonzero only on $1\le \tau\le 4$ and $h(t-\tau)$ is nonzero only on $t-2\le \tau\le t$. For what values of $t$ is there no overlap after the moving support passes $x(\tau)$?

**Explanation**

The cue is the phrase "after the moving support passes $x(\tau)$." That means the moving interval for $h(t-\tau)$ is entirely to the right of the fixed interval for $x(\tau)$. This is the opposite side from before contact, so the endpoint comparison also changes.

$$
\operatorname{supp}x=[1,4],\qquad \operatorname{supp}h(t-\tau)=[t-2,t]
$$

The fixed support ends at $\tau=4$. The moving support begins at $\tau=t-2$. After contact, that moving left endpoint must be greater than $4$.

$$
t-2>4
$$

Solving the endpoint inequality gives the after-contact $t$-region.

$$
t>6
$$

So for every $t>6$, the interval $[t-2,t]$ lies completely to the right of $[1,4]$. There is no interval of $\tau$ where both factors are nonzero, so the convolution output is zero on that after-contact region.

$$
(x*h)(t)=0\quad\text{for}\quad t>6
$$

## Finding All Zero-Output Regions from Support Endpoints

**Example:** Suppose $x(\tau)$ is nonzero only on $-1\le \tau\le 3$ and $h(t-\tau)$ is nonzero only on $t-2\le \tau\le t+1$. Find all $t$-regions where the convolution output is zero because the supports do not overlap.

**Explanation**

The cue is the word "all." We need both outside no-overlap regions: the before-contact region and the after-contact region. First record the fixed support and the moving support so each endpoint comparison is explicit.

$$
\operatorname{supp}x=[-1,3],\qquad \operatorname{supp}h(t-\tau)=[t-2,t+1]
$$

Before contact, the moving support is entirely to the left of the fixed support. Compare the moving right endpoint $t+1$ with the fixed left endpoint $-1$.

$$
t+1<-1\quad\Longrightarrow\quad t<-2
$$

After contact, the moving support is entirely to the right of the fixed support. Compare the moving left endpoint $t-2$ with the fixed right endpoint $3$.

$$
t-2>3\quad\Longrightarrow\quad t>5
$$

These two outside regions are the complete set of no-overlap regions. On either one, there is no interval of $\tau$ where both factors are nonzero, so the convolution output is zero.

$$
(x*h)(t)=0\quad\text{for}\quad t<-2\quad\text{or}\quad t>5
$$

<a id="adding-zero-output-regions-to-a-piecewise-convolution"></a>
## Adding Zero-Output Regions to a Piecewise Convolution

**Example:** A convolution has nonzero overlap only for $-2<t<5$, with evaluated pieces already planned for $-2<t<1$ and $1\le t<5$. Write the zero-output pieces that must be added outside the overlap region.

**Explanation**

The cue is that the nonzero-overlap interval is already given. Since the convolution has nonzero overlap only for $-2<t<5$, the zero-output regions are the two outside regions before and after that interval.

$$
\text{nonzero overlap only on }-2<t<5
$$

The region before the overlap starts is $t\le -2$. The region after the overlap ends is $t\ge 5$. On both regions, the supports do not overlap, so the convolution output is $0$.

$$
(x*h)(t)=0\quad\text{for}\quad t\le -2\quad\text{or}\quad t\ge 5
$$

The interior pieces should be preserved exactly as already planned. If those evaluated pieces are called $y_1(t)$ on $-2<t<1$ and $y_2(t)$ on $1\le t<5$, the zero-output regions frame them as the outer cases.

$$
(x*h)(t)=\begin{cases}0,&t\le -2\\y_1(t),&-2<t<1\\y_2(t),&1\le t<5\\0,&t\ge 5\end{cases}
$$

No interior formula is recomputed or moved. The only added information is that the convolution is zero outside the full nonzero-overlap interval.
