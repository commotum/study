## Introduction to Convolution of a Unit Step with a Decaying Exponential

This lesson computes a standard causal convolution: a unit step convolved with a decaying exponential. Use the base case $x(t)=u(t)$ and $h(t)=e^{-at}u(t)$, where $a>0$.

Start from the convolution integral. In the integral, $x(\tau)$ becomes $u(\tau)$, and $h(t-\tau)$ becomes $e^{-a(t-\tau)}u(t-\tau)$.

$$
y(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}u(\tau)e^{-a(t-\tau)}u(t-\tau)\,d\tau
$$

The two unit steps decide where the integrand is nonzero. The factor $u(\tau)$ requires $\tau \ge 0$. The factor $u(t-\tau)$ requires $t-\tau \ge 0$, which means $\tau \le t$.

$$
\tau \ge 0 \quad \text{and} \quad \tau \le t, \quad \text{so} \quad 0 \le \tau \le t \quad \text{when } t\ge 0
$$

If $t<0$, those two inequalities do not overlap, so the convolution output is zero. If $t\ge 0$, the convolution reduces to one exponential integral over the causal overlap interval.

$$
t\ge 0:\quad y(t)=\int_0^t e^{-a(t-\tau)}\,d\tau=\frac{1-e^{-at}}{a}
$$

The final unit step records the zero-output region for $t<0$. That gives the reusable result for this lesson.

$$
y(t)=\frac{1-e^{-at}}{a}u(t)
$$

## Finding the Causal Overlap Interval

**Example:** For $x(t)=u(t)$ and $h(t)=e$^{-2t}$u(t)$, find the values of $\tau$ where $x(\tau)$$h(t-\tau)$ is nonzero.

**Explanation**

The cue is the pair of unit-step factors in the convolution product. Rewrite the two signals in the convolution variable first.

$$
x(\tau)=u(\tau),\qquad h(t-\tau)=e^{-2(t-\tau)}u(t-\tau)
$$

The exponential factor changes the value of the product, but it does not decide where the product is zero. The nonzero interval comes from the two unit steps.

$$
x(\tau)h(t-\tau)=u(\tau)e^{-2(t-\tau)}u(t-\tau)
$$

The factor $u(\tau)$ is nonzero only when $\tau \ge 0$. The factor $u(t-\tau)$ is nonzero only when $t-\tau \ge 0$, which means $\tau \le t$.

$$
u(\tau):\ \tau\ge 0,\qquad u(t-\tau):\ t-\tau\ge 0 \Longleftrightarrow \tau\le t
$$

Now intersect the two conditions. There are $\tau$ values satisfying both inequalities only when $t \ge 0$.

$$
\begin{cases}0\le \tau\le t, & t\ge 0,\\ \text{no overlap}, & t<0.\end{cases}
$$

So $x(\tau)$$h(t-\tau)$ is nonzero on the causal overlap interval $0 \le \tau \le t$ when $t \ge 0$, and there is no nonzero product when $t<0$.

## Setting Up the Step-Exponential Convolution Integral

**Example:** Set up, but do not evaluate, $y(t)=u(t)*e$^{-2t}$u(t)$ as a convolution integral using the causal overlap interval.

**Explanation**

The cue is the product of two causal factors inside the convolution. Start with the full convolution integral, then use the causal overlap interval to replace the infinite bounds.

$$
y(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}u(\tau)e^{-2(t-\tau)}u(t-\tau)\,d\tau
$$

The factor $u(\tau)$ requires $\tau \ge 0$, and the factor $u(t - \tau)$ requires $\tau \le t$. Together, they leave the interval $0 \le \tau \le t$ when $t \ge 0$.

$$
\tau\ge 0 \quad \text{and}\quad \tau\le t \quad \Longrightarrow \quad 0\le \tau\le t \quad (t\ge 0)
$$

On that interval, the unit-step factors only act as gates, so the integrand left to accumulate is $e^{-2(t - \tau)}$. Replace the full bounds with the overlap bounds and stop before evaluating.

$$
t\ge 0:\quad y(t)=\int_0^t e^{-2(t-\tau)}\,d\tau
$$

If $t < 0$, the two causal factors have no shared $\tau$-values where they are both nonzero, so the convolution output is zero in that case.

$$
y(t)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_0^t e^{-2(t-\tau)}\,d\tau, & t\ge 0,\\ 0, & t<0.\end{cases}
$$

## Evaluating the Decaying-Exponential Integral

**Example:** Evaluate int_0^t $e^{-2(t-\tau)}$ d $\tau$ for $t \ge 0$.

**Explanation**

The cue is the bounded integral from the previous section. Here t is fixed, $\tau$ is the integration variable, and the job is to evaluate the positive-time branch.

$$
I(t)=\int_0^t e^{-2(t-\tau)}\,d\tau,\qquad t\ge 0
$$

Factor: separate the part that depends only on t from the part that depends on $\tau$. Since $e^{-2t}$ is constant with respect to $\tau$, it can come outside the integral.

$$
e^{-2(t-\tau)}=e^{-2t+2\tau}=e^{-2t}e^{2\tau}
$$

$$
I(t)=e^{-2t}\int_0^t e^{2\tau}\,d\tau
$$

Integrate: use the exponential antiderivative with $\tau$ as the variable.

$$
\int e^{2\tau}\,d\tau=\frac{1}{2}e^{2\tau}
$$

Substitute: apply the bounds from 0 to t, then keep the outside factor $e^{-2t}$.

$$
I(t)=e^{-2t}\left[\frac{1}{2}e^{2\tau}\right]_0^t=e^{-2t}\left(\frac{e^{2t}-1}{2}\right)
$$

Simplify: distribute $e^{-2t}$ through the numerator. This gives the evaluated convolution integral on the overlap region.

$$
I(t)=\frac{1-e^{-2t}}{2},\qquad t\ge 0
$$

## Writing the Step-Gated Convolution Result

**Example:** Write the full convolution result for $y(t)=u(t)*e$^{-2t}$u(t)$, including the zero-output region.

**Explanation**

The cue is that the integral has already been evaluated only on the overlap region. First keep that positive-time branch exactly as computed.

$$
t\ge 0:\quad y(t)=\frac{1-e^{-2t}}{2}
$$

For $t<0$, the two causal factors have no overlap, so the convolution output is zero.

$$
t<0:\quad y(t)=0
$$

Put those two time regions into one piecewise result before compressing the notation.

$$
y(t)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{1-e^{-2t}}{2}, & t\ge 0,\\ 0, & t<0.\end{cases}
$$

The unit step $u(t)$ is the compact way to encode this same zero-output region. Multiplying the positive-time expression by $u(t)$ turns it off for $t<0$ and leaves it active for $t\ge0$.

$$
y(t)=\frac{1-e^{-2t}}{2}u(t)
$$
