## Introduction to Convolution of Two One-Sided Exponentials

When two one-sided exponentials are convolved, the first move is to set up the integral correctly. In the convolution integral, t is the output time, and $\tau$ is the dummy variable where the two signals are compared.

$$
(x*h)(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x(\tau)h(t-\tau)\,d\tau
$$

Use the base case where both signals are causal exponentials. The constants a and b are positive, so the unit steps are what determine where each signal is turned on.

$$
x(t)=e^{-at}u(t),\qquad h(t)=e^{-bt}u(t),\qquad a>0,\ b>0
$$

Inside the convolution integral, rewrite the first signal as $x(\tau)$ and the second signal as $h(t - \tau)$. The unit-step arguments change in exactly the same way as the exponential arguments.

$$
x(\tau)=e^{-a\tau}u(\tau),\qquad h(t-\tau)=e^{-b(t-\tau)}u(t-\tau)
$$

Now use the unit steps to find the overlap. The factor $u(\tau)$ requires $\tau$ to be at least 0. The factor $u(t - \tau)$ requires t - $\tau$ to be at least 0, which means $\tau$ is at most t.

$$
\begin{aligned}\nu(\tau)&\Rightarrow \tau \ge 0,\\\nu(t-\tau)&\Rightarrow t-\tau \ge 0 \Rightarrow \tau \le t
\end{aligned}
$$

Both one-sided factors are nonzero only on the interval from 0 to t. That interval exists when t is nonnegative; when t is negative, there is no causal overlap.

$$
0\le \tau \le t\qquad \text{when }t\ge 0
$$

On that overlap interval, multiply the exponential parts to form the integrand. The unit steps have already done their job by setting the bounds.

$$
e^{-a\tau}e^{-b(t-\tau)}
$$

So the setup for the convolution is the causal integral below. The factor $u(t)$ records that the output is zero before the two one-sided signals overlap. Evaluating this integral is a later step.

$$
(x*h)(t)=u(t)\int_0^t e^{-a\tau}e^{-b(t-\tau)}\,d\tau
$$

## Rewriting One-Sided Exponentials in the Convolution Variable

**Example:** Rewrite $x(t)=e$^{-2t}$u(t)$ and $h(t)=e$^{-3t}$u(t)$ as $x(\tau)$ and $h(t-\tau)$ inside the convolution integral.

**Explanation**

The cue is the convolution product $x(\tau)$$h(t - \tau)$. Before finding bounds or multiplying exponentials, rewrite each original signal with the argument it has inside the integral.

$$
(x*h)(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x(\tau)h(t-\tau)\,d\tau
$$

Start with the original one-sided exponentials.

$$
x(t)=e^{-2t}u(t),\qquad h(t)=e^{-3t}u(t)
$$

For the first factor, replace t by $\tau$ everywhere in $x(t)$. The exponential argument and the unit-step argument both change.

$$
x(\tau)=e^{-2\tau}u(\tau)
$$

For the second factor, replace t by t - $\tau$ everywhere in $h(t)$. Again, carry that same substitution into both the exponential and the unit step.

$$
h(t-\tau)=e^{-3(t-\tau)}u(t-\tau)
$$

So the two factors that enter the convolution setup are $x(\tau)=e$^{-2$\tau$}$u(\tau)$ and $h(t - \tau)=e$^{-3(t - $\tau$)}$u(t - \tau)$. The next step is to use the unit-step factors to find the overlap bounds.

## Finding the Causal Overlap Bounds

**Example:** For $x(\tau)=e$^{-2$\tau$}$u(\tau)$ and $h(t-\tau)=e$^{-3(t-$\tau$)}$u(t-\tau)$, find the $\tau$-overlap bounds.

**Explanation**

The cue is the pair of unit-step factors. The exponential factors affect the value of the integrand, but the unit steps decide where the product can be nonzero.

$$
x(\tau)=e^{-2\tau}u(\tau),\qquad h(t-\tau)=e^{-3(t-\tau)}u(t-\tau)
$$

First read the support condition from $u(\tau)$. This factor is on when $\tau$ is at least 0.

$$
u(\tau)\Rightarrow \tau\ge 0
$$

Next read the support condition from $u(t - \tau)$. This factor is on when its argument is at least 0.

$$
u(t-\tau)\Rightarrow t-\tau\ge 0
$$

Solve that second inequality for $\tau$. Moving $\tau$ to the other side gives the upper bound.

$$
t-\tau\ge 0\Rightarrow \tau\le t
$$

Now intersect the two support conditions. Both factors can be nonzero only when $\tau$ is at least 0 and at most t.

$$
0\le \tau\le t
$$

This interval exists when t is nonnegative. If t is negative, the upper bound t is less than 0, so the two one-sided supports do not overlap.

$$
\text{overlap bounds: }0\le \tau\le t\text{ for }t\ge 0,\qquad \text{no overlap for }t<0
$$

```quiz
  0\le \tau\le t\quad \text{for }t\ge 0

```

## Multiplying the Shifted Exponential Terms

**Example:** For $x(\tau)=e$^{-2$\tau$}$u(\tau)$ and $h(t-\tau)=e$^{-3(t-$\tau$)}$u(t-\tau)$, multiply the exponential terms inside the overlap interval.

**Explanation**

The cue is that the unit-step factors have already set the overlap interval. On that interval, the algebraic work is to multiply only the exponential parts and simplify the product.

$$
e^{-2\tau}e^{-3(t-\tau)}
$$

Start with the shifted exponential factor. Distribute the -3 across t - $\tau$ so the t term and the $\tau$ term are visible.

$$
e^{-3(t-\tau)}=e^{-3t+3\tau}
$$

Substitute that back into the product. Now both $\tau$-dependent exponents are in the same expression, so they can be combined.

$$
e^{-2\tau}e^{-3(t-\tau)}=e^{-2\tau}e^{-3t+3\tau}
$$

When multiplying exponentials with the same base, add the exponents. The $\tau$ terms combine as $-2\tau + 3\tau = \tau$.

$$
e^{-2\tau}e^{-3t+3\tau}=e^{-3t+\tau}
$$

It is often useful to separate the t-only factor from the $\tau$-dependent factor, because the next setup step will place this expression inside the overlap integral.

$$
e^{-3t+\tau}=e^{-3t}e^{\tau}
$$

So the simplified exponential part of the integrand is $e^{-3t}$$e^{\tau}$ over the overlap interval $0 \le \tau \le t$. This is only the integrand preparation, not the evaluated convolution.

$$
e^{-2\tau}e^{-3(t-\tau)}=e^{-3t}e^{\tau}
$$

## Writing the Convolution Integral for Two One-Sided Exponentials

**Example:** Set up, but do not evaluate, the convolution integral for $x(t)=e$^{-2t}$u(t)$ and $h(t)=e$^{-3t}$u(t)$.

**Explanation**

The cue is that all three setup pieces are now available: the convolution form, the causal overlap bounds, and the exponential product. This section puts those pieces into one bounded integral.

$$
(x*h)(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x(\tau)h(t-\tau)\,d\tau
$$

First rewrite the two original one-sided exponentials in the arguments used inside convolution.

$$
x(\tau)=e^{-2\tau}u(\tau),\qquad h(t-\tau)=e^{-3(t-\tau)}u(t-\tau)
$$

Next use the unit-step factors to restrict the integral. The factor $u(\tau)$ gives $\tau \ge 0$, and the factor $u(t - \tau)$ gives $\tau \le t$, so the overlap is from 0 to t when t is nonnegative.

$$
0\le \tau\le t\qquad \text{for }t\ge 0
$$

On that interval, the unit steps have already done their job. The integrand is the product of the two exponential parts.

$$
e^{-2\tau}e^{-3(t-\tau)}
$$

Now replace the infinite convolution limits with the overlap limits. Attach a causal $u(t)$ factor to record that there is no overlap, and therefore zero output, when t is negative.

$$
(x*h)(t)=u(t)\int_0^t e^{-2\tau}e^{-3(t-\tau)}\,d\tau
$$

Using the simplified product from the previous step, the same setup can also be written with the simplified integrand. This is still only a setup; no antiderivative has been taken.

$$
(x*h)(t)=u(t)\int_0^t e^{-3t}e^{\tau}\,d\tau
$$
