## Introduction to Convolution of Unequal-Width Rectangular Pulses

For rectangular pulses, the convolution value at a time t comes from the overlap of two supports on the $\tau$-axis: the fixed support of $x(\tau)$ and the shifted support of $h(t - \tau)$. Since each pulse is constant on its support, the integral is controlled by how long those two supports overlap.

$$
y(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x(\tau)h(t-\tau)\,d\tau
$$

![](<../Source/7.2.2. Convolution of Unequal-Width Rectangular Pulses/Images/l002-s001-te-section-001.png>)

When the two rectangular pulses have unequal widths, the shorter pulse can become completely covered by the longer one. That creates three nonzero overlap behaviors as t increases: the overlap length grows, then stays equal to the shorter width, then shrinks back to zero.

The edge-aligned breakpoints are the t-values where one edge of the sliding support lines up with one edge of the fixed support. These breakpoints split the time axis into no overlap, growing overlap, full overlap, shrinking overlap, and no overlap again.

$$
y(t)=A_xA_hL(t)
$$

Here $L(t)$ is the overlap length of the two rectangular supports, and $A_xA$_h is the product of the pulse amplitudes. In the unit-height base case, the convolution is just the overlap length. With nonunit heights, find the same overlap length first, then scale it by $A_xA$_h.

The workflow for this lesson is to locate the edge-aligned breakpoints, write the overlap length on each nonzero region, and then multiply by the pulse amplitudes. Because the middle overlap length is constant, the final output has a trapezoidal shape: rising ramp, flat plateau, and falling ramp.

<a id="identifying-edge-aligned-breakpoints"></a>
## Identifying Edge-Aligned Breakpoints

**Example:** For $x(t)=2$ on 0$\le$ t$\le$ 4 and $h(t)=3$ on 0$\le$ t$\le$ 7, identify the edge-aligned breakpoints of $y(t)=x(t)*h(t)$.

**Explanation**

The cue is that both signals are rectangular pulses with different widths. Before writing any overlap-length formula, put both supports on the $\tau$-axis: $x(\tau)$ stays fixed, and $h(t-\tau)$ slides as t changes. The heights 2 and 3 will scale the convolution later; they do not move the support edges.

![](<../Source/7.2.2. Convolution of Unequal-Width Rectangular Pulses/Images/l002-s002-te-section-002.png>)

$$
\operatorname{supp}x(\tau)=[0,4]
$$

Since $h(t)=3$ on 0$\le$ t$\le$ 7, the shifted signal $h(t-\tau)$ is nonzero when t-$\tau$ lies between 0 and 7. Solving that inequality gives the moving support of $h(t-\tau)$.

$$
0\le t-\tau\le 7 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad t-7\le \tau\le t
$$

Now match the moving edges t-7 and t with the fixed edges 0 and 4 in the order those contacts occur. These four alignments are the only t-values where the overlap behavior can change.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{first contact:} &\quad t=0,\\
\text{start of full overlap:} &\quad t=4,\\
\text{end of full overlap:} &\quad t-7=0 \Rightarrow t=7,\\
\text{last contact:} &\quad t-7=4 \Rightarrow t=11.
\end{aligned}
$$

So the ordered edge-aligned breakpoints are $t=0$, $t=4$, $t=7$, and $t=11$. These breakpoints split the convolution into no overlap, growing overlap, full overlap, shrinking overlap, and no overlap again. The next step is to use these intervals to write the overlap length; this step only identifies the control points.

<a id="computing-the-growing-overlap-region"></a>
## Computing the Growing-Overlap Region

**Example:** For $x(t)=2$ on 0$\le$ t$\le$ 4 and $h(t)=3$ on 0$\le$ t$\le$ 7, compute $y(t)$ on the growing-overlap interval 0$\le$ t$\le$ 4.

**Explanation**

Use the first two breakpoints from the previous section: $t=0$ is first contact, and $t=4$ is the start of full overlap. The only task in this section is to compute the growing branch on 0$\le$ t$\le$ 4.

![](<../Source/7.2.2. Convolution of Unequal-Width Rectangular Pulses/Images/l002-s003-te-section-003.png>)

$$
\operatorname{supp}x(\tau)=[0,4],\qquad \operatorname{supp}h(t-\tau)=[t-7,t]
$$

During the growing interval, the right edge t of the shifted support has entered the fixed support, while the left edge t-7 is still to the left of 0. So the common nonzero interval starts at $\tau=0$ and ends at $\tau=t$.

$$
0\le t\le 4:\qquad \text{overlap interval }=[0,t]
$$

The overlap length is the right endpoint minus the left endpoint. On this interval, that gives a length that grows linearly from 0 to the shorter width 4.

$$
L(t)=t-0=t
$$

On the overlap, the product $x(\tau)$$h(t-\tau)$ is constant and equals 2$\cdot$ 3. Multiply the overlap length by this amplitude product to get the convolution value on the growing branch.

$$
y(t)=2\cdot 3\cdot L(t)=6t,\qquad 0\le t\le 4
$$

Thus the growing-overlap branch is $y(t)=6t$ on 0$\le$ t$\le$ 4. This branch should not be used on the plateau or shrinking intervals; those regions have their own overlap lengths.

<a id="computing-the-full-overlap-plateau"></a>
## Computing the Full-Overlap Plateau

**Example:** For $x(t)=2$ on 0$\le$ t$\le$ 4 and $h(t)=3$ on 0$\le$ t$\le$ 7, compute $y(t)$ on the full-overlap interval 4$\le$ t$\le$ 7.

**Explanation**

The next interval is 4$\le$ t$\le$ 7, from the start of full overlap to the end of full overlap. The cue is containment: the shorter support is completely covered, so the overlap length stops changing.

![](<../Source/7.2.2. Convolution of Unequal-Width Rectangular Pulses/Images/l002-s004-te-section-004.png>)

$$
\operatorname{supp}x(\tau)=[0,4],\qquad \operatorname{supp}h(t-\tau)=[t-7,t]
$$

For 4$\le$ t$\le$ 7, the left edge t-7 of the shifted support is at or to the left of $\tau=0$, and the right edge t is at or to the right of $\tau=4$. That means the whole fixed support of $x(\tau)$ is inside the shifted support of $h(t-\tau)$.

$$
4\le t\le 7:\qquad \text{overlap interval}=[0,4]
$$

Because the overlap interval is the whole shorter support, its length is the shorter width. It does not depend on t anywhere inside the plateau interval.

$$
L(t)=4-0=4
$$

Now scale that constant overlap length by the amplitude product. The product $x(\tau)$$h(t-\tau)$ equals 2$\cdot$ 3 on the entire overlap.

$$
y(t)=2\cdot 3\cdot 4=24,\qquad 4\le t\le 7
$$

So the full-overlap plateau branch is $y(t)=24$ on 4$\le$ t$\le$ 7. This value is constant because the overlap length is fixed at the shorter width throughout this interval.

## Computing the Shrinking-Overlap Region

**Example:** For $x(t)=2$ on 0$\le$ t$\le$ 4 and $h(t)=3$ on 0$\le$ t$\le$ 7, compute $y(t)$ on the shrinking-overlap interval 7$\le$ t$\le$ 11.

**Explanation**

The shrinking interval runs from the end of full overlap at $t=7$ to last contact at $t=11$. The cue is that the shifted support is exiting to the right, so the left edge of the overlap moves while the right edge stays fixed at $\tau=4$.

![](<../Source/7.2.2. Convolution of Unequal-Width Rectangular Pulses/Images/l002-s005-te-section-005.png>)

$$
\operatorname{supp}x(\tau)=[0,4],\qquad \operatorname{supp}h(t-\tau)=[t-7,t]
$$

For 7$\le$ t$\le$ 11, the right edge t of the shifted support is already to the right of $\tau=4$. The left edge t-7 moves from $\tau=0$ to $\tau=4$, so the remaining overlap starts at $\tau=t-7$ and ends at $\tau=4$.

$$
7\le t\le 11:\qquad \text{overlap interval}=[t-7,4]
$$

The overlap length is right endpoint minus left endpoint. This gives a decreasing length, which starts at 4 when $t=7$ and reaches 0 when $t=11$.

$$
L(t)=4-(t-7)=11-t
$$

Now multiply the shrinking overlap length by the amplitude product. The product of the two pulse heights is still 2$\cdot$ 3.

$$
y(t)=2\cdot 3\cdot L(t)=6(11-t),\qquad 7\le t\le 11
$$

So the shrinking-overlap branch is $y(t)=6(11-t)$ on 7$\le$ t$\le$ 11. This branch decreases from the plateau value to zero, and it should only be used after the full-overlap interval ends.

## Assembling the Piecewise Trapezoidal Output

**Example:** For $x(t)=2$ on 0$\le$ t$\le$ 4 and $h(t)=3$ on 0$\le$ t$\le$ 7, assemble the complete piecewise formula for $y(t)=x(t)*h(t)$.

**Explanation**

The cue is that all three nonzero overlap branches have already been found, so this step is not a new derivation. Assemble the output by placing each branch on the interval determined by the ordered breakpoints.

![](<../Source/7.2.2. Convolution of Unequal-Width Rectangular Pulses/Images/l002-s006-te-section-006.png>)

$$
\text{breakpoints:}\qquad 0,\ 4,\ 7,\ 11
$$

Before first contact and after last contact, the pulses do not overlap. That gives the two zero-output regions: $y(t)=0$ for $t<0$ and $y(t)=0$ for $t>11$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{growing branch:} &\quad y(t)=6t, &&0\le t\le 4,\\
\text{plateau branch:} &\quad y(t)=24, &&4\le t\le 7,\\
\text{shrinking branch:} &\quad y(t)=6(11-t), &&7\le t\le 11.
\end{aligned}
$$

Now place the branches in order. The complete convolution output is the zero branch, then the rising line, then the constant plateau, then the falling line, then the final zero branch.

$$
y(t)=\begin{cases}
0, & t<0,\\
6t, & 0\le t\le 4,\\
24, & 4\le t\le 7,\\
6(11-t), & 7\le t\le 11,\\
0, & t>11.
\end{cases}
$$

As a final check, compare adjacent branch values at the shared breakpoints. The rising branch reaches the plateau at $t=4$, the shrinking branch starts at the plateau value when $t=7$, and the output returns to zero at $t=11$.

$$
6\cdot 4=24,\qquad 6(11-7)=24,\qquad 6(11-11)=0
$$

So the assembled piecewise formula is continuous at the internal breakpoints and has the expected trapezoidal shape.
