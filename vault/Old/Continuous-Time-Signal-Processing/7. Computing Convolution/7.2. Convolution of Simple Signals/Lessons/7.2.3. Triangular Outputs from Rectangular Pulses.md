## Introduction to Triangular Outputs from Rectangular Pulses

The object to watch is the overlap interval between the fixed pulse $x(\tau)$ and the shifted pulse $h(t - \tau)$. For equal-width rectangular pulses, the convolution output is built by tracking how long that overlap interval is as t changes.

$$
y(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x(\tau)h(t-\tau)\,d\tau
$$

This integral only receives contribution from $\tau$-values where both factors are nonzero. For rectangular pulses, the product $x(\tau)$$h(t - \tau)$ is constant on the overlap interval, so the integral becomes a rectangle area: height product times overlap length.

$$
y(t)=(\text{height product})(\text{overlap length at time }t)
$$

![](<../Source/7.2.3. Triangular Outputs from Rectangular Pulses/Images/l003-s001-te-section-001.png>)

Use the simplest base case: two unit-height rectangular pulses supported on [0, 2]. In the convolution variable, $x(\tau)$ stays fixed on [0, 2]. The shifted factor $h(t - \tau)$ is nonzero when $0 \le t - \tau \le 2$, so its support in $\tau$ is [t - 2, t].

As t increases, the shifted interval [t - 2, t] first has no overlap with [0, 2]. Then it enters the fixed interval, so the overlap length grows linearly. After maximum overlap, it leaves the fixed interval, so the overlap length shrinks linearly. That grow-then-shrink pattern is what creates the triangular output.

The next sections use this same structure in four active steps: locate the time regions, compute the rising overlap area, compute the falling overlap area, and assemble the piecewise triangle.

## Locating the Time Regions of a Triangular Convolution Output

**Example:** For unit-height rectangular pulses with $x(\tau)=1$ on 0$\le$ $\tau$$\le$ 2 and $h(\tau)=1$ on 0$\le$ $\tau$$\le$ 2, locate the time regions where (x*h)(t) is zero, rising, and falling.

**Explanation**

The cue is that the two rectangular pulses have the same width. To locate the time regions, track the support of the shifted pulse $h(t - \tau)$ as it slides across the fixed support of $x(\tau)$.

![](<../Source/7.2.3. Triangular Outputs from Rectangular Pulses/Images/l003-s002-te-section-002.png>)

The fixed support of $x(\tau)$ is [0, 2]. Since h is nonzero on [0, 2], the shifted factor $h(t - \tau)$ is nonzero when $0 \le t - \tau \le 2$, so its support in $\tau$ is [t - 2, t].

$$
\operatorname{supp}x(\tau)=[0,2],\qquad \operatorname{supp}h(t-\tau)=[t-2,t]
$$

First contact happens when the right endpoint of the shifted support just reaches the left endpoint of the fixed support. That means $t=0$.

$$
t=0
$$

Maximum overlap happens when the two equal-width intervals are aligned. The shifted interval [t - 2, t] matches [0, 2] when $t=2$.

$$
[t-2,t]=[0,2]\quad\Rightarrow\quad t=2
$$

Last contact happens when the left endpoint of the shifted support just reaches the right endpoint of the fixed support. That means $t-2=2$, so $t=4$.

$$
t-2=2\quad\Rightarrow\quad t=4
$$

So the breakpoints are $t=0$, $t=2$, and $t=4$. Before first contact and after last contact, the output is zero. From $t=0$ to $t=2$ the overlap is growing, and from $t=2$ to $t=4$ the overlap is falling.

$$
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{time region} & \text{overlap behavior} \\
\hline
t<0 & \text{no overlap} \\
0\le t\le 2 & \text{rising overlap} \\
2\le t\le 4 & \text{falling overlap} \\
t>4 & \text{no overlap}
\end{array}
$$

<a id="computing-the-rising-overlap-area"></a>
## Computing the Rising Overlap Area

**Example:** For $x(\tau)=2$ on 0$\le$ $\tau$$\le$ 2 and $h(\tau)=3$ on 0$\le$ $\tau$$\le$ 2, find (x*h)(t) on the rising region 0$\le$ t$\le$ 2.

**Explanation**

The cue is the rising region $0 \le t \le 2$. The time region has already been located; now convert its growing overlap interval into a convolution area.

![](<../Source/7.2.3. Triangular Outputs from Rectangular Pulses/Images/l003-s003-te-section-003.png>)

Use the support picture in the convolution variable. The fixed pulse $x(\tau)$ is nonzero on [0, 2]. Since h is nonzero on [0, 2], the shifted factor $h(t - \tau)$ is nonzero on [t - 2, t].

$$
\operatorname{supp}x(\tau)=[0,2],\qquad \operatorname{supp}h(t-\tau)=[t-2,t]
$$

On the rising interval, the shifted pulse is entering the fixed pulse. The left endpoint t - 2 is at or to the left of 0, and the right endpoint t is between 0 and 2, so the overlap runs from 0 to t.

$$
[0,2]\cap[t-2,t]=[0,t],\qquad 0\le t\le 2
$$

The overlap length is the right endpoint minus the left endpoint.

$$
L(t)=t-0=t
$$

Over that overlap interval, the product $x(\tau)$$h(t - \tau)$ is constant. Its height is the product of the pulse heights, so the convolution value is the height product times the overlap length.

$$
y(t)=\int_0^t 2\cdot 3\,d\tau=6t,\qquad 0\le t\le 2
$$

So the rising branch is $y(t)$=6t for $0 \le t \le 2$. This computes only the entering-overlap branch, not the falling branch or the full piecewise output.

<a id="computing-the-falling-overlap-area"></a>
## Computing the Falling Overlap Area

**Example:** For $x(\tau)=2$ on 0$\le$ $\tau$$\le$ 2 and $h(\tau)=3$ on 0$\le$ $\tau$$\le$ 2, find (x*h)(t) on the falling region 2$\le$ t$\le$ 4.

**Explanation**

The cue is the falling region $2 \le t \le 4$. This uses the same area rule as the rising branch, but now the overlap interval is shrinking instead of growing.

![](<../Source/7.2.3. Triangular Outputs from Rectangular Pulses/Images/l003-s004-te-section-004.png>)

Use the same support picture as before. The fixed pulse $x(\tau)$ is nonzero on [0, 2], and the shifted factor $h(t - \tau)$ is nonzero on [t - 2, t].

$$
\operatorname{supp}x(\tau)=[0,2],\qquad \operatorname{supp}h(t-\tau)=[t-2,t]
$$

On the falling interval, the shifted pulse is leaving the fixed pulse. The left endpoint t - 2 has moved inside [0, 2], while the right endpoint t is at or to the right of 2. So the overlap runs from t - 2 to 2.

$$
[0,2]\cap[t-2,t]=[t-2,2],\qquad 2\le t\le 4
$$

The overlap length is the right endpoint minus the left endpoint.

$$
L(t)=2-(t-2)=4-t
$$

Over that overlap interval, the product $x(\tau)$$h(t - \tau)$ is still constant. The pulse-height product is 2 times 3, so multiply the shrinking overlap length by 6.

$$
y(t)=\int_{t-2}^{2}2\cdot 3\,d\tau=6(4-t),\qquad 2\le t\le 4
$$

So the falling branch is $y(t)=6$(4-t) for $2 \le t \le 4$. This expression decreases from the peak value at $t=2$ to zero at $t=4$.

```quiz
  $$
```

## Writing the Complete Piecewise Triangular Output

**Example:** For $x(\tau)=2$ on 0$\le$ $\tau$$\le$ 2 and $h(\tau)=3$ on 0$\le$ $\tau$$\le$ 2, write the complete piecewise formula for (x*h)(t).

**Explanation**

The cue is the word complete. Instead of computing a new branch, assemble the regions already found: no overlap, rising overlap, falling overlap, and no overlap again.

![](<../Source/7.2.3. Triangular Outputs from Rectangular Pulses/Images/l003-s005-te-section-005.png>)

The contact breakpoints are $t=0$, $t=2$, and $t=4$. Before first contact and after last contact, the supports do not overlap, so the convolution value is zero.

$$
t=0,\quad t=2,\quad t=4
$$

Use the two active branches from the previous computations. On the rising interval, the overlap area is 6t. On the falling interval, the overlap area is 6(4-t).

$$
y(t)=6t\quad(0\le t\le 2),\qquad y(t)=6(4-t)\quad(2\le t\le 4)
$$

Now write the cases in increasing order of t. The peak endpoint $t=2$ can be assigned to either active branch because both formulas give the same value there.

$$
(x*h)(t)=\begin{cases}
0, & t<0,\\
6t, & 0\le t\le 2,\\
6(4-t), & 2<t\le 4,\\
0, & t>4.
\end{cases}
$$

As a quick check, the two active branches meet at the peak time. At $t=2$, the rising expression and falling expression both give 12.

$$
6(2)=12,\qquad 6(4-2)=12
$$
