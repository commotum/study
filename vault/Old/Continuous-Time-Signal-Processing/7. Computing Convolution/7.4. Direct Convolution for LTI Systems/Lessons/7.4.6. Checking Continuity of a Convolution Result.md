## Introduction to Checking Continuity of a Convolution Result

Direct convolution often produces a piecewise output because the overlap between $x(\tau)$ and $h(t-\tau)$ changes as t moves. After assembling $y(t)$, one useful verification step is to inspect the time boundaries where the formula changes.

At a shared boundary $t=a$, evaluate the formula from the region just to the left and the formula from the region just to the right. The continuity check asks whether those two boundary values agree.

$$
y_{\text{left}}(a)=y_{\text{right}}(a)
$$

For the simplest zero-to-nonzero boundary, suppose the piecewise result has $y(t)=0$ for $t<a$ and $y(t)=t-a$ for a$\le$ $t<b$. The left piece gives 0 at $t=a$, and the right piece also gives 0.

$$
\begin{aligned}
y_{\text{left}}(a)&=0,\\
y_{\text{right}}(a)&=a-a=0
\end{aligned}
$$

Because the values match, the boundary passes the continuity check. If the values do not match, the check does not automatically give the corrected formula; it tells you to recheck the algebra, integration bounds, or region assignment near that boundary.

$$
y_{\text{left}}(a)=y_{\text{right}}(a)\Rightarrow \text{check passes},\qquad y_{\text{left}}(a)\ne y_{\text{right}}(a)\Rightarrow \text{recheck nearby work}
$$

In this lesson, the same boundary-value test is used first at an outer zero-to-nonzero boundary, then at an interior boundary between two nonzero pieces, then across all boundaries of a piecewise convolution result.

## Checking Continuity at a Zero-to-Nonzero Boundary

**Example:** Check whether the piecewise convolution result $y(t)=0$ for $t<1$ and $y(t)=t-1$ for $1\let<3$ is continuous at $t=1$.

**Explanation**

The cue is that the formula changes at $t=1$. To check this boundary, compare the value from the zero-output region with the value from the first nonzero formula at the same boundary time.

$$
y(t)=\begin{cases}
0, & t<1,\\
t-1, & 1\le t<3
\end{cases}
$$

The left-hand region is the zero piece, so its boundary value is 0. For the right-hand region, substitute $t=1$ into the formula t-1.

$$
\begin{aligned}
y_{\text{left}}(1)&=0,\\
y_{\text{right}}(1)&=1-1=0
\end{aligned}
$$

Both adjacent pieces give the same boundary value. Therefore, the piecewise convolution result passes the continuity check at $t=1$.

$$
y_{\text{left}}(1)=y_{\text{right}}(1)=0
$$

## Checking an Interior Boundary Between Two Nonzero Pieces

**Example:** Check whether $y(t) = t$ for $0 \le t < 1$ and $y(t) = 2 - t$ for $1 \le t < 2$ is continuous at $t = 1$.

**Explanation**

The cue is that $t=1$ is an interior boundary between two nonzero formulas. Use the same boundary-value rule, but now evaluate both adjacent pieces at $t=1$.

$$
y(t)=\begin{cases}
t, & 0\le t<1,\\
2-t, & 1\le t<2
\end{cases}
$$

For the left piece, substitute $t=1$ into the formula t. For the right piece, substitute $t=1$ into the formula 2-t.

$$
\begin{aligned}
y_{\text{left}}(1)&=1,\\
y_{\text{right}}(1)&=2-1=1
\end{aligned}
$$

The two formulas look different, but continuity is checked by comparing their boundary values, not their forms or slopes. Since both values are 1, the interior boundary passes the continuity check.

$$
y_{\text{left}}(1)=y_{\text{right}}(1)=1
$$

## Checking Continuity Across All Time-Region Boundaries

**Example:** Check continuity at every boundary for $y(t) = 0$ for $t < 0$, $y(t) = t$ for $0 \le t < 1$, $y(t) = 2 - t$ for $1 \le t < 2$, and $y(t) = 0$ for $t \ge 2$.

**Explanation**

The cue is that this piecewise result has several time-region boundaries. List the shared boundaries in increasing order, then compare the formula immediately to the left with the formula immediately to the right at each boundary.

$$
y(t)=\begin{cases}
0, & t<0,\\
t, & 0\le t<1,\\
2-t, & 1\le t<2,\\
0, & t\ge 2
\end{cases}
$$

The shared boundaries are $t=0$, $t=1$, and $t=2$. Check each one by evaluating only the two pieces that touch that boundary.

$$
\begin{aligned}
t=0:&\quad y_{\text{left}}(0)=0,\quad y_{\text{right}}(0)=0,\\
t=1:&\quad y_{\text{left}}(1)=1,\quad y_{\text{right}}(1)=2-1=1,\\
t=2:&\quad y_{\text{left}}(2)=2-2=0,\quad y_{\text{right}}(2)=0
\end{aligned}
$$

Every adjacent pair gives matching boundary values. Therefore, the piecewise convolution result passes the continuity check at all of its time-region boundaries.

$$
\text{All boundary checks pass.}
$$

## Using a Failed Continuity Check to Find an Error

**Example:** A computed convolution result is $y(t) = 0$ for $t < 0$, $y(t) = t$ for $0 \le t < 1$, $y(t) = 3 - t$ for $1 \le t < 2$, and $y(t) = 0$ for $t \ge 2$. Use a continuity check to identify where the result likely contains an error.

**Explanation**

The cue is that this is no longer just a pass-or-fail verification. Use the same boundary-value comparison, but scan the boundaries in order until a mismatch appears.

$$
y(t)=\begin{cases}
0, & t<0,\\
t, & 0\le t<1,\\
3-t, & 1\le t<2,\\
0, & t\ge 2
\end{cases}
$$

The shared boundaries are $t=0$, $t=1$, and $t=2$. Start at $t=0$ by comparing the zero piece on the left with the formula t on the right.

$$
t=0:\quad y_{\text{left}}(0)=0,\quad y_{\text{right}}(0)=0
$$

The boundary $t=0$ passes, so move to the next boundary. At $t=1$, compare the left formula t with the right formula 3-t.

$$
t=1:\quad y_{\text{left}}(1)=1,\quad y_{\text{right}}(1)=3-1=2
$$

The values at $t=1$ do not match. That flags the adjacent pieces around $t=1$, or the transition between them, as the likely place to investigate for an algebra, integration-bound, or region-assignment error.

$$
y_{\text{left}}(1)\ne y_{\text{right}}(1)\Rightarrow \text{recheck the pieces adjacent to }t=1
$$

This continuity check locates a likely problem area, but it does not by itself determine the corrected convolution formula.
