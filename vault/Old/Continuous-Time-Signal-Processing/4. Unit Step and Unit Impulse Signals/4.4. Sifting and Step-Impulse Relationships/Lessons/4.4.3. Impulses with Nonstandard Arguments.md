## Introduction to Impulses with Nonstandard Arguments

When an impulse is written as $\delta(g(t))$ instead of the standard shifted form $\delta(t-t_0)$, the job is to find where the argument is zero and rewrite the result as a shifted standard impulse. That zero tells you the impulse location, and the local slope tells you the scaling factor.

$$
\delta(at)=\frac{1}{|a|}\delta(t),\; a \neq 0
$$

For a pure scale, the impulse stays centered at $t=0$, but the argument is compressed or stretched by the factor $a$. The coefficient must therefore be $1/|a|$ so the impulse keeps unit area whether $a$ is positive or negative.

$$
\delta(at-b)=\frac{1}{|a|}\delta\left(t-\frac{b}{a}\right),\; a \neq 0
$$

For a shifted linear argument, solve $at-b=0$ to locate the impulse at $t=\frac{b}{a}$, then attach the same inverse-slope weight from the factor that vanishes there. When the argument later factors into several simple linear terms, apply the same idea at each zero and add the resulting shifted impulses.

## Rewriting an Impulse with a Pure Time Scale

**Example:** Rewrite $\delta(4t)$ as a standard-form impulse.

**Explanation**

The cue here is a pure time scale: the argument is $4t$, so the impulse is still centered at $t=0$. Match it to the template $\delta(at)$ and use the inverse-magnitude of the scale factor.

$$
\delta(at)=\frac{1}{|a|}\delta(t),\; a \neq 0
$$

Here $a=4$, so $|a|=4$ and the coefficient is $\frac{1}{4}$. The standard-form rewrite is $\delta(4t)=\frac{1}{4}\delta(t)$.

## Rewriting an Impulse with a Shifted Linear Argument

**Example:** Rewrite $\delta(3t-6)$ as a sum of weighted shifted impulses.

**Explanation**

The cue is a shifted linear argument, so match $3t-6$ to the template $at-b$. Solve the argument zero first: $3t-6=0$ gives $t=2$.

$$
\delta(3t-6)=\frac{1}{3}\delta(t-2)
$$

Here $a=3$, so the inverse-slope weight is $1/|a|=1/3$. With the zero at $t=2$, the standard-form impulse is $\frac{1}{3}\delta(t-2)$.

    \delta(2t+4)=\frac{1}{2}\delta(t+2)

## Rewriting an Impulse with a Negative Slope

**Example:** Rewrite $\delta(-2t+6)$ as a weighted shifted standard impulse.

**Explanation**

This still matches the linear template $at-b$, but now the slope is negative: $a=-2$. Solve the zero first: $-2t+6=0$ gives $t=3$.

$$
\delta(-2t+6)=\frac{1}{2}\delta(t-3)
$$

The negative sign changes how you locate the root, but the impulse weight uses $|a|$. So the coefficient is $1/2$, not $-1/2$, and the shifted impulse is centered at $t=3$.

## Rewriting an Impulse Argument with Multiple Simple Zeros

**Example:** Rewrite $\delta((t-1)(t+2))$ as a sum of shifted impulses.

**Explanation**

The cue is a product with two simple zeros, so treat the argument as $f(t)$ and use the multi-root rule $\delta(f(t))=\sum_k \frac{1}{|f'(t_k)|}\delta(t-t_k)$. Here $f(t)=(t-1)(t+2)$, so the zeros are $t=1$ and $t=-2$.

$$
f(t)=(t-1)(t+2), \quad f'(t)=2t+1, \quad |f'(1)|=3, \quad |f'(-2)|=3
$$

Each zero contributes one shifted impulse with its own inverse-slope weight. Since both derivative magnitudes are $3$, each term gets coefficient $1/3$.

$$
\delta((t-1)(t+2))=\frac{1}{3}\delta(t-1)+\frac{1}{3}\delta(t+2)
$$
