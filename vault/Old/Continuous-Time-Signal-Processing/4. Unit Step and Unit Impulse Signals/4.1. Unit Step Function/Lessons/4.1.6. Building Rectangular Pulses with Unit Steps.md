## Introduction to Building Rectangular Pulses with Unit Steps

A rectangular pulse is a signal that is active only over one finite interval and is zero everywhere else. In this lesson, the simplest pulse has unit height, so the main idea is to build that window by turning a signal on and then turning it off.

The unit step is the switch that makes that window possible. A shifted step like $u(t-a)$ turns the signal on at the leading edge $t=a$, and a second shifted step can remove it again at a later time.

$$
x(t)=u(t)-u(t-T)
$$

Read $x(t)=u(t)-u(t-T)$ as a unit pulse that starts at $t=0$ and ends at $t=T$. The first step creates the leading edge, the subtraction removes the signal at the trailing edge, and the result is 1 only across that finite window.

$$
x(t)=u(t-a)-u(t-b), a<b
$$

The same subtraction pattern works for any pulse that begins at $a$ and ends at $b$. The first shifted step marks the leading edge, the second shifted step marks the trailing edge, and their difference gives the rectangular pulse template used in the next examples.

## Creating a Unit-Height Pulse from $t = 0 to t = T$

**Example:** Write a unit-height pulse that is on from $t=0$ to $t=T$ and zero elsewhere.

**Explanation**

The cue is a finite window with leading edge at $t=0$ and trailing edge at $t=T$. To build that pulse, use the first unit step to turn the signal on at the leading edge and subtract a shifted step to turn it off at the trailing edge.

$$
x(t)=u(t)-u(t-T)
$$

This difference gives the desired interval behavior. For $t<0$, both steps are 0, so $x(t)=0$. For $0 \le t < T$, the first step is 1 and the second is 0, so $x(t)=1$. For $t \ge T$, both steps are 1, so the subtraction returns to 0. The result is a unit-height pulse on $[0,T)$.

## Moving the Pulse Window to Start at $t = a$

**Example:** Create a unit-height pulse that is on for $a \le t < a+T$.

**Explanation**

The cue is the same finite window as before, but the leading edge has moved from $t=0$ to $t=a$. To keep the pulse unit height, shift both steps by the same amount so the window starts at $a$ and keeps width $T$.

$$
x(t)=u(t-a)-u(t-a-T)
$$

Set the on-transition to $u(t-a)$ so the signal turns on at the leading edge $t=a$. Then set the off-transition to $u(t-a-T)$ so the signal turns off $T$ units later, at $t=a+T$.

Subtracting the two shifted steps preserves the same rectangular shape while moving the whole window. The result is 0 before $t=a$, 1 on $[a,a+T)$, and 0 again for $t \ge a+T$, so the pulse has the desired start time and unit height.

## Scaling the Pulse to a Chosen Amplitude

**Example:** Construct $x(t)=3[u(t-1)-u(t-4)]$ and describe its value for each time region.

**Explanation**

The cue is a rectangular pulse with the same start and stop times as before, but now the plateau height is $3$ instead of $1$. To scale the pulse correctly, place the coefficient in front of the entire step difference, not on just one step.

$$
x(t)=3[u(t-1)-u(t-4)]
$$

The factor $3$ multiplies the whole rectangular pulse. For $t<1$, both steps are 0, so $x(t)=0$. For $1 \le t < 4$, the first step is 1 and the second is 0, so the bracketed difference is 1 and the output is $3$. For $t \ge 4$, both steps are 1, so the difference returns to 0 and the pulse turns off.

## Writing a Rectangular Pulse from Its Height and Endpoints

**Example:** Write the expression for a pulse of height 4 active between $t=-2$ and $t=5$.

**Explanation**

The cue is a rectangular pulse with height $A=4$, starting at $t_1=-2$ and ending at $t_2=5$. Use the standard step-difference form: the first step turns the pulse on at the left endpoint, and the second step turns it off at the right endpoint.

Write the on-switch as $u(t-t_1)=u(t+2)$ and the off-switch as $u(t-t_2)=u(t-5)$. Then place the amplitude multiplier on the whole difference so the pulse keeps height 4 while it is active.

$$
x(t)=4[u(t+2)-u(t-5)]
$$

This means the signal is 0 for $t<-2$, equals 4 for $-2\le t<5$, and returns to 0 for $t\ge 5$. The form matches the endpoint specification directly: $u(t+2)$ starts the pulse at $t=-2$, and $u(t-5)$ ends it at $t=5$.

## Reading the On and Off Times from a Step-Difference Pulse

**Example:** From $x(t)=3[u(t+1)-u(t-4)]$, determine where the signal is non-zero and what its non-zero level is.

**Explanation**

The cue is a step-difference pulse written in the standard form $A[u(t-a)-u(t-b)]$. Read the first step as the on-switch and the second step as the off-switch, then use the leading coefficient as the pulse height.

Here $u(t+1)$ means the pulse turns on at $t=-1$, because $t+1=0$ at that time. Likewise, $u(t-4)$ means the pulse turns off at $t=4$. The coefficient $3$ sets the active level.

$$
x(t)=3[u(t+1)-u(t-4)] \;\Rightarrow\; \text{non-zero on } -1\le t<4
$$

So the signal is 0 for $t<-1$, equals 3 for $-1\le t<4$, and returns to 0 for $t\ge 4$. The reverse reading matches the construction rule exactly: first switch on at $t=-1$, then switch off at $t=4$.
