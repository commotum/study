## Introduction to Shifted Unit Step Functions

The object to recognize in this step is the unit-step argument. In this topic, the rule is: the switch-on instant is the time that makes the argument of $u(\cdot)$ equal to zero.

Start from the base case. The canonical unit step is defined by $u(t)=0$ for $t<0$ and $u(t)=1$ for $t\ge 0$, so the switch flips on at $t=0$.

$$
u(t)=\begin{cases}0, & t<0\1, & t\ge 0\end{cases}
$$

For the shifted form $u(t-T)$, solve $t-T=0$ to get the switch-on time $t=T$. That means $u(t-T)=0$ for $t<T$ and $u(t-T)=1$ for $t\ge T$.

$$
u(t-T):\; t<T \implies 0,\; t\ge T \implies 1
$$

Now contrast with $u(t+T)$. Set $t+T=0$, so the switch occurs at $t=-T$. For positive $T$, this is an advance: the function turns on before zero.

$$
u(t+T):\; t<-T \implies 0,\; t\ge -T \implies 1
$$

Use the same rule for any shift: in $u(t-t_0)$, the switch-on time is $t=t_0$. Do not introduce a new formula here—only change the solve-and-substitute step.

Across this lesson, future steps will keep this template unchanged and vary only the inside offset. The action is always: set the inside argument to zero and identify the resulting on-time.

## Finding the Switch-On Time from $u(t - T)$

**Example:** Find the switch-on time of $y(t)=u(t-3)$.

**Explanation**

The cue is the argument inside $u(\cdot)$: here it is $t-3$. For a shifted step, the function switches on when this argument becomes zero.

$$
t-3=0
$$

Solve the condition directly: $t-3=0 \implies t=3$.

$$
t=3
$$

So $u(t-3)$ turns on at $t=3$. Therefore the signal is $y(t)=0$ for $t<3$ and $y(t)=1$ for $t\ge3$.

$$
y(t)=\begin{cases}0, & t<3\\1, & t\ge3\end{cases}
$$

## Finding the Switch-On Time from $u(t + T)$

**Example:** Find the switch-on time of $y(t)=u(t+2)$.

**Explanation**

The cue is still the argument of the unit step: here it is $t+2$. Use the same rule as the previous section and set that argument to zero.

$$
t+2=0
$$

Solve for the switch-on time: $t+2=0 \implies t=-2$.

$$
t=-2
$$

So this function switches on at $t=-2$. It is off before the switch and on at and after it: $y(t)=0$ for $t<-2$ and $y(t)=1$ for $t\ge-2$.

$$
y(t)=\begin{cases}0, & t<-2\\1, & t\ge-2\end{cases}
$$

## Finding the Switch-On Time from $u(t - t0)$

**Example:** If $y(t)=u(t-t_0)$ and $t_0=-1$, find the switch-on time.

**Explanation**

The cue is the shifted argument inside $u(\cdot)$: here it is $t-t_0$. Use the same rule and set that argument to zero.

$$
t-t_0=0
$$

Rearrange the equation to solve for the switch time: $t=t_0$.

$$
t=t_0
$$

Substitute the given value $t_0=-1$, so the switch-on time is $t=-1$.

$$
t=-1
$$

The function is therefore $y(t)=0$ for $t<-1$ and $y(t)=1$ for $t\ge-1$.

$$
y(t)=\begin{cases}0, & t<-1\\1, & t\ge-1\end{cases}
$$

## Placing Shifted Unit Steps on the Time Axis

**Example:** Order the switch-on times of $y_1(t)=u(t-2)$, $y_2(t)=u(t+1)$, and $y_3(t)=u(t-5)$ from earliest to latest.

**Explanation**

Use the same cue as before: for each shifted step, set the inside of $u(\cdot)$ to zero and solve for the switch time.

$$
t-2=0,\; t+1=0,\; t-5=0
$$

$$
t=2,\; t=-1,\; t=5
$$

Match each result to its function: $y_1$ switches at $2$, $y_2$ switches at $-1$, and $y_3$ switches at $5$.

![](<../Source/4.1.3. Shifted Unit Step Functions/Images/l003-s005-te-section-005-step-axis.png>)

Sort the switch times from earliest to latest: $-1 < 2 < 5$, so the ordering is $y_2$, $y_1$, $y_3$.

$$
-1 < 2 < 5,\quad y_2\rightarrow\text{first},\; y_1\rightarrow\text{second},\; y_3\rightarrow\text{third}
$$
