## Introduction to Starting a Signal with a Unit Step

When a continuous-time signal is zero before a start time and follows one active formula after that time, the compact form is the active formula multiplied by a unit step. The unit step is the switch that marks where the signal turns on.

Think of the left side of the switch as the inactive interval and the right side as the active interval. In this lesson, the inactive side is $t<t_0$, the active side is $t\ge t_0$, and the step is what separates the two.

For the simplest case, place the switch at $t=0$. Then a signal that is $0$ for $t<0$ and $f(t)$ for $t\ge 0$ becomes a single gated expression.

$$
x(t)=f(t)u(t)
$$

For any later start time $t_0$, keep the active formula unchanged and shift only the step to $u(t-t_0)$. That shift moves the turn-on to the exact start time without changing the signal shape after the switch.

$$
x(t)=f(t)u(t-t_0)
$$

Use the lesson's boundary convention consistently at the switch instant, and remember the key rule: the step contributes the timing, while the active formula contributes the shape. That is the conversion you will reuse in the next steps.

## Starting an Active Formula at $t = 0$

**Example:** Write a unit-step form for $x(t)=0$ for $t<0$ and $x(t)=2t+1$ for $t\ge 0$.

**Explanation**

The cue is a two-case signal with zero before the start and one active formula after the start. Because the switch is at the origin, the gate is $u(t)$.

Keep the active branch exactly as $2t+1$. The step provides the on-off timing; it does not change the formula after $t=0$.

$$
x(t)=(2t+1)u(t)
$$

Check the inactive side: for $t<0$, $u(t)=0$, so the whole expression is zero before the start. That matches the original piecewise definition.

## Starting an Active Formula After $t = 0$

**Example:** Convert $x(t)=0$ for $t<2$ and $x(t)=2e^{-t}$ for $t\ge 2$ to a unit-step expression.

**Explanation**

The cue is a signal that is zero before $t=2$ and follows one active formula after that time. Because the switch happens at $t=2$, the gate is $u(t-2)$.

Keep the active branch exactly as $2e^{-t}$. Only the step argument shifts; the formula itself stays unchanged.

$$
x(t)=2e^{-t}u(t-2)
$$

Check both sides: for $t<2$, $u(t-2)=0$, so the expression is zero; for $t\ge 2$, $u(t-2)=1$, so the output is $2e^{-t}$. That matches the piecewise definition.

## Starting an Active Formula Before $t = 0$

**Example:** Convert $x(t)=0$ for $t<-1$ and $x(t)=\sin(t)$ for $t\ge -1$ into a unit-step expression.

**Explanation**

The cue is a signal that is zero before $t=-1$ and active from $t=-1$ onward. Since the switch turns on one unit before the origin, the gate is $u(t+1)$.

Keep the active branch exactly as $\sin(t)$. Only the step argument changes to reflect the negative start time.

$$
x(t)=\sin(t)u(t+1)
$$

Check both intervals: for $t<-1$, $u(t+1)=0$, so the signal is zero; for $t\ge -1$, $u(t+1)=1$, so the output is $\sin(t)$. That preserves the piecewise definition.
