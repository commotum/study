## Introduction to Multiplying a Function by an Impulse

The impulse $\delta(t)$ acts like an ideal point sampler at $t=0$. When it multiplies a function, only the function value at that point survives.

$$
f(t)\delta(t)=f(0)\delta(t)
$$

So to simplify a product such as $\left(2t+3\right)\delta(t)$, replace the multiplier by its value at $t=0$ and keep the impulse factor. Here, $f(0)=3$.

$$
\left(2t+3\right)\delta(t)=3\delta(t)
$$

The same rule also handles a zero value at the impulse location. If the multiplier evaluates to $0$ at $t=0$, then the whole product disappears.

$$
t\delta(t)=0\delta(t)=0
$$

This is the only mechanism needed here: later products with constants, polynomials, and other symbolic factors all reduce the same way by sampling the multiplier at $t=0$.

## Evaluating $f(0)$ in a Product $f(t)$$\delta$(t)

**Example:** Simplify $\left(4t+1\right)\delta(t)$.

**Explanation**

The cue is a multiplier attached to $\delta(t)$. Use the sampling rule from the previous step: replace the multiplier by its value at $t=0$, then keep the impulse factor.

$$
f(t)=4t+1
$$

Now evaluate the multiplier at $t=0$. For this function, $f(0)=4\cdot 0+1=1$.

$$
f(0)=4\cdot 0+1=1
$$

Substitute that sampled value back into the product and simplify the impulse term.

$$
\left(4t+1\right)\delta(t)=f(0)\delta(t)=1\delta(t)=\delta(t)
$$

So the product collapses to $\delta(t)$ because the impulse keeps only the coefficient at $t=0$.

## Simplifying Algebraic Factors Multiplying $\delta$(t)

**Example:** Simplify $(2t-3)(t+5)\delta(t)$.

**Explanation**

The cue is a factored algebraic multiplier attached to $\delta(t)$. Use the same sampling rule: evaluate each factor at $t=0$, then combine the resulting constants before keeping the impulse factor.

$$
f(t)=(2t-3)(t+5)
$$

Now sample each factor at $t=0$. The first factor becomes $2\cdot 0-3=-3$, and the second factor becomes $0+5=5$.

$$
f(0)=(2\cdot 0-3)(0+5)=(-3)(5)=-15
$$

Substitute the sampled coefficient back into the impulse product and simplify the result.

$$
(2t-3)(t+5)\delta(t)=f(0)\delta(t)=-15\delta(t)
$$

So the factored multiplier collapses to $-15\delta(t)$ because the impulse keeps only the value of the product at $t=0$.

## Simplifying Exponential and Sinusoidal Factors Multiplying $\delta$(t)

**Example:** Simplify $5e^{-2t}\sin(\pi t)\delta(t)$.

**Explanation**

Recognize the cue: a factor is multiplying $\delta(t)$, so the rule is to sample that factor at $t=0$ instead of expanding or integrating it.

$$
f(t)=5e^{-2t}\sin(\pi t)
$$

Set $f(t)=5e^{-2t}\sin(\pi t)$ and evaluate at $t=0$. The exponential becomes $e^0$ and the sine term becomes $\sin(0)$.

$$
f(0)=5\cdot e^0\cdot \sin(0)=0
$$

Substitute the sampled value back into the impulse product: $5e^{-2t}\sin(\pi t)\delta(t)=f(0)\delta(t)=0\delta(t)=0$.

$$
5e^{-2t}\sin(\pi t)\delta(t)=f(0)\delta(t)=0\delta(t)=0
$$

The impulse keeps only the value at $t=0$, and here that value is zero, so the entire product collapses to zero.

## Recognizing Zero-Weight Products with $\delta$(t)

**Example:** Simplify $t(2t+1)(t-4)\delta(t)$ and decide whether the result is zero.

**Explanation**

Recognize the cue: the multiplier contains an explicit $t$ factor, so the impulse rule says to evaluate the whole multiplier at $t=0$.

$$
f(t)=t(2t+1)(t-4)
$$

Compute the sampled value at $t=0$. The first factor is $0$, while the other factors become $1$ and $-4$.

$$
f(0)=0\cdot 1\cdot (-4)=0
$$

Substitute the result back into the impulse product: $t(2t+1)(t-4)\delta(t)=f(0)\delta(t)=0\delta(t)=0$.

$$
t(2t+1)(t-4)\delta(t)=f(0)\delta(t)=0\delta(t)=0
$$

The product is zero because the impulse keeps only the value at $t=0$, and any explicit $t$ factor makes that sampled value vanish.
