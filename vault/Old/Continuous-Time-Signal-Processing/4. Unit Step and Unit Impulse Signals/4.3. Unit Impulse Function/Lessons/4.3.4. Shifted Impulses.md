## Introduction to Shifted Impulses

A shifted impulse is still the unit impulse, but its location is wherever the inside of $\delta(\cdot)$ becomes zero. Read the argument first, then solve for the time value that activates the impulse.

$$
\delta(t)=\delta(t-0)
$$

Start with the base case $\delta(t)$. Because the argument is just $t$, the zero occurs at $t=0$, so this impulse is located at the origin.

$$
t-t_0=0 \Rightarrow t=t_0
$$

For a form like $\delta(t-t_0)$, set the argument equal to zero: $t-t_0=0$. Solving gives $t=t_0$, so a positive $t_0$ means the impulse is delayed to the right.

$$
t+t_0=0 \Rightarrow t=-t_0
$$

For $\delta(t+t_0)$, do the same thing: $t+t_0=0$. Solving gives $t=-t_0$, so the impulse is advanced to the left.

Keep the coefficient with the shifted impulse as its area weight. The location comes from the zero of the argument; the coefficient tells you how much impulse area sits at that same location.

## Locating an Impulse from $\delta$(t - t0)

**Example:** Locate the shifted impulse in $\delta(t-3)$.

**Explanation**

The cue is the shifted impulse $\delta(t-3)$. To locate it, set the internal argument equal to zero and solve for the time that activates the impulse.

$$
t-3=0
$$

Solving gives $t=3$, so the impulse is activated at time $3$.

$$
t=3
$$

So $\delta(t-3)$ is a right-shifted impulse located at $t=3$.

## Locating an Impulse from $\delta$(t + t0)

**Example:** Locate the impulse in $\delta(t+2)$.

**Explanation**

The cue is the shifted impulse $\delta(t+2)$. To locate it, set the internal argument equal to zero and solve for the time that activates the impulse.

$$
t+2=0
$$

Solving gives $t=-2$, so the impulse is activated at time $-2$.

$$
t=-2
$$

So $\delta(t+2)$ is an advanced impulse located at $t=-2$. The coefficient is $1$, so the impulse keeps unit area.

## Carrying the Area Weight of a Shifted Impulse

**Example:** Find location and area of $5\delta(t-2.5)$.

**Explanation**

The cue is the shifted impulse $5\delta(t-2.5)$. First locate the impulse by setting the internal argument equal to zero.

$$
t-2.5=0
$$

Solving gives $t=2.5$, so the impulse is activated at time $2.5$. The coefficient $5$ is the area weight and stays attached to the impulse.

$$
t=2.5
$$

So $5\delta(t-2.5)$ is a shifted impulse located at $t=2.5$ with area $5$.

## Reading Sums of Shifted Impulses

**Example:** Interpret $\delta(t-3)-\delta(t+1)+4\delta(t-0.5)$ by enumerating each impulse term.

**Explanation**

The cue is a sum of shifted impulses. Read each term separately: the zero of each argument gives the time, and the coefficient gives the area.

$$
\delta(t-3)-\delta(t+1)+4\delta(t-0.5)
$$

For $\delta(t-3)$, solve $t-3=0$ to get $t=3$, with area $1$.

For $-\delta(t+1)$, solve $t+1=0$ to get $t=-1$, with area $-1$.

For $4\delta(t-0.5)$, solve $t-0.5=0$ to get $t=0.5$, with area $4$.

So the complete ordered list is $(3,1)$, $(-1,-1)$, and $(0.5,4)$. Each shifted impulse is listed once and kept separate.
