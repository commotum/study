## Introduction to High-Pass Magnitude Response

The RC high-pass response starts from the complex frequency response $H(j\omega)=\frac{j\omega RC}{1+j\omega RC}$. For this step, ignore phase and focus only on the magnitude, because $|H(j\omega)|$ is the amplitude-only summary that tells us how strongly each frequency passes through the filter.

$$
H(j\omega)=\frac{j\omega RC}{1+j\omega RC}
$$

Taking magnitudes turns the complex ratio into a real gain formula. The move here is to read the numerator and denominator by magnitude only and simplify the result into a single expression for $|H(j\omega)|$.

$$
|H(j\omega)|=\frac{\omega RC}{\sqrt{1+(\omega RC)^2}}
$$

This base case is the starting point for the rest of the lesson: once the magnitude formula is visible, we can read off what happens at low frequency and at high frequency, and then interpret the circuit as a high-pass filter.

## Computing $|H(j\omega)|$ from the High-Pass Frequency Response

**Example:** Given $H(j\omega)=\frac{j\omega RC}{1+j\omega RC}$, compute $|H(j\omega)|$ and simplify it to a standard magnitude-response formula.

**Explanation**

The cue is the complex high-pass response $H(j\omega)=\frac{j\omega RC}{1+j\omega RC}$. To get the magnitude response, take the absolute value of the whole fraction and separate the numerator and denominator by magnitude.

$$
|H(j\omega)|=\left|\frac{j\omega RC}{1+j\omega RC}\right|
$$

The numerator has magnitude $|j\omega RC|=\omega RC$, and the denominator has magnitude $|1+j\omega RC|=\sqrt{1+(\omega RC)^2}$. That turns the complex response into a real gain formula.

$$
|H(j\omega)|=\frac{\omega RC}{\sqrt{1+(\omega RC)^2}}
$$

So the RC high-pass magnitude response is $|H(j\omega)|=\frac{\omega RC}{\sqrt{1+(\omega RC)^2}}$, a real, nonnegative amplitude-only summary that we will use in the next sections to read low-frequency attenuation and high-frequency passage.

## Interpreting Low-Frequency Attenuation

**Example:** Given $|H(j\omega)|=\frac{\omega RC}{\sqrt{1+(\omega RC)^2}}$, determine what happens as $\omega\to0$ and explain why the circuit suppresses low-frequency input.

**Explanation**

The cue is the low-frequency regime $\omega RC\ll1$ applied to the magnitude formula $|H(j\omega)|=\frac{\omega RC}{\sqrt{1+(\omega RC)^2}}$. That tells us to use the same amplitude-only response from the previous step and simplify it at small $\omega$.

$$
|H(j\omega)|=\frac{\omega RC}{\sqrt{1+(\omega RC)^2}}
$$

When $\omega RC\ll1$, the term $(\omega RC)^2$ is tiny, so $\sqrt{1+(\omega RC)^2}\approx1$. The gain therefore becomes proportional to $\omega$, which is exactly the low-frequency attenuation behavior we want to recognize.

$$
\begin{aligned}|H(j\omega)|&=\frac{\omega RC}{\sqrt{1+(\omega RC)^2}}\\&\approx\frac{\omega RC}{1}\\&=\omega RC\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\lim_{\omega\to0}|H(j\omega)|=0
$$

So the response shrinks to zero as frequency goes to zero, which means the RC high-pass filter strongly attenuates low-frequency input.

## Interpreting High-Frequency Passage

**Example:** Given $|H(j\omega)|=\frac{\omega RC}{\sqrt{1+(\omega RC)^2}}$, determine what happens as $\omega\to\infty$ and explain why the circuit passes high-frequency input.

**Explanation**

The cue is the high-frequency regime $\omega RC\gg1$ applied to the same magnitude formula $|H(j\omega)|=\frac{\omega RC}{\sqrt{1+(\omega RC)^2}}$. That tells us to check what the ratio becomes when the frequency is much larger than the cutoff scale.

$$
|H(j\omega)|=\frac{\omega RC}{\sqrt{1+(\omega RC)^2}}
$$

When $\omega RC\gg1$, both the numerator and the dominant term in the denominator scale like $\omega RC$. So the gain approaches $1$ rather than shrinking toward $0$, which is the signature of high-frequency passage.

$$
\begin{aligned}|H(j\omega)|&=\frac{\omega RC}{\sqrt{1+(\omega RC)^2}}\\&\approx\frac{\omega RC}{\omega RC}\\&=1\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\lim_{\omega\to\infty}|H(j\omega)|=1
$$

So for very large $\omega$, the RC high-pass filter passes the input with near-unity gain, which means the high-frequency part of the signal comes through essentially unchanged.

## Reading the High-Pass Magnitude Response as a Filter

**Example:** Using $|H(j\omega)|=\frac{\omega RC}{\sqrt{1+(\omega RC)^2}}$, explain why the RC circuit passes high frequencies while attenuating low frequencies.

**Explanation**

The cue is to combine the two endpoint facts from the same magnitude response. We already know how the response behaves near $\omega=0$ and as $\omega\to\infty$, so the task is to turn those limits into a filter classification.

$$
\begin{aligned}|H(j\omega)|&\to0 \quad \text{as } \omega\to0\\|H(j\omega)|&\to1 \quad \text{as } \omega\to\infty\end{aligned}
$$

That means low-frequency components are strongly attenuated because their output magnitude is near zero, while high-frequency components are passed because their output magnitude is near one. The magnitude response separates the input by frequency content rather than treating all frequencies alike.

So the RC circuit is a high-pass filter: the magnitude response suppresses low frequencies and passes high frequencies.
