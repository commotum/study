## Introduction to Sinusoids as Eigenfunctions of LTI Systems

The cue is a complex exponential input, especially $x(t)=e^{j\omega t}$. For an LTI system, the point of this section is to recognize that the system does not change the oscillation into a different frequency; it returns the same oscillation multiplied by a complex scalar.

$$
T\{x(t)\}=\lambda x(t)
$$

That is what it means for $x(t)$ to behave like an eigenfunction of the system: the output is only scaled, not reshaped. Here the scalar depends on the input frequency, so the gain is written $H(j\omega)$. You already saw $H(j\omega)$ in the previous lesson, so read it here as the system's complex gain at angular frequency $\omega$.

$$
T\{e^{j\omega t}\}=H(j\omega)e^{j\omega t}
$$

The important result is that the $e^{j\omega t}$ factor stays intact, so the angular frequency does not change. Only the complex multiplier changes the amplitude and phase, which is the eigenfunction behavior you will reuse when you rewrite sinusoids in complex-exponential form.

## Checking the Eigenfunction Form for $e^{j\omega t}$

**Example:** Given $x(t)=e^{j\omega_0 t}$ and $H(j\omega_0)=3e^{-j\pi/4}$, write the output and state why this is an eigenfunction response.

**Explanation**

The cue is the same-frequency complex exponential $x(t)=e^{j\omega_0 t}$. For an LTI system, we use the eigenfunction form from the previous step: the output is the input multiplied by the complex gain at that same frequency, $H(j\omega_0)$.

$$
y(t)=H(j\omega_0)x(t)
$$

Here $H(j\omega_0)=3e^{-j\pi/4}$, so substitute that gain and keep the exponential factor unchanged.

$$
y(t)=3e^{-j\pi/4}e^{j\omega_0 t}
$$

The factor $e^{j\omega_0 t}$ is still present, so the angular frequency is unchanged. The complex multiplier changes only the amplitude and phase, which is why this is an eigenfunction response.

## Applying the Eigenfunction Form to a Cosine Input

**Example:** Given $x(t)=\cos(\omega_0 t)$ and $H(j\omega_0)=2e^{j\pi/6}$, determine the output form and show that the frequency stays $\omega_0$.

**Explanation**

The cue is a real cosine input, $x(t)=\cos(\omega_0 t)$. To use the eigenfunction form from the previous step, rewrite the cosine as a pair of complex exponentials so the system response can be applied term by term.

$$
\cos(\omega_0 t)=\frac{1}{2}\big(e^{j\omega_0 t}+e^{-j\omega_0 t}\big)
$$

At the positive frequency, the gain is $H(j\omega_0)=2e^{j\pi/6}$. For the matching negative-frequency term, use the conjugate gain $2e^{-j\pi/6}$ so the recombined output stays real.

$$
y(t)=\frac{1}{2}\big(2e^{j\pi/6}e^{j\omega_0 t}+2e^{-j\pi/6}e^{-j\omega_0 t}\big)
$$

Now recombine the two exponentials into a cosine. The frequency factor is still $\omega_0$, so only the amplitude and phase have changed.

$$
y(t)=2\cos\big(\omega_0 t+\pi/6\big)
$$

This is the eigenfunction result in real-signal form: the output is still a cosine at angular frequency $\omega_0$, but the system has scaled it by $2$ and shifted its phase by $\pi/6$.

## Applying the Eigenfunction Form to a Sine Input

**Example:** Given $x(t)=\sin(\omega_0 t)$ and $H(j\omega_0)=2e^{j\pi/6}$, determine the output form and show that the frequency stays $\omega_0$.

**Explanation**

The cue is a real sine input, $x(t)=\sin(\omega_0 t)$. To use the eigenfunction form from the previous step, rewrite the sine as a difference of complex exponentials so the system response can be applied term by term.

$$
\sin(\omega_0 t)=\frac{1}{2j}\big(e^{j\omega_0 t}-e^{-j\omega_0 t}\big)
$$

At the positive frequency, the gain is $H(j\omega_0)=2e^{j\pi/6}$. For the matching negative-frequency term, use the conjugate gain $2e^{-j\pi/6}$ so the recombined output stays real.

$$
y(t)=\frac{1}{2j}\big(2e^{j\pi/6}e^{j\omega_0 t}-2e^{-j\pi/6}e^{-j\omega_0 t}\big)
$$

Now recombine the two exponentials into a sine. The frequency factor is still $\omega_0$, so only the amplitude and phase have changed.

$$
y(t)=2\sin\big(\omega_0 t+\pi/6\big)
$$

This is the eigenfunction result in real-signal form: the output is still a sine at angular frequency $\omega_0$, but the system has scaled it by $2$ and shifted its phase by $\pi/6$.

## Recognizing Eigenfunction Behavior from Input and Output Frequency

**Example:** Given input $x(t)=\cos(4t)$ and proposed output $y(t)=3\cos(4t-\pi/6)$, decide whether the pair is consistent with eigenfunction behavior and explain the frequency check.

**Explanation**

The cue is an input-output pair, so first read the angular frequency from each sinusoid. Here the input is $x(t)=\cos(4t)$ and the proposed output is $y(t)=3\cos(4t-\pi/6)$.

$$
x(t)=\cos(4t),\qquad y(t)=3\cos(4t-\pi/6)
$$

Both signals use the same angular frequency $4$. The output changes only the amplitude, from $1$ to $3$, and the phase, by $-\pi/6$.

$$
\omega_{\text{in}}=4,\qquad \omega_{\text{out}}=4
$$

That matches eigenfunction behavior for an LTI system: the frequency stays the same, while amplitude and phase are allowed to change. So this pair is consistent with the eigenfunction form.
