## Introduction to Fourier Transform of an LTI System Output

For one continuous-time LTI system, the output is determined by the impulse response $h(t)$ and the input $x(t)$. In this lesson, we start from the standard time-domain description of that output, not from a new system model.

$$
y(t)=x(t)*h(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x(\tau)h(t-\tau)\,d\tau
$$

That convolution equation is the starting point for the frequency-domain derivation. The next steps will apply the Fourier transform to both sides and use the convolution theorem to turn the time-domain convolution into a product.

$$
Y(j\omega)=X(j\omega)H(j\omega)
$$

Here $H(j\omega)$ names the Fourier transform of $h(t)$. So the output spectrum is the input spectrum $X(j\omega)$ multiplied by the system frequency response, and the later steps will show that identification in detail.

## Writing the LTI Output as a Convolution

**Example:** Given an LTI system with input $x(t)$ and impulse response $h(t)$, write the output $y(t)$ as a convolution integral.

**Explanation**

The cue is the standard LTI setup: input $x(t)$, impulse response $h(t)$, and output $y(t)$. For an LTI system, the output is written as the convolution of the input with the impulse response.

$$
y(t)=x(t)*h(t)
$$

Expand that convolution into its integral form so the time-domain relation is explicit. Keep the roles of $x(t)$, $h(t)$, and $y(t)$ distinct.

$$
y(t)=x(t)*h(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x(\tau)h(t-\tau)\,d\tau
$$

This is the time-domain starting point for the frequency-domain derivation. The next step will transform this same equation; here the only new move is writing the LTI output in standard convolution form.

## Applying the Fourier Transform to the Convolution Equation

**Example:** Starting from $y(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x(\tau)h(t-\tau)\,d\tau$, apply the Fourier transform to both sides and write the transformed relation.

**Explanation**

The cue is the convolution equation from the previous step. Apply the Fourier transform to both sides of that equation, and name the transformed output spectrum $Y(j\omega)$ without changing the system model.

$$
\begin{aligned}
Y(j\omega) &= \mathcal{F}\{y(t)\} \\
           &= \mathcal{F}\{x(t)*h(t)\}
\end{aligned}
$$

Keep the right-hand side symbolic at this stage; the convolution theorem will simplify it in the next step. The only new move here is moving the LTI output equation into Fourier form.

## Using the Convolution Theorem on the Output Spectrum

**Example:** Take the transformed convolution relation from the previous step and simplify it using the convolution theorem.

**Explanation**

The cue is the transformed convolution relation from the previous step. Apply the convolution theorem to replace the transformed convolution with the product of the two individual transforms, while keeping the same frequency variable $j\omega$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
Y(j\omega) &= \mathcal{F}\{x(t)*h(t)\} \\
           &= X(j\omega)\,\mathcal{F}\{h(t)\}
\end{aligned}
$$

This is the frequency-domain product form of the output relation. The only new move here is using the convolution theorem to replace the transformed convolution with a product of spectra, without yet renaming the transform of $h(t)$.

## Identifying $H(j\omega)$ as the Transform of $h(t)$

**Example:** Given the impulse response $h(t)$, identify its Fourier transform $H(j\omega)$ and substitute it into the output relation.

**Explanation**

The cue is the transform of the impulse response from the previous step. Name that transform as $H(j\omega)=\mathcal{F}\{h(t)\}$, then substitute it into the frequency-domain product so the system term is written with standard notation.

$$
\begin{aligned}
H(j\omega) &= \mathcal{F}\{h(t)\} \\
Y(j\omega) &= X(j\omega)\,\mathcal{F}\{h(t)\} = X(j\omega)H(j\omega)
\end{aligned}
$$

This is the frequency-response notation for the LTI system. The input spectrum stays $X(j\omega)$, the output spectrum stays $Y(j\omega)$, and the system contribution is now isolated as $H(j\omega)$.

## Deriving $Y(j\omega)=X(j\omega)H(j\omega)$

**Example:** Starting from the convolution equation and the definition of $H(j\omega)$, derive the final relation $Y(j\omega)=X(j\omega)H(j\omega)$.

**Explanation**

The cue is the full derivation from the time-domain LTI model to the frequency-domain output formula. Start with the convolution expression, transform both sides, use the convolution theorem, and then replace $\mathcal{F}\{h(t)\}$ with $H(j\omega)$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
y(t) &= x(t)*h(t) \\
Y(j\omega) &= \mathcal{F}\{y(t)\} \\
           &= \mathcal{F}\{x(t)*h(t)\} \\
           &= X(j\omega)\mathcal{F}\{h(t)\} \\
           &= X(j\omega)H(j\omega)
\end{aligned}
$$

This completes the lesson's core move. The output spectrum is the input spectrum multiplied by the system frequency response, so the final relation is $Y(j\omega)=X(j\omega)H(j\omega)$.
