## Introduction to Aperiodic Inputs Through an LTI System

For an aperiodic input through an LTI system, start by recognizing the convolution relation $y(t)=x(t)*h(t)$. The frequency-domain method keeps the work out of the convolution integral and turns that convolution into multiplication.

$$
y(t)=x(t)*h(t)
$$

Define $X(j\omega)=\mathcal{F}\{x(t)\}$ and $H(j\omega)=\mathcal{F}\{h(t)\}$. Once those spectra are identified, the output spectrum is $Y(j\omega)=X(j\omega)H(j\omega)$, so the workflow is transform, multiply, simplify, and invert.

$$
X(j\omega)=\mathcal{F}\{x(t)\},\qquad H(j\omega)=\mathcal{F}\{h(t)\}
$$

$$
Y(j\omega)=X(j\omega)H(j\omega)
$$

In the base case, use a simple causal exponential input and a simple causal first-order impulse response. Both transforms come from the table, so you can write the product explicitly before doing any inversion.

After the product is simplified into a standard transform pair or a form that can be matched with partial fractions, recover the output with the inverse Fourier transform. The final answer should keep the expected causal shape of the original input-system pair.

$$
y(t)=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\{Y(j\omega)\}
$$

Keep the checklist fixed: transform the input and impulse response, multiply to form $Y(j\omega)$, simplify until the inverse pair is visible, and invert to get $y(t)$.

## Finding Output from a Direct Aperiodic Transform Pair

**Example:** Given $x(t)=e^{-2t}u(t)$ and $h(t)=e^{-t}u(t)$, find $y(t)$ using the Fourier transform method.

**Explanation**

The cue is a causal exponential input together with a causal first-order impulse response, so the direct path is to read both spectra from the transform table and stay in the frequency domain.

$$
X(j\omega)=\frac{1}{2+j\omega},\qquad H(j\omega)=\frac{1}{1+j\omega}
$$

Multiply the spectra to form the output spectrum. This is the only algebraic step before the inverse transform.

$$
Y(j\omega)=X(j\omega)H(j\omega)=\frac{1}{(1+j\omega)(2+j\omega)}
$$

Rewrite the product in a form that matches standard inverse-transform pairs. Here, partial fractions give a difference of two simple first-order terms.

$$
\frac{1}{(1+j\omega)(2+j\omega)}=\frac{1}{1+j\omega}-\frac{1}{2+j\omega}
$$

Invert each term and combine the result in time domain. The output stays causal, and the inverse transform produces the expected difference of exponentials.

$$
y(t)=\big(e^{-t}-e^{-2t}\big)u(t)
$$

The direct transform-pair workflow is therefore table lookup, multiply, simplify, and invert. For this aperiodic input and first-order LTI system, that gives the causal output $y(t)=\big(e^{-t}-e^{-2t}\big)u(t)$.

## Multiplying an Aperiodic Input Spectrum by $H(j\omega)$

**Example:** Given $X(j\omega)=\frac{1}{2+j\omega}$ and $H(j\omega)=\frac{2+j\omega}{3+j\omega}$, find $y(t)$.

**Explanation**

The cue is that both the input and the system are already given in the frequency domain, so the task is to form $Y(j\omega)=X(j\omega)H(j\omega)$ and simplify the product before inverting.

$$
Y(j\omega)=X(j\omega)H(j\omega)=\frac{1}{2+j\omega}\cdot\frac{2+j\omega}{3+j\omega}
$$

The factor $2+j\omega$ cancels immediately. That leaves a single first-order term, which matches a standard inverse-transform pair.

$$
Y(j\omega)=\frac{1}{3+j\omega}
$$

Now invert the simplified spectrum. Since $\frac{1}{3+j\omega}$ is the transform of a causal exponential, the output is the corresponding decaying time signal.

$$
y(t)=e^{-3t}u(t)
$$

The pattern to copy is multiply first, cancel any common factor, then invert the reduced spectrum. The time-domain answer comes only after the product has been simplified to a known pair.

## Recovering $y(t)$ from a Simplified Frequency-Domain Product

**Example:** Given $Y(j\omega)=\frac{2}{(1+j\omega)(3+j\omega)}$, find $y(t)$.

**Explanation**

The cue is a rational spectrum with two simple poles, so the job is to split it into standard first-order terms and invert each one separately.

$$
Y(j\omega)=\frac{2}{(1+j\omega)(3+j\omega)}
$$

Write the product as a partial-fraction sum with unknown coefficients, then solve for the values that reproduce the numerator.

$$
\frac{2}{(1+j\omega)(3+j\omega)}=\frac{A}{1+j\omega}+\frac{B}{3+j\omega}
$$

Matching coefficients gives $A=1$ and $B=-1$, so the spectrum becomes a difference of two standard causal terms.

$$
\frac{2}{(1+j\omega)(3+j\omega)}=\frac{1}{1+j\omega}-\frac{1}{3+j\omega}
$$

Now invert term by term. Each fraction matches the causal exponential pair, and the time-domain result is the difference of the two decays.

$$
y(t)=\big(e^{-t}-e^{-3t}\big)u(t)
$$

The pattern to copy is recognize the poles, decompose the rational function, and invert each first-order term directly. The unit step stays attached to preserve causality.

## Finding Output for a Shifted Aperiodic Input

**Example:** If $x(t)=e^{-(t-2)}u(t-2)$ and $h(t)=e^{-t}u(t)$, find $y(t)$.

**Explanation**

The cue is a delayed exponential, so treat $x(t)$ as the base signal $x_0(t)=e^{-t}u(t)$ shifted by 2. The Fourier shift property contributes the factor $e^{-j2\omega}$ to $X(j\omega)$.

$$
X(j\omega)=e^{-j2\omega}\frac{1}{1+j\omega},\qquad H(j\omega)=\frac{1}{1+j\omega}
$$

Multiply the spectra and keep the shift factor visible. The delay does not disappear during the product; it stays attached to the whole frequency-domain expression.

$$
Y(j\omega)=X(j\omega)H(j\omega)=e^{-j2\omega}\frac{1}{(1+j\omega)^2}
$$

The unshifted inverse pair is $\frac{1}{(1+j\omega)^2}\leftrightarrow t e^{-t}u(t)$. Apply the delay factor to that base result instead of rebuilding the algebra from scratch.

$$
\frac{1}{(1+j\omega)^2}\leftrightarrow t e^{-t}u(t)
$$

The delay factor shifts the whole output by 2, so the time-domain response is the base output evaluated at $t-2$ and multiplied by $u(t-2)$.

$$
y(t)=\big(t-2\big)e^{-(t-2)}u(t-2)
$$

The pattern to copy is base exponential, shift factor, multiply in frequency domain, then delay the known time-domain result. The shift moves the entire response, not just the exponential term.

## Finding Output for a Sum of Aperiodic Inputs

**Example:** Given $x(t)=e^{-t}u(t)+e^{-2t}u(t)$ and $h(t)=e^{-t}u(t)$, find $y(t)$.

**Explanation**

The cue is a sum of two causal exponentials, so split $x(t)$ into two component signals and use linearity to process each one through the same LTI system.

$$
X_1(j\omega)=\frac{1}{1+j\omega},\qquad X_2(j\omega)=\frac{1}{2+j\omega},\qquad H(j\omega)=\frac{1}{1+j\omega}
$$

Multiply each input spectrum by the same $H(j\omega)$. The two components stay separate until the very end, so each output term can be inverted on its own.

$$
Y_1(j\omega)=\frac{1}{(1+j\omega)^2},\qquad Y_2(j\omega)=\frac{1}{(1+j\omega)(2+j\omega)}
$$

Invert each product using the same transform pairs from the earlier examples. The first term gives the repeated-pole response, and the second term gives the difference of two exponentials.

$$
y_1(t)=t e^{-t}u(t),\qquad y_2(t)=\big(e^{-t}-e^{-2t}\big)u(t)
$$

Add the component outputs to recover the full response, then combine like terms if desired. Superposition applies after inversion just as it did before the transform.

$$
y(t)=y_1(t)+y_2(t)=\big((t+1)e^{-t}-e^{-2t}\big)u(t)
$$

The pattern to copy is split, solve each part through the same system, and then recombine the outputs. For a sum of aperiodic inputs, linearity keeps the work organized and the final expression causal.
