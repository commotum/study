## Introduction to Filter Interpretation from Magnitude Response

A magnitude response shows how an LTI system scales sinusoidal inputs across frequency. For this step, read only the low-frequency end and the high-frequency end of $|H(j\omega)|$.

![](<../Source/10.2.7. Filter Interpretation from Magnitude Response/Images/l007-s001-te-section-001.png>)

Use the unity line $|H(j\omega)|=1$ as the reference. Values above that line mean gain, and values below it mean attenuation.

$$
|H(j\omega_{\text{low}})| \text{ vs. } |H(j\omega_{\text{high}})|
$$

If the low-frequency end is larger than the high-frequency end, the filter is low-pass. In that case, the system passes low frequencies more strongly than high frequencies.

$$
|H(j\omega_{\text{low}})| > |H(j\omega_{\text{high}})| \Rightarrow \text{low-pass}
$$

If the high-frequency end is larger than the low-frequency end, the filter is high-pass. If neither end clearly dominates, the response is neither.

$$
|H(j\omega_{\text{low}})| < |H(j\omega_{\text{high}})| \Rightarrow \text{high-pass}
$$

That is the rule you will reuse: read the two ends, compare them to each other and to the unity line, then classify the filter. The next sections will apply the same comparison to specific plots.

$$
\text{otherwise} \Rightarrow \text{neither}
$$

## Reading Low- and High-Frequency Gain from a Magnitude Response

**Example:** From the displayed magnitude-response curve, read the low-frequency gain and the high-frequency gain and state which end is larger.

**Explanation**

A magnitude-response plot shows how the system scales a sinusoid at each frequency. The cue in this step is the height of the curve at the low-frequency end and at the high-frequency end.

![](<../Source/10.2.7. Filter Interpretation from Magnitude Response/Images/l007-s002-te-section-002.png>)

Read the left plateau as the low-frequency gain and the right plateau as the high-frequency gain. On this curve, the low-frequency end sits above the unity line, and the high-frequency end sits below it.

$$
|H(j\omega_{\text{low}})| > 1,\qquad |H(j\omega_{\text{high}})| < 1
$$

That means the low-frequency end is larger than the high-frequency end. The end-behavior comparison is the point of the example, because it gives you the same reading rule you will use again in the next step.

$$
|H(j\omega_{\text{low}})| > |H(j\omega_{\text{high}})|
$$

## Classifying a Low-Pass Filter from Magnitude Response

**Example:** For the displayed magnitude-response curve, decide whether the filter is low-pass, high-pass, or neither.

**Explanation**

The cue is the comparison between the low-frequency end and the high-frequency end of the magnitude-response curve. Start by reading the two ends, then use their ordering to classify the filter.

![](<../Source/10.2.7. Filter Interpretation from Magnitude Response/Images/l007-s003-te-section-003.png>)

Here the low-frequency end sits above the unity line, while the high-frequency end sits below it. So the low-frequency gain is larger than the high-frequency gain.

$$
|H(j\omega_{\text{low}})| > |H(j\omega_{\text{high}})|
$$

By the rule from the previous step, that end comparison means the filter is low-pass. It passes low frequencies better than high frequencies.

$$
|H(j\omega_{\text{low}})| > |H(j\omega_{\text{high}})| \Rightarrow \text{low-pass}
$$

![](<../Source/10.2.7. Filter Interpretation from Magnitude Response/Images/l007-s004-te-section-004.png>)

$$
|H(j\omega_{\text{low}})| < |H(j\omega_{\text{high}})|
$$

$$
|H(j\omega_{\text{low}})| < |H(j\omega_{\text{high}})| \Rightarrow \text{high-pass}

## Classifying Neither Low-Pass Nor High-Pass Behavior

**Example:** For the displayed magnitude-response curve, decide whether the filter is low-pass, high-pass, or neither.

**Explanation**

The cue is still the comparison between the low-frequency end and the high-frequency end. Read those two ends first, because that is what determines low-pass and high-pass behavior.

![](<../Source/10.2.7. Filter Interpretation from Magnitude Response/Images/l007-s005-te-section-005.png>)

Here the low-frequency end and the high-frequency end sit at about the same height, and both are below the unity line. The middle hump does not change that end comparison.

$$
|H(j\omega_{\text{low}})| \approx |H(j\omega_{\text{high}})| < 1
$$

Since neither end is larger, the curve does not match the low-pass pattern and it does not match the high-pass pattern either. The correct classification is neither low-pass nor high-pass.

$$
\text{neither low-pass nor high-pass}
$$
