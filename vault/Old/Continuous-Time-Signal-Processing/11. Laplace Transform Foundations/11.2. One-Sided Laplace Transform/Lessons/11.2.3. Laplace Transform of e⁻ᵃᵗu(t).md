## Introduction to Laplace Transform of $e^{-at}u(t)$

The target move is to identify the one-sided transform setup for $x(t)=e^{-at}u(t)$ and turn it into a reusable exponential transform pair.

Use the base cue as the causal exponential step: in this topic, $u(t)$ is the on-off switch that is $1$ for $t\ge0$ and $0$ for $t<0$, so the one-sided transform uses the interval from $0^-$ to infinity.

$$
\mathcal{L}_{+}\{e^{-at}u(t)\}=\int_{0^-}^{\infty} e^{-at}u(t)e^{-st}\,dt
$$

Drop $u(t)$ inside the interval $t\ge0$ by substitution and combine exponents into one weighted exponential: $e^{-(s+a)t}$.

$$
\mathcal{L}_{+}\{e^{-at}u(t)\}=\int_{0^-}^{\infty} e^{-(s+a)t}\,dt
$$

This section stops here with setup; next sections evaluate the integral to get $1/(s+a)$ and then state the convergence condition $\operatorname{Re}(s)>-a$ before extending to named examples.

## Setting Up the One-Sided Integral for $e^{-at}u(t)$

**Example:** Set up the one-sided Laplace transform integral for $x(t)=e^{-3t}u(t)$, including all limits and factors.

**Explanation**

Start from the one-sided definition as the cue for this step: for any signal $x(t)$, the transform setup is $\mathcal{L}_{+}\{x(t)\}=\int_{0^-}^{\infty}x(t)e^{-st}\,dt$.

$$
\mathcal{L}_{+}\{x(t)\}=\int_{0^-}^{\infty}x(t)e^{-st}\,dt
$$

Apply that rule to the specific cue $x(t)=e^{-3t}u(t)$ by substituting the signal into the template.

$$
\mathcal{L}_{+}\{e^{-3t}u(t)\}=\int_{0^-}^{\infty}e^{-3t}u(t)e^{-st}\,dt
$$

Because this is one-sided, keep the lower limit at $0^-$ and keep the $u(t)$ factor visible, so the causal-support rule is explicit before any integral evaluation.

$$
\mathcal{L}_{+}\{e^{-3t}u(t)\}=\int_{0^-}^{\infty}e^{-(s+3)t}\,dt
$$

This is the complete setup for the next section: the concrete case $a=3$ is now written with correct limits and one-sided notation.

## Evaluating the Integral to Obtain $1/(s+a)$

**Example:** Evaluate $\mathcal{L}_+\{e^{-2t}u(t)\}$ from the one-sided integral and simplify the result.

**Explanation**

Use the one-sided setup from the previous section as the cue: evaluate the setup for the concrete signal $x(t)=e^{-2t}u(t)$ as a definite integral, not another setup.

$$
\mathcal{L}_+\{e^{-2t}u(t)\}=\int_{0^-}^{\infty} e^{-(s+2)t}\,dt
$$

Recognize the integrand as an exponential and use the antiderivative rule for $\int e^{-(s+2)t}dt$.

$$
\int e^{-(s+2)t}dt=-\frac{1}{s+2}e^{-(s+2)t}+C
$$

Apply bounds from $0^-$ to $\infty$ and require $\operatorname{Re}(s)>-2$ so the upper limit term decays.

$$
\mathcal{L}_+\{e^{-2t}u(t)\}=\left[-\frac{1}{s+2}e^{-(s+2)t}\right]_{0^-}^{\infty}=-\frac{1}{s+2}(0)-\left(-\frac{1}{s+2}\cdot 1\right)=\frac{1}{s+2}
$$

So the worked result is $\mathcal{L}_+\{e^{-2t}u(t)\}=1/(s+2)$, which confirms the target base-pair form for the same canonical family.

<a id="determining-the-convergence-condition-operatornameres-a"></a>
## Determining the Convergence Condition $\operatorname{Re}(s)>-a$

**Example:** Determine the ROC for $\mathcal{L}_+\{e^{-at}u(t)\}=\frac{1}{s+a}$ by using the limit behavior at infinity.

**Explanation**

Use the antiderivative boundary form from the previous step as the cue, now read as a condition check rather than another algebraic manipulation.

$$
\mathcal{L}_+\{e^{-at}u(t)\}=\left[-\frac{1}{s+a}e^{-(s+a)t}\right]_{0^-}^{\infty}
$$

A finite one-sided transform requires the upper-limit term to vanish, so evaluate $\lim_{t\to\infty}e^{-(s+a)t}$.

$$
\lim_{t\to\infty}e^{-(s+a)t}=0 \iff \operatorname{Re}(s+a)>0
$$

Convert that inequality to the ROC statement in terms of $s$ by subtracting $\operatorname{Re}(a)=a$ (assuming real $a$ in this topic convention).

$$
\operatorname{Re}(s)>-a
$$

Therefore the final transform statement is $\mathcal{L}_+\{e^{-at}u(t)\}=\frac{1}{s+a},\;\operatorname{Re}(s)>-a$.

## Reading Specific Exponential Signals as $1/(s+a)$ Pairs

**Example:** Write the transform pair for $e^{-5t}u(t)$ and list its ROC.

**Explanation**

Use the base-pair template as the cue: for a signal of the form $e^{-at}u(t)$, the transform is $1/(s+a)$ with the associated one-sided ROC.

$$
\mathcal{L}_+\{e^{-at}u(t)\}=\frac{1}{s+a},\qquad \operatorname{Re}(s)>-a
$$

Match $e^{-5t}u(t)$ directly to the template and identify $a=5$.

$$
\mathcal{L}_+\{e^{-5t}u(t)\}=\frac{1}{s+5},\qquad \operatorname{Re}(s)>-5
$$

The final result is a quick recognition move: parameter substitution into the shared template and ROC transfer, not a re-derivation.
