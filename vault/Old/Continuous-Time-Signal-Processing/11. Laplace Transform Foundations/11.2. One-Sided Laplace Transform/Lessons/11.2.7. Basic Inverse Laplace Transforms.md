## Introduction to Basic Inverse Laplace Transforms

The first move in basic inverse Laplace work is pattern recognition. Read the transform expression as a cue, choose the matching one-sided template, and then write the time-domain signal with the causal support rule.

Use one of these templates: $L^{-1}(1/s)=u(t)$, $L^{-1}(A/(s+a))=A e^{-at}u(t)$, $L^{-1}(A)=A\delta(t)$, and $L^{-1}(A e^{-sT})=A\delta(t-T)$, and in each case attach $u(t)$ only when the expression is one-sided and causal.

If a candidate transform implies a time support that is not one-sided-causal, the template is not applicable in this step. For example, a shifted-impulse form with negative shift is rejected at this stage and must be corrected before inversion.

Apply the one-line checklist before writing the result: identify denominator shift for exponentials, check whether the numerator is constant for an impulse at the origin, and check whether an extra factor $e^{-sT}$ means a delayed impulse rather than an ordinary step or exponential.

$$
x(t)=L^{-1}(X(s))
$$

$$
L^{-1}(1/s)=u(t)
$$

Keep notation stable: write $L^{-1}(\cdot)$, $s$-domain symbols like $X(s)$, and time functions like $x(t)$, $u(t)$, and $\delta(t)$ in the same form through later examples and future practice.

## Inverting the Unit-Step Pair

**Example:** Find the inverse one-sided transform of $X(s)=1/s$.

**Explanation**

The cue in this first inverse example is a pure unit-step form: $X(s)=1/s$. Match this transform directly to the stored base pair rule.

Use the direct substitution rule for this template and write the inverse without adding any scale or shift terms.

$$
L^{-1}(1/s)=u(t)
$$

Apply the one-sided causal support convention, so the time-domain result is active only for nonnegative time and zero before $t=0$.

$$
x(t)=u(t)
$$

## Inverting One-Sided Exponential Pairs

**Example:** Find the inverse one-sided transform of $X(s)=4/(s+3)$.

**Explanation**

The cue is that the denominator is shifted: $X(s)=\frac{4}{s+3}$, so this is an exponential-pair inversion, not a step or impulse pair.

Match the transform to the template $A/(s+a)$ and read off the parameters $A=4$ and $a=3$.

$$
\mathcal{L}^{-1}\left(\frac{A}{s+a}\right)=Ae^{-at}u(t)
$$

Apply the one-sided support rule and substitute the values: keep $u(t)$ for causality, then attach the mapped exponential decay.

$$
x(t)=4e^{-3t}u(t)
$$

So the inverse transform is $x(t)=4e^{-3t}u(t)$, which keeps the same cue and only adds the denominator-shift parameterization.

## Inverting the Unit-Impulse Pair

**Example:** Find the inverse one-sided transform of $X(s)=1$.

**Explanation**

The cue is a pure constant transform: $X(s)=1$. In this sequence, this indicates the unit-impulse template, not a step or exponential form.

Use the direct constant-to-impulse inversion rule and map the constant directly to the origin pulse.

$$
L^{-1}(1)=\delta(t)
$$

Then preserve one-sided causality by keeping the support at the origin only, with no step factor and no time-shift factor added.

$$
x(t)=\delta(t)
$$

So the final inverse is $x(t)=\delta(t)$, which is the one-sided inverse of a constant $X(s)$.

## Inverting Shifted-Impulse Pairs

**Example:** Find the inverse one-sided transform of $X(s)=2e^{-4s}$.

**Explanation**

The cue is a delayed-impulse factor: $X(s)=2e^{-4s}$, so this is not a step or exponential pair but the shifted-impulse template.

Match the expression to $A e^{-sT}$ and read $A=2$, $T=4$ as the coefficient and delay.

$$
L^{-1}(A e^{-sT})=A\,\delta(t-T),\quad T\ge 0
$$

Substitute $A=2$, $T=4$ and keep the shift positive so the support is at $t=4$ in the one-sided convention.

$$
x(t)=2\delta(t-4)
$$

So the final inverse is $x(t)=2\delta(t-4)$, which is the delayed impulse form under $T\ge 0$ support.

## Choosing Among Basic One-Sided Transform Pairs

**Example:** Classify and invert $X(s)=e^{-s}(1+4/(s+2))$ by selecting the best-fit basic one-sided pair.

**Explanation**

The cue is a delayed product: the outer factor is $e^{-s}$, so classify first by delay before choosing among the internal templates.

Use one-sided delay first: $L^{-1}(e^{-s}F(s))=f(t-1)u(t-1)$, so determine $f(s)$ from the bracketed factor $1+4/(s+2)$.

$$
\mathcal{L}^{-1}\left(e^{-s}F(s)\right)=f(t-1)u(t-1)
$$

Inside the bracket, split into $1$ and $4/(s+2)$. The constant maps to $\delta(t)$, and $4/(s+2)$ maps to $4e^{-2t}u(t)$.

$$
L^{-1}(1)=\delta(t),\qquad L^{-1}\left(\frac{4}{s+2}\right)=4e^{-2t}u(t)
$$

Apply the same $t\to t-1$ delay to each component because of the lead $e^{-s}$ factor.

$$
x(t)=\delta(t-1)+4e^{-2(t-1)}u(t-1)
$$

The final inverse is a shifted impulse plus a shifted causal exponential, both with support controlled by $u(t-1)$ where the step-based piece is shown in delayed form.
