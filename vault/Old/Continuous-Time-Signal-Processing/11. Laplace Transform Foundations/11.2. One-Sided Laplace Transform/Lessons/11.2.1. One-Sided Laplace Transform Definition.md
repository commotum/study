## Introduction to One-Sided Laplace Transform Definition

In causal signal and system analysis, we start from right-sided behavior, so the one-sided Laplace transform is the direct map used when signals and inputs are modeled on $t \ge 0^-$ rather than on the entire real line.

The complex frequency variable is written as $s=\sigma+j\omega$, and it is the independent frequency-domain coordinate that the time signal $x(t)$ is converted into.

$$
s=\\sigma+j\\omega
$$

The one-sided definition is $X(s)=\int_{0^-}^{\infty} x(t)e^{-st}\,dt$: we identify the time-domain signal $x(t)$, the exponential kernel $e^{-st}$, and the differential element $dt$ before evaluating anything.

$$
X(s)=\int_{0^-}^{\infty} x(t)e^{-st}\,dt
$$

Use $0^-$, not just $0$, so behavior at the origin is included correctly (such as impulses or jumps) while preserving the causal setup used for initial conditions.

Compared with the bilateral definition, the one-sided transform uses lower limit $0^-$ as its domain anchor, then keeps the same kernel and complex variable $s$, so the structural difference is the causal interval choice.

## Identifying the Time-Domain Signal and $s$-Domain Transform

**Example:** Write the one-sided Laplace setup for $x(t)=u(t)$ and identify the corresponding $X(s)$ setup notation.

**Explanation**

For this worked example, identify the time-domain signal from the prompt and map it directly into the one-sided transform definition; the move is recognition first, setup second, no integration yet.

$$
X(s)=\int_{0^-}^{\infty} x(t)e^{-st}\,dt
$$

The cue is $x(t)=u(t)$, which is causal by definition, so the target one-sided form still uses the same lower limit $0^-$ used in the section core move.

$$
X(s)=\int_{0^-}^{\infty} u(t)e^{-st}\,dt
$$

That expression is the required output: the time-domain signal is now written as $u(t)$, the weighting term is $e^{-st}$, and the differential is over $dt$ from $0^-$ to $\infty$; this preserves the causal one-sided transform setup for later evaluation and for the upcoming multiple-choice practice.

## Setting the One-Sided Integration Limits

**Example:** Set the integral limits for $X(s)$ when $x(t)=e^{-2t}u(t)$ and show why the lower limit is written $0^-$.

**Explanation**

The goal is to choose the one-sided integration interval before any algebraic simplification: the signal is causal and the transform setup must therefore keep the causal lower bound.

From the prompt, identify the cue $x(t)=e^{-2t}u(t)$, which is nonzero only for $t \ge 0$, so the time-domain support implies the one-sided interval rule $t \ge 0^-$.

$$
X(s)=\int_{0^-}^{\infty} x(t)e^{-st}\,dt
$$

Substitute $x(t)=e^{-2t}u(t)$ into the definition and keep both $0^-$ and the exponential weighting unchanged in place.

$$
X(s)=\int_{0^-}^{\infty} e^{-2t}u(t)e^{-st}\,dt
$$

Write the final result as $X(s)=\int_{0^-}^{\infty} e^{-2t}u(t)e^{-st}\,dt$. The $0^-$ lower limit is required so behavior at the origin is included in the causal convention used by the one-sided transform.

<a id="applying-the-exponential-weighting-factor"></a>
## Applying the Exponential Weighting Factor

**Example:** Write the one-sided integral for $x(t)=3e^{-t}u(t)$ showing the weighted integrand $x(t)e^{-st}$ clearly.

**Explanation**

The cue now is $x(t)=3e^{-t}u(t)$, and the task is to apply the one-sided rule from earlier with the exponential weighting term explicit.

$$
X(s)=\int_{0^-}^{\infty} x(t)e^{-st}\,dt
$$

Substitute $x(t)=3e^{-t}u(t)$ directly into the template and multiply by the kernel $e^{-st}$.

$$
X(s)=\int_{0^-}^{\infty} 3e^{-t}u(t)e^{-st}\,dt
$$

Combine exponentials to make the weighted integrand explicit before integration: $3e^{-t}e^{-st}=3e^{-(s+1)t}$, so the setup is $3u(t)e^{-(s+1)t}$ inside the one-sided integral.

$$
X(s)=\int_{0^-}^{\infty} 3e^{-(s+1)t}u(t)\,dt
$$

The final weighted-setup result is $X(s)=\int_{0^-}^{\infty} 3e^{-(s+1)t}u(t)\,dt$, with bounds unchanged at $0^-$ and $\infty$.

## Writing the Complete One-Sided Laplace Transform Setup

**Example:** Write the complete one-sided Laplace definition for $x(t)=u(t)$ as a final setup expression.

**Explanation**

Start from the target form in the required order: signal, kernel, and integral bounds in one line.

$$
X(s)=\int_{0^-}^{\infty} x(t)e^{-st}\,dt
$$

Substitute the signal $x(t)=u(t)$ from the cue and keep the same causal interval $0^-\le t < \infty$.

$$
X(s)=\int_{0^-}^{\infty} u(t)e^{-st}\,dt
$$

Verify there is no missing kernel term, no wrong integral limit, and no dropped one-sided qualifier: all three must appear together in the final template.

Final setup (correct form): $X(s)=\int_{0^-}^{\infty} u(t)e^{-st}\,dt$. A common confusion is using bilateral bounds, omitting $0^-$, or writing only $e^{-t}$ without the full $e^{-st}$ weight.
