## Introduction to Combining Simple Transform Pairs

Use the one-sided Laplace transform linearity rule with controlled term matching: identify the exact time-domain terms first, then map each one to a known transform before writing any composite result.

For this lesson, the core action is matching pairs and carrying coefficients and signs unchanged across domains. Start from the direct goal, $\mathcal{L}\{a_1x_1(t)+a_2x_2(t)\}=a_1X_1(s)+a_2X_2(s)$, then execute each term in order.

$$
\mathcal{L}\{a_1x_1(t)+a_2x_2(t)\}=a_1X_1(s)+a_2X_2(s)
$$

Use the base case from this step: $x(t)=e^{-t}u(t)+2u(t)$. Match $e^{-t}u(t)$ to $\frac{1}{s+1}$ and $2u(t)$ to $\frac{2}{s}$, then combine by keeping the coefficient $2$ attached to the constant term and $+$ signs attached to their terms.

Apply the same idea in the inverse direction when the expression is given in the $s$-domain. Match each known transform term to its time signal and add the reconstructed terms with the original multipliers and signs.

$$
\mathcal{L}^{-1}\{A_1X_1(s)+A_2X_2(s)\}=A_1x_1(t)+A_2x_2(t)
$$

Do not simplify or recombine the sum before matching: the highest-frequency error here is merging terms too early and then misplacing a sign or coefficient.

This topic uses the same move in two directions: matching in the time domain to build $X(s)$, and matching in the $s$-domain to build $x(t)$.

## Matching Time-Domain Sum Terms to Known Laplace Pairs

**Example:** Find the one-sided Laplace transform of $x(t)=3u(t)-2e^{-2t}u(t)$.

**Explanation**

Start by reading the expression as a termwise match, not a single combined transform. The cue is this form: $x(t)=3u(t)+(-2)e^{-2t}u(t)$, so each add/subtract term must be matched separately to a known one-sided pair.

Match the first term first: $3u(t)$ maps from $u(t)$, whose one-sided transform is $\frac{1}{s}$, so $3u(t)$ maps to $\frac{3}{s}$. Keep the scalar with that term.

Match the second term next with the sign carried through: $e^{-2t}u(t)$ maps to $\frac{1}{s+2}$, so $-2e^{-2t}u(t)$ maps to $-\frac{2}{s+2}$.

$$
\mathcal{L}_{+}\{3u(t)\}=\frac{3}{s}, \quad \mathcal{L}_{+}\{-2e^{-2t}u(t)\}=-\frac{2}{s+2}
$$

Apply one-sided linearity and write the composite result directly as the weighted sum: $X(s)=\frac{3}{s}-\frac{2}{s+2}$. Leave it in this separated term form for the next steps.

## Applying Coefficients and Signs to Build a Composite Transform

**Example:** Find $X(s)$ for $x(t)=2e^{-t}u(t)-\frac{1}{2}u(t)+3e^{-4t}u(t)$.

**Explanation**

Use the same cue as before: treat $x(t)$ as a sum of independent causal terms and apply one-sided linearity term-by-term before forming one final expression. Here $x(t)=2e^{-t}u(t)-\frac{1}{2}u(t)+3e^{-4t}u(t)$ has three terms to match.

Match each term independently. From $e^{-t}u(t)\leftrightarrow\frac{1}{s+1}$, $u(t)\leftrightarrow\frac{1}{s}$, and $e^{-4t}u(t)\leftrightarrow\frac{1}{s+4}$, so the transforms are ready to receive each coefficient and sign.

$$
\mathcal{L}_{+}\{e^{-t}u(t)\}=\frac{1}{s+1},\quad \mathcal{L}_{+}\{u(t)\}=\frac{1}{s},\quad \mathcal{L}_{+}\{e^{-4t}u(t)\}=\frac{1}{s+4}
$$

Apply coefficients and signs directly to each numerator: $2e^{-t}u(t)\leftrightarrow \frac{2}{s+1}$, $-\frac{1}{2}u(t)\leftrightarrow -\frac{1}{2s}$, and $3e^{-4t}u(t)\leftrightarrow \frac{3}{s+4}$.

Assemble the weighted transform sum in separated form so coefficient errors stay visible: $X(s)=\frac{2}{s+1}-\frac{1}{2s}+\frac{3}{s+4}$. This is the correct final structure for this section; simplification is not required yet.

## Matching s-Domain Sum Terms to Known Inverse Pairs

**Example:** Find $x(t)$ for $X(s)=\frac{3}{s}+\frac{2}{s+1}-\frac{1}{s+4}$.

**Explanation**

Use the inverse direction of the same matching move: identify each separated term in $X(s)$ first, then invert each term independently. The cue is to classify each piece as $\frac{1}{s}$, $\frac{1}{s+a}$, or a signed variant of that form.

Split the expression as $\frac{3}{s}+\frac{2}{s+1}-\frac{1}{s+4}$. This is one-to-one matching to the inverse templates with no extra algebra yet.

$$
\mathcal{L}_{+}^{-1}\left\{\frac{1}{s}\right\}=u(t),\quad \mathcal{L}_{+}^{-1}\left\{\frac{1}{s+a}\right\}=e^{-at}u(t)
$$

Apply the inverse rule term by term: $\frac{3}{s}\leftrightarrow 3u(t)$, $\frac{2}{s+1}\leftrightarrow 2e^{-t}u(t)$, and $-\frac{1}{s+4}\leftrightarrow -e^{-4t}u(t)$.

$$
x(t)=3u(t)+2e^{-t}u(t)-e^{-4t}u(t)
$$

Recombine the inverse terms with the same additive structure and preserved signs: $x(t)=3u(t)+2e^{-t}u(t)-e^{-4t}u(t)$. This keeps the mapping visible for the next practice question set.

## Recombining Inverse Pair Terms into a Time-Domain Signal

**Example:** Find $x(t)$ for $X(s)=\frac{5}{s+2}-\frac{3}{s}-\frac{2}{s+2}$.

**Explanation**

Use inverse matching as a terminal move: each term in $X(s)$ maps separately, then matching time-domain terms are recombined with linearity preserved.

Split the expression into identifiable parts: $\frac{5}{s+2}$, $-\frac{3}{s}$, and $-\frac{2}{s+2}$. This is a controlled repeat of $s+2$ that checks coefficient grouping before final simplification.

$$
\mathcal{L}_{+}^{-1}\left\{\frac{1}{s+a}\right\}=e^{-at}u(t),\quad \mathcal{L}_{+}^{-1}\left\{\frac{1}{s}\right\}=u(t)
$$

Invert each term: $\frac{5}{s+2}\leftrightarrow 5e^{-2t}u(t)$, $-\frac{3}{s}\leftrightarrow -3u(t)$, and $-\frac{2}{s+2}\leftrightarrow -2e^{-2t}u(t)$.

$$
x(t)=5e^{-2t}u(t)-3u(t)-2e^{-2t}u(t)
$$

Recombine like terms and keep one-sided support explicit: $x(t)=\left(5-2\right)e^{-2t}u(t)-3u(t)$, so the final form is $x(t)=3e^{-2t}u(t)-3u(t)$.
