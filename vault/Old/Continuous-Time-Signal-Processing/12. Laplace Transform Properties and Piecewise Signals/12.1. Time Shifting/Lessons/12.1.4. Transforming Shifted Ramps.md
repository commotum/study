## Introduction to Transforming Shifted Ramps

The key object for this topic is the shifted ramp in delayed causal form: whenever a ramp term can be written as $f(t-a)u(t-a)$, it is ready for one-sided time-shifting. In this lesson, that form is the cue to use the exact transform rule rather than re-deriving an integral each time.

Use the base seed first: the ramp with no shift is $t u(t)$, and its one-sided transform is the reference template for this section.

$$
\mathcal{L}\{t u(t)\}=\frac{1}{s^2}
$$

Now apply the shifted-ramp action in one line: rewrite delayed ramps as $f(t-a)u(t-a)$ and then apply $\mathcal{L}\{f(t-a)u(t-a)\}=e^{-a s}F(s)$. The substitution is $\tau=t-a$, and that same shift amount $a$ is the one that appears in the exponential multiplier in the transform.

$$
\mathcal{L}\{f(t-a)u(t-a)\}=e^{-a s}F(s)
$$

Before moving to examples, avoid three common failure points: use the wrong sign in the delay ($a$ should match the signal’s activation time), drop the unit step factor $u(t-a)$, or attach $e^{-a s}$ to the wrong base transform term. Keep these checks in this order: verify $a$, verify $f(t-a)$, then verify one-sided support with $u(t-a)$.

## Transforming a Basic Delayed Ramp

**Example:** Find $\mathcal{L}\{(t-2)u(t-2)\}$.

**Explanation**

The cue here is to first make the signal match the delayed canonical form $f(t-a)u(t-a)$; for this example, the expression already has that structure with $a=2$ and local base $f(t)=t$.

From the base seed, we use the known one-sided transform of a simple ramp:

$$
\mathcal{L}\{t u(t)\}=\frac{1}{s^2}
$$

Apply the time-shifting theorem directly: multiply the base transform by $e^{-a s}$ with the same delay $a=2$ that appears in the step gate.

$$
\mathcal{L}\{f(t-a)u(t-a)\}=e^{-a s}F(s)
$$

Substitute $f(t)=t$, $a=2$, and $F(s)=1/s^2$ to get

$$
\mathcal{L}\{(t-2)u(t-2)\}=e^{-2s}\frac{1}{s^2} = \frac{e^{-2s}}{s^2}
$$

Keep the delayed-ramp template exactly in this order for the next step: identify $a$, confirm one-sided support with $u(t-a)$, then apply $e^{-a s}$ to the base ramp seed.

## Transforming a Scaled Delayed Ramp

**Example:** Find $\mathcal{L}\{3(t-4)u(t-4)\}$.

**Explanation**

The cue is to match the delayed-ramp template $3(t-4)u(t-4)=3f(t-4)u(t-4)$ with the local seed $f(t)=tu(t)$.

Keep the scalar outside the transform first, so linearity gives the separation of the gain:

$$
\mathcal{L}\{3f(t-4)u(t-4)\}=3\,\mathcal{L}\{f(t-4)u(t-4)\}
$$

Then apply the one-sided shift rule to the seed base term. The same delay $a=4$ appears in both the local-time argument and the unit-step gate.

$$
\mathcal{L}\{f(t-a)u(t-a)\}=e^{-as}F(s)
$$

For $f(t)=tu(t)$, we use the base transform $\mathcal{L}\{t u(t)\}=1/s^2$, then attach the delay factor $e^{-4s}$.

$$
\mathcal{L}\{3(t-4)u(t-4)\}=3e^{-4s}\,\frac{1}{s^2}=\frac{3e^{-4s}}{s^2}
$$

## Transforming a Delayed Ramp with a Constant Offset

**Example:** Find $\mathcal{L}\{(t-2+5)u(t-2)\}$.

**Explanation**

The cue is to put the expression into local delayed pieces so we can apply the same one-sided shift template term by term.

$$
(t-2+5)u(t-2)=(t-2)u(t-2)+5u(t-2)
$$

Use linearity to transform each piece separately with the same delay $a=2$.

For the ramp piece, use the delayed-ramp seed rule:

$$
\mathcal{L}\{(t-a)u(t-a)\}=\frac{e^{-as}}{s^2},\quad a\ge 0
$$

For the delayed constant piece, use:

$$
\mathcal{L}\{u(t-a)\}=\frac{e^{-as}}{s},\quad a\ge 0
$$

Substitute $a=2$ and combine in the same delayed factor form.

$$
\mathcal{L}\{(t-2+5)u(t-2)\}=\frac{e^{-2s}}{s^2}+\frac{5e^{-2s}}{s}=e^{-2s}\left(\frac{1}{s^2}+\frac{5}{s}\right)
$$

That final factorized form preserves the same delay mechanism while making the ramp and offset contributions explicit.

## Transforming Sums of Shifted Ramp and Step Terms

**Example:** Find $\mathcal{L}\{(t-2)u(t-2)+4u(t-2)-u(t-5)\}$.

**Explanation**

The cue is to separate each delayed term because linearity is valid only after every part is in a shifted canonical form.

Write the signal as a sum of a delayed ramp seed and two delayed constants:

$$
(t-2)u(t-2)+4u(t-2)-u(t-5)=1\cdot (t-2)u(t-2)+4\,u(t-2)-1\cdot u(t-5)
$$

Apply the delayed-ramp rule to the first term with $a=2$ and the delayed-step rule to the two step terms with their own delays.

$$
\mathcal{L}\{(t-a)u(t-a)\}=\frac{e^{-as}}{s^2},\quad a\ge0
$$

$$
\mathcal{L}\{u(t-a)\}=\frac{e^{-as}}{s},\quad a\ge0
$$

Substitute the identified delays and coefficients.

$$
\mathcal{L}\{(t-2)u(t-2)\}=\frac{e^{-2s}}{s^2},\quad \mathcal{L}\{4u(t-2)\}=\frac{4e^{-2s}}{s},\quad \mathcal{L}\{u(t-5)\}=\frac{e^{-5s}}{s}
$$

$$
\mathcal{L}\{(t-2)u(t-2)+4u(t-2)-u(t-5)\}=\frac{e^{-2s}}{s^2}+\frac{4e^{-2s}}{s}-\frac{e^{-5s}}{s}=e^{-2s}\left(\frac{1}{s^2}+\frac{4}{s}\right)-\frac{e^{-5s}}{s}
$$

Keep this structure: transform each delayed piece separately, then combine by common delay factors if possible.
