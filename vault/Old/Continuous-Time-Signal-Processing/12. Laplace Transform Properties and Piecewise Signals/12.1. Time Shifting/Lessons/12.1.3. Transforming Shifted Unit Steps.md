## Introduction to Transforming Shifted Unit Steps

In one-sided Laplace work, the anchor object is the causal step $u(t)$, whose transform is read as $L\{u(t)\}=\frac{1}{s}$. That base pair is the template your future shifted-step rules must reuse.

$$
L\{u(t)\}=\frac{1}{s}
$$

A shifted unit step is written as $u(t-a)$ with $a\ge 0$. The value of $a$ is the time offset where the signal turns on; this same $a$ is the multiplier in the exponential delay factor in the Laplace result.

The direct rule is: if a signal matches $u(t-a)$, then $u(t-a) \leftrightarrow \frac{e^{-as}}{s}$. This is the exact move for this lesson: read the shift in time notation and immediately write the exponential delay marker in $s$-space.

$$
L\{u(t-a)\}=\frac{e^{-as}}{s},\quad a\ge 0
$$

Use the base case pattern to stay consistent: for $a=0$, $u(t)$ gives $\frac{1}{s}$, and for $a=2$, $u(t-2)$ gives $\frac{e^{-2s}}{s}$. The structure is unchanged; only the delay $a$ in $u(t-a)$ changes the exponential term.

## Transforming a Single Shifted Unit Step

**Example:** Find the one-sided Laplace transform of $u(t-2)$.

**Explanation**

The cue in this example is the shifted-step form $u(t-a)$, so the delay parameter is read directly from the argument and the signal is recognized as a one-sided step shifted by $a=2$.

Use the delayed-step transform pair for one-sided Laplace transforms: $u(t-a) \leftrightarrow \frac{e^{-as}}{s}$, with $a\ge 0$.

$$
\mathcal{L}\{u(t-a)\}=\frac{e^{-as}}{s},\quad a\ge 0
$$

Apply the pair with $a=2$ by substitution into the rule.

$$
\mathcal{L}\{u(t-2)\}=\frac{e^{-2s}}{s}
$$

Final check: the exponent is negative ($e^{-2s}$), and the denominator remains $s$ because the base transform is the unit step, consistent with one-sided Laplace form.

## Matching the Shift Amount to the Exponential Factor

**Example:** Find the inverse Laplace transform of $e^{-3s}/s$.

**Explanation**

This example switches direction from the prior forward-transform work: we now start with a Laplace-domain expression and identify the delayed-step time signal.

The cue is the factorized form $e^{-as}/s$, so we match it to the pair $\mathcal{L}^{-1}\{e^{-as}/s\}=u(t-a)$ with $a \ge 0$.

$$
\mathcal{L}^{-1}\{e^{-as}/s\}=u(t-a),\quad a\ge 0
$$

Read the exponent as the delay marker: $e^{-3s}$ means $a=3$. The remaining factor is the base transform $1/s$, the one-sided transform of $u(t)$.

$$
\mathcal{L}^{-1}\{e^{-3s}/s\}=u(t-3)
$$

Final result: $\mathcal{L}^{-1}\{e^{-3s}/s\}=u(t-3)$. This keeps a negative sign in the exponent and maps to a delay to the right by 3 seconds, as the one-sided convention requires.

## Transforming Scaled Shifted Unit Steps

**Example:** Find the one-sided Laplace transform of $-2u(t-4)$.

**Explanation**

This step keeps the same delay pattern and adds one new wrinkle: a scalar multiplier outside the shifted step.

Use the solution template from previous steps to identify the delayed step template and the delay index. Here the signal is $-2u(t-4)=(-2)\cdot u(t-4)$, so the shifted-step part is $u(t-4)$ with $a=4$.

Apply the delayed-step pair to the step component first: $u(t-a) \leftrightarrow e^{-as}/s$ with $a\ge 0$, so $u(t-4) \leftrightarrow e^{-4s}/s$.

$$
u(t-a) \leftrightarrow \frac{e^{-as}}{s},\quad a\ge 0
$$

Then keep the outside constant as a multiplicative gain and attach it to the transform result: $\mathcal{L}\{-2u(t-4)\} = -2\,\mathcal{L}\{u(t-4)\}$.

$$
\mathcal{L}\{-2u(t-4)\} = -2\cdot\frac{e^{-4s}}{s}
$$

Final result: $\mathcal{L}\{-2u(t-4)\}=-\dfrac{2e^{-4s}}{s}$. The delay marker $e^{-4s}$ and the coefficient $-2$ both remain, so neither the direction of the delay nor the one-sided step support is changed.

## Transforming Sums of Shifted Unit Steps

**Example:** Find the one-sided Laplace transform of $u(t-1)+2u(t-3)-u(t-5)$.

**Explanation**

This example adds one new wrinkle: the signal is a sum of shifted steps, but each term still uses the same delayed-step pattern.

Split the signal into separate terms and identify each delay: $u(t-1)$ with $a=1$, $2u(t-3)$ with $a=3$, and $-u(t-5)$ with $a=5$.

For each term, apply the delayed-step pair $u(t-a) \leftrightarrow e^{-as}/s$, which is valid for one-sided transforms.

$$
u(t-1) \leftrightarrow \frac{e^{-s}}{s},\quad 2u(t-3) \leftrightarrow 2\frac{e^{-3s}}{s},\quad -u(t-5) \leftrightarrow -\frac{e^{-5s}}{s}
$$

Now combine by linearity, keeping each delay and denominator separate so each term stays in the same transformed form.

$$
\mathcal{L}\{u(t-1)+2u(t-3)-u(t-5)\}=\frac{e^{-s}}{s}+\frac{2e^{-3s}}{s}-\frac{e^{-5s}}{s}
$$

Final result: $\mathcal{L}\{u(t-1)+2u(t-3)-u(t-5)\}=\dfrac{e^{-s}+2e^{-3s}-e^{-5s}}{s}$. Each term preserves its own delay marker and coefficient, with only the algebraic summation happening in the transform domain.
