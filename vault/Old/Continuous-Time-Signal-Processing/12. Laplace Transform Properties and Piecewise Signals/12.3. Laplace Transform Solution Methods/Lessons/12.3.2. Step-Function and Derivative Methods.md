## Introduction to Step-Function and Derivative Methods

This section uses the one-sided Laplace transform, so all computations are anchored at the causal domain $t \ge 0$. Keep that boundary explicit, and if you use a derivative-based transform, include the left-limit term $x(0^-)$ in the transform formula.

$$
\mathcal{L}\{x(t)\}=\int_0^\infty x(t)e^{-st}\,dt
$$

Start with the base signal $x(t)=u(t)-u(t-2)$ and keep it fully symbolic before expanding. The goal is to build the transform from a clean decomposition and then apply the same pattern to longer piecewise expressions.

Method A is step-function rewriting: split the signal into shifted steps and transform each term directly from the step template. For this signal, write $x(t)=u(t)-u(t-2)$ so the transform is computed term-by-term. Method B is derivative-first: differentiate the signal and use $\mathcal{L}\{x'(t)\}=sX(s)-x(0^-)$, which is often useful when slopes or piece changes create many segments.

$$
\mathcal{L}\{u(t-a)\}=\frac{e^{-as}}{s}
$$

$$
\mathcal{L}\{x'(t)\}=sX(s)-x(0^-)
$$

Use the method that does fewer transformations on paper: if the signal already has clear delayed-step boundaries, use the step rewrite; if breakpoints imply repeated slope transitions or derivative structure, use the derivative method. This step is the route selector for the rest of the lesson.

The section sequence now moves from step-function construction, to shifted-step transforms, to derivative-driven transforms, then to impulses from jumps and the initial-value correction in $\mathcal{L}\{x'(t)\}$ terms.

## Choosing a Step-Function Rewrite for a Piecewise Signal

**Example:** Write and transform a step-form expression for $x(t)=\begin{cases}1,&0\le t<2\\3,&2\le t<5\\0,&t\ge5\end{cases}$.

**Explanation**

The cue is a three-interval piecewise function with only boundary jumps at $t=2$ and $t=5$. Start by writing the waveform as a sum of delayed step differences so the active interval logic is explicit and preserves causality.

Use the step pattern for two windows: $u(t)$ turns on at $0$, $u(t-2)$ turns on at $2$, and $u(t-5)$ turns on at $5$. Combine them so $x(t)=1\cdot u(t)-1\cdot u(t-2)+3\cdot [u(t-2)-u(t-5)]$ for $t\ge0$.

$$
x(t)=u(t)-u(t-2)+3\left(u(t-2)-u(t-5)\right)
$$

Apply linearity, then the shifted-step transform rule term-by-term. This keeps the transform low-load because each term is already in a known basis.

$$
\mathcal{L}\{u(t-a)\}=\frac{e^{-as}}{s},\quad a\ge0
$$

$$
X(s)=\frac{1}{s}-\frac{e^{-2s}}{s}+\frac{3e^{-2s}}{s}-\frac{3e^{-5s}}{s}
$$

Group terms to show the final form clearly and check interval signs against boundaries.

$$
X(s)=\frac{1-e^{-2s}}{s}+\frac{3\left(e^{-2s}-e^{-5s}\right)}{s}
$$

Final result: the first pair of shifts produces value $1$ from $0\le t<2$, and the weighted pair produces value $3$ on $2\le t<5$. For $t\ge5$, both pairs cancel to zero, matching the target signal.

## Transforming Shifted Step Terms with Time Shifting

**Example:** Find $X(s)$ for $x(t)=2u(t-1)-u(t-3)+u(t-5)$ and simplify the shifted-step transform result.

**Explanation**

The cue here is a sum of delayed unit-step terms. Because each term is already in shifted-step form, this example applies the time-shifting rule directly, term by term.

List the pieces and signs exactly: $+2u(t-1)$ starts at $t=1$, $-u(t-3)$ starts at $t=3$, and $+u(t-5)$ starts at $t=5$ with signs that must stay attached to their shifts.

$$
x(t)=2u(t-1)-u(t-3)+u(t-5)
$$

Apply linearity and the one-sided step identity to each term: for each $a\ge0$, use $\mathcal{L}\{u(t-a)\}=\frac{e^{-as}}{s}$. Keep the three terms separate during substitution.

$$
X(s)=2\frac{e^{-1\cdot s}}{s}-\frac{e^{-3s}}{s}+\frac{e^{-5s}}{s}
$$

Now simplify only the delay constants and write the compact result. This keeps the exponent signs explicit and prevents the common mistake of flipping $e^{-as}$ into $e^{+as}$.

$$
X(s)=\frac{2e^{-s}}{s}-\frac{e^{-3s}}{s}+\frac{e^{-5s}}{s},\quad \operatorname{Re}\{s\}>0.
$$

Sanity check: each term has the same $1/s$ base factor and only delay factors in exponentials. As $s\to\infty$, each $e^{-as}/s\to0$, so the high-frequency tail decays as expected for delayed causal steps.

Result: the transform is a signed sum of delayed-step transforms, and the next questions should mirror this pattern by varying coefficients, signs, and one new shift at a time.

## Choosing a Derivative Method for a Piecewise Linear Signal

**Example:** Use the derivative property on the piecewise linear signal $x(t)=\begin{cases}0,0\le t<1\\t-1,1\le t<3\\2t-4,3\le t<5\\4, t\ge5\end{cases}$.

**Explanation**

The cue is a piecewise linear signal with slope changes at fixed breakpoints. Choose the derivative method: one causal derivative handles each slope region with fewer integrals than direct piecewise integration.

Differentiate each interval expression: on $0\le t<1$, $x'(t)=0$; on $1\le t<3$, $x'(t)=1$; on $3\le t<5$, $x'(t)=2$; and for $t\ge5$, $x'(t)=0$.

Now convert the slope pattern to step-weighted constants. To gate three active regions, use $x'(t)=1[u(t-1)-u(t-3)]+2[u(t-3)-u(t-5)]$, which simplifies to $x'(t)=u(t-1)+u(t-3)-2u(t-5)$.

Take the one-sided Laplace transform term-by-term and keep one shifted basis per region breakpoint.

$$
\mathcal{L}\{u(t-a)\}=\frac{e^{-as}}{s},\quad a\ge 0
$$

$$
\mathcal{L}\{x'(t)\}=\frac{e^{-s}}{s}+\frac{e^{-3s}}{s}-\frac{2e^{-5s}}{s}
$$

Apply the one-sided derivative rule $\mathcal{L}\{x'(t)\}=sX(s)-x(0^-)$ and use $x(0^-)=0$ for this causal signal.

$$
sX(s)=\frac{e^{-s}}{s}+\frac{e^{-3s}}{s}-\frac{2e^{-5s}}{s},\quad X(s)=\frac{e^{-s}+e^{-3s}-2e^{-5s}}{s^2}
$$

Check the interval boundaries: $x(1^-)=x(1^+)=0$, $x(3^-)=x(3^+)=2$, and $x(5^-)=x(5^+)=4$. Continuity means no extra impulse corrections are added in this step.

Final result: $X(s)=\dfrac{e^{-s}+e^{-3s}-2e^{-5s}}{s^2}$, and this expression is the compact target form for the next practice model with one added slope wrinkle.

<a id="accounting-for-impulses-from-jump-discontinuities"></a>
## Accounting for Impulses from Jump Discontinuities

**Example:** Compute the one-sided transform of $x(t)=\begin{cases}0,0\le t<1\\3,1\le t<4\\1,4\le t<6\\0,t\ge6\end{cases}$ by converting each jump into an impulse contribution.

**Explanation**

The cue is a piecewise constant signal with explicit jumps and no interior slopes. We must use the derivative method with impulse bookkeeping: each jump produces a $\delta$ term in $x'(t)$.

Find one-sided limits around each breakpoint: at $t=1$, $x(1^-)=0$, $x(1^+)=3$; at $t=4$, $x(4^-)=3$, $x(4^+)=1$; at $t=6$, $x(6^-)=1$, $x(6^+)=0$.

Compute jump magnitudes $\Delta x(t_i)=x(t_i^+)-x(t_i^-)$. For this signal, $\Delta x(1)=3$, $\Delta x(4)=-2$, and $\Delta x(6)=-1$. Keep signs explicit so downward jumps stay negative.

Convert to derivative form with impulses only:

$$
x'(t)=3\delta(t-1)-2\delta(t-4)-\delta(t-6)
$$

Transform the derivative and isolate $X(s)$.

$$
\mathcal{L}^+\{x'(t)\}=\mathcal{L}^+\{3\delta(t-1)-2\delta(t-4)-\delta(t-6)\}=3e^{-s}-2e^{-4s}-e^{-6s}
$$

Use the one-sided derivative identity $\mathcal{L}^+\{x'(t)\}=sX(s)-x(0^-)$ and here $x(0^-)=0$ because the signal is zero on $t<1$.

$$
sX(s)=3e^{-s}-2e^{-4s}-e^{-6s},\quad X(s)=\frac{3e^{-s}-2e^{-4s}-e^{-6s}}{s}
$$

Result: $X(s)=\dfrac{3e^{-s}-2e^{-4s}-e^{-6s}}{s}$, where the two negative exponentials come directly from downward jumps at $t=4$ and $t=6$.

<a id="including-initial-values-in-a-derivative-based-transform"></a>
## Including Initial Values in a Derivative-Based Transform

**Example:** Use the derivative transform with initial value to find $X(s)$ for $\frac{d}{dt}x(t)=u(t)-u(t-2)$ with $x(0^-)=2$.

**Explanation**

The cue is a derivative-side input containing a delayed step and a nonzero initial condition. The rule is the one-sided derivative identity: keep both the shifted-step derivative input and the $x(0^-)$ term explicit.

First, write the right side as shifted-step terms already aligned with one-sided transform pairs.

$$
\frac{d}{dt}x(t)=u(t)-u(t-2)
$$

$$
\mathcal{L}^+\{u(t-a)\}=\frac{e^{-as}}{s},\quad a\ge0
$$

$$
\mathcal{L}^+\{\frac{d}{dt}x(t)\}=\frac{1}{s}-\frac{e^{-2s}}{s}
$$

Apply the derivative rule and solve for $X(s)$ with the correct one-sided correction.

$$
\mathcal{L}^+\{x'(t)\}=sX(s)-x(0^-)
$$

$$
sX(s)-2=\frac{1}{s}-\frac{e^{-2s}}{s},
$$

Add $x(0^-)=2$ and divide by $s$. The initial term is additive and not multiplied by any delay factor.

$$
X(s)=\frac{2}{s}+\frac{1-e^{-2s}}{s^2}=\frac{2s+1-e^{-2s}}{s^2}
$$

Interpretation: the delayed derivative jump contributes $-e^{-2s}/s^2$, while the nonzero initial value contributes the additive $2/s$ offset.
