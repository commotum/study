## Introduction to Solving for $X(s)$ from a Derivative

The object for this step is the one-sided derivative transform identity, where the known input is usually $\mathcal{L}^+\{x'(t)\}$ and the constant term to track is $x(0^-)$. The target move is to isolate $X(s)$ from that identity using the same algebraic pattern on every example in this topic.

$$
\mathcal{L}^+\{x'(t)\}=sX(s)-x(0^-)
$$

Use this exact action sequence: first add $x(0^-)$ to both sides, then divide every term by $s$. The result is the canonical rearrangement. This is the same pattern whether the initial value is zero or nonzero.

$$
X(s)=\frac{\mathcal{L}^+\{x'(t)\}+x(0^-)}{s}
$$

Quick checkpoint before moving on: confirm the one-sided context, keep the $0^-$ initial term visible until the end, and never skip either algebra step. If you do this in order, the later examples become a direct substitution routine, not a fresh derivation.

Base case for the minimal cognitive load: with $x(0^-)=0$, the rule collapses to the simpler form $X(s)=\mathcal{L}^+\{x'(t)\}/s$. For a known derivative transform value such as $\mathcal{L}^+\{x'(t)\}=\frac{2}{s+1}$, write $X(s)=\frac{2}{s(s+1)}$ immediately.

## Solving for $X(s)$ with Zero Initial Value

**Example:** Given $\mathcal{L}^+\{x'(t)\}=\frac{5}{(s+2)^2}$ and $x(0^-)=0$, solve for $X(s)$.

**Explanation**

The cue is the one-sided derivative identity with a known initial value: $\mathcal{L}^+\{x'(t)\}=sX(s)-x(0^-)$. Use this rule first, then perform the isolation moves in a fixed order.

$$
\mathcal{L}^+\{x'(t)\}=sX(s)-x(0^-) = \frac{5}{(s+2)^2}
$$

Substitute the given data and write the target equation as $\frac{5}{(s+2)^2}=sX(s)-0$. The only thing to do next is isolate $X(s)$.

$$
sX(s)=\frac{5}{(s+2)^2}+0=\frac{5}{(s+2)^2}
$$

Add the initial term (which is zero) and then divide by $s$ to keep the one-sided form explicit.

$$
X(s)=\frac{\frac{5}{(s+2)^2}}{s}=\frac{5}{s(s+2)^2}
$$

## Solving for $X(s)$ with a Nonzero Initial Value

**Example:** Given $\mathcal{L}^+\{x'(t)\}=\frac{8}{s+1}+\frac{2}{s}$ and $x(0^-)=3$, solve for $X(s)$.

**Explanation**

The cue is the one-sided derivative transform form with a known derivative transform and explicit nonzero initial value, so the rule is $\mathcal{L}^+\{x'(t)\}=sX(s)-x(0^-)$. Identify $x'(t)$, keep the $x(0^-)$ term visible, and isolate $X(s)$.

$$
\mathcal{L}^+\{x'(t)\}=sX(s)-x(0^-)
$$

Substitute the given values into the identity: $\mathcal{L}^+\{x'(t)\}=\frac{8}{s+1}+\frac{2}{s}$ and $x(0^-)=3$. This gives the isolated equation form $\frac{8}{s+1}+\frac{2}{s}=sX(s)-3$.

$$
\frac{8}{s+1}+\frac{2}{s}=sX(s)-3
$$

Move the initial term to the left side first, then divide the entire equation by $s$ to isolate $X(s)$ completely.

$$
sX(s)=\frac{8}{s+1}+\frac{2}{s}+3
$$

$$
X(s)=\frac{\frac{8}{s+1}+\frac{2}{s}+3}{s}=\frac{8}{s(s+1)}+\frac{2}{s^2}+\frac{3}{s}
$$

## Solving for $X(s)$ from a Derivative with Step Terms

**Example:** Given $\mathcal{L}^+\{x'(t)\}=\frac{4}{s}-\frac{4e^{-2s}}{s}$ and $x(0^-)=0$, solve for $X(s)$.

**Explanation**

The cue is the one-sided derivative identity with a known derivative transform. Use $\mathcal{L}^+\{x'(t)\}=sX(s)-x(0^-)$. The only rule is the same isolation move used previously, now applied to delayed step-function terms in the transform.

$$
\mathcal{L}^+\{x'(t)\}=sX(s)-x(0^-)
$$

Substitute the given values: $x(0^-)=0$ and $\mathcal{L}^+\{x'(t)\}=\frac{4}{s}-\frac{4e^{-2s}}{s}$. That gives $\frac{4}{s}-\frac{4e^{-2s}}{s}=sX(s)-0$.

$$
\frac{4}{s}-\frac{4e^{-2s}}{s}=sX(s)
$$

Drop the zero term and divide the full right-hand side by $s$ to isolate the transform.

$$
X(s)=\frac{\frac{4}{s}-\frac{4e^{-2s}}{s}}{s}
$$

$$
X(s)=\frac{4}{s^2}-\frac{4e^{-2s}}{s^2}=\frac{4(1-e^{-2s})}{s^2}
$$

## Solving for $X(s)$ from a Derivative with Impulse Terms

**Example:** Given $\mathcal{L}^+\{x'(t)\}=\frac{2}{s}+3-\frac{2e^{-s}}{s}$ and $x(0^-)=1$, solve for $X(s)$.

**Explanation**

The cue is again the one-sided derivative identity, now with a derivative transform that includes mixed terms: $\mathcal{L}^+\{x'(t)\}=sX(s)-x(0^-)$. Keep the full right-hand-side expression intact until after isolation.

$$
\mathcal{L}^+\{x'(t)\}=sX(s)-x(0^-)
$$

Substitute the given data: $\mathcal{L}^+\{x'(t)\}=\frac{2}{s}+3-\frac{2e^{-s}}{s}$ and $x(0^-)=1$. Write the equation as $\frac{2}{s}+3-\frac{2e^{-s}}{s}=sX(s)-1$.

$$
\frac{2}{s}+3-\frac{2e^{-s}}{s}=sX(s)-1
$$

Add $1$ to both sides to isolate $sX(s)$, then divide everything by $s$. This is the same move sequence as earlier examples, only with additional mixed components.

$$
sX(s)=\frac{2}{s}+3-\frac{2e^{-s}}{s}+1
$$

$$
X(s)=\frac{\frac{2}{s}+3-\frac{2e^{-s}}{s}+1}{s}
$$

$$
X(s)=\frac{4}{s}+\frac{2}{s^2}-\frac{2e^{-s}}{s^2}
$$
