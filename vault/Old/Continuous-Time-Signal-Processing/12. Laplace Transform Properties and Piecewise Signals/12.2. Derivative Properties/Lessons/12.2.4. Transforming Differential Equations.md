## Introduction to Transforming Differential Equations

The target move is to convert a time-domain differential equation into one algebraic equation in the $s$-domain while preserving all pre-initial data needed to solve it.

This conversion is built from the two one-sided derivative identities. For a signal $y(t)$ with Laplace transform $Y(s)$, the rules are $\mathcal{L}\{y'(t)\}=sY(s)-y(0^-)$ and $\mathcal{L}\{y''(t)\}=s^2Y(s)-sy(0^-)-y'(0^-)$.

$$
\mathcal{L}\{y'(t)\}=sY(s)-y(0^-)
$$

$$
\mathcal{L}\{y''(t)\}=s^2Y(s)-sy(0^-)-y'(0^-)
$$

For each differential term in the left side, apply these rules directly and keep the same structure: transform term-by-term, preserve the powers of $s$, and leave $Y(s)$ as the unknown function you will solve for.

Use the base case $y'(t)+y(t)=0$ with $y(0^-)=0$. Taking the one-sided transform gives $sY(s)-y(0^-)+Y(s)=0$, so this reduces to $(s+1)Y(s)=0$ and therefore $Y(s)=0$.

$$
y'(t)+ay(t)=f(t)\;\Rightarrow\;(s+a)Y(s)-y(0^-)=F(s)
$$

The final step is to collect like terms of $Y(s)$ on one side and move any known forcing and initial-condition terms to the other side. That arrangement is the algebraic bridge that lets you solve for $Y(s)$ in the next step.

## Transforming a First-Order Differential Equation

**Example:** Transform $y'(t)+4y(t)=3e^{-t}$ with $y(0^-)=0$ into its $s$-domain equation.

**Explanation**

This step extends the tutorial workflow to a non-homogeneous first-order equation. The cue is the term-by-term transform of a whole differential equation in time, and the goal is to produce one algebraic equation involving $Y(s)$.

Apply the one-sided transform to both sides: $\mathcal{L}\{y'(t)+4y(t)\}=\mathcal{L}\{3e^{-t}\}$.

$$
\mathcal{L}\{y'(t)+4y(t)\}=\mathcal{L}\{3e^{-t}\}
$$

Use $\mathcal{L}\{y'(t)\}=sY(s)-y(0^-)$ and the given condition $y(0^-)=0$, so $\mathcal{L}\{y'(t)\}=sY(s)$. Also use $\mathcal{L}\{y(t)\}=Y(s)$ and $\mathcal{L}\{3e^{-t}\}=\frac{3}{s+1}$.

$$
sY(s)+4Y(s)=\frac{3}{s+1}
$$

Collect the unknown transform term on the left to get the algebraic equation for the response: $ (s+4)Y(s)=\frac{3}{s+1} $, and isolate it as $Y(s)=\frac{3}{(s+4)(s+1)}$.

$$
Y(s)=\frac{3}{(s+4)(s+1)}
$$

## Carrying a Nonzero Initial Condition Through the Transform

**Example:** Transform $y'(t)+2y(t)=1$ with $y(0^-)=7$ and simplify the resulting $s$-domain equation.

**Explanation**

This example changes only one thing from the previous step: the same first-order template now uses a nonzero pre-initial value, so the derivative term is no longer just $sY(s)$.

Start by transforming both sides: $\mathcal{L}\{y'(t)+2y(t)\}=\mathcal{L}\{1\}$.

$$
\mathcal{L}\{y'(t)+2y(t)\}=\mathcal{L}\{1\}
$$

Use $\mathcal{L}\{y'(t)\}=sY(s)-y(0^-)$ and substitute $y(0^-)=7$, then use $\mathcal{L}\{y(t)\}=Y(s)$.

$$
sY(s)-7+2Y(s)=\frac{1}{s}
$$

Now collect the transform terms and write the canonical algebraic equation: $(s+2)Y(s)=\frac{1}{s}+7$, so $Y(s)=\frac{1+7s}{s(s+2)}$.

$$
(s+2)Y(s)=\frac{1}{s}+7,\quad Y(s)=\frac{1+7s}{s(s+2)}
$$

## Transforming a Second-Order Differential Equation

**Example:** Transform $y''(t)+5y'(t)+6y(t)=e^{-t}$ with $y(0^-)=1$ and $y'(0^-)=3$, and write the resulting $s$-domain equation in simplified form.

**Explanation**

This example adds the second-order wrinkle: because the equation has a $y''(t)$ term, the derivative transform now contributes both $y(0^-)$ and $y'(0^-)$ in addition to the usual $s$-scaled $Y(s)$ term.

Start by transforming both sides term-by-term: $\mathcal{L}\{y''(t)+5y'(t)+6y(t)\}=\mathcal{L}\{e^{-t}\}$. This is the same cue-action-result pattern as before, now with one higher-order derivative.

$$
\mathcal{L}\{y''(t)+5y'(t)+6y(t)\}=\mathcal{L}\{e^{-t}\}
$$

Replace each derivative transform with the one-sided formula: $\mathcal{L}\{y''(t)\}=s^2Y(s)-sy(0^-)-y'(0^-)$ and $\mathcal{L}\{y'(t)\}=sY(s)-y(0^-)$. Also use $\mathcal{L}\{y(t)\}=Y(s)$.

$$
s^2Y(s)-sy(0^-)-y'(0^-)+5\left[sY(s)-y(0^- )\right]+6Y(s)=\frac{1}{s+1}
$$

Substitute the initials $y(0^-)=1$, $y'(0^-)=3$ and simplify the left-hand side.

$$
s^2Y(s)-s-3+5sY(s)-5+6Y(s)=\frac{1}{s+1}
$$

Now collect $Y(s)$ terms and move the constant initial-condition terms to the right: $(s^2+5s+6)Y(s)- (s+8)=\frac{1}{s+1}$, so $(s^2+5s+6)Y(s)=\frac{1}{s+1}+s+8$.

$$
(s^2+5s+6)Y(s)-(s+8)=\frac{1}{s+1},\quad (s^2+5s+6)Y(s)=\frac{1}{s+1}+s+8
$$

$$
Y(s)=\frac{\frac{1}{s+1}+s+8}{s^2+5s+6}
$$

## Transforming an Equation with Derivatives on Both Sides

**Example:** Transform $y''(t)+4y(t)=y'(t)$ with $y(0^-)=3$ and $y'(0^-)= -1$, and write the resulting $s$-domain equation in simplified form.

**Explanation**

This example introduces one structural wrinkle: a derivative term now appears on the right side, so you must apply the same one-sided transform rule in both places.

Transform both sides of the differential equation: $\mathcal{L}\{y''(t)+4y(t)\}=\mathcal{L}\{y'(t)\}$.

$$
\mathcal{L}\{y''(t)+4y(t)\}=\mathcal{L}\{y'(t)\}
$$

Apply the transform formulas term-by-term: $\mathcal{L}\{y''(t)\}=s^2Y(s)-sy(0^-)-y'(0^-)$, $\mathcal{L}\{y(t)\}=Y(s)$, and $\mathcal{L}\{y'(t)\}=sY(s)-y(0^-)$.

$$
s^2Y(s)-sy(0^-)-y'(0^-)+4Y(s)=sY(s)-y(0^-)
$$

Substitute the initial values $y(0^-)=3$, $y'(0^-)=-1$ and simplify.

$$
s^2Y(s)-3s+1+4Y(s)=sY(s)-3
$$

Collect the transform terms on one side and the constants on the other: $(s^2-s+4)Y(s)=3s-4$. This is the final algebraic equation in $Y(s)$ for this step.

$$
(s^2-s+4)Y(s)=3s-4
$$

## Collecting Transformed Terms into an Algebraic Equation

**Example:** Transform $y''(t)-4y'(t)+3y(t)=2y'(t)+1$ with $y(0^-)=2$ and $y'(0^-)= -1$, then write the final collected relation for $Y(s)$.

**Explanation**

The new step is pure algebraic collection: transform all terms, then line up all $Y(s)$ terms on one side and constants/forcing on the other.

Transform both sides term-by-term: $\mathcal{L}\{y''(t)-4y'(t)+3y(t)\}=\mathcal{L}\{2y'(t)+1\}$.

$$
\mathcal{L}\{y''(t)-4y'(t)+3y(t)\}=\mathcal{L}\{2y'(t)+1\}
$$

Substitute the derivative formulas: $\mathcal{L}\{y''(t)\}=s^2Y(s)-s y(0^-)-y'(0^-)$, $\mathcal{L}\{y'(t)\}=sY(s)-y(0^-)$, and $\mathcal{L}\{1\}=1/s$.

$$
\left[s^2Y(s)-sy(0^-)-y'(0^-)-4\left(sY(s)-y(0^-)\right)+3Y(s)\right]=2\left[sY(s)-y(0^-)\right]+\frac{1}{s}
$$

Plug in $y(0^-)=2$ and $y'(0^-)=-1$ and simplify each side.

$$
(s^2-4s+3)Y(s)-2s+1=2sY(s)-4+\frac{1}{s}
$$

Collect all $Y(s)$ terms on one side and non-$Y$ terms on the other: $(s^2-6s+3)Y(s)=\frac{1}{s}+2s-13$.

$$
(s^2-6s+3)Y(s)=\frac{1}{s}+2s-13,\quad Y(s)=\frac{2s^2-13s+1}{s(s^2-6s+3)}
$$
