## Introduction to Time Expansion

When you see $y(t)=x(at)$ with $0<a<1$, read it as time expansion. The waveform keeps the same amplitudes, but every time feature moves away from $t=0$.

$$
y(t)=x(at) \quad 0<a<1
$$

Use $x(t/2)$ as the clean base case. Here $a=\tfrac{1}{2}$, so a feature that was at $t_0$ in $x(t)$ appears at $t_{new}=t_0/a=2t_0$ in $x(t/2)$.

$$
y(t)=x(t/2) \qquad t_{new}=t_0/a
$$

The same $t_0/a$ rule applies to every other time feature, so the whole signal stretches horizontally. Only the time locations change; the heights stay the same.

## Recognizing $x(t/2)$ as a Time Expansion

**Example:** Decide whether $x(t/2)$ is a time expansion or a time compression, and state the stretch factor.

**Explanation**

The cue is the fraction inside the argument. In $x(t/2)$, rewrite the signal as $x(at)$ with $a=\tfrac{1}{2}$. Because $0<a<1$, this is a time expansion.

$$
a=\tfrac{1}{2}
$$

Now check one landmark time. If a feature was at $t_0$ in $x(t)$, then in $x(at)$ it appears where $at=t_0$, so $t_{new}=t_0/a$.

$$
t_{new}=t_0/a=2t_0
$$

So every time feature in the waveform moves twice as far from $t=0$ while its height stays the same. That is why $x(t/2)$ is a time expansion with stretch factor $2$.

## Mapping Original Time Points to t0/a

**Example:** A feature is located at $t_0=-6$ in $x(t)$. If the signal becomes $x(t/3)$, where does that feature move, and what is the general rule for any $t_0$?

**Explanation**

The cue is the factor inside the argument. In $x(t/3)$, rewrite the signal as $x(at)$ with $a=\tfrac{1}{3}$, so the location rule is $t_0 \mapsto t_0/a$.

$$
a=\tfrac{1}{3}
$$

Now apply that rule to the labeled time point. Solving $at=t_0$ gives $(\tfrac{1}{3})t=-6$, so the same feature moves to $t_{new}=t_0/a=-18$.

$$
t_{new}=t_0/a=-18
$$

The sign stays negative, but the point moves farther from $t=0$. That is the universal rule for this lesson: every original time point $t_0$ moves to $t_0/a$ when $0<a<1$.

## Finding Expanded Support with Unchanged Amplitude

**Example:** If $x(t)$ is nonzero on $[1,3]$ and has peak amplitude $5$, find the support of $x(t/2)$ and state what happens to the amplitude.

**Explanation**

The cue is the factor inside the argument. In $x(t/2)$, rewrite the signal as $x(at)$ with $a=\tfrac{1}{2}$. That means every original time point moves by the rule $t_{new}=t_0/a$.

$$
a=\tfrac{1}{2},\qquad t_{new}=t_0/a
$$

Apply the rule to the support endpoints. The left endpoint $1$ moves to $1/(1/2)=2$, and the right endpoint $3$ moves to $3/(1/2)=6$, so the new support is $[2,6]$.

$$
[1,3]\ \mapsto\ [2,6]
$$

The interval widens away from $t=0$, but the waveform heights do not change. So the support expands to $[2,6]$ and the peak amplitude stays $5$.
