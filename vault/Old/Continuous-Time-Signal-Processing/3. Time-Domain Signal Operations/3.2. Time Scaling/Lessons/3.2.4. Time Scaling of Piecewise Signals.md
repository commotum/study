## Introduction to Time Scaling of Piecewise Signals

A piecewise signal under $x(at)$ keeps the same values on each piece, but it does not keep the same breakpoint locations. The thing to recognize first is the rule for where each piece starts and ends after scaling.

$$
x(t)=\begin{cases}f_1(t), & t_0\le t<t_1\\f_2(t), & t_1\le t<t_2\end{cases} \Rightarrow x(at)=\begin{cases}f_1(at), & t_0/a\le t<t_1/a\\f_2(at), & t_1/a\le t<t_2/a\end{cases}
$$

To find a new breakpoint, solve $at=t_0$ for $t$. That gives $t=t_0/a$, so every original breakpoint moves to its divided-by-$a$ location while the formula on that interval is rewritten with $at$ inside it.

$$
x(t)=\begin{cases}2,& t<6\\-1,& t\ge 6\end{cases},\qquad x(3t)=\begin{cases}2,& t<2\\-1,& t\ge 2\end{cases}
$$

That is the whole first rule: replace $t$ by $at$ inside each interval formula, and move each breakpoint from $t_0$ to $t_0/a$. When $a>1$, the pieces move closer to $t=0$ and the signal is compressed; when $0<a<1$, the pieces move farther apart and the signal is expanded.

## Scaling the Breakpoints of a Simple Piecewise Signal

**Example:** Find the piecewise definition of $x(3t)$ for $x(t)=\begin{cases}2,& t<6\\-1,& t\ge 6\end{cases}$.

**Explanation**

The cue is a two-piece constant signal with one breakpoint, and the scale factor inside the argument is $3$. To locate the new breakpoint in $x(3t)$, solve $3t=6$ so the old breakpoint at $6$ moves to $t=2$.

$$
3t=6 \Rightarrow t=2
$$

Because this is time scaling only, the values on the left and right pieces stay $2$ and $-1$. The breakpoint changes location, but the interval values do not.

$$
x(3t)=\begin{cases}2,& t<2\\-1,& t\ge 2\end{cases}
$$

## Rewriting Piecewise Formulas After Replacing t with at

**Example:** Find $x(2t)$ for $x(t)=\begin{cases}t+1,& 0\le t<2\\3-t,& 2\le t<4\end{cases}$.

**Explanation**

The cue is a two-piece signal whose formulas depend on $t$, so this example uses both parts of the rule at once: move the breakpoint and rewrite each formula with $2t$ inside it. To move the breakpoint, solve $2t=2$.

$$
2t=2 \Rightarrow t=1
$$

Now substitute $2t$ into each interval formula. The first piece $t+1$ becomes $2t+1$, and the second piece $3-t$ becomes $3-2t$. The interval order stays the same, but the breakpoint moves from $2$ to $1$.

$$
x(2t)=\begin{cases}2t+1,& 0\le t<1\\3-2t,& 1\le t<2\end{cases}
$$

<a id="time-compression-of-a-multi-interval-piecewise-signal"></a>
## Time Compression of a Multi-Interval Piecewise Signal

**Example:** Find $x(2t)$ for $$x(t)=\begin{cases}0,& t<1\\t-1,& 1\le t<3\\2,& t\ge 3\end{cases}$$.

**Explanation**

The original breakpoints are $t=1$ and $t=3$. For $x(2t)$, the input to $x$ is $2t$, so each original breakpoint is reached when $2t$ equals that breakpoint.

$$
2t=1 \Rightarrow t=\frac{1}{2},\qquad 2t=3 \Rightarrow t=\frac{3}{2}
$$

Next rewrite the formula assigned to each interval. The first and last pieces stay $0$ and $2$ because they are constants. The middle formula $t-1$ becomes $2t-1$ after replacing the input variable with $2t$.

$$
x(2t)=\begin{cases}0,& 2t<1\\2t-1,& 1\le 2t<3\\2,& 2t\ge 3\end{cases}
$$

Finally express the interval conditions in terms of $t$. Dividing each breakpoint by $2$ compresses the signal toward the origin while preserving the left-to-right order of the pieces.

$$
x(2t)=\begin{cases}0,& t<\frac{1}{2}\\2t-1,& \frac{1}{2}\le t<\frac{3}{2}\\2,& t\ge \frac{3}{2}\end{cases}
$$

## Time Expansion of a Multi-Interval Piecewise Signal

**Example:** Find $x(t/2)$ for $x(t)=\begin{cases}0,& t<1\\t-1,& 1\le t<3\\2,& t\ge 3\end{cases}$.

**Explanation**

The cue is a three-piece signal, and the factor inside the argument is $1/2$. Because $1/2<1$, this is the expansion case: each breakpoint moves away from $t=0$ instead of toward it.

$$
\frac{1}{2}t=1 \Rightarrow t=2,\qquad \frac{1}{2}t=3 \Rightarrow t=6
$$

Now rewrite the formulas on each interval with $t/2$ inside them. The constant pieces stay $0$ and $2$, and the middle piece $t-1$ becomes $t/2-1$.

$$
x(t/2)=\begin{cases}0,& t<2\\t/2-1,& 2\le t<6\\2,& t\ge 6\end{cases}
$$
