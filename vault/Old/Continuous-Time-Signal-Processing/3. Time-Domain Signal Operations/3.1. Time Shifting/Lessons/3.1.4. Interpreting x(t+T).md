## Introduction to Interpreting $x(t+T)$

Read $x(t+T)$ as a time advance when $T>0$. The plus sign inside the argument means the signal moves left by $T$, not right.

$$
y(t)=x(t+T)
$$

Use one labeled feature time $t_0$ as the base case. If that feature appears at $t_0$ in $x(t)$, then it appears at $t_0-T$ in $x(t+T)$.

$$
t_0 \mapsto t_0-T
$$

Only the time labels change. The signal shape, amplitudes, and relative feature pattern stay the same while every landmark shifts left by $T$.

$$
x(t+T)\text{ is a }T\text{-unit advance}
$$

That sign convention is the one to reuse later: when you see $x(t+T)$ with $T>0$, move each original feature time to $t_0-T$ and read the result as an advance.

## Mapping One Feature Time in $x(t+T)$

**Example:** A signal $x(t)$ has a marked feature at $t_0=6$. Where does that feature appear in $x(t+2)$?

**Explanation**

The cue is the plus sign inside $x(t+T)$. For $T>0$, that sign means an advance: every feature moves left by $T$ while the shape stays the same.

$$
y(t)=x(t+2)
$$

Start from the feature time $t_0=6$ and apply the advance rule. The mapping is $t_0\mapsto t_0-T$.

$$
t_0 \mapsto t_0-T
$$

Substituting $T=2$ gives $6-2=4$, so the feature appears at $t=4$. The signal's value does not change; only the time location does.

$$
x(t+2)\text{ is a }2\text{-unit advance}
$$

## Mapping Multiple Feature Times in $x(t+T)$

**Example:** A signal $x(t)$ has features at $t=-1$, $t=2$, and $t=5$. Where do those features appear in $x(t+3)$?

**Explanation**

The cue is the plus sign inside $x(t+T)$. For $T>0$, that sign means an advance: every feature moves left by $T$ while the order and spacing stay the same.

$$
y(t)=x(t+3)
$$

Start from the three feature times $-1$, $2$, and $5$. Apply the same mapping $t_0\mapsto t_0-T$ to each time.

$$
-1 \mapsto -4,\quad 2 \mapsto -1,\quad 5 \mapsto 2
$$

For $T=3$, the mapped times are $-4$, $-1$, and $2$. The spacing is unchanged: the gaps are still $3$ units.

$$
x(t+3)\text{ is a }3\text{-unit advance}
$$

## Mapping the Support Interval of $x(t+T)$

**Example:** A signal $x(t)$ is nonzero on $1 \le t < 4$. What is the support of $x(t+2)$?

**Explanation**

The cue is the support interval $1 \le t < 4$. For $T>0$, $x(t+T)$ advances the whole signal left by $T$; the endpoints move by the same amount, and their open or closed status stays the same.

$$
y(t)=x(t+2)
$$

Read the original endpoints as $1$ and $4$. Apply the same shift rule to each endpoint: subtract $2$ from both.

$$
1 \mapsto -1,\quad 4 \mapsto 2
$$

The left endpoint stays closed and the right endpoint stays open, so the support of $x(t+2)$ is $-1 \le t < 2$. The interval keeps the same shape; it just moves left by $2$.

$$
x(t+2)\text{ is a }2\text{-unit advance}
$$
