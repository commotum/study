## Introduction to Time Reversal of Exponential Signals

Time reversal means replacing every $t$ in the signal with $-t$. For exponential signals, that substitution flips the sign in the exponent, so the same rule controls both growth or decay and complex rotation.

$$
x(t)=Ae^{\sigma t}
$$

Start with the real-exponential base case, where $\omega=0$. Replacing $t$ by $-t$ gives $x(-t)=Ae^{-\sigma t}$, so a positive $\sigma$ becomes decay and a negative $\sigma$ becomes growth.

$$
x(-t)=Ae^{-\sigma t}
$$

The same substitution works for a pure complex exponential. When $x(t)=Ae^{j\omega t}$, time reversal flips the sign of $\omega$, so the rotation direction in the complex plane reverses.

$$
x(t)=Ae^{j\omega t}
$$

After reversal, $x(-t)=Ae^{-j\omega t}$. The magnitude stays the same, but the direction of rotation is opposite.

$$
x(-t)=Ae^{-j\omega t}
$$

For the full exponential $x(t)=Ae^{(\sigma+j\omega)t}$, time reversal combines both effects at once. The result is $x(-t)=Ae^{-(\sigma+j\omega)t}$, which later examples can reuse one part at a time.

$$
x(t)=Ae^{(\sigma+j\omega)t}
$$

$$
x(-t)=Ae^{-(\sigma+j\omega)t}
$$

## Time Reversal of a Pure Real Exponential

**Example:** Let $x(t)=4e^{3t}$. Find $x(-t)$ and decide whether the resulting signal grows or decays as $t$ increases.

**Explanation**

Since this is the base case with $\omega=0$, use the substitution rule from the previous step: replace every $t$ with $-t$ and then simplify. The amplitude stays the same; only the exponent changes.

$$
x(t)=4e^{3t}
$$

Apply the rule directly: $x(-t)=4e^{3(-t)}$. This is the same signal with time reversed, so the positive exponent now points in the opposite direction.

$$
x(-t)=4e^{3(-t)}
$$

Now simplify the exponent to get $x(-t)=4e^{-3t}$. The coefficient $4$ does not change, because time reversal acts on $t$, not on the amplitude.

$$
x(-t)=4e^{-3t}
$$

Because the exponent is negative, the signal decays as $t$ increases. The reversal has turned the original growth into decay, which is exactly what the substitution rule predicts.

## Growth and Decay After Time Reversal

**Example:** Let $x(t)=5e^{-2t}$. Find $x(-t)$ and explain how the growth or decay behavior changes after reversal.

**Explanation**

The cue is a decaying real exponential, so use the same reversal rule as before: replace $t$ with $-t$ and keep the coefficient unchanged.

$$
x(t)=5e^{-2t}
$$

Substituting $-t$ gives $x(-t)=5e^{-2(-t)}$. The only change is the sign on the time variable inside the exponent.

$$
x(-t)=5e^{-2(-t)}
$$

Simplify the exponent to get $x(-t)=5e^{2t}$. The amplitude stays $5$, but the sign flip reverses the exponential behavior.

$$
x(-t)=5e^{2t}
$$

The original signal decays as $t$ increases, while the reversed signal grows as $t$ increases. The sign of the exponent is what controls the classification after time reversal.

## Time Reversal of a Pure Complex Exponential

**Example:** Let $x(t)=2e^{j4t}$. Find $x(-t)$ and identify the new rotation direction in the complex plane.

**Explanation**

Use the same substitution rule as before: replace every $t$ with $-t$ and simplify the exponent. Here the cue is a pure complex exponential, so the only thing that changes is the sign of the imaginary frequency term.

$$
x(t)=2e^{j4t}
$$

Substitute $-t$: $x(-t)=2e^{j4(-t)}$. The amplitude stays $2$.

$$
x(-t)=2e^{j4(-t)}
$$

Simplify the exponent to get $x(-t)=2e^{-j4t}$. The sign of $\omega$ has flipped, but its magnitude is unchanged.

$$
x(-t)=2e^{-j4t}
$$

Because the sign of the imaginary exponent reversed, the phasor rotates in the opposite direction in the complex plane. Time reversal changes only the direction of rotation, not the size of $\omega$ or the coefficient.

## Rotation Direction After Time Reversal

**Example:** Let $x(t)=3e^{-j5t}$. Find $x(-t)$ and state whether the phasor now rotates clockwise or counterclockwise.

**Explanation**

Use the same substitution rule: replace every $t$ with $-t$ and then simplify. The cue here is a pure complex exponential, so the sign of $\omega$ determines whether the phasor is clockwise or counterclockwise.

$$
x(t)=3e^{-j5t}
$$

Substitute $-t$: $x(-t)=3e^{-j5(-t)}$. The coefficient stays $3$ and the magnitude of $\omega$ stays $5$.

$$
x(-t)=3e^{-j5(-t)}
$$

Simplify to get $x(-t)=3e^{j5t}$. The original negative sign on $\omega$ has flipped to positive, so the direction of rotation flips with it.

$$
x(-t)=3e^{j5t}
$$

Because $e^{-j5t}$ rotates clockwise as $t$ increases, the reversed signal $e^{j5t}$ rotates counterclockwise. Time reversal swaps the direction while preserving the frequency magnitude.

## Time Reversal of a General Complex Exponential

**Example:** Let $x(t)=4e^{(2+j3)t}$. Find $x(-t)$ and explain how time reversal changes both the amplitude trend and the rotation direction.

**Explanation**

Use the time-reversal rule: replace every $t$ with $-t$. That flips the entire exponent, not just the real part or just the imaginary part.

$$
x(-t)=4e^{(2+j3)(-t)}=4e^{-(2+j3)t}=4e^{-2t}e^{-j3t}
$$

The factor $e^{-2t}$ means the amplitude now decays as $t$ increases, and the factor $e^{-j3t}$ means the phasor rotates in the opposite direction from the original signal.

So the reversed signal keeps the same scale factor $4$, but both behaviors flip together: a growing, counterclockwise exponential becomes a decaying, clockwise one.
