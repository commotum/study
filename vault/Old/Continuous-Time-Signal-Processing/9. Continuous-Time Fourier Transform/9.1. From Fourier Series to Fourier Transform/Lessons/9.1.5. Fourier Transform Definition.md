## Introduction to Fourier Transform Definition

Periodic continuous-time signals repeat with a finite period and are naturally described by Fourier series. An aperiodic signal does not repeat, so the forward Fourier transform gives its continuous-frequency description in terms of $\omega$.

$$
X(j\omega)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x(t)e^{-j\omega t}\,dt
$$

Read the definition term by term: $x(t)$ is the time-domain signal, $e^{-j\omega t}$ is the complex exponential that probes frequency $\omega$, and the integral adds the contribution from every time instant. The result $X(j\omega)$ is the continuous-frequency spectrum of $x(t)$.

$$
x(t)=\begin{cases}1, & -T/2 \le t \le T/2\\ 0, & \text{otherwise}\end{cases}
$$

For a compact-support signal like this rectangular pulse, the integral is still written over all time, but the zero-valued regions contribute nothing. The calculation therefore depends only on the support of $x(t)$, which is the first thing to notice before any later example evaluates $X(j\omega)$.

## Substituting a Signal into the Fourier Transform Integral

**Example:** Given $x(t)=1$ for $-T/2 \le t \le T/2$ and $0$ otherwise, write the Fourier transform integral for $X(j\omega)$ and substitute the signal into the integrand.

**Explanation**

The cue is the forward Fourier transform definition. Start from $X(j\omega)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x(t)e^{-j\omega t}\,dt$ and keep the infinite-time limits, because this step only inserts the signal into the integrand.

$$
X(j\omega)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x(t)e^{-j\omega t}\,dt
$$

Here the signal is the compact-support pulse from the prompt: $x(t)=1$ on $-T/2 \le t \le T/2$ and $0$ otherwise. Replace $x(t)$ by that piecewise form directly inside the integral.

$$
X(j\omega)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \begin{cases}1, & -T/2 \le t \le T/2\\ 0, & \text{otherwise}\end{cases} e^{-j\omega t}\,dt
$$

That is the pure substitution result. The integrand now contains the chosen $x(t)$, but the bounds are still $-\infty$ to $\infty$; the next section will reduce them to the support interval.

## Setting Integration Limits from Aperiodic Signal Support

**Example:** Using the same pulse, identify the nonzero time interval and rewrite $X(j\omega)$ with the correct integration limits.

**Explanation**

The cue is the pulse support from the prompt: $x(t)$ is nonzero only on $-T/2 \le t \le T/2$. So the Fourier transform integral should keep only that interval when we set the bounds.

$$
X(j\omega)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x(t)e^{-j\omega t}\,dt
$$

Because $x(t)=0$ outside that support, the infinite limits can be replaced by the nonzero interval without changing the transform. Keep the integrand the same and only shrink the bounds.

$$
X(j\omega)=\int_{-T/2}^{T/2} e^{-j\omega t}\,dt
$$

That is the bounded setup we wanted. The support has collapsed the full-time integral to the exact finite interval where $x(t)$ lives, and the next step will evaluate the result in terms of $\omega$.

<a id="evaluating-xj-omega-as-a-function-of-continuous-frequency"></a>
## Evaluating $X(j \omega)$ as a Function of Continuous Frequency

**Example:** Evaluate $X(j\omega)=\int_{-T/2}^{T/2} e^{-j\omega t}\,dt$ and simplify the result into a closed-form expression.

**Explanation**

The cue is the bounded Fourier transform integral from the previous step. Integrate $e^{-j\omega t}$ with respect to $t$, then evaluate the antiderivative at the two limits to turn the transform into a function of $\omega$.

$$
\begin{aligned}X(j\omega) &= \int_{-T/2}^{T/2} e^{-j\omega t}\,dt \\ &= \left[\frac{e^{-j\omega t}}{-j\omega}\right]_{-T/2}^{T/2} \\ &= \frac{e^{-j\omega T/2}-e^{j\omega T/2}}{-j\omega}\end{aligned}
$$

Now simplify the exponential difference into a trigonometric form. That keeps the result as a continuous-frequency spectrum and makes the dependence on $\omega$ easy to read.

$$
\begin{aligned}X(j\omega) &= \frac{e^{-j\omega T/2}-e^{j\omega T/2}}{-j\omega} \\ &= \frac{2\sin(\omega T/2)}{\omega}\end{aligned}
$$

So the closed-form transform is $X(j\omega)=\frac{2\sin(\omega T/2)}{\omega}$. At $\omega=0$, the removable limit is $X(0)=T$.

## Interpreting the Transform Result as a Continuous-Frequency Spectrum

**Example:** Given the closed-form $X(j\omega)$ from the previous step, explain what it says about the signal’s continuous-frequency spectrum and the role of $\omega$.

**Explanation**

The cue is the closed-form $X(j\omega)$ from the previous step. Read it as a function of continuous frequency: every real $\omega$ gives one value, so the result is a continuous spectrum rather than isolated harmonics or a discrete coefficient list.

$$
X(j\omega)=\frac{2\sin(\omega T/2)}{\omega}, \qquad X(0)=T
$$

The value at $\omega=0$ is the zero-frequency component, and here it equals the pulse area. That tells you how much DC content the finite pulse carries.

Because $x(t)$ is supported on a finite time interval, its transform is spread across a range of $\omega$ instead of living at one frequency index. The shape of $X(j\omega)$ is therefore a continuous-frequency spectrum whose width and sidelobes come from the time-limited pulse.
