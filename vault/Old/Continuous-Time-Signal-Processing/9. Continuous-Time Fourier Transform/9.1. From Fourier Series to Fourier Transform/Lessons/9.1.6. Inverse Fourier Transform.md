## Introduction to Inverse Fourier Transform

The inverse Fourier transform is the reverse of the forward transform: after a signal has been turned into $X(j\omega)$, the inverse operation recovers the original $x(t)$.

$$
x(t)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}X(j\omega)e^{j\omega t}\,d\omega
$$

Read that formula from left to right: the factor $\frac{1}{2\pi}$ sets the scale, the integral runs over all frequencies, and the kernel $e^{j\omega t}$ rebuilds the time signal. The spectrum $X(j\omega)$ supplies the frequency weights, and the integral recombines those contributions into $x(t)$.

$$
X(j\omega)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x(t)e^{-j\omega t}\,dt
$$

That is why the forward and inverse transforms form a pair: $x(t)\leftrightarrow X(j\omega)$. In the simplest known pair, applying the inverse integral reproduces the original time signal before any harder algebra appears.

$$
x(t)\leftrightarrow X(j\omega)
$$

Later examples will write the inverse integral for a given spectrum, evaluate it, and then verify the result by transforming back with the forward transform.

## Writing the Inverse Fourier Transform Integral

**Example:** Given a continuous-frequency spectrum $X(j\omega)$, write the inverse Fourier transform integral that reconstructs $x(t)$.

**Explanation**

The cue is that the spectrum $X(j\omega)$ is given and the task is to recover the time signal $x(t)$. That means you use the inverse Fourier transform, not the forward transform, and you keep the sign, limits, and normalization exactly as written.

$$
x(t)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}X(j\omega)e^{j\omega t}\,d\omega
$$

Read the setup from left to right: the factor $\frac{1}{2\pi}$ sets the scale, the limits run over all frequencies, and the kernel $e^{j\omega t}$ rebuilds the time signal from the spectrum. If a later prompt supplies a specific $X(j\omega)$, substitute it directly into the integrand; if not, leave the inverse integral in this unevaluated form.

This is the complete inverse-transform setup. The next examples will use the same form and then evaluate it for specific spectra.

## Evaluating the Inverse Transform Integral

**Example:** Evaluate the inverse Fourier transform of the even rectangular spectrum $X(j\omega)=1$ for $|\omega|\le \Omega$ and $0$ otherwise.

**Explanation**

The cue is an even rectangular spectrum centered at $\omega=0$. That means the inverse transform starts with the band $[-\Omega,\Omega]$, and the even symmetry lets you rewrite the complex exponential integral in a real form before you simplify anything.

$$
x(t)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\Omega}^{\Omega}e^{j\omega t}\,d\omega
$$

$$
x(t)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_0^\Omega \cos(\omega t)\,d\omega
$$

$$
x(t)=\frac{1}{\pi}\left[\frac{\sin(\omega t)}{t}\right]_0^\Omega=\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{\sin(\Omega t)}{t}
$$

The result is real and even, which matches the spectrum. At $t=0$, take the usual limit, and in sinc form this is the familiar bandlimited time signal produced by a flat rectangular spectrum.

## Reconstructing a Signal from a Piecewise Spectrum

**Example:** Reconstruct $x(t)$ from the piecewise spectrum $X(j\omega)=1$ for $\Omega_1\le |\omega|\le \Omega_2$ and $0$ otherwise.

**Explanation**

The cue is a piecewise even spectrum with a gap in the middle. Rewrite it as the difference of two simpler even rectangles so you can apply the inverse transform once to the outer band and once to the missing inner band.

$$
x(t)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\Omega_2}^{\Omega_2}e^{j\omega t}\,d\omega-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\Omega_1}^{\Omega_1}e^{j\omega t}\,d\omega
$$

$$
x(t)=\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{\sin(\Omega_2 t)}{t}-\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{\sin(\Omega_1 t)}{t}
$$

$$
x(t)=\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{\sin(\Omega_2 t)-\sin(\Omega_1 t)}{t}
$$

The result is the difference of two sinc-shaped terms, so it stays real and even. At $t=0$, use the usual limit and get $(\Omega_2-\Omega_1)/\pi$, which matches the net band width.

## Checking Reconstruction with the Forward Transform

**Example:** Check that the reconstructed $x(t)$ from the previous example produces the same piecewise $X(j\omega)$ under the forward Fourier transform.

**Explanation**

The cue is the reconstructed signal from the previous step. Since it is a difference of two sinc-shaped terms, check each term against the standard rectangle pair and use linearity to recover the target spectrum exactly.

$$
x(t)=\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{\sin(\Omega_2 t)-\sin(\Omega_1 t)}{t}
$$

$$
\frac{\sin(\Omega t)}{\pi t}\leftrightarrow\begin{cases}1,&|\omega|\le\Omega\\0,&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}
$$

$$
X(j\omega)=\begin{cases}1,&\Omega_1\le|\omega|\le\Omega_2\\0,&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}
$$

The outer rectangle from $\Omega_2$ minus the inner rectangle from $\Omega_1$ gives the same piecewise spectrum as the target. That exact match confirms that the reconstruction is correct.
