## Introduction to Zeros of the Rectangular Pulse Spectrum

For a width-$T$ rectangular pulse, the spectrum is already in sinc form. The zero locations come from the sine numerator, not from the denominator, and the point $\omega=0$ is the peak of the spectrum rather than a zero.

$$
X(j\omega)=AT\frac{\sin(\omega T/2)}{\omega T/2}
$$

So the zero condition is $\sin(\omega T/2)=0$. Set $\omega T/2=k\pi$ and solve for $\omega$ to get the indexed family of spectral nulls.

$$
\omega_k=\frac{2\pi k}{T},\quad k\in\mathbb{Z},\;k\ne 0
$$

Exclude $k=0$, since that gives the center value of the sinc, not a zero. The first nonzero zeros are the symmetric pair at $\omega=\pm 2\pi/T$, and the same formula shows that adjacent nulls are spaced by $\Delta\omega=2\pi/T$.

## Finding the First Nonzero Zeros from Pulse Width

**Example:** A rectangular pulse has width $T=6$. Find the first nonzero angular-frequency zeros of its spectrum.

**Explanation**

The cue is the sinc-form spectrum from the previous step. To find zeros, set the sine numerator equal to zero and ignore the center point $\omega=0$, which is not a zero.

$$
\sin(\omega T/2)=0
$$

Write the zero condition as $\omega T/2=k\pi$ and solve for $\omega$. That gives the indexed family of spectral nulls, and the first nonzero pair comes from $k=\pm1$.

$$
\omega_k=\frac{2\pi k}{T},\quad k\in\mathbb{Z},\;k\ne 0
$$

Substitute $T=6$ and keep only the first nonzero pair. The result is the symmetric pair $\omega=\pm\pi/3$.

$$
\omega=\pm\frac{2\pi}{6}=\pm\frac{\pi}{3}
$$

## Finding the Full Zero Pattern of a Rectangular Pulse Spectrum

**Example:** A rectangular pulse has width $T=6$. Write the full set of nonzero angular-frequency zeros of its spectrum.

**Explanation**

The cue is still the sinc-form spectrum from the previous steps. The first pair showed that the zeros come from the sine numerator, and now we keep the same root condition but let the integer index run through every nonzero value.

$$
\omega_k=\frac{2\pi k}{T},\quad k\in\mathbb{Z},\;k\ne 0
$$

Substitute $T=6$ into the indexed family. That gives a repeating zero pattern with spacing $\pi/3$ between adjacent nulls.

$$
\omega_k=\frac{2\pi k}{6}=\frac{k\pi}{3},\quad k\in\mathbb{Z},\;k\ne 0
$$

So the full nonzero zero set is $\ldots,-\pi,-\frac{2\pi}{3},-\frac{\pi}{3},\frac{\pi}{3},\frac{2\pi}{3},\pi,\ldots$. The sign symmetry comes from the integer index $k$, and $k=0$ is still excluded because it is the center value of the sinc, not a zero.

<a id="computing-null-spacing-from-pulse-width"></a>
## Computing Null Spacing from Pulse Width

**Example:** A rectangular pulse has width $T=6$. What is the spacing between adjacent nonzero zeros of its spectrum?

**Explanation**

The cue is the indexed zero family from the previous step. Adjacent zeros are consecutive values of $k$, so the spacing comes from subtracting one indexed zero from the next.

$$
\omega_k=\frac{2\pi k}{T},\qquad \omega_{k+1}=\frac{2\pi (k+1)}{T}
$$

Subtract the two expressions. The $k$ terms cancel, so the angular-frequency gap is constant and does not depend on which pair of adjacent zeros you choose.

$$
\Delta\omega=\omega_{k+1}-\omega_k=\frac{2\pi}{T}
$$

Now substitute $T=6$. The spacing between adjacent nonzero zeros is $\Delta\omega=2\pi/6=\pi/3$.

$$
\Delta\omega=\frac{2\pi}{6}=\frac{\pi}{3}
$$

<a id="inferring-pulse-width-from-null-spacing"></a>
## Inferring Pulse Width from Null Spacing

**Example:** The spacing between adjacent nonzero zeros of a rectangular pulse spectrum is $\Delta\omega=\pi/3$. Find the pulse width $T$.

**Explanation**

The cue is the spacing relation from the previous step. To recover the pulse width, start from $\Delta\omega=2\pi/T$ and solve that formula for $T$.

$$
\Delta\omega=\frac{2\pi}{T}
$$

Rearrange the equation to isolate $T$. This gives the reciprocal form $T=2\pi/\Delta\omega$, so the pulse width is determined directly by the measured zero spacing.

$$
T=\frac{2\pi}{\Delta\omega}
$$

Now substitute $\Delta\omega=\pi/3$. The result is $T=2\pi/(\pi/3)=6$, which has the correct units of time for a pulse width.

$$
T=\frac{2\pi}{\pi/3}=6
$$
