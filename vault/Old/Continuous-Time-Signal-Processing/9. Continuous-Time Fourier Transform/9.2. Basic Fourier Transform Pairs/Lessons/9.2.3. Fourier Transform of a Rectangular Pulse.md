## Introduction to Fourier Transform of a Rectangular Pulse

Start with the centered unit-height rectangular pulse $x(t)=1$ for $|t|\le T/2$ and $0$ otherwise. For this base case, the continuous-time Fourier transform only needs the interval where the pulse is nonzero.

$$
X(\omega)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x(t)e^{-j\omega t}\,dt
$$

$$
x(t)=\begin{cases}1, & |t|\le T/2\\0, & \text{otherwise}\end{cases}
$$

Because the pulse is centered, its transform is real and even. That lets us replace the infinite integration range with the support interval $[-T/2,\,T/2]$.

$$
X(\omega)=\int_{-T/2}^{T/2} e^{-j\omega t}\,dt
$$

Evaluating the antiderivative and subtracting the endpoint values gives the sine-over-argument form. This is the same transform, now written in a way that makes the spectrum shape visible.

$$
X(\omega)=\frac{2\sin(\omega T/2)}{\omega}=T\,\mathrm{sinc}\!\left(\frac{\omega T}{2}\right)
$$

This centered pulse is the base case to remember. Later changes in amplitude, width, or time shift will reuse the same sinc envelope and adjust only the scale or phase.

## Evaluating the Transform Integral for a Centered Unit-Height Pulse

**Example:** Find the CTFT of $x(t)=1$ for $-T/2\le t\le T/2$ and $0$ otherwise, starting from the transform integral and simplifying the answer as far as possible.

**Explanation**

The cue is a centered, unit-height rectangular pulse of width $T$, so its support is exactly $[-T/2,\,T/2]$. Start from the CTFT definition and restrict the integral to that interval.

$$
X(\omega)=\int_{-T/2}^{T/2} e^{-j\omega t}\,dt
$$

Integrate $e^{-j\omega t}$ to get $\frac{e^{-j\omega t}}{-j\omega}$, then evaluate the antiderivative at the two endpoints. That gives the exponential-difference form below.

$$
X(\omega)=\left.\frac{e^{-j\omega t}}{-j\omega}\right|_{-T/2}^{T/2}=\frac{1}{-j\omega}\left(e^{-j\omega T/2}-e^{j\omega T/2}\right)
$$

Use Euler's relation to rewrite the exponential difference as a sine term. The centered pulse therefore produces a real, even spectrum with the compact sine-over-$\omega$ form below.

$$
X(\omega)=\frac{2\sin(\omega T/2)}{\omega}
$$

By continuity, the value at $\omega=0$ is $X(0)=T$. This is the base transform shape that later amplitude, width, and shift changes will modify.

<a id="converting-the-centered-pulse-transform-into-sinc-form"></a>
## Converting the Centered Pulse Transform into Sinc Form

**Example:** Given $X(\omega)=\frac{2\sin(\omega T/2)}{\omega}$ for the centered rectangular pulse, rewrite the answer in sinc form and identify the equivalent compact expression.

**Explanation**

The transform has already been found, so the cue here is the algebraic rewrite. Keep the same centered pulse notation and convert the sine-over-$\omega$ expression into the standard sinc form.

$$
X(\omega)=\frac{2\sin(\omega T/2)}{\omega}
$$

Factor out $T$ so the denominator becomes $\omega T/2$ and the remaining ratio matches the standard sinc definition. This is the same spectrum, just written in the compact form that makes the pulse-width scaling explicit.

$$
X(\omega)=T\,\frac{\sin(\omega T/2)}{\omega T/2}=T\,\mathrm{sinc}\!\left(\frac{\omega T}{2}\right)
$$

With $\mathrm{sinc}(u)=\frac{\sin u}{u}$, the rewrite preserves the real, even spectrum from the previous step. At $\omega=0$, the limit is still $X(0)=T$, so the compact form matches the original transform exactly.

## Including Amplitude and Width in the Scaled Sinc Transform

**Example:** Find the CTFT of $x(t)=A$ for $-T/2\le t\le T/2$ and $0$ otherwise, and express the answer in scaled sinc form.

**Explanation**

The cue is the same centered rectangular pulse as before, but now the pulse height is $A$ instead of $1$. Keep the same support interval and factor the amplitude out of the transform integral first.

$$
X(\omega)=\int_{-T/2}^{T/2} A\,e^{-j\omega t}\,dt
$$

The remaining integral is exactly the centered unit-height case, so the same sine-over-$\omega$ structure appears. The only new job is to preserve the factor $A$ all the way through the calculation.

$$
X(\omega)=A\,\frac{2\sin(\omega T/2)}{\omega}
$$

Rewrite the result in sinc form exactly as before, now with the amplitude factor still attached. The width $T$ appears in the sinc argument and in the overall scale factor.

$$
X(\omega)=AT\,\mathrm{sinc}\!\left(\frac{\omega T}{2}\right)
$$

This shows the scaling rule clearly: multiplying the pulse by $A$ multiplies the spectrum by $A$, and changing the width $T$ changes both the height and the horizontal spread of the sinc envelope. The centered pulse is still the same base shape, just rescaled.

## Including the Phase Factor for an Off-Center Rectangular Pulse

**Example:** Find the CTFT of $x(t)=1$ for $0\le t\le T$ and $0$ otherwise, and rewrite the result as a sinc spectrum with the correct linear phase factor.

**Explanation**

The cue is the support interval $0\le t\le T$. This is the same width-$T$ rectangle as before, but shifted right by $T/2$, so the new feature is a linear phase factor rather than a new sinc envelope.

$$
X(\omega)=\int_0^T e^{-j\omega t}\,dt
$$

Integrate over the nonzero interval and evaluate the endpoints. The result is the same exponential-difference form that appears for any shifted rectangle.

$$
X(\omega)=\frac{1-e^{-j\omega T}}{j\omega}
$$

Now factor the midpoint term $e^{-j\omega T/2}$ so the phase and magnitude separate cleanly. That leaves the same sine-over-$\omega$ structure as the centered pulse.

$$
X(\omega)=e^{-j\omega T/2}\frac{2\sin(\omega T/2)}{\omega}=T\,e^{-j\omega T/2}\,\mathrm{sinc}\left(\frac{\omega T}{2}\right)
$$

The sinc magnitude is unchanged from the centered case. The only new factor is the linear phase ramp from shifting the pulse by $T/2$.
