## Introduction to Time Reversal

Time reversal is the Fourier-transform property you use when you already know a pair and want the reflected one. In this section, the cue is a signal written as $x(-t)$, and the only new operation is to negate the time argument in the signal and the frequency argument in the spectrum. Use the simplest base case from the outline: start from one known pair with no scaling, shifting, or modulation.

$$
x(t) \leftrightarrow X(j\omega)
$$

Write the reversed pair directly by flipping the sign of the time variable in the signal and the sign of the frequency variable in the spectrum. That gives $x(-t)$ with transform $X(-j\omega)$, and nothing else changes.

$$
x(-t) \leftrightarrow X(-j\omega)
$$

The rule does not introduce a new amplitude factor or a different Fourier property; it only mirrors the time-domain signal and the frequency-domain spectrum. The next sections will apply that same one-step sign flip to an explicit pair, to a written spectrum expression, and to a reflected signal that must be recognized first.

## Reversing a Known Transform Pair

**Example:** Given the known pair $x(t)=e^{-2t}u(t) \leftrightarrow X(j\omega)=\frac{1}{2+j\omega}$, recognize that $e^{2t}u(-t)$ is $x(-t)$ and find its Fourier transform.

**Explanation**

The cue is the known Fourier pair $x(t)=e^{-2t}u(t)$ with spectrum $X(j\omega)=\frac{1}{2+j\omega}$. Because this is a time-reversal problem, keep the same pair and change only the time sign in the signal and the frequency sign in the spectrum.

$$
x(t)=e^{-2t}u(t) \leftrightarrow X(j\omega)=\frac{1}{2+j\omega}
$$

Reflect the signal by replacing $t$ with $-t$, which gives $x(-t)=e^{2t}u(-t)$. The causal exponential becomes anti-causal, but the amplitude form itself stays the same.

Now reflect the spectrum by replacing $j\omega$ with $-j\omega$, which gives $X(-j\omega)=\frac{1}{2-j\omega}$. No other Fourier property is involved.

$$
x(-t)=e^{2t}u(-t) \leftrightarrow X(-j\omega)=\frac{1}{2-j\omega}
$$

So the reversed transform pair is $e^{2t}u(-t) \leftrightarrow \frac{1}{2-j\omega}$: the time-domain signal is reflected, and the spectrum mirrors by the same sign flip. The next examples will reuse that one-step rule with different constants and with reflected forms that must first be recognized as $x(-t)$.

## Replacing Frequency Signs in a Spectrum

**Example:** If $X(j\omega)=\frac{2+j\omega}{4+j\omega}$, find the spectrum of $x(-t)$.

**Explanation**

The cue is the explicit spectrum $X(j\omega)=\frac{2+j\omega}{4+j\omega}$. This is the algebraic version of the time-reversal rule, so keep the same expression and replace only the frequency sign.

$$
X(j\omega)=\frac{2+j\omega}{4+j\omega}
$$

Replace $\omega$ by $-\omega$ everywhere in the spectrum. That changes every $j\omega$ into $-j\omega$, so the numerator becomes $2-j\omega$ and the denominator becomes $4-j\omega$.

$$
X(-j\omega)=\frac{2-j\omega}{4-j\omega}
$$

So the spectrum of the time-reversed signal is $X(-j\omega)=\frac{2-j\omega}{4-j\omega}$, with the constants unchanged and only the frequency signs flipped. The next examples will reuse the same substitution on different rational forms and on reflected signals that must first be recognized as $x(-t)$.

    $$
    X(-j\omega)=(1+j)\frac{1+j\omega}{3-j\omega}
    $$

    So the spectrum of $x(-t)$ is $(1+j)\frac{1+j\omega}{3-j\omega}$. The coefficients stay attached; only the frequency signs flip.

    $(1-j)\frac{1+j\omega}{3-j\omega}$

## Recognizing Reflected Signals Before Applying Time Reversal

**Example:** If $x(t)=e^{-2t}u(t)$ has transform $X(j\omega)=\frac{1}{2+j\omega}$, recognize that $e^{2t}u(-t)$ is $x(-t)$ and find its Fourier transform.

**Explanation**

The cue is the known pair $x(t)=e^{-2t}u(t)$ with spectrum $X(j\omega)=\frac{1}{2+j\omega}$, but the signal you are given is not written in the standard form yet. Recognize $e^{2t}u(-t)$ as the reflected signal $x(-t)$ before applying the property.

$$
x(t)=e^{-2t}u(t) \leftrightarrow X(j\omega)=\frac{1}{2+j\omega}
$$

Replacing $t$ with $-t$ gives $x(-t)=e^{2t}u(-t)$. That is the only recognition step needed here: once the reflected form is identified, the time-reversal rule applies directly.

Now flip the frequency sign in the spectrum. Replacing $j\omega$ by $-j\omega$ gives $X(-j\omega)=\frac{1}{2-j\omega}$, with no other changes.

$$
x(-t)=e^{2t}u(-t) \leftrightarrow X(-j\omega)=\frac{1}{2-j\omega}
$$

So the reflected signal $e^{2t}u(-t)$ has transform $\frac{1}{2-j\omega}$. The important move is recognizing the algebraic form as $x(-t)$ first, then applying the same sign flip used in the earlier examples.
