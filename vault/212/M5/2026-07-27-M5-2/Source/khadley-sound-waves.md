## Sound Waves

[![[Images/lwave-red-2.gif]]](http://www.acs.psu.edu/drussell/Demos/waves-intro/Lwave-Red-2.html)

[Animation courtesy of Dr. Dan Russell, Grad. Prog. Acoustics, Penn State](http://www.acs.psu.edu/drussell/demos.html)

Sound waves are longitudinal waves, consisting of compressions and rarefactions that carry the signal through a solid, liquid or gas.

<iframe src="https://phet.colorado.edu/sims/html/wave-interference/latest/wave-interference_en.html" width="800" height="600" allowfullscreen=""></iframe>

[PhET simulation](https://phet.colorado.edu/en/simulation/wave-interference)

Sound waves propagate out in a spherical pattern from the source. Increasing the amplitude of the wave makes the sound louder. Increasing the frequency of the wave makes the pitch higher.

The range of human hearing is about 20 Hz to 20 kHz.

[freemosquitoringtones.org/hearing\_test/](http://www.freemosquitoringtones.org/hearing_test/)

[audiocheck.net/audiotests\_frequencychecklow.php](http://www.audiocheck.net/audiotests_frequencychecklow.php)

[![[Images/monopole.gif]]](http://www.acs.psu.edu/drussell/Demos/rad2/monopole.html)

[Animation courtesy of Dr. Dan Russell, Grad. Prog. Acoustics, Penn State](http://www.acs.psu.edu/drussell/Demos/rad2/mdq.html)

When you hear a sound, the oscillating source produces a compression wave that propagates through the air (vsound = 343 m/s) to your eardrum, producing vibrations on your eardrum that your brain interprets.

## Practice Problems

```quiz

content: |-
  **Question 1**

  Using $v_{\mathrm{sound}}=343\ \mathrm{m/s}$, find the wavelength of a $20\ \mathrm{Hz}$ sound. Enter meters: ==17==
feedback: |-
  The wave relation gives $\lambda=v/f=(343\ \mathrm{m/s})/(20\ \mathrm{Hz})=17.15\ \mathrm m$, which rounds to $17\ \mathrm m$.
```

```quiz
  **Question 2**

  Listeners at $x=-7.0\ \mathrm m$ and $x=+3.0\ \mathrm m$ detect the same wavefront simultaneously from a source on the $x$-axis. Find the source's $x$-coordinate in meters: ==-2.0==
feedback: |-
  Simultaneous detection means equal source-to-listener distances, so the source lies at the midpoint: $(-7.0+3.0)/2=-2.0\ \mathrm m$.
```

```quiz
type: blank

content: |-
  **Question 3**

  A third listener lies on the positive $y$-axis and detects that wavefront simultaneously. Find the listener's $y$-coordinate in meters: ==4.6==
feedback: |-
  The wavefront radius is $5.0\ \mathrm m$. With source $(-2.0,0)$ and listener $(0,y)$, $2.0^2+y^2=5.0^2$, so $y=\sqrt{21}=4.6\ \mathrm m$.
```
