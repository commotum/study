## Center of Mass

The center of mass of an object or a system of objects is a weighted sum, defined as the sum of the produce of mass and position weighted by the total mass.

## Practice Problem

```quiz
type: free
id: khadley-rigid-rotation-q1
content: |-
  **Question 1**

  Two cubes of uniform density $\rho$, with side lengths $2L$ and $L$, are joined as shown. Find the system's horizontal center of mass, taking the large cube's left face as $x=0$.

  ![[Images/blocks.jpg]]
correct: |-
  The masses are proportional to volume: $M_1=8\rho L^3$ and $M_2=\rho L^3$. Their centers are at $x_1=L$ and $x_2=5L/2$, so
  $$x_{\mathrm{cm}}=\frac{M_1x_1+M_2x_2}{M_1+M_2}=\frac{7L}{6}.$$
feedback: |-
  Use each cube's geometric center for its position and weight that position by the cube's mass. Equal density does not mean equal mass because the cubes have different volumes.
```
