## Problem 4

**Question 4**

A uniform board of mass $M$ and length $L$ rests on supports A and B, located at $L/4$ and $3L/4$ from the board's left end. A box of mass $m$ is placed a distance $x$ to the right of support B.

![](<Source/2026-07-19-PQ-2/Images/problem-4-board-tipping.png>)

```quiz
type: free
id: q-4a
content: |-
  **Part A.** On paper, draw an extended free-body diagram of the board and a separate free-body diagram of the box. Label every force and relevant distance, and indicate your pivot point.
correct: |-
  Use support B as the pivot for the tipping condition.

  - Board: upward support forces $N_A$ and $N_B$; the board's weight $Mg$ downward at its center; and the box's downward contact force $N$ at the box's location.
  - Box: the board's normal force $N$ upward and the box's weight $mg$ downward.
  - Relevant lever arms about B: $L/4$ for the board's weight and $x$ for the box's contact force.
  - At the threshold of tipping, the board loses contact with support A, so $N_A=0$.
```

```quiz
type: blank
id: q-4b
require_exact: false
content: |-
  **Part B.** Find the farthest distance to the right of support B at which the box can be placed without tipping the board.

  The maximum distance is ==$x_{\max}=\dfrac{ML}{4m}$==.
feedback: |-
  At the threshold of tipping, take torques about support B:

  $$
  Mg\left(\frac{L}{4}\right)-mgx_{\max}=0.
  $$

  Therefore,

  $$
  x_{\max}=\frac{ML}{4m}.
  $$
```

## Problem 5

**Question 5**

Four identical stars, each of mass $m$, occupy the corners of a square of side length $L$. The stars move in circular orbits about their common center of mass.

![](<Source/2026-07-19-PQ-2/Images/problem-5-four-star-orbit.png>)

For all parts, show your work and express your answers in terms of the given variables.

```quiz
type: free
id: q-5a
content: |-
  **Part A.** On paper, draw a completely labeled free-body diagram of the top star.
correct: |-
  Draw three gravitational forces on the top star:

  - One force toward the left star, with magnitude $Gm^2/L^2$.
  - One force toward the right star, with magnitude $Gm^2/L^2$.
  - One force toward the bottom star, with magnitude $Gm^2/(2L^2)$ because the diagonal separation is $\sqrt{2}L$.

  The horizontal components of the forces from the left and right stars cancel. The remaining net force points downward, toward the center of the square.
```

```quiz
type: blank
id: q-5b
require_exact: false
content: |-
  **Part B.** Determine the net force on the top star.

  The net force has magnitude ==$F_{\mathrm{net}}=\left(\sqrt{2}+\dfrac{1}{2}\right)\dfrac{Gm^2}{L^2}$== and points toward the center of the square.
feedback: |-
  Each adjacent star contributes an inward component

  $$
  \frac{Gm^2}{L^2}\cos 45^\circ.
  $$

  The opposite star is $\sqrt{2}L$ away and contributes $Gm^2/(2L^2)$. Therefore,

  $$
  F_{\mathrm{net}}
  =2\left(\frac{Gm^2}{L^2}\cos45^\circ\right)+\frac{Gm^2}{2L^2}
  =\left(\sqrt{2}+\frac12\right)\frac{Gm^2}{L^2}.
  $$
```

```quiz
type: blank
id: q-5c
require_exact: false
content: |-
  **Part C.** Determine the speed of each star.

  The orbital speed is ==$v=\sqrt{\dfrac{Gm}{L}\left(1+\dfrac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}\right)}$==.
feedback: |-
  The orbital radius is the distance from a corner to the square's center:

  $$
  r=\frac{L}{\sqrt{2}}.
  $$

  Using $F_{\mathrm{net}}=mv^2/r$ gives

  $$
  v^2
  =\frac{F_{\mathrm{net}}r}{m}
  =\frac{Gm}{L}\left(1+\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}\right).
  $$
```

```quiz
type: blank
id: q-5d
require_exact: false
content: |-
  **Part D.** Determine the total gravitational potential energy of the four-star system.

  The total gravitational potential energy is ==$U=-\dfrac{Gm^2}{L}\left(4+\sqrt{2}\right)$==.
feedback: |-
  Count each pair once. There are four adjacent pairs separated by $L$ and two diagonal pairs separated by $\sqrt{2}L$:

  $$
  U
  =-Gm^2\left(\frac{4}{L}+\frac{2}{\sqrt{2}L}\right)
  =-\frac{Gm^2}{L}\left(4+\sqrt{2}\right).
  $$
```
