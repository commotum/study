## Lecture Outline (Physics 212 Course Orientation)

### 1. Course Introduction
- Course:
  $$ \text{Physics 212: General Physics with Calculus} $$
- Instructor:
  $$ \text{Kathy Hadley} $$
- The instructor explains that the course uses:
  - Canvas
  - course webpages
  - Zoom lectures
  - online homework
  - online labs
  - Poll Everywhere
  - Gradescope

---

### 2. Course Webpages and Canvas
- Canvas is the main course hub.
- Important Canvas module: 
$$ \text{Course Information} $$
- The course webpage link is found under:
$$ \text{Course Information} \rightarrow \text{General Information} $$
- The course webpages contain the instructor’s lecture materials.
- These webpages are not a full textbook replacement, but they highlight the material emphasized in lecture.

---

### 3. Required Textbook
- Required textbook:
$$ \text{Knight, Physics for Scientists and Engineers, 5th edition} $$
- The lecture materials focus on selected important topics from the textbook.
- Students are still expected to use the textbook as the official reference.

---

### 4. Zoom Lectures
- Regular Zoom lecture schedule:
$$ \text{Monday--Thursday, 11:00 AM Pacific Time} $$
- The same Zoom link is used throughout the term.
- The Zoom information is found in Canvas under:
$$ \text{Course Information} \rightarrow \text{Zoom Info} $$

---

### 5. Recorded Lectures
- Zoom lectures are recorded.
- Recordings are usually posted within a couple of hours after class.
- The instructor estimates that recordings are typically posted by:
  $$ 2:00\text{ PM} $$
  after an 11 AM lecture.
- If technical issues delay the recording, the instructor will post a message.

---

### 6. Where to Find Lecture Recordings
- Lecture videos are posted under:
  $$ \text{Student Resources} \rightarrow \text{Circular Motion Learning Materials} $$
- Recorded lecture videos appear below the instructional/tutorial videos.

---

### 7. Pre-Lecture Questions
- Pre-lecture questions are required for everyone.
- They are due:
  $$ 1\text{ hour before lecture} $$
- For an 11 AM lecture, the pre-lecture assignment is due at:
  $$ 10:00\text{ AM} $$
- Pre-lecture answers are posted shortly after the deadline.
- Because answers are posted immediately after the due time, pre-lecture questions are not accepted late.

---

### 8. Pre-Lecture Assignment Timing
- Pre-lecture assignments are usually posted at least:
  $$ 48\text{ hours} $$
  before they are due.
- The instructor typically posts the week’s pre-lecture questions on Saturday morning.
- There is generally a pre-lecture question before every lecture, except quiz days.

---

### 9. Pre-Lecture Partial Credit
- For calculation-type pre-lecture questions:
  - students may get another chance if the first answer is incorrect.
- For multiple-choice questions:
  - there is one submission attempt.
  - students can request partial credit by emailing the instructor within:
    $$ 48\text{ hours} $$
    of the due date.
- The email should explain:
  - what reasoning was used
  - what physics concepts were involved
  - what the correct answer is
  - any work that supports the answer
- Partial credit can be up to:
  $$ \frac{1}{2}\text{ credit} $$

---

### 10. Participation Options
- Students can earn participation credit in either of two ways:
  1. attend the Zoom lecture and answer Poll Everywhere questions
  2. complete the asynchronous participation assignment afterward
- Students cannot receive credit for both the Zoom participation and the asynchronous participation for the same lecture.

---

### 11. Zoom Participation
- During live Zoom lectures, participation is done through:
  $$ \text{Poll Everywhere} $$
- Poll Everywhere is free for students.
- If a student completes the Poll Everywhere questions during lecture, they are done for that day’s participation.

---

### 12. Asynchronous Participation
- If a student does not attend Zoom, or if they miss part of the Poll Everywhere questions, they can complete the asynchronous assignment.
- Asynchronous participation assignments are posted about:
  $$ 2\text{ hours after lecture} $$
- They are due approximately:
  $$ 48\text{ hours after posting} $$
- The instructor gives an additional grace period before marking them late.

---

### 13. Switching Between Live and Asynchronous Participation
- Students may switch between:
  - attending live Zoom lectures
  - completing asynchronous assignments
- Students do not need to use the same format every day.
- This flexibility allows students to adjust based on their schedule.

---

### 14. Homework
- Homework assignments are online in Canvas.
- Students do not need to buy Mastering Physics.
- Homework links are found on the required activities page.
- Homework is part of the course grade.

---

### 15. Quizzes
- There are three quizzes during the term.
- Quiz grade contribution:
  $$ 40\% $$
- The lowest quiz score is dropped.
- Quizzes are shorter than traditional midterms and are split into parts.

---

### 16. Quiz Format
- Each quiz has:
  - Part A
  - Part B
- Part A includes:
  - three multiple-choice questions
  - one short written question
- Part B includes:
  - a longer written question
- Written work is submitted through Gradescope.

---

### 17. Quiz Timing
- Each quiz part has:
  $$ 20\text{ minutes} + 5\text{ minutes for upload} $$
- There is a short break between Part A and Part B.
- Total quiz time is about:
  $$ 55\text{ minutes} $$

---

### 18. Quiz Proctoring Options
- Students may take quizzes:
  - live in Zoom
  - through Proctorio
- Zoom quiz sessions require:
  - working webcam
  - webcam on during the quiz
- There will also be evening Zoom quiz sessions, usually around:
  $$ 6:00\text{ PM} $$

---

### 19. Proctorio Quizzes
- Proctorio versions of quizzes are usually open for about:
  $$ 48\text{ hours} $$
- They typically open at:
  $$ 5:00\text{ PM Saturday} $$
- They typically close at:
  $$ 5:00\text{ PM Monday} $$
- Students using Proctorio must complete the Proctorio practice quiz first.

---

### 20. Quiz Question Versions
- Zoom quiz sessions and Proctorio quizzes may use different versions of questions.
- Proctorio questions are selected from a question bank.
- The difficulty level is intended to be the same, but not every student receives identical questions.

---

### 21. Final Exam
- The final exam is worth:
  $$ 15\% $$
- It uses a similar format to the quizzes.
- It is longer, approximately:
  $$ 90\text{ minutes} $$
- The final may include an additional part, often multiple choice or a mixture of formats.
- The final Zoom session is scheduled during the final class meeting.

---

### 22. Note Sheets for Quizzes and Exams
- Students may use a note sheet.
- The note sheet must:
  - be handwritten
  - be the student’s own work
  - be between half a page and one full page
  - include the student’s photo ID at the top when submitted
- It must be submitted before starting the quiz.
- Other reference sheets are not allowed during quizzes or exams.
- The note sheet is worth points as part of the quiz.

---

### 23. Gradescope
- Gradescope is used for submitting written work.
- Students will have a practice assignment for uploading to Gradescope.
- Written quiz work must be scanned and uploaded within the allowed upload window.

---

### 25. Lab Requirement
- Students must pass the lab to pass the course.
- To pass lab, students must:
  - submit every lab report
  - average at least:
    $$ 70\% $$
    in the lab component
- If a student does not pass the lab, they do not pass the course.

---

### 26. Lab Opt-Out Option
- Students may opt out of lab only if they took the same course at OSU within the last two years and passed the lab/course with at least:
  $$ D^- $$
- To request a lab opt-out, students should email the instructor with:
  - subject line:
    $$ \text{Lab opt-out request for Physics 212} $$
  - name
  - OSU ID
  - term and year taken
  - previous instructor
  - grade earned
- The instructor verifies the information before approving the opt-out.
- If approved, the lab portion is removed from the grade calculation.

---

### 27. Lab Format
- Labs are completed individually at home using accessible materials.
- Students may substitute equivalent household materials when appropriate.
- A materials list is available at the start of the course.
- Most students should not need to buy many materials.

---

### 28. Weekly Lab Workflow
- The lab document becomes available early in the week.
- By Wednesday, students should usually:
  - perform the experiment
  - collect initial data
  - answer initial questions
  - post in the group discussion
- Students then meet with their lab TA.
- By Saturday, the group submits one consolidated lab report.

---

### 29. Lab Groups
- Students choose their own lab groups.
- Groups may have up to:
  $$ 5\text{ students} $$
- Students introduce themselves in the course introduction discussion.
- Students use the discussion to find compatible group members based on:
  - schedule
  - time zone
  - availability
  - major or interests

---

### 30. Lab TA Meetings
- Students are required to meet with a lab TA.
- Credit for the TA meeting depends on active participation, not just attendance.
- Students should:
  - attend the meeting
  - turn on their webcam
  - ask questions
  - participate in discussion
- If a student’s schedule changes week to week, they should coordinate with the TA to attend another group’s meeting when necessary.

---

### 31. Lab 1 Special Case
- Lab 1 is handled differently because groups are still forming.
- The first lab report is submitted individually.
- After Lab 1, groups submit one group report.

---

### 32. Virtual Wormhole
- The Virtual Wormhole is free tutoring through the physics department.
- It is available through the course information page.
- Students can use it for academic help.

---

### 33. Late Policies
- Lab assignments:
  $$ 10\%\text{ deduction per day late} $$
  but not below:
  $$ 50\% $$
- Participation assignments:
  $$ 1\text{ point per day late} $$
  but not below:
  $$ 50\% $$
- Homework assignments:
  $$ 1\text{ point per day late} $$
  and not accepted after solutions are posted.
- Quizzes, final exam, pre-lecture questions, and extra credit assignments are not accepted late.

---

### 34. Course Calendar and Due Dates
- The syllabus calendar lists major deadlines.
- Canvas calendar may show assignments, but unpublished or placeholder assignments can make it confusing.
- The instructor recommends using:
  - the syllabus calendar
  - the required activities page
  - Canvas assignments

---

### 35. Poll Everywhere Practice
- The instructor uses a Poll Everywhere word cloud as practice.
- Students answer:
  $$ \text{“What is your biggest concern?”} $$
- Common concerns include:
  - time
  - lab
  - falling behind
  - due dates
  - exams
  - workload

---

### 36. Main Course Success Advice
- Do not fall behind.
- Set aside time every day for the course.
- Suggested minimum daily work time:
  $$ 1\text{ hour per day} $$
- Additional time is needed for:
  - reading
  - homework
  - labs
  - quiz preparation

---

### 37. Office Hour
- The instructor holds office hour immediately after class.
- Office hour is not recorded.
- Students can stay after lecture to ask individual questions.

---

### 38. Main Takeaways
- Physics 212 uses a structured online format with:
  - Zoom lectures
  - Canvas activities
  - Poll Everywhere participation
  - online homework
  - Gradescope submissions
  - group-based labs
- Pre-lecture questions are required before lectures.
- Participation can be completed live or asynchronously.
- Quizzes require webcams or Proctorio and handwritten work submitted to Gradescope.
- Lab is mandatory unless officially opted out.
- Staying current with deadlines is essential for success.
