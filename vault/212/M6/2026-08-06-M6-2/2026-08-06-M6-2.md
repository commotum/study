
## Problem 1

```quiz
type: blank
id: m6-2pre-q1
require_exact: true
content: |-
  **Question 1**

  A helium-neon laser of wavelength $633\ \mathrm{nm}$ illuminates a single slit, and fringes are observed on a screen $1.50\ \mathrm{m}$ beyond the slit. The distance between the first and second minima in the diffraction pattern is $5.75\ \mathrm{mm}$.

  What is the width of the slit in millimeters? Enter a number only: ==0.165==
feedback: |-
  Adjacent single-slit minima are separated by $\Delta y\approx\lambda L/a$, so

  $$
  a
  =\frac{\lambda L}{\Delta y}
  =\frac{(633\times10^{-9}\ \mathrm{m})(1.50\ \mathrm{m})}{5.75\times10^{-3}\ \mathrm{m}}
  =1.6513\ldots\times10^{-4}\ \mathrm{m}.
  $$

  To three significant figures, the slit width is $0.165\ \mathrm{mm}$, entered as `0.165`.
```

## Problem 2

```quiz
type: blank
id: m6-2lec-q1
require_exact: true
content: |-
  **Question 1**

  A helium-neon laser with wavelength $\lambda=633\ \mathrm{nm}$ illuminates a diffraction grating. On a screen $2.4\ \mathrm{m}$ away, the distance between the two $m=1$ bright fringes is $170\ \mathrm{cm}$.

  How many slits per millimeter are in the diffraction grating? Enter a number only: ==530==
feedback: |-
  The two first-order fringes are symmetric, so each is $y_1=0.85\ \mathrm{m}$ from the center. The angle is

  $$
  \theta_1=\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{0.85}{2.4}\right)=19.502\ldots^\circ.
  $$

  For $m=1$, $d\sin\theta_1=\lambda$, so the line density is

  $$
  \frac{1}{d}
  =\frac{\sin\theta_1}{\lambda}
  =5.274\ldots\times10^5\ \mathrm{lines/m}
  =527.4\ldots\ \mathrm{lines/mm}.
  $$

  The limiting measured value has two significant figures, so the result is $5.3\times10^2\ \mathrm{lines/mm}$, entered as `530`.
```

## Problem 3

```quiz
type: blank
id: m6-2lec-q2
require_exact: true
content: |-
  **Question 2**

  A diffraction grating with lines spaced $1.8\ \mu\mathrm{m}$ apart is illuminated by a laser with wavelength $633\ \mathrm{nm}$. If a $2.4\ \mathrm{m}$-wide screen is placed $0.85\ \mathrm{m}$ from the grating, how many bright fringes are seen on the screen? ==5==
feedback: |-
  Grating maxima satisfy $d\sin\theta_m=m\lambda$. The physical limit gives

  $$
  |m|\leq\frac{d}{\lambda}
  =\frac{1.8\times10^{-6}}{633\times10^{-9}}
  =2.84\ldots,
  $$

  so only $m=0,\pm1,\pm2$ can exist. The second-order maxima land at $|y_2|=0.841\ \mathrm{m}$, within the screen's $1.2\ \mathrm{m}$ half-width. Therefore all five allowed bright fringes are visible.
```

## Problem 5

```quiz
type: blank

require_exact: true
content: |-
  **Question 4**

  A laser of wavelength $633\ \mathrm{nm}$ is incident on a single slit of width $0.145\ \mathrm{mm}$, producing a diffraction pattern on a screen $1.28\ \mathrm{m}$ away. What is the distance from the central maximum to the second bright fringe? Enter your answer in centimeters as a number only, using three significant figures: ==1.40==
feedback: |-
  In the approximation used in this lecture, the second secondary bright fringe lies halfway between the second and third dark minima. Thus,

  $$
  y_{\mathrm{bright},2}
  \approx\frac{y_2+y_3}{2}
  =\frac{5\lambda L}{2a}
  =\frac{5(633\times10^{-9}\ \mathrm{m})(1.28\ \mathrm{m})}{2(0.145\times10^{-3}\ \mathrm{m})}
  =1.3969\ldots\ \mathrm{cm}.
  $$

  To three significant figures, the distance is $1.40\ \mathrm{cm}$, entered as `1.40`.
```
