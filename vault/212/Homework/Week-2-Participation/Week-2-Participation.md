A person is riding on a Ferris wheel which is rotating at constant angular speed. How does the magnitude of the normal force of the Ferris Wheel on the person at the top of the wheel compare to the magnitude of the normal force of the wheel on the person at the bottom of the wheel?

Group of answer choices

Ntop > Nbottom

Ntop = Nbottom

Ntop < Nbottom

**Ntop < Nbottom**

At the **top**, centripetal acceleration points downward, so gravity helps provide the inward force:

[  
mg - N_{\text{top}} = \frac{mv^2}{r}  
]

[  
N_{\text{top}} = mg - \frac{mv^2}{r}  
]

At the **bottom**, centripetal acceleration points upward, so the normal force must overcome gravity and provide the inward force:

[  
N_{\text{bottom}} - mg = \frac{mv^2}{r}  
]

[  
N_{\text{bottom}} = mg + \frac{mv^2}{r}  
]

So the normal force is **larger at the bottom** than at the top.

Question 2

1 pts

A person rides on a Ferris wheel of radius $r$ at constant angular velocity $\omega$. How does the normal force exerted on the rider from their seat at the top compare to the normal force on the rider from their seat at the bottom? Explain.
◯ $\mathrm{N}_{\text {top }}=\mathrm{N}_{\text {bottom }}$
◯ $\mathrm{N}_{\text {top }}>\mathrm{N}_{\text {bottom }}$
◯ $\mathrm{N}_{\text {top }}<\mathrm{N}_{\text {bottom }}$

[  
\boxed{N_{\text{top}} < N_{\text{bottom}}}  
]

At the top of the Ferris wheel, the centripetal acceleration points downward, so:

[  
mg - N_{\text{top}} = m\omega^2 r  
]

[  
N_{\text{top}} = mg - m\omega^2 r  
]

At the bottom, the centripetal acceleration points upward, so:

[  
N_{\text{bottom}} - mg = m\omega^2 r  
]

[  
N_{\text{bottom}} = mg + m\omega^2 r  
]

Therefore:

[  
\boxed{N_{\text{top}} < N_{\text{bottom}}}  
]

Question 5

1 pts

A 1.3 g coin on a turntable at radius 0.35 m has max static friction coefficient between the coin and surface of 0.18 . Find $\omega$ in $\mathrm{rad} / \mathrm{s}$ such that the coin just starts to slip.
□

At the point where the coin just starts to slip, static friction is at its maximum and provides the centripetal force:

[  
f_s = m\omega^2r  
]

[  
\mu_s mg = m\omega^2r  
]

Cancel (m):

[  
\mu_s g = \omega^2r  
]

[  
\omega = \sqrt{\frac{\mu_s g}{r}}  
]

Substitute:

[  
\omega = \sqrt{\frac{(0.18)(9.8)}{0.35}}  
]

[  
\omega = \sqrt{5.04}  
]

[  
\omega = 2.24\text{ rad/s} \rightarrow \boxed{2.2\text{ rad/s}}  
]

For the answer box:

[  
\boxed{2.2}  
]

◯ A
◯ B
◯ C
◯ D
◯ $E$

**B**

Because the curve is **icy**, there is **no friction**. The only forces on the car are:

[  
mg  
]

downward, and the **normal force** perpendicular to the banked surface.

The normal force points **up and toward the center of the curve**, which is up-left in the side view. So the correct free-body diagram is **B**.

A particle is moving around a circle, with an arrow depicting the magnitude and direction of the net force acting on the particle. Which diagram represents a particle speeding up in the counterclockwise direction?

![[Pasted image 20260706020242.png]]

◯ A
◯ B
◯ C
◯ D
◯ E
◯ F
◯ G
◯ H

**C**

For a particle speeding up while moving **counterclockwise**, the net force must have:

[  
F_{\text{net}} = F_{\text{centripetal}} + F_{\text{tangential}}  
]

At the lower-right side of the circle:

- **Centripetal force** points inward, toward the center: up-left.
    
- **Tangential force** for counterclockwise speeding up points along motion: up-right.
    

The net force should point somewhere between those directions, mostly upward. That matches **diagram C**.
