## [PH 212](http://khadley.com/Courses/Physics/ph_212/topics/index.aspx)

![[Images/13-19-figure.jpg]]

The energy diagram above shows the energy of a satellite in a circular orbit.

A satellite orbiting a massive object is a bound system. Total energy in a bound system is negative, reflecting the fact that the satellite cannot escape.

- Suppose satellite is in orbit at r 1 and we want to increase the orbit to r 2
	- Need to add energy to the satellite
- Satellite moves more slowly in the larger orbit r2, has less kinetic energy
- Total energy increases as r increases
- Satellite had to fire thruster to move to a higher orbit

Escape velocity is the threshold velocity needed for a projectile launched from a massive body to just reach infinity before falling back down. An object launched with a speed faster than escape speed will get away from the planet (become unbound).

## Practice Problems

```quiz
type: free
id: khadley-gravitational-energy-q1
content: |-
  **Question 1**

  Find the escape speed from a planet of mass $M$ and radius $r$.
correct: |-
  Setting the escape-threshold total energy to zero gives
  $$v_{\mathrm{esc}}=\sqrt{\frac{2GM}{r}}.$$
feedback: |-
  At the escape threshold, the object reaches infinity with zero speed, where gravitational potential energy is defined as zero.
```

```quiz
type: free
id: khadley-gravitational-energy-q2
content: |-
  **Question 2**

  Find the altitude of a geostationary satellite above a planet of mass $M$ and radius $R$, using rotation period $T$.
correct: |-
  $$h=\left(\frac{GMT^2}{4\pi^2}\right)^{1/3}-R.$$
feedback: |-
  The circular-orbit equation gives distance from the planet's center, so subtract the planetary radius to obtain altitude.
```

```quiz
type: free
id: khadley-gravitational-energy-q3
content: |-
  **Question 3**

  Stars of masses $2.5\times10^{30}\ \mathrm{kg}$ and $5.0\times10^{30}\ \mathrm{kg}$ are separated by $3.0\times10^{12}\ \mathrm m$. Locate the center of mass from the larger star and find the orbital period.
correct: |-
  $$x_{\mathrm{cm}}=1.0\times10^{12}\ \mathrm m,$$
  measured toward the smaller star, and
  $$T=2\pi\sqrt{\frac{r^3}{G(M_1+M_2)}}=1.5\times10^9\ \mathrm s,$$
  or about $46$ years.
feedback: |-
  Use the full star-to-star separation in the two-body period formula. Center-of-mass distances divide that separation inversely to the masses.
```
