| $\sum_i$ | add the contribution from every indexed object $i$ |
| $\sum_{i<j}$ | add every unique pair once, avoiding double counting |
| $\int$ | continuously add infinitesimal contributions |
| $d/dt$ | rate of change with respect to time |
| $\times$ | vector cross product when placed between vectors |
| $|\tau|$ | magnitude of the torque vector |
| $\Rightarrow$ | implies or therefore |
| $\ne$ | is not equal to |
| $\perp$ | perpendicular |

## Important Same-Letter Warnings

- $G$ is the universal gravitational constant; lowercase $g$ is local gravitational acceleration.
- $\vec p$ is one particle’s linear momentum; $\vec P$ is the total momentum of a system.
- $L$ usually means a geometric length, while $\vec L$ or a labeled $L$ means angular momentum.
- $T_1$ and $T_2$ are tensions, while $T_{\mathrm{orbit}}$ is an orbital period.
- $a$ normally means linear acceleration, but in

  $$
  T^2=\frac{4\pi^2}{GM}a^3
  $$

  it means the orbit’s semimajor axis.
- The subscript $i$ means “object number $i$” inside a sum, but it means “initial” when paired with a final subscript $f$.
- $M$ and $m$ do not inherently mean different kinds of mass. Problems conventionally use $M$ for a total, larger, pulley, or central mass and $m$ for an individual or smaller mass.
- $r$, $R$, $d$, and $L$ are all distances; the diagram and equation determine whether each one is a radius, separation, lever arm, axis offset, or overall length.
