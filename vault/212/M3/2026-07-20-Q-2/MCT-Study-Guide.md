## Optimized Study Order

### 1. Torque and Center of Mass

1. [ ] [Calculate Signed Torque from a Force's Line of Action](<Lessons/MCT-Problem-1.md>) — identify the pivot, perpendicular lever arm, and clockwise/counterclockwise sign.
2. [ ] [Use Torque Balance to Trade Force for Lever Arm](<Lessons/MCT-Problem-2.md>) — set opposing moments equal and solve for the unknown force or distance.
3. [ ] [Locate a Center of Mass from a Coordinate Ledger](<Lessons/MCT-Problem-3.md>) — choose an origin, record signed coordinates, and divide the mass moment by total mass.
4. [ ] [Place an Added Mass to Hit a Target Center of Mass](<Lessons/MCT-Problem-4.md>) — put the unknown position into the center-of-mass equation before solving.

### 2. Build and Shift Rotational Inertia

5. [ ] [Rank Rotational Inertia from Mass Distribution](<Lessons/MCT-Problem-5.md>) — reason from the $r^2$ weighting before reaching for a formula.
6. [ ] [Build the Moment of Inertia of Discrete Masses About a Chosen Axis](<Lessons/MCT-Problem-6.md>) — measure every mass from the stated axis and sum $m_ir_i^2$.
7. [ ] [Shift a Known Center-of-Mass Inertia to a Parallel Axis](<Lessons/MCT-Problem-7.md>) — distinguish the body's center-of-mass axis from the displaced parallel axis.

### 3. Rotational Dynamics and Static Equilibrium

8. [ ] [Find the Acceleration of a Falling Mass Driving a Massive Pulley](<Lessons/MCT-Problem-8.md>) — coordinate the hanging mass's force equation with $\tau=I\alpha$ and $a=\alpha R$.
9. [ ] [Solve a Two-Block System with a Massive Pulley](<Lessons/MCT-Problem-9.md>) — keep the two string tensions distinct and solve the coupled translation–rotation system.
10. [ ] [Solve Particle Equilibrium with Cable Components](<Lessons/MCT-Problem-10.md>) — resolve each cable before imposing zero net force.
11. [ ] [Choose a Pivot to Solve Unknown Support Reactions](<Lessons/MCT-Problem-11.md>) — eliminate the most inconvenient unknown by choosing the torque axis deliberately.
12. [ ] [Solve a Hinged Beam Supported by a Cable](<Lessons/MCT-Problem-12.md>) — combine torque balance with horizontal and vertical force balance.
13. [ ] [Solve Ladder Equilibrium and the Static-Friction Threshold](<Lessons/MCT-Problem-13.md>) — find the required friction, then compare it with $\mu_sN$.

### 4. Rotational Energy and Angular Momentum

14. [ ] [Calculate Rotational Kinetic Energy from the Correct Inertia](<Lessons/MCT-Problem-14.md>) — select the inertia for the actual shape and axis before using $K_r=\tfrac12I\omega^2$.
15. [ ] [Find Rolling Speed by Including Both Kinetic-Energy Terms](<Lessons/MCT-Problem-15.md>) — retain translation and rotation and impose $v=\omega R$ only for rolling without slipping.
16. [ ] [Use Angular Impulse to Update Angular Momentum](<Lessons/MCT-Problem-16.md>) — turn torque over time into $\Delta L$ with a consistent sign convention.
17. [ ] [Conserve Angular Momentum When the System's Inertia Changes](<Lessons/MCT-Problem-17.md>) — choose the isolated system, build $I_i$ and $I_f$, and solve $I_i\omega_i=I_f\omega_f$.

### 5. Gravitation and Orbits

18. [ ] [Solve a Pairwise Universal-Gravitation Equation](<Lessons/MCT-Problem-18.md>) — prepare center-to-center separation and isolate the requested mass, force, or distance.
19. [ ] [Calculate Gravitational Field Strength at a Radius or Altitude](<Lessons/MCT-Problem-19.md>) — convert altitude to distance from the planet's center before applying the inverse-square law.
20. [ ] [Solve Circular-Satellite Speed, Period, and Geostationary Altitude](<Lessons/MCT-Problem-20.md>) — equate gravity with the required radial force and carry the result into orbit period or radius.
21. [ ] [Use Kepler's Third Law for Period, Radius, or Central Mass](<Lessons/MCT-Problem-21.md>) — compare orbit ratios only after identifying the common central mass.
22. [ ] [Add Vectors with a Signed Component Ledger](<Lessons/MCT-Problem-22.md>) — resolve pairwise forces into components and preserve signs through the final resultant.

- Problems 18–21 cover universal gravitation, field strength, satellite motion, geostationary orbit, and Kepler ratios.
- Problem 22 preserves the component-addition workflow used for the multi-body gravitational examples.
