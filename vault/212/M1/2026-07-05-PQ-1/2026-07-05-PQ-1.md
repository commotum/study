## Problem 4

A coin of mass $m$ sits a distance $r$ from the axis of a horizontal turntable rotating at constant angular speed. The coefficient of static friction between the coin and turntable is $\mu_s$.

![](<Source/Images/problem-4-turntable.png>)

```quiz
type: free
id: q-4a
content: |-
  **Question 4 — Part A**

  Draw a free-body diagram of the coin. Label all force vectors and the radial and vertical axes, and keep vector directions and lengths consistent.
correct: |-
  ![](<Source/Images/problem-4-free-body-diagram.png>)

  Draw three forces:

  - the normal force $N$ upward;
  - the weight $mg$ downward;
  - static friction $f_s$ horizontally inward toward the rotation axis.

  Vertical equilibrium requires $N=mg$. Static friction supplies the inward radial force.
```

```quiz
type: blank
id: q-4b
require_exact: false
content: |-
  **Question 4 — Part B**

  Find the smallest rotation period the turntable can have without the coin slipping. Solve symbolically in terms of the given variables.

  Enter a keyboard-form expression using pi, r, mu_s, and g, then reveal the reference result: ==2*pi*sqrt(r/(mu_s*g))==
feedback: |-
  At the threshold of slipping, static friction reaches its maximum value:

  $$
  f_{s,\max}=\mu_sN=\mu_smg.
  $$

  This force supplies the radial acceleration:

  $$
  \mu_smg=m\omega^2r.
  $$

  Therefore,

  $$
  \omega_{\max}=\sqrt{\frac{\mu_sg}{r}},
  \qquad
  T_{\min}=\frac{2\pi}{\omega_{\max}}
  =2\pi\sqrt{\frac{r}{\mu_sg}}.
  $$
```

## Problem 5

A small bead of mass $m$ slides without friction around the inside of an inverted cone. Its horizontal circular path has radius $r$ and is at height $h$ above the cone's vertex.

![](<Source/Images/problem-5-inverted-cone.png>)

```quiz
type: free
id: q-5a
content: |-
  **Question 5 — Part A**

  Draw a free-body diagram of the bead. Label all force vectors, axes, and angles, and keep vector directions and lengths consistent.
correct: |-
  ![](<Source/Images/problem-5-free-body-diagram.png>)

  Draw two forces:

  - the weight $mg$ vertically downward;
  - the normal force $N$ perpendicular to the cone's surface, directed upward and inward.

  Resolve $N$ into an upward component $N\sin\theta$ and an inward radial component $N\cos\theta$.
```

```quiz
type: blank
id: q-5b
require_exact: false
content: |-
  **Question 5 — Part B**

  Find the bead's orbital period. Solve symbolically in terms of the given variables.

  Enter a keyboard-form expression using pi, r, g, and h, then reveal the reference result: ==2*pi*r/sqrt(g*h)==
feedback: |-
  The force equations are

  $$
  N\sin\theta=mg,
  \qquad
  N\cos\theta=\frac{mv^2}{r}.
  $$

  Dividing the equations gives

  $$
  \tan\theta=\frac{gr}{v^2}.
  $$

  The cone geometry gives $\tan\theta=r/h$, so

  $$
  v=\sqrt{gh}.
  $$

  Therefore, the orbital period is

  $$
  T=\frac{2\pi r}{v}
  =\frac{2\pi r}{\sqrt{gh}}.
  $$
```

```quiz
type: blank
id: q-5c
require_exact: true
content: |-
  **Question 5 — Part C**

  Using the given variables, identify two ways to decrease the orbital period.

  To decrease the period, ==decrease== $r$ or ==increase== $h$.
feedback: |-
  Since

  $$
  T=\frac{2\pi r}{\sqrt{gh}},
  $$

  decreasing $r$ reduces the numerator, while increasing $h$ increases the denominator. Either change decreases the period.
```
