# Physics 212: Course Orientation, Assessments, and Laboratory Structure

Welcome to Physics 212.

My name is Kathy Hadley, and I will be your instructor this term. I am an astrophysicist, and I also developed this version of the course.

Today’s lecture will focus on the structure of Physics 212: where to find the course materials, how the assignments and assessments work, how laboratory groups are organized, and what you should do to remain successful in this accelerated summer course.

### Physics 212 Course Webpages

The Physics 212 webpages contain the lecture material that I use when teaching the course.

When this course was taught in a large lecture hall, I used PowerPoint slides on the classroom screens. When the course moved online, I reorganized that material into web-based lecture pages.

These pages contain the concepts, diagrams, simulations, examples, and problems that we will emphasize during class. They are not intended to replace the textbook.

## Participation

There are two ways to earn participation credit:

1. Attend the live Zoom lecture and answer the Poll Everywhere questions.
2. Watch the lecture recording and complete the asynchronous participation assignment.

You may switch between these two methods throughout the term. You do not have to attend every lecture live or complete every lecture asynchronously.

However, you may receive participation credit only once for each lecture. Completing both the Poll Everywhere questions and the asynchronous assignment does not provide additional or extra credit.

If you attend the live lecture but cannot finish the Poll Everywhere questions because of an internet or technical problem, you may complete the asynchronous assignment instead.

### Poll Everywhere

Poll Everywhere is free for students in this course.

When a question is activated during class, follow the Poll Everywhere link and submit your response. The first time you use the system, you may need to register.

Live participation is based on making a genuine attempt. You generally receive participation credit for submitting an answer, whether or not that answer is correct.

### Asynchronous Participation Assignments

An asynchronous participation assignment is normally posted about two hours after the corresponding lecture.

The listed deadline is approximately $48$ hours after the assignment is posted. There is also an additional one-day grace period before a late deduction begins.

After the grace period:

- The score is reduced by one point per day late.
- The score does not fall below $50\%$ solely because of lateness.
- The assignment generally remains available throughout the term.

When Poll Everywhere synchronizes with Canvas, students who did not attend the live lecture may temporarily see a zero in the gradebook. That temporary score will be corrected after the corresponding asynchronous assignments are graded.

## Pre-Lecture Questions

A pre-lecture assignment will normally be due one hour before each regular lecture.

There will not be a pre-lecture assignment before a quiz session.

The assignments for the upcoming week are generally posted on Saturday morning. This provides at least $48$ hours to complete the first assignment of the week and more time for the later assignments.

Pre-lecture assignments are different from asynchronous participation assignments:

- Pre-lecture questions are due one hour before class.
- The solutions are posted shortly after the deadline.
- Pre-lecture assignments are therefore not accepted late.
- Every student completes the same pre-lecture assignment, regardless of whether the lecture itself is attended live or asynchronously.

### Attempts and Partial Credit

For numerical calculation questions, you will normally have a second attempt if your first answer is incorrect.

Multiple-choice questions generally permit only one submission.

If you submit a pre-lecture question on time but answer it incorrectly, you may request up to half credit by emailing me within $48$ hours of the deadline.

Your email should explain:

- The reasoning that led to your original answer
- The physics concepts you were using
- The correct answer
- Why the corrected answer is appropriate
- Any work supporting your explanation

The emphasis should be on your physics reasoning rather than simply stating that you made a mistake.

## Assessment Note Sheets

You are required to prepare your own note sheet for each quiz and for the final exam.

Each note sheet must:

- Be written entirely by hand
- Be written on physical paper
- Be at least one-half page
- Be no more than one full page
- Contain material relevant to the assessment
- Be legible
- Be submitted to Gradescope before you begin the assessment
- Be photographed or scanned with a valid photo ID placed on top of it

An Oregon State University ID or another valid photo ID may be used. You may cover sensitive information, such as an address, before taking the photograph.

The note sheet is the only reference material permitted during a quiz. A printed reference sheet, reduced photocopy, or equation sheet from another course may be useful for studying, but it may not be used during the assessment.

Part of the Quiz Part A score is awarded for the note sheet. The grading considers whether it:

- Meets the size requirements
- Is handwritten
- Is legible
- Contains relevant material
- Was submitted before the quiz began
- Includes the required photo ID

You should build each note sheet as the unit progresses rather than waiting until the day of the quiz.

## Quiz Structure

There are three quizzes during the accelerated summer term, followed by a final exam.

The course does not have a separate traditional midterm examination. Each quiz is divided into two required parts.

### Quiz Part A

Part A contains:

- Three multiple-choice questions
- One short written-response question

You will have:

- $20$ minutes to complete the questions
- $5$ additional minutes to upload the written work

### Quiz Part B

Part B contains one longer written-response problem.

You will have:

- $20$ minutes to complete the problem
- $5$ additional minutes to upload the written work

There is normally a five-minute break between Parts A and B.

Both parts are required. They are not alternative versions of the same quiz.

### Quiz Coverage

According to the course schedule:

- Quiz 1 covers Chapters 4 and 8.
- Quiz 2 covers Chapters 12 and 13.
- Quiz 3 covers Chapters 15, 16, and 17.

Exact assessment dates are listed in the PDF syllabus.

## Laboratory Requirement

The laboratory component is required.

To pass the course, you must:

- Submit every required laboratory report
- Earn an average of at least $70\%$ in the laboratory component

A student who does not pass the laboratory component cannot pass Physics 212, regardless of the scores earned in the other parts of the course.

This situation is rare, but the requirement is firm.

## Laboratory Opt-Out Policy

You may be eligible to opt out of the laboratory component if:

- You previously completed Physics 212 at Oregon State University
- You completed it within the previous two years
- You earned a grade of D-minus or better

To request a laboratory opt-out, send an email with a subject such as:

`Physics 212 Lab Opt-Out Request`

Include:

- Your full name
- Your OSU ID
- The term and year in which you completed the course
- The name of your previous instructor
- The grade you earned

The information will be verified with the previous instructor.

If the request is approved, the laboratory component will be removed from your grade calculation. Your course grade will then be based on the remaining components rather than treating the laboratory as a zero.

Submit the request promptly. If you intend to opt out, do not begin submitting laboratory assignments while the request is being processed.

## Laboratory Materials

The laboratory experiments are performed individually at home using accessible materials.

A laboratory-materials list is provided near the beginning of the course. Many of the listed objects may already be available in your home.

Reasonable substitutions are allowed. For example, if an experiment asks for a board and a piece of stiff cardboard performs the same function, you may use the cardboard.

The purpose is to perform the experiment and investigate the physics, not to purchase a specific commercial object.

## Weekly Laboratory Workflow

A typical laboratory week follows this structure.

### 1. Complete the Experiment

The laboratory document will normally become available near the beginning of the week.

Perform the experiment individually, collect initial data, and answer the preliminary questions.

### 2. Post in the Group Discussion

Post your data, observations, and initial answers in your laboratory group’s Canvas discussion.

These posts are normally due Wednesday.

### 3. Meet with a Laboratory TA

Each group meets with a laboratory teaching assistant.

Use this meeting to:

- Discuss the experiment
- Ask questions
- Compare results
- Address difficulties
- Clarify the analysis
- Prepare for the group report

Active participation is required. Simply entering the Zoom room without contributing is not sufficient for full credit.

You should:

- Turn on your webcam
- Participate in the conversation
- Ask or answer questions
- Discuss your experimental results

### 4. Prepare the Group Report

After discussing the experiment, the group combines its results, analysis, and answers into one report.

Only one group report is submitted for the entire group.

The report is normally due Saturday and is submitted through Gradescope.

Your group may meet outside the TA session, but a separate live group meeting is not required. Some groups collaborate asynchronously through Canvas, shared documents, email, or other communication tools.

## Laboratory Group Formation

Laboratory groups may contain up to five students.

To form a group:

1. Post an introduction in the course-introduction discussion.
2. Include information about your schedule and availability.
3. Read the introductions posted by other students.
4. Contact students whose schedules appear compatible.
5. Choose an available group in the Canvas **People** section.
6. Have every group member join the same Canvas group.

You can use `Ctrl+F` or your browser’s search tool to search the introduction discussion for:

- A time zone
- A major or academic program
- A particular day or time
- Another relevant keyword

Choosing your own group has generally worked better than having the instructor assign groups because students have more information about their own schedules.

## Scheduling the TA Meeting

After joining a group, open the group’s laboratory discussion.

The lead laboratory TA will provide a shared Google document in which each group member records their availability.

Once the group identifies a workable time:

1. Open the TA Information and Scheduler page.
2. Review the available TAs and meeting times.
3. Select a $30$-minute meeting slot.
4. Enter the group information in one available cell.
5. Attend the meeting using the link supplied by the TA.

Only one scheduling cell is needed for the group.

If your schedule changes from week to week, choose the meeting time that works for most weeks and coordinate directly with your TA about exceptions. On a week when you cannot attend the normal meeting, the TA may arrange for you to join another group’s meeting.

Whenever possible, work with the same TA so that your participation can be tracked consistently.

## Laboratory Discussion Credit

## First-Week Laboratory Exception

The first week is slightly different because groups and meeting schedules are still being established.

For Lab 1:

- The group-formation discussion has a later deadline than usual.
- The first laboratory report is submitted individually.
- Later laboratories use one combined report per group.

This gives everyone additional time to find group members and establish a weekly meeting schedule.

If you experience problems forming a group or arranging a meeting, contact the lead laboratory TA and copy me on the email.

## Staying on Schedule

This is an accelerated eight-week summer course, so the material moves quickly.

During the opening Poll Everywhere activity, the most common concerns were time management and the laboratory structure. Those are reasonable concerns.

To avoid falling behind:

- Check Canvas and the PDF syllabus regularly.
- Enable appropriate course notifications.
- Complete pre-lecture work before its deadline.
- Reserve time for each lecture or recording.
- Complete participation promptly.
- Begin homework before the due date.
- Perform the laboratory experiment early enough to discuss it with your group.
- Build each quiz note sheet throughout the unit.
- Contact the course staff quickly when a problem arises.

At a minimum, plan to devote approximately one focused hour to each lecture and its participation activity. Additional time will be necessary for reading, homework, laboratory work, and assessment preparation.

The most important strategy is consistency. Because the course is compressed, losing several days can quickly become difficult to recover from.
