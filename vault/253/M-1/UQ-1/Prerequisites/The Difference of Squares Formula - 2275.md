## Introduction

The **difference of squares formula** states the product of the sum and the difference of two monomials is equal to the difference of their squares:

$$
(a + b)(a - b) = a^2 - b^2
$$

We can check that this result is correct by expanding the binomial in the usual way, as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned} ({\color{blue}a} + {\color{red}b})(a - b) \\[5pt] &= {\color{blue}a}(a - b) + {\color{red}b}(a - b) \\[5pt] &= a^2 - ab + ab - b^2 \\[5pt] &= a^2 - b^2 \,& {\color{green}{\checkmark}} \end{aligned}
$$

Notice that the middle two terms $-ab$ and $ab$ cancel each other out, leaving only $a^2 - b^2$.

If the order of multiplication is switched, the formula still remains the same:

$$
(a - b)(a + b) = a^2 - b^2
$$

<a id="finding-a-product-using-the-difference-of-squares-formula"></a>
## Finding a Product Using the Difference of Squares Formula

**Example:** Expand $(x + 3)(x - 3)$.

**Explanation**

We will use the difference of square formula:

$$
(a + b)(a - b) = a^2 - b^2
$$

Substituting $a=x$ and $b=3$, we have

$$
\begin{aligned} (x + 3)(x - 3) \\[5pt] &= x^2 - 3^2 \\[5pt] &= x^2 - 9. \end{aligned}
$$

## Finding the Product of Two Binomials With Leading Coefficients

**Example:** Expand $(5x - 4)(5x + 4)$.

**Explanation**

We will use the difference of square formula:

$$
(a + b)(a - b) = a^2 - b^2
$$

Substituting $a=5x$ and $b=4$, we have

$$
\begin{aligned} (5x - 4)(5x + 4) \\[5pt] &= (5x)^2 - (4)^2 \\[5pt] &= 25x^2 - 16. \end{aligned}
$$

## Finding the Product of Two Nonlinear Binomials

**Example:** Expand $(6x + y^2)(6x - y^2)$.

**Explanation**

We will use the difference of square formula:

$$
(a + b)(a - b) = a^2 - b^2
$$

Substituting $a=6x$ and $b=y^2$, we have

$$
\begin{aligned} (6x + y^2)(6x - y^2) \\[5pt] &= (6x)^2 - \left(y^2\right)^2 \\[5pt] &= 36x^2 - y^4. \end{aligned}
$$

## Finding the Product of Two Binomials Containing General Exponents

**Example:** Expand $(2x^n-1)(2x^n+1)$.

**Explanation**

We will use the difference of square formula:

$$
(a + b)(a - b) = a^2 - b^2
$$

Substituting $a=2x^n$ and $b=1$, we have

$$
\begin{aligned}
(2x^{n} - 1)(2x^{n} + 1) &=  \\
(2x^{n})^{2} - (1)^{2} &=  \\
2^{2} \cdot (x^{n})^{2} - 1 &=  \\
&= 4x^{2n} - 1
\end{aligned}
$$
