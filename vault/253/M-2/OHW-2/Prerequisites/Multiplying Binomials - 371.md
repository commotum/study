## Introduction

When we want to multiply two binomials, we take each term of the first binomial and multiply it by the second binomial. Then, we simplify by collecting like terms.

For example, to multiply $({\color{blue}{x}} + {\color{red}{3}})$ and $(x + 2)$, we multiply ${\color{blue}{x}}$ and $(x + 2)$, and then we add this to the product of ${\color{red}{3}}$ and $(x+2)$:

$$
\begin{aligned} ({\color{blue}{x}} + {\color{red}{3}})(x + 2) \\[5pt] &= {\color{blue}{x}}(x + 2) + {\color{red}{3}}(x + 2) \\[5pt] &= x^2 + 2x + 3x + 6 \\[5pt] &= x^2 + 5x + 6 \end{aligned}
$$

## Multiplying Binomials

**Example:** Expand and simplify $(y + 5)(y - 2)$.

**Explanation**

We take each term of the first binomial and multiply it by the second binomial. Then, we simplify by collecting like terms.

$$
\begin{aligned} (y + 5)(y - 2) \\ &= y(y-2) + 5(y-2) \\ &= y^2-2y +5y-10 \\ &= y^2 + 3y - 10 \end{aligned}
$$

## Multiplying Binomials with Leading Coefficients

**Example:** Expand and simplify $(2n - 1)(3n - 6)$.

**Explanation**

We take each term of the first binomial and multiply it by the second binomial. Then, we simplify by collecting like terms.

$$
\begin{aligned} (2n - 1)(3n - 6) \\ &= 2n(3n - 6) + (-1)(3n - 6) \\ &= 6n^2 - 12n - 3n + 6 \\ &= 6n^2 - 15n + 6 \end{aligned}
$$

## Multiplying Binomials with Higher Powers and Multiple Variables

**Example:** Expand $(p^2-q^2)(2p+3q)$.

**Explanation**

We take each term of the first binomial and multiply it by the second binomial. Then, we simplify by collecting like terms.

$$
\begin{aligned}
(p^{2} - q^{2})(2p + 3q) &=  \\
p^{2}(2p + 3q) + (-q^{2})(2p + 3q) &=  \\
&= 2p^{3} + 3p^{2}q - 2pq^{2} - 3q^{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

We cannot simplify the above expression any further because there are no like terms.
