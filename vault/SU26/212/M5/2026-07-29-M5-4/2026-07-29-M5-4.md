## Problem 1

```quiz
type: radio
id: m5-4pre-q1
shuffle: true
content: |-
  **Question 1**

  A fire engine is racing toward you with its siren sounding. How does the frequency you hear compare with the frequency heard by the fire engine's driver?
options:
- id: a
  content: |-
    You hear a higher frequency than the driver does.
  correct: true
  feedback: |-
    Correct. Because the fire engine is moving toward you, the wavefronts ahead of it are compressed and you observe a higher frequency. The driver moves with the siren and hears approximately its emitted frequency.
- id: b
  content: |-
    You hear a lower frequency than the driver does.
  feedback: |-
    An approaching source compresses the wavefronts ahead of it, so you hear a higher—not lower—frequency than the driver.
- id: c
  content: |-
    You hear the same frequency as the driver does.
  feedback: |-
    The driver hears approximately the siren's emitted frequency, but the approaching motion compresses the wavefronts that reach you, so you hear a higher frequency.
```

## Problem 2

```quiz
type: blank
id: m5-4lec-q1
require_exact: true
content: |-
  **Question 1**

  A wire is $0.85\ \mathrm{m}$ long, has a mass of $0.0022\ \mathrm{kg}$, and is under $52\ \mathrm{N}$ of tension. What is its fundamental frequency?

  Enter the fundamental frequency in hertz as a number only: ==83==
feedback: |-
  The wire's linear mass density is $\mu=m/L$. For a wire fixed at both ends,

  $$
  f_1=\frac{1}{2L}\sqrt{\frac{T}{\mu}}
  =\frac12\sqrt{\frac{T}{mL}}.
  $$

  Substitution gives

  $$
  f_1
  =\frac12\sqrt{\frac{52\ \mathrm{N}}{(0.0022\ \mathrm{kg})(0.85\ \mathrm{m})}}
  =83.377\ldots\ \mathrm{Hz}.
  $$

  The measured givens each have two significant figures, so $f_1=83\ \mathrm{Hz}$.
```

## Problem 3

```quiz
type: blank
id: m5-4lec-q2
require_exact: true
content: |-
  **Question 2**

  The wire between the wall and pulley has a mass of $0.0035\ \mathrm{kg}$. What is the frequency of its third harmonic? Assume the hanging mass is stationary and use $g=9.81\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$.

  ![](<Source/Images/wire-pulley-hanging-mass.png>)

  Enter the third-harmonic frequency in hertz as a number only: ==130==
feedback: |-
  The $8.2\ \mathrm{kg}$ hanging mass supplies the tension $T=Mg$, and the wire's linear mass density is $\mu=m_w/L$. For the third harmonic of a wire fixed at both ends,

  $$
  f_3=\frac{3}{2L}\sqrt{\frac{T}{\mu}}
  =\frac{3}{2L}\sqrt{\frac{MgL}{m_w}}.
  $$

  Therefore,

  $$
  f_3
  =\frac{3}{2(3.1\ \mathrm{m})}
  \sqrt{\frac{(8.2\ \mathrm{kg})(9.81\ \mathrm{m/s^2})(3.1\ \mathrm{m})}{0.0035\ \mathrm{kg}}}
  =129.156\ldots\ \mathrm{Hz}.
  $$

  The measured values limit the answer to two significant figures, so $f_3=1.3\times10^2\ \mathrm{Hz}$.
```

## Problem 4

```quiz
type: blank
id: m5-4lec-q3
require_exact: true
content: |-
  **Question 3**

  An open–closed pipe is $0.85\ \mathrm{m}$ long. Use a sound speed of $343\ \mathrm{m/s}$. What is its third-harmonic frequency? Be sure to draw the corresponding displacement standing-wave pattern.

  Enter the frequency in hertz as a number only: ==300==
feedback: |-
  The drawing should show a displacement node at the closed end, followed by an antinode at $x=L/3$, a node at $x=2L/3$, and a displacement antinode at the open end. This places three quarter-wavelength segments inside the pipe:

  ![](<Source/Images/open-closed-pipe-third-harmonic.png>)

  $$
  L=\frac{3\lambda_3}{4}.
  $$

  For the third harmonic of an open–closed pipe,

  $$
  f_3=\frac{v}{\lambda_3}=\frac{3v}{4L}.
  $$

  Thus,

  $$
  f_3
  =\frac{3(343\ \mathrm{m/s})}{4(0.85\ \mathrm{m})}
  =302.647\ldots\ \mathrm{Hz}.
  $$

  The pipe length has two significant figures, so $f_3=3.0\times10^2\ \mathrm{Hz}$.
```

  $$
  f_5
