```quiz
    $T_A=16T_B$
  feedback: |-
    Since $T\propto 1/\sqrt{k}$ for equal masses, $T_A/T_B=\sqrt{k_B/k_A}$. A ratio of $16$ would require $k_B/k_A=256$, not $4$.
- id: b
  content: |-
    $T_A=8T_B$
  feedback: |-
    The period ratio is the square root of the inverse stiffness ratio. A ratio of $8$ would require $k_B/k_A=64$, not $4$.
- id: c
  content: |-
    $T_A=4T_B$
  feedback: |-
    Period does not vary inversely with spring constant itself. Because $T\propto 1/\sqrt{k}$, a fourfold stiffness change produces a twofold period ratio.
- id: d
  content: |-
    $T_A=2T_B$
  correct: true
  feedback: |-
    For a mass-spring oscillator, $T=2\pi\sqrt{m/k}$. The masses are equal and $k_B=4k_A$, so $T_A/T_B=\sqrt{k_B/k_A}=\sqrt{4}=2$.
- id: e
  content: |-
    $T_A=T_B$
  feedback: |-
    Equal masses do not give equal periods when the spring constants differ. Equal periods here would require equal spring constants.
- id: f
  content: |-
    $2T_A=T_B$
  feedback: |-
    This makes the stiffer system B have the longer period. Increasing stiffness decreases period, so $T_B$ must be shorter than $T_A$.
- id: g
  content: |-
    $4T_A=T_B$
  feedback: |-
    This reverses the stiffness effect and gives $T_A/T_B=1/4$. The actual ratio is $\sqrt{k_B/k_A}=2$.
- id: h
  content: |-
    $8T_A=T_B$
  feedback: |-
    This gives the stiffer system B a period eight times as long. Since $T\propto 1/\sqrt{k}$, B instead has half of A's period.
- id: i
  content: |-
    $16T_A=T_B$
  feedback: |-
    This gives the stiffer system B the longer period and a ratio unsupported by the fourfold stiffness change. The square-root relation gives $T_B=T_A/2$.
```

```quiz
  **Question 2**

  A $0.28\ \mathrm{kg}$ shoe hangs from a string. At $t=0.0\ \mathrm{s}$, it is raised to an angle of $\theta=12^\circ$ (small angle) and released from rest. It then oscillates with angular frequency $2.2\ \mathrm{rad/s}$. Treat the shoe as a point mass.

  Which statement describes the shoe $3.8\ \mathrm{s}$ later?

  ![](<Source/2026-08-03-Q-3/Images/shoe-pendulum-initial-displacement.png>)
options:
- id: a
  content: It is to the left of center and speeding up.
  feedback: |-
    The phase $\omega t=8.36\ \mathrm{rad}$ puts the shoe left of center, but its velocity points farther left while the restoring acceleration points right. Opposite velocity and acceleration mean it is slowing down.
- id: b
  content: It is to the left of center and slowing down.
  correct: true
  feedback: |-
    Released from the right turning point, the motion is $\theta(t)=\theta_0\cos(\omega t)$. At $\omega t=8.36\ \mathrm{rad}$, $\cos(8.36)<0$ and $-\sin(8.36)<0$, so the shoe is left of center and moving left. Its restoring acceleration points right, opposite its velocity, so it is slowing down.
- id: c
  content: It is to the right of center and speeding up.
  feedback: |-
    Position follows $\theta(t)=\theta_0\cos(2.2t)$. Since $\cos(8.36)<0$, the shoe is left of center at $3.8\ \mathrm{s}$, not right.
- id: d
  content: It is to the right of center and slowing down.
  feedback: |-
    The phase at $3.8\ \mathrm{s}$ is $8.36\ \mathrm{rad}$, for which the cosine is negative. The shoe is therefore left of equilibrium; the right-side descriptions do not match its position.
```

## Problem 4

```quiz
type: free
id: q-4
content: |-
  **Question 4**

  Duck A remains on a ledge and emits sound at $1400\ \mathrm{Hz}$. Duck B, which emits sound at $1600\ \mathrm{Hz}$, falls from rest through a vertical distance $d$, as shown.

  What frequency from duck A does duck B hear at the shown position? Show all work and solve symbolically before inserting numerical values.

  ![](<Source/2026-08-03-Q-3/Images/falling-duck-doppler-setup.png>)
correct: |-
  After falling from rest through distance $d$, duck B's speed is

  $$
  v_B=\sqrt{2gd}.
  $$

  Duck A is a stationary source, while duck B is an observer moving away from it. Therefore,

  $$
  \boxed{
  f_{B\leftarrow A}
  =f_A\left(1-\frac{v_B}{c_s}\right)
  =f_A\left(1-\frac{\sqrt{2gd}}{c_s}\right)
  }.
  $$

  Using $f_A=1400\ \mathrm{Hz}$, $g=9.8\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$, and $c_s=343\ \mathrm{m/s}$,

  $$
  \boxed{
  f_{B\leftarrow A}
  =(1400\ \mathrm{Hz})
  \left(1-\frac{\sqrt{(19.6\ \mathrm{m/s^2})d}}{343\ \mathrm{m/s}}\right)
  }.
  $$

  Duck B's own $1600\ \mathrm{Hz}$ emission does not set the Doppler-shifted frequency it receives from duck A.
feedback: |-
  First use free-fall kinematics to find B's observer speed at distance $d$. Then classify the Doppler roles: A is the stationary source and B is the moving observer. Because B moves away from A, use the minus sign in the moving-observer numerator and check that the heard frequency is below $1400\ \mathrm{Hz}$.
```

## Problem 5

A solid iron sphere of mass $m_1$ rests on an incline at angle $\phi$. A thread of mass $m_2$ and length $L$ is tangent to the sphere, parallel to the incline, and fixed to the wall as shown. A traveling wave of wavelength $\lambda$ moves along the thread. The maximum transverse speed of a particle in the thread is $v_m$. Assume there are no reflections, so this is not a standing wave.

![](<Source/2026-08-03-Q-3/Images/sphere-thread-incline-setup.png>)

```quiz
type: free
id: q-5a
content: |-
  **Question 5 — Part A**

  Draw a free-body diagram of the sphere.
correct: |-
  The free-body diagram should show four forces:

  - the weight $m_1g$ vertically downward through the center;
  - the normal force $N$ perpendicular to the plank through the contact point;
  - the thread tension $T$ directed up the incline at the upper tangent point;
  - static friction $f_s$ directed up the incline at the sphere-plank contact point.

  Torque equilibrium about the center requires $f_sR=TR$, so $f_s=T$. Force equilibrium along the incline then gives

  $$
  T+f_s-m_1g\sin\phi=0.
  $$
feedback: |-
  Include forces at their actual points of application because Part B depends on torque balance. The tension at the upper tangent point and the friction at the lower contact point produce opposite torques. The normal force and weight pass through the center and produce no torque about it.
```

```quiz
type: blank
id: q-5b
input_mode: math
require_exact: false
content: |-
  **Part B**

  Find the speed of the traveling wave in the thread in terms of the given variables. Show all work.

  Enter a keyboard-form expression using `m1`, `m2`, `g`, `L`, and `phi`, then reveal the reference result: ==sqrt(m1*g*L*sin(phi)/(2*m2))==
feedback: |-
  Static torque equilibrium about the sphere's center gives $f_s=T$. Along the incline,

  $$
  T+f_s=m_1g\sin\phi
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  T=\frac{m_1g\sin\phi}{2}.
  $$

  The thread's linear density is $\mu=m_2/L$. Therefore,

  $$
  \boxed{
  v=\sqrt{\frac{T}{\mu}}
  =\sqrt{\frac{m_1gL\sin\phi}{2m_2}}
  }.
  $$

  The expression under the square root has units $\mathrm{m^2/s^2}$, so the result has units $\mathrm{m/s}$.
```

```quiz
  $$
  \boxed{
  f=\frac{v}{\lambda}

  $$

  No standing-wave boundary condition is used because the prompt specifies a traveling wave without reflections.
```

```quiz
type: blank
id: q-5d
input_mode: math
require_exact: false
content: |-
  **Part D**

  Find the maximum displacement of any point in the thread in terms of the given variables. Show all work.

  Enter a keyboard-form expression using `vm`, `lambda`, `m1`, `m2`, `g`, `L`, and `phi`, then reveal the reference result: ==vm*lambda/(2*pi)*sqrt(2*m2/(m1*g*L*sin(phi)))==
feedback: |-
  Every thread element oscillates with the wave's angular frequency $\omega=2\pi f$. If $A$ is the maximum displacement, its maximum transverse particle speed is $v_m=A\omega$. Thus,

  $$
  A=\frac{v_m}{2\pi f}
  =\frac{v_m\lambda}{2\pi v}.
  $$

  Substituting the wave speed from Part B gives

  $$
  \boxed{
  A
  =\frac{v_m\lambda}{2\pi}
  \sqrt{\frac{2m_2}{m_1gL\sin\phi}}
  }.
  $$

  The propagation speed $v$ and the maximum transverse particle speed $v_m$ are different quantities; $v_m=A\omega$ is the relation that connects the given particle speed to the displacement amplitude.
```
