## The Wave Nature of Light

### 9. Historical Question

- A major historical question in physics was whether light behaves as:
  - a particle
  - a wave
- Different experiments reveal different aspects of light.
- This lecture focuses on experiments that demonstrate the wave behavior of light.

---

### 10. Particle Prediction for a Double Slit

- Imagine spraying paintballs toward a barrier containing two narrow slits.
- Some particles pass through the upper slit and others pass through the lower slit.
- A screen behind the barrier would show two broad concentrations approximately aligned with the two openings.
- This simple particle model predicts two overlapping distributions rather than a repeating sequence of bright and dark bands.

---

### 11. Wave Prediction for a Double Slit

- A plane wave approaches a barrier containing two narrow slits.
- After passing through each slit, the wave diffracts and spreads outward.
- Each slit behaves approximately like a coherent secondary wave source.
- The waves from the two slits overlap and interfere on the screen.

---

### 12. Observed Result

- Laser light passing through a double slit produces alternating bright and dark bands.
- These bands are called **interference fringes**:
  - bright fringes correspond to constructive interference
  - dark fringes correspond to destructive interference
- The appearance of an interference pattern demonstrates the wave behavior of light in this experiment.

---

## Wave-Optics Terminology

### 13. Reflection

- Reflection occurs when light returns from a surface or boundary.
- Informally, the light “bounces” from the surface.

---

### 14. Refraction

- Refraction is the change in the direction of light as it passes from one medium into another.
- It occurs because the wave speed changes at the boundary.

---

### 15. Diffraction

- Diffraction is the spreading of a wave after it passes through a narrow opening or around an obstacle.
- Even a plane incident wave can spread outward after passing through a narrow slit.
- In the double-slit experiment, diffraction at each slit allows the two emerging waves to overlap.

---

### 16. Interference

- Interference occurs when overlapping waves combine according to superposition.
- In the double-slit experiment:
  - diffraction produces spreading waves from the openings
  - interference between those waves produces bright and dark fringes
- A diffraction grating extends this idea to a large number of regularly spaced openings.

---

## Double-Slit Interference Pattern

### 17. Bright and Dark Fringes

- Bright fringes occur where the waves arrive in phase.
- Their electric fields interfere constructively, producing maximum intensity.
- Dark fringes occur where the waves arrive completely out of phase.
- Their electric fields interfere destructively.
- For equal-amplitude waves, an ideal dark fringe has zero resultant field and intensity.

---

### 18. Fringe Orders

- The central bright fringe is assigned:
  $$ m=0 $$
- The bright fringes on either side are labeled:
  $$ m=\pm1,\pm2,\pm3,\ldots $$
- If only one side of the pattern is shown, the orders may be written:
  $$ m=0,1,2,3,\ldots $$

---

### 19. Effect of Screen Distance

- Let $L$ be the distance between the slits and the screen.
- Moving the screen farther away makes the fringes farther apart.
- Moving the screen closer makes the fringes closer together.
- Therefore:
  $$ \boxed{\text{Fringe spacing increases as }L\text{ increases.}} $$

---

## Coherent Light and Phase Difference

### 20. Coherent Laser Light

- Coherent waves maintain a stable phase relationship.
- Light reaching both slits from the same laser is modeled as having:
  - the same frequency
  - the same wavelength
  - the same wave number
  - a fixed initial phase relationship
- For symmetrically illuminated slits:
  $$ \phi_{1,0}=\phi_{2,0} $$

---

### 21. Waves Arriving from the Two Slits

- Represent the two waves at observation point $P$ as:
  $$ D_1(P,t)=A\sin(kr_1-\omega t+\phi_{1,0}) $$
  $$ D_2(P,t)=A\sin(kr_2-\omega t+\phi_{2,0}) $$
- Here:
  - $r_1$ and $r_2$ are the two path lengths
  - $k$ is the wave number
  - $\omega$ is the angular frequency
  - $\phi_{1,0}$ and $\phi_{2,0}$ are the initial phases

---

### 22. Derive the Phase Difference

- The phases of the two waves are:
  $$ \phi_1=kr_1-\omega t+\phi_{1,0} $$
  $$ \phi_2=kr_2-\omega t+\phi_{2,0} $$
- Their phase difference is:
  $$ \Delta\phi=\phi_2-\phi_1 $$
- Therefore:
  $$
  \Delta\phi
  =
  k(r_2-r_1)
  +
  \left(\phi_{2,0}-\phi_{1,0}\right)
  $$
- For in-phase illumination:
  $$ \phi_{2,0}-\phi_{1,0}=0 $$
- Define the path-length difference:
  $$ \Delta r=r_2-r_1 $$
- Then:
  $$ \Delta\phi=k\Delta r $$

---

### 23. Express Phase Difference Using Wavelength

- The wave number is:
  $$ k=\frac{2\pi}{\lambda} $$
- Therefore:
  $$ \boxed{\Delta\phi=\frac{2\pi\Delta r}{\lambda}} $$

---

### 24. Constructive-Interference Condition

- Complete constructive interference occurs when:
  $$ \Delta\phi=2\pi m $$
- Here:
  $$ m\in\mathbb{Z} $$
- Combine this with:
  $$ \Delta\phi=\frac{2\pi\Delta r}{\lambda} $$
- Then:
  $$ 2\pi m=\frac{2\pi\Delta r}{\lambda} $$
- Therefore:
  $$ \boxed{\Delta r=m\lambda} $$

---

### 25. Meaning of the Path Condition

- Constructive interference occurs whenever the path-length difference is an integer number of wavelengths.
- At the central maximum:
  $$ m=0,\qquad\Delta r=0 $$
- At the first bright fringe:
  $$ |m|=1,\qquad|\Delta r|=\lambda $$
- At the second bright fringe:
  $$ |m|=2,\qquad|\Delta r|=2\lambda $$
- At the third bright fringe:
  $$ |m|=3,\qquad|\Delta r|=3\lambda $$

---

### 26. Destructive-Interference Condition

- Complete destructive interference occurs when:
  $$ \Delta\phi=(2m+1)\pi $$
- Therefore, the path-length difference for a dark fringe is:
  $$ \boxed{\Delta r=\left(m+\frac{1}{2}\right)\lambda} $$

---

## Double-Slit Geometry

### 27. Geometric Quantities

- Let:
  - $d$ be the center-to-center slit separation
  - $L$ be the distance from the slits to the screen
  - $y_m$ be the vertical position of the $m$th bright fringe
  - $\theta_m$ be the angle from the central axis to the fringe
  - $r_1$ and $r_2$ be the two path lengths
- The path-length difference is:
  $$ \Delta r=r_2-r_1 $$

---

### 28. Path Difference and Angle

- When the screen is far from the slits, the two paths to a given point are approximately parallel.
- The small triangle near the slits gives the standard far-field relation:
  $$ \boxed{\Delta r=d\sin\theta_m} $$
- For a bright fringe:
  $$ \Delta r=m\lambda $$
- Therefore:
  $$ \boxed{d\sin\theta_m=m\lambda} $$

---

### 29. Screen Geometry

- The larger triangle between the slits and the screen gives:
  $$ \tan\theta_m=\frac{y_m}{L} $$
- Therefore:
  $$ \boxed{y_m=L\tan\theta_m} $$

---

### 30. Exact Bright-Fringe Position

- From:
  $$ d\sin\theta_m=m\lambda $$
- Solve for the angle:
  $$ \theta_m=\sin^{-1}\left(\frac{m\lambda}{d}\right) $$
- Substitute into:
  $$ y_m=L\tan\theta_m $$
- The exact screen-position relation within the far-field model is:
  $$
  \boxed{
  y_m
  =
  L\tan\left[
  \sin^{-1}\left(\frac{m\lambda}{d}\right)
  \right]
  }
  $$

---

## Small-Angle Approximation

### 31. Small-Angle Relationships

- For sufficiently small angles measured in radians:
  $$ \sin\theta\approx\theta $$
  $$ \tan\theta\approx\theta $$
  $$ \cos\theta\approx1 $$
- Therefore:
  $$ \sin\theta\approx\tan\theta $$

---

### 32. Bright-Fringe Angle

- Begin with:
  $$ d\sin\theta_m=m\lambda $$
- For a small angle:
  $$ d\theta_m\approx m\lambda $$
- Therefore:
  $$ \boxed{\theta_m\approx\frac{m\lambda}{d}} $$

---

### 33. Position of the $m$th Bright Fringe

- From the screen geometry:
  $$ \tan\theta_m=\frac{y_m}{L} $$
- For small angles:
  $$ \theta_m\approx\frac{y_m}{L} $$
- Combine this with:
  $$ \theta_m\approx\frac{m\lambda}{d} $$
- Then:
  $$ \frac{y_m}{L}\approx\frac{m\lambda}{d} $$
- Therefore:
  $$ \boxed{y_m\approx\frac{m\lambda L}{d}} $$

---

### 34. Adjacent Bright-Fringe Spacing

- The positions of adjacent bright fringes are:
  $$ y_m\approx\frac{m\lambda L}{d} $$
  $$ y_{m+1}\approx\frac{(m+1)\lambda L}{d} $$
- Their separation is:
  $$ \Delta y=y_{m+1}-y_m $$
- Therefore:
  $$
  \Delta y
  \approx
  \frac{(m+1)\lambda L}{d}
  -
  \frac{m\lambda L}{d}
  $$
- Thus:
  $$ \boxed{\Delta y\approx\frac{\lambda L}{d}} $$

---

### 35. Parameter Dependence

- From:
  $$ \Delta y\approx\frac{\lambda L}{d} $$
- The fringes move farther apart when:
  - the wavelength $\lambda$ increases
  - the screen distance $L$ increases
  - the slit separation $d$ decreases
- The fringes move closer together when:
  - the wavelength decreases
  - the screen is moved closer
  - the slit separation increases

---

## Example 1: Path Difference at a Bright Fringe

### 36. Identify the Fringe

- A point is marked on the second bright fringe away from the central maximum.
- The fringe orders are:
  $$ m=0,\quad m=1,\quad m=2,\ldots $$
- The marked point is therefore on:
  $$ m=2 $$

---

### 37. Determine the Path Difference

- For a bright fringe:
  $$ \Delta r=m\lambda $$
- Therefore:
  $$ \boxed{\Delta r=2\lambda} $$
- One path to the marked point is two wavelengths longer than the other.

---

## Interference-Intensity Graphs

### 38. Reading an Intensity Graph

- A double-slit pattern may be represented by plotting intensity against position on the screen.
- Bright fringes appear as peaks.
- Dark fringes appear as minima between the peaks.
- The central maximum occurs at:
  $$ y=0 $$
- The distance between adjacent intensity peaks is:
  $$ \Delta y $$
- For real slits with finite width, the peak brightness may also be modulated by a broader diffraction envelope.

---

## Example 2: Laser Wavelength from a Double-Slit Pattern

### 39. Given Information

- Distance from the slits to the screen:
  $$ L=0.85\ \mathrm{m} $$
- Center-to-center slit separation:
  $$ d=0.062\ \mathrm{mm} $$
- The intensity graph shows an adjacent-fringe spacing of:
  $$ \Delta y=1.0\ \mathrm{cm} $$
- Find:
  $$ \lambda $$

---

### 40. Convert to SI Units

- Slit separation:
  $$
  d
  =
  0.062\ \mathrm{mm}
  =
  6.2\times10^{-5}\ \mathrm{m}
  $$
- Fringe spacing:
  $$
  \Delta y
  =
  1.0\ \mathrm{cm}
  =
  1.0\times10^{-2}\ \mathrm{m}
  $$

---

### 41. Solve for the Wavelength

- Begin with:
  $$ \Delta y=\frac{\lambda L}{d} $$
- Rearrange:
  $$ \lambda=\frac{\Delta y\,d}{L} $$
- Substitute:
  $$
  \lambda
  =
  \frac{
  (1.0\times10^{-2}\ \mathrm{m})
  (6.2\times10^{-5}\ \mathrm{m})
  }
  {0.85\ \mathrm{m}}
  $$
- Therefore:
  $$ \lambda\approx7.3\times10^{-7}\ \mathrm{m} $$
- In nanometers:
  $$ \boxed{\lambda\approx730\ \mathrm{nm}} $$

---

### 42. Interpret the Result

- A wavelength near:
  $$ 730\ \mathrm{nm} $$
  lies at the deep-red end of the visible spectrum.
- A standard red helium–neon laser has a wavelength of approximately:
  $$ 633\ \mathrm{nm} $$
- The calculated value is not the standard helium–neon wavelength, but it remains physically reasonable for red light.

---

### 43. Rearranging the Fringe-Spacing Equation

- The equation:
  $$ \Delta y=\frac{\lambda L}{d} $$
  contains four measurable quantities.
- It may be rearranged to find:
  $$ \lambda=\frac{\Delta y\,d}{L} $$
  $$ L=\frac{\Delta y\,d}{\lambda} $$
  $$ d=\frac{\lambda L}{\Delta y} $$
- A diagram or intensity graph may provide values such as:
  - $\Delta y$
  - $y_m$
  - $m$
  - $L$
  - $d$

---

## Diffraction Gratings

### 44. Structure of a Diffraction Grating

- A diffraction grating contains many evenly spaced narrow slits or lines.
- A typical grating may contain approximately:
  $$ 300\text{ to }3600\ \mathrm{lines}/\mathrm{mm} $$
- The center-to-center separation between neighboring lines is denoted:
  $$ d $$
- If a width $W$ contains $N$ lines:
  $$ \boxed{d=\frac{W}{N}} $$
- Equivalently, the line density is:
  $$ \boxed{\frac{N}{W}=\frac{1}{d}} $$

---

### 45. Interference from Many Slits

- Each illuminated slit acts as a coherent source.
- At particular angles, light from all the slits arrives in phase and produces a principal maximum.
- Interference from many slits produces principal maxima that are:
  - narrower
  - more sharply defined
  - separated by broad low-intensity regions
- The light is redistributed into these narrow principal maxima.

---

### 46. Diffraction-Grating Maximum Condition

- The path difference between neighboring slits is:
  $$ \Delta r=d\sin\theta_m $$
- A principal bright maximum occurs when:
  $$ \Delta r=m\lambda $$
- Therefore:
  $$ \boxed{d\sin\theta_m=m\lambda} $$
- Here:
  $$ m=0,\pm1,\pm2,\ldots $$

---

### 47. Screen Position of a Grating Maximum

- The angle and position are related by:
  $$ \tan\theta_m=\frac{y_m}{L} $$
- Therefore:
  $$ \boxed{\theta_m=\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{y_m}{L}\right)} $$
- Combining this with the grating equation gives:
  $$
  \boxed{
  \lambda
  =
  \frac{d}{m}
  \sin\left[
  \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{y_m}{L}\right)
  \right]
  }
  $$

---

### 48. Why Grating Maxima Are Not Generally Equally Spaced

- The grating condition gives:
  $$ \sin\theta_m=\frac{m\lambda}{d} $$
- The corresponding screen position is:
  $$ y_m=L\tan\theta_m $$
- Therefore:
  $$
  \boxed{
  y_m
  =
  L\tan\left[
  \sin^{-1}\left(\frac{m\lambda}{d}\right)
  \right]
  }
  $$
- Because the sine and tangent functions are nonlinear, $y_m$ is not generally linear in $m$.
- At larger angles, successive principal maxima become progressively farther apart.

---

### 49. When to Use the Small-Angle Approximation

- A diffraction grating does not automatically prevent use of the small-angle approximation.
- The approximation is valid only when the actual angle is sufficiently small:
  $$ \sin\theta\approx\tan\theta\approx\theta $$
- For the diffraction-grating problems in this lecture, the relevant angles are not necessarily small.
- Unless a small-angle approximation is justified, use:
  $$ d\sin\theta_m=m\lambda $$
  and:
  $$ y_m=L\tan\theta_m $$
  separately or combine them using the exact trigonometric expression.

## Example 3: Wavelength from a Diffraction Grating

### 50. Given Information

- Grating line spacing:
  $$ d=3.0\ \mu\mathrm{m} $$
- Distance from the grating to the screen:
  $$ L=1.8\ \mathrm{m} $$
- The third bright fringe is:
  $$ y_3=120\ \mathrm{cm} $$
  from the central maximum.
- Therefore:
  $$ m=3 $$
- Find:
  $$ \lambda $$

---

### 51. Convert to SI Units

- Line spacing:
  $$
  d
  =
  3.0\ \mu\mathrm{m}
  =
  3.0\times10^{-6}\ \mathrm{m}
  $$
- Third-order fringe position:
  $$
  y_3
  =
  120\ \mathrm{cm}
  =
  1.20\ \mathrm{m}
  $$

---

### 52. Determine the Diffraction Angle

- Use:
  $$ \theta_3=\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{y_3}{L}\right) $$
- Substitute:
  $$
  \theta_3
  =
  \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{1.20}{1.8}\right)
  $$
- Therefore:
  $$ \boxed{\theta_3\approx33.7^\circ} $$
- This angle is too large for the small-angle approximation to be accurate.

---

### 53. Solve for the Wavelength

- Begin with:
  $$ d\sin\theta_3=3\lambda $$
- Solve:
  $$ \lambda=\frac{d\sin\theta_3}{3} $$
- Substitute:
  $$
  \lambda
  =
  \frac{
  (3.0\times10^{-6}\ \mathrm{m})
  \sin(33.7^\circ)
  }
  {3}
  $$
- Therefore:
  $$ \lambda\approx5.55\times10^{-7}\ \mathrm{m} $$
- To two significant figures:
  $$ \boxed{\lambda\approx550\ \mathrm{nm}} $$

---

### 54. Physical Interpretation

- A wavelength of approximately:
  $$ 550\ \mathrm{nm} $$
  lies in the visible green region.
- Visible light occupies approximately:
  $$ 400\ \mathrm{nm}\lesssim\lambda\lesssim750\ \mathrm{nm} $$
- A result far outside this range may indicate:
  - an incorrect unit conversion
  - use of the wrong interference order
  - incorrect trigonometry
  - an inappropriate approximation

---

### 55. Error from the Small-Angle Approximation

- If the linear small-angle equation were incorrectly used:
  $$ y_m\approx\frac{m\lambda L}{d} $$
- Solving for wavelength would give:
  $$ \lambda_{\mathrm{approx}}\approx\frac{y_m d}{mL} $$
- Substitute:
  $$
  \lambda_{\mathrm{approx}}
  \approx
  \frac{
  (1.20\ \mathrm{m})(3.0\times10^{-6}\ \mathrm{m})
  }
  {(3)(1.8\ \mathrm{m})}
  $$
- This gives:
  $$ \lambda_{\mathrm{approx}}\approx6.7\times10^{-7}\ \mathrm{m} $$
- Therefore:
  $$ \lambda_{\mathrm{approx}}\approx670\ \mathrm{nm} $$
- This differs substantially from the correct result:
  $$ \lambda\approx550\ \mathrm{nm} $$

### 56. Main Physics Takeaways

- Constructive-interference path condition:
  $$ \Delta r=m\lambda $$
- Double-slit and diffraction-grating maximum condition:
  $$ \boxed{d\sin\theta_m=m\lambda} $$
- Screen geometry:
  $$ \boxed{y_m=L\tan\theta_m} $$
- For small angles:
  $$ \sin\theta_m\approx\tan\theta_m\approx\theta_m $$
- Small-angle double-slit fringe position:
  $$ \boxed{y_m\approx\frac{m\lambda L}{d}} $$
- Adjacent bright-fringe spacing:
  $$ \boxed{\Delta y\approx\frac{\lambda L}{d}} $$
- Exact fringe position:
  $$
  \boxed{
  y_m
  =
  L\tan\left[
  \sin^{-1}\left(\frac{m\lambda}{d}\right)
  \right]
  }
  $$
- A diffraction grating produces narrow, sharply defined principal maxima.
- At large angles, the exact trigonometric relationships must be used.

---

### 57. Main Problem-Solving Strategy

1. Identify whether the optical system is:
   - a double slit
   - a diffraction grating
2. Identify the interference order:
   $$ m $$
3. Convert every distance to consistent SI units.
4. Use the maximum condition:
   $$ d\sin\theta_m=m\lambda $$
5. Relate angle and screen position using:
   $$ \tan\theta_m=\frac{y_m}{L} $$
6. Use:
   $$ y_m\approx\frac{m\lambda L}{d} $$
   only when the angle is sufficiently small.
7. For adjacent small-angle double-slit fringes, use:
   $$ \Delta y\approx\frac{\lambda L}{d} $$
8. Check that the calculated wavelength is physically reasonable.
9. For visible light, expect a result on the order of:
   $$ 10^{-7}\ \mathrm{m} $$
10. Retain sufficient precision during intermediate calculations before rounding the final answer.
