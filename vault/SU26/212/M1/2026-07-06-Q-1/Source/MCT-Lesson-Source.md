Use the transcripts as source material, but correct imprecise wording rather than repeating it. In particular:

- Radius is conventionally a length, not literally a quantity with units of meters per radian; the radian is dimensionless, although writing the conversion factor as $r\,\mathrm{m/rad}$ can help track units.
- Angular velocity carries a sign or direction; angular speed is its magnitude.
- $a_t=r\alpha$ gives tangential acceleration, not radial or total acceleration.
- Constant-angular-acceleration equations may be used only when $\alpha$ is constant.
- In the M1-1 Problem 4 display, $60\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ is the requested instantaneous angular speed, not an average angular speed.
- The M1-2 nonuniform-circle car has $F_{\mathrm{net}}\approx9899\,\mathrm N$, despite inconsistent spoken values.
- At the top of the M1-3 inside loop, maintaining contact requires $v\geq\sqrt{rg}$; the source's final spoken inequality is reversed.
- On the frictionless M1-4 bank, speeds above the design speed tend up/outward and speeds below it tend down/inward.
- At the side of a fixed vertical circle, the rope is radial and gravity is tangential; do not reproduce the source's tilted-rope resultant as an “exact” side tension.

Each generated lesson must follow the core-move-lesson structure: a fast recognition cue, minimal prerequisites, a canonical worked example, controlled variants, misconception-based radio practice with response-specific feedback, and a concise summary.

Recognition cues:

Core move: **At the top, bottom, or side of a vertical circle, project tension and weight onto the inward radial direction before solving the location-dependent tension.**

Recognition cues:

- A mass on a rope moves around a vertical circle.
- Tension is requested at the top, bottom, or side/middle.
- The learner is tempted to use $T=mv^2/r$ at every position.

Required content:

1. Set inward positive independently at each position.
2. At the top, both tension and gravity are inward: $T+mg=mv^2/r$.

5. At equal speed, rank the tensions bottom $>$ side $>$ top.
6. State that constant speed around a vertical circle generally requires an external tangential agent away from top and bottom.

Do not import the conical-pendulum component construction into a fixed vertical circle. Keep the normal-force contact threshold in Problem 8; a rope instead has $T\geq0$ and can go slack, which may appear only as a brief extension.
