## Problem 4

A key of mass $m$ is attached to a string of length $L$ and moves in a vertical circle. At the instant shown, the string tension has magnitude $T$. Use $r$-$t$ axes, with the $r$-axis pointing inward along the string, and solve completely symbolically in terms of the given variables.

![](<Source/2026-07-06-Q-1/Images/quiz-1a-q4-vertical-rotation-key.png>)

```quiz
type: free
id: q-4a
content: |-
  **Question 4 — Part A**

  Draw a free-body diagram of the key. Label the $r$- and $t$-axes and all force vectors, and keep vector directions and lengths consistent.
correct: |-
  Draw only the two real forces on the key:

  - Tension $T$ points along the inward $+r$ direction toward the center.
  - Weight $mg$ points vertically downward.

  At the position shown, the direction of motion and $+t$ are tangent to the circle and point generally upward and left. Gravity has an outward radial component $mg\sin\theta$ and a tangential component of magnitude $mg\cos\theta$ opposite the motion.
```

```quiz
type: blank
id: q-4b
require_exact: false
content: |-
  **Question 4 — Part B**

  Find the magnitude of the key's total acceleration at that instant. Show all work on paper.

  Enter a keyboard-form expression using `T`, `m`, `g`, and `theta`, then reveal the reference result: ==sqrt((T/m-g*sin(theta))^2+(g*cos(theta))^2)==
feedback: |-
  Because $\theta$ is measured below the horizontal as drawn, the radial component of gravity points outward and the tangential component points opposite the motion. Therefore,

  $$
  T-mg\sin\theta=ma_r,
  \qquad
  -mg\cos\theta=ma_t.
  $$

  Thus,

  $$
  a_r=\frac{T}{m}-g\sin\theta,
  \qquad
  a_t=-g\cos\theta,
  $$

  and the total acceleration magnitude is

  $$
  |\vec a|
  =\sqrt{a_r^2+a_t^2}
  =\sqrt{\left(\frac{T}{m}-g\sin\theta\right)^2+\left(g\cos\theta\right)^2}.
  $$
```

```quiz
type: blank
id: q-4c
require_exact: false
content: |-
  **Question 4 — Part C**

  Find the key's angular speed at that instant. Show all work on paper.

  Enter a keyboard-form expression using `T`, `m`, `g`, `theta`, and `L`, then reveal the reference result: ==sqrt((T/m-g*sin(theta))/L)==
feedback: |-
  The inward radial acceleration is both

  $$
  a_r=\frac{T}{m}-g\sin\theta
  $$

  and

  $$
  a_r=\omega^2L.
  $$

  Therefore,

  $$
  \omega
  =\sqrt{\frac{T/m-g\sin\theta}{L}}
  =\sqrt{\frac{T-mg\sin\theta}{mL}}.
  $$
```

## Problem 5

```quiz
type: blank
id: q-5
require_exact: true
content: |-
  **Question 1**

  A $7.27\ \mathrm{kg}$ greyhound holds a rope wound around a spool of radius $2.2\ \mathrm{m}$. At $t=0.0\ \mathrm{s}$, the greyhound starts from rest at the spool and moves straight away from it, unwinding the rope.

  The greyhound's velocity is

  $$
  v(t)=At+Bt^2,
  $$

  where $A=1.2\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$ and $B=2.4\ \mathrm{m/s^3}$. How many revolutions has the spool made after $4.5\ \mathrm{s}$? Solve symbolically before inserting numerical values.

  ![](<Source/2026-07-06-Q-1/Images/quiz-1b-q1-greyhound-spool.png>)

  Enter the number of revolutions to two significant figures as a number only: ==6.2==
feedback: |-
  The rope length unwound equals the greyhound's displacement:

  $$
  s(t)
  =\int_0^t\left(At'+Bt'^2\right)\,dt'
  =\frac{At^2}{2}+\frac{Bt^3}{3}.
  $$

  Because $s=r\phi$ and one revolution is $2\pi$ radians,

  $$
  N
  =\frac{\phi}{2\pi}
  =\frac{s}{2\pi r}
  =\frac{\frac12At^2+\frac13Bt^3}{2\pi r}.
  $$

  Substituting the given values gives

  $$
  s=85.05\ \mathrm{m},
  \qquad
  N=6.1527\ldots\ \text{revolutions}.
  $$

  The radius, time, $A$, and $B$ are each given to two significant figures, so the final result is $6.2$ revolutions. The greyhound's mass is not needed for this kinematic calculation.
```
