# MCT Q2 Lesson Source Specification

This file defines the canonical 22-file MCT lesson sequence for Quiz 2. In course order, it moves from M2 torque and center of mass through rotational inertia, pulley dynamics, equilibrium, rotational energy and angular momentum, then into M3 gravitation, circular orbits, Kepler scaling, and vector addition by components. Coverage is limited to the 16 attachment-selected videos and their seven paired lecture notes; duplicate treatments are merged into a single reusable core move.

## Scope and method

- Scope is exactly the 16 attachment videos listed below: no `Video-Matches` JSON was used and no reused video outside an attachment group was added.
- Every listed SRT was read from beginning to end. Repeating auto-caption cues were normalized into chronological spoken text while retaining cue start times.
- The corresponding seven `Source/Lecture-Notes.md` files were also read completely.
- When captions depended on an unseen drawing, the matching MP4 was checked at the relevant timestamp. In particular, frames were checked for `qGvFAl5CK_c` at 24:45, 32:55, 44:55, and 57:15; `QghXDDJtJeQ` at 04:12; and `Ep1jIhHdf2A` at 03:55, 04:10, 07:15, and 10:08.
- Each target teaches one reusable decision or procedure. When videos repeat the same procedure, their worked problems are assigned to one target. The specification keeps a source-video problem when it appears as an example or comparison under a different lecture label.

## Canonical lesson targets

## Problem 1

### Lesson 1 — Calculate Signed Torque from a Force's Line of Action

Lecture label: **M2-1**

Target file: `../Lessons/MCT-Problem-1.md`

**Operational core move:** Choose a pivot, replace an angled force by either its perpendicular component or its perpendicular moment arm, assign a clockwise/counterclockwise sign, and sum `tau = F d_perp = r F sin(theta)`.

**Recognition cues:** a force is applied away from a hinge/pivot; an angle is shown between `r` and `F`; the question asks torque, net torque, or rotation direction; several forces compete around one axis.

**Exact transcript and timestamps:** `jg4e8W44_E4` (exact path in source map), 00:00:01–00:06:18 for door/moment-arm geometry and equivalent formulas; 00:06:24–00:09:04 for signed two-force torque; 00:09:11–00:13:04 for a three-force net torque.

**Source-video material to preserve:**

- Door comparison: equal forces at positions A, B, C; farther from the hinge gives a larger moment arm and torque.
- Derive `d_perp = r sin(theta)` from the line of action and show the equivalent `F_perp = F sin(theta)` route.
- Two-force bar: `200 N` at `3 m` counterclockwise gives `+600 N m`; `400 N` at `1.5 m` clockwise gives `-600 N m`; net torque `0`.
- Three-force propeller: `300 N` at `4 m` clockwise, `600 N` at `3 m` and `60 degrees` counterclockwise, `500 N` at `5 m` and `50 degrees` counterclockwise; net approximately `+2274 N m`, hence counterclockwise.

**Required content:** pivot and line of action first; the angle must be between `r` and `F`; consistent sign convention; units `N m`; zero torque for a force whose line of action passes through the pivot; diagram-based plausibility check before arithmetic.

**Exclusions:** do not teach torque as “force times distance” without perpendicular qualification; do not introduce static-equilibrium reaction forces or `sum tau = I alpha` here.

**Merged/supplemental section:** use the M2-1 lecture-note teeter-totter motivation and torque definition as a short application, but leave solving an unknown balance force/distance to Lesson 2.

## Problem 2

### Lesson 2 — Use Torque Balance to Trade Force for Lever Arm

Lecture label: **M2-1**

Target file: `../Lessons/MCT-Problem-2.md`

**Operational core move:** At equilibrium, equate opposing torque magnitudes and solve the single unknown force or lever arm; then interpret the force ratio as ideal mechanical advantage.

**Recognition cues:** lever, seesaw, shovel, fulcrum, balanced torques, unknown output force, or mechanical advantage; no angular acceleration.

**Exact transcript and timestamps:** `jg4e8W44_E4`, 00:13:09–00:16:42 for the seesaw lever and 00:16:42–00:20:54 for the shovel.

**Source-video material to preserve:**

- Ideal seesaw: input `300 N` at `6 m`, output arm `3 m`; `F_out = 600 N`; actual/ideal mechanical advantage `600/300 = 6/3 = 2`.
- Shovel: input `200 N` at `1.0 m`, output arm `0.10 m`; ideal output `2000 N`, mechanical advantage `10`.
- Shovel variation: move the input hand to the midpoint (`0.50 m` effective input arm); mechanical advantage falls to `5` and ideal output to `1000 N`.

**Required content:** define input/output arms from the same pivot; write `F_in d_in = F_out d_out`; distinguish ideal from actual performance; explain the force-distance tradeoff without claiming energy creation.

**Exclusions:** no general machine survey; no rotational acceleration; no unsupported claim that the two torques act in the same rotational direction—the equilibrium ledger uses opposite signed torques.

**Merged/supplemental section:** connect to the M2-1 lecture-note balance condition `m1 r1 = m2 r2` and to support-at-center-of-mass intuition.

## Problem 3

### Lesson 3 — Locate a Center of Mass from a Coordinate Ledger

Lecture label: **M2-1**

Target file: `../Lessons/MCT-Problem-3.md`

**Operational core move:** Choose one origin, list every mass with its signed coordinate, compute each coordinate independently with `x_cm = sum(m_i x_i)/sum(m_i)`, and check that the result lies toward the heavier concentration.

**Recognition cues:** several point masses/objects have known locations; the question asks the combined center; a 2D arrangement requires `x_cm` and `y_cm`; a composite group can be represented by its mass at its own center.

**Exact transcript and timestamps:** `2uszSnvzBEU`, 00:00:01–00:02:04 (Problem 1) and 00:04:19–00:09:26 (Problem 3).

**Source-video material to preserve:**

- Problem 1: `5 kg` at `x=0`, `9 kg` at `x=2 m`; `x_cm = 18/14 = 1.29 m`, between the objects and closer to `9 kg`.
- Problem 3: masses `4, 6, 7, 10 kg` at `(0,0), (4,0), (0,5), (5,6) m`; `x_cm=74/27=2.74 m`, `y_cm=95/27=3.519 m`.

**Required content:** signed coordinates; separate x/y ledgers; total mass in every denominator; prediction of answer region; grouping identical/uniform subobjects at their individual centers.

**Exclusions:** do not mix torque balance into the main algorithm; do not assume the center is a geometric midpoint or that it divides mass equally.

**Merged/supplemental section:** add the M2-1 lecture-note six-block/two-block/two-block grouping (`6m` at `1.5 cm`, `2m` at `4.0 cm`, `2m` at `5.5 cm`, result `2.8 cm`) and attached `2L`/`L` cubes (`x_cm=7L/6`) as controlled composite examples.

## Problem 5

### Lesson 5 — Rank Rotational Inertia from Mass Distribution

Lecture label: **M2-2**

Target file: `../Lessons/MCT-Problem-5.md`

**Operational core move:** Hold the stated variables fixed, compare how much mass lies far from the specified axis, and use the `r^2` weighting or the correct shape factor to rank resistance to angular acceleration.

**Recognition cues:** same mass and outer radius but different shapes; “harder to spin,” “greater angular acceleration under the same torque,” or mass moved inward/outward; axis is explicitly shown.

**Exact transcript and timestamps:** `Ic_wFYu8xVs`, 00:00:01–00:03:30 for translational-inertia contrast and 00:03:30–00:08:50 for hoop-versus-disk rotational inertia; 00:08:50–00:11:34 for the `sum m r^2` and `sum tau = I alpha` connection.

**Source-video material to preserve:**

- Same `50 N` applied to `10 kg` and `100 kg`: accelerations `5` and `0.5 m/s^2`; use only as analogy for inertia, not as the rotational target.
- Thin hoop and solid disk, both `10 kg`, radius `2 m`, same tangential `100 N`: `I_hoop=MR^2`, `I_disk=(1/2)MR^2`; hoop has larger I and smaller angular acceleration under the same torque.
- Compare standard factors mentioned: hoop `1`, disk `1/2`, solid sphere `2/5` in `I=cMR^2`.

**Required content:** axis dependence; mass distribution rather than mass alone; `I` units `kg m^2`; same-torque implication `alpha=tau/I`; explicitly state which object/axis each formula describes.

**Exclusions:** do not present the video's point-mass derivation as a derivation of every rigid-body shape factor; do not conflate inertia with torque or energy.

**Merged/supplemental section:** align with the M2-2 lecture derivation of `K_rot=(1/2)I omega^2` and the rod comparison `I_end=4 I_cm` as additional ranking checks.

## Problem 6

### Lesson 6 — Build the Moment of Inertia of Discrete Masses About a Chosen Axis

Lecture label: **M2-2**

Target file: `../Lessons/MCT-Problem-6.md`

**Operational core move:** Measure each point mass's perpendicular distance from the stated axis, square that distance, and sum `I=sum m_i r_i^2`, exploiting symmetry only after the distance ledger is correct.

**Recognition cues:** blocks/point masses at known offsets; a diagram gives an axis and asks total moment of inertia; several identical masses share a radius.

**Exact transcript and timestamps:** `JrkimXqnCLw`, 00:00:02–00:02:45 (direct calculations for two masses about two axes) and 00:04:20–00:07:45 (direct calculations for four masses about two axes).

**Source-video material to preserve:**

- Two `10 kg` blocks separated by `10 m`: about the midpoint each is `5 m` away, so `I_cm=2(10)(5^2)=500 kg m^2`; about the left mass, radii `0` and `10 m`, so `I=1000 kg m^2`.
- Four `4 kg` blocks, each `5 m` from the central axis: `I_cm=4(4)(5^2)=400 kg m^2`. Shift the axis `9 m` left; two masses lie `4 m` away and two `14 m` away, giving `I=2(4)(4^2)+2(4)(14^2)=1696 kg m^2`.

**Required content:** perpendicular distance, not separation between masses; zero contribution for a point mass on the axis; units; direct-sum method as a validation tool.

**Exclusions:** do not use a catalog shape formula for separated point masses; do not invoke the parallel-axis theorem until the direct ledger is understood.

**Merged/supplemental section:** the M2-2 lecture's rod-plus-`3M` point mass (`I_total=(1/3)ML^2+3ML^2=10ML^2/3`) can be a short composite-body extension, with intrinsic and point-mass terms kept distinct.

**Operational core move:** Verify that the known inertia is about a parallel axis through the center of mass, measure the axis separation `d`, then apply `I_new=I_cm+M_total d^2` and check that the shift increases I.

**Recognition cues:** same rigid body, new parallel rotation axis; `I_cm` is known; an end/offset axis is requested; direct integration would repeat known work.

**Exact transcript and timestamps:** `JrkimXqnCLw`, 00:02:45–00:04:17 (two-mass shift), 00:07:45–00:08:41 (four-mass shift), and 00:08:41–00:11:07 (uniform rod center-to-end derivation).

**Source-video material to preserve:**

- Two-block check: `I_cm=500`, total mass `20 kg`, `d=5 m`; `I_new=500+20(5^2)=1000 kg m^2`.
- Four-block check: `I_cm=400`, total mass `16 kg`, `d=9 m`; `I_new=400+16(9^2)=1696 kg m^2`.

## Problem 8

### Lesson 8 — Find the Acceleration of a Falling Mass Driving a Massive Pulley

Lecture label: **M2-3**

Target file: `../Lessons/MCT-Problem-8.md`

**Operational core move:** Couple `mg-T=ma`, `TR=I alpha`, and `a=alpha R` to reduce pulley inertia to the effective term `I/R^2`, then use the resulting constant acceleration for the requested kinematics.

**Recognition cues:** one hanging mass unwinds a string from a fixed massive pulley; pulley spins but its center does not translate; the request is `a`, `alpha`, speed, distance, or tension.

**Exact transcripts and timestamps:** `dbvr-L5rxdg`, 00:00:01–00:09:44; merged duplicate treatment from `REIP2mf6sIQ`, 00:07:00–00:18:13.

**Source-video material to preserve:**

- M2-3 problem: hanging `m=10 kg`, solid disk `M=20 kg`, `R=2 m`, released from rest. Derive `alpha=mgR/(I+mR^2)=2.45 rad/s^2`, `a=alpha R=4.9 m/s^2`, speed after `8 s` is `39.2 m/s`, and fall distance in `8 s` is `156.8 m`.
- M2-5 duplicate/extension: same mass pairing, block released `500 m` above ground. Energy ledger `mgh=(1/2)mv^2+(1/2)I omega^2` gives `v=70 m/s`; Newton/torque gives `a=4.9 m/s^2` and the same endpoint speed.

**Required content:** general denominator `m+I/R^2`; for a solid disk `I/R^2=M/2`; distinct block mass and pulley mass symbols; no-slip constraint; energy as a verification/endpoint shortcut, not a second lesson.

**Exclusions/corrections:** do not call `I/R^2` literal added mass without explaining the effective-inertia reduction; do not assume tension equals weight; do not duplicate the M2-5 version as a separate lesson.

**Merged/supplemental section:** the M2-3 lecture spool fixed at its spindle (`alpha=2T/(mR)`) is a short limiting case showing that a support force at the pivot can be nonzero while its torque is zero.

## Problem 10

### Lesson 10 — Solve Particle Equilibrium with Cable Components

Lecture label: **M2-4**

Target file: `../Lessons/MCT-Problem-10.md`

**Operational core move:** Resolve each cable tension into signed x/y components, enforce `sum Fx=0` and `sum Fy=0`, and solve the resulting two equations without introducing torque when all forces meet at a point.

**Recognition cues:** knot, sign, or hanging mass supported by two cables; angles and weight are given; all forces are concurrent; asked for cable tensions.

**Exact transcript and timestamps:** `qGvFAl5CK_c`, 00:07:47–00:12:30 and 00:12:30–00:17:56.

**Source-video material to preserve:**

- `50 kg` sign: one cable at `60 degrees` above horizontal and the other horizontal. `T1 sin60=490 N`, so `T1=565.8 N`; `T2=T1 cos60=282.9 N`.
- `80 kg` mass with cables at `40 degrees` and `50 degrees` above horizontal. Use `T1 cos40=T2 cos50` and `T1 sin40+T2 sin50=784`; results `T1=503.9 N`, `T2=600.5 N`.

**Required content:** an explicit FBD; component directions/signs; angles measured from horizontal as drawn; two equations for two unknowns; unit and vertical-support checks.

**Exclusions:** no torque equation for a concurrent-force particle; no assumption that equal-looking cables have equal tension when their angles differ.

**Merged/supplemental section:** retain the lecture-note reminder that static equilibrium requires both translational and rotational equilibrium. For this concurrent-force point model, the rotational equation is automatically satisfied.
