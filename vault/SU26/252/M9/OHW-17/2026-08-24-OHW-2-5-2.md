```quiz
  An inverted right circular cone has top radius $a=3\ \mathrm{m}$ and height $b=6\ \mathrm{m}$, and is half full of water as shown. Place its vertex at the origin, with its axis on the $y$-axis and its opening above the $x$-axis in quadrants I and II.

  ![Inverted cone with radius a and height b](<Source/2026-08-24-OHW-2-5-2/Images/inverted-cone-half-height-water.jpg>)

  Choose the volume $V(y_i)$ of a thin horizontal slice at height $y_i$ with thickness $\Delta y$.
options:
- id: a
  content: |-
    $V(y_i)=\pi\cdot\frac36y_i^2\cdot\Delta y$
  feedback: |-
    The ratio $3/6$ gives radius per unit height, not area per squared height. Since $r=(3/6)y_i$, the disk area contains $(3/6)^2=9/36$.
- id: b
  content: |-
    $V(y_i)=\pi\cdot\frac9{36}y_i^2\cdot\Delta y$
  correct: true
  feedback: |-
    A thin slice is a disk of volume $\pi r^2\Delta y$. Similar triangles give $r/y_i=3/6$, so $V(y_i)=\pi(9/36)y_i^2\Delta y$.
- id: c
  content: |-
    $V(y_i)=\pi\cdot\frac{36}9y_i^2\cdot\Delta y$
  feedback: |-
    This reverses the radius-to-height ratio. The cone reaches radius $3$ at height $6$, so $r=(3/6)y_i$, not $(6/3)y_i$.
- id: d
  content: |-
    $V(y_i)=\pi\cdot y_i^2\cdot\Delta y$
  feedback: |-
    This assumes $r=y_i$, which would require the cone's radius to equal its height. Here $r=y_i/2$, so the disk area is $\pi y_i^2/4$.
```

```quiz
  **Question 4**

  A rectangular swimming pool is $8\ \mathrm{ft}$ deep, $38\ \mathrm{ft}$ long, and $24\ \mathrm{ft}$ wide. It contains water to a depth of $7\ \mathrm{ft}$. The water's weight density is $\rho g=62.43\ \mathrm{lb/ft^3}$.

  Find the work required to pump all the water over the top of the pool. Round to the nearest whole number and enter the number only.

  $$
  W\approx ==1793489==\ \mathrm{ft\cdot lb}
  $$
feedback: |-
  Measure $y$ upward from the pool floor. Each slice has volume $38\cdot24\,dy$, weight $62.43(38)(24)\,dy$, and lifting distance $8-y$. Thus

  $$
  W=62.43(38)(24)\int_0^7(8-y)\,dy
   =1{,}793{,}489.04\ \mathrm{ft\cdot lb}.
  $$

  The nearest whole number is $1{,}793{,}489\ \mathrm{ft\cdot lb}$. Do not multiply by gravity again: $62.43$ is already a weight density.
```

## Problem 8

```quiz
type: blank
id: q-8
input_mode: math
require_exact: false
content: |-
  **Question 8**

  A hemispherical bowl-shaped tank has radius $8\ \mathrm{m}$ and contains water to a depth of $1\ \mathrm{m}$ above its bottom. The density of water is $1000\ \mathrm{kg/m^3}$.

  ![Hemispherical tank with radius r and water depth d](<Source/2026-08-24-OHW-2-5-2/Images/hemispherical-tank-water-depth.jpg>)

  Find the work required to pump all the water over the top of the tank. Use symbolic notation and fractions where needed.

  For this study answer, take $g=9.8\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$; the source does not specify a value of $g$.

  $$
  W= ==551250pi==\ \mathrm{J}
  $$
feedback: |-
  Measure $y$ upward from the bowl's bottom. The sphere's center is at height $8$, so the slice radius satisfies $r(y)^2=64-(y-8)^2=16y-y^2$. Each slice rises $8-y$:

  $$
  \begin{aligned}
  W&=1000g\pi\int_0^1(16y-y^2)(8-y)\,dy\\
   &=1000g\pi\left[64y^2-8y^3+\frac{y^4}{4}\right]_0^1\\
   &=56250g\pi.
  \end{aligned}
  $$

  Using $g=9.8\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$ gives $W=551250\pi\ \mathrm{J}$. The depth is measured from the bottom, so the bounds are $0$ to $1$, not $0$ to $8$.
```

## Problem 9

```quiz
type: blank
id: q-9
input_mode: math
require_exact: false
content: |-
  **Question 9**

  A hemispherical bowl-shaped tank of radius $8\ \mathrm{m}$ is full of water. The water density is $1000\ \mathrm{kg/m^3}$.

  Find the work required to pump all the water to a point $5\ \mathrm{m}$ above the top of the tank. Use symbolic notation and fractions where needed.

  For this study answer, take $g=9.8\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$; the source does not specify a value of $g$.

  $$
  W= ==80281600pi/3==\ \mathrm{J}
  $$
feedback: |-
  Measure $y$ upward from the bottom. The disk area is $\pi(16y-y^2)$, and the outlet is $8+5=13$ meters above the bottom. Thus

  $$
  \begin{aligned}
  W&=1000g\pi\int_0^8(16y-y^2)(13-y)\,dy\\
   &=1000g\pi\left[104y^2-\frac{29}{3}y^3+\frac{y^4}{4}\right]_0^8\\
   &=\frac{8192000g\pi}{3}.
  \end{aligned}
  $$

  At $g=9.8\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$, this is $\frac{80281600\pi}{3}\ \mathrm{J}$. The extra outlet height changes the lift to $13-y$; it does not change the water's bounds $0\le y\le8$.
```
