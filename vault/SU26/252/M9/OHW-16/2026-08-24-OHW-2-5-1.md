## Problem 1

```quiz
type: blank
id: q-1
input_mode: math
require_exact: true
content: |-
  **Question 1**

  A metal bar is $13\ \mathrm{m}$ long. At distance $x$ meters from one end, its mass density is

  $$
  \rho(x)=980+x-\sqrt{x}\quad\mathrm{kg/m^3}.
  $$

  Find the average mass density along the entire bar. Use decimal notation, rounded to three decimal places. Enter the number only.

  $$
  \bar{\rho}= ==984.096==\ \mathrm{kg/m^3}
  $$
feedback: |-
  The average value of a function is its integral divided by the interval length:

  $$
  \bar{\rho}
  =\frac1{13}\int_0^{13}(980+x-\sqrt{x})\,dx
  =980+\frac{13}{2}-\frac23\sqrt{13}
  \approx984.096299\ \mathrm{kg/m^3}.
  $$

  To three decimal places, the average density is $984.096\ \mathrm{kg/m^3}$.
```

## Problem 2

```quiz
type: blank
id: q-2
input_mode: math
require_exact: false
content: |-
  **Question 2**

  A force $F(x)=(40-x)\ \mathrm{N}$ moves an object in the force's direction along a straight line from $x=6\ \mathrm{m}$ to $x=15\ \mathrm{m}$. Find the work. Use symbolic notation and fractions where needed.

  $$
  W= ==531/2==\ \mathrm{J}
  $$
feedback: |-
  Work accumulates as force times displacement. Since the force is parallel to the motion,

  $$
  W=\int_6^{15}(40-x)\,dx
   =\left[40x-\frac{x^2}{2}\right]_6^{15}
   =\frac{531}{2}\ \mathrm{J}.
  $$
```

## Problem 3

```quiz
type: blank
id: q-3
input_mode: math
require_exact: false
content: |-
  **Question 3**

  A force $F(x)=\frac8x\ \mathrm{N}$ moves an object in the force's direction along a straight line from $x=5\ \mathrm{m}$ to $x=7\ \mathrm{m}$. Find the exact work. Use symbolic notation and fractions where needed.

  $$
  W= ==8ln(7/5)==\ \mathrm{J}
  $$
feedback: |-
  Integrate the changing force over the displacement interval:

  $$
  W=\int_5^7\frac8x\,dx
   =8[\ln x]_5^7
   =8\ln\left(\frac75\right)\ \mathrm{J}.
  $$

  The logarithm is positive because $7/5>1$, consistent with a force acting in the direction of motion.
```

## Problem 4

```quiz
type: blank
id: q-4
input_mode: math
require_exact: true
content: |-
  **Question 4**

  Holding a spring $\frac14\ \mathrm{ft}$ beyond its equilibrium length requires an external force of $6\ \mathrm{lb}$. Find its spring constant.

  $$
  k= ==24==\ \mathrm{lb/ft}
  $$
feedback: |-
  Hooke's law relates the holding-force magnitude to extension: $F=kx$. Here $x$ is the extension, not the spring's total length.

  $$
  k=\frac Fx=\frac6{1/4}=24\ \mathrm{lb/ft}.
  $$
```

## Problem 5

```quiz
type: blank
id: q-5
input_mode: math
require_exact: false
content: |-
  **Question 5**

  Evaluate exactly. Use symbolic notation and fractions where needed.

  $$
  \int_1^3\frac{e^{4x}}{e^{4x}-5}\,dx
  = ==(1/4)ln((e^12-5)/(e^4-5))==
  $$
feedback: |-
  The numerator is one-fourth of the denominator's derivative. With $u=e^{4x}-5$, $du=4e^{4x}\,dx$:

  $$
  \int_1^3\frac{e^{4x}}{e^{4x}-5}\,dx
  =\frac14[\ln(e^{4x}-5)]_1^3
  =\frac14\ln\left(\frac{e^{12}-5}{e^4-5}\right).
  $$

  The denominator is positive throughout $[1,3]$.
```

## Problem 6

```quiz
type: blank

input_mode: math

content: |-
  **Question 6**

  Evaluate exactly. Use $C$ for the arbitrary constant and absorb constant terms into $C$ where possible.

  $$
  \int\left[x^{13}+\sqrt{14+13x}\right]\,dx
  = ==x^14/14+(2/39)(14+13x)^(3/2)+C==
  $$
feedback: |-
  Integrate $x^{13}$ by the power rule. For the second term, use $u=14+13x$, so $dx=du/13$:

  $$
  \int\left[x^{13}+\sqrt{14+13x}\right]\,dx
  =\frac{x^{14}}{14}+\frac{2}{39}(14+13x)^{3/2}+C.
  $$

  Differentiating the second term multiplies by $13$, recovering $\sqrt{14+13x}$.
```

## Problem 7

```quiz
type: blank
id: q-7
input_mode: math
require_exact: false
content: |-
  **Question 7**

  Find the exact area under $y=xe^{10x}$ from $x=0$ to $x=2$. Use symbolic notation and fractions where needed.

  Area: ==(19e^20+1)/100==
feedback: |-
  The function is nonnegative on $[0,2]$, so its integral gives the area. Integration by parts with $u=x$ and $dv=e^{10x}\,dx$ gives

  $$
  \begin{aligned}
  A&=\int_0^2xe^{10x}\,dx\\
   &=\left[\frac{x e^{10x}}{10}-\frac{e^{10x}}{100}\right]_0^2\\
   &=\frac{19e^{20}+1}{100}.
  \end{aligned}
  $$

  Subtracting the lower-endpoint value $-1/100$ contributes the positive $1/100$.
```
