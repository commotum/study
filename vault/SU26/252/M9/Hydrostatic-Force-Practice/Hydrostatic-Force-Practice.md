## Problem 1

A full semicircular trough has radius $a=3\ \mathrm{m}$ and length $b=8\ \mathrm{m}$. Consider the hydrostatic force on one semicircular end.

Place the end face in the lower half of the circle centered at $(0,0)$ with radius $3\ \mathrm{m}$, with the top rim on the $x$-axis. Choose the area $A(y_i)$ of the $i$th thin horizontal strip at height $y_i$ and thickness $\Delta y$.

![Semicircular water trough with radius a and length b](<Source/Hydrostatic-Force-Practice/Images/semicircular-water-trough.jpg>)

- $A(y_i)=16\sqrt{3^2-y_i^2}\cdot\Delta y$
- $A(y_i)=16(3^2-y_i^2)\cdot\Delta y$
- $A(y_i)=2\sqrt{3^2-y_i^2}\cdot\Delta y$
- $A(y_i)=8\sqrt{3^2-y_i^2}\cdot\Delta y$
- $A(y_i)=\pi\left(\sqrt{3^2-y_i^2}\right)^2\cdot\Delta y$

## Problem 3

A trapezoidal trough is $10\ \mathrm{m}$ long. Its end face has bottom width $a=4\ \mathrm{m}$, top width $c=12\ \mathrm{m}$, and height $b=7\ \mathrm{m}$. Center the face on the $y$-axis, with its bottom on the $x$-axis and its interior above it in quadrants I and II.

Use water density $\rho=1000\ \mathrm{kg/m^3}$ and $g=9.8\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$. Both parts ask for the total hydrostatic force $F$, in newtons, on one trapezoidal end.

**Part A — Full trough**

The trough is full of water. Choose the integral for the force on one end.

![Trapezoidal water trough with bottom width a, height b, top width c, and length 10 meters](<Source/Hydrostatic-Force-Practice/Images/trapezoidal-water-trough.jpg>)

- $F=\displaystyle\int_0^7 9.8\cdot1000\cdot(7-y)\cdot10\cdot\frac47\left(y+\frac{28}8\right)\,dy$
- $F=\displaystyle\int_0^7 9.8\cdot1000\cdot(y-7)\cdot2\cdot\frac47\left(y+\frac{28}8\right)\,dy$
- $F=\displaystyle\int_0^7 9.8\cdot1000\cdot(7-y)\cdot2\cdot\frac47\left(y+\frac{28}8\right)\,dy$
- $F=\displaystyle\int_{-7}^0 9.8\cdot1000\cdot(7-y)\cdot10\cdot2\cdot\frac47\left(y+\frac{28}8\right)\,dy$

**Part B — Submerged end**

Keep the same orientation and dimensions, but submerge the trapezoidal end so the water surface is $d=4\ \mathrm{m}$ above its top edge. Choose the integral for the total force on the submerged end.

![Submerged trapezoidal end with bottom width a, height b, and top width c](<Source/Hydrostatic-Force-Practice/Images/submerged-trapezoidal-end.jpg>)

- $F=\displaystyle\int_0^9 9.8\cdot1000\cdot(7-y)\cdot10\cdot2\cdot\frac47\left(y-\frac{28}8\right)\,dy$
- $F=\displaystyle\int_0^7 9.8\cdot1000\cdot(11-y)\cdot2\cdot\frac47\left(y+\frac{28}8\right)\,dy$
- $F=\displaystyle\int_0^7 9.8\cdot1000\cdot(y-11)\cdot2\cdot\frac47\left(y+\frac{28}8\right)\,dy$
- $F=\displaystyle\int_0^7 9.8\cdot1000\cdot(4-y)\cdot10\cdot\frac47\left(y+\frac{28}8\right)\,dy$
- $F=\displaystyle\int_0^{11}9.8\cdot1000\cdot(4-y)\cdot2\cdot\frac47\left(y+\frac{28}8\right)\,dy$
- $F=\displaystyle\int_0^7 9.8\cdot1000\cdot(7-y)\cdot2\cdot\frac47\left(y+\frac{28}8\right)\,dy$
- $F=\displaystyle\int_0^7 9.8\cdot1000\cdot(4-y)\cdot2\cdot\frac47\left(y+\frac{28}8\right)\,dy$

## Problem 4

A semicircular water trough has radius $a=4\ \mathrm{m}$ and length $b=10\ \mathrm{m}$. Place its end face in the lower half of the circle centered at $(0,0)$ with radius $4\ \mathrm{m}$, with the top rim on the $x$-axis.

Use $\rho=1000\ \mathrm{kg/m^3}$ and $g=9.8\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$. Both parts ask for the total hydrostatic force $F$, in newtons, on one semicircular end.

![Semicircular trough with radius a and length b](<Source/Hydrostatic-Force-Practice/Images/semicircular-water-trough.jpg>)

**Part A — Full trough**

Choose the integral for the force when the trough is full.

- $F=\displaystyle\int_{-4}^0 9.8\cdot1000\cdot(-y)\cdot\pi\left(\sqrt{4^2-y^2}\right)^2\,dy$
- $F=\displaystyle\int_{-4}^0 9.8\cdot1000\cdot(4-y)\cdot20\sqrt{4^2-y^2}\,dy$
- $F=\displaystyle\int_0^4 9.8\cdot1000\cdot(-y)\cdot2\sqrt{4^2-y^2}\,dy$
- $F=\displaystyle\int_{-4}^0 9.8\cdot1000\cdot(-y)\cdot2\sqrt{4^2-y^2}\,dy$
- $F=\displaystyle\int_{-4}^0 9.8\cdot1000\cdot y\cdot20\sqrt{4^2-y^2}\,dy$

**Part B — Half-full trough**

Using the same orientation, choose the integral for the force when the trough is half full of water.

- $F=\displaystyle\int_{4/2}^4 9.8\cdot1000\cdot\left(\frac42-y\right)\cdot2\sqrt{4^2-y^2}\,dy$
- $F=\displaystyle\int_{-4}^{-4/2}9.8\cdot1000\cdot\left(-\frac42-y\right)\cdot2\sqrt{4^2-y^2}\,dy$
- $F=\displaystyle\int_0^{4/2}9.8\cdot1000\cdot\left(-\frac42-y\right)\cdot2\sqrt{4^2-y^2}\,dy$
- $F=\displaystyle\int_{-4}^{-4/2}9.8\cdot1000\cdot(-4-y)\cdot2\sqrt{4^2-y^2}\,dy$
