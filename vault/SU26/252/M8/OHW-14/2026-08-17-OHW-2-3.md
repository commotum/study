## Problem 1

```quiz
type: blank
id: q-1
input_mode: math
require_exact: false
content: |-
  **Question 1**

  Use cylindrical shells to find the exact volume formed by rotating the region under

  $$
  y=x^2+7,\qquad 0\le x\le6
  $$

  and above the $x$-axis about the $y$-axis.

  $$
  V= ==900pi==
  $$
feedback: |-
  A vertical shell has radius $x$ and height $x^2+7$. Therefore

  $$
  V=2\pi\int_0^6x(x^2+7)\,dx
   =2\pi\left[\frac{x^4}{4}+\frac{7x^2}{2}\right]_0^6
   =900\pi.
  $$
```

## Problem 4

Let $R$ be the region bounded by

$$
y=\sin(\pi x)+1,\qquad y=3-\frac{x^2}{2},\qquad x=-1.
$$

The curves meet at $x=2$. Set up each shell-method integral, but do not evaluate it.

![[Source/2026-08-17-OHW-2-3/Images/sine-parabola-shell-region.png]]

```quiz
type: radio
id: q-4a
shuffle: true
content: |-
  **Question 4 — Part A**

  Rotate $R$ about $x=-1$. Choose the correct integral.
options:
- id: a
  content: |-
    $$\displaystyle \int_{-1}^{2}2\pi(x-1)\left(3-\frac{x^2}{2}-\sin(\pi x)-1\right)\,dx$$
  feedback: |-
    The shell radius is the distance from $x$ to the axis $x=-1$, which is $x-(-1)=x+1$. The factor $x-1$ measures distance from $x=1$, so this radius is incorrect.
- id: b
  content: |-
    $$\displaystyle \int_{-1}^{2}2\pi(1-x)\left(3-\frac{x^2}{2}-\sin(\pi x)-1\right)\,dx$$
  feedback: |-
    Distance from the shell at $x$ to $x=-1$ is $x+1$. The expression $1-x$ instead measures a signed distance from $x=1$ and becomes negative on part of the interval.
- id: c
  content: |-
    $$\displaystyle \int_{-1}^{2}2\pi(x+1)\left(3-\frac{x^2}{2}-\sin(\pi x)-1\right)\,dx$$
  correct: true
  feedback: |-
    A vertical shell has radius $x+1$ and height

    $$
    \left(3-\frac{x^2}{2}\right)-\left(\sin(\pi x)+1\right).
    $$

    The region runs from $x=-1$ to $x=2$, so this is the shell integral.
- id: d
  content: |-
    $$\displaystyle \int_{-1}^{2}\pi\left(3-\frac{x^2}{2}-\sin(\pi x)-1\right)^2\,dx$$
  feedback: |-
    Squaring the vertical gap creates a disk-style area. Cylindrical shells require $2\pi(\text{radius})(\text{height})$, including the radius $x+1$.
- id: e
  content: |-
    $$\displaystyle \int_{-1}^{2}2\pi(x+1)\left(3-\frac{x^2}{2}-\sin(\pi x)-1\right)^2\,dx$$
  feedback: |-
    The radius $x+1$ is correct, but shell height is the vertical gap itself, not its square. The height factor must remain linear.
```

```quiz
  A vertical shell has radius $x$ and height $e^{-3x^2}$. With $u=-3x^2$,

  $$
  V=2\pi\int_0^3xe^{-3x^2}\,dx

   =\frac{\pi}{3}\left(1-e^{-27}\right).
  $$
```

```quiz
  Evaluate the definite integral exactly.

  $$
  \int_{\ln 3}^{\ln 4}\frac{e^x}{e^x+1}\,dx= ==ln(5/4)==
  $$
feedback: |-
  Since $\frac{d}{dx}(e^x+1)=e^x$,

  $$
  \int_{\ln 3}^{\ln 4}\frac{e^x}{e^x+1}\,dx
   =\left[\ln(e^x+1)\right]_{\ln 3}^{\ln 4}
   =\ln 5-\ln 4
   =\ln\left(\frac54\right).
  $$
```

## Problem 9

Let $R$ be the region bounded by

$$
y=\sqrt[3]{x}\qquad\text{and}\qquad 4y=x.
$$

The curves meet at $(0,0)$ and $(8,2)$. Rotate $R$ about $y=-10$, where $c=10$. Set up each integral, but do not evaluate it.

![[Source/2026-08-17-OHW-2-3/Images/cube-root-line-shell-region.png]]

```quiz
type: radio
id: q-9a
shuffle: true
content: |-
  **Question 9 — Part A**

  Choose the correct shell-method integral.
options:
- id: a
  content: |-
    $$\displaystyle \int_0^8 2\pi(x+10)\left(x^3-\frac{x}{4}\right)\,dx$$
  feedback: |-
    A horizontal axis requires horizontal shells, so the shell variable must be $y$. This option integrates with respect to $x$ and uses expressions that belong to the curves solved as $x$-functions of $y$.
- id: b
  content: |-
    $$\displaystyle \int_0^8 2\pi(10-x)\left(x^3-\frac{x}{4}\right)\,dx$$
  feedback: |-
    Shells about $y=-10$ must use horizontal distance $y+10$ and integrate with respect to $y$. The radius $10-x$ instead treats the axis as vertical.
- id: c
  content: |-
    $$\displaystyle \int_0^8 2\pi(x-10)\left(x^3-\frac{x}{4}\right)\,dx$$
  feedback: |-
    The shell radius is a nonnegative vertical distance from $y$ to $-10$, namely $y+10$. This option uses the wrong variable and a signed horizontal expression.
- id: d
  content: |-
    $$\displaystyle \int_0^2 2\pi(y+10)(4y-y^3)\,dy$$
  correct: true
  feedback: |-
    Horizontal shells run from $y=0$ to $y=2$. Their radius is $y+10$, and their height is

    $$
    x_{\mathrm{right}}-x_{\mathrm{left}}=4y-y^3.
    $$

    These factors give the correct shell integral.
- id: e
  content: |-
    $$\displaystyle \int_0^2 2\pi(10-y)(4y-y^3)\,dy$$
  feedback: |-
    The axis is below the region at $y=-10$, so the distance to a shell at $y$ is $y-(-10)=y+10$, not $10-y$.
- id: f
  content: |-
    $$\displaystyle \int_0^2 2\pi(y-10)(4y-y^3)\,dy$$
  feedback: |-
    Radius is distance and must be nonnegative. From $y$ to the axis $y=-10$, that distance is $y+10$; $y-10$ measures from $y=10$ instead.
```

```quiz
options:
- id: a
  content: |-
    $$\displaystyle \int_0^2\left[\pi\left(y^3-(-10)\right)^2-\pi\left(4y-(-10)\right)^2\right]\,dy$$

- id: b
  content: |-
    $$\displaystyle \int_0^2 2\pi(y+10)(y^3-4y)\,dy$$
  feedback: |-
    This has the shell factor $2\pi(\text{radius})(\text{height})$, not a washer area. Its height is also reversed: on $0\le y\le2$, $4y-y^3\ge0$.
- id: c
  content: |-
    $$\displaystyle \int_0^2\left[\pi(y^3-10)^2-\pi(4y-10)^2\right]\,dy$$
  feedback: |-
    The integral uses horizontal slices even though washers about a horizontal axis require vertical slices. It also measures from $y=10$ instead of the requested axis $y=-10$.
- id: d
  content: |-
    $$\displaystyle \int_0^8\left[\pi\left(\sqrt[3]{x}-10\right)^2-\pi\left(\frac{x}{4}-10\right)^2\right]\,dx$$
  feedback: |-
    The vertical-slice structure is appropriate, but each radius must be measured from $y=-10$. The distances are $\sqrt[3]{x}+10$ and $x/4+10$, not quantities minus $10$.
- id: e
  content: |-
    $$\displaystyle \int_0^8\left[\pi\left(\sqrt[3]{x}-(-10)\right)^2-\pi\left(\frac{x}{4}-(-10)\right)^2\right]\,dx$$
  correct: true
  feedback: |-
    For $0\le x\le8$, the upper curve is $y=\sqrt[3]{x}$ and the lower curve is $y=x/4$. Their distances from $y=-10$ are the outer and inner radii, so this is $\pi(R^2-r^2)$.

  content: |-
    $$\displaystyle \int_0^8\pi\left(\sqrt[3]{x}-\frac{x}{4}-10\right)^2\,dx$$
  feedback: |-
```

## Problem 10

```quiz
type: blank
id: q-10
input_mode: math
require_exact: true
content: |-
  **Question 10**

  Let

  $$
  f(x)=
  \begin{cases}
  5x^3-2, & x\le1,\\
  3\sqrt{x}, & x>1.
  \end{cases}
  $$

  Evaluate

  $$
  \int_{-1}^{4}f(x)\,dx= ==10==
  $$
feedback: |-
  Split the integral where the formula changes:

  $$
  \int_{-1}^{1}(5x^3-2)\,dx+\int_1^4 3\sqrt{x}\,dx.
  $$

  The odd term $5x^3$ integrates to zero on $[-1,1]$, so the first integral is $-4$. The second is $14$, giving a total of $10$.
```
