## Problem 1

Section 2.3, Problem 124:

Use cylindrical shells to find the volume of the solid formed by rotating the region between the curve and the $x$-axis about the $y$-axis.

$$
y=\frac{1}{1+x^2},\qquad x=0,\qquad x=3
$$

### Solution

A shell at $x$ has radius $x$, height $\frac{1}{1+x^2}$, and thickness $dx$. Therefore

$$
\begin{aligned}
V
&=2\pi\int_0^3 x\left(\frac{1}{1+x^2}\right)\,dx\\
&=\pi\int_0^3\frac{2x}{1+x^2}\,dx\\
&=\pi\left[\ln(1+x^2)\right]_0^3\\
&=\pi\ln(10).
\end{aligned}
$$

## Problem 2

Section 2.3, Problem 146:

Find the volume formed by rotating the region between

$$
y=\sqrt{x}\qquad\text{and}\qquad y=x^2
$$

about the line $x=2$.

### Solution

The curves intersect where $\sqrt{x}=x^2$, so the bounded region runs from $x=0$ to $x=1$. Using vertical cylindrical shells, the radius is $2-x$ and the height is $\sqrt{x}-x^2$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
V
&=2\pi\int_0^1(2-x)(\sqrt{x}-x^2)\,dx\\
&=2\pi\int_0^1\left(2x^{1/2}-x^{3/2}-2x^2+x^3\right)\,dx\\
&=2\pi\left[\frac{4}{3}x^{3/2}-\frac{2}{5}x^{5/2}-\frac{2}{3}x^3+\frac14x^4\right]_0^1\\
&=2\pi\left(\frac{31}{60}\right)\\
&=\frac{31\pi}{30}.
\end{aligned}
$$

## Problem 3

Section 2.3, Problem 148:

Find the volume formed by rotating the region between

$$
x=y^2\qquad\text{and}\qquad y=x
$$

about the line $y=2$.

### Solution

The curves intersect at $(0,0)$ and $(1,1)$. Using horizontal cylindrical shells for $0\le y\le1$, the radius is $2-y$ and the shell height is

$$
x_{\mathrm{right}}-x_{\mathrm{left}}=y-y^2.
$$

Thus

$$
\begin{aligned}
V
&=2\pi\int_0^1(2-y)(y-y^2)\,dy\\
&=2\pi\int_0^1(2y-3y^2+y^3)\,dy\\
&=2\pi\left[y^2-y^3+\frac14y^4\right]_0^1\\
&=2\pi\left(\frac14\right)\\
&=\frac{\pi}{2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

## Problem 4

Section 2.4, Problem 183:

Find the exact arc length of

$$
x=5y^{3/2}
$$

from $y=0$ to $y=1$. Keep the answer in exact form; do not use a calculator.

### Solution

For $x$ as a function of $y$,

$$
\frac{dx}{dy}=\frac{15}{2}\sqrt{y}.
$$

Therefore

$$
\begin{aligned}
L
&=\int_0^1\sqrt{1+\left(\frac{dx}{dy}\right)^2}\,dy\\
&=\int_0^1\sqrt{1+\frac{225}{4}y}\,dy.
\end{aligned}
$$

Let $u=1+\frac{225}{4}y$, so $du=\frac{225}{4}\,dy$. Then

$$
\begin{aligned}
L
&=\frac{4}{225}\int_1^{229/4}u^{1/2}\,du\\
&=\frac{8}{675}\left[u^{3/2}\right]_1^{229/4}\\
&=\frac{8}{675}\left(\left(\frac{229}{4}\right)^{3/2}-1\right)\\
&=\frac{229\sqrt{229}-8}{675}.
\end{aligned}
$$
