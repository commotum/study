## Introduction to Rational Functions in $s$

In this topic, the target move is to read a Laplace-domain expression in rational form, where one expression is written as a numerator polynomial divided by a denominator polynomial in $s$, then carry that structure forward into simplification and inversion preparation.

Start by identifying $X(s)$ as a ratio, even when it appears as a single term or a sum of terms. Write that ratio as $X(s)=\frac{N(s)}{D(s)}$, where $N(s)$ is the numerator polynomial and $D(s)$ is the denominator polynomial.

$$
X(s)=\\frac{N(s)}{D(s)}
$$

For the base case $X(s)=\frac{3s+2}{s^2+4s+5}$, set $N(s)=3s+2$ and $D(s)=s^2+4s+5$. Use this exact split every time: polynomial terms with descending powers of $s$, explicit signs, and clear grouping before any algebraic combining.

$$
X(s)=\\frac{3s+2}{s^2+4s+5} \\quad N(s)=3s+2,\\; D(s)=s^2+4s+5
$$

When expressions are not already a single ratio, do not jump to final simplification. First label numerator and denominator pieces separately, then classify whether you have improper terms or split pieces so the algebra step stays controlled.

Use this workflow on every section: identify $N(s)$ and $D(s)$, rewrite into the required ratio form, and check each part before moving on to practice problems.

## Identifying Numerator and Denominator Polynomials in $s$

**Example:** Find the numerator and denominator polynomials for $X(s)=\frac{4s^3-2s+7}{s^2+5s+6}$.

**Explanation**

Apply the same core move as the tutorial: identify the two polynomial pieces of one rational expression $X(s)$ and label them as $N(s)$ and $D(s)$. That is the target pattern for this section.

$$
X(s)=\\frac{4s^3-2s+7}{s^2+5s+6}
$$

Step 1: keep the expression as a single fraction and read the top part as the numerator polynomial and the bottom part as the denominator polynomial.

Step 2: expand or collect powers only if needed. Here the terms are already separated, so we do not expand. Step 3: verify descending order in $s$. The numerator is $4s^3+0s^2-2s+7$, already ordered; the denominator is $s^2+5s+6$, also ordered.

$$
N(s)=4s^3-2s+7,\\;D(s)=s^2+5s+6
$$

Final result: $N(s)=4s^3-2s+7$ and $D(s)=s^2+5s+6$. Use this exact labeling before moving into any combination or inversion work.

## Rewriting Laplace-Domain Expressions as Single Rational Functions

**Example:** Rewrite $\frac{1}{s+1}+\frac{2}{s+2}+\frac{1}{s+1}$ as a single rational function.

**Explanation**

Target: convert a small sum of rational terms into one standard ratio. Treat every term as part of a common-denominator form so the result is immediately reusable for matching, combining, and inverse-transform preparation.

$$
X(s)=\\frac{1}{s+1}+\\frac{2}{s+2}+\\frac{1}{s+1}
$$

Step 1: read the denominators $s+1$ and $s+2$. The common denominator is $D(s)=(s+1)(s+2)$.

Step 2: scale each numerator by the missing factor from the other term's denominator. This gives three terms with one common denominator.

$$
\\frac{1}{s+1}=\\frac{s+2}{(s+1)(s+2)},\\quad\\frac{2}{s+2}=\\frac{2(s+1)}{(s+1)(s+2)},\\quad\\frac{1}{s+1}=\\frac{s+2}{(s+1)(s+2)}
$$

Step 3: add scaled numerators and simplify the result into one polynomial $N(s)$. Then write the full ratio form with $D(s)= (s+1)(s+2)$. 

$$
N(s)= (s+2)+2(s+1)+(s+2)=4s+6,\\; D(s)=(s+1)(s+2)
$$

$$
X(s)=\\frac{4s+6}{(s+1)(s+2)}=\\frac{2(2s+3)}{(s+1)(s+2)}
$$

Final result: $N(s)=4s+6$ and $D(s)=(s+1)(s+2)$, with one fully combined rational function suitable for later simplification.

## Combining Rational Terms Over a Common Denominator

**Example:** Combine $\frac{4}{s+1} + \frac{3}{s(s+1)} - \frac{2}{s}$ into a single rational expression.

**Explanation**

Goal: combine three structurally different Laplace-domain fractions into one standard form. This is the same core move as the previous step, now with subtraction and a broader denominator set.

Step 1: identify all denominators and pick the common denominator $D(s)=s(s+1)$. Every term will be rewritten with this same denominator.

$$
X(s)=\\frac{4}{s+1}+\\frac{3}{s(s+1)}-\\frac{2}{s}
$$

Step 2: convert each term by multiplying numerator and denominator with the missing factor so that each has denominator $s(s+1)$.

$$
\\frac{4}{s+1}=\\frac{4s}{s(s+1)},\\quad\\frac{3}{s(s+1)}=\\frac{3}{s(s+1)},\\quad-\\frac{2}{s}=-\\frac{2(s+1)}{s(s+1)}
$$

Step 3: collect the numerators and combine coefficients on the same powers of $s$ in $N(s)=4s+3-2(s+1)=2s+1$. Keep the final denominator explicit as $s(s+1)$.

$$
N(s)=2s+1,\\; D(s)=s(s+1)
$$

Final result: $X(s)=\\frac{2s+1}{s(s+1)}$. This is now a single rational function with a single numerator-denominator pair.

## Simplifying Rational Functions in $s$ After Combining Terms

**Example:** Simplify $\frac{(s+2)^2 - (s-1)}{s^2+s}$ and rewrite it as numerator and denominator polynomials in reduced form.

**Explanation**

The cue is the combined ratio form. Keep one ratio structure and simplify only the algebra, not the core target of rational form.

$$
X(s)=\\frac{(s+2)^2-(s-1)}{s^2+s}
$$

Step 1: expand the numerator and collect powers of $s$. Expand $(s+2)^2=s^2+4s+4$, then subtract $(s-1)$.

$$
(s+2)^2-(s-1)=\left(s^2+4s+4\right)-(s-1)=s^2+3s+5
$$

Step 2: simplify the denominator only if possible and keep descending-power order. Here $s^2+s=s(s+1)$ is equivalent and clear for algebra checks.

$$
s^2+s=s(s+1)
$$

Step 3: report the standardized rational form with collected numerator terms.

$$
X(s)=\\frac{s^2+3s+5}{s(s+1)}
$$

Final result: $N(s)=s^2+3s+5$ and $D(s)=s(s+1)$. No extra factors are canceled, and the expression is ready for partial-fraction preparation.

<a id="recognizing-rational-functions-ready-for-partial-fraction-setup"></a>
## Recognizing Rational Functions Ready for Partial-Fraction Setup

**Example:** Check readiness of $X(s)=\frac{s^3+2s^2+s+1}{s^2+3s+2}$ and describe the necessary adjustment before partial fractions.

**Explanation**

Read the expression as a candidate input for partial fractions and first check rational properness.

$$
X(s)=\\frac{s^3+2s^2+s+1}{s^2+3s+2}
$$

Step 1: compare degrees. Numerator degree is $3$, denominator degree is $2$, so $\deg N(s) \ge \deg D(s)$; therefore the expression is improper and not yet in final-ready partial-fraction form.

Step 2: perform polynomial division to separate a polynomial part from a proper fraction.

$$
s^3+2s^2+s+1=(s-1)(s^2+3s+2)+(2s+3)
$$

Step 3: divide both sides by $s^2+3s+2$ to get $X(s)=s-1+\frac{2s+3}{s^2+3s+2}$. The remaining fraction now has numerator degree $1$, denominator degree $2$, so it is proper.

$$
X(s)=s-1+\\frac{2s+3}{(s+1)(s+2)}
$$

Final readiness state for partial-fraction setup: perform long division when needed, then pass the proper remainder term $\frac{2s+3}{(s+1)(s+2)}$ to partial fractions.
