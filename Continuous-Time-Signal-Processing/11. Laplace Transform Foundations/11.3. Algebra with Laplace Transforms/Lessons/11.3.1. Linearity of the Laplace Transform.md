## Introduction to Linearity of the Laplace Transform

The move in this step is one-sided linearity: if signals are causal, then algebraic operations in time carry directly into the transform domain with the same scalar and sum structure. A causal signal satisfies $x(t)=0$ for $t<0$, so the one-sided transform operator $\mathcal{L}_+$ is the one used in this topic.

Use the learning goal by starting with the base case. For a known causal transform pair $x(t) \leftrightarrow X(s)$, multiply the time signal by a scalar $a$ and carry that coefficient outside the transform operator before doing any further expansion.

$$
\mathcal{L}_+\{a x(t)\} = aX(s)
$$

Next, add the two-term sum rule. For causal signals, add transform-domain terms term-by-term in the same order so each original signal keeps its own transformed identity.

$$
\mathcal{L}_+\{x_1(t)+x_2(t)\} = X_1(s) + X_2(s)
$$

Combine both ideas into a single operational rule and keep a short notation checklist: pull out coefficients, match each time-domain term, then write the transform sum with the same coefficients and signs.

$$
\mathcal{L}_+\{a x_1(t)+b x_2(t)\} = aX_1(s)+bX_2(s)
$$

## Transforming a Scaled Causal Signal

**Example:** Find the one-sided Laplace transform of $x(t)=3e^{-2t}u(t)$.

**Explanation**

Cue: identify the expression as a single causal exponential scaled by a constant. Write $x(t)=3\,e^{-2t}u(t)$ as a scalar multiple of the base pair $e^{-2t}u(t)$, where $u(t)$ confirms one-sided causality.

$$
\mathcal{L}_+\{e^{-2t}u(t)\}=\frac{1}{s+2},\quad \Re\{s\}>-2
$$

Use linearity to pull the coefficient $3$ outside the one-sided transform operator first, then apply the matched pair without introducing any additional transform-algebra.

$$
\mathcal{L}_+\{3e^{-2t}u(t)\}=3\,\mathcal{L}_+\{e^{-2t}u(t)\}=3\left(\frac{1}{s+2}\right).
$$

Result: $X(s)=\dfrac{3}{s+2}$, with causality preserved by the one-sided transform context.

## Transforming a Sum of Causal Signals

**Example:** Find $\mathcal{L}_+\{e^{-t}u(t)+e^{-3t}u(t)\}$.

**Explanation**

Cue: the input is a sum of causal exponentials, so match each term independently before applying linearity to the whole sum. The structure is $x_1(t)+x_2(t)$ with $x_1(t)=e^{-t}u(t)$ and $x_2(t)=e^{-3t}u(t)$.

$$
\mathcal{L}_+\{e^{-t}u(t)\}=\frac{1}{s+1},\quad \mathcal{L}_+\{e^{-3t}u(t)\}=\frac{1}{s+3}
$$

Now choose the linearity rule for sums: transform the whole expression as the sum of transformed terms, keeping the same ordering and no coefficient changes because both terms are unit-scaled.

$$
\mathcal{L}_+\{e^{-t}u(t)+e^{-3t}u(t)\}=\mathcal{L}_+\{e^{-t}u(t)\}+\mathcal{L}_+\{e^{-3t}u(t)\}
$$

$$
\mathcal{L}_+\{e^{-t}u(t)+e^{-3t}u(t)\}=\frac{1}{s+1}+\frac{1}{s+3}
$$

Result (kept separated for the next step): $X(s)=\frac{1}{s+1}+\frac{1}{s+3}$.

## Transforming a Scaled Sum of Causal Signals

**Example:** Transform $x(t)=4e^{-t}u(t)-\frac{1}{2}e^{-4t}u(t)$ using linearity.

**Explanation**

Cue: this signal already has two causal terms, each with its own scalar, and opposite signs in the sum. Write it as two scalar-weighted components $4x_1(t)-\frac{1}{2}x_2(t)$ so both the previous rules apply together.

Define the core pieces: $x_1(t)=e^{-t}u(t)$ and $x_2(t)=e^{-4t}u(t)$. Then use the known one-sided pairs for each piece.

$$
\mathcal{L}_+\{x_1(t)\}=\frac{1}{s+1},\quad \mathcal{L}_+\{x_2(t)\}=\frac{1}{s+4}
$$

Now split and transform with linearity in one pass, preserving the exact coefficient sign and value on each matched term.

$$
\mathcal{L}_+\{4e^{-t}u(t)-\frac{1}{2}e^{-4t}u(t)\}=4\mathcal{L}_+\{e^{-t}u(t)\}-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{L}_+\{e^{-4t}u(t)\}
$$

$$
\mathcal{L}_+\{4e^{-t}u(t)-\frac{1}{2}e^{-4t}u(t)\}=\frac{4}{s+1}-\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{s+4}
$$

Final result: $X(s)=\dfrac{4}{s+1}-\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot\dfrac{1}{s+4}$, with each coefficient and sign kept explicit before later simplification steps.

<a id="simplifying-the-resulting-expression-in-the-s-domain"></a>
## Simplifying the Resulting Expression in the s-Domain

**Example:** Transform $x(t)=3e^{-t}u(t)-e^{-3t}u(t)$ and simplify $\frac{3}{s+1}-\frac{1}{s+3}$ to a single fraction.

**Explanation**

Cue: the step already has the linearity-mapped split form. Keep the termwise matches from previous steps and use the added algebraic step: combine $\frac{3}{s+1}-\frac{1}{s+3}$ into one ratio.

First restate the linearity result from the time-domain side and keep the two terms identified before simplifying:

$$
\mathcal{L}_+\{3e^{-t}u(t)-e^{-3t}u(t)\}=\frac{3}{s+1}-\frac{1}{s+3}
$$

Now perform common-denominator combining. The shared denominator is $(s+1)(s+3)$.

$$
\frac{3}{s+1}-\frac{1}{s+3}=\frac{3(s+3)}{(s+1)(s+3)}-\frac{(s+1)}{(s+1)(s+3)}
$$

$$
\frac{3(s+3)}{(s+1)(s+3)}-\frac{(s+1)}{(s+1)(s+3)}=\frac{2s+8}{(s+1)(s+3)}
$$

Final simplified result: $\mathcal{L}_+\{x(t)\}=\frac{2s+8}{(s+1)(s+3)}$.
