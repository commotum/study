## Introduction to Inverse Laplace Transform by Known Pairs

In this step, your target is to map a prepared one-sided transform expression $X(s)$ into time-domain pieces. Each term in $X(s)$ must be recognized first, then converted to its matched known inverse pair before anything else is combined.

Use linearity as the action rule: identify the template for each term, convert each term independently, then add the time-domain pieces back together. The smallest case is $\mathcal{L}_+^{-1}\{1/(s+a)\}=e^{-at}u(t)$ and $\mathcal{L}_+^{-1}\{1/s\}=u(t)$.

$$
\mathcal{L}^{-1}\{F(s)+G(s)\}=f(t)+g(t)
$$

Then apply the scaled version term-by-term. If a term has an outside constant, keep that constant attached to the matched time-domain term.

$$
\mathcal{L}^{-1}\{kF(s)\}=kf(t)
$$

So for the base check, $\mathcal{L}_+^{-1}\{3/(s+2)\}=3e^{-2t}u(t)$: first match $1/(s+2)$, then carry the scalar through.

Common failures are forgetting the $u(t)$ factor, changing the sign in the shift $s+a$, or matching after doing denominator algebra instead of before. Use this checklist: identify template, apply inverse pair, apply coefficients/signs, then sum outputs.

## Matching a Single Prepared Term to a Known Pair

**Example:** Find $x(t)$ for $X(s)=7/(s+6)$.

**Explanation**

Identify the term as a one-sided inverse-transform pattern first. Your cue is a rational term with denominator in the shifted form $s+a$, so you should match it against the template before doing any algebraic rearrangement.

Rewrite the prompt term as $\frac{7}{s+6} = 7 \cdot \frac{1}{s+6}$. This isolates the scalar from the canonical core $\frac{1}{s+a}$ with $a=6$.

$$
\\mathcal{L}_+^{-1}\\left\\{\\frac{1}{s+a}\\right\\} = e^{-a t}u(t)
$$

Apply the pair with linearity: for $k/(s+a)$, the inverse is $k e^{-a t}u(t)$. Here $k=7$ and $a=6$, so the time-domain term is $7e^{-6t}u(t)$.

Final result: $x(t)=7e^{-6t}u(t)$. Keep the sign in the exponent and the one-sided factor $u(t)$ exactly as shown; this is the single new wrinkle from the tutorial because the coefficient is non-unit while the base term structure remains the same.

## Inverting a Sum of Prepared Terms

**Example:** Find $x(t)$ for $X(s)=4/(s+2)+3/(s+5)$.

**Explanation**

The cue is that $X(s)$ is already prepared as a sum of standard inverse-transform templates, so the move is to split, invert, and then recombine by linearity.

First split the transform into separate prepared terms: $4/(s+2)$ and $3/(s+5)$.

$$
X(s)=\frac{4}{s+2}+\frac{3}{s+5}=4\cdot \frac{1}{s+2}+3\cdot \frac{1}{s+5}
$$

Apply the template from the previous steps to each term: for each $a>0$, $\mathcal{L}_+^{-1}\{\frac{1}{s+a}\}=e^{-a t}u(t)$. Thus, $\mathcal{L}_+^{-1}\{4/(s+2)\}=4e^{-2t}u(t)$ and $\mathcal{L}_+^{-1}\{3/(s+5)\}=3e^{-5t}u(t)$.

Finally, combine the inverse components in the time domain by linearity to get one result: $x(t)=4e^{-2t}u(t)+3e^{-5t}u(t)$.

Do not invert the sum as a whole before decomposition. Keep each term explicit in $s$-domain, then attach one $u(t)$ factor to each causal component before adding.

## Using Linearity with Scaled Inverse Transform Terms

**Example:** Find $x(t)$ for $X(s)=4/(5(s+1))+7/(2(s-2))-3/(s+4)$.

**Explanation**

Use this as a coefficient-check step: the cue is a sum in $X(s)$ where each term has a scalar multiplier and one is signed negative.

$$
X(s)=\frac{4}{5(s+1)}+\frac{7}{2(s-2)}-\frac{3}{s+4}=\frac{4}{5}\cdot \frac{1}{s+1}+\frac{7}{2}\cdot \frac{1}{s-2}-3\cdot \frac{1}{s+4}
$$

Apply the same one-sided pair template to each prepared fraction: $\mathcal{L}_+^{-1}\{\frac{1}{s+a}\}=e^{-a t}u(t)$. This gives matched pairs for $a=1$, $a=-2$, and $a=4$.

Bring each coefficient outside its corresponding inverse and keep the sign on the third term: $\mathcal{L}_+^{-1}\{4/(5(s+1))\}=\frac{4}{5}e^{-t}u(t)$, $\mathcal{L}_+^{-1}\{7/(2(s-2))\}=\frac{7}{2}e^{2t}u(t)$, $\mathcal{L}_+^{-1}\{-3/(s+4)\}=-3e^{-4t}u(t)$.

$$
x(t)=\frac{4}{5}e^{-t}u(t)+\frac{7}{2}e^{2t}u(t)-3e^{-4t}u(t)
$$

Final result: $x(t)=\frac{4}{5}e^{-t}u(t)+\frac{7}{2}e^{2t}u(t)-3e^{-4t}u(t)$. The only control move is coefficient and sign bookkeeping before summing.

## Recombining a Prepared Partial-Fraction Form into $x(t)$

**Example:** Given $X(s)=2/(s+1)-1/(s+4)+3/(s-2)$, compute $x(t)$ and combine the time-domain terms.

**Explanation**

This is a recombination step: first finish each inverse map on the prepared partial-fraction terms, then add the three time-domain pieces into one $x(t)$ expression.

$$
X(s)=\frac{2}{s+1}-\frac{1}{s+4}+\frac{3}{s-2}=2\cdot \frac{1}{s+1}-1\cdot \frac{1}{s+4}+3\cdot \frac{1}{s-2}
$$

Use the same template $\mathcal{L}_+^{-1}\{1/(s+a)\}=e^{-at}u(t)$ term by term. With $a=1$, $a=4$, and $a=-2$, the three inverses are $2e^{-t}u(t)$, $-e^{-4t}u(t)$, and $3e^{2t}u(t)$.

$$
\mathcal{L}_+^{-1}\{X(s)\}=2e^{-t}u(t)-e^{-4t}u(t)+3e^{2t}u(t)
$$

So the final coherent inverse is $x(t)=2e^{-t}u(t)-e^{-4t}u(t)+3e^{2t}u(t)$. All terms keep their own coefficient, sign, and $u(t)$ factor, and signs are checked before final sum.

A common failure here is changing the sign in $e^{-a t}$ by treating every pole as decaying; keep $a=-2$ explicitly to get $e^{2t}u(t)$ for the $1/(s-2)$ term.
