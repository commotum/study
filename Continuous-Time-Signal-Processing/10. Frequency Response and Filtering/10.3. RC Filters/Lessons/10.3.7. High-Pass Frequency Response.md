## Introduction to High-Pass Frequency Response

Frequency response asks what the high-pass circuit does to one sinusoid at frequency $\omega$. For that base case, we rewrite the differential equation in the frequency domain, where $X(j\omega)$ and $Y(j\omega)$ represent the input and output at the same sinusoidal frequency.

$$
RC\,\frac{dx(t)}{dt}=y(t)+RC\,\frac{dy(t)}{dt}
$$

In steady state, a time derivative becomes multiplication by $j\omega$ for a sinusoidal input. Apply that rule term by term to the high-pass equation: the derivative of the input becomes $j\omega X(j\omega)$, and the derivative of the output becomes $j\omega Y(j\omega)$.

$$
j\omega RC\,X(j\omega)=(1+j\omega RC)Y(j\omega)
$$

Once the derivatives have been replaced, the circuit equation is an algebraic relation between $X(j\omega)$ and $Y(j\omega)$. That is the form we need before we can isolate the response of the circuit itself.

$$
H(j\omega)=\frac{Y(j\omega)}{X(j\omega)}
$$

This ratio is the frequency response. In the next steps, we will solve for $H(j\omega)$ and use it to read the amplitude scaling and phase shift of the high-pass filter at each input frequency.

## Replacing Derivatives with $j\omega$ in the High-Pass Equation

**Example:** Convert the high-pass differential equation $RC\,\frac{dx(t)}{dt}=y(t)+RC\,\frac{dy(t)}{dt}$ into its frequency-domain form by replacing each derivative with $j\omega$.

**Explanation**

Start with the high-pass differential equation from the previous step. For a single sinusoid at frequency $\omega$, our goal is to rewrite that time-domain relation in terms of $X(j\omega)$ and $Y(j\omega)$.

$$
RC\,\frac{dx(t)}{dt}=y(t)+RC\,\frac{dy(t)}{dt}
$$

In steady state, each time derivative becomes multiplication by $j\omega$. Apply that rule to both derivative terms, and keep the non-derivative terms as the frequency-domain quantities $X(j\omega)$ and $Y(j\omega)$.

$$
j\omega RC\,X(j\omega)=(1+j\omega RC)Y(j\omega)
$$

That is the algebraic frequency-domain relation for the high-pass circuit. We have not solved for $H(j\omega)$ yet; this step only replaces the derivatives and puts the equation in the form we will use next.

<a id="solving-the-high-pass-equation-for-hjomega"></a>
## Solving the High-Pass Equation for $H(j\omega)$

**Example:** Starting from $j\omega RC\,X(j\omega)=(1+j\omega RC)Y(j\omega)$, solve for $H(j\omega)=\frac{Y(j\omega)}{X(j\omega)}$.

**Explanation**

Start from the frequency-domain equation produced in the previous step. The ratio $Y(j\omega)/X(j\omega)$ is the transfer function, so isolate that ratio without moving on to interpretation yet.

$$
\begin{aligned}
j\omega RC\,X(j\omega) &= (1+j\omega RC)Y(j\omega) \\
\frac{Y(j\omega)}{X(j\omega)} &= \frac{j\omega RC}{1+j\omega RC} \\
H(j\omega) &= \frac{j\omega RC}{1+j\omega RC}
\end{aligned}
$$

That is the standard high-pass frequency response. The next step will use $H(j\omega)$ to write the sinusoidal output in amplitude-phase form.

<a id="using-hjomega-to-write-the-high-pass-sinusoidal-output"></a>
## Using $H(j\omega)$ to Write the High-Pass Sinusoidal Output

**Example:** For an input sinusoid $x(t)=A\cos(\omega t+\phi)$, use $H(j\omega)=\frac{j\omega RC}{1+j\omega RC}$ to write the corresponding high-pass output in amplitude-phase form.

**Explanation**

Start with the input sinusoid and the transfer function at the same frequency $\omega$. The output keeps the same angular frequency; only the amplitude and phase change.

$$
\begin{aligned}
H(j\omega) &= \frac{j\omega RC}{1+j\omega RC} \\
|H(j\omega)| &= \frac{\omega RC}{\sqrt{1+(\omega RC)^2}} \\
\angle H(j\omega) &= \frac{\pi}{2}-\tan^{-1}(\omega RC) \\
y(t) &= A|H(j\omega)|\cos\!\bigl(\omega t+\phi+\angle H(j\omega)\bigr) \\
&= A\frac{\omega RC}{\sqrt{1+(\omega RC)^2}}\cos\!\Bigl(\omega t+\phi+\frac{\pi}{2}-\tan^{-1}(\omega RC)\Bigr)
\end{aligned}
$$

The gain multiplies the input amplitude, and the phase of $H(j\omega)$ adds to the input phase. That gives the real high-pass output in amplitude-phase form.
