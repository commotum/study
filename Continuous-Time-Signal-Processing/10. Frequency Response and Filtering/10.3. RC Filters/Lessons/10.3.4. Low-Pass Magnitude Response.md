## Introduction to Low-Pass Magnitude Response

The magnitude response is the gain curve $|H(j\omega)|$ of the RC low-pass filter. Read it when you want to know how much a sinusoid at frequency $\omega$ is scaled, without tracking the phase.

$$
H(j\omega)=\frac{1}{1+j\omega RC}
$$

Start from the known low-pass frequency response $H(j\omega)=\frac{1}{1+j\omega RC}$. The magnitude response is the absolute value of that complex response, so the phase factor does not change the gain.

$$
|H(j\omega)|=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+(\omega RC)^2}}
$$

The cleanest check is the zero-frequency case: at $\omega=0$, the gain is $1$. So very low frequencies pass through the filter with essentially no attenuation.

$$
|H(j0)|=1
$$

At the opposite extreme, the gain falls toward $0$ as $\omega$ gets large. That is the low-pass signature: low frequencies are passed, high frequencies are attenuated.

$$
\lim_{\omega\to\infty}|H(j\omega)|=0
$$

So the magnitude response condenses the filter into one nonnegative curve: near $1$ at low frequency and near $0$ at high frequency. That compact gain curve is the frequency-domain statement of pass-low, attenuate-high behavior.

## Computing |$H(j\omega)$| from the Low-Pass Frequency Response

**Example:** Given $H(j\omega)=\frac{1}{1+j\omega RC}$, compute $|H(j\omega)|$ and simplify it to a real-valued gain expression.

**Explanation**

The cue is the known RC low-pass frequency response $H(j\omega)=\frac{1}{1+j\omega RC}$. The action is to take its magnitude and simplify the complex denominator until only a real gain remains.

$$
\left|H(j\omega)\right|=\left|\frac{1}{1+j\omega RC}\right|=\frac{|1|}{|1+j\omega RC|}
$$

The numerator has magnitude $1$. For the denominator, use the magnitude of $a+jb$ with $a=1$ and $b=\omega RC$.

$$
|1+j\omega RC|=\sqrt{1+(\omega RC)^2}
$$

Substitute that denominator magnitude back into the gain expression.

$$
|H(j\omega)|=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+(\omega RC)^2}}
$$

That is the simplified RC low-pass magnitude response: a real, nonnegative gain formula written directly from the frequency response.

## Evaluating Low-Frequency Gain for the RC Low-Pass Filter

**Example:** Starting from $|H(j\omega)|=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+(\omega RC)^2}}$, compute the low-frequency gain $|H(j0)|$ and explain why the filter behaves like a pass-through near DC.

**Explanation**

The cue is the low-frequency endpoint of the magnitude formula from the previous step. Use direct substitution at $\omega=0$ in $|H(j\omega)|=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+(\omega RC)^2}}$ to evaluate the gain near DC.

$$
|H(j\omega)|=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+(\omega RC)^2}}
$$

Substitute $\omega=0$ and simplify the denominator. The squared term vanishes, so the response becomes a unit gain.

$$
|H(j0)|=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+(0\cdot RC)^2}}=1
$$

So the RC low-pass filter passes a constant or slowly varying component with gain $1$ at DC. That is the low-frequency endpoint that shows the filter behaves like a pass-through near zero frequency.

## Evaluating High-Frequency Gain for the RC Low-Pass Filter

**Example:** Using $|H(j\omega)|=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+(\omega RC)^2}}$, evaluate the high-frequency gain as $\omega\to\infty$ and explain why the response goes to 0.

**Explanation**

The cue is the high-frequency endpoint of the same magnitude formula. Use the limit $\omega\to\infty$ on $|H(j\omega)|=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+(\omega RC)^2}}$ so we can see what happens to rapid oscillations.

$$
|H(j\omega)|=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+(\omega RC)^2}}
$$

As $\omega$ grows, the denominator grows without bound while the numerator stays $1$. That forces the gain to shrink toward zero.

$$
\lim_{\omega\to\infty}|H(j\omega)|=0
$$

So the RC low-pass filter attenuates high-frequency components: rapid oscillations are suppressed because their gain approaches zero.

## Interpreting the Low-Pass Magnitude Response as Passing and Attenuating Frequencies

**Example:** Given $|H(j\omega)|=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+(\omega RC)^2}}$, explain how the response behaves at low and high frequencies and use that to classify the filter as low-pass.

**Explanation**

The cue is the same magnitude curve from the previous steps. We already know the low-frequency gain is $1$ at $\omega=0$ and the high-frequency gain tends to $0$ as $\omega\to\infty$.

$$
|H(j\omega)|=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+(\omega RC)^2}}
$$

That means the curve starts near $1$ at low frequency and falls toward $0$ as frequency increases. So the response gives strong gain to slowly varying components and weak gain to rapid oscillations.

$$
|H(j0)|=1,\qquad \lim_{\omega\to\infty}|H(j\omega)|=0
$$

Therefore the RC filter is low-pass: low frequencies are passed, while high frequencies are attenuated. The magnitude response is the frequency-domain summary of that behavior.
