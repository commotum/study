## Introduction to Deriving the Output for a Complex Exponential Input

Treat $x(t)=e^{j\omega t}$ as a probe input for an LTI system. The goal of this step is to set up the convolution so the factor that depends only on $t$ will be easy to see in the next algebraic move.

$$
y(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x(t-\tau)h(\tau)\,d\tau
$$

Write the convolution in shifted-input form so the substitution matches $x(t-\tau)$ exactly. With that form in place, insert the input as $x(t-\tau)=e^{j\omega(t-\tau)}$.

$$
x(t-\tau)=e^{j\omega(t-\tau)}
$$

Now the output is written with the complex exponential inside the integral, and the integrand is ready to be separated into its $t$-dependent and $\tau$-dependent parts. That separation is the move that will expose the system's complex gain at $\omega$.

$$
y(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{j\omega(t-\tau)}h(\tau)\,d\tau
$$

Keep this setup in mind: the next step will rewrite the exponential, factor out $e^{j\omega t}$, and leave the remaining $\tau$-integral as the frequency-response factor.

## Substituting $e^{j\omega t}$ into the LTI Convolution Integral

**Example:** Start from $y(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x(t-\tau)h(\tau)\,d\tau$ and substitute $x(t-\tau)=e^{j\omega(t-\tau)}$.

**Explanation**

Use the shifted-input convolution form. The cue is the slot $x(t-\tau)$ inside $y(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x(t-\tau)h(\tau)\,d\tau$; in this step, replace only that input term and leave $h(\tau)$ unchanged.

$$
y(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x(t-\tau)h(\tau)\,d\tau
$$

Now substitute the probe input directly into the shifted slot: $x(t-\tau)=e^{j\omega(t-\tau)}$. Keep the $t-\tau$ dependence intact.

$$
y(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{j\omega(t-\tau)}h(\tau)\,d\tau
$$

That is the finished example result. Stop before factoring out $e^{j\omega t}$; this step is only about making the substitution cleanly.

## Rewriting $e^{j\omega(t-\tau)}$ to Separate $t$ and $\tau$

**Example:** Rewrite $e^{j\omega(t-\tau)}$ so the factor depending only on $t$ is separated from the factor depending only on $\tau$.

**Explanation**

The cue is the shifted exponential inside the integral. Use the exponent rule to split the factor that depends only on $t$ from the factor that depends only on $\tau$.

$$
y(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{j\omega(t-\tau)}h(\tau)\,d\tau
$$

Apply the difference rule for exponents: $e^{a-b}=e^ae^{-b}$. Here that gives $e^{j\omega(t-\tau)}=e^{j\omega t}e^{-j\omega\tau}$.

$$
y(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{j\omega t}e^{-j\omega\tau}h(\tau)\,d\tau
$$

The result is the same integral with the $t$ dependence now isolated as $e^{j\omega t}$. Do not factor it out yet; this step only makes the separation visible.

## Factoring $e^{j\omega t}$ Outside the Integral

**Example:** Take the separated form of the integrand and pull $e^{j\omega t}$ outside the integral.

**Explanation**

The cue is that $e^{j\omega t}$ does not depend on $\tau$. When a factor is independent of the integration variable, move it outside and leave the $\tau$-integral unchanged.

$$
y(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{j\omega t}e^{-j\omega\tau}h(\tau)\,d\tau
$$

Now factor out the $t$-only term and keep the remaining $\tau$-dependent part inside the integral.

$$
y(t)=e^{j\omega t}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-j\omega\tau}h(\tau)\,d\tau
$$

This is the factored form the later step will interpret. The output now has a pure $t$ factor multiplying a single constant integral.

## Identifying the Remaining Integral as the Complex Gain at $\omega$

**Example:** Recognize the remaining $\tau$-integral as the system's complex gain at $\omega$ and rewrite the output in gain-times-input form.

**Explanation**

The cue is the remaining $\tau$-integral after the factorization step. Name that integral $H(j\omega)$; it is the system's complex gain at the probe frequency.

$$
y(t)=e^{j\omega t}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-j\omega\tau}h(\tau)\,d\tau
$$

Define the remaining integral as $H(j\omega)$ so the frequency-dependent system response has a clear name.

$$
H(j\omega)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-j\omega\tau}h(\tau)\,d\tau
$$

Substituting that definition gives the final gain-times-input form. The same complex exponential stays at frequency $\omega$, while the system contributes the complex multiplier $H(j\omega)$, which changes amplitude and phase.

$$
y(t)=H(j\omega)e^{j\omega t}
$$
