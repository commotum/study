## Introduction to Building Triangular Signals

A finite-duration triangle is easiest to read as three regions: a rising line, a peak boundary, and a falling line, with zero before the start and after the end. The job in this first tutorial step is to recognize that each nonzero region can be written as one linear term that is active only over its own interval.

Use step differences to gate each linear piece so it stays on only where it belongs. A rise term can be written as $r_1(t)(u(t-t_0)-u(t-t_p))$, and a fall term can be written as $r_2(t)(u(t-t_p)-u(t-t_1))$.

$$
x(t)=r_1(t)(u(t-t_0)-u(t-t_p))+r_2(t)(u(t-t_p)-u(t-t_1))
$$

For the base case, keep the unit-height, unit-width triangle in mind: it rises on $[0,1]$, peaks at $t=1$, and falls on $[1,2]$. That base triangle is the reference shape for later examples, even when the endpoints, slopes, or peak height change.

$$
x(t)=0\quad\text{for }t<t_0\text{ or }t>t_1
$$

So the first thing to do with any triangular signal is identify the start time, peak time, and end time, then match each interval to the correct linear segment. Once those windows are clear, the rest of the construction is just a matter of placing the rise and fall pieces into the right step gates.

## Building a Basic Triangle from Rising and Falling Segments

**Example:** Find a unit-step representation for the triangle $x(t)$ with $x(t)=0$ for $t<0$ and $t>2$, $x(t)=t$ on $0 \le t < 1$, and $x(t)=2-t$ on $1 \le t < 2$.

**Explanation**

The cue is the unit-height triangle from $t=0$ to $t=2$: it is zero outside that interval, rises linearly on $[0,1)$, and falls linearly on $[1,2)$. The goal is to write those two linear pieces in a step-based form that keeps each piece active only where it belongs.

Write the rise and fall as separate linear pieces, then gate them with adjacent step differences. The rise segment is $t$ on $[0,1)$, so it uses $u(t)-u(t-1)$, and the fall segment is $2-t$ on $[1,2)$, so it uses $u(t-1)-u(t-2)$.

$$
x(t)=t(u(t)-u(t-1))+(-t+2)(u(t-1)-u(t-2))
$$

This expression is continuous at $t=1$, reaches the peak value $1$ there, and is zero for $t<0$ or $t>2$. The two gated linear pieces match at the peak, so the triangle has the right shape without needing any extra terms.

## Building a Shifted Triangle from Start, Peak, and End Times

**Example:** Construct the step-form expression for $x(t)$ where $x(t)=\frac{1}{3}(t-2)$ on $[2,5)$, and $x(t)=-\frac{3}{2}t+\frac{15}{2}$ on $[5,8)$.

**Explanation**

The cue is the shifted triangle: it starts at $t=2$, rises to height $3$ at $t=5$, and falls back to zero at $t=8$. The same two-window pattern from the previous example still works; only the breakpoints and line formulas change.

On $[2,5)$, the rise line is $\frac{1}{3}(t-2)$, and on $[5,8)$, the fall line is $-\frac{3}{2}t+\frac{15}{2}$. Attach the rise line to $u(t-2)-u(t-5)$ and the fall line to $u(t-5)-u(t-8)$ so each piece is active only on its own interval.

$$
x(t)=\frac{1}{3}(t-2)(u(t-2)-u(t-5))+\left(-\frac{3}{2}t+\frac{15}{2}\right)(u(t-5)-u(t-8))
$$

This expression is continuous at $t=5$, gives the peak value $3$, and is zero for $t<2$ or $t>8$. The construction is the same as before: identify the start, peak, and end times, then place each shifted line inside the matching step window.

## Building a Nonunit-Height Triangular Signal

**Example:** Find a step-form model for $x(t)$ with $x(t)=2t$ on $0\le t<2$, $x(t)=8-2t$ on $2\le t<4$, and $x(t)=0$ otherwise.

**Explanation**

The cue is the nonunit-height triangle: it starts at $t=0$, rises to height $4$ at $t=2$, and falls back to zero at $t=4$. The same two-window pattern still works; only the amplitudes inside the linear pieces change.

On $[0,2)$, the rise line is $2t$, and on $[2,4)$, the fall line is $8-2t$. Attach the rise line to $u(t)-u(t-2)$ and the fall line to $u(t-2)-u(t-4)$ so each piece is active only on its own interval.

$$
x(t)=2t\bigl(u(t)-u(t-2)\bigr)+(8-2t)\bigl(u(t-2)-u(t-4)\bigr)
$$

This expression is continuous at $t=2$, gives the peak value $4$, and is zero for $t<0$ or $t>4$. The construction matches the previous triangles exactly: identify the two linear pieces, gate each one to its interval, and keep the peak value in the line formulas.

## Building an Asymmetric Triangular Signal with Unequal Slopes

**Example:** Find a step-function expression for $x(t)=2t$ on $0\le t<1$, $x(t)=\frac{8-2t}{3}$ on $1\le t<4$, and $x(t)=0$ otherwise.

**Explanation**

The cue is the asymmetric triangle: it starts at $t=0$, reaches height $2$ at $t=1$, and then falls more slowly to zero at $t=4$. The same two-window pattern still works; only the rise and fall slopes are different.

Write the rise and fall as separate line segments from the given interval data. Attach $2t$ to $u(t)-u(t-1)$ and attach $\frac{8-2t}{3}$ to $u(t-1)-u(t-4)$ so each line is active only on its own region.

$$
x(t)=2t\bigl(u(t)-u(t-1)\bigr)+\frac{8-2t}{3}\bigl(u(t-1)-u(t-4)\bigr)
$$

This expression is continuous at $t=1$, gives the peak value $2$, and is zero for $t<0$ or $t>4$. The construction is unchanged: keep the interval boundaries explicit, then place each slope inside the correct step window.
