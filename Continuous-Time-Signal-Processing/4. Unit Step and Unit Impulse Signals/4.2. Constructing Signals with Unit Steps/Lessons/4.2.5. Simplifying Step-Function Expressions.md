## Introduction to Simplifying Step-Function Expressions

Treat each shifted unit-step term as a gate that changes only when its argument crosses zero. In a sum of shifted steps, the switch times are the only time points where the expression can change value.

$$
f(t)=u(t-1)-u(t-2)
$$

To simplify, list every switch time, sort them, and split the time axis into intervals between those times. On each interval, decide which step terms are active and add their coefficients; that sum is the active value for the interval.

$$
\text{Step boundaries: }t=1,2\;\Rightarrow\;\text{active interval values are computed piecewise}
$$

For the base case, start with $x(t)=u(t)-u(t-1)+u(t-1)$. The boundaries are $t=0$ and $t=1$, so the interval values are $0$ for $t<0$, $1$ for $0\le t<1$, and $1$ for $t\ge 1$; that collapses to the compact form $u(t)$.

Use the same bookkeeping for expressions like $2u(t-1)-u(t-2)+3u(t-3)$: find the boundaries, combine the active coefficients interval by interval, and rewrite the result as a compact equivalent expression with no redundant overlap.

## Identifying Active Intervals in Shifted Unit Step Expressions

**Example:** Find the active intervals for $x(t)=3u(t-1)-u(t-3)+2u(t-5)$.

**Explanation**

Read each shifted unit-step term as a gate that turns on at its own shift time and stays on to the right. For this expression, the shift times are the only boundaries that matter.

$$
\text{Switch times: }t=1,3,5
$$

Sort those switch times and split the time axis into the four intervals $t<1$, $1\le t<3$, $3\le t<5$, and $t\ge 5$. On each interval, keep only the terms that are active there and add their coefficients.

$$
x(t)=\begin{cases}0, & t<1\\3, & 1\le t<3\\2, & 3\le t<5\\4, & t\ge 5\end{cases}
$$

So the active-interval map is $0$, then $3$, then $2$, then $4$. That is the output this step needs: the interval values written cleanly before any compact simplification.

The next step will reuse the same boundaries to combine these regions into a shorter equivalent step expression. For now, the key skill is identifying exactly which terms are active on each interval and summing them correctly.

## Combining Overlapping Constant Step Terms

**Example:** Simplify $x(t)=u(t-2)+2u(t-4)-3u(t-6)$ and write a compact equivalent expression.

**Explanation**

Each shifted unit-step term is constant on the intervals between its switch times, so the first step is to list those times and read the active value on each interval.

$$
\text{Switch times: }t=2,4,6
$$

Split the time axis into $t<2$, $2\le t<4$, $4\le t<6$, and $t\ge 6$. On each interval, add the coefficients of the terms that are active there.

$$
x(t)=\begin{cases}0, & t<2\\1, & 2\le t<4\\3, & 4\le t<6\\0, & t\ge 6\end{cases}
$$

The interval values are $0$, then $1$, then $3$, then $0$. Read those changes at the switch times as the step coefficients: a jump of $1$ at $t=2$, a further jump of $2$ at $t=4$, and a drop of $3$ at $t=6$.

$$
x(t)=u(t-2)+2u(t-4)-3u(t-6)
$$

So the compact step expression is the same coefficient pattern written directly in shifted-step form. The active-interval map and the step sum agree on every interval, which is the bookkeeping rule this example is modeling.

## Combining Overlapping Sloped Step Terms

**Example:** Simplify $x(t)=(t-2)u(t-2)-(t-4)u(t-4)$ and express it as a compact piecewise form.

**Explanation**

The cue is that each shifted unit step turns a linear factor on only after its own switch time. Work interval by interval, and evaluate the slope only where the term is active.

$$
t=2,4
$$

Use the breakpoints to split the line into $t<2$, $2 \le t < 4$, and $t \ge 4$. In each region, keep only the active terms and simplify the linear expression that remains.

$$
x(t)=\begin{cases}0, & t<2\\t-2, & 2 \le t < 4\\2, & t \ge 4\end{cases}
$$

$$
(t-2)-(t-4)=2
$$

The overlap cancels the slopes after $t=4$, so the piecewise form above is already the compact result. If you want the same answer in step notation, one equivalent form is $x(t)=(t-2)(u(t-2)-u(t-4))+2u(t-4)$.

## Rewriting Redundant Step Terms into Compact Expressions

**Example:** Rewrite $x(t)=u(t-2)-u(t-4)+u(t-4)-u(t-6)$ as a compact equivalent expression.

**Explanation**

The cue is the canceling pair in $x(t)=u(t-2)-u(t-4)+u(t-4)-u(t-6)$. Before removing it, split the signal at the breakpoints $t=2,4,6$ and evaluate each interval separately.

$$
x(t)=\begin{cases}0, & t<2\\1, & 2 \le t < 4\\1, & 4 \le t < 6\\0, & t \ge 6\end{cases}
$$

The middle two intervals match, so the boundary at $t=4$ is redundant. That means the pair $-u(t-4)+u(t-4)$ contributes nothing and can be removed.

$$
x(t)=u(t-2)-u(t-4)+u(t-4)-u(t-6)=u(t-2)-u(t-6)
$$

One test point from each remaining interval confirms the equivalence: before $2$ the value is $0$, between $2$ and $6$ it is $1$, and after $6$ it returns to $0$. The compact form keeps only the net-effective switch events.

## Checking Simplified Step Expressions by Time Interval

**Example:** Verify that $x(t)=2u(t-1)-u(t-3)-u(t-5)+u(t-4)$ is equivalent to $y(t)=2u(t-1)-u(t-3)+u(t-4)-u(t-5)$ by checking each active interval.

**Explanation**

The cue is the ordered list of switch times $t=1,3,4,5$. Test one representative from each interval so you check the interior of every segment and the behavior at each boundary.

$$
\begin{array}{c|ccccc}t & 0 & 2 & 3.5 & 4.5 & 6\\\hline x(t) & 0 & 2 & 1 & 2 & 1\\ y(t) & 0 & 2 & 1 & 2 & 1\end{array}
$$

Every sample matches, so there is no interval mismatch and no boundary error. The candidate has the same active-value pattern as the original on all canonical intervals.

$$
x(t)=y(t)
$$

That is the verification rule to reuse: simplify first, then test one point from each canonical interval before you release the result.
