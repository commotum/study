## Introduction to Time Reversal of Piecewise Signals

A piecewise signal is read as formulas attached to time intervals. To reverse time, replace every $t$ with $-t$ and then rewrite the breakpoint locations so the intervals run from left to right in the new time variable.

$$
x(t)=\begin{cases} t, & 0\le t<2 \\ 4-t, & 2\le t\le 4 \end{cases}
$$

Here the breakpoint at $t=2$ moves to $t=-2$. That means the right-hand piece in $x(t)$ becomes the left-hand piece in $x(-t)$, because the time axis has been reflected across $t=0$.

$$
x(-t)=\begin{cases} 4+t, & -4\le t\le -2 \\ -t, & -2<t\le 0 \end{cases}
$$

Read the result in increasing time order: first the interval on the left, then the next one. The rule to reuse is simple: reflect the breakpoints, substitute $-t$ into each piece, and reorder the intervals.

<a id="reflecting-breakpoints-in-a-piecewise-signal"></a>
## Reflecting Breakpoints in a Piecewise Signal

**Example:** Given a piecewise signal with breakpoints at $-3$, $-1$, $2$, and $4$, identify the reflected breakpoint locations and the new interval order for $x(-t)$.

**Explanation**

Given breakpoint values at $-3$, $-1$, $2$, and $4$, the cue for time reversal is to reflect each breakpoint across $t=0$ first. Do not rewrite any segment formulas yet; this step only changes the breakpoint locations.

$$
-3 \rightarrow 3,\quad -1 \rightarrow 1,\quad 2 \rightarrow -2,\quad 4 \rightarrow -4
$$

Now sort the reflected breakpoints from left to right on the time axis. The order is determined by the new positions after reflection, not by the original order of the values.

$$
-4,\ -2,\ 1,\ 3
$$

So the breakpoint list for $x(-t)$ is $-4$, $-2$, $1$, $3$. Stop here: this step ends with the reflected breakpoint locations and their left-to-right order only.

## Substituting $-t$ Into Piecewise Segment Formulas

**Example:** A two-piece signal has $2t+1$ on $[-2,1)$ and $4-t$ on $[1,3]$. Rewrite the formulas in $x(-t)$ and keep the reflected interval order from the previous step.

**Explanation**

The cue is the reflected interval order from the previous step. Keep that order fixed and substitute $-t$ into each segment formula; this step changes the algebra inside the pieces, not the breakpoint locations.

$$
x(t)=\begin{cases} 2t+1, & -2\le t<1 \\ 4-t, & 1\le t\le 3 \end{cases}
$$

Start with the left-hand formula $2t+1$ and the right-hand formula $4-t$. Replace every $t$ with $-t$ in each one and simplify the signs.

$$
2(-t)+1=-2t+1,\qquad 4-(-t)=4+t
$$

Attach the simplified formulas to the reflected intervals from left to right. The piece with $4+t$ belongs on the left, and the piece with $-2t+1$ belongs on the right.

$$
x(-t)=\begin{cases} 4+t, & -3\le t\le -1 \\ -2t+1, & -1<t\le 2 \end{cases}
$$

So the new work in this step is formula substitution and simplification. The interval order stays fixed from the previous step; the formulas change by replacing $t$ with $-t$.

<a id="reordering-reversed-piecewise-intervals"></a>
## Reordering Reversed Piecewise Intervals

**Example:** A three-piece signal uses $1$ on $[-4,-1)$, $t+2$ on $[-1,2)$, and $3-t$ on $[2,5]$. Rewrite $x(-t)$ in increasing time order.

**Explanation**

The cue is the reflected interval set from the previous steps. After time reversal, the pieces do not stay in the original order, so sort the new intervals from smallest to largest $t$ before writing the final piecewise definition.

$$
x(t)=\begin{cases} 1, & -4\le t<-1 \\ t+2, & -1\le t<2 \\ 3-t, & 2\le t\le 5 \end{cases}
$$

Reflect the intervals first. The original rightmost piece on $[2,5]$ becomes the leftmost piece on $[-5,-2]$, the middle piece on $[-1,2)$ becomes $(-2,1]$, and the original left piece on $[-4,-1)$ becomes $(1,4]$.

$$
[2,5]\to[-5,-2],\qquad [-1,2)\to(-2,1],\qquad [-4,-1)\to(1,4]
$$

Now write the pieces left to right in the new order: $[-5,-2]$, $(-2,1]$, $(1,4]$. The formulas stay attached to their reflected intervals.

$$
x(-t)=\begin{cases} 3+t, & -5\le t\le -2 \\ 2-t, & -2<t\le 1 \\ 1, & 1<t\le 4 \end{cases}
$$

The new skill in this step is ordering the reflected intervals before you write the piecewise definition. The formulas do not move on their own; they follow the interval that each reflected piece belongs to.

## Reversing a Multi-Interval Piecewise Signal

**Example:** A four-piece signal is $0$ on $[-5,-2)$, $t+1$ on $[-2,0)$, $2-t$ on $[0,3)$, and $4$ on $[3,6]$. Find the complete piecewise definition of $x(-t)$.

**Explanation**

The cue is the complete reversal procedure from the previous steps. First reflect the breakpoints, then substitute $-t$ into each formula, and finally sort the transformed pieces from left to right.

$$
x(t)=\begin{cases} 0, & -5\le t<-2 \\ t+1, & -2\le t<0 \\ 2-t, & 0\le t<3 \\ 4, & 3\le t\le 6 \end{cases}
$$

Reflect the breakpoints one piece at a time. The transformed intervals appear in reverse order: $[3,6]\to[-6,-3]$, $[0,3)\to(-3,0]$, $[-2,0)\to(0,2]$, and $[-5,-2)\to(2,5]$.

$$
[3,6]\to[-6,-3],\qquad [0,3)\to(-3,0],\qquad [-2,0)\to(0,2],\qquad [-5,-2)\to(2,5]
$$

Now substitute $-t$ into each formula and simplify. The constant pieces stay constant, while the affine pieces change by sign inside the variable term.

$$
0,\qquad -t+1=1-t,\qquad 2-(-t)=2+t,\qquad 4
$$

Write the pieces in increasing time order: $[-6,-3]$, $(-3,0]$, $(0,2]$, $(2,5]$. Attach the simplified formulas to those intervals in that same left-to-right sequence.

$$
x(-t)=\begin{cases} 4, & -6\le t\le -3 \\ 2+t, & -3<t\le 0 \\ 1-t, & 0<t\le 2 \\ 0, & 2<t\le 5 \end{cases}
$$

This example combines all three operations: reflect the breakpoints, substitute $-t$ into every segment, and then sort the transformed intervals. The answer is correct only when the formulas and intervals are matched together in the final left-to-right order.
