## Introduction to Reflecting a Signal Across $t=0$

Reflecting a signal across $t=0$ means reading the graph left to right on the other side of the vertical axis. In $x(-t)$, every feature keeps the same amplitude but moves from time $t_0$ to time $-t_0$.

![](<../Source/3.3.2. Reflecting a Signal Across $t=0$/Images/l002-s001-te-section-001.png>)

Start with one isolated feature at a positive time. If the feature sits at $t_0$, its mirror in $x(-t)$ sits at $-t_0$ with the same height, so the sign change belongs to time, not to amplitude.

$$
y(t)=x(-t)
$$

That same rule applies to every other time location on the graph. A feature already on $t=0$ does not move, because $0$ changes to $-0$, which is still $0$.

$$
t_0 \to -t_0
$$

So the first step is simple: find the mirror line at $t=0$, flip each time coordinate, and leave each amplitude unchanged. Later examples will reuse this same sign-flip rule with more features, but the base move is already complete here.

## Reflecting a Single Graph Feature Across $t=0$

**Example:** Given a graph of $x(t)$ with one isolated peak at $t=3$ and height $2$, reflect the graph across $t=0$ to obtain $x(-t)$.

**Explanation**

The cue is a single isolated peak at a positive time. To reflect across $t=0$, move that peak to the mirrored negative time and keep its height and shape unchanged.

![](<../Source/3.3.2. Reflecting a Signal Across $t=0$/Images/l002-s002-te-section-002.png>)

For this graph, the peak at $t=3$ with height $2$ moves to $t=-3$ with the same height. The only change is the sign of the time coordinate.

$$
3 \to -3
$$

The height stays $2$, and the shape stays the same, because reflection across $t=0$ changes time location but not amplitude.

$$
y(t)=x(-t)
$$

So the reflected graph is the same peak on the left side of $t=0$, now located at $t=-3$ with height $2$.

## Reflecting a One-Sided Signal Segment Across $t=0$

**Example:** Given a right-sided triangular segment on $0 \le t \le 4$ with a peak at $t=2$, reflect the entire segment across $t=0$.

**Explanation**

The cue is a connected segment on the positive-time side. To reflect it across $t=0$, move the whole segment to the negative-time side and keep the same interior shape.

![](<../Source/3.3.2. Reflecting a Signal Across $t=0$/Images/l002-s003-te-section-003.png>)

For this segment, the left endpoint at $t=0$ stays fixed, the right endpoint at $t=4$ moves to $t=-4$, and the peak at $t=2$ moves to $t=-2$. The segment is the same triangle, just read in reverse order.

$$
0 \le t \le 4 \to -4 \le t \le 0
$$

The heights do not change. Each interior point lands at the mirrored negative time, so the triangle keeps its shape while its support interval flips horizontally.

$$
2 \to -2
$$

So the reflected signal is the same triangular segment on $-4 \le t \le 0$, with the peak now at $t=-2$ and the same height as before.

<a id="reflecting-multiple-graph-features-across-t0"></a>
## Reflecting Multiple Graph Features Across $t=0$

**Example:** Given a graph with a pulse at $t=-2$, a dip at $t=1$, and a short flat segment from $t=3$ to $t=4$, reflect the whole graph across $t=0$.

**Explanation**

The cue is several separated features on both sides of $t=0$. Start by listing each feature and its time coordinate, then reflect the graph by negating every time coordinate while keeping every amplitude and shape the same.

![](<../Source/3.3.2. Reflecting a Signal Across $t=0$/Images/l002-s004-te-section-004.png>)

The pulse at $t=-2$ moves to $t=2$, the dip at $t=1$ moves to $t=-1$, and the flat segment from $t=3$ to $t=4$ moves to $t=-4$ to $t=-3$. The features switch sides, but none of their heights change.

$$
\begin{aligned}
-2 &\to 2 \\
1 &\to -1 \\
3 \le t \le 4 &\to -4 \le t \le -3
\end{aligned}
$$

After the reflection, rebuild the graph in left-to-right order on the new time locations. The flat segment now appears on the left, the dip sits at $t=-1$, and the pulse sits at $t=2$.

$$
y(t)=x(-t)
$$

This is the complete reflection: every visible feature moved to its mirrored time, and nothing was skipped or duplicated.

<a id="reflecting-features-that-lie-on-t0"></a>
## Reflecting Features That Lie on $t=0$

**Example:** Given a graph with a feature on $t=0$ and matching features at $t=\pm 2$, reflect the signal across $t=0$.

**Explanation**

The cue is a feature on $t=0$ together with a matching pair on either side of the axis. The rule is that $t=0$ is a fixed point, so the on-axis feature stays in place while every off-axis feature moves to the opposite side with the same amplitude and shape.

![](<../Source/3.3.2. Reflecting a Signal Across $t=0$/Images/l002-s005-te-section-005.png>)

The feature at $t=0$ does not move. The feature at $t=-2$ moves to $t=2$, and the feature at $t=2$ moves to $t=-2$. Because the off-axis pair matches, the reflected graph keeps the same overall appearance off the axis even though the time coordinates swap.

$$
\begin{aligned}
0 &\to 0 \\
-2 &\to 2 \\
2 &\to -2
\end{aligned}
$$

Rebuild the graph by leaving the axis feature where it is and placing each off-axis feature on the mirrored side. Nothing on $t=0$ changes position, and nothing off the axis changes amplitude.

$$
y(t)=x(-t)
$$

So the special case is simple: a feature on $t=0$ stays fixed, and the usual reflection rule still applies to every other feature.
