## Introduction to Combined Shift and Scale Operations

Start with one labeled time point in $x(t)$ and a positive scale factor. For $y(t)=x(at+b)$, read the transformed signal by asking which original time point $t_0$ satisfies $t_0=at+b$.

$$
y(t)=x(at+b)
$$

Solve that relation for $t$ to locate the same feature in the transformed signal: $t=(t_0-b)/a$. This inverse map is the rule you will reuse for every labeled time point.

$$
t=(t_0-b)/a
$$

Keep the roles separate: $a$ controls compression, expansion, and possible reversal, while $b$ controls the shift after scaling is accounted for. If $a<0$, the same inverse map flips the order of the time points.

$$
t_0=at+b
$$

Apply the same inverse map to every endpoint or labeled feature, and you get the transformed support interval or any other multi-point description without changing notation. The symbolic rule stays the same point by point.

## Mapping Key Time Points for $x(at+b)$

**Example:** Suppose $x(t)$ has labeled landmarks at $t_0=0$, $t_0=4$, and $t_0=8$, and let $y(t)=x(2t-4)$. Find the transformed times of those landmarks in $y(t)$.

**Explanation**

The cue is the inside argument of $x$ in $y(t)=x(2t-4)$. Read each landmark by setting the original time $t_0$ equal to that argument, so $t_0=2t-4$.

$$
t_0=2t-4
$$

Solve that relation for $t$ to get the inverse map $t=(t_0+4)/2$. This tells you where any labeled time point in $x(t)$ appears in $y(t)$.

$$
t=(t_0+4)/2
$$

Now substitute the labeled times from $x(t)$: $t_0=0$ gives $t=2$, $t_0=4$ gives $t=4$, and $t_0=8$ gives $t=6$.

$$
t_0=0 \Rightarrow t=2,\quad t_0=4 \Rightarrow t=4,\quad t_0=8 \Rightarrow t=6
$$

Because $a=2>0$, the order stays the same in the transformed signal. The landmarks appear at $t=2$, $t=4$, and $t=6$ in that order.

## Finding the Transformed Support Interval

**Example:** Suppose $x(t)$ is nonzero on $[0,6]$ and let $y(t)=x(3t-6)$. Find the support of $y(t)$.

**Explanation**

The cue is the support interval of $x(t)$, which runs from $t_0=0$ to $t_0=6$. Use the same inverse map from the previous step and apply it to both endpoints of the interval.

$$
t=(t_0+6)/3
$$

Map the left endpoint first: when $t_0=0$, the transformed time is $t=2$. Then map the right endpoint: when $t_0=6$, the transformed time is $t=4$.

$$
t_0=0 \Rightarrow t=2,\quad t_0=6 \Rightarrow t=4
$$

Because $a=3>0$, the order of the endpoints stays the same, so the transformed signal is nonzero on $[2,4]$. The support is obtained by transforming both endpoints and writing the result in increasing order.

## Interpreting Combined Compression and Shift

**Example:** Suppose $x(t)$ is nonzero on $[0,8]$ and has landmarks at $t_0=0$, $t_0=4$, and $t_0=8$. For $y(t)=x(4t-8)$, find the transformed support and describe how the spacing between the landmarks changes.

**Explanation**

The cue is the interval support together with the landmark spacing in $x(t)$. Read $y(t)=x(4t-8)$ by setting the original time equal to the inside argument, so $t_0=4t-8$.

$$
t_0=4t-8
$$

Solve for $t$ to get the inverse map $t=(t_0+8)/4$. This map tells you where each original landmark lands in the transformed signal.

$$
t=(t_0+8)/4
$$

Now map the endpoints and the middle landmark: $t_0=0$ goes to $t=2$, $t_0=4$ goes to $t=3$, and $t_0=8$ goes to $t=4$.

$$
t_0=0 \Rightarrow t=2,\quad t_0=4 \Rightarrow t=3,\quad t_0=8 \Rightarrow t=4
$$

Because $a=4>0$, the order stays the same, but every spacing is divided by $4$. The support becomes $[2,4]$, and the transformed signal is compressed in time while the whole interval shifts right.

## Interpreting Combined Expansion and Shift

**Example:** Suppose $x(t)$ is nonzero on $[0,4]$ and let $y(t)=x(0.5t-1)$. Find the support of $y(t)$ and describe the change in width.

**Explanation**

The cue is the support interval of $x(t)$, which runs from $t_0=0$ to $t_0=4$. Use the same inverse map as before and apply it to both endpoints of the interval.

$$
t=2(t_0+1)
$$

Map the left endpoint first: when $t_0=0$, the transformed time is $t=2$. Then map the right endpoint: when $t_0=4$, the transformed time is $t=10$.

$$
t_0=0 \Rightarrow t=2,\quad t_0=4 \Rightarrow t=10
$$

Because $a=0.5<1$, the order of the endpoints stays the same, but the width doubles from $4$ to $8$. The transformed signal is nonzero on $[2,10]$, and the whole interval shifts right while expanding in time.

## Handling Negative Scale Factors in $x(at+b)$

**Example:** Suppose $x(t)$ has labeled landmarks at $t_0=2$, $t_0=4$, and $t_0=6$, and let $y(t)=x(-2t+6)$. Find where those landmarks appear in $y(t)$ and state the order they appear in.

**Explanation**

The cue is the negative scale factor in $y(t)=x(-2t+6)$ and the labeled times $t_0=2$, $t_0=4$, and $t_0=6$. Use the inverse map $t=(t_0-b)/a$ with $a=-2$ and $b=6$, so the transformed time is $t=(6-t_0)/2$.

$$
t=\frac{6-t_0}{2}
$$

Now apply that rule to each landmark. The point at $t_0=2$ moves to $t=2$, the point at $t_0=4$ moves to $t=1$, and the point at $t_0=6$ moves to $t=0$. The mapped times descend as the original times increase, which is the reversal check.

$$
t_0=2 \Rightarrow t=2,\quad t_0=4 \Rightarrow t=1,\quad t_0=6 \Rightarrow t=0
$$

If $t_0=2$ and $t_0=6$ are the support endpoints, then the transformed support is written in increasing order as $[0,2]$. The interior landmark still lands at $t=1$, but the left-right order is flipped by the negative scale factor.
