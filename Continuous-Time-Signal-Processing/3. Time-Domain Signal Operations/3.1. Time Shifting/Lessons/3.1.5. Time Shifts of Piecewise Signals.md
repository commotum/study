## Introduction to Time Shifts of Piecewise Signals

When a signal is written piecewise, the key thing to recognize is the breakpoint structure. A time shift does not change the formulas themselves; it moves each breakpoint in the shift direction and keeps each formula attached to the corresponding interval.

Use the simplest base case first: a two-piece signal with one breakpoint. Write the original signal so the interval structure is visible before any shift is applied.

$$
x(t)=\begin{cases}A,&a\le t<b\\B,&b\le t<c\end{cases}
$$

For a delay by $T$, the shifted signal is $y(t)=x(t-T)$. That means every breakpoint moves right by $T$, and the same formulas stay attached to the shifted intervals in the same order.

$$
y(t)=x(t-T)=\begin{cases}A,&a+T\le t<b+T\\B,&b+T\le t<c+T\end{cases}
$$

An advance uses the same rule with the breakpoints moved left instead of right. So the real job in every piecewise shift is to move each breakpoint, preserve their order, and rebuild the definition one interval at a time.

## Delaying a Piecewise Constant Signal

**Example:** Delay the piecewise constant signal $x(t)=\begin{cases}2,&t<1\\5,&1\le t\end{cases}$ by $T>0$ and write $y(t)=x(t-T)$ as a piecewise definition.

**Explanation**

A delay by $T>0$ means the input time becomes $t-T$. For a piecewise constant signal, the constants do not change; only the breakpoint moves right by $T$.

$$
x(t)=\begin{cases}2,&t<1\\5,&1\le t\end{cases}
$$

Here the original breakpoint is at $t=1$, so after the delay it moves to $t=1+T$. The left constant stays on the left of the new breakpoint, and the right constant stays on the right.

$$
y(t)=x(t-T)=\begin{cases}2,&t<1+T\\5,&1+T\le t\end{cases}
$$

So the delayed signal is the same two constants with a shifted breakpoint. That is the pattern to reuse: move the breakpoint, keep the piece values attached to their original sides, and rewrite the intervals in the new order.

## Advancing a Piecewise Constant Signal

**Example:** Advance the piecewise constant signal $x(t)=\begin{cases}2,&t<1\\5,&1\le t\end{cases}$ by $T>0$ and write $y(t)=x(t+T)$ as a piecewise definition.

**Explanation**

An advance by $T>0$ means the input time becomes $t+T$. For a piecewise constant signal, the constants do not change; only the breakpoint moves left by $T$.

$$
x(t)=\begin{cases}2,&t<1\\5,&1\le t\end{cases}
$$

Here the original breakpoint is at $t=1$, so after the advance it moves to $t=1-T$. The left constant stays on the left of the new breakpoint, and the right constant stays on the right.

$$
y(t)=x(t+T)=\begin{cases}2,&t<1-T\\5,&1-T\le t\end{cases}
$$

So the advanced signal is the same two constants with a shifted breakpoint. That is the pattern to reuse: move the breakpoint left, keep the piece values attached to their original sides, and rewrite the intervals in the new order.

## Shifting Piecewise Formulas That Depend on t

**Example:** Delay the piecewise signal $x(t)=\begin{cases}t+2,&t<1\\3-t,&1\le t\end{cases}$ by $T>0$ and write the shifted signal as a piecewise definition.

**Explanation**

The cue is that the pieces are formulas in $t$. For this kind of delay, move the breakpoint right by $T$, but do not rewrite the formulas by substituting $t-T$ into them; keep each formula exactly as written on its new interval.

$$
x(t)=\begin{cases}t+2,&t<1\\3-t,&1\le t\end{cases}
$$

Here the breakpoint at $t=1$ moves to $t=1+T$. The formula $t+2$ stays with the left piece, and the formula $3-t$ stays with the right piece.

$$
y(t)=\begin{cases}t+2,&t<1+T\\3-t,&1+T\le t\end{cases}
$$

So the delayed signal is obtained by translating the interval bounds and reattaching the same formulas. The only change is where each formula lives.

## Shifting a Piecewise Signal with Multiple Breakpoints

**Example:** Advance the piecewise signal $x(t)=\begin{cases}1,&t<2\\4,&2\le t<5\\0,&5\le t\end{cases}$ by $T>0$ and write the shifted signal as a piecewise definition.

**Explanation**

The cue is that there are two breakpoints, at $t=2$ and $t=5$. Because this is an advance by $T>0$, move both breakpoints left by $T$ and keep each original constant value attached to its interval.

$$
\begin{aligned}
2 &\mapsto 2-T \\
5 &\mapsto 5-T
\end{aligned}
$$

Now rebuild the intervals from left to right. The first piece stays first, the middle piece stays between the shifted breakpoints, and the last piece stays last.

$$
y(t)=\begin{cases}1,&t<2-T\\4,&2-T\le t<5-T\\0,&5-T\le t\end{cases}
$$

That final form uses each original piece exactly once, with both breakpoints translated in the same direction by the same amount.

<a id="writing-a-complete-shifted-piecewise-definition"></a>
## Writing a Complete Shifted Piecewise Definition

**Example:** Delay the complete piecewise signal $x(t)=\begin{cases}t+1,&t<0\\3,&0\le t<2\\5-t,&2\le t<4\\1,&4\le t\end{cases}$ by $T>0$ and write the final shifted piecewise definition with all intervals and formulas in order.

**Explanation**

The cue is that this is a complete piecewise definition: it has several breakpoints and a mix of constant and $t$-dependent pieces. For a delay by $T>0$, move every breakpoint right by $T$ and keep each formula attached to the same piece.

$$
x(t)=\begin{cases}t+1,&t<0\\3,&0\le t<2\\5-t,&2\le t<4\\1,&4\le t\end{cases}
$$

The breakpoints at $0$, $2$, and $4$ move to $T$, $2+T$, and $4+T$. Rebuild the intervals in order, then reattach the same formulas to the corresponding shifted pieces.

$$
\begin{aligned}
0 &\mapsto T \\
2 &\mapsto 2+T \\
4 &\mapsto 4+T
\end{aligned}
$$

That gives the complete shifted definition with no missing or duplicated pieces.

$$
y(t)=\begin{cases}t+1,&t<T\\3,&T\le t<2+T\\5-t,&2+T\le t<4+T\\1,&4+T\le t\end{cases}
$$
