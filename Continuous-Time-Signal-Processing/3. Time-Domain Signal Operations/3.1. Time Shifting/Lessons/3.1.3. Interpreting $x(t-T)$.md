## Introduction to Interpreting $x(t-T)$

When $T>0$, reading $x(t-T)$ means the signal is delayed by $T$ time units. The minus sign inside the argument tells you to move the waveform to the right, not to the left.

$$
y(t)=x(t-T)
$$

To see the rule on one feature, suppose a particular feature of $x(t)$ occurs at time $t_0$. In the shifted signal, that same feature appears later, at $t_0+T$.

$$
t_0\mapsto t_0+T
$$

Nothing about the shape or values changes. The only change is time: each original feature time moves right by $T$, so $x(t-T)$ is a $T$-unit delay.

## Recognizing $x(t-T)$ as a Delay

**Example:** Given $y(t)=x(t-4)$, decide whether the signal is delayed or advanced and state the amount of shift.

**Explanation**

For $x(t-T)$ with $T>0$, the cue is the minus sign inside the argument. That sign means delay: the signal keeps the same shape, but every feature moves right by $T$.

$$
y(t)=x(t-4)
$$

In this example, the shift amount is $T=4$. So the rule says each original feature time $t_0$ moves to $t_0+4$.

$$
t_0\mapsto t_0+4
$$

So $y(t)=x(t-4)$ is a $4$-unit delay. The waveform is unchanged except for the rightward shift.

## Mapping One Feature Time to $t_0+T$

**Example:** A signal $x(t)$ has a spike at $t_0=2$. Where does that spike appear in $y(t)=x(t-5)$?

**Explanation**

The spike at $t_0=2$ is the feature to track, and the cue is still the delay rule for $x(t-T)$ with $T>0$. Subtracting a positive amount inside the argument shifts that feature right by $T$ without changing its value.

$$
y(t)=x(t-5)
$$

Here $T=5$, so the feature time moves from $t_0=2$ to $t_0+T=2+5=7$. The waveform value at the spike stays the same.

$$
t_0+T=2+5=7
$$

So the spike appears at $t=7$, and $y(t)$ is a $5$-unit delay of $x(t)$.

## Mapping Multiple Feature Times in $x(t-T)$

**Example:** Original feature times are at $t=1$, $t=3$, and $t=7$. Locate them in $y(t)=x(t-2)$.

**Explanation**

The cue is the same delay rule: with $x(t-T)$ and $T>0$, add $T$ to every original feature time. Because the same shift is applied to each feature, the spacing between features stays the same.

$$
t_i\mapsto t_i+T
$$

Here the original feature times are $1$, $3$, and $7$, and the shift is $T=2$. So each time becomes $1+2$, $3+2$, and $7+2$.

$$
\{1,3,7\}\mapsto\{3,5,9\}
$$

So the features appear at $3$, $5$, and $9$, and the order and spacing are unchanged. $y(t)=x(t-2)$ is a $2$-unit delay of $x(t)$.

## Mapping Support Intervals in $x(t-T)$

**Example:** If $x(t)$ is nonzero on $0 \le t < 2$, what is the support interval of $y(t)=x(t-3)$?

**Explanation**

The cue is the support interval of $x(t)$, and the rule is the same delay rule used for feature times: when $x(t-T)$ has $T>0$, add $T$ to each endpoint and keep each endpoint's open or closed status.

$$
[0,2)\mapsto[3,5)
$$

Here the original support interval is $0 \le t < 2$ and the shift amount is $T=3$. So the left endpoint becomes $0+3=3$ and the right endpoint becomes $2+3=5$.

$$
0\le t<2\mapsto 3\le t<5
$$

So the support interval of $y(t)=x(t-3)$ is $3 \le t < 5$. The closed-open structure stays the same, and the interval is shifted right by $3$ units.
