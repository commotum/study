## Introduction to How Time Scaling Moves Key Time Points

When you see $x(at)$, the feature you are tracking stays the same feature, but its time coordinate changes. The cue is the scale factor inside the time argument, and the amplitude stays unchanged.

$$
x(at)
$$

To find the new location, solve $at=t_0$ for $t$. That gives $t=t_0/a$, so the time point at $t_0$ in $x(t)$ appears at $t_0/a$ in $x(at)$.

$$
at=t_0 \Rightarrow t=\frac{t_0}{a}
$$

$$
t_0 \mapsto \frac{t_0}{a}
$$

Base case: a peak at $t_0=6$ in $x(t)$ moves to $t=3$ in $x(2t)$. The peak is still a peak, and its amplitude does not change; only the horizontal placement changes.

## Mapping One Key Time Point in $x(at)$

**Example:** In $x(t)$, a peak occurs at $t_0=6$. Where does that peak appear in $x(2t)$?

**Explanation**

The cue is the scale factor $2$ inside $x(2t)$. Use the same rule from the tutorial: keep the peak, solve $at=t_0$, and find the new time coordinate.

$$
\begin{aligned}2t&=6\\t&=3\end{aligned}
$$

So the peak appears at $t=3$ in $x(2t)$. The peak itself does not change, and its amplitude stays the same; only the horizontal position moves.

## Mapping Start and Stop Times

**Example:** In $x(t)$, the signal is nonzero on $2 \le t \le 10$. Where is it nonzero in $x(2t)$?

**Explanation**

The cue is the nonzero support interval. Use the same rule from the tutorial: map each endpoint with $2t=t_0$ and then rewrite the new interval in order.

$$
\begin{aligned}2t&=2 &\Rightarrow&\ t=1\\2t&=10 &\Rightarrow&\ t=5\end{aligned}
$$

So the signal is nonzero on $1 \le t \le 5$ in $x(2t)$. The start and stop times move independently, and because the scale factor is positive, the interval keeps the same order.

## Mapping Corners and Peaks

**Example:** In $x(t)$, a piecewise waveform has a corner at $t=1$, a peak at $t=4$, and another corner at $t=7$. Where do these three features appear in $x(3t)$?

**Explanation**

The cue is a waveform with several labeled features. Use the same rule on each one: solve $3t=t_0$, keep the label, and map every original time to $t_0/3$.

$$
\begin{aligned}t_0=1&\Rightarrow t=\frac{1}{3}\\t_0=4&\Rightarrow t=\frac{4}{3}\\t_0=7&\Rightarrow t=\frac{7}{3}\end{aligned}
$$

So the corner at $t=1$ moves to $t=\frac{1}{3}$, the peak at $t=4$ moves to $t=\frac{4}{3}$, and the corner at $t=7$ moves to $t=\frac{7}{3}$. Listed left to right, the features stay in the same order because all three times are divided by the same positive factor.

## Mapping Zero Crossings

**Example:** In $x(t)$, the signal crosses zero at $t=-6$, $t=0$, and $t=9$. Where are the zero crossings in $x(3t)$?

**Explanation**

The cue is a set of zero-crossing times, including a negative time and the origin. Use the same rule on each one: solve $3t=t_0$ and map every crossing time to $t_0/3$.

$$
\begin{aligned}t_0=-6&\Rightarrow t=-2\\t_0=0&\Rightarrow t=0\\t_0=9&\Rightarrow t=3\end{aligned}
$$

So the zero crossings in $x(3t)$ are at $t=-2$, $t=0$, and $t=3$. The origin stays fixed, and the order is preserved because every time is divided by the same positive factor.

## Rebuilding a Scaled Time Axis from Key Points

**Example:** A signal in $x(t)$ has key points at $t=-4$ (zero crossing), $t=-1$ (corner), $t=2$ (peak), and $t=5$ (stop time). Rebuild the key-point axis for $x(2t)$.

**Explanation**

The cue is a mixed list of labeled key points. Use the same rule on each one: solve $2t=t_0$, keep the label attached, and then rebuild the axis from left to right.

$$
\begin{aligned}-4&\mapsto -2 && \text{zero crossing}\\-1&\mapsto -\frac12 && \text{corner}\\2&\mapsto 1 && \text{peak}\\5&\mapsto \frac52 && \text{stop time}\end{aligned}
$$

$$
-2\ (\text{zero crossing}) < -\frac12\ (\text{corner}) < 1\ (\text{peak}) < \frac52\ (\text{stop time})
$$

So the scaled time axis for $x(2t)$ is zero crossing at $t=-2$, corner at $t=-\frac12$, peak at $t=1$, and stop time at $t=\frac52$. The labels stay attached, and the left-to-right order is preserved.
