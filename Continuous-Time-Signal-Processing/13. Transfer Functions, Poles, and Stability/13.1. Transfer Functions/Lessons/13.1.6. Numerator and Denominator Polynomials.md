## Introduction to Numerator and Denominator Polynomials

A rational transfer function is written in the form $H(s)=\frac{N(s)}{D(s)}$. The numerator polynomial is $N(s)$, and the denominator polynomial is $D(s)$.

$$
H(s)=\frac{N(s)}{D(s)}
$$

$H(s)$ is the transfer function itself, while $N(s)$ and $D(s)$ are ordinary polynomials in $s$. When you separate the fraction, keep each coefficient, degree, and sign with the polynomial it belongs to.

Use the smallest nontrivial case $H(s)=\frac{s+2}{s+1}$ to see the split clearly: $N(s)=s+2$ and $D(s)=s+1$. The notation stays symbolic, so the job is to read each polynomial exactly as it appears in the fraction.

$$
\text{zero(s)}:\ N(s)=0,\quad \text{pole(s)}:\ D(s)=0
$$

This split matters because later you will use $N(s)=0$ to find zeros and $D(s)=0$ to find poles. For now, the key move is just to separate the transfer function into its numerator and denominator polynomials without changing the algebra inside either one.

## Identifying Numerator and Denominator Polynomials in $H(s)$

**Example:** Identify $N(s)$ and $D(s)$ for $H(s)=\frac{3s^2+2s-1}{s^2+4s+5}$.

**Explanation**

The cue is the single fraction form. For a rational transfer function, the expression above the bar is the numerator polynomial $N(s)$, and the expression below the bar is the denominator polynomial $D(s)$.

$$
H(s)=\frac{3s^2+2s-1}{s^2+4s+5}
$$

Read the top and bottom exactly as they appear. The top polynomial is $N(s)=3s^2+2s-1$, and the bottom polynomial is $D(s)=s^2+4s+5$.

$$
N(s)=3s^2+2s-1,\qquad D(s)=s^2+4s+5
$$

Nothing moves between the two parts. The example only asks you to separate the transfer function into its numerator and denominator polynomials, keeping every coefficient, power, and sign with the part it already belongs to.

## Writing $H(s)$ as $N(s)/D(s)$

**Example:** Write $H(s)=\frac{(s-1)(s+2)}{(s+3)(s-4)}$ in polynomial-over-polynomial form and identify $N(s)$ and $D(s)$.

**Explanation**

The cue is the factored fraction form. To write $H(s)$ as $N(s)/D(s)$, expand the factors in the numerator and denominator separately, then place each polynomial on the correct side of the fraction bar.

$$
H(s)=\frac{(s-1)(s+2)}{(s+3)(s-4)}
$$

Expand the top first: $(s-1)(s+2)=s^2+s-2$. That is the numerator polynomial $N(s)$.

$$
N(s)=(s-1)(s+2)=s^2+s-2
$$

Now expand the bottom: $(s+3)(s-4)=s^2-s-12$. That is the denominator polynomial $D(s)$, and the terms stay in descending powers of $s$.

$$
D(s)=(s+3)(s-4)=s^2-s-12
$$

Putting the two results together gives $H(s)=\frac{N(s)}{D(s)}=\frac{s^2+s-2}{s^2-s-12}$. Reversing the expansion returns the original factored form, so the rewrite is algebraically equivalent.

## Finding Roots of the Numerator Polynomial

**Example:** Find all zeros of $H(s)=\frac{s^2-4}{s^2+4s+3}$ by solving $N(s)=0$.

**Explanation**

The cue is the numerator polynomial $N(s)$. To find zeros of $H(s)$, set the numerator equal to zero and solve that equation first.

$$
N(s)=s^2-4
$$

Factor the numerator and use the zero-product rule. The polynomial becomes $(s-2)(s+2)=0$, so each factor gives one root.

$$
s^2-4=(s-2)(s+2)=0
$$

The solutions are $s=2$ and $s=-2$. These numerator roots are the zeros of $H(s)$, so the example result is $\{2,-2\}$.

## Finding Roots of the Denominator Polynomial

**Example:** Find all poles of $H(s)=\frac{s+1}{s^2+4s+3}$ and classify their denominator origin.

**Explanation**

The cue is the denominator polynomial $D(s)$. To find poles of $H(s)$, set the denominator equal to zero and solve that equation.

$$
D(s)=s^2+4s+3
$$

Factor the denominator and use the zero-product rule. The polynomial becomes $(s+1)(s+3)=0$, so each factor gives one root.

$$
s^2+4s+3=(s+1)(s+3)=0
$$

The solutions are $s=-1$ and $s=-3$. These denominator roots are the poles of $H(s)$, so the example result is $\{-1,-3\}$.

## Labeling Numerator Roots as Zeros and Denominator Roots as Poles

**Example:** For $H(s)=\frac{(s-2)(s+1)}{s(s+3)}$, list zeros and poles using the numerator/denominator split.

**Explanation**

The cue is the factorized transfer function. Read the numerator factors as $N(s)$ and the denominator factors as $D(s)$, then use the rule: numerator roots are zeros and denominator roots are poles.

$$
H(s)=\frac{(s-2)(s+1)}{s(s+3)}=\frac{N(s)}{D(s)}
$$

Set $N(s)=0$ to find the zeros. The numerator factors give $s-2=0$ and $s+1=0$, so the numerator roots are $s=2$ and $s=-1$.

Set $D(s)=0$ to find the poles. The denominator factors give $s=0$ and $s+3=0$, so the denominator roots are $s=0$ and $s=-3$.

So the zeros are $\{2,-1\}$ and the poles are $\{0,-3\}$. The classification depends only on which polynomial produced the root, not on the sign of the root itself.
