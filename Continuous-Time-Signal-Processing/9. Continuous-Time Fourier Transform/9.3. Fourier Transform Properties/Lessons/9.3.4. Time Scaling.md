## Introduction to Time Scaling

If you already know a pair like $x(t) \leftrightarrow X(j\omega)$, time scaling tells you how to reuse it when the time variable is multiplied by a nonzero factor. The move is to keep the same spectrum shape, substitute $\omega/a$ for $\omega$, and carry the $1/|a|$ factor.

$$
x(t) \leftrightarrow X(j\omega)
$$

Start with $a=2$. This is the simplest compression case: $x(2t)$ runs twice as fast in time, so its spectrum widens on the frequency axis and drops to half height.

$$
x(at) \leftrightarrow \frac{1}{|a|}X\left(j\frac{\omega}{a}\right)
$$

So the concrete result is $x(2t) \leftrightarrow \frac12 X\left(j\frac{\omega}{2}\right)$. The reciprocal frequency substitution is the same rule in every case; only the scale factor changes.

Use the same rule on the expansion case $x(t/2)$. With $a=\frac12$, the spectrum becomes $2X(j2\omega)$, which confirms that stretching time compresses frequency while doubling the amplitude.

## Transforming $x(2t)$ from a Known Fourier Pair

**Example:** Given $x(t) \leftrightarrow X(j\omega)$, find the Fourier transform of $x(2t)$.

**Explanation**

The cue here is the scale factor $a=2$ multiplying time. Start from the known pair $x(t) \leftrightarrow X(j\omega)$ and treat $x(2t)$ as the same signal with time compressed by a factor of 2.

$$
x(t) \leftrightarrow X(j\omega)
$$

Apply the time-scaling rule $x(at) \leftrightarrow \frac{1}{|a|}X\left(j\frac{\omega}{a}\right)$ with $a=2$. The frequency variable becomes $\omega/2$, and the spectrum picks up the factor $1/2$.

$$
x(2t) \leftrightarrow \frac12 X\left(j\frac{\omega}{2}\right)
$$

So the transform of $x(2t)$ is $\frac12 X\left(j\frac{\omega}{2}\right)$. Compression in time widens the spectrum and lowers its height by one-half.

## Transforming $x(t/2)$ from a Known Fourier Pair

**Example:** Given $x(t) \leftrightarrow X(j\omega)$, find the Fourier transform of $x(t/2)$.

**Explanation**

The cue here is the expansion factor hidden in $x(t/2)$. Start from the known pair $x(t) \leftrightarrow X(j\omega)$ and treat $x(t/2)$ as the same signal with time stretched by a factor of 2.

$$
x(t) \leftrightarrow X(j\omega)
$$

Apply the time-scaling rule $x(at) \leftrightarrow \frac{1}{|a|}X\left(j\frac{\omega}{a}\right)$ with $a=\frac12$. The frequency variable becomes $\omega/(1/2)=2\omega$, and the spectrum picks up the factor $1/|1/2|=2$.

$$
x(t/2) \leftrightarrow 2X(j2\omega)
$$

So the transform of $x(t/2)$ is $2X(j2\omega)$. Stretching time compresses the spectrum on the frequency axis and doubles its height.

<a id="applying-the-1a-magnitude-factor"></a>
## Applying the 1/|a| Magnitude Factor

**Example:** Given $x(t) \leftrightarrow X(j\omega)$, find the Fourier transform of $3x(2t)$.

**Explanation**

The cue here is the outside scalar $3$ multiplying a time-scaled signal. Start from the known pair $x(t) \leftrightarrow X(j\omega)$, separate the amplitude factor from the time scaling, and handle $3x(2t)$ in two steps.

$$
x(t) \leftrightarrow X(j\omega)
$$

First apply the time-scaling rule to $x(2t)$ with $a=2$. That gives $\frac12 X\left(j\frac{\omega}{2}\right)$, and the scalar $3$ still sits outside the transform.

$$
3x(2t) \leftrightarrow \frac{3}{2}X\left(j\frac{\omega}{2}\right)
$$

So the final transform is $\frac{3}{2}X\left(j\frac{\omega}{2}\right)$. The scalar multiplies the spectrum after time scaling, so the coefficient and the reciprocal factor combine cleanly.

<a id="using-time-scaling-with-a-general-scale-factor"></a>
## Using Time Scaling with a General Scale Factor

**Example:** Given $x(t) \leftrightarrow X(j\omega)$ and a nonzero scale factor $a$, state the Fourier transform of $x(at)$.

**Explanation**

The cue is the general nonzero scale factor $a$. Start from the known pair and keep the scale symbol explicit so the result works for every nonzero value of $a$.

$$
x(t) \leftrightarrow X(j\omega)
$$

For $x(at)$, replace $\omega$ by $\omega/a$ in the transform argument and attach the magnitude factor $1/|a|$. The absolute value keeps the scaling correct whether $a$ is positive or negative.

$$
x(at) \leftrightarrow \frac{1}{|a|}X\left(j\frac{\omega}{a}\right)
$$

So the general time-scaling rule is $x(at) \leftrightarrow \frac{1}{|a|}X\left(j\frac{\omega}{a}\right)$. This one formula contains the earlier numeric cases and is the version to reuse in later work.
