## Introduction to Fourier Transform of a Convolution

When an LTI output is written as $y(t)=x(t)*h(t)$, the Fourier-transform label for that output is $Y(j\omega)$. Read $Y(j\omega)$ as the output spectrum of the same signal, not as a new signal.

$$
y(t)=x(t)*h(t)
$$

To move from the time domain to the frequency domain, apply the convolution theorem to that same output. The output spectrum is still labeled $Y(j\omega)$, and it matches the product form of the input and impulse-response spectra.

$$
\mathcal{F}\{y(t)\}=Y(j\omega)
$$

So the same LTI output has two matching views: the time-domain convolution $y(t)$ and the Fourier-domain spectrum $Y(j\omega)$. The spectrum keeps the output label and simply changes the representation.

$$
Y(j\omega)=X(j\omega)H(j\omega)
$$

In later sections, you will read the same mapping from equivalent convolution forms, including reversed factors and the expanded convolution integral. The rule stays the same: convolution in time becomes multiplication in frequency.

## Transforming $y(t)=x(t)*h(t) into the Output Spectrum$

**Example:** Given an LTI output $y(t)=x(t)*h(t)$, write the Fourier-domain expression for $Y(j\omega)$.

**Explanation**

The cue is the convolution form $y(t)=x(t)*h(t)$. Since the output is already written as an LTI convolution, the right move is to read it in the Fourier domain.

$$
y(t)=x(t)*h(t)
$$

Applying the convolution theorem keeps the same output label and turns the time-domain output into the product of the input and impulse-response spectra. The Fourier-domain output is $Y(j\omega)$.

$$
Y(j\omega)=X(j\omega)H(j\omega)
$$

So the transformed output is $Y(j\omega)$, and its frequency-domain form is exactly the product $X(j\omega)H(j\omega)$. That is the Fourier-domain representation of the same LTI output.

## Transforming $h(t)$*$x(t)$ into the Same Product Spectrum

**Example:** Given $y(t)=h(t)*x(t)$, write the Fourier transform of the output.

**Explanation**

The cue is the reversed convolution order $y(t)=h(t)*x(t)$. Because convolution is commutative, this is the same LTI output written with the factors swapped.

$$
y(t)=h(t)*x(t)
$$

Apply the same convolution theorem to the reordered output. The Fourier-domain output label stays $Y(j\omega)$, and the spectrum is still the product of the input and impulse-response spectra.

$$
Y(j\omega)=X(j\omega)H(j\omega)
$$

So swapping the order of the time-domain factors does not change the output spectrum. The same LTI output is still represented by the product $X(j\omega)H(j\omega)$ in the Fourier domain.

## Transforming a Convolution Integral into a Product Spectrum

**Example:** Given $y(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x(\tau)h(t-\tau)\,d\tau$, write the Fourier-domain expression for the output.

**Explanation**

The cue is the integral form of the output: it already matches the standard convolution pattern with $x(\tau)$ and $h(t-\tau)$.

$$
y(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x(\tau)h(t-\tau)\,d\tau
$$

Read that integral as $y(t)=x(t)*h(t)$, then apply the convolution theorem to move the same output into the Fourier domain.

$$
Y(j\omega)=X(j\omega)H(j\omega)
$$

The result is the product spectrum for the same LTI output, now represented by $Y(j\omega)$.

## Identifying $X(j\omega)$$H(j\omega)$ as the Fourier Transform of $x(t)$*$h(t)$

**Example:** Given $Y(j\omega)=X(j\omega)H(j\omega)$, identify the corresponding time-domain output $y(t)$.

**Explanation**

The cue is the product spectrum $X(j\omega)H(j\omega)$. Read that product as the Fourier-domain form of a convolution output, not as pointwise multiplication in time.

$$
Y(j\omega)=X(j\omega)H(j\omega)
$$

So the matching time-domain signal is the LTI output $y(t)=x(t)*h(t)$. This is the same output written in the time domain instead of the Fourier domain.

$$
y(t)=x(t)*h(t)
$$

The product spectrum and the convolution output are two views of the same system response.
