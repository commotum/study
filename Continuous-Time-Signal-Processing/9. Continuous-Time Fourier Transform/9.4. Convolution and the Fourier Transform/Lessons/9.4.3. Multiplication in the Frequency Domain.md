## Introduction to Multiplication in the Frequency Domain

For an LTI output written as $y(t)=x(t)*h(t)$, the Fourier-domain rule is $Y(j\omega)=X(j\omega)H(j\omega)$. Read it one frequency at a time: at each fixed $\omega$, multiply the two complex values.

$$
Y(j\omega)=X(j\omega)H(j\omega)
$$

That product is the frequency-domain form of the same output, not a new time-domain operation. The move in this lesson is to replace the convolution with pointwise multiplication of the spectra.

$$
X(j\omega)=|X(j\omega)|e^{j\angle X(j\omega)},\quad H(j\omega)=|H(j\omega)|e^{j\angle H(j\omega)}
$$

When each factor is written in polar form, the magnitude and phase of the product are visible immediately.

$$
Y(j\omega)=|X(j\omega)||H(j\omega)|e^{j(\angle X(j\omega)+\angle H(j\omega))}
$$

So the output spectrum is built frequency by frequency, and the next sections will use the same rule to read the product, its magnitude, its phase, and its meaning as the spectrum of $x(t)*h(t)$.

## Pointwise Multiplication of $X(j\omega)$ and $H(j\omega)$

**Example:** Given $X(j\omega)=2e^{j\pi/6}e^{-j\omega/2}$ and $H(j\omega)=3e^{-j\pi/3}e^{j\omega}$, compute $Y(j\omega)=X(j\omega)H(j\omega)$ and simplify the result.

**Explanation**

The cue is the pointwise product: keep the same $\omega$ and multiply the two spectra directly. Start by writing $Y(j\omega)=X(j\omega)H(j\omega)$.

$$
Y(j\omega)=X(j\omega)H(j\omega)=\left(2e^{j\pi/6}e^{-j\omega/2}\right)\left(3e^{-j\pi/3}e^{j\omega}\right)
$$

Multiply the magnitudes first: $2\cdot 3=6$. Then combine the exponential factors by adding the constant phase terms and the $\omega$-dependent terms.

$$
\begin{aligned}
Y(j\omega) &= 6e^{j(\pi/6-\pi/3)}e^{j(-\omega/2+\omega)}\\
&= 6e^{j(-\pi/6)}e^{j\omega/2}\\
&= 6e^{j(\omega/2-\pi/6)}
\end{aligned}
$$

So the simplified spectrum is $Y(j\omega)=6e^{j(\omega/2-\pi/6)}$. The magnitude is $6$, and the phase is $\omega/2-\pi/6$.

## Magnitude of a Frequency-Domain Product

**Example:** Using the same $X(j\omega)=2e^{j\pi/6}e^{-j\omega/2}$ and $H(j\omega)=3e^{-j\pi/3}e^{j\omega}$, find $|Y(j\omega)|$ for $Y(j\omega)=X(j\omega)H(j\omega)$.

**Explanation**

The cue is magnitude only, so use the product rule for magnitudes: $|Y(j\omega)|=|X(j\omega)H(j\omega)|=|X(j\omega)|\,|H(j\omega)|$.

$$
|Y(j\omega)|=\left|2e^{j\pi/6}e^{-j\omega/2}\right|\left|3e^{-j\pi/3}e^{j\omega}\right|
$$

Each complex exponential has unit magnitude, so the phase factors do not change the size of the product. Only the real coefficients remain.

$$
\begin{aligned}
|Y(j\omega)| &= 2\cdot 3 \\
&= 6
\end{aligned}
$$

So the magnitude of the frequency-domain product is constant: $|Y(j\omega)|=6$.

## Phase of a Frequency-Domain Product

**Example:** Using the same $X(j\omega)=2e^{j\pi/6}e^{-j\omega/2}$ and $H(j\omega)=3e^{-j\pi/3}e^{j\omega}$, find $\angle Y(j\omega)$ for $Y(j\omega)=X(j\omega)H(j\omega)$.

**Explanation**

The cue is phase, so read the argument of each factor and add them pointwise in $\omega$. The positive coefficients $2$ and $3$ contribute no phase.

$$
\angle X(j\omega)=\pi/6-\omega/2,\quad \angle H(j\omega)=-\pi/3+\omega
$$

Now add the phase terms from the two spectra. Keep the dependence on $\omega$ together and simplify the constant offset.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\angle Y(j\omega) &= \angle X(j\omega)+\angle H(j\omega) \\
&= \left(\pi/6-\omega/2\right)+\left(-\pi/3+\omega\right) \\
&= \omega/2-\pi/6
\end{aligned}
$$

So the phase of the frequency-domain product is $\angle Y(j\omega)=\omega/2-\pi/6$.

## Reading $X(j\omega)$$H(j\omega)$ as the Spectrum of $x(t)$*$h(t)$

**Example:** Given the product $Y(j\omega)=X(j\omega)H(j\omega)$, identify the corresponding time-domain output $y(t)=x(t)*h(t)$ and state what the product means in the Fourier domain.

**Explanation**

The cue is interpretation: the same pointwise product in frequency is the spectrum of the convolution output in time.

$$
\begin{aligned}
y(t) &= x(t)*h(t) \\
Y(j\omega) &= X(j\omega)H(j\omega)
\end{aligned}
$$

So $Y(j\omega)$ is not a time-domain product or a sum. It is the Fourier-domain representation of $y(t)=x(t)*h(t)$, and its magnitude and phase describe that output spectrum.

$$
Y(j\omega)=6e^{j(\omega/2-\pi/6)}
$$

That compact product is the spectrum of the convolution output, so the earlier magnitude and phase results are now read as features of $x(t)*h(t)$.
