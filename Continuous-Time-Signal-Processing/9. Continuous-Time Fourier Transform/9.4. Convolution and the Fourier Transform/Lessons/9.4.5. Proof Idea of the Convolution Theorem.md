## Introduction to Proof Idea of the Convolution Theorem

Start with one continuous-time LTI output, written as $y(t)=(x*h)(t)$. The target is to rewrite its Fourier transform until it becomes $Y(j\omega)=X(j\omega)H(j\omega)$.

$$
y(t)=(x*h)(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x(\tau)h(t-\tau)\,d\tau
$$

The other ingredient is the Fourier transform definition of the output spectrum, $Y(j\omega)$. These two formulas are the only pieces needed before the algebra begins.

$$
Y(j\omega)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}y(t)e^{-j\omega t}\,dt
$$

The proof path is symbolic: substitute the convolution integral into the transform definition, rewrite the result as a double integral, swap the order of integration, and then recognize the factors that depend only on $t$ or $\tau$.

$$
Y(j\omega)=X(j\omega)H(j\omega)
$$

Keep the focus on the proof idea rather than on formal convergence details. The next step will begin the algebraic rewrite immediately.

<a id="substituting-the-convolution-integral-into-the-fourier-transform"></a>
## Substituting the Convolution Integral into the Fourier Transform

**Example:** Start from $Y(j\omega)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}(x*h)(t)e^{-j\omega t}\,dt$ and substitute the convolution definition for $(x*h)(t)$.

**Explanation**

The cue is the convolution symbol $(x*h)(t)$ inside the Fourier transform. The first move is to replace that symbol with its integral definition and leave the Fourier kernel $e^{-j\omega t}$ untouched.

$$
Y(j\omega)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}(x*h)(t)e^{-j\omega t}\,dt
$$

Use the convolution definition $(x*h)(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x(\tau)h(t-\tau)\,d\tau$ and insert it directly into the transform integral.

$$
(x*h)(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x(\tau)h(t-\tau)\,d\tau
$$

After substitution, the transform becomes a nested integral with $t$ outside and $\tau$ inside. That is the whole move in this step: write the convolution inside the transform and stop before changing the order of integration.

$$
Y(j\omega)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x(\tau)h(t-\tau)\,d\tau\right)e^{-j\omega t}\,dt
$$

No variables are changed yet, and the order of integration stays the same. The result is the substituted Fourier transform in a form that is ready for the next rewrite.

## Rewriting the Transform as an Iterated Integral

**Example:** Rewrite the substituted Fourier transform as an iterated integral with respect to $\tau$ and $t$.

**Explanation**

The cue is the nested integral from the previous step. Keep the outer variable $t$ attached to the Fourier kernel $e^{-j\omega t}$ and rewrite the same expression so both integration variables are visible.

$$
Y(j\omega)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x(\tau)h(t-\tau)\,d\tau\right)e^{-j\omega t}\,dt
$$

Now flatten that structure into a true iterated integral. The inner integral is still over $\tau$, and the outer integral is still over $t$; only the writing changes, not the algebra.

$$
Y(j\omega)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x(\tau)h(t-\tau)e^{-j\omega t}\,d\tau\,dt
$$

That is the entire move in this step: make the double-integral structure explicit so the next step can justify changing the order of integration.

<a id="interchanging-the-order-of-integration"></a>
## Interchanging the Order of Integration

**Example:** Take the iterated integral for $Y(j\omega)$ and interchange the order of integration so the $t$-integral sits inside.

**Explanation**

The cue is the double integral from the previous step. Keep the same integrand, but rewrite the expression so the $\tau$-integral is outside and the $t$-integral is inside.

$$
Y(j\omega)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x(\tau)h(t-\tau)e^{-j\omega t}\,d\tau\,dt
$$

Move $x(\tau)$ outside the inner $t$-integral and keep $h(t-\tau)e^{-j\omega t}$ inside. That is the only new wrinkle here: the structure changes, but the integrand does not.

$$
Y(j\omega)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x(\tau)\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}h(t-\tau)e^{-j\omega t}\,dt\right)d\tau
$$

The result is the same Fourier transform written in a form that isolates the shifted impulse response. No transform pair is recognized yet; this step only positions the inner integral for the next move.

<a id="exposing-the-shifted-transform-factor"></a>
## Exposing the Shifted Transform Factor

**Example:** In the inner integral, make the substitution $u=t-\tau$ and expose the factor that carries the shift dependence.

**Explanation**

The cue is the inner integral inside the swapped-order expression. Let $u=t-\tau$ so the shift moves off of $h$ and onto a clean exponential factor.

$$
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}h(t-\tau)e^{-j\omega t}\,dt
$$

Replace $t$ by $u+\tau$ and $dt$ by $du$. Then $e^{-j\omega t}=e^{-j\omega(u+\tau)}=e^{-j\omega\tau}e^{-j\omega u}$, so the shift dependence separates.

$$
u=t-\tau
$$

$$
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}h(u)e^{-j\omega(u+\tau)}\,du = e^{-j\omega\tau}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}h(u)e^{-j\omega u}\,du = e^{-j\omega\tau}H(j\omega)
$$

The result is the shifted transform factor $e^{-j\omega\tau}H(j\omega)$, ready to be paired with the outer $x(\tau)$ integral in the next step.

## Recognizing the Product of the Two Individual Spectra

**Example:** Recognize the separated factors in the transformed convolution expression and conclude that $Y(j\omega)=X(j\omega)H(j\omega)$.

**Explanation**

The cue is the separated factor form from the previous step. The outer integral over $\tau$ matches the Fourier transform of $x(t)$, and the remaining factor is $H(j\omega)$.

$$
Y(j\omega)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x(\tau)e^{-j\omega\tau}\,d\tau\;H(j\omega)
$$

Name the outer integral $X(j\omega)$ and keep the transform factor $H(j\omega)$ as the multiplier that came out of the inner integral.

$$
Y(j\omega)=X(j\omega)H(j\omega)
$$

That finishes the proof idea: the convolution in time becomes multiplication in frequency, with each factor identified as the spectrum of one signal.
