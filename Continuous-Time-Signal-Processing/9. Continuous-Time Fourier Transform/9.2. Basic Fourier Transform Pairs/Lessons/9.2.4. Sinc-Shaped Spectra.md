## Introduction to Sinc-Shaped Spectra

A rectangular pulse turns into a sinc-shaped spectrum. The cue is the flat time-domain pulse: in frequency, look for a broad central lobe at zero frequency with alternating sidelobes on both sides.

![](<../Source/9.2.4. Sinc-Shaped Spectra/Images/l004-s001-te-section-001.png>)

For the base case, write the pulse as $x(t)=A\,\mathrm{rect}(t/T)$. If $A=1$, that is the unit-height pulse from the outline; $T$ sets the pulse duration.

$$
x(t)=A\,\mathrm{rect}(t/T)
$$

$$
X(\omega)=AT\,\mathrm{sinc}(\omega T/2)
$$

$$
\mathrm{sinc}(u)=\frac{\sin u}{u}
$$

Read the pair this way: changing $T$ changes horizontal width, and changing $A$ rescales the whole spectrum vertically without changing the lobe pattern.

## Identifying the Central Lobe and Sidelobes

**Example:** Given a plotted sinc spectrum of a rectangular pulse transform, identify the central lobe and the first sidelobes.

**Explanation**

The cue is the sinc spectrum from the rectangular pulse transform. Read the middle hump as the central lobe, and read the smaller lobes on either side as the sidelobes.

![](<../Source/9.2.4. Sinc-Shaped Spectra/Images/l004-s002-te-section-002.png>)

Start at zero frequency. The lobe that contains $\omega=0$ is the central lobe, even if the whole plot has been scaled vertically.

$$
X(\omega)=AT\,\mathrm{sinc}(\omega T/2)
$$

Then move outward from the center: the first lobes immediately to the left and right are the first sidelobes. Symmetry makes the left and right labels match.

## Relating Pulse Duration to Spectrum Width

**Example:** Given two rectangular pulses with different durations but the same amplitude, identify which transform has the narrower central lobe and which has the wider sidelobe spacing.

**Explanation**

The cue is a comparison of two centered rectangular pulses with the same amplitude. Hold amplitude fixed and use duration as the only variable: shorter pulse, wider spectrum; longer pulse, narrower spectrum.

![](<../Source/9.2.4. Sinc-Shaped Spectra/Images/l004-s003-te-section-003.png>)

Start with the rectangular-pulse transform rule. For $x(t)=A\,\mathrm{rect}(t/T)$, the spectrum is $X(\omega)=AT\,\mathrm{sinc}(\omega T/2)$. When $T$ gets smaller, the sinc stretches horizontally, so the central lobe gets wider and the sidelobe zeros move farther apart.

$$
X(\omega)=AT\,\mathrm{sinc}(\omega T/2)
$$

$$
\text{larger }T \Rightarrow \text{narrower spectrum}
$$

So the shorter pulse matches the wider central lobe and wider sidelobe spacing, and the longer pulse matches the narrower spectrum with tighter sidelobe spacing. The comparison is one-dimensional: duration controls horizontal width, not vertical scale.

## Relating Pulse Amplitude to Spectrum Scaling

**Example:** Given two rectangular pulses with the same duration but different amplitudes, identify how their sinc spectra differ.

**Explanation**

The cue is a comparison of two centered rectangular pulses with the same duration. Hold duration fixed and treat amplitude as the only change: taller pulse, taller spectrum; same width, same zero locations.

![](<../Source/9.2.4. Sinc-Shaped Spectra/Images/l004-s004-te-section-004.png>)

Use amplitude as a scale factor on the whole transform. If one pulse is $k$ times taller than the other, then every frequency value in its sinc spectrum is multiplied by $k$ as well.

$$
X_2(\omega)=kX_1(\omega)
$$

$$
X(\omega)=AT\,\mathrm{sinc}(\omega T/2)
$$

So the taller pulse gives the taller sinc spectrum, but the central-lobe width and sidelobe spacing do not change because the duration stayed fixed. The comparison is a vertical rescaling, not a shape change.

## Comparing Sinc Spectra from Different Rectangular Pulses

**Example:** Given several rectangular pulses with different widths and amplitudes, match each pulse to its sinc spectrum and justify the match by width and scaling.

**Explanation**

The cue is a matching task with several centered rectangular pulses. Use the two control knobs in order: duration sets the horizontal width, and amplitude sets the vertical scale.

![](<../Source/9.2.4. Sinc-Shaped Spectra/Images/l004-s005-te-section-005.png>)

First sort by duration. The wider pulse maps to the narrower sinc spectrum, and any two pulses with the same duration must have the same lobe spacing and zero locations.

$$
X(\omega)=AT\,\mathrm{sinc}(\omega T/2)
$$

$$
\text{larger }T \Rightarrow \text{narrower spectrum}
$$

$$
\text{larger }A \Rightarrow \text{larger vertical scale}
$$

Then use amplitude only after the width match is fixed. The correct pair preserves both the horizontal shape and the vertical size, so the final match agrees on width first and scale second.
