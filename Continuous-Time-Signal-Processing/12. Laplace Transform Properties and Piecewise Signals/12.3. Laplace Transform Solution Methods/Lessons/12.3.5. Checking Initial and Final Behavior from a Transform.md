## Introduction to Checking Initial and Final Behavior from a Transform

Different transforms can look similar, but behavior checks fail if the theorem conditions are not verified first. In this section, you will use one-sided limits as a diagnostic layer: the checks for $x(0^+)$ and $x(\infty)$ prevent false conclusions before any graphing or substitution-heavy arithmetic.

For the initial value, use the one-sided initial-value rule and a minimal algebra step. Start from the transform expression, form $sX(s)$, then take the $s\to\infty$ limit while keeping track of the highest-order terms.

$$
x(0^+) = \lim_{s\to\infty} sX(s)
$$

For the final value, first confirm the pole condition on $sX(s)$, then use the one-sided limit. If the poles of $sX(s)$ are not all in the left half-plane (except the required simple pole at $s=0$), do not use the formula, even if the arithmetic is easy.

$$
x(\infty)=\lim_{s\to 0} sX(s), \text{valid only when poles of } sX(s) \text{ are in } \Re\{s\}<0\text{ (except one at } s=0\text{)}.
$$

Use this checklist: identify which limit you need, check the theorem validity condition, evaluate the required transform-domain limit, and record the result. Next sections apply this same move to a concrete initial-value example, then a final-value example, then a rejection case.

## Checking the Initial Value from $X(s)$

**Example:** Find $x(0^+)$ for $X(s)=\frac{3s+4}{s(s+2)}$.

**Explanation**

The cue is to recover the initial time value directly from a one-sided transform, using the initial-value rule from the prior tutorial. For this section, the workflow is the same as the base move: multiply by $s$, then evaluate the $s\to\infty$ limit.

Apply the initial-value theorem in symbolic form and rewrite the expression before limiting:

$$
x(0^+)=\lim_{s\to\infty}sX(s)=\lim_{s\to\infty}\frac{3s+4}{s+2}
$$

Take the limit by keeping the highest-order terms: $(3s+4)/(s+2) \to 3s/s=3$ as $s\to\infty$.

$$
x(0^+)=3
$$

Result: $x(0^+)=3$. This limit is valid in this example because it is a causal one-sided transform, so the rule applies directly.

## Checking the Final Value When Pole Conditions Hold

**Example:** For $X(s)=\frac{4}{s(s+3)}$, verify pole conditions for $sX(s)$ and compute $x(\infty)$.

**Explanation**

The cue is a final-value request from a one-sided transform, so the workflow must start by checking validity before arithmetic. For this step, the governing rule is: form $sX(s)$, verify its poles satisfy the stability condition, then evaluate the final-value limit.

Apply the final-value relation and rewrite the expression to isolate the pole structure:

$$
x(\infty)=\lim_{s\to 0}sX(s),\quad X(s)=\frac{4}{s(s+3)} \Rightarrow sX(s)=\frac{4}{s+3}
$$

Now list poles of $sX(s)=\frac{4}{s+3}$: the only pole is at $s=-3$, which is strictly in the left half-plane, so the final-value theorem is applicable here.

$$
x(\infty)=\lim_{s\to 0}\frac{4}{s+3}=\frac{4}{3}
$$

Final result: the transform satisfies the pole check, so the one-sided final value is $x(\infty)=\frac{4}{3}$.

## Recognizing When the Final Value Theorem Does Not Apply

**Example:** Determine if final-value theorem applies to $X(s)=\frac{1}{s(s^2+4)}$ and explain the result before attempting a limit.

**Explanation**

The cue is a requested final-value check, so apply the same workflow as before but stop at the validity gate: form $sX(s)$, factor poles, and verify the allowed pole region before any substitution.

$$
sX(s)=\frac{1}{s^2+4}
$$

Now check poles of $\frac{1}{s^2+4}$. They are at $s=\pm 2j$, both on the imaginary axis, and the validity rule for the one-sided final-value theorem requires poles of $sX(s)$ to be in $\Re\{s\}<0$ except possibly one at $s=0$.

Since this condition is violated, do not apply $x(\infty)=\lim_{s\to0}sX(s)$. The theorem is not applicable here for final value in this form.

A safe next step is to obtain $x(t)$ by inverse Laplace or perform a separate long-term analysis of the reconstructed signal before claiming a steady-state value.

## Checking a Reconstructed Signal with Initial and Final Limits

**Example:** For $X(s)=\frac{2}{s}-\frac{1}{s+1}+\frac{3}{s+2}$, compute $x(0^+)$ and $x(\infty)$ and verify them against $x(t)=2- e^{-t}+3e^{-2t}$.

**Explanation**

The cue is a consistency check for a reconstructed signal, so this step combines both initial- and final-value rules on the same $X(s)$. First compute the limits in the transform domain, then compare them with the values from the provided $x(t)$ form.

$$
sX(s)=2-\frac{s}{s+1}+\frac{3s}{s+2}
$$

Compute the initial limit from the one-sided rule:

$$
x(0^+)=\lim_{s\to\infty}sX(s)=\lim_{s\to\infty}\left(2-\frac{s}{s+1}+\frac{3s}{s+2}\right)=4
$$

$$
sX(s)=\frac{2(s+1)(s+2)-s(s+2)+3s(s+1)}{s(s+1)(s+2)}=\frac{3s^2+6s+2}{s(s+1)(s+2)}
$$

For the final-value check, verify poles of $sX(s)$: the denominator gives poles at $s=0,-1,-2$. All are allowable for the one-sided final-value condition (LHP poles only, with at most one simple pole at $s=0$), so the theorem can be used.

$$
x(\infty)=\lim_{s\to0}sX(s)=\lim_{s\to0}\left(2-\frac{s}{s+1}+\frac{3s}{s+2}\right)=2
$$

Evaluate the reconstructed signal: $x(0^+)=2-1+3=4$ and $x(\infty)=2-0+0=2$. These match $x(0^+)=4$ and $x(\infty)=2$ from transform limits, so the reconstruction is behaviorally consistent.
