## Introduction to Direct and Time-Shifting Laplace Methods

One-sided Laplace starts from the same transform idea as the bilateral form but changes the energy window to the causal side only. For causal analysis, you use $\mathcal{L}_+\{x(t)\}$, where the variable $t$ is restricted to $t\ge 0$, so the lower integration bound is $0$ instead of $-\infty$.

$$
\mathcal{L}_+\{x(t)\}=\int_0^\infty x(t)e^{-st}dt
$$

For the base case, start from $x(t)=e^{-t}u(t)$. Write the transform directly as $\int_0^\infty e^{-t}e^{-st}dt$ and evaluate it exactly. If you can keep the lower limit and causal factor explicit, you can reduce mistakes on signs, convergence, and conditions.

Then use the delay template only when the signal has an explicit delayed-causal form. For $x(t-t_0)u(t-t_0)$, the one-sided rule is to factor out the delay as $e^{-st_0}$ and then transform the base causal kernel: $x(t)u(t)$. This is valid when $t_0\ge 0$ and the signal is causal (zero for $t< t_0$ in the shifted representation).

$$
\mathcal{L}_+\{x(t-t_0)u(t-t_0)\}=e^{-st_0}\mathcal{L}_+\{x(t)u(t)\}
$$

Use this section decision rule: if you can see a clean delayed-on-causal form like $x(t-t_0)u(t-t_0)$, use the shifting property; otherwise split by regions and integrate directly on $[0,\infty)$. For this lesson, your immediate workflow is select method, execute in one line form, and keep the causal notation stable.

$$
\mathcal{L}_+\{u(t-t_0)-u(t)\}=\frac{e^{-st_0}}{s}-\frac{1}{s}
$$

## Finding a One-Sided Laplace Transform by Direct Integration

**Example:** Find the one-sided Laplace transform of $x(t)=(2t+3)u(t)$.

**Explanation**

Use the one-sided definition directly because the signal is causal and already written as a polynomial times $u(t)$, so $\mathcal{L}_+\{x(t)\}=\int_0^\infty x(t)e^{-st}dt$.

$$
\mathcal{L}_+\{(2t+3)u(t)\}=\int_0^\infty (2t+3)e^{-st}dt
$$

Set up the direct sum on the same $[0,\infty)$ support: split the integral into a linear and a constant part so each piece is standard for one-sided transform tables.

$$
\mathcal{L}_+\{(2t+3)u(t)\}=2\int_0^\infty t e^{-st}dt+3\int_0^\infty e^{-st}dt
$$

For the constant piece, use the known one-sided result $\int_0^\infty e^{-st}dt=1/s$ for $s>0$. For $\int_0^\infty t e^{-st}dt$, use one-step integration by parts.

$$
\int_0^\infty t e^{-st}dt =\left[-\frac{t e^{-st}}{s}\right]_0^\infty +\frac{1}{s}\int_0^\infty e^{-st}dt=0+\frac{1}{s}\cdot\frac{1}{s}=\frac{1}{s^2},
$$

Substitute both pieces and combine into one rational expression. This preserves the causal transform workflow used later for larger polynomials.

$$
\mathcal{L}_+\{(2t+3)u(t)\}=\frac{2}{s^2}+\frac{3}{s}=\frac{3s+2}{s^2},\quad s>0
$$

## Applying Time Shifting to a Delayed Signal

**Example:** Compute the one-sided transform of $x(t)=e^{-(t-2)}u(t-2)$.

**Explanation**

The cue is a delayed causal signal, written as $x(t-a)u(t-a)$; here $a=2$ and the base shape is $e^{-t}$.

Use the one-sided time-shifting property rather than setting up a full integral from $2$ to $\infty$, so the step stays reusable for any similar delayed term.

$$
\mathcal{L}_+\{x(t-a)u(t-a)\}=e^{-as}\mathcal{L}_+\{x(t)u(t)\},\quad a\ge 0
$$

Match the template with $x(t-2)u(t-2)=e^{-(t-2)}u(t-2)$. This means $a=2$ and $x(t)=e^{-t}u(t)$.

$$
\mathcal{L}_+\{e^{-(t-2)}u(t-2)\}=e^{-2s}\mathcal{L}_+\{e^{-t}u(t)\}
$$

Apply the base transform and then attach the delay factor: $\mathcal{L}_+\{e^{-t}u(t)\}=1/(s+1)$ (from the standard one-sided exponential result).

$$
\mathcal{L}_+\{e^{-(t-2)}u(t-2)\}=e^{-2s}\frac{1}{s+1}=\frac{e^{-2s}}{s+1},\quad s>-1
$$

Final result: only the delay factor is new, so the example confirms that delayed causality is handled by multiplying the base one-sided transform by a pure $e^{-as}$ term.

## Finding a Piecewise Signal Transform by Splitting the Integral

**Example:** Find $\mathcal{L}_+\{x(t)\}$ for $x(t)=\begin{cases}1,&0\le t<2\\3,&t\ge 2\end{cases}$ using the definition.

**Explanation**

The cue here is a piecewise causal signal, so we keep the one-sided definition and write separate support intervals for $x(t)$: $x(t)=1$ on $[0,2)$ and $x(t)=3$ on $[2,\infty)$.

$$
\mathcal{L}_+\{x(t)\}=\int_0^\infty x(t)e^{-st}dt
$$

Split the integral at the breakpoint $t=2$, then use the two interval constants as independent integrands.

$$
\mathcal{L}_+\{x(t)\}=\int_0^2 e^{-st}dt+3\int_2^\infty e^{-st}dt
$$

Evaluate each piece with the standard one-sided exponential rule.

$$
\int_0^2 e^{-st}dt=\frac{1-e^{-2s}}{s},\quad\int_2^\infty e^{-st}dt=\frac{e^{-2s}}{s},\quad s>0
$$

Add the two contributions and simplify into one symbolic expression.

$$
\mathcal{L}_+\{x(t)\}=\frac{1-e^{-2s}}{s}+3\frac{e^{-2s}}{s}=\frac{1+2e^{-2s}}{s},\quad s>0
$$

This result keeps the same direct-integration template as the previous section, with one new wrinkle: interval splitting handles piecewise support cleanly and preserves causality in the limits.

Quick check: as $s\to\infty$, $\mathcal{L}_+\{x(t)\}\to 0$, so the result is consistent with a bounded positive-time signal.

## Choosing Between Direct Integration and Time Shifting

**Example:** Compute the one-sided transform of $x(t)=e^{-t}u(t)-e^{-(t-2)}u(t-2)$ and show the chosen method for each term.

**Explanation**

This signal contains one direct causal term and one delayed causal term, so the method split is intentional: use direct one-sided transform on $e^{-t}u(t)$ and time-shifting on $e^{-(t-2)}u(t-2)$.

$$
x(t)=e^{-t}u(t)-e^{-(t-2)}u(t-2)
$$

For the non-shifted causal exponential, apply the base one-sided rule directly.

$$
\mathcal{L}_+\{e^{-t}u(t)\}=\frac{1}{s+1},\quad s> -1
$$

For the delayed term, use the one-sided time-shift template with $a=2$ to avoid another full integral.

$$
\mathcal{L}_+\{e^{-(t-2)}u(t-2)\}=e^{-2s}\mathcal{L}_+\{e^{-t}u(t)\}=\frac{e^{-2s}}{s+1},\quad s> -1
$$

Subtract the delayed contribution from the direct contribution, then keep one simplified result.

$$
X(s)=\frac{1}{s+1}-\frac{e^{-2s}}{s+1}=\frac{1-e^{-2s}}{s+1},\quad s> -1
$$

Method rule to carry forward: use the shift property when a clean $x(t-a)u(t-a)$ pattern is visible; use direct integration when the signal does not match that pattern or is not factored for an immediate shift rewrite.
