## Introduction to Even and Odd Signal Symmetry

Signal symmetry is tested by pairing time values. For any positive time $t$, compare the signal value at $t$ with the value at the matching negative time $-t$. The classification is about the whole signal, so the same relationship must hold for every matched pair.

![](<../Source/1.1.5. Even and Odd Signal Symmetry/Images/l005-s001-te-section-001.png>)

A signal is even when each matched pair has the same value. On a graph, this looks like a mirror image across the vertical axis $t=0$. For example, if $x(1)=2$ and $x(-1)=2$, that pair supports even symmetry; the same equality must hold for all other matched pairs.

$$
x(-t)=x(t)
$$

A signal is odd when each matched pair has equal magnitude and opposite sign. On a graph, the left and right sides are related through the origin, not by vertical-axis mirroring. For example, if $x(1)=2$ and $x(-1)=-2$, that pair supports odd symmetry.

$$
x(-t)=-x(t)
$$

If a signal fails both whole-signal tests, it is neither even nor odd. One matched pair can rule out even symmetry if the two values are not equal, and one matched pair can rule out odd symmetry if the two values are not negatives of each other. Sometimes the same pair rules out both.

$$
\text{neither: the signal satisfies neither } x(-t)=x(t) \text{ nor } x(-t)=-x(t) \text{ for all } t
$$

Use the same routine each time: choose matching $t$ and $-t$ values, compare their signal values, then classify the signal as even, odd, or neither. The next examples apply this routine to graphs first and then to a piecewise definition.

## Identifying Even Symmetry from a Graph

**Example:** Determine whether the signal shown in the graph is even, odd, or neither.

**Explanation**

![](<../Source/1.1.5. Even and Odd Signal Symmetry/Images/l005-s002-te-section-002.png>)

The cue is the shape of the graph around $t=0$. To test for even symmetry, compare signal values at matching positive and negative times, such as $t=1$ and $t=-1$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x(-1)&=1, & x(1)&=1 \\
x(-2)&=0, & x(2)&=0
\end{aligned}
$$

These matched values have the same height. The same mirror pattern continues across the graph: every point on the right side has a matching point at the same height on the left side.

$$
x(-t)=x(t)
$$

Since the graph satisfies the even-symmetry rule for matching positive and negative times, the signal is even.

## Identifying Odd Symmetry from a Graph

**Example:** Determine whether the signal shown in the graph is even, odd, or neither.

**Explanation**

![](<../Source/1.1.5. Even and Odd Signal Symmetry/Images/l005-s003-te-section-003.png>)

This time, the matched values should not have the same height. To test for odd symmetry, compare values at matching times and check whether they have equal magnitude with opposite signs.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x(-1)&=-1, & x(1)&=1 \\
x(-2)&=-2, & x(2)&=2 \\
x(0)&=0
\end{aligned}
$$

At each matched pair, the negative-time value is the negative of the positive-time value. The graph also passes through the origin, which matches the odd-symmetry rule at $t=0$.

$$
x(-t)=-x(t)
$$

Since the graph satisfies the odd-symmetry rule for matching positive and negative times, the signal is odd.

## Recognizing Neither Even Nor Odd Symmetry

**Example:** Determine whether the signal shown in the graph is even, odd, or neither.

**Explanation**

![](<../Source/1.1.5. Even and Odd Signal Symmetry/Images/l005-s004-te-section-004.png>)

For a neither classification, look for a matched pair that breaks the symmetry rules. The graph gives a clear pair at $t=-1$ and $t=1$.

$$
x(-1)=1, \qquad x(1)=2
$$

These values are not equal, so this pair rules out even symmetry.

$$
x(-1)\ne x(1)
$$

The values are also not negatives of each other. Since $-x(1)=-2$, the value $x(-1)=1$ does not satisfy the odd-symmetry rule.

$$
x(-1)\ne -x(1)
$$

The same matched pair rules out both even and odd symmetry, so the signal is neither even nor odd.

## Classifying Symmetry from a Piecewise Definition

**Example:** Classify $x(t)=\begin{cases}2+t,& -2\le t<0\\2-t,& 0\le t\le 2\\0,& \text{otherwise}\end{cases}$ as even, odd, or neither.

**Explanation**

The task is the same symmetry test, but now the signal is given by intervals instead of a graph. Start with a positive time $t$ in the nonzero interval. For $0<t\le 2$, the matching time $-t$ lies in the interval $-2\le -t<0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x(t)&=2-t \\
x(-t)&=2+(-t)=2-t
\end{aligned}
$$

So every positive time in the support matches a negative time with the same signal value. At $t=0$, the comparison is also unchanged because $-0=0$.

$$
x(-t)=x(t)
$$

The otherwise case also matches. If $|t|>2$, then both $t$ and $-t$ fall outside the nonzero intervals, so both signal values are $0$.

$$
|t|>2 \quad \Longrightarrow \quad x(t)=0 \text{ and } x(-t)=0
$$

Since the matched values agree on the positive and negative intervals and also agree outside the support, the signal is even.
