## Introduction to Basic Signal Parameters: Amplitude, Offset, and Duration

When you read a continuous-time signal, three basic parameters describe the active part of the signal: how tall it is, where it is centered vertically, and how long it lasts. These are called amplitude, vertical offset, and duration.

![](<../Source/1.1.6. Basic Signal Parameters: Amplitude, Offset, and Duration/Images/l006-s001-te-section-001.png>)

Amplitude is a vertical distance. If the active part of the signal has maximum value $M$ and minimum value $m$, then the peak-to-peak range is $M-m$, so the amplitude is half of that range. The vertical offset is the center level halfway between the same maximum and minimum.

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= \frac{M-m}{2} \\
C &= \frac{M+m}{2}
\end{aligned}
$$

Duration is a horizontal length. First identify the nonzero support interval: the time interval where the signal is not zero. If that interval starts at $t_{\text{start}}$ and ends at $t_{\text{end}}$, then the duration is the end time minus the start time.

$$
D=t_{\text{end}}-t_{\text{start}}
$$

Use the same order each time: identify the active extrema, identify the baseline or center level, locate the nonzero support endpoints, and then report amplitude, vertical offset, and duration.

## Finding Amplitude from Signal Extrema

**Example:** Use the graph shown to find the amplitude of the continuous-time signal.

**Explanation**

![](<../Source/1.1.6. Basic Signal Parameters: Amplitude, Offset, and Duration/Images/l006-s002-te-section-002.png>)

The cue is the word amplitude. To find amplitude from a graph, first read the highest and lowest values on the active part of the signal.

From the graph, the maximum active value is $M=4$, and the minimum active value is $m=-2$.

$$
M=4,\qquad m=-2
$$

The peak-to-peak range is the difference between the maximum and the minimum.

$$
\begin{aligned}
M-m &= 4-(-2) \\
&= 6
\end{aligned}
$$

Amplitude is half of the peak-to-peak range, so divide this vertical range by $2$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= \frac{M-m}{2} \\
&= \frac{6}{2} \\
&= 3
\end{aligned}
$$

The amplitude of the signal is $3$.

## Finding Vertical Offset from the Baseline Level

**Example:** Use the graph shown to find the vertical offset of the continuous-time signal.

**Explanation**

![](<../Source/1.1.6. Basic Signal Parameters: Amplitude, Offset, and Duration/Images/l006-s003-te-section-003.png>)

The cue is the phrase vertical offset. The offset is the baseline or center level of the active signal, so we read the active maximum and minimum values and find the level halfway between them.

From the graph, the maximum active value is $M=5$, and the minimum active value is $m=1$.

$$
M=5,\qquad m=1
$$

The center level is the average of the maximum and minimum values. This is different from amplitude: amplitude uses half the range, while offset uses the midpoint level.

$$
\begin{aligned}
C &= \frac{M+m}{2} \\
&= \frac{5+1}{2} \\
&= 3
\end{aligned}
$$

The vertical offset of the signal is $3$.

## Finding Duration from the Nonzero Support Interval

**Example:** Use the graph shown to find the duration of the continuous-time signal.

**Explanation**

![](<../Source/1.1.6. Basic Signal Parameters: Amplitude, Offset, and Duration/Images/l006-s004-te-section-004.png>)

The cue is the word duration. Duration is a horizontal length, so we look for the interval where the signal is nonzero rather than the height of the signal.

From the graph, the signal first becomes nonzero at $t=-1$ and returns to zero at $t=4$.

$$
t_{\text{start}}=-1,\qquad t_{\text{end}}=4
$$

The duration is the end time minus the start time.

$$
\begin{aligned}
D &= t_{\text{end}}-t_{\text{start}} \\
&= 4-(-1) \\
&= 5
\end{aligned}
$$

The duration of the signal is $5$.

## Extracting All Three Parameters from a Graph

**Example:** Use the graph shown to find the amplitude, vertical offset, and duration of the continuous-time signal.

**Explanation**

![](<../Source/1.1.6. Basic Signal Parameters: Amplitude, Offset, and Duration/Images/l006-s005-te-section-005.png>)

This prompt asks for all three parameters, so use the fixed order: read the active extrema, find the center level, then locate the nonzero support endpoints.

For amplitude and offset, use the extrema on the active part of the signal. From the graph, the active maximum is $M=6$, and the active minimum is $m=2$.

$$
M=6,\qquad m=2
$$

The amplitude is half of the active peak-to-peak range.

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= \frac{M-m}{2} \\
&= \frac{6-2}{2} \\
&= 2
\end{aligned}
$$

The vertical offset is the midpoint between the same active maximum and minimum.

$$
\begin{aligned}
C &= \frac{M+m}{2} \\
&= \frac{6+2}{2} \\
&= 4
\end{aligned}
$$

For duration, use the time interval where the signal is nonzero. The graph shows the support starting at $t=-2$ and ending at $t=3$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
D &= t_{\text{end}}-t_{\text{start}} \\
&= 3-(-2) \\
&= 5
\end{aligned}
$$

So the amplitude is $2$, the vertical offset is $4$, and the duration is $5$.

## Extracting All Three Parameters from a Piecewise Definition

**Example:** Given $x(t)=0$ for $t<-2$, $x(t)=t+4$ for $-2\le t<0$, $x(t)=4-t$ for $0\le t\le2$, and $x(t)=0$ for $t>2$, find the amplitude, vertical offset, and duration.

**Explanation**

The cue is that the signal is given by a piecewise definition instead of a graph. Use the same extraction order, but read the support interval and active extrema from the conditions and formulas.

$$
x(t)=\begin{cases}
0,&t<-2\\
t+4,&-2\le t<0\\
4-t,&0\le t\le 2\\
0,&t>2
\end{cases}
$$

The nonzero formula pieces cover $-2\le t<0$ and $0\le t\le2$. Together, those conditions show that the nonzero support runs from $t=-2$ to $t=2$.

$$
t_{\text{start}}=-2,\qquad t_{\text{end}}=2
$$

To find the active extrema, evaluate the active pieces at the endpoints and the join point. These straight-line pieces reach their low and high values at those points.

$$
x(-2)=(-2)+4=2,\qquad x(0)=4-0=4,\qquad x(2)=4-2=2
$$

So the active maximum is $M=4$, and the active minimum is $m=2$.

$$
M=4,\qquad m=2
$$

Now compute amplitude from half the active peak-to-peak range.

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &= \frac{M-m}{2} \\
&= \frac{4-2}{2} \\
&= 1
\end{aligned}
$$

Compute vertical offset from the midpoint of the active maximum and minimum.

$$
\begin{aligned}
C &= \frac{M+m}{2} \\
&= \frac{4+2}{2} \\
&= 3
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, compute duration from the support endpoints.

$$
\begin{aligned}
D &= t_{\text{end}}-t_{\text{start}} \\
&= 2-(-2) \\
&= 4
\end{aligned}
$$

The amplitude is $1$, the vertical offset is $3$, and the duration is $4$.

```quiz
  \end{aligned}
  $$

  So the ordered triple $(\text{amplitude},\text{vertical offset},\text{duration})$ is $(1,3,4)$.
```
