## Introduction to Rational Period Ratios and Periodicity

A sum of periodic continuous-time signals is periodic only if every component comes back to the same state after one positive time shift. The cue is a shared repeat time: look for a single $T>0$ that works for every component.

$$
x(t+T)=x(t)
$$

Start with the base case of two component fundamental periods, $T_1$ and $T_2$. If one period is an integer multiple of the other, they already share a repeat time. For example, periods of $2$ s and $6$ s line up again after $6$ s.

$$
\frac{T_1}{T_2}\in\mathbb{Q}
$$

In general, the periods must have a rational ratio. If $T_1/T_2=p/q$ in lowest terms, then multiplying through gives a common period.

$$
\frac{T_1}{T_2}=\frac{p}{q}\quad(\gcd(p,q)=1)
$$

$$
qT_1=pT_2
$$

The least common period is the smallest positive shared multiple. For any finite sum of periodic components, find the smallest $T>0$ that equals an integer multiple of every component period.

$$
T_{\text{LC}}=\min\{T>0:\,T=mT_1=nT_2,\,m,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\}
$$

If the ratio is irrational, the components never line up exactly at any positive shift, so no finite common period exists and the sum is aperiodic.

## Testing a Sum with an Integer Period Ratio

**Example:** A sum $x(t)=x_1(t)+x_2(t)$ has component fundamental periods $T_1=2$ s and $T_2=6$ s. Determine whether $x(t)$ is periodic and, if so, find a least common period.

**Explanation**

Use the rational-ratio cue from the tutorial: a sum of periodic signals is periodic when the component fundamental periods share a rational ratio. Here $x(t)=x_1(t)+x_2(t)$ with $T_1=2$ s and $T_2=6$ s, so the first check is the ratio.

$$
\frac{T_2}{T_1}=\frac{6}{2}=3
$$

The ratio is an integer, so the periods are commensurate. Because $6=3\cdot 2$, the larger period is already a shared repeat time for both components.

$$
T_{\text{LC}}=6\text{ s}
$$

Therefore, $x(t)$ is periodic, and its least common period is $6$ s.

## Finding a Least Common Period from a Noninteger Rational Ratio

**Example:** A sum $x(t)=x_1(t)+x_2(t)$ has component fundamental periods $T_1=2$ s and $T_2=3$ s. Determine whether $x(t)$ is periodic and, if so, find a least common period.

**Explanation**

Use the rational-ratio cue from the tutorial and the previous example: a sum is periodic when the component fundamental periods have a rational ratio. Here $x(t)=x_1(t)+x_2(t)$ with $T_1=2$ s and $T_2=3$ s, so the ratio is rational but not an integer.

$$
\frac{T_2}{T_1}=\frac{3}{2}
$$

Even though neither period is an integer multiple of the other, the periods are commensurate. Find integers $m$ and $n$ with $mT_1=nT_2$; here $3T_1=2T_2=6$, so both components repeat after $6$ s.

$$
T_{\text{LC}}=6\text{ s}
$$

Therefore, $x(t)$ is periodic, and its least common period is $6$ s.

## Classifying a Sum with an Irrational Period Ratio

**Example:** A sum $x(t)=x_1(t)+x_2(t)$ has component fundamental periods $T_1=2$ s and $T_2=2\sqrt{2}$ s. Determine whether $x(t)$ is periodic.

**Explanation**

Use the rational-ratio test again. Here $x(t)=x_1(t)+x_2(t)$ with $T_1=2$ s and $T_2=2\sqrt{2}$ s, so first check the ratio of the component periods.

$$
\frac{T_2}{T_1}=\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{2}=\sqrt{2}
$$

Because $\sqrt{2}$ is irrational, the periods are not commensurate. That means there are no positive integers $m$ and $n$ with $mT_1=nT_2$, so the sum has no common repeat time.

Therefore, $x(t)$ is aperiodic.

<a id="finding-the-least-common-period-for-multiple-periodic-components"></a>
## Finding the Least Common Period for Multiple Periodic Components

**Example:** A sum $x(t)=x_1(t)+x_2(t)+x_3(t)$ has component fundamental periods $T_1=2$ s, $T_2=3$ s, and $T_3=4$ s. Determine whether $x(t)$ is periodic and, if so, find a least common period.

**Explanation**

Use the same rational-ratio test, but now all three component periods must fit one shared repeat time. Here $x(t)=x_1(t)+x_2(t)+x_3(t)$ with $T_1=2$ s, $T_2=3$ s, and $T_3=4$ s.

$$
\frac{T_2}{T_1}=\frac{3}{2}, \quad \frac{T_3}{T_1}=2, \quad \frac{T_3}{T_2}=\frac{4}{3}
$$

Each ratio is rational, so the three periods are commensurate. Look for a single time that is a multiple of all three periods. The smallest one is $12$ s, since $12=6T_1=4T_2=3T_3$.

$$
T_{\text{LC}}=12\text{ s}
$$

Therefore, $x(t)$ is periodic, and its least common period is $12$ s.
