## Introduction to Sinusoidal Signals

A continuous-time sinusoid is a signal you can write as a sine or cosine with a linear argument in $t$.

$$
x(t)=\cos(\omega t)
$$

The same template also covers sine, so the two standard forms are the cosine form and the sine form.

$$
x(t)=\sin(\omega t)
$$

The number multiplying $t$ is the angular frequency $\omega$, not the period. Read that coefficient first.

$$
T=\frac{2\pi}{\omega}
$$

Use $T=\frac{2\pi}{\omega}$ to convert $\omega$ into a period, and keep the units consistent: radians per second for $\omega$ and seconds for $T$.

$$
\omega=\frac{2\pi}{T}
$$

For the base case $x(t)=\cos(t)$, $\omega=1$ rad/s and $T=2\pi$ s, so this is the simplest reference sinusoid.

$$
x(t)=\cos(t)
$$

## Recognizing Sine and Cosine Signals

**Example:** Recognize that $x(t)=\cos(3t)$ is a sinusoidal signal written in cosine form.

**Explanation**

The cue is the cosine template from the previous step: a sinusoid is a sine or cosine with a linear time argument.

$$
x(t)=\cos(3t)
$$

This matches the cosine form because the argument $3t$ is linear in $t$.

$$
x(t)=\cos(\omega t)
$$

So $x(t)=\cos(3t)$ is a sinusoidal signal written in cosine form. Keep the focus on the form; period conversion comes later.

## Reading Angular Frequency from a Sinusoid

**Example:** For $x(t)=2\sin(\frac{5}{3}t)$, identify the angular frequency.

**Explanation**

The cue is the sinusoid template from the previous step: a sine or cosine with a linear time argument. The angular frequency is the number multiplying $t$.

$$
x(t)=2\sin\left(\frac{5}{3}t\right)
$$

Here the argument is $\frac{5}{3}t$, so the coefficient of $t$ is $\omega=\frac{5}{3}$. The amplitude factor $2$ does not affect the angular frequency.

$$
\omega=\frac{5}{3}
$$

So the angular frequency is $\omega=\frac{5}{3}$ rad/s. Keep the task at this read-off step; period conversion comes later.

## Finding the Period of a Sinusoidal Signal

**Example:** For $x(t)=\cos(\pi t)$, find the period.

**Explanation**

The cue is the sinusoid rule from the earlier steps: first read $\omega$ from the coefficient of $t$, then convert it to period with $T=2\pi/\omega$.

$$
x(t)=\cos(\pi t)
$$

Here the coefficient of $t$ is $\omega=\pi$ rad/s. That is the value to substitute into the period formula.

$$
T=\frac{2\pi}{\omega}
$$

$$
T=\frac{2\pi}{\pi}=2
$$

So the period is $T=2$ s. The key move is the conversion from angular frequency to time period.

## Writing a Sinusoid from Its Angular Frequency

**Example:** Write a cosine signal with angular frequency $4$ rad/s and verify its period.

**Explanation**

The cue is the given angular frequency. In the simplest construction, use cosine form with unit amplitude and zero phase, and place the number multiplying $t$ inside the cosine.

$$
x(t)=\cos(4t)
$$

This signal has angular frequency $\omega=4$ rad/s because the coefficient of $t$ is $4$. The period comes from the same conversion rule used earlier.

$$
T=\frac{2\pi}{\omega}
$$

$$
T=\frac{2\pi}{4}=\frac{\pi}{2}
$$

So one valid answer is $x(t)=\cos(4t)$, and its period is $T=\pi/2$ s. The frequency is set by the time coefficient, not by the amplitude.
