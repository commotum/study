## Introduction to Amplitude and Phase of a Sinusoid

Start with the simplest standard sinusoid, $x(t)=A\cos(\omega t)$, so you can see the amplitude before any phase offset appears. In the general standard form, the same signal is written as either $x(t)=A\cos(\omega t+\phi)$ or $x(t)=A\sin(\omega t+\phi)$.

$$
x(t)=A\cos(\omega t+\phi)
$$

$$
x(t)=A\sin(\omega t+\phi)
$$

Read $A$ as the amplitude. It is the positive peak deviation from the center line, so it tells you how far the sinusoid rises above and falls below its middle value. Do not confuse it with the angular frequency $\omega$ or with the sign of the coefficient.

$$
A>0
$$

Read $\phi$ as the phase. It is the constant angular offset inside the argument, and it locates the waveform within one period. The same reading rule works in both cosine and sine form.

For now, the job is only to recognize the standard form and extract $A$ and $\phi$ cleanly. Later sections will handle sign changes and rewrite wrinkles without changing this basic reading rule.

## Identifying Amplitude and Phase in Cosine Form

**Example:** Identify the amplitude and phase of $x(t)=4\cos(3t+\pi/6)$.

**Explanation**

Match the signal to the standard cosine form $x(t)=A\cos(\omega t+\phi)$. In this form, the coefficient in front of cosine is the amplitude, and the constant term inside the argument is the phase.

$$
x(t)=4\cos(3t+\pi/6)
$$

Here the coefficient is $4$, so the amplitude is $A=4$. That is the positive peak deviation, not the angular frequency $3$.

$$
(A,\phi)=(4,\pi/6)
$$

The constant term inside the cosine argument is $\pi/6$, so the phase is $\phi=\pi/6$. The amplitude-phase pair is $(4,\pi/6)$, and no rewrite is needed because the cosine form is already standard.

## Rewriting a Negative Coefficient with Positive Amplitude

**Example:** Rewrite $x(t)=-4\cos(3t-\pi/6)$ in positive-amplitude form and identify its amplitude and phase.

**Explanation**

Start from the negative-coefficient cosine form. Use $-\cos(\theta)=\cos(\theta+\pi)$ so the sign moves into the phase and the amplitude becomes positive.

$$
x(t)=-4\cos(3t-\pi/6)
$$

Apply the identity inside the cosine argument and keep the frequency term unchanged. The coefficient becomes $4$, and the phase gains a $\pi$ shift.

$$
x(t)=4\cos((3t-\pi/6)+\pi)=4\cos(3t+5\pi/6)
$$

$$
(A,\phi)=(4,5\pi/6)
$$

The positive-amplitude standard form is $x(t)=4\cos(3t+5\pi/6)$. The amplitude is $A=4$, and the phase is $\phi=5\pi/6$ after absorbing the negative sign into the phase.
