Generated step/example content:
Tutorial blocks:
- paragraph:
  text: For rectangular pulses, the convolution value at a time t comes from the overlap of two supports on the tau-axis: the fixed support of x(tau) and the shifted support of h(t - tau). Since each pulse is constant on its support, the integral is controlled by how long those two supports overlap.
- display-math:
  latex: y(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x(\tau)h(t-\tau)\,d\tau
- image:
  image_slot_id: section-001
- paragraph:
  text: When the two rectangular pulses have unequal widths, the shorter pulse can become completely covered by the longer one. That creates three nonzero overlap behaviors as t increases: the overlap length grows, then stays equal to the shorter width, then shrinks back to zero.
- paragraph:
  text: The edge-aligned breakpoints are the t-values where one edge of the sliding support lines up with one edge of the fixed support. These breakpoints split the time axis into no overlap, growing overlap, full overlap, shrinking overlap, and no overlap again.
- display-math:
  latex: y(t)=A_xA_hL(t)
- paragraph:
  text: Here L(t) is the overlap length of the two rectangular supports, and A_xA_h is the product of the pulse amplitudes. In the unit-height base case, the convolution is just the overlap length. With nonunit heights, find the same overlap length first, then scale it by A_xA_h.
- paragraph:
  text: The workflow for this lesson is to locate the edge-aligned breakpoints, write the overlap length on each nonzero region, and then multiply by the pulse amplitudes. Because the middle overlap length is constant, the final output has a trapezoidal shape: rising ramp, flat plateau, and falling ramp.
