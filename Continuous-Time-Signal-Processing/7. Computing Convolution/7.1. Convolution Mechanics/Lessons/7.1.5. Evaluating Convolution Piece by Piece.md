## Introduction to Evaluating Convolution Piece by Piece

In the previous convolution steps, the overlap intervals for $x(\tau)$ and $h(t-\tau)$ were already found. This lesson starts after those bounds are known. The new move is to evaluate the convolution integral separately on each time region where the overlap bounds have a fixed formula.

$$
y(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x(\tau)h(t-\tau)\,d\tau
$$

On one time region, the overlap gives a lower bound and an upper bound for $\tau$. Those bounds replace $-\infty$ and $\infty$ in the convolution integral. The variable of integration is $\tau$, while $t$ is treated as a fixed parameter during that regional calculation.

$$
y_i(t)=\int_{L_i(t)}^{U_i(t)}x(\tau)h(t-\tau)\,d\tau,\qquad t\text{ in region }i
$$

For each region, use the same workflow: choose the current time region, insert its bounds $L_i(t)$ and $U_i(t)$, integrate with respect to $\tau$, simplify the result as a formula in $t$, and keep the region condition attached to that formula.

The final output is not one formula produced from one set of bounds. It is a piecewise function assembled from the regional formulas, listed in the same order as the time regions.

$$
y(t)=\begin{cases}y_1(t),&t\in R_1\\y_2(t),&t\in R_2\\y_3(t),&t\in R_3\end{cases}
$$

## Evaluating the First Overlap Region

**Example:** For $0 \le t \le 1$, evaluate the first-region convolution integral $y(t)=\int_0^t 1\,d\tau$.

**Explanation**

The current time region is $0 \le t \le 1$. On this region, the overlap bounds have already been found: $0 \le \tau \le t$. The task is only to evaluate the regional integral and keep the time condition attached.

$$
y(t)=\int_0^t 1\,d\tau,\qquad 0\le t\le 1
$$

The integration variable is $\tau$, so $t$ is treated as fixed while we take the antiderivative.

$$
\int 1\,d\tau=\tau
$$

Now substitute the upper bound $t$ and the lower bound $0$.

$$
y(t)=\left[\tau\right]_0^t=t-0=t
$$

So the first overlap region contributes the regional formula $y(t)=t$ for $0 \le t \le 1$. This is the first piece, not the full piecewise output.

## Evaluating a Middle Region with New Bounds

**Example:** For $1 \le t \le 2$, evaluate the middle-region convolution integral $y(t)=\int_0^1 1\,d\tau$.

**Explanation**

The current time region is $1 \le t \le 2$. The cue is that this is a middle region, so the bounds are not inherited from the first region. Here the overlap bounds are fixed: $0 \le \tau \le 1$.

$$
y(t)=\int_0^1 1\,d\tau,\qquad 1\le t\le 2
$$

The integration variable is still $\tau$. Since the integrand is constant and the bounds are fixed, this regional value will be constant on the whole interval.

$$
\int 1\,d\tau=\tau
$$

Now substitute the fixed upper bound $1$ and lower bound $0$.

$$
y(t)=\left[\tau\right]_0^1=1-0=1
$$

So the middle overlap region contributes the regional value $y(t)=1$ for $1 \le t \le 2$. The method stayed the same; the bounds changed.

## Evaluating the Last Overlap Region

**Example:** For $2 \le t \le 3$, evaluate the last-region convolution integral $y(t)=\int_{t-2}^1 1\,d\tau$.

**Explanation**

The current time region is $2 \le t \le 3$. The cue is that this is the last nonzero overlap region, so the overlap is shrinking. The lower bound now depends on $t$: $t-2 \le \tau \le 1$.

$$
y(t)=\int_{t-2}^1 1\,d\tau,\qquad 2\le t\le 3
$$

The integration variable is still $\tau$, so $t$ is treated as fixed while we take the antiderivative.

$$
\int 1\,d\tau=\tau
$$

Now substitute upper minus lower carefully. The upper bound is $1$, and the lower bound is $t-2$.

$$
y(t)=\left[\tau\right]_{t-2}^1=1-(t-2)=3-t
$$

So the last overlap region contributes the regional formula $y(t)=3-t$ for $2 \le t \le 3$. The result decreases because the lower bound moves upward as $t$ increases.

## Assembling the Piecewise Convolution Output

**Example:** Given $y(t)=t$ for $0 \le t \le 1$, $y(t)=1$ for $1 \le t \le 2$, and $y(t)=3-t$ for $2 \le t \le 3$, assemble the nonzero piecewise convolution output.

**Explanation**

The cue is that the regional formulas have already been evaluated. The task is not to integrate again; it is to preserve each formula with its own time interval and list the pieces in increasing order of $t$.

$$
\begin{aligned}y(t)&=t,\qquad 0\le t\le 1\\y(t)&=1,\qquad 1\le t\le 2\\y(t)&=3-t,\qquad 2\le t\le 3\end{aligned}
$$

Now place those three formula-interval pairs under one piecewise definition for $y(t)$.

$$
y(t)=\begin{cases}t,&0\le t\le 1\\1,&1\le t\le 2\\3-t,&2\le t\le 3\end{cases}
$$

This is the nonzero piecewise convolution output. The formulas stay attached to their original intervals, and no outside zero-output regions are added because the prompt asks only for the nonzero overlap pieces.
