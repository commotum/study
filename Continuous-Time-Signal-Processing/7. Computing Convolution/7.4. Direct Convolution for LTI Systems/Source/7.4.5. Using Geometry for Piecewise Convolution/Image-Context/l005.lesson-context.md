Generated step/example content:
Tutorial blocks:
- paragraph:
  text: For a direct convolution, the output at one fixed time t is found by integrating over the horizontal variable tau. The value of t stays fixed while tau moves across the overlap between x(tau) and h(t - tau).
- display-math:
  latex: y(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x(\tau)h(t-\tau)\,d\tau
- paragraph:
  text: After the convolution has been split into time regions, each region has its own overlap interval. On one such region, the nonzero contribution comes only from the tau-values where both factors are active.
- display-math:
  latex: y(t)=\int_{a(t)}^{b(t)}x(\tau)h(t-\tau)\,d\tau
- paragraph:
  text: The geometric shortcut is to look at the product x(tau)h(t - tau), not either signal alone. The integral is the signed area under that product over the current overlap interval.
- image:
  image_slot_id: section-001
- paragraph:
  text: In the simplest case, the product is constant over the whole overlap interval. Then the shaded product region is a rectangle: its width is the overlap length in tau, and its height is the constant product value.
- display-math:
  latex: \int_{a(t)}^{b(t)} C\,d\tau=C\bigl(b(t)-a(t)\bigr)
- paragraph:
  text: This is the recurring move for the lesson: identify the product shape for the current time region, read its geometric dimensions, compute the area, and attach that area formula to the correct piece of y(t). Later sections use the same idea for rectangles, triangles, trapezoids, composite regions, and final piecewise assembly.
