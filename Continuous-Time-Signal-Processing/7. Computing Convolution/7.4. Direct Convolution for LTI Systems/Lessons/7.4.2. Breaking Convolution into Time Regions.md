## Introduction to Breaking Convolution into Time Regions

A direct convolution is split into time regions because the $\tau$-values that contribute to the integral change as t changes. For a fixed output time t, only $\tau$-values where both factors are nonzero can affect $y(t)$.

$$
y(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x(\tau)h(t-\tau)\,d\tau
$$

In the simplest finite-support case, $x(\tau)$ gives a fixed support constraint on the $\tau$-axis. If $x(\tau)$ is nonzero on [a,b], then those endpoints do not move as t changes.

$$
x(\tau)\neq 0 \text{ on } [a,b]
$$

The other factor, $h(t - \tau)$, creates the moving support constraint. If h is nonzero on [c,d] in its own input variable, then $h(t - \tau)$ is nonzero on the $\tau$-interval [t - d, t - c].

$$
h(t-\tau)\neq 0 \text{ on } [t-d,t-c]
$$

![](<../Source/7.4.2. Breaking Convolution into Time Regions/Images/l002-s001-te-section-001.png>)

So the useful $\tau$-values are exactly the overlap of the fixed support interval and the moving support interval. When that overlap changes, the integration bounds must change too.

$$
\text{overlap in }\tau=[a,b]\cap[t-d,t-c]
$$

A contact time is a t-value where an endpoint of the moving interval [t - d, t - c] lines up with an endpoint of the fixed interval [a,b]. These contact times are the breakpoints for the convolution calculation.

The open intervals between consecutive contact times are the time regions. Inside one such region, the overlap pattern stays the same, so the same kind of $\tau$-bounds applies throughout that region.

The routine for this lesson is to find the contact times, order them on the t-axis, decide which regions have overlap, and then write the $\tau$ integration bounds for each overlap region.

## Finding Contact Times Between $x(\tau)$ and $h(t - \tau)$

**Example:** For $x(\tau)$ nonzero on 0$\le$ $\tau$$\le$ 3 and $h(t-\tau)$ nonzero on t-2$\le$ $\tau$$\le$ t, find the contact times between their support endpoints.

**Explanation**

The cue is endpoint contact: a contact time happens when an endpoint of the moving support of $h(t-\tau)$ has the same $\tau$-location as an endpoint of the fixed support of $x(\tau)$.

$$
x(\tau)\neq 0 \text{ on } [0,3],\qquad h(t-\tau)\neq 0 \text{ on } [t-2,t]
$$

![](<../Source/7.4.2. Breaking Convolution into Time Regions/Images/l002-s002-te-section-002.png>)

The fixed endpoints are 0 and 3. The moving endpoints are t-2 and t. To find every contact time, set each moving endpoint equal to each fixed endpoint.

$$
t-2=0,\qquad t-2=3,\qquad t=0,\qquad t=3
$$

Now solve those four endpoint-alignment equations for t. The solutions are t-values because t is the amount that moves the support of $h(t-\tau)$.

$$
\begin{aligned} t-2=0&\Rightarrow t=2 \\ t-2=3&\Rightarrow t=5 \\ t=0&\Rightarrow t=0 \\ t=3&\Rightarrow t=3 \end{aligned}
$$

Sort the resulting t-values from earliest to latest. These are the contact times where the overlap pattern can change.

$$
t=0,\ 2,\ 3,\ 5
$$

<a id="ordering-breakpoints-on-the-t-axis"></a>
## Ordering Breakpoints on the t-Axis

**Example:** For $x(\tau)$ nonzero on -1$\le$ $\tau$$\le$ 2 and $h(t-\tau)$ nonzero on t-3$\le$ $\tau$$\le$ t-1, find the contact times and order the resulting breakpoints on the t-axis.

**Explanation**

The cue is the same endpoint contact used before, but now the result must become a t-axis partition. First find the contact times, then sort them from left to right.

$$
x(\tau)\neq 0 \text{ on } [-1,2],\qquad h(t-\tau)\neq 0 \text{ on } [t-3,t-1]
$$

The fixed endpoints are -1 and 2. The moving endpoints are t-3 and t-1, so each moving endpoint is set equal to each fixed endpoint.

$$
\begin{aligned} t-3=-1&\Rightarrow t=2 \\ t-3=2&\Rightarrow t=5 \\ t-1=-1&\Rightarrow t=0 \\ t-1=2&\Rightarrow t=3 \end{aligned}
$$

![](<../Source/7.4.2. Breaking Convolution into Time Regions/Images/l002-s003-te-section-003.png>)

The candidate breakpoints are $t=2$, $t=5$, $t=0$, and $t=3$ in the order they were found. If two equations gave the same t-value, we would keep it only once; here all four values are distinct.

$$
0<2<3<5
$$

These ordered breakpoints divide the t-axis into open time regions. Include the exterior region before the first breakpoint and the exterior region after the last breakpoint.

$$
(-\infty,0),\quad (0,2),\quad (2,3),\quad (3,5),\quad (5,\infty)
$$

So the ordered breakpoints are $t=0$,2,3,5, and the listed open intervals are the candidate time regions. This section stops at the partition; it does not decide yet which regions have overlap.

<a id="classifying-no-overlap-and-overlap-regions"></a>
## Classifying No-Overlap and Overlap Regions

**Example:** Using the ordered breakpoints $t=0$,2,3,5 for $x(\tau)$ supported on [0,3] and $h(t-\tau)$ supported on [t-2,t], classify each resulting t-region as no-overlap or overlap.

**Explanation**

The ordered breakpoints divide the t-axis into open regions. To classify a region, test one representative t-value from inside that region and check whether the fixed support [0,3] shares any $\tau$-values with the moving support [t-2,t].

$$
(-\infty,0),\quad (0,2),\quad (2,3),\quad (3,5),\quad (5,\infty)
$$

Use an interior representative for each open region, not a boundary value. Endpoint-only contact occurs at a breakpoint, so it does not make an entire open region an overlap region.

![](<../Source/7.4.2. Breaking Convolution into Time Regions/Images/l002-s004-te-section-004.png>)

For each representative t-value, substitute into the moving support [t-2,t] and compare that interval with the fixed support [0,3].

$$
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c} \text{region} & \text{test }t & h(t-\tau)\text{ support} & \text{classification} \\ \hline t<0 & -1 & [-3,-1] & \text{no-overlap} \\ 0<t<2 & 1 & [-1,1] & \text{overlap} \\ 2<t<3 & \frac{5}{2} & [\frac{1}{2},\frac{5}{2}] & \text{overlap} \\ 3<t<5 & 4 & [2,4] & \text{overlap} \\ t>5 & 6 & [4,6] & \text{no-overlap} \end{array}
$$

So the exterior regions $t<0$ and $t>5$ produce no overlap. The three middle regions $0<t<2$, $2<t<3$, and $3<t<5$ produce nonempty overlap and are the regions that can contribute to the convolution.

$$
\begin{array}{c|c} \text{t-region} & \text{status} \\ \hline t<0 & \text{no-overlap} \\ 0<t<2 & \text{overlap} \\ 2<t<3 & \text{overlap} \\ 3<t<5 & \text{overlap} \\ t>5 & \text{no-overlap} \end{array}
$$

This classification only says where the product $x(\tau)$$h(t-\tau)$ can be nonzero. It does not yet assign the $\tau$ integration bounds for the overlap regions.

## Assigning Integration Bounds for Each Time Region

**Example:** For $x(\tau)$ supported on [0,3] and $h(t-\tau)$ supported on [t-2,t], assign the $\tau$ integration bounds for each overlap time region.

**Explanation**

Start from the regions already classified as overlap regions. The exterior regions $t<0$ and $t>5$ have no overlap, so they give zero contribution and do not need integration bounds.

$$
0<t<2,\qquad 2<t<3,\qquad 3<t<5
$$

For any overlap region, the $\tau$-bounds come from the intersection of the fixed support and the moving support. The lower bound is the larger left endpoint, and the upper bound is the smaller right endpoint.

$$
[0,3]\cap[t-2,t],\qquad \tau_{\min}=\max(0,t-2),\qquad \tau_{\max}=\min(3,t)
$$

![](<../Source/7.4.2. Breaking Convolution into Time Regions/Images/l002-s005-te-section-005.png>)

Now simplify the larger-left-endpoint and smaller-right-endpoint choices inside each overlap region.

$$
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c} \text{t-region} & \text{left endpoints} & \text{right endpoints} & \text{tau bounds} \\ \hline 0<t<2 & 0>t-2 & t<3 & 0\le \tau\le t \\ 2<t<3 & t-2>0 & t<3 & t-2\le \tau\le t \\ 3<t<5 & t-2>0 & 3<t & t-2\le \tau\le 3 \end{array}
$$

The result is a piecewise bounds list matched to the overlap regions. These are the bounds needed for the later convolution integrals, but no integral is evaluated here.

$$
\begin{array}{c|c} \text{t-region} & \text{tau integration bounds} \\ \hline 0<t<2 & 0\le \tau\le t \\ 2<t<3 & t-2\le \tau\le t \\ 3<t<5 & t-2\le \tau\le 3 \end{array}
$$
