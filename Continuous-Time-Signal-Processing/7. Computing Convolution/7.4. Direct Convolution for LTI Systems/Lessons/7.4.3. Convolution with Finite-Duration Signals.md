## Introduction to Convolution with Finite-Duration Signals

For an LTI system, the output is the convolution of the input signal with the impulse response. The direct calculation starts with the convolution integral, where $t$ is the output time and $\tau$ is the integration variable.

$$
y(t)=(x*h)(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x(\tau)h(t-\tau)\,d\tau
$$

Finite-duration signals are zero outside bounded support intervals. That means most values of $\tau$ contribute nothing to the integral. The only useful values of $\tau$ are the values where both $x(\tau)$ and $h(t-\tau)$ are nonzero at the same time.

$$
\begin{aligned}
x(\tau)&\neq 0 \text{ on } [a,b],\\
h(t-\tau)&\neq 0 \text{ when } t-\tau\in[c,d]
\end{aligned}
$$

The first interval is fixed on the $\tau$-axis. The second interval moves as $t$ changes. Since $h(t-\tau)$ is nonzero when $t-\tau$ lies between $c$ and $d$, its active $\tau$-interval is $[t-d,t-c]$.

$$
\tau\in[a,b]\cap[t-d,t-c]
$$

That intersection is the overlap interval. For each time region, the endpoints of the overlap interval become the integration bounds for that piece of $y(t)$. If the intersection is empty, the output is zero in that time region.

For the simplest finite-duration case, take $x(t)=1$ on $0\le t\le 3$ and $h(t)=1$ on $0\le t\le 2$, with both signals zero outside those intervals. Then $x(\tau)$ is fixed on $[0,3]$, while $h(t-\tau)$ is active on $[t-2,t]$. Every nonzero piece of the convolution comes from the overlap $[0,3]\cap[t-2,t]$.

$$
\operatorname{supp}(x*h)\subseteq[a+c,b+d]
$$

So the workflow is: find the possible output support, split time into regions where the overlap changes, set the $\tau$-bounds from the overlap in each region, evaluate each bounded integral, and assemble the final piecewise output $y(t)$.

## Finding the Output Support from Finite-Duration Signal Supports

**Example:** Suppose $x(t)$ is nonzero only on $0\le t\le 3$ and $h(t)$ is nonzero only on $1\le t\le 4$. Find the possible nonzero support of $y(t)=(x*h)(t)$.

**Explanation**

The cue is that both signals have finite support. To find the possible support of the convolution output, use the endpoints of the two input supports before doing any integration.

$$
\operatorname{supp}(x)=[0,3],\qquad \operatorname{supp}(h)=[1,4]
$$

The first possible time when the output can be nonzero comes from adding the two left endpoints. This is the earliest time when the finite supports can first touch in the convolution.

$$
0+1=1
$$

The last possible time when the output can be nonzero comes from adding the two right endpoints. This is the latest time before the finite supports have fully separated.

$$
3+4=7
$$

Therefore, the convolution output can be nonzero only between these two endpoint sums. This gives the outer support interval, not the detailed piecewise formula for $y(t)$.

$$
\operatorname{supp}(y)\subseteq[1,7]
$$

<a id="setting-integral-bounds-in-a-partial-overlap-region"></a>
## Setting Integral Bounds in a Partial-Overlap Region

**Example:** Let $x(t)=1$ on $0\le t\le 3$ and $h(t)=1$ on $0\le t\le 2$, with both signals zero otherwise. For $0<t<2$, set the $\tau$ integration bounds for $y(t)=\int x(\tau)h(t-\tau)\,d\tau$.

**Explanation**

The cue is the stated time region $0<t<2$. In this section, the goal is not to evaluate the convolution; the goal is to find which values of $\tau$ make both factors nonzero in that region.

First rewrite the fixed signal as a support condition on $\tau$. Since $x(t)$ is nonzero on $0\le t\le 3$, the factor $x(\tau)$ is nonzero only on this fixed interval:

$$
0\le \tau\le 3
$$

Next convert the support of $h$ into a condition on $\tau$. The factor $h(t-\tau)$ is nonzero when its input $t-\tau$ lies between $0$ and $2$.

$$
0\le t-\tau\le 2\quad\Longrightarrow\quad t-2\le \tau\le t
$$

The integration bounds come from the overlap of the fixed interval and the moving interval.

$$
\tau\in[0,3]\cap[t-2,t]
$$

Now use the region condition $0<t<2$. In this region, $t-2<0$, so the larger lower endpoint is $0$. Also $t<2<3$, so the smaller upper endpoint is $t$.

$$
[0,3]\cap[t-2,t]=[0,t]\qquad(0<t<2)
$$

Therefore, for the rising partial-overlap region, the $\tau$ bounds are $0\le\tau\le t$. The bounded convolution setup is

$$
y(t)=\int_{0}^{t} x(\tau)h(t-\tau)\,d\tau\qquad(0<t<2)
$$

<a id="setting-integral-bounds-in-a-full-overlap-region"></a>
## Setting Integral Bounds in a Full-Overlap Region

**Example:** Let $x(t)=1$ on $0\le t\le 3$ and $h(t)=1$ on $0\le t\le 2$, with both signals zero otherwise. For $2<t<3$, set the $\tau$ integration bounds for $y(t)=\int x(\tau)h(t-\tau)\,d\tau$.

**Explanation**

The cue is that the signals are the same as before, but the time region is now $2<t<3$. That region is a full-overlap region: the moving support from $h(t-\tau)$ sits entirely inside the fixed support of $x(\tau)$.

Start with the same support intersection. The factor $x(\tau)$ is nonzero on $[0,3]$, and the factor $h(t-\tau)$ is nonzero when $t-2\le \tau\le t$.

$$
\tau\in[0,3]\cap[t-2,t]
$$

Now compare endpoints using $2<t<3$. Since $t>2$, the left endpoint $t-2$ is greater than $0$, so the lower integration bound comes from the moving interval.

$$
t-2>0
$$

Also, since $t<3$, the right endpoint $t$ is less than $3$, so the upper integration bound also comes from the moving interval.

$$
t<3
$$

Therefore, in the region $2<t<3$, the moving interval $[t-2,t]$ is fully inside the fixed interval $[0,3]$.

$$
[0,3]\cap[t-2,t]=[t-2,t]\qquad(2<t<3)
$$

So the full-overlap $\tau$ bounds are $t-2\le\tau\le t$. The bounded convolution setup is

$$
y(t)=\int_{t-2}^{t} x(\tau)h(t-\tau)\,d\tau\qquad(2<t<3)
$$

<a id="evaluating-finite-duration-convolution-pieces"></a>
## Evaluating Finite-Duration Convolution Pieces

**Example:** Let $x(t)=2$ on $0\le t\le 3$ and $h(t)=1$ on $0\le t\le 2$, with both signals zero otherwise. Evaluate $y(t)$ on the regions $0<t<2$ with bounds $0\le\tau\le t$ and $2<t<3$ with bounds $t-2\le\tau\le t$.

**Explanation**

The cue is that the time regions and $\tau$ bounds are already supplied. This section adds the evaluation step: substitute the constant signal values into the integrand and evaluate each bounded integral.

On both stated overlap regions, the bounds have already restricted $\tau$ to values where both factors are nonzero. Therefore $x(\tau)=2$ and $h(t-\tau)=1$ inside each integral.

$$
x(\tau)h(t-\tau)=2\cdot 1=2
$$

For the partial-overlap region $0<t<2$, use the supplied bounds $0\le\tau\le t$.

$$
y(t)=\int_{0}^{t}2\,d\tau=2t\qquad(0<t<2)
$$

For the full-overlap region $2<t<3$, use the supplied bounds $t-2\le\tau\le t$.

$$
y(t)=\int_{t-2}^{t}2\,d\tau=2\bigl(t-(t-2)\bigr)=4\qquad(2<t<3)
$$

So the evaluated pieces are $y(t)=2t$ on $0<t<2$ and $y(t)=4$ on $2<t<3$. These are region-specific formulas, not the full piecewise output yet.

<a id="assembling-the-piecewise-finite-duration-output"></a>
## Assembling the Piecewise Finite-Duration Output

**Example:** Let $x(t)=1$ on $0\le t\le 3$ and $h(t)=2$ on $0\le t\le 2$, with both signals zero otherwise. Compute and assemble the full piecewise output $y(t)=(x*h)(t)$.

**Explanation**

The cue is that the full output is requested. That means we need the zero regions, the nonzero time regions, the bounds in each nonzero region, and the evaluated formula for each piece.

First find the possible output support from the endpoint sums. The support of $x$ is $[0,3]$, and the support of $h$ is $[0,2]$.

$$
\operatorname{supp}(y)\subseteq[0+0,3+2]=[0,5]
$$

So $y(t)=0$ outside $[0,5]$. Inside that support, use the same overlap representation as before: $x(\tau)$ is fixed on $[0,3]$, and $h(t-\tau)$ is active on $[t-2,t]$.

$$
\tau\in[0,3]\cap[t-2,t]
$$

The endpoint contacts split the nonzero support into a rising partial-overlap region, a full-overlap region, and a falling partial-overlap region.

$$
\begin{aligned}
0<t<2 &: \quad 0\le\tau\le t,\\
2\le t\le 3 &: \quad t-2\le\tau\le t,\\
3<t<5 &: \quad t-2\le\tau\le 3
\end{aligned}
$$

On each overlap interval, the integrand is constant because $x(\tau)=1$ and $h(t-\tau)=2$.

$$
x(\tau)h(t-\tau)=1\cdot 2=2
$$

Now evaluate the bounded integral on each nonzero region and keep each formula attached to the region that produced it.

$$
\begin{aligned}
0<t<2 &: \quad y(t)=\int_0^t 2\,d\tau=2t,\\
2\le t\le 3 &: \quad y(t)=\int_{t-2}^t 2\,d\tau=4,\\
3<t<5 &: \quad y(t)=\int_{t-2}^3 2\,d\tau=2(5-t)=10-2t
\end{aligned}
$$

Finally, assemble the zero regions and the three evaluated nonzero pieces into one piecewise output.

$$
y(t)=\begin{cases}
0, & t\le 0,\\
2t, & 0<t<2,\\
4, & 2\le t\le 3,\\
10-2t, & 3<t<5,\\
0, & t\ge 5.
\end{cases}
$$
