## Introduction to Convolution with Sloped Signals

For an LTI system, direct convolution still starts with the same integral. The output time t is held fixed, and $\tau$ is the variable that moves through the overlap between $x(\tau)$ and $h(t - \tau)$.

$$
y(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x(\tau)h(t-\tau)\,d\tau
$$

![](<../Source/7.4.4. Convolution with Sloped Signals/Images/l004-s001-te-section-001.png>)

With rectangular pulses, the product is often constant on the overlap, so the main work is finding the overlap length. With a sloped segment, the signal value changes as $\tau$ changes. That means the integrand must use the actual formula for the overlapping signal piece.

In the simplest case, let the input piece be a ramp and let the shifted impulse-response piece be constant on the overlap.

$$
x(\tau)=\tau \text{ on } 0\le \tau\le 1,\qquad h(t-\tau)=1 \text{ on } t-1\le \tau\le t
$$

Only $\tau$-values inside both active intervals contribute to the integral. If $a(t)$ is the lower overlap bound and $b(t)$ is the upper overlap bound, the one-region setup uses the ramp value $\tau$ throughout that overlap.

$$
y(t)=\int_{a(t)}^{b(t)} \tau\cdot 1\,d\tau=\int_{a(t)}^{b(t)}\tau\,d\tau
$$

So the recurring workflow for sloped convolution is: write each overlapping piece as a function of $\tau$, find the time-dependent overlap bounds, integrate the product over that interval, and then place the result in the correct piece of $y(t)$.

## Rewriting a Sloped Segment as a Function of Tau

**Example:** A signal $x(\tau)$ is zero outside $0 \le \tau \le 2$ and rises linearly from 0 at $\tau = 0$ to 4 at $\tau = 2$. Write $x(\tau)$ on its nonzero interval.

**Explanation**

The cue is the highlighted sloped segment. Before this signal can be used inside a convolution integral, the segment needs a formula in the integration variable $\tau$, together with the interval where that formula applies.

![](<../Source/7.4.4. Convolution with Sloped Signals/Images/l004-s002-te-section-002.png>)

Read the two endpoint coordinates from the segment. The signal value is 0 when $\tau = 0$, and the signal value is 4 when $\tau = 2$.

$$
(0,0)\quad\text{and}\quad(2,4)
$$

Use change in signal value divided by change in $\tau$ to find the slope of the line segment.

$$
m=\frac{4-0}{2-0}=2
$$

Now write the line in the form $x(\tau) = m$ $\tau$ + b. Since the segment passes through the origin, the intercept is 0.

$$
0=2(0)+b\quad\Longrightarrow\quad b=0
$$

Attach the active interval to the formula. The segment is nonzero only from $\tau = 0$ through $\tau = 2$, so the usable $\tau$-domain expression is

$$
x(\tau)=2\tau,\qquad 0\le \tau\le 2.
$$

<a id="setting-bounds-for-a-sloped-segment-overlap"></a>
## Setting Bounds for a Sloped Segment Overlap

**Example:** Let $x(\tau)$ be nonzero on $0 \le \tau \le 2$, and let $h(t - \tau)$ be nonzero on $t - 1 \le \tau \le t$. For $0 \le t \le 1$, set the $\tau$-bounds for their overlap.

**Explanation**

The cue is that both factors are only nonzero on certain $\tau$-intervals. To set the convolution bounds, intersect the fixed support of $x(\tau)$ with the moving support of $h(t - \tau)$.

![](<../Source/7.4.4. Convolution with Sloped Signals/Images/l004-s003-te-section-003.png>)

The fixed support comes from $x(\tau)$. It contributes only when $\tau$ is between 0 and 2.

$$
0\le \tau\le 2
$$

The shifted impulse-response factor has a moving support because its endpoints depend on t.

$$
t-1\le \tau\le t
$$

The overlap interval is the intersection of those two supports. The lower bound is the larger left endpoint, and the upper bound is the smaller right endpoint.

$$
[0,2]\cap[t-1,t]
$$

$$
\text{lower bound}=\max(0,t-1),\qquad \text{upper bound}=\min(2,t)
$$

Now use the stated time region. If $0 \le t \le 1$, then $t - 1 \le 0$, so the larger left endpoint is 0. Also $t \le 1 \le 2$, so the smaller right endpoint is t.

$$
\max(0,t-1)=0,\qquad \min(2,t)=t
$$

So in this early time region, the overlap that will become the integration interval is

$$
0\le \tau\le t,\qquad 0\le t\le 1.
$$

<a id="integrating-a-sloped-segment-times-a-constant-segment"></a>
## Integrating a Sloped Segment Times a Constant Segment

**Example:** Suppose $x(\tau) = 2 \tau$ on $0 \le \tau \le 2$ and $h(t - \tau) = 3$ on $t - 1 \le \tau \le t$. For $0 \le t \le 1$, evaluate the convolution contribution over the overlap.

**Explanation**

The cue is that the overlap bounds are already determined for this early time region. The new step is to multiply the sloped factor by the constant factor and integrate with respect to $\tau$.

![](<../Source/7.4.4. Convolution with Sloped Signals/Images/l004-s004-te-section-004.png>)

From the overlap rule used in the previous section, the active $\tau$-interval for $0 \le t \le 1$ is

$$
0\le \tau\le t.
$$

On that interval, substitute the two signal pieces into the integrand. The sloped piece is $x(\tau) = 2$ $\tau$, and the shifted constant piece is $h(t - \tau) = 3$.

$$
\int_0^t (2\tau)(3)\,d\tau
$$

Multiply the factors before integrating. The variable of integration is $\tau$, while t stays fixed as the upper bound.

$$
\int_0^t 6\tau\,d\tau
$$

Now integrate with respect to $\tau$ and apply the time-dependent upper and lower bounds.

$$
\int_0^t 6\tau\,d\tau=\left[3\tau^2\right]_0^t
$$

$$
\left[3\tau^2\right]_0^t=3t^2-0=3t^2
$$

So the convolution contribution for this stated time region is

$$
3t^2,\qquad 0\le t\le 1.
$$

<a id="integrating-products-of-two-sloped-segments"></a>
## Integrating Products of Two Sloped Segments

**Example:** Suppose $x(\tau) = \tau$ on $0 \le \tau \le 2$ and $h(t - \tau) = t - \tau$ on $t - 1 \le \tau \le t$. For $0 \le t \le 1$, evaluate the convolution contribution over the overlap.

**Explanation**

The cue is that both overlapping pieces are linear functions of $\tau$. This is like the previous section, except the shifted $h(t - \tau)$ factor is no longer constant.

![](<../Source/7.4.4. Convolution with Sloped Signals/Images/l004-s005-te-section-005.png>)

Bounds: for the stated early time region, the same support intersection gives the overlap interval

$$
0\le \tau\le t,\qquad 0\le t\le 1.
$$

Integrand: on that overlap, use $x(\tau) = \tau$ and $h(t - \tau) = t - \tau$. The regional convolution contribution is therefore

$$
\int_0^t \tau(t-\tau)\,d\tau
$$

Expansion: multiply the two linear factors before integrating. Treat t as fixed because $\tau$ is the integration variable.

$$
\tau(t-\tau)=t\tau-\tau^2
$$

$$
\int_0^t \tau(t-\tau)\,d\tau=\int_0^t (t\tau-\tau^2)\,d\tau
$$

Integration: integrate term by term with respect to $\tau$, then apply the bounds.

$$
\int_0^t (t\tau-\tau^2)\,d\tau=\left[\frac{t\tau^2}{2}-\frac{\tau^3}{3}\right]_0^t
$$

Evaluation: substituting the upper bound $\tau = t$ gives two powers of t that combine into one simplified polynomial term.

$$
\left[\frac{t\tau^2}{2}-\frac{\tau^3}{3}\right]_0^t=\frac{t^3}{2}-\frac{t^3}{3}=\frac{t^3}{6}
$$

So the convolution contribution for this stated time region is

$$
\frac{t^3}{6},\qquad 0\le t\le 1.
$$

<a id="assembling-the-piecewise-result-for-sloped-convolution"></a>
## Assembling the Piecewise Result for Sloped Convolution

**Example:** For a finite-duration sloped convolution, the regional calculations have already produced these results: no overlap for $t < 0$, contribution $t^3$/6 for $0 \le t < 1$, contribution 1/6 for $1 \le t < 2$, contribution (3 - t)^3/6 for $2 \le t \le 3$, and no overlap for $t > 3$. Assemble the complete piecewise output $y(t)$.

**Explanation**

The cue is that the hard regional work has already been done. This step is not another integration step; it is the bookkeeping step that turns region contributions into one output signal $y(t)$.

![](<../Source/7.4.4. Convolution with Sloped Signals/Images/l004-s006-te-section-006.png>)

List the regions in increasing order of t, including the no-overlap regions outside the total support.

$$
t<0,\qquad 0\le t<1,\qquad 1\le t<2,\qquad 2\le t\le 3,\qquad t>3
$$

Now attach each evaluated contribution to its matching interval. The exterior no-overlap regions become zero pieces.

$$
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{time region} & \text{output piece} \\
\hline
t<0 & 0 \\
0\le t<1 & \dfrac{t^3}{6} \\
1\le t<2 & \dfrac{1}{6} \\
2\le t\le 3 & \dfrac{(3-t)^3}{6} \\
t>3 & 0
\end{array}
$$

Check the assembly before writing the final answer: the intervals are ordered, every active contribution appears once, and the zero regions outside the support are included.

Therefore, the complete piecewise convolution output is

$$
y(t)=\begin{cases}
0, & t<0,\\
\dfrac{t^3}{6}, & 0\le t<1,\\
\dfrac{1}{6}, & 1\le t<2,\\
\dfrac{(3-t)^3}{6}, & 2\le t\le 3,\\
0, & t>3.
\end{cases}
$$
