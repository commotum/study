## Introduction to Equal-Exponent Exponential Convolution

The equal-exponent case occurs when both one-sided exponentials have the same decay rate. In the base case, use $x(t)=e^{-at}u(t)$ and $h(t)=e^{-at}u(t)$ with $a>0$. The action is to use the usual causal overlap interval, then simplify the exponential product before integrating.

$$
(x*h)(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x(\tau)h(t-\tau)\,d\tau
$$

Rewrite both signals in the convolution variable. The first unit step requires $\tau\ge 0$, and the shifted unit step requires $t-\tau\ge 0$, or $\tau\le t$. So the product is nonzero only on $0\le \tau\le t$ when $t\ge 0$.

$$
x(\tau)=e^{-a\tau}u(\tau),\qquad h(t-\tau)=e^{-a(t-\tau)}u(t-\tau)
$$

$$
u(\tau)u(t-\tau)\neq 0\quad\text{only when}\quad 0\le \tau\le t
$$

The repeated exponent is the key feature. When the exponential parts are multiplied, the $\tau$ terms cancel. For fixed $t$, the remaining factor $e^{-at}$ is constant with respect to $\tau$.

$$
e^{-a\tau}e^{-a(t-\tau)}=e^{-a\tau-at+a\tau}=e^{-at}
$$

After that cancellation, the integral is just a constant-in-$\tau$ expression over an interval of length $t$. The length of the causal overlap supplies the factor $t$, and the final $u(t)$ records that there is no overlap for $t<0$.

$$
(x*h)(t)=u(t)\int_0^t e^{-at}\,d\tau=te^{-at}u(t)
$$

So the pattern to recognize is: same causal exponent, same causal overlap, cancellation of $\tau$, then a ramp-weighted exponential output.

## Setting Up the Repeated-Exponent Causal Integral

**Example:** Set up, but do not evaluate, the convolution integral for $x(t)=e^{-2t}u(t)$ and $h(t)=e^{-2t}u(t)$.

**Explanation**

The cue is that both signals are one-sided exponentials with the same decay rate. For this section, the task is only to set up the causal convolution integral, so keep the exponential product unsimplified and do not evaluate the integral.

$$
(x*h)(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x(\tau)h(t-\tau)\,d\tau
$$

Rewrite each signal in the argument it has inside the convolution integral. The first signal uses $\tau$, and the shifted second signal uses $t-\tau$.

$$
x(\tau)=e^{-2\tau}u(\tau),\qquad h(t-\tau)=e^{-2(t-\tau)}u(t-\tau)
$$

Now use the unit-step factors to find the overlap. The factor $u(\tau)$ requires $\tau\ge 0$, and the factor $u(t-\tau)$ requires $t-\tau\ge 0$, or $\tau\le t$. These conditions overlap on $0\le \tau\le t$ when $t\ge 0$.

$$
0\le \tau\le t\qquad (t\ge 0)
$$

Replace the infinite limits with the causal overlap bounds and attach $u(t)$ to record that there is no overlap for $t<0$. This is the setup only; the next step would simplify and evaluate it.

$$
(x*h)(t)=u(t)\int_0^t e^{-2\tau}e^{-2(t-\tau)}\,d\tau
$$

## Deriving the Ramp-Weighted Exponential Output

**Example:** Compute $(e^{-2t}u(t))*(e^{-2t}u(t))$.

**Explanation**

The cue is that the two one-sided exponentials have the same exponent. Start from the bounded causal integral from the setup step, then simplify the repeated-exponent product before integrating.

$$
(x*h)(t)=u(t)\int_0^t e^{-2\tau}e^{-2(t-\tau)}\,d\tau
$$

Now combine the exponents inside the integrand. Because the decay rates match, the $\tau$ terms cancel, leaving a factor that depends on $t$ but not on $\tau$.

$$
e^{-2\tau}e^{-2(t-\tau)}=e^{-2\tau-2t+2\tau}=e^{-2t}
$$

Since $e^{-2t}$ is constant with respect to $\tau$, the integral only multiplies it by the length of the overlap interval. The interval from $0$ to $t$ has length $t$.

$$
(x*h)(t)=u(t)\int_0^t e^{-2t}\,d\tau=u(t)e^{-2t}\big[\tau\big]_0^t=t e^{-2t}u(t)
$$

So the repeated-exponent convolution produces a ramp-weighted exponential. The factor $t$ comes from the overlap length, and $u(t)$ records that the output is zero before the causal overlap begins.

## Including Scale Factors in the Equal-Exponent Result

**Example:** Compute the convolution of $x(t)=4e^{-2t}u(t)$ and $h(t)=3e^{-2t}u(t)$.

**Explanation**

The cue is that the two signals still have the same exponent, but now each has a leading scale factor. Use the same equal-exponent setup and carry the constants through the integral.

$$
(x*h)(t)=u(t)\int_0^t \left(4e^{-2\tau}\right)\left(3e^{-2(t-\tau)}\right)\,d\tau
$$

Multiply the leading scale factors first. The constants $4$ and $3$ produce the coefficient $12$, while the exponential part keeps the same repeated-exponent structure as before.

$$
\left(4e^{-2\tau}\right)\left(3e^{-2(t-\tau)}\right)=12e^{-2\tau}e^{-2(t-\tau)}
$$

Now apply the repeated-exponent cancellation. The $\tau$ terms in the exponent cancel, so the integrand becomes constant with respect to $\tau$.

$$
12e^{-2\tau}e^{-2(t-\tau)}=12e^{-2\tau-2t+2\tau}=12e^{-2t}
$$

The remaining integral multiplies $12e^{-2t}$ by the overlap length $t$. Attach $u(t)$ to keep the zero-output region for $t<0$.

$$
(x*h)(t)=u(t)\int_0^t 12e^{-2t}\,d\tau=12t e^{-2t}u(t)
$$

So scale factors do not change the equal-exponent pattern; they multiply the ramp-weighted exponential result.
