## Introduction to Periodic Signals as Sums of Sinusoids

A periodic continuous-time signal can be read as a weighted sum of sinusoidal harmonic components organized around a fundamental frequency. The constant part is the DC term, and the oscillating parts sit at frequencies $k\omega_0$ with weights that tell how much of each harmonic is present.

$$
x(t)=A_0+A_1\cos(\omega_0 t)+B_1\sin(\omega_0 t)
$$

This is the smallest usable case: one constant offset plus the first harmonic at $\omega_0$. Read the constant term as the DC component and read the sinusoidal terms as the first weighted harmonic contribution.

$$
x(t)=A_0+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left[A_k\cos(k\omega_0 t)+B_k\sin(k\omega_0 t)\right]
$$

Higher harmonics follow the same pattern at $2\omega_0$, $3\omega_0$, and beyond. The next sections will use this lens to identify one component, read its weight, and then interpret a full harmonic sum as one periodic waveform.

## Identifying One Sinusoidal Component in a Periodic Signal

**Example:** Given $x(t)=3+2\cos(\omega_0 t)-\sin(2\omega_0 t)$, identify one sinusoidal component and state which harmonic it is.

**Explanation**

A single sinusoidal component is identified by its frequency $k\omega_0$, not by its coefficient alone. In a harmonic-sum description, the DC term sits separately and each sinusoid is tagged by its harmonic frequency.

$$
x(t)=A_0+A_1\cos(\omega_0 t)+B_1\sin(\omega_0 t)+A_2\cos(2\omega_0 t)
$$

Now apply that rule to $x(t)=3+2\cos(\omega_0 t)-\sin(2\omega_0 t)$. The DC term is $3$, so the first harmonic sinusoidal component is $2\cos(\omega_0 t)$.

$$
x(t)=3+2\cos(\omega_0 t)-\sin(2\omega_0 t)
$$

That component has harmonic index $k=1$, so its frequency is $\omega_0$. The coefficient $2$ is the weight on that harmonic, while the term $-\sin(2\omega_0 t)$ is the second harmonic at $2\omega_0$. Read the frequency first, then name the term it identifies.

## Interpreting Weights on Sinusoidal Components

**Example:** For $x(t)=1+\tfrac{3}{2}\cos(\omega_0 t)-\tfrac{1}{2}\sin(2\omega_0 t)+4\cos(3\omega_0 t)$, explain what the weight on each sinusoidal component says about its contribution.

**Explanation**

In a harmonic-sum description, each coefficient is the weight on its sinusoidal component. Compare the weights by magnitude first; the sign can change orientation or phase, but it does not change the fact that the term is present.

$$
x(t)=A_0+A_1\cos(\omega_0 t)+B_1\sin(2\omega_0 t)+A_3\cos(3\omega_0 t)
$$

Now apply that rule to $x(t)=1+\tfrac{3}{2}\cos(\omega_0 t)-\tfrac{1}{2}\sin(2\omega_0 t)+4\cos(3\omega_0 t)$. The weights have magnitudes $\tfrac{3}{2}$, $\tfrac{1}{2}$, and $4$, so the $3\omega_0$ term is the strongest contribution.

$$
x(t)=1+\tfrac{3}{2}\cos(\omega_0 t)-\tfrac{1}{2}\sin(2\omega_0 t)+4\cos(3\omega_0 t)
$$

The coefficient $4$ is the largest magnitude, so $4\cos(3\omega_0 t)$ contributes most strongly. The minus sign on $-\tfrac{1}{2}\sin(2\omega_0 t)$ changes the term's orientation, but it is still a smaller contribution than the $3\omega_0$ harmonic. Read coefficient magnitude as weight and keep the harmonic frequency fixed while comparing terms.

<a id="interpreting-a-sum-of-harmonic-sinusoids"></a>
## Interpreting a Sum of Harmonic Sinusoids

**Example:** Given $x(t)=2+\cos(\omega_0 t)-\tfrac{1}{2}\sin(\omega_0 t)+\tfrac{3}{4}\cos(2\omega_0 t)$, interpret the signal as a sum of harmonic sinusoids and identify which frequencies appear.

**Explanation**

Start by reading the whole expression as one periodic signal. The constant term $2$ is the DC part, and the remaining terms sit at harmonic frequencies of the same fundamental spacing.

$$
x(t)=A_0+A_1\cos(\omega_0 t)+B_1\sin(\omega_0 t)+A_2\cos(2\omega_0 t)+B_2\sin(2\omega_0 t)
$$

Now apply that grouping to $x(t)=2+\cos(\omega_0 t)-\tfrac{1}{2}\sin(\omega_0 t)+\tfrac{3}{4}\cos(2\omega_0 t)$. The terms at $\omega_0$ are $\cos(\omega_0 t)$ and $-\tfrac{1}{2}\sin(\omega_0 t)$, and the next harmonic is $\tfrac{3}{4}\cos(2\omega_0 t)$.

$$
x(t)=2+\cos(\omega_0 t)-\tfrac{1}{2}\sin(\omega_0 t)+\tfrac{3}{4}\cos(2\omega_0 t)
$$

So the signal contains frequency components at $0$, $\omega_0$, and $2\omega_0$. Read the full sum as one periodic waveform built from the DC term and integer multiples of the fundamental frequency.

## Relating a Sinusoidal Sum to the Periodic Waveform

**Example:** Given a harmonic sum such as $x(t)=1+2\cos(\omega_0 t)-\tfrac{1}{2}\sin(2\omega_0 t)$, describe the waveform features implied by the DC term and the harmonic weights.

**Explanation**

Start by reading the sum as the waveform description itself. The constant term $1$ sets the vertical offset, and the largest harmonic term usually controls the main oscillation you notice first.

$$
x(t)=A_0+A_1\cos(\omega_0 t)+B_2\sin(2\omega_0 t)
$$

Now apply that rule to $x(t)=1+2\cos(\omega_0 t)-\tfrac{1}{2}\sin(2\omega_0 t)$. The waveform is centered at $1$, the $2\cos(\omega_0 t)$ term gives the strongest up-and-down motion, and the $2\omega_0$ term adds a smaller distortion to the shape.

$$
x(t)=1+2\cos(\omega_0 t)-\tfrac{1}{2}\sin(2\omega_0 t)
$$

So the signal is one periodic waveform, not separate pieces. It repeats with the same fundamental spacing, but the second harmonic makes the shape less purely sinusoidal than $2\cos(\omega_0 t)$ alone.
