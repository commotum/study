## Introduction to Sine and Cosine Basis Functions

This step is about the basis-function view of the trigonometric Fourier series, not about computing coefficients. The objects to recognize are the sine and cosine waves at integer multiples of the fundamental angular frequency $\omega_0$; the coefficients that appear later are weights, not basis functions.

$$
\cos(k\omega_0 t),\ \sin(k\omega_0 t),\quad k=1,2,3,\dots
$$

Start with the first harmonic pair, $\cos(\omega_0 t)$ and $\sin(\omega_0 t)$. Once you see the $k=1$ case, the same pattern extends to every harmonic by substituting $k=2,3,\dots$ into the same sine and cosine arguments.

$$
\cos(\omega_0 t),\ \sin(\omega_0 t)
$$

The key action is to separate the basis waves from the coefficients. The basis functions tell you which trigonometric waves are available; the coefficients only tell you how much of each wave is present.

$$
x(t)=\frac{a_0}{2}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left[a_k\cos(k\omega_0 t)+b_k\sin(k\omega_0 t)\right]
$$

So when you read a trigonometric Fourier series, identify each harmonic pair by its integer multiple of $\omega_0$, then treat the coefficients as the weights that build the periodic signal from those basis functions.

## Identifying the Fundamental Sine and Cosine Basis Pair

**Example:** For a periodic signal with fundamental angular frequency $\omega_0$, identify the sine and cosine basis functions that make up the first harmonic pair.

**Explanation**

The cue here is the first harmonic pair, so read the harmonic index as $k=1$. In the trigonometric Fourier-series basis view, each harmonic uses the same two families: the cosine and sine terms at $k\omega_0$.

$$
\cos(k\omega_0 t),\ \sin(k\omega_0 t),\quad k=1,2,3,\dots
$$

Now substitute $k=1$ into both families. That gives the basis functions for the fundamental harmonic.

$$
\cos(\omega_0 t),\ \sin(\omega_0 t)
$$

So the fundamental sine and cosine basis pair is $\cos(\omega_0 t)$ and $\sin(\omega_0 t)$. These are the basis waves themselves; the coefficients that appear later are only the weights on these two functions.

## Identifying Sine and Cosine Basis Functions at Higher Harmonics

**Example:** Identify the sine and cosine basis functions for the $k=3$ harmonic of a signal with fundamental angular frequency $\omega_0$.

**Explanation**

The cue is the harmonic index $k=3$. Keep the same basis-function pattern from the earlier example and substitute $k=3$ into the cosine and sine families.

$$
\cos(k\omega_0 t),\ \sin(k\omega_0 t)
$$

Now replace $k$ with $3$ in both arguments. The harmonic number changes the frequency multiplier, but it does not change the basis-function pattern.

$$
\cos(3\omega_0 t),\ \sin(3\omega_0 t)
$$

So the third-harmonic basis pair is $\cos(3\omega_0 t)$ and $\sin(3\omega_0 t)$. These are the basis waves before any coefficients are applied.

## Recognizing Weights on Harmonic Sine and Cosine Basis Functions

**Example:** Given the terms $4\cos(2\omega_0 t)-7\sin(2\omega_0 t)$, identify the weights on the $k=2$ sine and cosine basis functions.

**Explanation**

The cue is the harmonic index $k=2$. Read each weight from the number in front of the matching basis function and keep the sign attached to that coefficient.

$$
4\cos(2\omega_0 t)-7\sin(2\omega_0 t)
$$

The cosine term carries the weight $a_2=4$. The sine term carries the weight $b_2=-7$, so the sign stays with the sine coefficient.

$$
a_2=4,\quad b_2=-7
$$

So the coefficient pair is $(a_2,b_2)=(4,-7)$. The basis pair remains the $k=2$ harmonic; only the weights are being read off.

<a id="interpreting-a-periodic-signal-as-a-weighted-sum-of-harmonic-basis-functions"></a>
## Interpreting a Periodic Signal as a Weighted Sum of Harmonic Basis Functions

**Example:** Interpret the signal $x(t)=\frac{a_0}{2}+a_1\cos(\omega_0 t)+b_1\sin(\omega_0 t)+a_2\cos(2\omega_0 t)+b_2\sin(2\omega_0 t)$ as a weighted sum of harmonic basis functions.

**Explanation**

The cue is the expanded trigonometric Fourier series. Start by separating the DC term from the harmonic terms, then group each cosine and sine pair by its shared harmonic index.

$$
x(t)=\frac{a_0}{2}+a_1\cos(\omega_0 t)+b_1\sin(\omega_0 t)+a_2\cos(2\omega_0 t)+b_2\sin(2\omega_0 t)
$$

The $k=1$ cosine and sine terms form one weighted basis pair, and the $k=2$ cosine and sine terms form the next weighted basis pair. The coefficients stay attached to the correct function and harmonic index.

$$
x(t)=\frac{a_0}{2}+\big[a_1\cos(\omega_0 t)+b_1\sin(\omega_0 t)\big]+\big[a_2\cos(2\omega_0 t)+b_2\sin(2\omega_0 t)\big]
$$

So the periodic signal is the DC term plus a sum of weighted harmonic basis pairs. This is the reusable basis-function view: the sine and cosine terms are the basis functions, and the coefficients are their weights.
