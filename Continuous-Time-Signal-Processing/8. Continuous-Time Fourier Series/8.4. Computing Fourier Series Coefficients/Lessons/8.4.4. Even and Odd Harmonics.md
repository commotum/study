## Introduction to Even and Odd Harmonics

When a Fourier-series coefficient formula contains $(-1)^k$, test the parity of $k$ before you simplify anything else. Write even harmonics as $k = 2m$ and odd harmonics as $k = 2m + 1$, and keep $k = 0$ separate as the DC term.

$$
\begin{aligned}(-1)^{2m} &= 1 \\ (-1)^{2m + 1} &= -1\end{aligned}
$$

That parity split is what makes cancellation visible in coefficient formulas. A factor like $1 + (-1)^k$ survives on even indices and vanishes on odd indices, while $1 - (-1)^k$ does the opposite.

$$
\begin{aligned}1 + (-1)^{2m} &= 2 \\ 1 + (-1)^{2m + 1} &= 0 \\ 1 - (-1)^{2m} &= 0 \\ 1 - (-1)^{2m + 1} &= 2\end{aligned}
$$

So the operational test is simple: inspect the coefficient formula, substitute the even form $k = 2m$ and the odd form $k = 2m + 1$, simplify the parity factor, and list the harmonic indices whose coefficients stay nonzero.

## Identifying Even and Odd Harmonic Indices

**Example:** Given the indices $k = 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6$, identify the even harmonic indices, the odd harmonic indices, and the DC term.

**Explanation**

Start with the index list $k = 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6$. The task here is classification: decide which indices are even, which are odd, and keep $k = 0$ separate as the DC term.

$$
\begin{aligned}k = 0 &\text{ is the DC term} \\ k = 2, 4, 6 &\text{ are the even harmonic indices} \\ k = 1, 3, 5 &\text{ are the odd harmonic indices}\end{aligned}
$$

So the even harmonic set is $\{2, 4, 6\}$ and the odd harmonic set is $\{1, 3, 5\}$. That bookkeeping is the piece you will reuse later when a coefficient formula tells you which parity survives and which parity cancels.

## Detecting Even-Harmonic Cancellations

**Example:** Given $c_k = A(1 - (-1)^k)$ for positive $k$, determine which harmonic indices are zero and which survive.

**Explanation**

The cue is the factor $1 - (-1)^k$ inside the coefficient formula. Test it on even and odd positive indices, then read off which parity class makes the factor vanish.

$$
\begin{aligned}k = 2m &\Rightarrow (-1)^k = 1 \Rightarrow 1 - (-1)^k = 0 \\ k = 2m + 1 &\Rightarrow (-1)^k = -1 \Rightarrow 1 - (-1)^k = 2\end{aligned}
$$

So $c_{2m} = 0$ for every even harmonic index, while $c_{2m+1} = 2A$ for every odd harmonic index. The surviving set is the odd harmonics.

## Detecting Odd-Harmonic Cancellations

**Example:** Given $c_k = A(1 + (-1)^k)$ for positive $k$, determine which harmonic indices are zero and which survive.

**Explanation**

The cue is the factor $1 + (-1)^k$ inside the coefficient formula. Test it on even and odd positive indices, then read off which parity class makes the factor vanish. The only change from the previous case is the sign, and that swaps the surviving family.

$$
\begin{aligned}k = 2m &\Rightarrow (-1)^k = 1 \Rightarrow 1 + (-1)^k = 2 \\ k = 2m + 1 &\Rightarrow (-1)^k = -1 \Rightarrow 1 + (-1)^k = 0\end{aligned}
$$

So $c_{2m+1} = 0$ for every odd harmonic index, while $c_{2m} = 2A$ for every even harmonic index. The surviving set is the even harmonics.

<a id="listing-nonzero-harmonics-from-a-coefficient-formula"></a>
## Listing Nonzero Harmonics from a Coefficient Formula

**Example:** Given $c_k = A(1 - (-1)^k) + B(1 + (-1)^k)$ for positive $k$, determine which harmonic families are nonzero and list the surviving even and odd harmonics.

**Explanation**

The cue is the full coefficient formula with one factor that cancels even harmonics and one factor that cancels odd harmonics. Test the even and odd positive indices separately, then name every parity class that survives.

$$
\begin{aligned}c_{2m} &= A(1 - (-1)^{2m}) + B(1 + (-1)^{2m}) = A(1 - 1) + B(1 + 1) = 2B \\ c_{2m+1} &= A(1 - (-1)^{2m+1}) + B(1 + (-1)^{2m+1}) = A(1 - (-1)) + B(1 + (-1)) = 2A\end{aligned}
$$

So neither parity class cancels completely. The even harmonics survive through the $B$ term, and the odd harmonics survive through the $A$ term, so the nonzero harmonic families are both even and odd.

    $$

    Since both $c_{2m}$ and $c_{2m+1}$ are nonzero, both even and odd harmonic families survive.
