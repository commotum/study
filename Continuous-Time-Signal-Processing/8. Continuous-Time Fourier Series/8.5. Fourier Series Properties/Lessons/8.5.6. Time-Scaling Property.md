## Introduction to Time-Scaling Property

When a periodic signal is written as $y(t)=x(at)$, the key object to track is the scale factor $a$ acting on the original signal $x(t)$. Start from $x(t)$ with fundamental period $T_0$ and Fourier coefficients $c_k$.

$$
T_y=\frac{T_0}{|a|}
$$

This means time compression or expansion changes the fundamental period but does not create a new coefficient sequence. The new fundamental angular frequency follows the same rescaling on the frequency grid, so a smaller period corresponds to a larger angular frequency and a larger period corresponds to a smaller angular frequency.

$$
\omega_y=|a|\omega_0
$$

For $a>0$, the harmonic index $k$ keeps the same coefficient $c_k$ on the new frequency grid. The values stay in the same order; only the period and angular spacing change.

For $a<0$, the sign of $a$ adds time reversal, so the coefficient at harmonic index $k$ becomes $c_{-k}$. The period and angular frequency still use $|a|$, but the index direction flips because the signal has been reversed in time.

So the rule to remember is: time scaling changes the period and fundamental angular frequency, while positive scaling reuses the same coefficient sequence and negative scaling reverses the harmonic indices.

## Finding the New Period After Time Scaling

**Example:** If $x(t)$ has fundamental period $T_0=12$, find the fundamental period of $y(t)=x(3t)$.

**Explanation**

The cue is a periodic signal written as $y(t)=x(at)$. Here the scaling factor is $a=3$, so the goal is to apply the period rule from the introduction rather than recompute anything from scratch.

$$
T_y=\frac{T_0}{|a|}
$$

Now substitute the original period and the scale factor: $T_y=12/|3|$. Since $|3|=3$, the new signal is compressed in time by a factor of $3$, so its period must shrink by the same factor.

$$
T_y=\frac{12}{3}=4
$$

So the fundamental period of $y(t)=x(3t)$ is $4$. The result stays positive because period depends on $|a|$, even though the signal itself has been time-compressed.

## Finding the New Fundamental Angular Frequency After Time Scaling

**Example:** If $x(t)$ has fundamental angular frequency $\omega_0=5$ rad/s, find the fundamental angular frequency of $y(t)=x(2t)$.

**Explanation**

The cue is still the time-scaling form $y(t)=x(at)$, but now we read the frequency side of the property instead of the period side. For $y(t)=x(2t)$, the scaling factor is $a=2$ and the original fundamental angular frequency is $\omega_0=5$ rad/s.

$$
\omega_y=|a|\omega_0
$$

Now substitute the values: $\omega_y=|2|(5)$. Because $|2|=2$, the frequency grid stretches by a factor of $2$ when the signal is compressed in time, so the fundamental angular frequency doubles.

$$
\omega_y=|2|(5)=10\,\text{rad/s}
$$

So the new fundamental angular frequency is $10$ rad/s. This is the frequency-side counterpart of the shorter period from the previous example: time compression shrinks the period and increases the fundamental angular frequency by the same factor.

## Reusing Coefficients for Positive Time Scaling

**Example:** Let $x(t)$ have CTFS coefficients $c_k$ with fundamental angular frequency $\omega_0$. For $y(t)=x(2t)$, identify $d_k$ and the new fundamental angular frequency.

**Explanation**

The cue is a positive time scaling in CTFS form. For $y(t)=x(2t)$, the scaling factor is $a=2$, so we keep the original coefficient sequence but read it on a new frequency grid with doubled spacing.

$$
x(2t)=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty} c_k e^{jk\omega_0(2t)}
$$

Rewrite the exponent as $jk(2\omega_0)t$. That identifies the new fundamental angular frequency as $2\omega_0$, which is exactly what positive time compression should do to the harmonic spacing.

$$
y(t)=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty} c_k e^{jk(2\omega_0)t}
$$

Reading this against the standard CTFS form shows that $d_k=c_k$ for every harmonic index $k$ and $\omega_y=2\omega_0$. Positive scaling changes the frequency grid, not the coefficient values.

## Applying Index Reversal for Negative Time Scaling

**Example:** Let $x(t)$ have coefficients $c_k$. For $y(t)=x(-2t)$, state the new fundamental angular frequency and the coefficient rule for $d_k$.

**Explanation**

The cue is the same time-scaling form, but the negative sign means the frequency-grid update must be paired with time reversal. For $y(t)=x(-2t)$, separate the operation into scaling by $2$ and reversing time.

$$
x(-2t)=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty} c_k e^{jk\omega_0(-2t)}
$$

Use $|a|$ for the frequency change, so the new fundamental angular frequency is $2\omega_0$. The negative sign does not change the size of the frequency step; it only flips the index direction.

$$
y(t)=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty} c_{-k} e^{jk(2\omega_0)t}
$$

So the new fundamental angular frequency is $2\omega_0$ and the coefficient rule is $d_k=c_{-k}$. Negative scaling changes the index direction, not the coefficient values themselves.
