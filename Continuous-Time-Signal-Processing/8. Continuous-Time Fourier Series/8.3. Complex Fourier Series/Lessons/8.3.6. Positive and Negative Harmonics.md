## Introduction to Positive and Negative Harmonics

Read the complex Fourier series by integer harmonic index. $k=0$ is the DC term $C_0$, and every nonzero harmonic is read as a $+k$ / $-k$ pair.

$$
x(t)=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty} C_k e^{jk\omega_0 t}
$$

Start with the simplest nonzero harmonic, $k=1$. Read $C_1e^{j\omega_0 t}$ and $C_{-1}e^{-j\omega_0 t}$ as one paired contribution for the first harmonic.

$$
C_1e^{j\omega_0 t}+C_{-1}e^{-j\omega_0 t}
$$

The same pairing rule holds for every $k>0$. The positive and negative indices are two contributions to one nonzero harmonic, not two separate harmonics.

$$
x(t)=C_0+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\big(C_k e^{jk\omega_0 t}+C_{-k}e^{-jk\omega_0 t}\big)
$$

Keep $C_0$ separate as the unpaired DC term. That cleanly separates the DC component from all nonzero harmonics in later examples.

## Identifying Positive and Negative Harmonic Indices

**Example:** Given $x(t)=C_0+C_2e^{j2\omega_0 t}+C_{-2}e^{-j2\omega_0 t}$, identify which term is DC, which term is positive index, and which term is negative index.

**Explanation**

The cue is the signed harmonic index attached to each term. Read the sign on the index to decide whether the term is zero, positive, or negative.

$$
x(t)=C_0+C_2e^{j2\omega_0 t}+C_{-2}e^{-j2\omega_0 t}
$$

$C_0$ is the zero-index DC term. $C_2e^{j2\omega_0 t}$ is the $+2$ harmonic term, and $C_{-2}e^{-j2\omega_0 t}$ is the $-2$ harmonic term.

$$
C_0 \rightarrow 0,\qquad C_2e^{j2\omega_0 t} \rightarrow +2,\qquad C_{-2}e^{-j2\omega_0 t} \rightarrow -2
$$

So the signed indices are $0$, $+2$, and $-2$. The sign on the harmonic index, not the coefficient name alone, is what classifies each term.

<a id="matching-the-positive-and-negative-terms-for-the-same-harmonic"></a>
## Matching the Positive and Negative Terms for the Same Harmonic

**Example:** From the terms $C_1e^{j\omega_0 t}$, $C_{-1}e^{-j\omega_0 t}$, $C_2e^{j2\omega_0 t}$, and $C_{-2}e^{-j2\omega_0 t}$, match the positive and negative terms that belong to the same harmonic.

**Explanation**

The cue is the absolute harmonic index. Terms match only when the magnitude is the same and the signs are opposite.

$$
C_1e^{j\omega_0 t},\qquad C_{-1}e^{-j\omega_0 t},\qquad C_2e^{j2\omega_0 t},\qquad C_{-2}e^{-j2\omega_0 t}
$$

Match $C_1e^{j\omega_0 t}$ with $C_{-1}e^{-j\omega_0 t}$, and match $C_2e^{j2\omega_0 t}$ with $C_{-2}e^{-j2\omega_0 t}$. Each pair uses the same harmonic number with opposite signs.

$$
\big(C_1e^{j\omega_0 t},\,C_{-1}e^{-j\omega_0 t}\big),\qquad \big(C_2e^{j2\omega_0 t},\,C_{-2}e^{-j2\omega_0 t}\big)
$$

So the answer is two matched harmonic pairs: one pair for harmonic number $1$ and one pair for harmonic number $2$. Nothing else is left unpaired in this example.

<a id="interpreting-a-harmonic-pair-as-one-nonzero-frequency-component"></a>
## Interpreting a Harmonic Pair as One Nonzero Frequency Component

**Example:** Interpret the pair $C_4e^{j4\omega_0 t}+C_{-4}e^{-j4\omega_0 t}$ as one nonzero harmonic component and state what harmonic number it represents.

**Explanation**

The cue is the matched $+4$ / $-4$ pair. Read the two terms together as one nonzero harmonic component, not as separate harmonics.

$$
C_4e^{j4\omega_0 t}+C_{-4}e^{-j4\omega_0 t}
$$

This is the $k=4$ harmonic, so the frequency magnitude is $4\omega_0$.

$$
k=4,\qquad \omega=4\omega_0
$$

So the pair names one nonzero frequency component at $4\omega_0$, and the two coefficients together describe that single component.

<a id="separating-nonzero-harmonic-pairs-from-the-dc-term"></a>
## Separating Nonzero Harmonic Pairs from the DC Term

**Example:** Separate the DC term from the nonzero harmonic pairs in $x(t)=C_0+C_1e^{j\omega_0 t}+C_{-1}e^{-j\omega_0 t}+C_2e^{j2\omega_0 t}+C_{-2}e^{-j2\omega_0 t}$.

**Explanation**

The cue is the unpaired $C_0$ term. Everything with a $+k$ / $-k$ index belongs to a nonzero harmonic pair, while $C_0$ is the zero-frequency DC term.

$$
x(t)=C_0+C_1e^{j\omega_0 t}+C_{-1}e^{-j\omega_0 t}+C_2e^{j2\omega_0 t}+C_{-2}e^{-j2\omega_0 t}
$$

So isolate $C_0$ first, then group the $+1$ and $-1$ terms together and the $+2$ and $-2$ terms together.

$$
x(t)=C_0+\big(C_1e^{j\omega_0 t}+C_{-1}e^{-j\omega_0 t}\big)+\big(C_2e^{j2\omega_0 t}+C_{-2}e^{-j2\omega_0 t}\big)
$$

That leaves one DC term and two nonzero harmonic pairs, one for $k=1$ and one for $k=2$. The DC term stays separate from every nonzero harmonic pair.
