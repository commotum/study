## Introduction to Complex-Exponential Fourier Series Form

A periodic continuous-time signal can be written as a sum of complex exponentials at integer multiples of the fundamental angular frequency $\omega_0$.

In this notation, $C_k$ is the complex coefficient on the $k$th harmonic, $k$ is the integer label of that harmonic, and $\omega_0$ sets the spacing between harmonics. A simple starting picture is a signal with a DC term and one positive/negative harmonic pair.

$$
x(t)=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty} C_k e^{jk\omega_0 t}
$$

Because $k$ runs over all integers, $k=0$ gives the DC term, while positive and negative values of $k$ give the matching $+k$ and $-k$ harmonic terms.

$$
x(t)=C_0+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\big(C_k e^{jk\omega_0 t}+C_{-k} e^{-jk\omega_0 t}\big)
$$

That grouped form makes the structure easy to read: $C_0$ stands alone, and each nonzero harmonic appears as a matched pair of complex exponentials with coefficients $C_k$ and $C_{-k}$.

Use this complex-exponential Fourier series form as the reusable template throughout the module: one coefficient per harmonic, one integer index per harmonic, and one fundamental frequency that sets the harmonic spacing.

## Writing a Periodic Signal as a Complex Harmonic Sum

**Example:** Given the explicit harmonic terms $C_{-2} e^{-j2\omega_0 t}$, $C_{-1} e^{-j\omega_0 t}$, $C_0$, $C_1 e^{j\omega_0 t}$, and $C_2 e^{j2\omega_0 t}$, write the signal in compact complex-exponential Fourier series form.

**Explanation**

The cue is an explicit list of harmonic terms. Start by recognizing the DC term $C_0$ and the matching positive/negative harmonic pairs that already follow the $C_k e^{jk\omega_0 t}$ pattern.

$$
x(t)=C_{-2} e^{-j2\omega_0 t}+C_{-1} e^{-j\omega_0 t}+C_0+C_1 e^{j\omega_0 t}+C_2 e^{j2\omega_0 t}
$$

Read $C_0$ as the $k=0$ contribution, then group the $k=1$ and $k=2$ pairs by their integer indices. The only change is the notation: the same harmonic content is being written in compact sum form.

$$
x(t)=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty} C_k e^{jk\omega_0 t}
$$

This compact form says one coefficient weights one harmonic, and the integer index $k$ tells you which harmonic each coefficient belongs to. That is the reusable complex-exponential Fourier series form.

<a id="substituting-the-fundamental-frequency-into-the-series-form"></a>
## Substituting the Fundamental Frequency into the Series Form

**Example:** For a periodic signal with period $T_0=4$, substitute $\omega_0=\pi/2$ into $x(t)=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty} C_k e^{jk\omega_0 t}$.

**Explanation**

The cue is the stated period $T_0=4$. That fixes the fundamental angular frequency at $\omega_0=\frac{2\pi}{T_0}=\frac{\pi}{2}$, and the only new move is to replace $\omega_0$ with that value while keeping the harmonic pattern unchanged.

$$
\omega_0=\frac{2\pi}{T_0}=\frac{2\pi}{4}=\frac{\pi}{2}
$$

Now substitute $\omega_0=\pi/2$ everywhere it appears in the series. Leave the coefficient labels $C_k$ and the integer index range exactly as they are.

$$
x(t)=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty} C_k e^{jk(\pi/2)t}
$$

The structure is still the same complex-exponential Fourier series: one coefficient per harmonic, one integer index per harmonic, and the exponent now uses the concrete frequency $\pi/2$ instead of a symbolic $\omega_0$.

## Expanding the Sum into Indexed Harmonic Terms

**Example:** Expand $\sum_{k=-2}^{2} C_k e^{jk\omega_0 t}$ into explicit harmonic terms.

**Explanation**

The cue is the finite index range from $k=-2$ to $k=2$. Read that as the five harmonic indices $-2,-1,0,1,2$ and keep the coefficient/exponential pattern $C_k e^{jk\omega_0 t}$ unchanged.

Substitute each integer value of $k$ into the same harmonic template. Write the negative-index terms first, include the $k=0$ DC term, and then continue with the positive-index terms in order.

$$
C_{-2}e^{-j2\omega_0 t}+C_{-1}e^{-j\omega_0 t}+C_0+C_1e^{j\omega_0 t}+C_2e^{j2\omega_0 t}
$$

The result makes the harmonic structure explicit: $C_0$ is the DC term, and the negative and positive harmonics appear as matched pairs around it. The index order matches the original bounds, so the expanded form still reflects the same series.

## Identifying the Coefficient Weight on a Harmonic Term

**Example:** Given the term $C_{-3} e^{-j3\omega_0 t}$, identify the coefficient that weights that harmonic.

**Explanation**

The cue is a single harmonic term. Read the exponential first: $e^{-j3\omega_0 t}$ means the harmonic index is $k=-3$.

$$
C_{-3}e^{-j3\omega_0 t}
$$

Match that index to the coefficient subscript. The weighting coefficient uses the same index and the same sign as the harmonic term.

$$
C_{-3}
$$

So the coefficient that weights the term is $C_{-3}$, not the exponential term itself. The coefficient label is the reusable part to read from the series.
