## Introduction to Polar Form of a Phasor Sum

After a same-frequency cosine sum has been collected into a rectangular phasor $Z = a + jb$, the next step is to write it in polar magnitude-angle form so the corresponding sinusoid's amplitude and phase can be read directly from the phasor.

$$
Z = a + jb
$$

The magnitude is the length of the phasor vector in the complex plane, so compute it from the rectangular components with the Pythagorean formula. That magnitude is the amplitude of the resultant sinusoid.

$$
|Z| = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2}
$$

To get the angle, first read a reference angle from the component ratio, then place that angle in the correct quadrant using the signs of $a$ and $b$. Writing $\theta = \operatorname{atan2}(b, a)$ keeps those two steps together.

$$
\theta = \operatorname{atan2}(b, a)
$$

For the clean base case $Z = 4 + 3j$, the phasor lies in the first quadrant, so the magnitude is $5$ and the angle is the first-quadrant angle with $\tan\theta = 3/4$. The polar form is $Z = |Z|\angle\theta$, and later worked examples will vary only the signs and quadrant choice.

$$
Z = |Z|\angle\theta
$$

## Computing the Magnitude of a Rectangular Phasor Sum

**Example:** Find the magnitude of the rectangular phasor sum $Z = (3 + j4) + (1 + j2)$.

**Explanation**

The cue is a rectangular phasor sum, so first add the real parts and the imaginary parts separately. That gives the single rectangular phasor whose magnitude we want.

$$
Z = (3 + j4) + (1 + j2) = 4 + j6
$$

Now the phasor is in rectangular form with $a = 4$ and $b = 6$. Apply the Euclidean norm $|Z| = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2}$ to get the magnitude.

$$
|Z| = \sqrt{4^2 + 6^2} = \sqrt{52} = 2\sqrt{13}
$$

So the magnitude of the rectangular phasor sum is $|Z| = 2\sqrt{13}$. That is the value you carry forward when the phasor is written in polar form.

## Computing the Reference Angle of a Rectangular Phasor Sum

**Example:** From the sum $Z = -4 + 3j$, compute the reference angle.

**Explanation**

The cue is a rectangular phasor with mixed signs, so isolate the acute reference angle from the component ratio. For this step, use absolute values so the sign pattern does not change the angle itself.

$$
Z = -4 + 3j
$$

Here $a = -4$ and $b = 3$, so the acute ratio is $|b|/|a| = 3/4$. Apply the reference-angle rule $\theta_{\text{ref}} = \tan^{-1}(|b|/|a|)$ to get the angle.

$$
\theta_{\text{ref}} = \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)
$$

So the reference angle is $\theta_{\text{ref}} = \tan^{-1}(3/4)$. That acute angle is the value you carry into the later quadrant step.

<a id="choosing-the-correct-quadrant-for-the-phasor-angle"></a>
## Choosing the Correct Quadrant for the Phasor Angle

**Example:** For the rectangular phasor $Z = -5 + 12j$, find the quadrant-adjusted phase angle.

**Explanation**

The cue is a rectangular phasor whose signs already tell you the quadrant. First find the acute reference angle from the component ratio, then apply the quadrant rule to turn it into the principal phase angle.

$$
Z = -5 + 12j
$$

Here $a = -5$ and $b = 12$, so the reference angle is based on the absolute values: $\theta_{\text{ref}} = \tan^{-1}(12/5)$. Because $a < 0$ and $b > 0$, the phasor lies in quadrant II.

$$
\theta_{\text{ref}} = \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{12}{5}\right)
$$

Quadrant II means the principal angle is the supplement of the reference angle, so subtract it from $\pi$. That gives the final phase angle for the phasor.

$$
\theta = \pi - \theta_{\text{ref}} = \pi - \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{12}{5}\right)
$$

So the quadrant-adjusted phase angle is $\theta = \pi - \tan^{-1}(12/5)$. The workflow is always the same: read the signs, get the reference angle, then apply the quadrant correction.

## Writing the Polar Phasor Sum and Reading Its Magnitude and Angle

**Example:** Convert the rectangular phasor sum $Z = 1 - \sqrt{3}j$ into polar form and state the corresponding magnitude and phase.

**Explanation**

The cue is a rectangular phasor sum, so the job is to convert it into polar form and read off both the magnitude and the phase. Use the rectangular components to get the magnitude first, then use the sign pattern to place the angle in the correct quadrant.

$$
Z = 1 - \sqrt{3}j
$$

Here $a = 1$ and $b = -\sqrt{3}$. The magnitude is $|Z| = \sqrt{1^2 + (\sqrt{3})^2} = 2$, and the absolute-value ratio gives the reference angle $\theta_{\text{ref}} = \tan^{-1}(\sqrt{3}/1) = \pi/3$.

$$
|Z| = 2, \qquad \theta_{\text{ref}} = \frac{\pi}{3}
$$

Because $a > 0$ and $b < 0$, the phasor lies in quadrant IV, so the principal phase angle is the negative of the reference angle. That gives the final polar form.

$$
\theta = -\frac{\pi}{3}, \qquad Z = 2ngle -\frac{\pi}{3}
$$

So the phasor is $Z = 2ngle -\pi/3$. The magnitude $2$ is the sinusoid's amplitude, and the angle $-\pi/3$ is its phase.
