## Introduction to Growing Sinusoids

Define $x(t)=e^{st}$ as a continuous-time signal with $s=\sigma+j\omega$ held fixed and $t$ as the variable. In this lesson, we isolate the case $\sigma>0$ so the same formula can be read as a growing sinusoid.

$$
e^{(1+j\omega)t}=e^t e^{j\omega t}
$$

In the simple base case $s=1+j\omega$, the signal already splits into a real exponential factor and an oscillatory complex exponential factor. That is the pattern to keep in view before generalizing to arbitrary $\sigma>0$.

$$
e^{st}=e^{(\sigma+j\omega)t}=e^{\sigma t}e^{j\omega t}
$$

When $\sigma>0$, the factor $e^{\sigma t}$ increases with $t$, so it acts as the envelope of the signal. The factor $e^{j\omega t}$ supplies the oscillation.

$$
|e^{st}|=e^{\sigma t}
$$

That magnitude formula ties the envelope directly to the real part of $s$: $\sigma$ controls the growth, and $\omega$ controls the oscillation rate.

So the classification rule is simple: if $s=\sigma+j\omega$ and $\sigma>0$, then $e^{st}$ is a growing sinusoid with increasing envelope $e^{\sigma t}$ and oscillation rate $\omega$.

## Recognizing Positive $\sigma$ in $s=\sigma+j\omega$

**Example:** Classify $x(t)=e^{(2+j3)t}$ by identifying $\sigma$ and $\omega$ and deciding whether the signal grows, decays, or stays constant.

**Explanation**

Start by reading the exponent in standard form. In $x(t)=e^{(2+j3)t}$, the fixed parameter is $s=2+j3$, so the cue is a positive real part.

$$
s=2+j3=\sigma+j\omega,\qquad \sigma=2,\ \omega=3
$$

Because $\sigma=2>0$, the real part is positive. That means the exponential factor is growing, not decaying or staying constant.

$$
x(t)=e^{(2+j3)t}=e^{2t}e^{j3t}
$$

The factor $e^{2t}$ is the increasing envelope, and $e^{j3t}$ is the oscillatory factor. The number $3$ sets the oscillation rate, while $2$ sets the growth rate.

$$
|x(t)|=e^{2t}
$$

So the signal is a growing sinusoid: positive $\sigma$ gives growth, and $\omega$ still controls the oscillation rate.

## Separating $e^{\sigma t}$ from $e^{j\omega t}$

**Example:** Rewrite $e^{(1.5+j4)t}$ as a product and name which factor controls growth and which factor controls oscillation.

**Explanation**

Start by reading the exponent in standard form. Here $s=1.5+j4$, so the cue is to split the real part from the imaginary part.

$$
s=1.5+j4=\sigma+j\omega,\qquad \sigma=1.5,\ \omega=4
$$

Now apply the split inside $e^{st}$. The real part becomes the growth factor $e^{\sigma t}$, and the imaginary part stays in the oscillatory factor $e^{j\omega t}$.

$$
e^{(1.5+j4)t}=e^{1.5t}e^{j4t}
$$

The factor $e^{1.5t}$ controls the increasing envelope because its magnitude grows with $t$. The factor $e^{j4t}$ controls the oscillation rate.

So the product form makes the roles explicit: $\sigma=1.5$ gives growth, and $\omega=4$ gives oscillation.

## Interpreting $e^{\sigma t}$ as an Increasing Envelope

**Example:** For $x(t)=e^{0.5t}e^{j4t}$, identify the envelope and explain how it changes as $t$ increases.

**Explanation**

For $x(t)=e^{0.5t}e^{j4t}$, match the real exponential factor $e^{\sigma t}$ to the envelope. Here that factor is $e^{0.5t}$.

$$
x(t)=e^{0.5t}e^{j4t}
$$

Here $\sigma=0.5>0$, so the envelope is increasing rather than shrinking or staying constant.

$$
|x(t)|=e^{0.5t}
$$

As $t$ increases, $e^{0.5t}$ gets larger, so the oscillation keeps the same rate but rides inside a widening envelope.

So the envelope is $e^{0.5t}$, and the positive real part is exactly what makes it grow.

## Reading Growth Rate and Oscillation Rate Together

**Example:** For $x(t)=e^{0.75t}e^{j8t}$, state the growth rate, the oscillation rate, and the overall classification.

**Explanation**

Start by reading the exponent in standard form. The cue is that $s=0.75+j8$, so the real part gives growth and the imaginary part gives oscillation.

$$
s=0.75+j8=\sigma+j\omega,\qquad \sigma=0.75,\ \omega=8
$$

Since $\sigma=0.75>0$, the envelope is $e^{0.75t}$ and it increases with time.

$$
|x(t)|=e^{0.75t}
$$

So the growth rate is $0.75$, the oscillation rate is $8$, and the signal is a growing sinusoid with envelope $e^{0.75t}$.
