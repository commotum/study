## Introduction to The Generalized Exponential Signal $e^{st}$

Read $x(t)=e^{st}$ as a continuous-time signal in the variable $t$, with $s$ held fixed as the parameter that sets the signal’s behavior.

$$
x(t)=e^{st}
$$

When $s$ is fixed, the formula no longer names a general algebraic expression. It names one specific signal in time, and changing the value of $s$ changes which signal you get.

Start with the simple real choice $s=-1$. Then the same form becomes the familiar decaying exponential $x(t)=e^{-t}$, which is the cleanest base case for this module.

$$
x(t)=e^{-t}
$$

This is why $e^{st}$ is called the generalized exponential signal: the same template will later cover real exponentials first, then complex choices of $s$, and then the damped or growing sinusoidal cases that follow.

For now, keep the roles separate: $t$ is the variable, $s$ is the fixed parameter, and $e^{st}$ is the signal form that ties the later special cases together.

## Reading $e^{st}$ as a Continuous-Time Signal with Fixed $s$

**Example:** Let $s=-2+j3$. Write the corresponding signal as $x(t)=e^{st}$ and explain what stays fixed and what changes with time.

**Explanation**

The cue is a fixed complex value of $s$. Once $s$ is fixed, $x(t)=e^{st}$ names one specific continuous-time signal in $t$, not a formula with two variables competing for the same role.

$$
x(t)=e^{(-2+j3)t}
$$

Substitute the fixed parameter directly into the exponent: $s=-2+j3$ becomes $x(t)=e^{(-2+j3)t}$. The parameter stays fixed, and $t$ is the variable that traces the signal over time.

So the notation $x(t)=e^{st}$ means a concrete time signal once $s$ is chosen. This is the same generalized exponential form from the previous step, now read with one fixed parameter value instead of left abstract.

## Matching Real Values of $s$ to Real Exponential Signals

**Example:** Let $s=-3$. Match this value to the corresponding signal and classify it as growing, decaying, or constant.

**Explanation**

The cue here is a real value of $s$. Once $s$ is fixed and real, $x(t)=e^{st}$ is a pure real exponential signal in $t$, and the sign of $s$ determines whether it grows, decays, or stays constant.

$$
x(t)=e^{-3t}
$$

Substitute $s=-3$ into $x(t)=e^{st}$ to get $x(t)=e^{-3t}$. The parameter is fixed, and $t$ is the variable that traces the signal over time.

The negative coefficient means the signal decays as $t$ increases, so the matching classification is decaying. If $s=0$, the same form would become $x(t)=e^{0t}=1$, which is the constant case.

## Matching Pure Imaginary Values of $s$ to Complex Exponential Signals

**Example:** Let $s=j4$. Match this value to the corresponding signal and interpret the result as a pure complex exponential.

**Explanation**

The cue here is a pure imaginary value of $s$. When $s$ has no real part, $x(t)=e^{st}$ becomes a complex exponential with constant magnitude and oscillatory phase rather than growth or decay.

$$
x(t)=e^{j4t}
$$

Substitute $s=j4$ into $x(t)=e^{st}$ to get $x(t)=e^{j4t}$. The parameter stays fixed, and $t$ is the variable that traces the oscillation over time.

So $s=j4$ matches the pure complex exponential $x(t)=e^{j4t}$. In Euler-style language, this is a constant-magnitude oscillation with no exponential growth or decay.

<a id="recognizing-complex-values-of-s-as-combined-exponential-and-oscillatory-behavior"></a>
## Recognizing Complex Values of $s$ as Combined Exponential and Oscillatory Behavior

**Example:** Let $s=-2+j5$. Separate $x(t)=e^{st}$ into envelope and oscillation factors and describe the resulting behavior.

**Explanation**

The cue is a complex value of $s$ with both a real part and an imaginary part. Write $s=\sigma+j\omega$ so the real part sets the envelope rate and the imaginary part sets the oscillation rate.

$$
x(t)=e^{(-2+j5)t}=e^{-2t}e^{j5t}
$$

Substitute $s=-2+j5$ into $x(t)=e^{st}$ and separate the result into the real-part factor and the imaginary-part factor. In general, $e^{(\sigma+j\omega)t}=e^{\sigma t}e^{j\omega t}$.

Here $e^{-2t}$ is the envelope and $e^{j5t}$ is the oscillation. Because the real part is negative, the envelope decays, so $s=-2+j5$ gives a damped complex exponential.
