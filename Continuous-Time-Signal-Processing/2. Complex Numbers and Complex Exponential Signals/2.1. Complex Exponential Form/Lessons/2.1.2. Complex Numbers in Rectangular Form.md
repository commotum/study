## Introduction to Complex Numbers in Rectangular Form

In this course, a complex quantity in rectangular form is written as $z = a + jb$. Read it by treating $a$ as the real part and the coefficient of $j$ as the imaginary part.

$$
z = a + jb
$$

Use the simplest base case, $z = 3 + j2$. Here the real part is $3$ and the imaginary part is $2$ because the number is already split into a real term and a $j$-term.

$$
z = 3 + j2
$$

$$
a = 3
$$

$$
b = 2
$$

You can think of the pair $(a, b)$ as rectangular coordinates for the same complex quantity. That coordinate reading is the one this module will reuse later when the lesson moves from rectangular form toward polar form, phasors, and complex exponentials.

## Identifying Real and Imaginary Parts in a + jb

**Example:** Given $z = 6 - j3$, identify its real part and imaginary part.

**Explanation**

The cue is a rectangular-form complex number written as $z = a + jb$. In this notation, the term without $j$ is the real part, and the coefficient of $j$ is the imaginary part.

For $z = 6 - j3$, separate the parts by reading the constant term and the $j$-term. The real part is $6$, and the imaginary part is $-3$ because the coefficient of $j$ keeps its sign.

So the expression is read as $a = 6$ and $b = -3$. That is the same extraction rule the next examples will reuse, even when the sign or order changes.

## Writing Complex Signal Quantities in a + jb Form

**Example:** A complex quantity has real part $-4$ and imaginary part $7$. Write it in $a + jb$ form.

**Explanation**

The cue is that the real part and imaginary part are already given. To write the quantity in rectangular form, put the real part first and then attach $j$ to the imaginary coefficient.

Here the real part is $-4$, so the $a$ term is $-4$. The imaginary part is $7$, so the $b$ term becomes $+j7$.

Combine the two pieces to get $-4 + j7$. That is the rectangular-form expression that matches the given parts.

## Interpreting a and b as Rectangular Coordinates

**Example:** A point is plotted at $(4, -1)$ on the complex plane. Write the corresponding complex number in rectangular form.

**Explanation**

![](<../Source/2.1.2. Complex Numbers in Rectangular Form/Images/l002-s004-te-section-004-image-1.png>)

Use the complex-plane axes as the cue: the horizontal axis gives the real part and the vertical axis gives the imaginary part. For the plotted point $(4, -1)$, that means $a = 4$ and $b = -1$.

Write the number in rectangular form as $a + jb$. Substituting the coordinates gives $4 + j(-1)$.

So the complex number is $4 + j(-1)$, which is the same as $4 - j$. The plotted point and the rectangular form match exactly.

## Recognizing Zero Real or Imaginary Components

**Example:** Simplify $5 + j0$ and $0 + j(-4)$, and identify which component is zero in each case.

**Explanation**

The cue is the zero imaginary coefficient in $5 + j0$. Because $j0 = 0$, the imaginary term disappears, so $5 + j0 = 5$.

The cue in $0 + j(-4)$ is a zero real part. That leaves only the imaginary term, so $0 + j(-4) = -4j$.

So the zero imaginary component belongs to the first expression, and the zero real component belongs to the second. In both cases, the nonzero component stays unchanged.
