Tutorial blocks:
- paragraph:
  text: The convolution output at time t is found by building one product on the $\tau$-axis and then integrating that product over $\tau$. In this setup, t is the output time you hold fixed, while $\tau$ is the variable that sweeps across the input timeline.
- display-math:
  latex: y(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x(\tau)h(t-\tau)\,d\tau
- paragraph:
  text: The flip-shift-multiply-integrate procedure is the concrete way to set up that integral. First flip the impulse response to form $h(-\tau)$. Next shift that flipped response by the fixed output time t to form $h(t-\tau)$. Then multiply it by $x(\tau)$. Finally, integrate the product over $\tau$.
- image:
  image_slot_id: section-001
- paragraph:
  text: The important point is that $h(t-\tau)$ is not drawn by moving $\tau$ itself. You choose one value of t, keep that value fixed, and view $h(t-\tau)$ as the flipped impulse response shifted along the $\tau$-axis.
- display-math:
  latex: h(\tau)\rightarrow h(-\tau)\rightarrow h(t-\tau)\rightarrow x(\tau)h(t-\tau)\rightarrow \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x(\tau)h(t-\tau)\,d\tau
- paragraph:
  text: This lesson uses the procedure to set up $y(t)$. Later work can focus on identifying overlap intervals and evaluating the integral, but the setup always follows the same order: flip, shift, multiply, integrate.
